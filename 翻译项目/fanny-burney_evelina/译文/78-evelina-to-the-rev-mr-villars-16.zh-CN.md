LXXV Evelina to the Rev. Mr. Villars
Clifton, Oct. 3rd.
This morning I saw from my window, that Lord Orville was walking in the garden; but I would not go downstairs till breakfast was ready: and then, he paid me his compliments almost as coldly as Lady Louisa paid hers.
I took my usual place, and Mrs. Belmont, Lady Louisa, and Mrs. Selwyn, entered into their usual conversation.﻿—Not so your Evelina: disregarded, silent, and melancholy, she sat like a cipher, whom, to nobody belonging, by nobody was noticed.
Ill brooking such a situation, and unable to suport the neglect of Lord Orville, the moment breakfast was over I left the room, and was going upstairs; when, very unpleasantly, I was stopped by Sir Clement Willoughby, who, flying into the hall, prevented my proceeding.
He enquired very particularly after my health, and entreated me to return into the parlour. Unwillingly, I consented, but thought anything preferable to continuing alone with him; and he would neither leave me, nor suffer me to pass on. Yet, in returning, I felt not a little ashamed at appearing thus to take the visit of Sir Clement to myself. And, indeed, he endeavoured, by his manner of addressing me, to give it that air.
He stayed, I believe, an hour; nor would he, perhaps, even then have gone, had not Mrs. Beaumont broken up the party, by proposing an airing in her coach. Lady Louisa consented to accompany her; but Mrs. Selwyn, when applied to, said, “If my Lord, or Sir Clement, will join us, I shall be happy to make one;﻿—but really a trio of females will be nervous to the last degree.”
Sir Clement readily agreed to attend them; indeed, he makes it his evident study to court the favour of Mrs. Beaumont. Lord Orville excused himself from going out; and I retired to my own room. What he did with himself I know not, for I would not go downstairs till dinner was ready: his coldness, though my own change of behaviour had occasioned it, so cruelly depresses my spirits, that I know not how to support myself in his presence.
At dinner, I found Sir Clement again of the party. Indeed, he manages everything his own way; for Mrs. Beaumont, though by no means easy to please, seems quite at his disposal.

今天早晨，我从窗口望见奥维尔勋爵在园中散步；可我直到早饭备好才肯下楼：而他给我的问安，几乎同路易莎小姐的一般冷淡。

这样的处境实在难受，奥维尔勋爵的冷淡又受不住，早饭一完我便离了客厅，正要上楼，却被克莱门特·威洛比爵士很不痛快地拦住了；他一阵风闯进厅堂，拦住了我的去路。
他絮絮地问起我的健康，恳求我回客厅去。我勉强应了，心想怎样都比同他单独待在一起强；可他既不肯走开，又不放我过去。回客厅时，我倒颇有些惭愧，觉得像是把克莱门特爵士的拜访整个儿摊到了自己身上。而且，说真的，他同我说话的那副做派，也存心要造成这种印象。
他待了，我想，足足一个钟头；若不是博蒙特夫人提议乘她的马车兜风，把这伙人散了，只怕他到那时还不肯走。路易莎小姐答应陪她去；问到塞尔温夫人时，她说：“勋爵也好，克莱门特爵士也好，只要肯赏光同去，我乐意凑一个；——可三个女的凑作一伙，实在神经受不住。”
克莱门特爵士一口应承；真的，讨好博蒙特夫人的欢心，显然是他着意用心的功课。奥维尔勋爵辞谢不出；我便退回自己房里。他独自做些什么，我不知道，因为到晚饭备好我才下楼：他的冷淡——虽说原是我自己改了态度招来的——狠狠压着我的精神，我在他面前简直不知怎样撑持。
晚饭时，我发现克莱门特爵士又在此列。真的，事事都由着他的意思办；因为博蒙特夫人虽极难讨好，如今却仿佛全凭他摆布。
