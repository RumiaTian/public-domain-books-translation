## Letter / 书信
===Original===

XVIII Evelina in Continuation
I had just finished my letter to you this morning, when a violent rapping at the door made me run downstairs; and who should I see in the drawing room, but﻿—Lord Orville!
He was quite alone, for the family had not assembled to breakfast. He enquired first of mine, then of the health of Mrs. and Miss Mirvan, with a degree of concern that rather surprised me, till he said he had just been informed of the accident we had met with at Ranelagh. He expressed his sorrow upon the occasion with utmost politeness, and lamented that he had not been so fortunate as to hear of it in time to offer his services. “But I think,” he added, “Sir Clement Willoughby had the honour of assisting you?”
“He was with Captain Mirvan, my Lord.”
“I had heard of his being of your party.”
I hope that flighty man has not been telling Lord Orville he only assisted *me*! however, he did not pursue the subject: but said, “This accident though extremely unfortunate, will not, I hope, be the means of frightening you from gracing Ranelagh with your presence in future?”
“Our time, my Lord, for London, is almost expired already.”
“Indeed! do you leave town so very soon?”
“O yes, my Lord, our stay has already exceeded our intentions.”
“Are you, then, so particularly partial to the country?”
“We merely came to town, my Lord, to meet Captain Mirvan.”
“And does Miss Anville feel no concern at the idea of the many mourners her absence will occasion?”
“O, my Lord﻿—I’m sure you don’t think﻿—” I stopped there; for, indeed, I hardly knew what I was going to say. My foolish embarrassment, I suppose, was the cause of what followed; for he came to me, and took my hand saying, “I *do* think, that whoever has once seen Miss Anville, must receive an impression never to be forgotten.”
This compliment﻿—from Lord Orville﻿—so surprised me, that I could not speak; but felt myself change colour, and stood for some moments silent, and looking down: however, the instant I recollected my situation, I withdrew my hand, and told him that I would see if Mrs. Mirvan was not dressed. He did not oppose me﻿—so away I went.
I met them all on the stairs, and returned with them to breakfast.

第十八封 伊芙琳娜续

今天早晨我刚给您写完信，一阵急促的打门声叫我跑下楼去；在客厅里看见的，竟是——奥维尔勋爵！

他独自一人，一家人还没下来吃早饭。他先问我的安康，又问米尔文太太和小姐的安康，那份关切叫我颇为诧异，直到他说，刚听说我们在拉内拉夫出的那桩事故。他就此致歉，礼数周全到极点，又惋惜自己没能及时得到消息、好效一份力。“不过我想，”他补了一句，“是克莱门特·威洛比爵士有幸助了您一臂？”

“他当时同米尔文舰长在一起，大人。”

“我听说他也在你们一伙里。”

但愿那个轻浮的人没有对奥维尔勋爵说，他帮的只是*我一个人*！好在他没有再追这个话头，只说：“这桩事故固然极其不幸，可我希望，不至于吓得您从此不敢再光临拉内拉夫吧？”

“我们留在伦敦的日子，大人，差不多已经到头了。”

“当真！这么快就走？”

“哦是的，大人，我们此来已超出了原定的打算。”

“那么，小姐对乡下情有独钟了？”

“我们进城，大人，专为迎候米尔文舰长。”

“那么，安维尔小姐一想到她这一走要留下多少伤心人，就毫不动心么？”

“哦，大人——我确信您并不以为——”我说到这儿停住了；因为说实话，我几乎不知道自己要说什么。想必是我那副傻态惹出了下文；他走到我跟前，拉起我的手说：“我*确实*以为，凡是见过安维尔小姐一面的人，得到的印象都终生难忘。”

这句恭维话——出自奥维尔勋爵——叫我惊讶得说不出话，只觉得脸色变了，低着头默默站了片刻；可我一醒悟过来自己的处境，立刻抽回手，说去看看米尔文太太穿好衣裳没有。他没有拦我——我就此走掉了。

我在楼梯上碰见他们全家，随他们回去用了早饭。

===Original===

I have since been extremely angry with myself for neglecting so excellent an opportunity of apologizing for my behaviour at the ridotto: but, to own the truth, that affair never once occurred to me during the short tête-à-tête which we had together. But, if ever we should happen to be so situated again, I will certainly mention it; for I am inexpressibly concerned at the thought of his harbouring an opinion that I am bold or impertinent, and I could almost kill myself for having given him the shadow of a reason for so shocking an idea.
But was not it very odd that he should make me such a compliment? I expected it not from him;﻿—but gallantry, I believe, is common to all men, whatever other qualities they may have in particular.
Our breakfast was the most agreeable meal, if it may be called a *meal*, that we have had since we came to town. Indeed, but for Madame Duval, I should like London extremely.
The conversation of Lord Orville is really delightful. His manners are so elegant, so gentle, so unassuming, that they at once engage esteem, and diffuse complacence. Far from being indolently satisfied with his own accomplishments, as I have already observed many men here are, though without any pretensions to his merit, he is most assiduously attentive to please and to serve all who are in his company, and, though his success is invariable, he never manifests the smallest degree of consciousness.
I could wish that *you*, my dearest Sir, knew Lord Orville, because I am sure you would love him; and I have felt that wish for no other person I have seen since I came to London. I sometimes imagine, that when his youth is flown, his vivacity abated, and his life is devoted to retirement, he will, perhaps, resemble him whom I most love and honour. His present sweetness, politeness, and diffidence, seem to promise in future the same benevolence, dignity, and goodness. But I must not expatiate upon this subject.
When Lord Orville was gone﻿—and he made but a very short visit﻿—I was preparing, most reluctantly, to wait upon Madame Duval; but Mrs. Mirvan proposed to the Captain, that she should be invited to dinner in Queen Ann Street; and he readily consented, for he said he wished to ask after her Lyons negligee.
The invitation is accepted, and we expect her every moment.

过后我大大地生自己的气：竟错过了那么好一个机会，为我在里多托的行为道歉。可是说老实话，我们单独相对的那一小会儿，那件事我压根儿一次也没想起。不过，倘若再遇上那样的场合，我一定提它；因为一想到他也许认为我冒失、无礼，我就说不出的难受；一想到自己竟给了他一丁点理由，生出这样骇人的想法，我几乎想杀了自己。

可他对我讲那样一句恭维话，岂不很奇怪？我没有料到出自他；——不过我想，殷勤是男人的通病，无论他们各自还另有怎样的品性。

我们这顿早饭，是进京以来最可心的一餐——如果它也算得上一*餐*的话。真的，若没有杜瓦尔夫人，我会非常喜欢伦敦。

奥维尔勋爵的谈吐实在动人。他的举止那样典雅、那样温文、那样不自矜，一下子就赢得敬重，又让满座如沐春风。他绝不像我在此地已经见惯的许多男人那样——毫无他的才具，却懒洋洋地自我满足于自己那点本事——他殷勤备至，一心取悦、服侍身边的每一个人；虽然他无往不利，却从不露出一丝自觉。

我多么希望*您*，最亲爱的先生，认识奥维尔勋爵，因为我确信您会爱他；自到伦敦以来，我见的人里，惟独对他起过这个念头。我有时想象，等他的青春飞逝、锐气收敛、把一生交给闲退之后，他也许会同我最爱最敬的那一位相像。他今日的温婉、谦和与不自恃，似乎预示着来日的仁厚、端方与善良。可这个题目我不能再铺陈下去了。

奥维尔勋爵走后——他只坐了很短一会儿——我正老大不情愿地准备去杜瓦尔夫人那儿；米尔文太太却提议舰长，请她来女王安妮街吃饭；他一口应承，因为他说他想问问她那件里昂家常袍的下文。

请帖接下了，我们时时刻刻都在等她来。

But to me, it is very strange, that a woman who is the uncontrolled mistress of her time, fortune, and actions, should choose to expose herself voluntarily to the rudeness of a man who is openly determined to make her his sport. But she has very few acquaintance; and, I fancy, scarce knows how to employ herself.
How great is my obligation to Mrs. Mirvan, for bestowing her time in a manner so disagreeable to herself, merely to promote my happiness! Every dispute in which her undeserving husband engages, is productive of pain and uneasiness to herself; of this I am so sensible, that I even besought her not to send to Madame Duval; but she declared she could not bear to have me pass all my time, while in town, with her only. Indeed she could not be more kind to me, were she your daughter.

===Chinese===

可我总觉得奇怪极了：一个女人，时间、财产、行动全都由自己做主，偏要自愿送到一个男人面前受粗鲁——那男人还明摆着打定主意拿她耍笑。可她的相识实在太少；我猜，她几乎不知道怎么打发自己。

我对米尔文太太负着多大的情分啊——只为成全我的幸福，把自己的光阴花在这样一件她自己也厌烦的事上！她那位不配的丈夫每挑起一场争执，带给她的全是痛苦与不安；这个我太明白了，以致我恳求过她别去接杜瓦尔夫人；可她声言，不忍心看我在城里期间，把全部光阴都耗在她一个人身上。说真的，倘若是您的女儿，她也不过如此了。
