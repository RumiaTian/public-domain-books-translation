## XVI / 十六
===Original===

They Come to the House of the Sorceress

On the morrow the kind people delayed them little, though they sorrowed for their departure, and before noon were their old way-leaders ready for them; and the old man and his two grandsons (for such they were) were much honoured of the simple people for their way-leading of the Heavenly Folk; for so they called Ralph and Ursula. So they gat them to the way in suchlike guise as before, only they had with them five sumpter oxen instead of two; for the old man told them that not only was their way longer, but also they must needs pass through a terrible waste, wherein was naught for their avail, neither man, nor beast, nor herb. Even so they found it as he said; for after the first day’s ride from the town they came to the edge of this same waste, and on the fourth day were deep in the heart of it: a desert it was, rather rocky and stony and sandy than mountainous, though they had hills to cross also: withal there was but little water there, and that foul and stinking. Long lasted this waste, and Ralph thought indeed that it had been hard to cross, had not their way-leaders been; therefore he made marks and signs by the wayside, and took note of the bearings of rocks and mounds against the day of return.

Twelve days they rode this waste, and on the thirteenth it began to mend somewhat, and there was a little grass, and sweet waters, and they saw ahead the swelling hills of a great woodland, albeit they had to struggle through marshland and low scrubby thicket for a day longer, or ever they got to the aforesaid trees, which at first were naught but pines; but these failed in a while, and they rode a grass waste nearly treeless, but somewhat well watered, where they gat them good store of venison. Thereafter they came on woods of oak and sweet-chestnut, with here and there a beechwood.

他们来到女巫之屋

次日，善良的人们虽为他们的离去难过，却没有多加挽留；不到正午，原先那几位带路人已为他们备好。老人和他的两个孙子（正是这层关系）因给天人带路，在纯朴的人们中间备受尊荣——天人便是他们对拉尔夫和厄休拉的称呼。于是他们照旧启程，只是驮畜由两头牛换成了五头；因为老人告诉他们，此去不但路程更长，还须穿过一片可怕的荒原，那里于人无益，无兽、无草。果然如他所说：离开城后第一天的路程，他们便到了这片荒原的边缘，第四天已深入它的腹地。那是一片沙漠，与其说多山，不如说多岩、多石、多沙——虽然也有山丘要翻越；此外那里水极少，而且又臭又浑。这片荒原走了很久；拉尔夫心想，若不是有带路人，实在难以穿越；因此他在路旁做下记号，记下岩石和土冈的方位，以备归程之用。

他们在荒原上走了十二天，第十三天地面渐渐好转，有了些草，水也甜了；他们望见前方一片大林地的隆起山丘，只是还须在沼泽和低矮的灌木丛中挣扎一天，才能到得上述的树林——起初那不过是些松树；不久松林尽了，又走一片几乎无树的草地荒原，但水源还算充足，他们在那里猎得了不少野味。此后便来到橡树和甜栗的树林，间或一片山毛榉。

Long and long they rode the woodland, but it was hard on May when they entered it, and it was pleasant therein, and what with one thing, what with another, they had abundant livelihood there. Yet was June at its full when at last they came within sight of the House of the Sorceress, on the hottest of a fair afternoon. And it was even as Ralph had seen it pictured in the arras of the hall of the Castle of Abundance; a little house built after the fashion of houses in his own land of the west; the thatch was trim, and the windows and doors were unbroken, and the garth was whole, and the goats feeding therein, and the wheat was tall and blossoming in the little closes, whereas he had looked to see all broken down and wild, and as to the house, a mere grass-grown heap, or at the most a broken gable fast crumbling away.

Then waxed his heart sore with the memory of that passed time, and the sweetness of his short-lived love, though he refrained him all he might: yet forsooth Ursula looked on him anxiously, so much his face was changed by the thoughts of his heart.

But the elder of the way-leaders saw that he was moved, and deemed that he was wondering at that house so trim and orderly amidst the wildwood, so he said: “Here also do we after our behest to that marvellous and lovely Lady, that we suffer not this house to go to ruin: ever are some of our folk here, and every year about this season we send two or more to take the places of those who have dwelt in the House yearlong: so ever is there someone to keep all things trim. But as to strangers, I have never in my life seen any Seeker of the Well herein, save once, and that was an old hoar man like to me, save that he was feebler in all wise than I be.”

Now Ralph heard him talking, yet noted his words but little; for it was with him as if all the grief of heart which he had penned back for so long a while swelled up within him and burst its bounds; and he turned toward Ursula and their eyes met, and she looked shy and anxious on him and he might no longer refrain himself, but put his hands to his face (for they had now drawn rein at the garth-gate) and brake out a weeping, and wept long for the friend whose feet had worn that path so often, and whose heart, though she were dead, had brought them thither for their thriving; and for love and sorrow of him Ursula wept also.

他们在林中走了很久很久；进林时已近五月，林中景致宜人，加上这样那样的出产，生计绰绰有余。待终于望见女巫之屋时，六月已圆，那是一个晴好午间最炎热的时分。那屋子正如拉尔夫在丰饶堡大厅的挂毯上见过的一样：一座小屋，样式如同他西部家乡的房屋；茅草修得整整齐齐，门窗完好无损，园圃齐整，山羊在里面吃草，小块的围地里麦子长得高高的，正在抽穗开花。他原以为会见到一片残破荒芜，屋子呢，至多是一堆长满荒草的废墟，或是一堵正在崩塌的断山墙。

旧日时光和那段短促爱情的甜美涌上心头，他心里痛楚起来，尽管极力克制；厄休拉却不安地望着他，因为他心中的思绪已使面色大变。

带路人中的老人见他那般激动，只当他是在惊异荒林之中竟有这样齐整的宅子，便说：「我们遵着那位奇妙可爱的夫人的嘱托，在这里也不许这座屋子倾颓：我们的族人常年有人在此，每年这个时节，我们都会派两三个或更多的人来，接替在屋里住满一年的人，所以总有人把一切收拾得妥妥帖帖。至于外人，我平生在这里只见过一位寻泉客，仅此一回；那是个白发苍苍的老人，与我相仿，只是处处比我衰弱。」

拉尔夫听见他说话，却几乎没听进那些话；只觉得久来强压在心头的悲伤涨了起来，冲破了堤岸；他转向厄休拉，两人目光相遇，她羞怯而不安地望着他；他再也忍不住，双手掩面（这时他们已在园门口勒住马），放声哭了出来，久久哭泣——为那位曾经无数次踏熟这条小径的朋友；她虽然死了，她的心却引他们来到这里，让他们得享兴盛。出于对他的爱与哀伤，厄休拉也哭了。

But the old man and his grandsons turned their heads away from his weeping, and got off their horses, and went up to the house-door, whereby were now standing a carle and a quean of their people. But Ralph slowly gat off his horse and stood by Ursula who was on the ground already, but would not touch her, for he was ashamed. But she looked on him kindly and said: “Dear friend, there is no need for shame; for though I be young, I know how grievous it is when the dead that we have loved come across our ways, and we may not speak to them, nor they to us. So I will but bid thee be comforted and abide in thy love for the living and the dead.” His tears brake out again at that word, for he was but young, and for a while there was a lull in the strife that had beset his days. But after a little he looked up, and dashed the tears from his eyes and smiled on Ursula and said: “The tale she told me of this place, the sweetness of it came back upon me, and I might not forbear.” She said: “O friend, thou art kind, and I love thee.”

So then they joined hands and went through the garth together, and up to the door, where stood the wardens, who, when they saw them turning thither, came speedily down the path to them, and would have knelt in worship to them; but they would not suffer it, but embraced and kissed them, and thanked them many times for their welcome. The said wardens, both carle and quean, were goodly folk of middle age, stalwart, and kind of face.

So then they went into the house together, and entered into the selfsame chamber, where of old the Lady of Abundance had sickened for fear of the Sorceress sitting naked at her spell-work.

老人和他的孙子们把脸从他哭泣的方向转开，下了马，向屋门走去——门口已站着一男一女两个他们的族人。拉尔夫缓缓下马，站到厄休拉身旁——她早已下了地——却不敢碰她，因为他心里羞愧。她温柔地望着他，说：「亲爱的朋友，不必羞愧；我虽然年轻，却知道那是怎样难堪：我们爱过的死者横在路上，我们不能对他们说话，他们也不能对我们说。我只想劝你宽心，把对生者与死者的爱都守在心里。」听了这话他的眼泪又涌出来，因为他毕竟还年轻；而这一刻，连日缠绕他的纷争也有了个间歇。过了一会儿他抬起头，抹去眼泪，对厄休拉笑了笑，说：「她从前给我讲这地方的故事，那份甜美又涌上心头，我忍不住。」她说：「啊，朋友，你真好，我爱你。」

于是两人携起手，一同穿过园圃，向屋门走去。门口守着屋子的那两人见他们转来，急忙顺着小径迎上前，要跪拜他们；他们却不肯受，反而拥抱亲吻他们，为这番欢迎再三道谢。那两个守屋人，一男一女，都是中年良善之辈，身强力壮，面容和善。

随后他们一同进屋，走进的正是当年那间屋子——丰饶夫人在那里因惧怕赤身裸体作法的女巫而生过一场大病。

Great joy they made together, and the wardens set meat and drink before the guests, and they ate and drank and were of good cheer. But the elder who had brought them from Chestnut-dale said: “Dear friends, I have told you that these two young men are my grandchildren, and they are the sons of this man and woman whom ye see; for the man is my son. And so it is, that amongst us the care of the Quest of the Well at the World’s End hath for long been the heritage of our blood, going with us from father to son. Therefore is it naught wonderful, though I have been sundry times at this house, and have learned about the place all that may be learned. For my father brought me hither when I was yet a boy; that time it was that I saw the last man of whom we know for sure that he drank of the Water of the Well, and he was that old hoar man like unto me, but, as I said, far weaker in all wise; but when he came back to us from the Well he was strong and stalwart, and a better man than I am now; and I heard him tell his name to my father, that he was called the Sage of Swevenham.”

Ralph looked on Ursula and said: “Yea, father, and it was through him that we had our lore concerning the way hither; and it was he that bade us abide your coming in the rock-house of the Vale of Sweet-chestnuts.”

“Then he is alive still,” said the elder. Said Ralph: “Yea, and as fair and strong an old man as ye may lightly see.” “Yea, yea,” said the elder, “and yet fifty years ago his course seemed run.”

Then said Ralph: “Tell me, father, have none of your own folk sought to the Well at the World’s End?” “Nay, none,” said the elder. Said Ralph: “That is strange, whereas ye are so nigh thereto, and have such abundant lore concerning the way.”

众人一起欢叙，守屋人在客人面前摆上酒肉，大家吃喝快活。从栗谷送他们来的老人说：「亲爱的朋友，我告诉过你们，这两个年轻人是我的孙子，是你们眼前这一男一女的儿子——那男人是我的儿子。事情就是这样：在我们中间，照管世界尽头之泉探寻之旅，久已是我们血脉的世传，父子相承。所以我来过这屋子好几回、把这地方能学的都学了，一点也不奇怪。我父亲带我到这里来时，我还是个孩子；正是那一次，我见到了我们所确知喝过泉水的最后一人；他就是那个和我相仿的白发老人，但如我所说，处处比我衰弱得多；可他从泉边回到我们那里时，强壮魁伟，比现在的我还要强健；我听他对我父亲通报姓名，说他叫斯韦文汉的贤者。」

拉尔夫望了厄休拉一眼，说：「是啊，老丈，我们得以来此的路途学问，正是得自他；是他嘱咐我们在甜栗之谷的石屋里等候你们到来。」

「那么他还活着。」老人说。拉尔夫说：「活着，还是一位你们轻易见得到的健旺强壮的老人。」「是啊，是啊，」老人说，「可五十年前，他的寿数好象就到头了。」

拉尔夫又问：「老丈，告诉我，你们本族的人就没有去寻过世界尽头之泉吗？」「没有，一个也没有。」老人说。拉尔夫说：「这就怪了，你们离得这么近，又有这么完备的路途学问。」

“Son,” said the elder, “true it is that the water of that Well shall cause a man to thrive in all ways, and to live through many generations of men, maybe, in honour and good-liking; but it may not keep any man alive forever; for so have the Gods given us the gift of death lest we weary of life. Now our folk live well and hale, and without the sickness and pestilence, such as I have heard oft befall folk in other lands: even as I heard the Sage of Swevenham say, and I wondered at his words. Of strife and of war also we know naught: nor do we desire aught which we may not easily attain to. Therefore we live long, and we fear the Gods if we should strive to live longer, lest they should bring upon us war and sickness, and overweening desire, and weariness of life. Moreover it is little that all of us should seek to the Well at the World’s End; and those few that sought and drank should be stronger and wiser than the others, and should make themselves earthly gods, and, maybe, should torment the others of us and make their lives a very burden to be borne. Of such matters are there tales current amongst us that so it hath been of yore and in other lands; and ill it were if such times came back upon us.”

Ralph hung his head and was silent; for the joy of the Quest seemed dying out as the old man’s words dropped slowly from his mouth. But he smiled upon Ralph and went on: “But for you, guests, it is otherwise, for ye of the World beyond the Mountains are stronger and more godlike than we, as all tales tell; and ye wear away your lives desiring that which ye may scarce get; and ye set your hearts on high things, desiring to be masters of the very Gods. Therefore ye know sickness and sorrow, and oft ye die before your time, so that ye must depart and leave undone things which ye deem ye were born to do; which to all men is grievous. And because of all this ye desire healing and thriving, whether good come of it, or ill. Therefore ye do but right to seek to the Well at the World’s End, that ye may the better accomplish that which behoveth you, and that ye may serve your fellows and deliver them from the thralldom of those that be strong and unwise and unkind, of whom we have heard strange tales.”

Ralph reddened as he spake, and Ursula looked on him anxiously, but that talk dropped for the present, and they fell to talking of lighter and more familiar matters.

「孩子，」老人说，「那泉水确实能叫人事事兴旺，也许还能让人体面安康地活过好几代人；可它不能叫任何人永生不死；诸神正是因此才把死亡赐给我们，免得我们对生命厌倦。我们的族人活得康健，没有疾病瘟疫——我常听说别处的人常遭这些灾殃，正如我听斯韦文汉的贤者说过的那样，我当时就对他的话称奇。争斗和战争我们也一无所知；我们也不贪求任何不易得到的东西。所以我们长寿；而我们若想活得更长，便怕诸神降下战争、疾病、骄纵的贪欲和对生命的厌倦。再说，我们若人人都去寻世界尽头之泉，也很不妥：寻到喝过的那几个，就会比旁人更强更智，自命为人间的神明，说不定还要折磨我们其余的人，把他们的生命变成一副重担。这类事，我们中间流传的故事说，古时、在别处，就是这样的；那样的时代若再回来，就糟了。」

拉尔夫低下头，默默无言；随着老人的话一字一字缓缓落下，探寻的欢欣仿佛正在熄灭。老人却朝他微微一笑，接着说：「至于你们，客人，却是另一回事：山外世界的人比我们强大、比我们更近神明，一切故事都这么说；你们把生命耗在渴求难以到手的东西上；你们把心放在高处，连诸神也想驾驭。所以你们有病痛忧愁，常常未尽天年而死，撇下你们认为生来要做的事没有做完——这对谁都是痛事。正因为这一切，你们渴望疗治与兴旺，不论它带来善还是恶。所以你们去寻世界尽头之泉并没有错：好教你们更圆满地成就分内之事，好教你们服务同类，把他们从那些强大、愚蠢又冷酷者的奴役中解救出来——关于这类人，我们听过种种奇怪的故事。」

他说这些话时拉尔夫脸红了，厄休拉不安地望着他；这个话题眼下就此打住，他们转而谈起轻松些的家常来。

Thereafter they wandered about the woods with the wardens and the way-leaders, and the elder brought them to the ancient altar in the wood whereon the Sorceress had offered up the goat; and the howe of the woman dight with the necklace of the Quest whom the Lady found dead in the snow; and the place nigh the house where the Sorceress used to torment her thrall that was afterwards the Lady of Abundance; yea, and they went further afield till they came to the Vale of Lore, and the Heath above it where they met, the King’s Son and the Lady. All these and other places were now become as hallowed ground to the Innocent People, and to Ralph no less. In the house, moreover, was a fair ark wherein they kept matters which had belonged to the Lady, as her shoes and her smock, wrapped in goodly cloth amidst well-smelling herbs; and these things they worshipped as folk do with relics of the saints. In another ark also they showed the seekers a book wherein was written lore concerning the Well, and the way thereto. But of this book had the Sage forewarned Ralph and his mate, and had bidden them look to it that they should read in it, and no otherwhere than at that ancient altar in the wood, they two alone, and clad in suchlike gear as they wore when they hearkened to his reading by his hermitage. And so it was that they found the due raiment in the ark along with the book. Therefore day after day betimes in the morning they bore the said book to the altar and read therein, till they had learned much wisdom.

Thus they did for eight days, and on the ninth they rested and were merry with their hosts: but on the tenth day they mounted their horses and said farewell, and departed by the ways they had learned of, they two alone. And they had with them bread and meal, as much as they might bear, and water-skins moreover, that they might fill them at the last sweet water before they came to the waterless desert.

此后他们同守屋人和带路人一起在林中各处游逛；老人领他们去看林中那座古祭坛——女巫当年曾在上面献祭山羊；去看那座坟莹——葬着佩戴探寻项链、被夫人发现死在雪里的女子；去看屋旁那处地方——女巫从前在那里折磨她的女奴，那女奴后来成了丰饶夫人；是的，他们还走得更远，直到学问之谷和谷上方的那片荒原——王子与夫人当年相会之处。所有这些地方以及其他地方，如今在无辜子民眼中都成了圣地，在拉尔夫眼中也不例外。屋里还有一口漂亮的柜子，里面收藏着夫人的遗物，如她的鞋子和衬衣，用贵重的布裹着，衬着香草；这些物件他们奉若圣徒的遗物。在另一口柜子里，他们还给两位寻泉客看了一本书，书里写着关于那泉与路途的学问。这本书贤者早对拉尔夫和他的同伴预先叮嘱过，嘱咐他们务必只在林中那座古祭坛前读，只许他们两人独读，并且要穿在他们隐庐听读时所穿的那类衣裳。果然，他们在这口柜子里连书带应穿的衣裳都找到了。于是一天又一天，他们一早把那本书捧到祭坛前诵读，直到学得了许多智慧。

这样过了八天，第九天他们歇息，与主人们一同欢乐；第十天他们上马道别，循所学得的路径出发了，只他们两人。他们带了面包和面粉，能带多少带多少，还带了水囊，好在到达无水的荒漠之前，在最后一处甜水泉灌满。
