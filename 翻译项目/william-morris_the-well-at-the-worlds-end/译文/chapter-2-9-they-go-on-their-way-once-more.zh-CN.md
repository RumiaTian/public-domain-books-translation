## IX / 九
===Original===

They Go on Their Way Once More

“Look now,” she said, “I have held thee so long in talk, that the afternoon is waning; now is it time for us to be on the way again; not because I misdoubt me of thy foeman, but because I would take thee to a fairer dwelling of the desert, and one where I have erst abided; and moreover, there thou shalt not altogether die of hunger. See, is it not as if I had thought to meet thee here?”

“Yea, in good sooth,” said he, “I wot that thou canst see the story of things before they fall.”

She laughed and said: “But all this that hath befallen since I set out to meet thee at the Castle of Abundance I foresaw not, any more than I can foresee tomorrow. Only I knew that we must needs pass by the place whereto I shall now lead thee, and I made provision there. Lo! now the marvel slain: and in such wise shall perish other marvels which have been told of me; yet not all. Come now, let us to the way.”

So they joined hands and left the pleasant place, and were again going speedily amidst the close pinewoods awhile, where it was smooth underfoot and silent of noises withal.

Now Ralph said: “Beloved, thou hast told me of many things, but naught concerning how thou camest to be wedded to the Knight of the Sun, and of thy dealings with him.”

他们重又上路

“你瞧，”她说，“我拉着你说得太久，下午都过半了；该再上路了：不是我怕你的仇家，是我想带你去荒野里一处更好的住处，一处我从前住过的；再说，在那里你也不至饿死。看，这不正像我早料到会在这里遇着你么？”

“正是，当真，”他说，“我知道你看得见事情还没发生时的来龙去脉。”

她笑了：“可我动身去丰饶之堡会你以后发生的这一切，我并没有先见——同我望不见明天一样。我只知道我们必得经过我这就领你去的地方，就在那里备了置。瞧！奇事这就掳破了：人家传说我的别的奇事，也要这样一件件破掉——可不会全破。来吧，上路。”

两人携起手，离开那快活地方，又在密密的松林里快步穿行了一阵：脚下平坦，四下没有声息。

拉尔夫说：“亲亲，你讲了我许多事，只没讲你怎么嫁了太阳骑士，同他又是一段怎样的来往。”

Said she, reddening withal: “I will tell thee no more than this, unless thou compel me: that he would have me wed him, as it were against my will, till I ceased striving against him, and I went with him to Sunway, which is no great way from the Castle of Abundance, and there befell that treason of Walter the Black, who loved me and prayed for my love, and when I gainsaid him, swore by all that was holy, before my lord, that it was I who sought his love, and how I had told and taught him ways of witchcraft, whereby we might fulfill our love, so that the Baron should keep a wife for another man. And the Knight of the Sun, whose heart had been filled with many tales of my wisdom, true and false, believed his friend whom he loved, and still believeth him, though he burneth for the love of me now; whereas in those first days of the treason, he burned with love turned to hatred. So of this came that shaming and casting-forth of me. Whereof I will tell thee but this, that the brother of my lord, even the tall champion whom thou hast seen, came upon me presently, when I was cast forth; because he was coming to see the Knight of the Sun at his home; and he loved me, but not after the fashion of his brother, but was kind and mild with me. So then I went with him to Hampton and the Dry Tree, and great joy made the folk thereof of my coming, whereas they remembered their asking of aforetime that I would come to be a Queen over them, and there have I dwelt ever since betwixt Hampton and the Castle of Abundance; and that tall champion has been ever as a brother unto me.”

Said Ralph, “And thou art their Queen there?” “Yea,” she said, “in a fashion; yet have they another who is mightier than I, and might, if she durst, hang me over the battlements of the Scaur, for she is a fierce and hard woman, and now no longer young in years.”

“Is it not so then,” said Ralph, “that some of the ill deeds that are told of thee are of her doing?”

“It is even so,” she said, “and whiles when she has spoken the word I may not be against her openly, therefore I use my wisdom which I have learned, to set free luckless wights from her anger and malice. More by token the last time I did thus was the very night of the day we parted, after thou hadst escaped from the Burg.”

她红着脸说：“我只讲到这里，除非你逼我：他要娶我，几乎是强我所难，直到我不再挣扎，随他去了太阳道——那地方离丰饶之堡不远。在那里出了黑衣沃尔特的背叛：他爱我，求我的爱；我回绝了，他便当着我主人的面，指着一应圣物起誓，反说是我勾引他，说我教他妖法的门道，好成全我们的私情，叫男爵替别的男人养老婆。太阳骑士——他心里装满了我智慧的种种传闻，真的假的——信了他所爱的朋友，至今还信，虽然他如今为爱我在受煎熬；而奸情初起那几天，他烧的是化成了恨的爱。因此有了那场羞辱同驱逐。这上头我只想告诉你一句：我主人那位兄弟——就是你见过的那位高大勇士——我刚刚被逐，他就撞见了我，因为他正要去太阳道家里看太阳骑士；他也爱我，却同他兄弟两个路数，待我温存和气。我便随他去了汉普顿同枯树会；我一来，会众欢喜极了——他们记得先前求过我去做他们的女王；从此我就住在汉普顿同丰饶之堡之间；那位高大的勇士，始终像我的兄长。”

拉尔夫说：“那么你是他们的女王？”“是，”她说，“算是吧；可他们还有一位比我权大的，她若敢，恨不得把我吊死在崖头的雉堞上：她是个凶悍狠硬的女人，如今上了年纪。”

“那么，”拉尔夫说，“外头传你的那些恶事，有几件莫不是她做的？”

“正是，”她说，“有几分是她发的话，我明里又拗不得她，只好用我学来的智慧，把落难的可怜人从她的怒火毒计里悄悄放走。尤其上一回我这样做，正是你我分手那天的当夜——你逃出四弗里斯堡之后。”

“In what wise was that?” said Ralph. She said: “When I rode away from thee on that happy day of my deliverance by thee, my heart laughed for joy of the life thou hadst given me, and of thee the giver, and I swore to myself that I would set free the first captive or death-doomed creature that I came across, in honour of my pleasure and delight: now speedily I came to Hampton and the Scaur; for it is not very far from the want-ways of the wood: and there I heard how four of our folk had been led away by the men of the Burg, therefore it was clear to me that I must set these men free if I could; besides, it pleased me to think that I could walk about the streets of the foemen safely, who had been but just led thitherward to the slaughter. Thou knowest how I sped therein. But when I came back again to our people, after thou hadst ridden away from us with Roger, I heard these tidings, that there was one new-come into our prison, a woman to wit, who had been haled before our old Queen for a spy and doomed by her, and should be taken forth and slain, belike, in a day or two. So I said to myself that I was not free of my vow as yet, because those friends of mine, I should in any case have done my best to deliver them: therefore I deemed my oath bound me to set that woman free. So in the night-tide when all was quiet I went to the prison and brought her forth, and led her past all the gates and wards, which was an easy thing to me, so much as I had learned, and came with her into the fields betwixt the thorp of Hampton and the wood, when it was more daylight than dawn, so that I could see her clearly, and no word as yet had we spoken to each other. But then she said to me: ‘Am I to be slain here or led to a crueller prison?’ And I said: ‘Neither one thing nor the other: for lo! I have set thee free, and I shall look to it that there shall be no pursuit of thee till thou hast had time to get clear away.’ But she said: ‘What thanks wilt thou have for this? Wherefore hast thou done it?’ And I said, ‘It is because of the gladness I have gotten.’ Said she, ‘And would that I might get gladness!’ So I asked her what was amiss now that she was free. She said: ‘I have lost one thing that I loved, and found another and lost it also.’ So I said: ‘Mightest thou not seek for the lost?’ She said, ‘It is in this wood, but when I shall find it I shall not have it.’

“那是怎么回事？”拉尔夫说。她说：“你救了我、我快活地骑马离开你那天，我的心为你给我的这条命、也给你这给命的人，笑了一路；我暗暗起誓：路上头一个撞见的俘虏或死囚，我要放了他，算对我这份欢喜的酬答。不久我就到了汉普顿同崖堡——那地方离林中岔路口不远——听说我们的人叫堡里人掳走了四个；我明白：这四个人，我必得设法救出来；再者，想到方才还被押去杀头的人，竟能在仇家的街上安然行走，我也觉得有趣。我怎样办成的，你知道。可我送走你同罗杰之后回到我们的人那里，听到一个信：牢里新关进来一个女人，被咱们的老女王当奸细提了去，判了死，看这一两天就要提出去杀。我自忖：我的誓还不算完——那几个朋友，我横竖是要救的；所以这誓还拘着我，要我放那女人。夜里万籁俱寂，我进牢把她带出来，领她过一道道门岗——这于我是易事，我学过的——走到汉普顿村同林子之间的田野里，天已大亮，我把她看得清清楚楚；我们还没交一言。这时她对我说：‘是在这里杀我，还是押我去更狠的牢？’我说：‘都不是：看，我放了你；我还担保，等你走脱了，也没人追你。’她说：‘你这恩要什么报？图什么？’我说：‘图我心里得的欢喜。’她说：‘巴不得我也能得着欢喜！’我问她如今自由了，还有什么不如意。她说：‘我丢了一样心爱的，寻着了另一样，又丢了。’我说：‘丢了的，就不能再寻么？’她说：‘就在这林子里；可我寻着它的时候，也就不得它了。’

‘It is love that thou art seeking,’ said I. ‘In what semblance is he?’

“What wilt thou, my friend? Straightway she fell to making a picture of thee in words; so that I knew that she had met thee, and belike after I had departed from thee, and my heart was sore thereat; for now I will tell thee the very truth, that she was a young woman and exceeding fair, as if she were of pearl all over, and as sweet as eglantine; and I feared her lest she should meet thee again in these wildwoods. And so I asked her what would she, and she said that she had a mind to seek to the Well at the World’s End, which quencheth all sorrow; and I rejoiced thereat, thinking that she would be far away from thee, not thinking that thou and I must even meet to seek to it also. So I gave her the chaplet which my witch-mistress took from the dead woman’s neck; and went with her into the wildwood, and taught her wisdom of the way and what she was to do. And again I say to thee that she was so sweet and yet with a kind of pity in her both of soul and body, and wise withal and quiet, that I feared her, though I loved her; yea and still do: for I deem her better than me, and meeter for thee and thy love than I be.﻿—Dost thou know her?”

“Yea,” said Ralph, “and fair and lovely she is in sooth. Yet hast thou naught to do to fear her. And true it is that I saw her and spake with her after thou hadst ridden away. For she came by the want-ways of the Wood Perilous in the dawn of the day after I had delivered thee; and in sooth she told me that she looked either for Death, or the Water of the Well to end her sorrow.”

Then he smiled and said; “As for that which thou sayest, that she had been meeter for me than thou, I know not this word. For look you, beloved, she came, and passed, and is gone, but thou art there and shalt endure.”

She stayed, and turned and faced him at that word; and love so consumed her, that all sportive words failed her; yea and it was as if mirth and lightheartedness were swallowed up in the fire of her love; and all thought of other folk departed from him as he felt her tears of love and joy upon his face, and she kissed and embraced him there in the wilderness.

‘你寻的是爱，’我说，‘他生得什么模样？’

“有什么法子呢，我的朋友：她开口就用言辞描起你的模样来；我于是知道她见过你——多半还是我离开你之后；我心里为此好一阵发酸。现在我对你说实话：她是个年轻女人，美极了，通身像珍珠，甜如野蔷薇；我怕她，怕她在这荒林里再撞见你。我问她要怎样；她说想寻世界尽头之井，灭一切忧伤的井。我听了欢喜，心想她要离你远远的了——没想到你我偏偏也要寻到那里去相会。我就把女妖主人从死女人颈上取下的那串珠子给了她；又送她进荒林，教她路上的智识、该做的事。我还要对你说：她那样甜，通身通心又带着一种哀怜，又聪明又安静，我怕她，虽然也爱她；如今还爱：因为我看她比我好，比我更配你、配你的爱。——你可认得她？”

“认得，”拉尔夫说，“她确实又美又可爱。可你不必怕她。真的，你骑马走后，我见过她，也同她说过话：我救你的次日破晓，她来到凶险之林的岔路口；她当真对我说过，她等的不是死，就是井水，来了结她的忧伤。”

随后他微笑道：“至于你说她比我配——我没听懂这话。你瞧，亲亲：她来了，过去了，走了；你却在这里，还要长存。”

听到这话她站住，回身对着他：爱把她烧得那样，一切玩笑话都没了；真的，好像轻快欢喜全叫她爱火吞了。他觉到她爱与喜的泪落在脸上，别的念头也都散了；就在这荒野里，她吻他，抱他。
