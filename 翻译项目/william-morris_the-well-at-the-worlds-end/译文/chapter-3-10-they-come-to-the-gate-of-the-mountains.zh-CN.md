## X / 十
===Original===

They Come to the Gate of the Mountains

That day they herded their horses thereabout, and from time to time the Sage tried those two if they were perfect in the lore of the road; and he found that they had missed nothing.

They lay down in the selfsame place again that night, and arose betimes on the morrow and went their ways over the plain as the Sage led, till it was as if the mountains and their terror hung over their very heads, and the hugeness and blackness of them were worse than a wall of fire had been. It was still a long way to them, so that it was not till noon of the third day from the rock-sea that they came to the very feet of that fire-scorched ness, and wonderful indeed it seemed to them that anything save the eagles could have aught to tell of what lay beyond it.

There were no foothills or downs betwixt the plain and the mountains, naught save a tumble of rocks that had fallen from the cliffs, piled up strangely, and making a maze through which the Sage led them surely; and at last they were clear even of this, and were underneath the flank of that ness, which was so huge that there seemed that there could scarce be any more mountain than that. Little of its huge height could they see, now they were close to it, for it went up sheer at first and then beetled over them till they could see no more of its side; as they wound about its flank, and they were long about it, the Sage cried out to those two and stretched out his hand, and behold! the side of the black cliff plain and smooth and shining as if it had been done by the hand of men or giants, and on this smooth space was carven in the living rock the image of a warrior in mail and helm of ancient fashion, and holding a sword in his right hand. From head to heel he seemed some sixty feet high, and the rock was so hard, that he was all clean and clear to see; and they deemed of him that his face was keen and stern of aspect.

===Chinese===

他们来到群山之门

那一天他们在附近放牧马匹，贤者不时考较二人，看他们是否把路上的学问记得纯熟；发觉他们一样不落，毫无遗漏。

当夜他们仍在原地歇下，次日一早起身，由贤者领着穿过平原赶路；走着走着，那群山和山中的恐怖仿佛就悬在他们头顶上，山的巍峨黢黑，比一道火墙还可怕。到山跟前还有好一段路，从离开岩石之海算起，直到第三天正午，他们才走到那道被地火烧灼过的山嘴脚下；在他们看来，除了鹰隼，居然还有生灵能知道山那边的事，这真不可思议。

平原与群山之间没有丘陵冈阜，只有从悬崖上崩落下来、堆积得奇形怪状的乱石，构成一座迷宫，贤者领着他们稳稳穿了过去；最后连乱石也走尽了，他们便到了那道山嘴的侧腹底下——山嘴如此巨大，仿佛天下再没有比它更多的山了。到了近处，它巍峨的高度反倒看不见多少，因为先是笔直上去，继而倾覆过来压在他们头顶，侧面再什么也瞧不见；他们沿着它的侧腹绕行，绕了很久，忽然贤者向二人喊了一声，伸手一指，看哪！黑色的崖壁上有一片平整光滑、莹然发亮的地方，象是出于人或巨人之手；就在这平滑的石面上，就着山岩本色刻着一尊武士像，披古式的锁子甲、戴古式的头盔，右手握着一柄剑。从头顶到脚跟，他约有六十尺高；岩石坚硬，所以像身干净清晰，历历可见。他们觉得他那副面容，精悍而威严。

So there they stood in an awful bight of the mountain, made by that ness, and the main wall from which it thrust out. But after they had gazed awhile and their hearts were in their mouths, the Sage turned on those twain and said: “Here then is the end of my journey with you; and ye wot all that I can tell you, and I can say no word more save to bid you cast all fear aside and thrive. Ye have yet for this day’s journey certain hours of such daylight as the mountain pass will give you, which at the best is little better than twilight; therefore redeem ye the time.”

But Ralph got off his horse, and Ursula did in likewise, and they both kissed and embraced the old man, for their hearts were full and fain. But he drew himself away from them, and turned about with no word more, and went his ways, and presently was hidden from their eyes by the rocky maze which lay about the mountain’s foot. Then the twain mounted their horses again and set forth silently on the road, as they had been bidden.

In a little while the rocks of the pass closed about them, leaving but a way so narrow that they could see a glimmer of the stars above them as they rode the twilight; no sight they had of the measureless stony desert, yet in their hearts they saw it. They seemed to be wending a straight-walled prison without an end, so that they were glad when the dark night came on them.

于是他们站在山的一处骇人的湾澳里——那是由山嘴和它所伸出的主山墙夹成的。他们凝望了一阵，心都提到了嗓子眼；随后贤者转过身来，对两人说：「我与你们同行，到这里就到头了；我能告诉你们的，你们都知道了，再没有别的话可说，只有一句：抛开一切恐惧，好生兴旺。今日这段路，山隘里还能给你们几个钟头的亮光，那亮光至多也不过同暮色差不多；所以要抓紧时辰。」

拉尔夫下了马，厄休拉也随着下来，两人都亲吻拥抱老人，因为心里满是眷恋。他却从他们怀抱中挣脱出来，一言不发地转过身，自去了；不多时，山脚周围的乱石迷宫便把他的身影遮没，再也看不见了。于是两人重新上马，依照嘱咐，默默地踏上路程。

不多时，隘口的岩石把他们夹在中间，只剩一条窄路，走在暮色里，抬头只见头顶上一线星光；那无边无际的石头荒漠他们望不见了，心里却看得见。他们象是在一座四壁陡立的、没有尽头的监狱里赶路，因此黑夜到来时，他们反倒高兴。

Ralph found some shelter in the cleft of a rock above a mound where was little grass for the horses. He drew Ursula into it, and they sat down there on the stones together. So long they sat silent that a great gloom settled upon Ralph, and he scarce knew whether he were asleep or waking, alive or dead. But amidst of it fell a sweet voice on his ears, and familiar words asking him of what like were the fields of Upmeads, and the flowers; and of the fish of its water, and of the fashion of the building of his father’s house; and of his brethren, and the mother that bore him. Then was it to him at first as if a sweet dream had come across the void of his gloom, and then at last the gloom and the dread and the deadness left him, and he knew that his friend and fellow was talking to him, and that he sat by her knee to knee, and the sweetness of her savoured in his nostrils as she leaned her face toward him, and he knew himself for what he was; and yet for memory of that past horror, and the sweetness of his friend and whatnot else, he fell a-weeping. But Ursula bestirred herself and brought out food from her wallet, and sat down beside him again, and he wiped the tears from his eyes and laughed, and chid himself for being as a child in the dark, and then they ate and drank together in that dusk nook of the wilderness. And now was he happy and his tongue was loosed, and he fell to telling her many things of Upmeads, and of the tale of his forefathers, and of his old loves and his friends, till life and death seemed to him as they had seemed of time past in the merry land of his birth. So there anon they fell asleep for weariness, and no dreams of terror beset their slumbers.

拉尔夫在一处土冈上找到一个岩缝可以栖身，冈上有一点草，好歹够马吃。他把厄休拉拉进岩缝，两人便一起坐在石头上。他们默默坐了许久，一片巨大的阴郁压上了拉尔夫的心头，他几乎分不清自己是睡着还是醒着，是活着还是死了。正在这时，一个甜美的声音落进他耳中，说的是些熟悉的话，问他阿普米兹的田野是什么模样、有什么花；问那水里的鱼，问他父亲房屋的建制格局；问他的兄弟，问生养他的母亲。起初他觉得，象是一场甜梦飘过他阴郁的虚空；到后来，阴郁、恐惧和麻木全都离他而去，他知道是他的朋友、他的旅伴在对他说话，知道他与她膝挨着膝坐在一处，她把脸凑近他时，发肤的芬芳沁入他的鼻息；他认清了自己究竟是谁。然而，想起方才那阵恐怖，又感受着朋友的温存和种种情味，他哭了起来。厄休拉赶忙起身，从行囊里取出食物，又坐到他身旁；他抹去眼泪，笑了一笑，怪自己象个怕黑的孩子；随后两人便在那荒野的幽暗角落里一同吃喝。这时他快活起来，话匣子也打开了，便对她说起阿普米兹的种种，说起祖辈的故事，说起旧日的情人和朋友，直到生与死在他心里又恢复了旧时光故乡乐土上的分量。不久，两人便因疲惫沉沉睡去，梦中再无恐怖相扰。
