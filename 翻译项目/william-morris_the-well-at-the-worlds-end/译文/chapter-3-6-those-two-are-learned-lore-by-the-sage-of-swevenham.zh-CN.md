## VI / 六
===Original===

Those Two Are Learned Lore by the Sage of Swevenham

Now the Sage led them through the wood till they came to a grassy lawn amidst of which was a table of stone, which it seemed to Ralph must be like to that whereon the witch-wife had offered up the goat to her devils as the Lady of Abundance had told him; and he changed countenance as the thought came into his mind. But the Sage looked on him and shook his head and spake softly: “In these wastes and wilds are many suchlike places, where of old time the ancient folks did worship to the Gods of the Earth as they imagined them: and whereas the lore in this book cometh of such folk, this is no ill place for the reading thereof. But if ye fear the book and its writers, who are dead long ago, there is yet time to go back and seek the Well without my helping; and I say not but that ye may find it even thus. But if ye fear not, then sit ye down on the grass, and I will lay the book on this most ancient table, and read in it, and do ye hearken heedfully.”

So they sat down side by side, and Ralph would have taken Ursula’s hand to caress it, but she drew it away from him; howbeit she found it hard to keep her eyes from off him. The Elder looked on them soberly, but nowise in anger, and presently began reading in the book. What he read shall be seen hereafter in the process of this tale; for the more part thereof had but to do with the way to the Well at the World’s End, all things concerning which were told out fully, both great and small. Long was this a-reading, and when the Sage had done, he bade now one, now the other answer him questions as to what he had read; and if they answered amiss he read that part again, and yet again, as children are taught in the school. Until at last when he asked any question Ralph or the maiden answered it rightly at once; and by this time the sun was about to set. So he bade them home to his house that they might eat and sleep there.

“But tomorrow,” said he, “I shall give you your last lesson from this book, and thereafter ye shall go your ways to the Rock of the Fighting Man, and I look not for it that ye shall come to any harm on the way; but whereas I seem today to have seen the foes of Utterbol seeking you, I will lead you forth a little.”

斯韦文汉贤者授二人古学

于是贤者领他们穿过树林，来到一片绿草如茵的草坪，草坪中央有一张石桌。拉尔夫一见，心想这必定和那巫婆宰羊祭她那些魔鬼的石台是一路货色——丰饶夫人当年原对他讲过；这念头一起，他不由得变了脸色。贤者瞧着他，摇了摇头，轻声说道：「这些荒野僻壤里，这样的地方多得很，古时候的人照着各自的心思向大地诸神献祭，用的便是这种石台。这书里的学问既然得自那样的古人，在这儿诵读倒也正合适。可你们若怕这书和写书的人——他们早死了不知多少年——如今回头、不靠我帮着去找那泉水，也还来得及；我不敢说你们这样便找不着。可你们若是不怕，那就请在草地上坐下，我把书搁在这张最古的石台上念，你们俩仔细听着。」

于是两人并肩坐下。拉尔夫想握住厄休拉的手抚弄一番，她却把手抽了回去；不过她仍难把目光从他身上挪开。老人神情端庄地望着他们，却毫无愠色，随即开始诵读那本书。他念了些什么，本书后文自有交代；因为书里大部分内容，讲的无非是通往世界尽头之泉的路途，大事小情，都交代得清清楚楚。这一读读了很久。贤者念完之后，便一会儿叫这个、一会儿叫那个，回答他关于所念内容的提问；若是答错了，他就把那一段重念一遍，再念一遍，象学堂里教孩子那样。直到后来，无论他问什么，拉尔夫或那姑娘都能立刻答对；这时太阳已快落山了。他便叫他们回他家里去，好在那里吃饭过夜。

「不过明天，」他说，「我要把这本书里最后的一课教给你们，此后你们就动身往斗士岩去。我想你们路上不至于遇到什么凶险；只是今天我仿佛看见乌特博尔的仇敌在搜寻你们，我会送你们一程。」

So they went home to the house, and he made them the most cheer that he might, and spake to them in friendly and pleasant mood, so that they were merry.

When it was morning they went again to the ancient altar, and again they learned lore from the Elder, till they were waxen wise in the matters of the Well at the World’s End, and long they sat and hearkened him till it was evening again, and once more they slept in the house of the Sage of Swevenham.

于是他们回到那座屋子，老人竭尽所能款待他们，言谈和蔼可亲，逗得二人快活起来。

到了早晨，他们又到那座古祭坛去，又从老人那里受学，直到把世界尽头之泉的种种事理学得精熟。他们久坐听讲，又到了晚间，便又在斯韦文汉贤者家中宿了一夜。
