## XXI / 兄弟俩的谈话
===Original===

Talk Between Those Two Brethren

Ralph asked Hugh first if he wotted aught of Gregory their brother. Hugh laughed and pointed to Higham, and said: “He is yonder.” “What,” said Ralph, “in the Abbot’s host?” “Yea,” said Hugh, laughing again, “but in his spiritual, not his worldly host: he is turned monk, brother; that is, he is already a novice, and will be a brother of the Abbey in six months’ space.” Said Ralph: “And Launcelot Longtongue, thy squire, how hath he sped?” Said Hugh: “He is yonder also, but in the worldly host, not the spiritual: he is a sergeant of theirs, and somewhat of a catch for them, for he is no ill man-at-arms, as thou wottest, and besides he adorneth everything with words, so that men hearken to him gladly.” “But tell me,” said Ralph, “how it befalleth that the Abbot’s men of war be so churlish, and chary of the inside of their town; what have they to fear? Is not the Lord Abbot still a mighty man?” Hugh shook his head: “There hath been a change of days at Higham; though I say not but that the knights are over careful, and much over fearful.” “What has the change been?” said Ralph. Hugh said: “In time past my Lord Abbot was indeed a mighty man, and both this town of Higham was well garnished of men-at-arms, and also many of his manors had castles and strong-houses on them, and the yeomen were ready to run to their weapons whenso the gathering was blown. In short, Higham was as mighty as it was wealthy; and the Abbot’s men had naught to do with any, save with thy friends here who bear the Tree Leafless; all else feared those holy walls and the well-blessed men who warded them. But the Dry Tree feared, as men said, neither man nor devil (and I hope it may be so still since they are become thy friends), and they would whiles lift in the Abbot’s lands when they had no merrier business on hand, and not seldom came to their above in their dealings with his men. But all things come to an end; for, as I am told, some year and a half ago, the Abbot had debate with the Westland Barons, who both were and are ill men to deal with, being both hungry and doughty.

拉尔夫先问休，可知道他们兄弟格雷戈里的消息。休笑着朝海厄姆一指，说：「他就在那边。」「什么，」拉尔夫说，「在院长的军队里？」「是啊，」休又笑道，「不过在他属灵的军队里，不是属世的：他当修士去了，兄弟；也就是说，已经做了见习修士，再过六个月就是修道院的正式修士了。」拉尔夫又问：「那你的侍从长舌兰斯洛特呢，他混得怎样？」休说：「他也在那边，不过在属世的军队里，不是属灵的：在他们手下当军士，还是他们捞到的一号人物——你知道的，他不是个差劲的武士，再说他什么话都能说得花团锦簇，人人爱听他讲话。」

「可你告诉我，」拉尔夫说，「院长的武人怎么这样粗鲁无礼，把城门守得这么紧；他们有什么可怕的？院长大人不还是位大有权势的人物么？」休摇摇头：「海厄姆换天地了；不过我倒不是说那些骑士不该小心，他们只是小心得过了头，胆子也小得过了头。」「怎么个换法？」拉尔夫问。

休说：「从前，我主院长确实是位大有权势的人：海厄姆这座城里武备充足，他的许多庄园上也有城堡和堡垒，号角一响，庄户人便都会奔向兵器。一句话，海厄姆的权势同它的财富一样大；院长的人同谁都不必打交道——除了你身边这些佩戴秃叶树的朋友；别的人全都敬畏那些圣洁的城墙和守墙的蒙福之人。可枯树呢，据说人也不怕、鬼也不怕（如今他们成了你的朋友，我希望还是这样）；他们手头没有更快活的营生时，时常到院长的地界上劫掠一番，同他的人交手，还往往占上风。可天下没有不散的筵席；我听说，约莫一年半以前，院长同西境的男爵们起了争端——这帮人一向难缠，如今也难缠：又贪又悍。

The quarrel grew till my Lord must needs defy them, and to make a long tale short, he himself in worldly armour led his host against them, and they met some twenty miles to the west in the field of the Wry Bridge, and there was Holy Church overthrown; and the Abbot, who is as valiant a man as ever sang mass, though not overwise in war, would not flee, and as none would slay him, might they help it, they had to lead him away, and he sits to this day in their strongest castle, the Red Mount west-away. Well, he being gone, and many of his wisest warriors slain, the rest ran into gates again; but when the Westlanders beset Higham and thought to have it good cheap, the monks and their men warded it not so ill but that the Westlanders broke their teeth over it. Forsooth, they turned away thence and took most of the castles and strong-houses of the Abbot’s lands; burned some and put garrisons into others, and drave away a mighty spoil of chattels and men and women, so that the lands of Higham are half ruined; and thereby the monks, though they be stout enough within their walls, will not suffer their men to ride abroad. Whereby, being cooped up in a narrow place, and with no deeds to hand to cheer their hearts withal, they are grown sour and churlish.”

“But, brother,” said Ralph, “howsoever churlish they may be, and howso timorous, I cannot see why they should shut their gates in our faces, a little band, when there is no foe anear them.”

“Ralph,” said Hugh, “thou must think of this once more, that the Dry Tree is no good let-pass to flourish in honest men’s faces; specialiter if they be monks. Amongst the brothers of Higham the tale goes that those Champions have made covenant with the devil to come to their above whensoever they be not more than one to five. Nay, moreover, it is said that there be very devils amongst them; some in the likeness of carles, and some (God help us) dressed up in women’s flesh; and fair flesh also, meseemeth. Also today they say in Higham that no otherwise might they ever have overcome the stark and cruel carles of the Burg of the Four Friths and chased them out of their town, as we know they have done. Hah! what sayest thou?”

「争端越闹越大，我主院长只好同他们兵戎相见。长话短说：他亲自披上尘世的甲胄，率军迎战；两军在西边约二十里外的歪桥原野相遇，圣教会一败涂地。院长这人，唱弥撒的教士里数得着的勇武，只是不大懂兵事，不肯逃；人家既然能不杀他就不杀，只好把他架走了：至今他还被押在他们最坚固的红山堡里，在西边。他一去，麾下最足智多谋的武士又阵亡了许多，余下的纷纷退回城里；后来西境人围攻海厄姆，本以为唾手可得，修士们和他们的部下守得却不坏，西境人在城下崩了牙。话虽如此，他们掉头而去，把院长地界上的城堡和堡垒占了大半：烧了一些，另一些派了驻军，还驱走了大批财物和男女俘虏，海厄姆的地界毁了一半；因此，修士们自己虽在墙内还算顽强，却不许部下出城骑行。部下们困守一隅，手头又没有提气的功业，就变得又酸又横。」

「可是，兄弟，」拉尔夫说，「不管他们多横、多胆小，我不明白：附近又没有敌人，凭什么当着咱们这支小小队伍的面关城门。」

「拉尔夫，」休说，「你得再想想这一层：枯树的旗号，可不是在正派人面前挥舞的好通行证；尤其是对修士。海厄姆的修士中间流传着一个说法：那帮勇士同魔鬼立了约，只要人数不处于一比五的劣势，就稳操胜券。还不止呢，据说他们队里就有货真价实的魔鬼：有的顶着男人的形貌，有的（上帝保佑我们）裹着女人的皮肉——依我看，还挺俊的皮肉。今天海厄姆城里还说，若非如此，他们绝无可能打败四弗里斯堡那帮狠毒的硬汉、把他们赶出城去——咱们知道，这事他们确实干成了。哈！你怎么说？」

“I say, Hugh,” quoth Ralph angrily, “that thou art a fool to go about with a budget of slanderous old wives’ tales.” Hugh laughed. “Be not so wroth, little lord, or I shall be asking thee tales of marvels also. But hearken. I shall smooth out thy frowns with a smile when thou hast heard this: this folk are not only afeard of their old enemies, the devil-led men, but also they fear those whom the devil-led men have driven out of house and home, to wit, the Burgers. Yet again they fear the Burgers yet more, because they have beaten some of the very foes of Higham, to wit, the Westland Barons; for they have taken from them some of their strongholds, and are deemed to be gathering force.”

Ralph pondered a while, and then he said: “Brother, hast thou any tidings of Upmeads, or that these Burgers have gone down thither?” “God forbid!” said Hugh. “Nay, I have had no tidings of Upmeads since I was fool enough to leave it.”

“What! brother,” said Ralph, “thou hast not thriven then?”

“I have had ups and downs,” said Hugh, “but the ups have been one rung of the ladder, and the downs three﻿—or more. Three months I sat in prison for getting me a broken head in a quarrel that concerned me not. Six months was I besieged in a town whither naught led me but ill-luck. Two days I wore in running thence, having scaled the wall and swam the ditch in the night. Three months I served squire to a knight who gave me the business of watching his wife of whom he was jealous; and to help me out of the weariness of his house I must needs make love myself to the said wife, who sooth to say was perchance worth it. Thence again I went by night and cloud. Ten months I wore away at the edge of the wildwood, and sometimes in it, with a sort of fellows who taught me many things, but not how to keep my hands from other men’s goods when I was hungry. There was I taken with some five others by certain sergeants of Higham, whom the warriors of the town had sent out cautiously to see if they might catch a few men for their ranks. Well, they gave me the choice of the gallows-tree or service for the Church, and so, my choice made, there have I been ever since, till I saw thy face this evening, fair sir.”

“Well, brother,” said Ralph, “all that shall be amended, and thou shalt back to Upmeads with me. Yet wert thou to amend thyself somewhat, it might not be ill.”

「我说，休，」拉尔夫恼道，「你真是个傻子，随身带着一袋子长舌妇的浑话到处走。」休大笑。「别这么大火气，小主公，不然我也要向你讨奇闻听了。且听着。等听完这个，我保管把你皱着的眉头熨平：这帮人不光怕他们的老对头——那些魔鬼引路的人——还怕被魔鬼引路的人赶出家门的那一路人，就是堡里人。而且他们怕堡里人怕得更厉害，因为堡里人打败的正是一些海厄姆的仇敌，也就是西境男爵；他们从男爵们手里夺了几处堡垒，据说还在聚兵。」

拉尔夫沉吟片刻，然后说：「兄弟，你有阿普米兹的消息吗？堡里人会不会南下打到那边去了？」「千万别！」休说，「没有，自从我蠢得离开那里，就再没有阿普米兹的消息。」

「怎么！兄弟，」拉尔夫说，「你混得不好？」

「起起落落倒是有，」休说，「只是上的是一级梯磴，下的是三级——还不止。为一桩与我毫不相干的争吵挨了个头破血流，我蹲了三个月监狱。被围在一座城里六个月——那城纯粹是厄运把我引去的。夜里翻墙游过壕沟，逃出来跑了两整天。给一位骑士当了三个月侍从——他派给我的差事是看住他那位让他犯醋的妻子；为了排解他家的烦闷，我只好自己去同那位夫人谈情说爱——说实话，她或许值这个价。后来又是趁夜趁黑走的。在莽林边上、有时在林子里，同一伙弟兄混了十个月：他们教会我许多本事，就是没教会我饿的时候如何不把手伸向别人的财物。就在那里，我同另外五个人被海厄姆的几个军士拿住了——那是城里的武人小心派出来想抓几个壮丁补充队伍的。他们给了我两条路：绞架，或者为教会效力；于是我选了，从此便一直待在那里——直到今天傍晚见到你的脸，好爵士。」

「好了，兄弟，」拉尔夫说，「这一切都会好起来的，你要跟我回阿普米兹去。不过你自己也该收敛着点，倒不是坏事。」

Quoth Hugh: “It shall be tried, brother. But may I ask thee somewhat?” Said Ralph: “Ask on.” “Fair Sir,” said Hugh, “thou seemedst grown into a pretty man when I saw thee e’en now before this twilight made us all alike; but the men at thy back are not wont to be led by men who have not earned a warrior’s name, yet they follow thee: how cometh that about? Again, before the twilight gathered I saw the woman that rideth anigh us (who is now but a shadow) how fair and gentle she is: indeed there is no marvel in her following thee (though if she be an earl’s daughter she is a fair getting for an imp of Upmeads), for thou art a well shapen lad, little lord, and carriest a sweet tongue in thy mouth. But tell me, what is she?”

“Brother,” said Ralph kindly, “she is my wife.”

“I kiss her hands,” said Hugh; “but of what lineage is she?”

“She is my wife,” said Ralph. Said Hugh: “That is, forsooth, a high dignity.” Said Ralph: “Thou sayest sooth, though in mockery thou speakest, which is scarce kind to thine own mother’s son: but learn, brother, that I am become a Friend of the Well, and were meet to wed with the daughters of the best of the Kings: yet is this one meeter to wed with me than the highest of the Queens; for she also is a Friend of the Well. Moreover, thou sayest it that the champions of the Dry Tree, who would think but little of an earl for a leader, are eager to follow me: and if thou still doubt what this may mean, abide, till in two days or three thou see me before the foeman. Then shalt thou tell me how much changed I am from the stripling whom thou knewest in Upmeads a little while ago.”

Then was Hugh somewhat abashed, and he said: “I crave thy pardon, brother, but never had I a well filed tongue, and belike it hath grown no smoother amid the hard haps which have befallen me of late. Besides it was dull in there, and I must needs try to win a little mirth out of kith and kin.”

“So be it, lad,” quoth Ralph kindly, “thou didst ask and I told, and all is said.”

“Yet forsooth,” said Hugh, “thou hast given me marvel for marvel, brother.” “Even so,” said Ralph, “and hereafter I will tell thee more when we sit safe by the wine at Upmeads.”

Now cometh back one of the fore-riders and draweth rein by Ralph and saith that they are hard on a little thorp under the hanging of the hill that was the beginning of the Down country on that road. So Ralph bade make stay there and rest the night over, and seek new tidings on the morrow; and the man told Ralph that the folk of the thorp were fleeing fast at the tidings of their company, and that it were best that he and some half score should ride sharply into the thorp, so that it might not be quite bare of victuals when they came to their night’s lodging. Ralph bids him so do, but to heed well that he hurt no man, or let fire get into any house or roof; so he takes his knot of men and rides off on the spur, and Ralph and the main of them come on quietly; and when they came into the street of the thorp, lo there by the cross a big fire lighted, and the elders standing thereby cap in hand, and a score of stout carles with weapons in their hands. Then the chief man came up to Ralph and greeted him and said: “Lord, when we heard that an armed company was at hand we deemed no less than that the riders of the Burg were upon us, and deemed that there was nought for it but to flee each as far and as fast as he might. But now we have heard that thou art a good lord seeking his own with the help of worthy champions, and a foeman to those devils of the Burg, we bid thee look upon us and all we have as thine, lord, and take kindly such guesting as we may give thee.”

The old man’s voice quavered a little as he looked on the stark shapes of the Dry Tree; but Ralph looked kindly on him, and said: “Yea, my master, we will but ask for a covering for our heads, and what victual thou mayst easily spare us in return for good silver, and thou shalt have our thanks withal. But who be these stout lads with staves and bucklers, or whither will they tonight?”

这时，一名前哨折返回来，在拉尔夫身旁勒住马，禀报说他们已快到山脚下的一个小村子——那道山是这条路进入丘原的起点。拉尔夫便吩咐在那里歇宿过夜，明天再探新消息；那人又禀告，村里人听到他们这支队伍的消息正纷纷出逃，最好由他带上十来个人先赶进村去，免得等大队到了宿处，吃的被搬得精光。拉尔夫准他照办，但再三嘱咐：不许伤人，不许让火进了任何屋子草顶。于是那人带上一小队人，策马疾驰而去；拉尔夫率大队缓缓跟进。等他们进了村中街巷，看哪，十字架旁已燃起一大堆火，村中长者手捧帽子侍立火边，二十来条壮汉手握兵器。为首的老人走上前来向拉尔夫行礼，说：「主公，先前听说有武装队伍逼近，我们只当是那座堡的骑手来了，寻思除了各自逃命、逃得越远越快越好，别无生路。如今听说您是位贤明的主公，在正义勇士的辅佐下讨还自家的东西，是那帮堡中魔鬼的仇敌，我们便请您把我们的性命和一切财产都看作您的，主公，并请笑纳我们力所能及的这一点招待。」

老人望着枯树武士们凛凛的身影，声音微微发颤；拉尔夫和颜悦色地看着他，说：「是了，老人家，我们只求一个遮头的屋顶，再求一些你们宽裕得出的吃食——照价付银子，还要送上我们的谢意。不过这几条拿着棍棒圆盾的壮实小伙子是谁？他们今夜要去哪里？」

Thereat a tall young man with a spear in his hand and girt with a short-sword came forth and said boldly: “Lord, we be a few who thought when we heard that the Burg-devils were at hand that we might as well die in the field giving stroke for stroke, as be hauled off and drop to pieces under the hands of their tormentors; and now thou hast come, we have little will to abide behind, but were fain to follow thee, and do thee what good we can: and after thou hast come to thine above, when we go back to our kin thou mayst give us a gift if it please thee: but we deem that no great matter if thou but give us leave to have the comfort of thee and thy Champions for a while in these hard days.”

When he had done speaking there rose up from the Champions a hum as of praise, and Ralph was well-pleased withal, deeming it a good omen; so he said: “Fear not, good fellows, that I shall forget you when we have overcome the foemen, and meanwhile we will live and die together. But thou, ancient man, show our sergeants where our riders shall lie tonight, and what they shall do with their horses.”

So the elders marshalled the little host to their abodes for that night, lodging the more part of them in a big barn on the western outskirt of the thorp. The elder who led them thither, brought them victual and good drink, and said to them: “Lords, ye were best to keep a good watch tonight because it is on this side that we may look for an onfall from the foemen if they be abroad tonight; and sooth to say that is one cause we have bestowed you here, deeming that ye would not grudge us the solace of knowing that your valiant bodies were betwixt us and them, for we be a poor unwalled people.”

Stephen to whom he spake laughed at his word, and said: “Heart-up, carle! within these few days we shall build up a better wall than ye may have of stone and lime; and that is the overthrow of our foemen in the open field.”

So there was kindness and good fellowship betwixt the thorp-dwellers and the riders, and the country folk told those others many tales of the evil deeds of the Burg-devils, as they called them; but they could not tell them for certain whether they had gone down into Upmeads.

话音未落，一个手持长矛、腰挎短剑的高个子青年站了出来，大胆地说：「主公，我们几个人原先听说堡里的魔鬼要来，心想与其被他们掳去、在他们酷刑吏手里零受碎剐，不如在野地里拼一个够本；如今您来了，我们实在不愿留在后头，情愿追随您，为您尽力：等您大功告成，我们回转亲族身边时，您高兴的话赏我们点什么；不过那都无关紧要——只求您恩准我们在这艰难时日里，得您和您麾下勇士们的庇护，就心满意足了。」

他说完，勇士们中间响起一片赞许的低语，拉尔夫也十分欢喜，认作吉兆；于是他说：「好汉们，别怕打完仗我会忘了你们；眼下嘛，咱们同生共死。老人家，请指引我们的军士：骑士们今夜宿在哪里，马匹如何安顿。」

于是长者们把这支小小的队伍分派到各自的宿处，大半安置在村子西缘一座大谷仓里。领路的那位长者给他们送来饭食和好酒，说：「列位大人，今夜最好严加守夜，因为敌人若是今夜出没，会从这一边来犯；说实话，这也是我们把诸位安置在这里的一个缘故——想到你们英勇的身躯挡在我们和他们之间，我们便得了安慰；我们是个没有城墙的可怜族群，想诸位不会见怪。」

对他说话的斯蒂芬听了大笑，说：「振作点，汉子！不出几天，我们就会给你们筑起一道比石头石灰更好的墙——那就是在旷野上把敌人打个落花流水。」

村民和骑手们之间融洽亲善；乡人们讲了许多他们所说的堡中魔鬼的恶行，只是说不准那些人是否南下去了阿普米兹。

===Original===

As to Ralph and Ursula they, with Richard and Roger, were lodged in the headman’s house, and had good feast there, and he also talked over the whereabouts of the Burgers with the thorp-dwellers, but might have no certain tidings. So he and Ursula and his fellows went to bed and slept peacefully for the first hours of the night.

===Chinese===

至于拉尔夫和厄休拉，他们同理查、罗杰一起宿在村长家里，受到盛宴款待；他又同村民商谈了堡里人的去向，依旧得不到确实音信。于是他同厄休拉和伙伴们上床安歇，上半夜睡得安稳。
