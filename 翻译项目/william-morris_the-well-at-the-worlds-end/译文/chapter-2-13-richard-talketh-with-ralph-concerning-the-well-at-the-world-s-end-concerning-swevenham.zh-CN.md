## XIII / 十三
===Original===

Richard Talketh with Ralph Concerning the Well at the World’s End. Concerning Swevenham

On the morrow Blaise went to his chaffer and to visit the men of the Port at the Guildhall: he bade Ralph come with him, but he would not, but abode in the hall of the hostel and sat pondering sadly while men came and went; but he heard no word spoken of the Well at the World’s End. In like wise passed the next day and the next, save that Richard was among those who came into the hall, and he talked long with Ralph at whiles; that is to say that he spake, and Ralph made semblance of listening.

Now as is aforesaid Richard was old and wise, and he loved Ralph much, more belike than Lord Blaise his proper master, whereas he had no mind for chaffer, or aught pertaining to it: so he took heed of Ralph and saw that he was sad and weary-hearted; so on the sixth day of their abiding at Whitwall, in the morning when all the chapmen were gone about their business, and he and Ralph were left alone in the Hall, he spake to Ralph and said: “This is no prison, lord.” “Even so,” quoth Ralph. “Nay, if thou doubtest it,” said Richard, “let us go to the door and try if they have turned the key and shot the bolt on us.” Ralph smiled faintly and stood up, and said: “I will go with thee if thou willest it, but sooth to say I shall be but a dull fellow of thine today.” Said Richard: “Wouldst thou have been better yesterday, lord, or the day before?” “Nay,” said Ralph. “Wilt thou be better tomorrow?” said Richard. Ralph shook his head. Said Richard: “Yea, but thou wilt be, or thou mayst call me a fool else.” “Thou art kind, Richard,” said Ralph; “and I will come with thee, and do what thou biddest me; but I must needs tell thee that my heart is sick.” “Yea,” quoth Richard, “and thou needest not tell me so much, dear youngling; he who runs might read that in thee. But come forth.”

理查同拉尔夫谈世界尽头的井；谈斯文汉

第二天，布莱兹去做他的买卖，到行会大厅拜会港里的头面人物；他叫拉尔夫同去，拉尔夫不肯，只坐在客栈厅堂里闷闷地出神，看人来人往，却没有听见谁提世界尽头的井。第二天、第三天也是这样，只是理查也到厅里来了，不时同拉尔夫长谈——所谓长谈，是他讲，拉尔夫装作听。

前文说过，理查又老又有见识，他很爱拉尔夫——多半胜过爱他自己真正的主公布莱兹爵爷，因为他全无心于买卖以及买卖的一切；所以他留意拉尔夫，见他郁郁、心灰。到他们在惠特沃尔住的第六天早晨，商人们都出门干事去了，厅里只剩他和拉尔夫，他便对拉尔夫说：“这里不是监狱，大人。”“可不是。”拉尔夫说。“你要不信，”理查说，“我们去门口看看，他们可曾把我们锁了、闩了。”拉尔夫淡淡一笑，站起来说：“你愿意，我就同你去；只是说实话，今天我是个没趣的同伴。”“昨天你就有趣么，大人，还是前天？”“不。”“明天就会有趣么？”理查说。拉尔夫摇头。理查说：“会的——不然你骂我傻子。”“你心好，理查，”拉尔夫说，“我同你去，你叫我做什么我做什么；只是我得告诉你：我的心病了。”“是啊，”理查说，“这话你不用告诉我，亲爱的小家伙；跑着路过的人都看得出来。来吧。”

So into the street they went, and Richard brought Ralph into the marketplace, and showed him where was Blaise’s booth (for he was thriving greatly) but Ralph would not go anigh it lest his brother should entangle him in talk; and they went into the Guildhall which was both great and fair, and the smell of the new-shaven oak (for the roof was not yet painted) brought back to Ralph’s mind the days of his childhood when he was hanging about the building of the water-reeve’s new house at Upmeads. Then they went into the Great Church and heard a Mass at the altar of St. Nicholas, Ralph’s very friend; and the said church was great to the letter, and very goodly, and somewhat new also, since the blossom-tide of Whitwall was not many years old: and the altars of its chapels were beyond anything for fairness that Ralph had seen save at Higham on the Way.

But when they came forth from the church, Ralph looked on Richard with a face that was both blank and weary, as who should say: “What is to do now?” And forsooth so woebegone he looked, that Richard, despite his sorrow and trouble for him, could scarce withhold his laughter. But he said: “Well, foster son (for thou art pretty much that to me), since the good town pleasureth thee little, go we further afield.”

So he led him out of the marketplace, and brought him to the east gate of the town which hight Petergate Bar, and forth they went and out into the meadows under the walls, and stayed him at a little bridge over one of the streams, for it was a land of many waters; there they sat down in a nook, and spake Richard to Ralph, saying:

“Lord Ralph, ill it were if the Upmeads kindred came to naught, or even to little. Now as for my own master Blaise, he hath, so please you, the makings of a noble chapman, but not of a noble knight; though he sayeth that when he is right rich he will cast aside all chaffer; naught of which he will do. As for the others, my lord Gregory is no better, or indeed worse, save that he shall not be rich ever, having no mastery over himself; while lord Hugh is like to be slain in some empty brawl, unless he come back speedily to Upmeads.”

于是两人上了街。理查领拉尔夫到集市广场，指给他看布莱兹的铺面（他买卖兴隆得很）；拉尔夫却不肯走近，怕哥哥缠住他说话。他们又进了行会大厅——又大又漂亮；新刨的橡木气味（屋顶还没上漆）勾起拉尔夫小时候的记忆：那时他整天在阿普米兹河泊官盖新房的工地上转。随后他们进大教堂，在圣尼古拉——拉尔夫的恩主——的祭坛前听了一台弥撒；那教堂真大，非常华丽，也还新，因为惠特沃尔的繁盛年月没有多少年；各小堂的祭坛之精美，拉尔夫只在大道旁的海厄姆见过能比的。

出了教堂，拉尔夫望着理查，一脸茫然和倦怠，像在问：“现在做什么？”他那副愁苦相，害得理查——尽管为他发愁——几乎忍不住笑。可他说：“这样吧，义子（你差不多就是我的义子），既然这良城不中你的意，我们走远些。”

他领他出了集市，带到城东的门——名叫彼得门闸，出了城，到城墙下的草场上，在一条小溪的小桥边停住——这是多水的乡邦；两人在一个角落里坐下，理查对拉尔夫说：

“拉尔夫大人，阿普米兹一族的血脉若断了，或者衰微了，那可不好。先说我自家的主公布莱兹：他有那份材料——你听着——做个了不起的买卖人，却没有做了不起的骑士的材料；虽说等他富透了就要抛开一切买卖——他才不会呢。再说那几位：格雷戈里大人不比他强，实在还更差，好在他永远不会富——他管不住自己；休大人呢，若不早早回阿普米兹，多半要死在哪一场无谓的斗殴里。”

“Yea, yea,” said Ralph, “what then? I came not hither to hear thee missay my mother’s sons.” But Richard went on: “As for thee, lord Ralph, of thee I looked for something; but now I cannot tell; for the heart in thee seemeth to be dead; and thou must look to it lest the body die also.” “So be it!” said Ralph.

Said Richard: “I am old now, but I have been young, and many things have I seen and suffered, ere I came to Upmeads. Old am I, and I cannot feel certain hopes and griefs as a young man can; yet have I bought the knowledge of them dear enough, and have not forgotten. Whereby I wot well that thy drearihead is concerning a woman. Is it not so?” “Yea,” quoth Ralph. Said Richard: “Now shalt thou tell me thereof, and so lighten thine heart a little.” “I will not tell thee,” said Ralph; “or, rather, to speak more truly, I cannot.” “Yea,” said Richard, “and though it were now an easier thing for me to tell thee of the griefs of my life than for thee to hearken to the tale, yet I believe thee. But mayhappen thou mayst tell me of one thing that thou desirest more than another.” Said Ralph: “I desire to die.” And the tears started in his eyes therewith. But Richard spake, smiling on him kindly: “That way is open for thee on any day of the week. Why hast thou not taken it already?” But Ralph answered naught. Richard said: “Is it not because thou hopest to desire something; if not today, then tomorrow, or the next day or the next?” Still Ralph spake no word; but he wept. Quoth Richard: “Maybe I may help thee to a hope, though thou mayest think my words wild. In the land and the thorp where I was born and bred there was talk now and again of a thing to be sought, which should cure sorrow, and make life blossom in the old, and uphold life in the young.” “Yea,” said Ralph, looking up from his tears, “and what was that? and why hast thou never told me thereof before?” “Nay,” said Richard, “and why should I tell it to the merry lad I knew in Upmeads? but now thou art a man, and hast seen the face of sorrow, it is meet that thou shouldest hear of **The Well at the World’s End**.”

===Chinese===

“是，是，”拉尔夫说，“那又怎样？我不是来听你数落我母亲生的儿子们的。”理查却接着说：“至于你，拉尔夫大人，我本对你指望些什么；可如今我说不上了：你心里的那颗心像是死了；你得当心，别连身子也一起死了。”“那就随它去！”拉尔夫说。

理查说：“我如今老了，可我也年轻过；来阿普米兹之前，我见过、也吃过许多事。我老了，年轻人那种确确实实的指望和悲苦，我已经感不着了；可我当年是花了大价钱才识得它们的，没有忘。所以我一望便知：你这块阴郁，是为着一个女人。是不是？”“是。”拉尔夫说。理查说：“如今你把它讲给我听，心里也好轻一轻。”“我不讲，”拉尔夫说，“或者说得真些：我讲不出。”“是了，”理查说，“虽说如今要我对你说说我一生的苦楚，比你要听那故事还容易，我还是信你的话。可有一件你兴许肯告诉我：你有没有比什么都想要的东西？”拉尔夫说：“我想死。”眼泪随之涌上眼眶。理查和气地笑着对他说：“那条路，一星期里哪一天都开着。你怎么至今不走？”拉尔夫不答。理查说：“是不是因为你指望自己还会想要点什么——今天不，明天，或者后天、大后天？”拉尔夫仍不作声，只是哭。理查说：“兴许我能帮你找一个指望出来，虽说你多半当我的话是疯话。在我生身长身的那个地方、那个村子，人们时不时谈起一件该去寻的东西，说它能治好悲伤，叫老人的生命重新开花，叫年轻人的生命撑得住。”“是么，”拉尔夫从泪眼里抬起头，“那是什么？你从前怎么从来不告诉我？”“咳，”理查说，“从前何必告诉阿普米兹那个快快活活的小子？如今你是男人了，见过悲伤的脸了，才配听人讲**世界尽头的井**。”

Ralph sprang to his feet as he said the word, and cried out eagerly: “Old friend, and where then wert thou bred and born?” Richard laughed and said: “See, then, there is yet a deed and a day betwixt thee and death! But turn about and look straight over the meadows in a line with yonder willow-tree, and tell me what thou seest.” Said Ralph: “The fair plain spreading wide, and a river running through it, and little hills beyond the water, and blue mountains beyond them, and snow yet lying on the tops of them, though the year is in young July.” “Yea,” quoth Richard; “and seest thou on the first of the little hills beyond the river, a great grey tower rising up and houses anigh it?” “Yea,” said Ralph, “the tower I see, and the houses, for I am farsighted; but the houses are small.” “So it is,” said Richard; “now yonder tower is of the Church of Swevenham, which is under the invocation of the Seven Sleepers of Ephesus; and the houses are the houses of the little town. And what has that to do with me? sayest thou: why this, that I was born and bred at Swevenham. And indeed I it was who brought my lord Blaise here to Whitwall, with tales of how good a place it was for chaffer, that I might see the little town and the great grey tower once more. Forsooth I lied not, for thy brother is happy here, whereas he is piling up the coins one upon the other. Forsooth thou shouldest go into his booth, fair lord; it is a goodly sight.”

But Ralph was walking to and fro hastily, and he turned to Richard and said: “Well, well! but why dost thou not tell me more of the Well at the World’s End?”

这词一出口，拉尔夫跳起来，急切地喊：“老朋友，那你在哪里生、哪里长？”理查笑道：“瞧，你同死亡之间，到底还有一件事、一天呢！转过身，顺着那棵柳树的方向，笔直望过草场，告诉我你看见了什么。”拉尔夫说：“美丽的大平原铺展开去，一条河穿流其间；河那边是小山，山后是青山，山顶上还压着雪——虽然已经是七月初了。”“是了，”理查说，“河那边头一重小山上，你看见一座灰色大塔立起来、旁边一片房子么？”“看见了，”拉尔夫说，“塔看见，房子也看见——我眼尖；只是房子小。”“正是，”理查说，“那座塔是斯文汉的教堂，奉的是以弗所七眠者；房子便是那小城的房子。你问这同我什么相干？相干在这儿：我就是斯文汉生、斯文汉长的。老实说，当年就是我领着布莱兹主公来惠特沃尔的，把这里买卖多么好吹了一通——为的是再看看那小城、那灰塔。说实话我没撒谎：你兄弟在这里快活得很，钱正一枚一枚往上摆呢。说真的你该进他铺子里看看，好大人；很值得一看。”

可拉尔夫已在急急踱步，他转向理查说：“好了好了！你为什么不快些给我讲世界尽头的井？”

Said Richard: “I was going to tell thee somewhat which might be worth thy noting; or might not be worth it: hearken! When I dwelt at Swevenham over yonder, and was but of eighteen winters, who am now of threescore and eight, three folk of our township, two young men and one young woman, set out thence to seek the said Well: and much lore they had concerning it, which they had learned of an old man, a nigh kinsman of one of them. This ancient carle I had never seen, for he dwelt in the mountains a way off, and these men were some five years older than I, so that I was a boy when they were men grown; and such things I heeded not, but rather sport and play; and above all, I longed for the play of war and battle. God wot I have had my bellyful of it since those days! Howbeit I mind me the setting forth of these three. They had a sumpter-ass with them for their livelihood on the waste; but they went afoot crowned with flowers, and the pipe and tabour playing before them, and much people brought them on the way. By St. Christopher! I can see it all as if it were yesterday. I was sorry of the departure of the damsel; for though I was a boy I had loved her, and she had suffered me to kiss her and toy with her; but it was soon over. Now I call to mind that they had prayed our priest, Sir Cyprian, to bless them on their departure, but he naysaid them; for he held that such a quest came of the inspiration of the devils, and was but a memory of the customs of the ancient gentiles and heathen. But as to me, I deemed it naught, and was sorry that my white-bosomed, sweet-breathed friend should walk away from me thus into the clouds.”

“What came of it?” said Ralph, “did they come back, or any of them?” “I wot not,” said Richard, “for I was weary of Swevenham after that, so I girt myself to a sword and laid a spear upon my shoulder and went my ways to the Castle of the Waste March, sixty miles from Swevenham town, and the Baron took me in and made me his man: and almost as little profit were in my telling thee again of my deeds there, as there was in my doing them: but the grey tower of Swevenham I have never seen again till this hour.”

Said Ralph: “Now then it behoveth me to go to Swevenham straightway: wilt thou come with me? it seemeth to be but some four miles hence.”

理查说：“我正要讲件也许值得你留意的事——也许不值得：听着！我住在那边斯文汉的时候，才十八岁——如今我六十八了——我们镇上三个人，两个年轻男人、一个年轻女人，动身去寻那口井；关于它，他们肚里有许多学问，是从一个老人那里学来的——那老人是他们一个的近亲。这老汉我没有见过：他住在老远的山里；那几个人又比我大五岁上下，他们成年时我还是孩子，这些事我不上心，只顾玩耍；顶要紧的，我一心想玩打仗。上帝知道，那些年月之后我可玩了个够！可我还记得他们三个出发的光景：带一头驮驴，驮着荒野里的口粮；人却是步行，头上戴着花冠，笛鼓在前头吹打着，许多人送他们上路。圣克里斯多福在上！我如今还看得清清楚楚，像昨天一样。那姑娘一走我心里难过：我虽是孩子，已经爱她了，她容我亲她、同她玩耍；可这事很快便过去了。如今我记得，他们动身前求过我们的神父西普里安，请他祝福；神父回绝了：他认为这样的寻求出自魔鬼的默感，不过是古时异教徒旧俗的一点残影。至于我，全当它没事，只难过我那白胸脯、气息甜甜的伙伴就这样走进云彩里去了。”

“后来呢？”拉尔夫说，“他们回来了么？有一个回来的么？”“不知道，”理查说，“打那以后我就腻了斯文汉：我束上一柄剑，扛上一杆矛，径直去了荒边堡——离斯文汉城六十里；男爵收了我，叫我做了他的人。我在那里的作为，再讲给你听，同我当年干的差不多一样没益处；只是斯文汉的灰塔，直到此刻，我再没见过。”

拉尔夫说：“那么如今我该立刻去斯文汉；你同我一起去么？看样子不过四里路。”

Richard held his peace and knit his brows as if pondering the matter, and Ralph abided till he spake: so he said: “Foster-son, so to call thee, thou knowest the manner of upcountry carles, that tales flow forth from them the better if they come without over much digging and hoeing of the ground; that is, without questioning; so meseems better it will be if I go to Swevenham alone, and better if I be asked to go, than if I go of myself. Now tomorrow is Saturday, and high market in Whitwall; and I am not so old but that it is likeliest that there will be some of my fellows alive and on their legs in Swevenham: and if such there be, there will be one at the least in the market tomorrow, and I will be there to find him out: and then it will go hard if he bring me not to Swevenham as a well-beloved guest; and when I am there, and telling my tidings, and asking them of theirs, if there be any tales concerning the Well at the World’s End working in their bellies, then shall I be the midwife to bring them to birth. Ha? Will it do?”

“Yea,” said Ralph, “but how long wilt thou be?” Said Richard: “I shall come back speedily if I find the land barren; but if the field be in ear I shall tarry to harvest it. So keep thou thy soul in patience.” “And what shall I do now?” said Ralph. “Wear away the hours,” said Richard. “And to begin with, come back within the gates with me and let us go look at thy brother’s booth in the marketplace: it is the nethermost of a goodly house which he is minded to dwell in; and he will marry a wife and sit down in Whitwall, so well he seemeth like to thrive; for they have already bidden him to the freedom of the city, and to a brother of the Faring-Knights, whereas he is not only a stirring man, but of good lineage also: for now he hideth not that he is of the Upmeads kindred.”

理查不响，皱起眉，像在掂量；拉尔夫等他开口。末了他说：“义子——恕我这样叫你——你晓得乡下汉子的脾气：他们肚里的故事，不必怎么刨地锄草，自己就淌出来；就是说，不必人发问。所以我看，顶好是我一个人去斯文汉，而且是被请了去，不是我自家找上门去。明天是星期六，惠特沃尔的大集；我不算太老，斯文汉多半还有我当年的伙伴活着、腿脚还利索；有的话，明天集市上至少会有一个，我去把他认出来；那时他不把我当贵客领回斯文汉才怪。等我在那里讲了我的消息、问了他们的消息，他们肚里若正攒着世界尽头的井的什么传说，我就给他们接生。哈？行不行？”

“行，”拉尔夫说，“可你要去多久？”理查说：“地里若什么都没有，我马上回来；田里若已抽穗，我就候着收完。你且把性子按住。”“那我现在做什么？”拉尔夫说。“把钟头磨过去，”理查说，“先跟我回城里，去看看你兄弟在集市的铺子：那是一座好宅子的最下一进，他有意住进去；他还要娶亲，在惠特沃尔安家——他看样子要发透了：人家已经请他入城籍、加入行商骑士会了；他不但是个勤快人，门第也好——如今他不瞒着自己是阿普米兹一族的人了。”
