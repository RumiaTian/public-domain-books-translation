## VII / 途中奇遇
===Original===

An Adventure by the Way

When morrow dawned they arose betimes and did on their worldly raiment; and when they had eaten a morsel they made them ready for the road, and the elder gave them victual for the way in their saddlebags, saying: “This shall suffice for the passing days, and when it is gone ye have learned what to do.”

Therewithall they gat to horse; but Ralph would have the Elder ride his nag, while he went afoot by the side of Ursula. So the Sage took his bidding, but smiled therewith, and said: “Thou art a King’s son and a friendly young man, else had I said nay to this; for it needeth not, whereas I am stronger than thou, so hath my draught of the Well dealt with me.”

Thus then they went their ways; but Ralph noted of Ursula that she was silent and shy with him, and it irked him so much, that at last he said to her: “My friend, doth aught ail me with thee? Wilt thou not tell me, so that I may amend it? For thou art grown of few words with me and turnest thee from me, and seemest as if thou heedest me little. Thou art as a fair spring morning gone cold and overcast in the afternoon. What is it then? we are going a long journey together, and belike shall find little help or comfort save in each other; and ill will it be if we fall asunder in heart, though we be nigh in body.”

She laughed and reddened therewithal; and then her countenance fell and she looked piteously on him and said: “If I seemed to thee as thou sayest, I am sorry; for I meant not to be thus with thee as thou deemest. But so it is that I was thinking of this long journey, and of thee and me together in it, and how we shall be with each other if we come back again alive, with all things done that we had to do.”

She stayed her speech awhile, and seemed to find it hard to give forth the word that was in her; but at last she said: “Friend, thou must pardon me; but that which thou sawest in me, I also seemed to see in thee, that thou wert grown shy and cold with me; but now I know it is not so, since thou hast seen me wrongly; but that I have seen thee wrongly, as thou hast me.”

翌日清晨天刚亮，他们便早早起身，穿上尘世的衣装；吃了几口东西，就整装待发。贤者往他们的鞍囊里装了路粮，说道："这些够你们应付眼下这几天；吃完了，你们也已经知道该怎么办。"

于是他们上马出发；拉尔夫却要让贤者骑他那匹小马，自己步行走在厄休拉身旁。贤者依了他，却微笑着说："你是王子，又是个和善的年轻人，不然我可要回绝了；其实不必如此——我比你强壮，因为我喝过世界尽头之泉，泉水已在我身上起了作用。"

一行人就这样上了路；可拉尔夫发觉厄休拉待他沉默而羞怯，心里颇为烦恼，终于忍不住对她说："我的朋友，是我有什么地方叫你不痛快吗？何不告诉我，好让我改正？你对我话越来越少，扭过脸去避着我，好像不把我放在心上。你就像一个晴美的春晨，到了午后却变冷阴沉下来。到底怎么回事？我们要一同走漫长的旅途，除了彼此，多半找不到别的帮手和安慰；若是身子相近，心却散了，那可就糟了。"

她闻言笑了，脸也红了；随即神色一黯，可怜巴巴地望着他说："若是在你眼里我真是那样，我很抱歉；我本不是有意像你想的那样待你。只是我一直在想这漫长的旅途，想你我二人同行一路，想着等我们做完了该做的事、活着回来之后，彼此之间会是如何。"

她顿住话头，似有难言之隐；终于还是说道："朋友，你一定得原谅我；你在我身上看到的，我也仿佛在你身上看到了——只当你对我变得羞怯冷淡；如今我知道并非如此：是你错看了我，正如我也错看了你。"

===Original===

Therewith she reached her hand to him, and he took it and kissed it and caressed it while she looked fondly at him, and they fared on sweetly and happily together. But as this was a-saying and a-doing betwixt them, and a while after, they had heeded the Elder little or not at all, though he rode on the right hand of Ralph. And for his part the old man said naught to them and made as if he heard them not, when they spake thuswise together.

Now they rode the wood on somewhat level ground for a while; then the trees began to thin, and the ground grew broken; and at last it was very rugged, with high hills and deep valleys, and all the land populous of wild beasts, so that about sunset they heard thrice the roar of a lion. But ever the Sage led them by winding ways that he knew, round the feet of the hills, along stream-sides for the most part, and by passes over the mountain-necks when they needs must, which was twice in the day.

Dusk fell on them in a little valley, through which ran a stream bushed about its edges, and which for the rest was grassy and pleasant, with big sweet-chestnut trees scattered about it.

“Now,” quoth the Elder; “two things we have to beware of in this valley, the lions first; which, though belike they will not fall upon weaponed men, may well make an onslaught on your horses, if they wind them; and the loss of the beasts were sore to you as now. But the second thing is the chase from Utterbol. As to the lions, if ye build up a big fire, and keep somewhat aloof from the stream and its bushes, and tether you horses anigh the fire, ye will have no harm of them.”

“Yea,” said Ralph, “but if the riders of Utterbol are anigh us, shall we light a candle for them to show them the way?” Said the Sage: “Were ye by yourselves, I would bid you journey nightlong, and run all risk rather than the risk of falling into their hands. But whereas I am your guide, I bid you kindle your fire under yonder big tree, and leave me to deal with the men of Utterbol; only whatso I bid you, that do ye straightway.”

===Chinese===

说着她向他伸出手来，他握住那只手，吻了吻，轻轻爱抚着，她则深情地望着他；两人甜甜蜜蜜、欢欢喜喜地继续赶路。只是这一番倾诉温存之间，以及其后好一阵子，他们几乎没把贤者放在心上——虽然老人就骑在拉尔夫右手边。而贤者也只作不知，他们这样低声交谈时，他便一言不发，只当没听见。

起初他们在林中较为平坦的地面上骑行了一阵；随后树木渐疏，地势起伏破碎；最后变得极为崎岖，高山深谷相间，遍地皆是野兽，日落时分他们三度听见狮吼。好在贤者总引着他们走他熟识的曲折小径——绕着山脚，多半沿着溪边，万不得已时才翻越山梁隘口，一天之中也不过两回。

暮色降临时，他们来到一条小山谷，谷中有一道溪流，两岸灌木丛生，余处绿草如茵，景致宜人，散落着高大的甜栗树。

"这样吧，"贤者说道，"在这山谷里有两桩事要提防：头一桩是狮子——它们虽多半不会袭击带武器的人，可若嗅到马匹的气味，很可能会扑上来；眼下丢了马匹，对你们可是沉重的损失。第二桩是乌特博尔的追兵。至于狮子，只要你们生起一大堆火，离溪水和灌木丛远一些，再把马拴在火旁，就不会受到伤害。"

"话是这么说，"拉尔夫道，"可要是乌特博尔的骑手就在左近，我们岂不是点起蜡烛给他们引路？"贤者说："若是你们自己赶路，我会叫你们连夜兼程，宁可冒一切风险，也别落在他们手里。但既然有我为你们引路，我要你们就在那棵大树底下生火，乌特博尔的人交给我来对付；只一样：我叫你们做什么，你们就立刻照办。"

===Original===

“So be it,” said Ralph, “I have been bewrayed so oft of late, that I must needs trust thee, or all help shall fail me. Let us to work.” So they fell to and built up a big bale and kindled it, and their horses they tethered to the tree; and by then they had done this, dark night had fallen upon them. So they cooked their victual at the fire (for Ralph had shot a hare by the way) and the Sage went down to the stream and fetched them water in a lethern budget: “For,” said he, “I know the beasts of the wood and they me, and there is peace betwixt us.” There then they sat to meat unarmed, for the Sage had said to them: “Doff your armour; ye shall not come to handystrokes with the Utterbol Riders.”

So they ate their meat in the wilderness, and were nowise ungleeful, for to those twain the world seemed fair, and they hoped for great things. But though they were glad, they were weary enough, for the way had been both rugged and long; so they lay them down to sleep while the night was yet young. But or ever Ralph closed his eyes he saw the Sage standing up with his cloak wrapped about his head, and making strange signs with his right hand; so that he deemed that he would ward them by wizardry. So therewith he turned about on the grass and was asleep at once.

After a while he started and sat up, half awake at first; for he felt someone touch him; and his half-dreams went back to past days, and he cried out: “Hah Roger! is it thou? What is toward?” But therewith he woke up fully, and knew that it was the Sage that had touched him, and withal he saw hard by Ursula, sitting up also.

There was still a flickering flame playing about the red embers of their fire, for they had made it very big; and the moon had arisen and was shining bright in a cloudless sky.

The Sage spake softly but quickly: “Lie down together, ye two, and I shall cast my cloak over you, and look to it that ye stir not from out of it, nor speak one word till I bid you, whate’er may befall: for the riders of Utterbol are upon us.”

===Chinese===

"就这样吧，"拉尔夫说，"近来我屡屡遭人出卖，如今除了信你，也别无出路了。动手吧。"于是他们动手堆起一大堆柴，点着了火，把马拴在树上；待一切停当，夜幕已经降临。他们在火边做饭（拉尔夫路上打到一只野兔），贤者下到溪边，用一只皮囊取了水来。"因为，"他说，"我认得林中的野兽，野兽也认得我，我们之间相安无事。"于是他们卸下兵器坐下用饭，因为贤者吩咐过："解下甲胄吧；你们不会跟乌特博尔的骑手动起手来。"

就这样，他们在荒野中吃着饭食，心里并非不快活——在这两人眼里，世界显得那样美好，他们盼望着伟大的前程。他们虽然欢喜，却也着实疲惫，因为这一路又崎岖又漫长；夜还早，他们便躺下睡了。拉尔夫合眼之前，看见贤者站在那里，斗篷裹住头，右手比划着奇怪的手势；他心想，这是要用法术护佑他们。于是他在草地上翻了个身，立刻睡着了。

过了一会儿，他猛地一惊坐起，起初还半睡半醒；只觉得有人碰他，蒙眬的睡梦退回到往昔岁月，他喊道："啊，罗杰！是你吗？出什么事了？"随即他完全醒转，认出碰他的是贤者，又看见厄休拉就在近旁，也坐了起来。

他们生的火很大，红色的余烬上还跳动着摇曳的火苗；月亮已经升起，在无云的夜空里皎皎生辉。

贤者轻声而急促地说："你们两个躺到一处去，我把斗篷盖在你们身上；千万记住，不管出什么事，没我叫你们，就不许从斗篷底下动一动，也不许说一个字：乌特博尔的骑手来了。"

===Original===

They did as he bade them, but Ralph got somewhat of an eyeshot out of a corner of the cloak, and he could see that the Sage went and stood up against the tree-trunk holding a horse by the bridle, one on each side of him. Even therewith Ralph heard the clatter of horse-hoofs over the stones about the stream, and a man’s voice cried out: “They will have heard us; so spur over the grass to the fire and the big tree: for then they cannot escape us.” Then came the thump of horse-hoofs on the turf, and in half a minute they were amidst of a rout of men a-horseback, more than a score, whose armour and weapons gleamed in the moonlight: yet when these riders were gotten there, they were silent, till one said in a quavering voice as if afeard: “Otter, Otter! what is this? A minute ago and we could see the fire, and the tree, and men and horses about them: and now, lo you! there is naught save two great grey stones lying on the grass, and a man’s bare bones leaning up against the tree, and a ruckle of old horse-bones on either side of him. Where are we then?”

Then spake another; and Ralph knew the voice for Otter’s: “I wot not, lord; naught else is changed save the fire and the horses and the men: yonder are the hills, yonder overhead is the moon, with the little light cloud dogging her; even that is scarce changed. Belike the fire was an earth-fire, and for the rest we saw wrong in the moonlight.”

二人依言而行，拉尔夫却从斗篷一角留出一线视野，看见贤者走过去背靠树干站定，两手各牵着一匹马的缰绳。恰在此时，拉尔夫听见溪边乱石间响起嘚嘚的马蹄声，一个男人喊道："他们肯定听见咱们了；快策马从草地上直扑火堆和大树：那样他们就跑不掉了。"接着便是马蹄踏在草皮上的闷响，不到半分钟，他们已陷在一大群骑手之中，足有二十多骑，甲胄兵刃在月光下泛着寒光：然而这队骑手赶到之后却都哑然无声，好一会儿才有一人颤声说道，像是吓着了："奥特，奥特！这是怎么回事？刚才一分钟咱们还看得见火堆、大树，还有旁边的人和马：可现在，你瞧！草地上只剩下两块灰色的大石头，一具人的骸骨靠在树上，两边各是一堆散乱的陈年马骨。咱们这是到了什么地方？"

另一个声音响起，拉尔夫听出那是奥特："我不知道，大人；除了火堆、马匹和人，别的都没变：那边是群山，头顶上是月亮，还有那朵追着它跑的小白云；连它都几乎没变。多半那火是地火，其余的嘛，是咱们在月光下看岔了。"

===Original===

Spake the first man again, and his voice quavered yet more: “Nay nay, Otter, it is not so. Lo you the skeleton and the bones and the grey stones! And the fire, here this minute, there the next. O Otter, this is an evil place of an evil deed! Let us go seek elsewhere; let us depart, lest a worse thing befall us.” And so with no more ado he turned his horse and smote his spurs into him and galloped off by the way he had come, and the others followed, nothing loth; only Otter tarried a little, and looked around him and laughed and said: “There goes my Lord’s nephew; like my Lord he is not over bold, save in dealing with a shackled man. Well, for my part if those others have sunk into the earth, or gone up into the air, they are welcome to their wizardry, and I am glad of it. For I know not how I should have done to have seen my mate that out-tilted me made a gelded wretch of; and it would have irked me to see that fair woman in the hands of the tormentors, though forsooth I have oft seen such sights. Well, it is good; but better were it to ride with my mate than serve the Devil and his Nephew.”

Therewith he turned rein and galloped off after the others, and in a little while the sound of them had died off utterly into the night, and they heard but the voices of the wild things, and the wimbrel laughing from the hillsides. Then came the Sage and drew the cloak from those two, and laughed on them and said: “Now may ye sleep soundly, when I have mended our fire; for ye will see no more of Utterbol for this time, and it yet lacks three hours of dawn: sleep ye then and dream of each other.” Then they arose and thanked the Sage with whole hearts and praised his wisdom. But while the old man mended the fire Ralph went up to Ursula and took her hand, and said: “Welcome to life, fellow-farer!” and he gazed earnestly into her eyes, as though he would have her fall into his arms: but whereas she rather shrank from him, though she looked on him lovingly, if somewhat shyly, he but kissed her hand, and laid him down again, when he had seen her lying in her place. And therewith they fell asleep and slept sweetly.

先前那人又开口了，声音抖得更厉害了："不，不，奥特，不是这么回事。你瞧那骸骨、那堆骨头、那灰石头！还有那火，方才还在这儿，一转眼就没了。哦奥特，这是块凶地，出过凶事！咱们到别处找去吧；快走，免得遭上更凶的祸。"说罢不再多话，拨转马头，双刺一夹，顺着来路飞驰而去；其余的人巴不得如此，都跟了上去。只有奥特略停了停，环顾四周，笑道："那边跑掉的是我爵爷的侄儿；跟他叔叔一个样，除了对付捆住的囚徒，谈不上有什么胆量。哼，要我说，那几个人不管是钻进地里去了，还是飞上天去了，他们的法术爱怎么使就怎么使，我倒高兴。因为真要让我眼看着我那位把我挑落马下的伙伴被阉成废人，我可不知道自己会干出什么来；眼看那美貌女子落到酷刑吏手里，我心里也不好受——虽说这种场面我见得多了。唉，这样很好；不过，能同我伙伴一起骑马，总比伺候魔鬼和他的侄儿强。"

说罢他勒转缰绳，追着众人疾驰而去；不多时，蹄声便完全消逝在黑夜里，只余下野兽的鸣声，还有山腰上麻鹬的啼笑。这时贤者走过来，掀开两人身上的斗篷，笑着对他们说："等我再把火弄旺，你们就可以安睡无虞了；这一回再不会见到乌特博尔的人，离天亮还有三个钟头呢；睡吧，梦里见彼此去吧。"两人起身，由衷感谢贤者，赞叹他的智慧。趁老人添火的当口，拉尔夫走到厄休拉跟前，握住她的手，说："欢迎回到人间，同路的伙伴！"他恳切地凝视她的眼睛，仿佛盼着她倒入自己怀中；可她虽说含情脉脉、略带羞涩地望着他，却向后缩了缩，他只得吻了吻她的手，看着她躺回原处，自己也重新躺下。于是他们沉沉睡去，睡得香甜。
