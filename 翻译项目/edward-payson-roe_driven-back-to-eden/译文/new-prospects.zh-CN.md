## III New Prospects / III 新的前景

===Original===
There is no need of dwelling further on the reasons for or against the step we proposed. We thought a great deal and talked it over several times. Finally my wife agreed that the change would be wise and best for all. Then the children were taken into our confidence, and they became more delighted every day as the prospect grew clearer to them.

“We’ll all be good soon, won’t we?” said my youngest, who had a rather vivid sense of his own shortcomings, and kept them in the minds of others as well.

“Why so, Bobsey?”

“ ’Cause mamma says that God put the first people in a garden and they was very good, better’n any folks afterwards. God oughter know the best place for people.”

===Chinese===
我们打算迈出的这一步，赞成也好、反对也罢，个中缘由不必再多费笔墨。我们反复思量，又商量了好几回。末了，妻子同意：这一变动是明智的，对大家都最好。随后，我们把孩子们也引为心腹，前景在他们眼里一天比一天清晰，他们的欢喜也一天比一天增多。

“我们很快就会个个都变好，是不是？”我家最小的这个说道。他对自己身上的毛病有一种格外鲜明的自觉，而且还总能让旁人也把这些毛病牢记在心。

“怎么这么说呢，鲍勃西（Bobsey）？”

“因为妈妈说，上帝把最早的人安置在一座园子里，他们可乖啦，比后来哪一辈的人都强。上帝最该知道人住在什么地方最好。”

===Original===
Thus Bobsey gave a kind of divine sanction to our project. Of course we had not taken so important a step without asking the Great Father of all to guide us; for we felt that in the mystery of life we too were but little children who knew not what should be on the morrow, or how best to provide for it with any certainty. To our sanguine minds there was in Bobsey’s words a hint of something more than permission to go up out of Egypt.

So it was settled that we should leave our narrow suite of rooms, the Daggetts and the Ricketts, and go to the country. To me naturally fell the task of finding the land flowing with milk and honey to which we should journey in the spring. Meantime we were already emigrants at heart, full of the bustle and excitement of mental preparation.

I prided myself somewhat on my knowledge of human nature, which, in regard to children, conformed to comparatively simple laws. I knew that the change would involve plenty of hard work, self-denial and careful managing, which nothing could redeem from prose; but I aimed to add to our exodus, so far as possible, the elements of adventure and mystery so dear to the hearts of children. The question where we should go was the cause of much discussion, the studying of maps, and the learning of not a little geography.

Merton’s counsel was that we should seek a region abounding in Indians, bears, and “such big game.” His advice made clear the nature of some of his recent reading. He proved, however, that he was not wanting in sense by his readiness to give up these attractive features in the choice of locality.

===Chinese===
鲍勃西这番话，等于给我们的计划盖上了一道神意的印可。当然，如此重大的一步，我们原就不曾不求万有的天父引领便贸然迈出；因为我们感到，在人生的奥秘面前，我们自己也不过是一群小孩子，不知道明天会有什么，也不知道怎样才能十拿九稳地为明天作好打算。而在我们这些满怀希望的人心里，鲍勃西的话隐隐透出的意味，可不止是恩准我们出埃及这一层。

于是事情就这么定了：我们要离开那几间狭窄的屋子，离开达格茨（Daggett）家和里克茨（Rickett）家，到乡下去。为我们开春后要迁往的那块流奶与蜜之地寻一个去处，这担子自然落在我的肩上。与此同时，我们在心里早已是移民了，满怀着精神筹备带来的忙碌和兴奋。

我对自己洞悉人性的本事颇有些自负，而就孩子而论，人性遵循的规律还算比较简单。我知道，这一变动意味着大量的苦活、克己和精打细算，任凭怎么点染也改不了它平淡的本色；但我打算给我们这场“出埃及”式的迁徙，尽可能添上孩子们最心爱的冒险与神秘的成分。我们该往哪儿去，这个问题引出了许许多多的商议、一遍遍的看地图，还顺带学到了不少地理知识。

默顿（Merton）出的主意是，我们该去找一处印第安人、熊和“这类大猎物”成群出没的地方。他这番高见，让人明白了他近来读的都是些什么书。不过，在挑选安家地点时，他痛痛快快地放弃了这些诱人的条件，倒也证明他并不缺少头脑。

Mousie’s soft black eyes always lighted up at the prospect of a flower garden that should be as big as our sitting room. Even in our city apartments, poisoned by gas and devoid of sunlight, she usually managed to keep a little houseplant in bloom, and the thought of placing seeds in the open ground, where, as she said, “the roots could go down to China if they wanted to,” brought the first color I had seen in her face for many a day.

Winnie was our strongest child, and also the one who gave me the most anxiety. Impulsive, warmhearted, restless, she always made me think of an overfull fountain. Her alert black eyes were as eager to see as was her inquisitive mind to pry into everything. She was sturdily built for a girl, and one of the severest punishments we could inflict was to place her in a chair and tell her not to move for an hour. We were beginning to learn that we could no more keep her in our sitting room than we could restrain a mountain brook that foams into a rocky basin only to foam out again. Melissa Daggett was of a very different type—I could never see her without the word “sly” coming into my mind—and her small mysteries awakened Winnie’s curiosity. Now that the latter was promised chickens, and rambles in the woods, Melissa and her secrets became insignificant, and the ready promise to keep aloof from her was given.

As for Bobsey, he should have a pig which he could name and call his own, and for which he might pull weeds and pick up apples. We soon found that he was communing with that phantom pig in his dreams.

===Chinese===
一想到将有一座跟我们家客厅一般大的花园，小耗子（Mousie）那双温柔的黑眼睛总会倏地亮起来。哪怕在被煤气熏害、终年不见阳光的城市公寓里，她通常也能设法让一盆小小的花草开着花；而一想到要把种子撒进露天的泥土——照她说，“根要是愿意，尽可以一直扎到中国去”——她的脸上便泛起了血色，那是我多日以来头一回见到的颜色。

温妮（Winnie）是我们孩子里最结实的一个，也是最叫我操心费神的一个。她性子急、心肠热、一刻也闲不住，总让我想起一泓满得直往外溢的泉水。她那双机灵的黑眼睛什么都急于看个真切，那颗好探究的心也什么都想探个究竟。一个女孩家，她的身子骨算生得结实了，我们能加诸她的最重的惩罚之一，就是让她坐在一把椅子上，告诉她一个钟头之内不许动。我们渐渐明白，想把她圈在我们家客厅里，就跟拦住一道山涧一样办不到——那涧水白沫翻腾地涌进石潭，转眼又翻腾着漫了出去。梅莉茜·达格茨（Melissa Daggett）却是截然不同的另一路孩子——我一见到她，脑子里准会蹦出“狡黠”两个字——她那些小小的神秘勾起了温妮的好奇心。如今温妮既已得到鸡和林间漫游的许诺，梅莉茜和她的秘密顿时变得无足轻重，温妮便痛痛快快地答应不再同她来往。

至于鲍勃西嘛，他该有一头小猪，可以给它起名字、管它叫自己的，还能为它拔草、捡苹果。没过多久我们就发现，他连做梦都在跟那头幻影小猪拉家常。
