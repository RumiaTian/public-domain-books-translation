## XI A Voyage Up the Hudson / XI 溯哈德逊河而上

===Original===
As four p.m. approached, I took the children to a great glass window in the cabin, through which we could see the massive machinery.

“Now,” said I, “watch the steel giant; he is motionless, but in a moment or two he will move.”

True enough, he appeared to take a long breath of steam, and then slowly lifted his polished arms, or levers, and the boat that had been like a part of the wharf began to act as if it were alive and were waking up.

“Now,” I asked, “shall we go to the afterdeck and take our last look at the city, or forward and see the river and whither we are going?”

“Forward! forward!” cried all in chorus.

===Chinese===
快到下午四点，我带着孩子们走到船舱里一面巨大的玻璃窗前，透过窗子，我们望得见那副庞大无比的机器。

“瞧，”我说，“看好这个钢铁巨人；他此刻一动不动，可过不了一两分钟，他就要动起来了。”

果然不错，他仿佛深深吸进一大口蒸汽，随即缓缓举起那双擦得锃亮的手臂——也就是杠杆——于是那条方才还仿佛是码头一部分的船，开始活动起来，就像一个活物正在苏醒。

“那么，”我问道，“咱们是去后甲板，最后看一眼这座城市呢，还是上船头去，看看这条河，看看咱们正往哪儿去？”

“去船头！去船头！”孩子们齐声喊道。

===Original===
“That’s the difference between youth and age,” I thought. “With the young it is always ‘forward.’ ” But we found that we could not go out on the forward deck, for the wind would have carried away my light, frail Mousie, like a feather. Indeed it was whistling a wild tune as we stood in a small room with glass windows all round. The waves were crowned with foaming whitecaps, and the small craft that had to be out in the gale were bobbing up and down, as if possessed. On the river was a strange and lurid light, which seemed to come more from the dashing water than from the sky, so dark was the latter with skurrying clouds.

Mousie clung timidly to my side, but I reassured her by saying: “See how steadily, how evenly and boldly, our great craft goes out on the wide river. In the same way we must go forward, and never be afraid. These boats run every day after the ice disappears, and they are managed by men who know what to do in all sorts of weather.”

She smiled, but whispered, “I think I’ll go back and stay with mamma;” but she soon found much amusement in looking at passing scenes from the windows of the warm after cabin—scenes that were like pictures set in oval frames.

===Chinese===
“这就是年轻和年老的分别，”我心想。“年轻人嘴边总挂着‘向前’两个字。”可我们发现，前甲板根本出不去——那风非把我家小耗子（Mousie）那轻盈纤弱的小身板像片羽毛似的卷走不可。我们此刻正站在一间四围镶着玻璃窗的小舱室里，风在外面呼啸着，吹的是一支野腔野调的曲子。浪涛头上顶着白沫飞溅的白色浪冠，那些不得不顶着大风留在河上的小船，颠得上上下下，像着了魔一般。河面上泛着一种怪异而阴惨的光，与其说它来自天空，不如说来自浪花撞击迸溅之处，因为乱云疾走，天空正黑沉得厉害。

小耗子怯生生地偎在我身边，我便宽慰她说：“瞧，我们这条大船开进宽阔的河面，走得多么安稳，多么从容，多么有胆量。我们也该这样朝前走，什么都不必怕。冰一消融，这些船天天都开，管船的人不管遇上什么样的天气，都知道该怎么办。”

她笑了笑，却小声说：“我想我还是回去陪着妈妈吧。”不过很快，她便发觉，坐在暖烘烘的后舱里，隔着窗子看两岸掠过的景致，着实趣味无穷——那一幅幅景色，宛如镶在椭圆框子里的图画。

===Original===
The other children appeared fascinated by the scene, especially Winnie, whose bold black eyes flashed with excitement.

“I want to see everything and know everything,” she said.

“I wish you to see and know about things like these,” I replied, “but not such things as Melissa Daggett would show you.”

“Melissy Daggett, indeed!” cried Winnie. “This beats all her stories. She tried to tell me the other day about a theatre at which a woman killed a man—”

===Chinese===
别的孩子们也看得出神，尤其是温妮（Winnie），一双乌黑大胆的眼睛兴奋得闪闪发亮。

“我什么都想看，什么都想知道，”她说。

“像这样的东西，我巴不得你多看多知道，”我答道，“可梅莉茜·达格茨（Melissa Daggett）给你看的那些，就完全是另一回事了。”

“又是梅莉茜·达格茨！”温妮叫了起来。“这个可比她讲的那些故事都强。前几天她还想跟我说，有家戏园子里一个女人杀了个男人——”

===Original===
“Horrid! I hope you didn’t listen?”

“Only long enough to know the man came to life again, and danced in the next—”

“That will do. I’m not interested in Melissa’s vulgar stories. As you say, this, and all like this, is much better, and will never prevent you from becoming a lady like mamma.”

Winnie’s ambition to become a lady promised to be one of my strong levers in uplifting her character.

===Chinese===
“吓死人！我希望你没听下去？”

“只听了一丁点儿，晓得那个男的又活了过来，还在下一幕里跳舞——”

“行了，够了。梅莉茜那些粗俗故事，我不感兴趣。正像你说的，这个，以及一切像这样的东西，要好得多，决不会妨碍你长成一位像妈妈那样的淑女。”

温妮想当淑女的心愿，看来会是我砥砺她的品性时一根有力的杠杆。

===Original===
I confess that I did not like the looks of the sky or of the snowflakes that began to whirl in the air, but the strong steamer plowed her way rapidly past the city and the villa-crowned shores beyond. The gloom of the storm and of early coming night was over all, and from the distant western shores the Palisades frowned dimly through the obscurity.

My wife came, and after a brief glance shivered and was turning away, when I said, “You don’t like your first glimpse of the country, Winifred?”

“It will look different next June. The children will take cold here. Let them come and watch the machinery.”

This we all did for a time, and then I took them on excursions about the enclosed parts of the boat. The lamps were already lighted, and the piled-up freight stood out in grotesque light and shadow.

===Chinese===
我承认，天色和开始在空中回旋飞舞的雪花，都叫我瞧着不大放心；可是这艘坚固的轮船照旧破浪疾进，驶过了城市，又驶过再远处坐落着幢幢别墅的河岸。风暴与提前降临的暮色，把一切笼罩在阴沉里；西边远岸的帕利塞兹峭壁（the Palisades），隔着昏暗隐隐地蹙着眉头。

妻子走了过来，匆匆看了一眼，便打了个寒噤，正要转身走开，我说：“温妮弗蕾德（Winifred），你头一回看乡下，就不喜欢吗？”

“到明年六月，看上去就两样了。孩子们在这儿要着凉的。让他们来看机器吧。”

大家便依着她的话看了一阵机器，随后我带着他们在船上有舱棚遮蔽的各处逛了一圈。灯已经点上，堆积的货物在光影相映之间显出种种古怪的轮廓。

===Original===
Before very long we were standing by one of the furnace rooms, and the sooty-visaged man threw open the iron doors of the furnace. In the glare of light that rushed forth everything near stood out almost as vividly as it would have done in a steady gleam of lightning. The fireman instantly became a startling silhouette, and the coal that he shovelled into what was like a flaming mouth of a cavern seemed sparkling black diamonds. The snowflakes glimmered as the wind swept them by the wide open window, and in the distance were seen the lights and the dim outline of another boat rushing toward the city. Clang! the iron doors are shut, and all is obscure again.

“Now the boat has had its supper,” said Bobsey. “O dear! I wish I could have a big hot supper.”

The smoking room door stood open, and we lingered near it for some moments, attracted first by a picture of a great fat ox, that suggested grassy meadows, plowing, juicy steaks, and other pleasant things. Then our attention was drawn to a man, evidently a cattle dealer, who was holding forth to others more or less akin to him in their pursuits.

===Chinese===
没过多久，我们便站在了一间锅炉房旁边，满面烟灰的司炉猛地敞开炉膛的铁门。强光扑面涌出，近处的一切霎时显影分明，几乎像被一道持续的闪电照亮一般。司炉工转眼成了个触目的黑色剪影；他一铲铲送进那张岩洞火口般的大嘴里的煤块，竟像一颗颗乌黑发亮的钻石在闪光。风把雪花卷过敞开的大窗，雪花微微地闪烁；远处望得见另一条正向城里疾驶的轮船，灯光点点，轮廓依稀。哐当！铁门重新关上，一切又归于昏暗。

“这下子船吃过晚饭啦，”鲍勃西（Bobsey）说。“哎呀！真希望我也能吃上一顿热腾腾的大晚饭。”

吸烟室的门敞开着，我们在门口流连了片刻，起先吸引我们的，是一幅画着一头肥硕大公牛的图画，叫人联想到青青的草场、犁地的营生、多汁的牛排，还有种种惬意的事情。随后，我们的注意力又让一个汉子引了过去，此人显然是个牛贩子，正对着几个行当跟他多少相近的人高谈阔论。

===Original===
“Yes,” he was saying, “people in the country eat a mighty lot of cow beef, poor and old at that. I was buying calves out near Shawangunk Mountains last week, and stopped at a small tavern. They brought me a steak and I tried to put my knife in it—thought the knife might be dull, but knew my grinders weren’t. Jerusalem! I might have chawed on that steak till now and made no impression. I called the landlord, and said, ‘See here, stranger, if you serve me old boot leather for steak again I’ll blow on your house.’—‘I vow,’ he said, ‘it’s the best I kin get in these diggin’s. You fellers from the city buy up every likely critter that’s for sale, and we have to take what you leave.’ You see, he hit me right between the horns, for it’s about so. Bless your soul, if I’d took in a lot of cow beef like that to Steers and Pinkham, Washington Market, they’d ’a taken my hide off and hung me up ’longside of my beef.”

“Grantin’ all that,” said another man, “folks in the country would be a sight better off if they’d eat more cow beef and less pork. You know the sayin’ about ‘out of the frying pan into the fire’? Well, in some parts I’ve travelled they had better get out of the fryin’ pan, no matter where they fetch up.”

We went away laughing, and I said: “Don’t you be troubled, Mousie; we won’t go to the frying pan altogether to find roses for your cheeks. We’ll paint them red with strawberries and raspberries, the color put on from the inside.”

===Chinese===
“可不是嘛，”他说，“乡下人吃的老母牛肉可真叫多，还净是又瘦又老的。上礼拜我到沙旺克山（Shawangunk Mountains）那一带收牛犊，在一家小客栈落脚。他们给我端来一块牛排，我拿刀往里切——起初还当是刀不快，可我这口后槽牙利不利索，自己心里有数。老天爷！那块牛排我就是从那时一直嚼到这会儿，也休想嚼动半分。我把店家叫来，对他说：‘喂，外乡的朋友，你要是再拿旧靴底子当牛排端给我，我就把你这店给抖搂出去。’——‘我对天起誓，’他说，‘这地界儿上，我能弄到的顶好的就是这个了。你们这些城里来的老哥，把凡是像样点的牲口全买了个精光，剩下的只好我们自个儿吃。’你们瞧，他这话正戳在我两只犄角当间，因为情形差不多就是这个样。老天爷在上，我要是把那样的老母牛肉运一大批给华盛顿市场（Washington Market）的斯蒂尔斯-平克汉姆商号（Steers and Pinkham），他们准会剥了我的皮，把它挂在我的牛肉旁边一起示众。”

“就算你说的全在理，”另一个人说，“乡下人要是少吃些猪肉，多吃些老母牛肉，日子还要好过得多。你们总知道那句老话，‘才出煎锅，又落火坑’吧？嗐，在我走过的一些地方，他们顶好是先出了煎锅再说，哪怕往后落到哪儿都成。”

我们大笑着走开了。我说：“小耗子，你别犯愁；要给你的面颊添玫瑰色，我们才不会一股脑儿指靠那口煎锅。咱们要用草莓和树莓把脸蛋染红——这颜色可是从里头往外透出来的。”

===Original===
As time passed, the storm increased, and the air became so thick with driving snow that the boat’s speed was slackened. Occasionally we “slowed up” for some moments. The passengers shook their heads and remarked, dolefully, “There’s no telling when we’ll arrive.”

I made up my mind that it would be good economy for us all to have a hearty hot supper, as Bobsey had suggested; and when, at last, the gong resounded through the boat, we trooped down with the others to the lower cabin, where there were several long tables, with colored waiters in attendance. We had not been in these lower regions before, and the eyes of the children soon wandered from their plates to the berths, or sleeping bunks, which lined the sides of the cabin.

“Yes,” I replied, in answer to their questions; “it is a big supper room now, but by and by it will be a big bedroom, and people will be tucked away in these berths, just as if they were laid on shelves, one over the other.”

The abundant and delicious supper, in which steaks, not from cow beef, were the chief feature, gave each one of us solid comfort and satisfaction. Bobsey ate until the passengers around him were laughing, but he, with superb indifference, attended strictly to business.

===Chinese===
时间一点点过去，风暴愈来愈紧，空中卷着漫天飞雪，浓得化不开，船速也随之放慢了。我们时不时便“减速缓行”那么一阵子。乘客们直摇头，愁眉苦脸地念叨：“天晓得什么时候才能到。”

我打定主意，像鲍勃西提议的那样，叫全家饱饱地吃一顿热晚饭，这才是最划算不过的事。锣声终于在船上当当响开时，我们便随着众人拥下底舱。那里摆着几张长桌，有色人种侍者在旁伺候。我们从前不曾到过船舱底层，孩子们的目光很快便从盘子上溜开，去打量沿舱壁排开的一张张铺位——也就是睡觉的床铺。

“是呀，”我回答他们的追问，“这儿眼下是大饭厅，过不多久就要变成大卧室，人们会被安顿进这些铺位里，就像搁到架子上去的物件一样，一层摞着一层。”

这顿晚饭丰盛可口，主打的是牛排——可不是老母牛肉做的——叫我们每个人吃得结结实实、舒心满意。鲍勃西一直吃到周围的乘客都笑出声来，他却一脸天塌不管的气定神闲，只顾埋头办他的正事。

===Original===
My wife whispered, “You must all eat enough to last a week, for I shan’t have time to cook anything;” and I was much pleased at the good example which she and Mousie set us.

Both before and after supper I conducted Bobsey to the washroom, and he made the people laugh as he stood on a chair and washed his face. But he was a sturdy little fellow, and only laughed back when a man said he looked as though he was going to dive into the basin.

Mousie at last began to show signs of fatigue; and learning that it would be several hours still before we could hope to arrive, so severe was the storm, I procured the use of a stateroom, and soon Bobsey was snoring in the upper berth, and my invalid girl smiling and talking in soft tones to her mother in the lower couch. Winnie, Merton, and I prowled around, spending the time as best we could. Occasionally we looked through the windows at the bow, and wondered how the pilot could find his way through the tempest. I confess I had fears lest he might not do this, and felt that I should be grateful indeed when my little band was safe on shore. The people in charge of the boat, however, knew their business.

妻子悄声说：“你们都得吃到能顶一个星期才好，我可腾不出工夫做饭了。”她和小耗子给我们立了个好榜样，我心里着实欢喜。

饭前饭后，我都领着鲍勃西去了一趟盥洗室；他站在椅子上洗脸，逗得众人直乐。好在这小家伙结实得很，有人说他瞧着像要一头扎进脸盆里去，他也只回敬人家一阵笑。
