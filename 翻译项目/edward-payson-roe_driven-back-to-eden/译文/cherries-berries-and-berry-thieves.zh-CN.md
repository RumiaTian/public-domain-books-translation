## XXXII Cherries, Berries, and Berry-Thieves / XXXII 樱桃、莓果与偷莓贼

===Original===
An evening or two after this we were taught that not even in our retired nook had we escaped the dangers of city life. Winnie and Bobsey, in their rambles after strawberries, had met two other children, and, early in the acquaintance, fortunately brought them to the house. The moment I saw the strange girl, I recognized a rural type of Melissa Daggett, while the urchin of Bobsey’s age did not scruple to use vile language in my hearing. I doubt whether the poor little savage had any better vernacular. I told them kindly but firmly that they must not come on the place again without my permission.

After supper I went over and asked Mr. Jones about these children, and he replied, significantly, looking around first to make sure that no one heard him:

“Mr. Durham, steer clear of those people. You know there are certain varmints on a farm to which we give a wide berth and kill ’em when we can. Of course we can’t kill off this family, although a good contribution could be taken up any day to move ’em a hundred miles away. Still about everybody gives ’em a wide berth, and is civil to their faces. They’ll rob you more or less, and you might as well make up your mind to it, and let ’em alone.”

===Chinese===
这一两晚之后，我们便领教了：即便躲在我们这处僻静的角落，也未能逃开城市生活的种种危险。温妮（Winnie）和鲍勃西（Bobsey）出去寻草莓游逛的时候，遇见了另外两个孩子，而且刚一结识，就幸运地把他们带到了家里。我一见那陌生姑娘，便认出她是梅莉茜·达格茨（Melissa Daggett）一类的乡下翻版；而那个同鲍勃西年纪相仿的小顽童，竟当着我的面毫无顾忌地满口脏话。我疑心这可怜的小野人嘴里也吐不出什么更好的语言。我和气然而坚决地告诉他们，不经我的许可，不许再踏进我们这个地方。

晚饭后我过去问琼斯先生（Mr. Jones），这几个孩子是什么来路。他先朝四下里张望，确信没有旁人听见，才意味深长地答道：

“达勒姆先生（Mr. Durham），那路人你可得避而远之。你知道，农场上总有那么几种祸害，我们向来绕着走，能除掉的时候就除掉。这一家子我们当然没法除掉，虽说随便哪一天都募得起一笔可观的捐款，把他们送到一百英里以外去。话虽如此，差不多人人见了他们都绕道走，当面倒还客客气气。他们多少要偷你一点，你索性认了这件事，别去招惹他们。”

===Original===
“Suppose I don’t let them alone?”

“Well—remember, now, this is wholly between ourselves—there’s been barns burned around here. Everybody’s satisfied who sot ’em afire, but nothin’ can be proved. Your cow or horse, too, might suddenly die. There’s no tellin’ what accidents would happen if you got their ill will.”

“I can’t take the course you suggest toward this family,” I said, after a little thought. “It seems to me wrong on both sides. On one hand, they are treated as outlaws, and that would go far to make them such; on the other, they are permitted to levy a sort of blackmail and commit crime with impunity. Of course I must keep my children away from them; but, if the chance offers, I shall show the family kindness, and if they molest me I shall try to give them the law to the utmost.”

“Well,” concluded Mr. Jones, with a shrug, “I’ve warned you, if they git down on yer, yer’ll find ’em snakes in the grass.”

===Chinese===
“倘若我不随他们去呢？”

“嗯——记着，这话只在你我之间说——这一带烧过谷仓。放火的人个个心安理得，可什么证据也抓不着。你的牛啊马啊，也说不定哪天突然就死了。你要是招了他们的恨，会出什么样的‘意外’，谁也说不准。”

“对这一家人，我可不能照你说的法子办，”我略想了想，说道，“依我看，这事两头都不对。一头，人们把他们当法外之徒看待，这本身就会把他们真逼成法外之徒；另一头，又听任他们勒索一种变相的买路钱，犯了事也逍遥法外。当然，我一定要让孩子离他们远远的；可只要有机会，我会对这一家表示善意；他们若来侵害我，我就尽力依法究办，一追到底。”

“好吧，”琼斯先生耸耸肩，收住了话头，“反正我提醒过你了：他们要是恨上你，你就会发现，他们是草里藏着的蛇。”

===Original===
Returning home, I said nothing to Winnie and Bobsey against their recent companions, but told them that if they went with them again, or made the acquaintance of other strangers without permission, they would be put on bread and water for an entire day—that all such action was positively forbidden.

It was evident, however, that the Melissa Daggett element was present in the country, and in an aggravated form. That it was not next door, or, rather, in the next room, was the redeeming feature. Residents in the country are usually separated by wide spaces from evil association.

It must not be thought that my wife and children had no society except that afforded by Mr. Jones’s family. They were gradually making pleasant and useful acquaintances, especially among those whom we met at church; but as these people have no material part in this simple history, they are not mentioned.

===Chinese===
回到家，我没有在温妮和鲍勃西面前数落他们的新伙伴，只告诉他们：要是再同那两个孩子一起玩，或者不经许可同别的陌生人结识，就要罚他们一整天只许吃面包、喝白水——这一类举动是断然禁止的。

不过显而易见，梅莉茜·达格茨那路成分，乡下也有，而且有过之而无不及。所幸它不在我们隔壁，——更确切地说，不在隔壁的房间里。这算是一桩可取之处。乡下人家彼此相隔开阔，通常离恶习的熏染远远的。

但切不可以为，我的妻子和孩子除了琼斯先生一家，便没有别的社交。他们正渐渐结交一些可亲而有益的相识，尤其在教堂里遇见的那些人当中；只是这些人在这部简朴的历史里算不上要紧角色，所以不曾提到。

===Original===
The most important activities of the season were now drawing very near. The cherries were swelling fast; the currants were growing red, and were already pronounced “nice for pies;” and one morning Merton came rushing in with a red raspberry from the Highland Hardy variety. I was glad the time was at hand when I should begin to receive something besides advice from Mr. Bogart; for, careful as we had been, the drain on my capital had been long and steady, and were eager for the turn of the tide.

I had bought a number of old Mr. Jamison’s crates, had painted out his name and replaced it with mine. I now wrote to Mr. Bogart for packages best adapted to the shipping of cherries, currants, and raspberries. For the first he sent me baskets that held about a peck. These baskets were so cheap that they could be sold with the fruit. For currants, crates containing twenty-four quart baskets were forwarded. These, he wrote, would also do for blackcaps this season, and for strawberries next year. For the red raspberries he sent me quite different crates, filled with little baskets holding only half a pint of fruit. Limited supplies of these packages were sent, for he said that a telegram would bring more the same day.

The corn and potatoes were becoming weedy again. This time I made use of a light plow, Merton leading old Bay as at first. Then, with our hoes, we gave the rows a final dressing out. By the time we had finished, some of our grass was fit to cut, the raspberries needed a careful picking over, and the cherries on one tree were ready for market. The children and robins had already feasted, but I was hungry for a check from New York.

===Chinese===
这个季节顶要紧的农事，眼看就要到了。樱桃鼓胀得飞快；红醋栗渐渐泛红，已经被评定为“做馅饼很好了”；一天早晨，默顿（Merton）捧着一颗“高地耐寒”（Highland Hardy）品种的红树莓，冲进屋里来。我暗自欢喜：不久之后，博加特先生（Mr. Bogart）给我的就不光是指导，还会有别的进项了；因为尽管我们处处节俭，我的本钱却长年不断地往外流，我们一心巴望着时来运转。

我买下了老贾姆森先生（Mr. Jamison）的几只板条箱，用油漆涂掉箱上他的名字，换上了我的。这时我写信给博加特先生，要一批最适合装运樱桃、红醋栗和树莓的包装。装樱桃用的，他寄来约莫一配克一只的篮子。这种篮子便宜得很，可以连篮带果一并出卖。装红醋栗的，寄来的是装有二十四只夸脱篮的板条箱。他信上说，这种箱子这一季还能装黑帽树莓（blackcaps），明年还能装草莓。红树莓用的则是全然不同的板条箱，里面盛着只装半品脱果子的小篮。这些包装寄来的数量有限，因为他说，只要拍个电报，当天就能再运一批来。

玉米和马铃薯地里又长起草来。这一回我用的是轻犁，仍像当初那样，由默顿牵着老枣红马（old Bay）在前头走。随后我们又用锄头把垄间最后清理了一遍。活刚干完，我们的草场已有一部分可以开割，树莓需要细心拣摘一遍，有一棵树上的樱桃也可以应市了。孩子们和知更鸟早已大饱口福，我馋的却是一张从纽约寄来的支票。

===Original===
I had long since decided not to attempt to carry on haying alone at this critical season, but had hired a man, too aged to hold his own among the harvesters on the neighboring farms. Mr. Jones had said of him: “He’s a careful, trusty old fellow, who can do a good day’s work yet if you don’t hurry him. Most of your grass is in the meadow, some parts fit to cut before the others. Let the old man begin and mow what he can, every day. Then you won’t have to cure and get in a great lot of hay all at once, and perhaps, too, when your raspberries most need pickin’.”

So, during the last days of June, old Mr. Jacox, who came at moderate wages, put in his scythe on the uplands. I spread the grass and raked it up when dry, and, with the aid of Merton and a rude, extemporized rack on the market wagon, got the hay gradually into the barn. This labor took only part of the day; the rest of the time was employed in the garden and in picking fruit.

On the last day of June we gathered a crate of early raspberries and eight baskets of cherries. In the cool of the afternoon, these were placed in the wagon, and with my wife and the three younger children, I drove to the Maizeville Landing with our first shipment to Mr. Bogart.

===Chinese===
在这个紧要关头，我早已打定主意，不独自张罗割草晒草的事，而是雇了一个人——他年纪太老，在邻近农场的割草人当中已挣不来饭碗。琼斯先生谈起他时说过：“这老头儿仔细、可靠，只要你别催他，一天的整活他还是做得动的。你的草多半在草场上，有的地方先熟，有的地方后熟。就让老头儿每天开镰，能割多少割多少。这样你就不必把一大批干草一下子晒好、收进；再说，赶上你树莓顶需要采摘的时候，人手说不定正好腾得开。”

于是，在六月末的几天里，老杰科克斯先生（Mr. Jacox）以适中的工钱应雇而来，到高处的旱地上挥镰开割。草由我摊开翻晒，干了便耙拢起来；凭着默顿搭手，加上运货马车上现凑的一个粗陋草架，干草渐渐运进了谷仓。这桩活只占去一天里的一部分；其余的时间，便用来料理园子和采摘果子。

六月的最后一天，我们收了一箱早树莓、八篮樱桃。下午凉快下来的时候，这些果子装上了马车；我带着妻子和三个小的，赶到梅兹维尔码头（Maizeville Landing），把头一批货发给博加特先生。

===Original===
“We are ‘p’oducers,’ at last, as Bobsey said,” I cried, joyously. “And I trust that this small beginning will end in such big loads as will leave us no room for wife and children, but will eventually give them a carriage to ride in.”

Merton remained on guard to watch our precious ripening fruit.

After our departure he began a vigilant patrol of the place, feeling much like a sentinel left on guard. About sundown, he told me, as he was passing through the raspberry field, he thought he caught a glimpse of an old straw hat dodging down behind the bushes. He bounded toward the spot, a moment later confronting three children with tin pails. The two younger proved to be Winnie’s objectionable acquaintances that I had told to keep off the place. The eldest was a boy, not far from Merton’s age, and had justly won the name of being the worst boy in the region. All were the children of the dangerous neighbor against whom Mr. Jones had warned me.

===Chinese===
“咱们终于是‘生厂者’啦——这是鲍勃西的说法，”我喜滋滋地大声说。“我指望这个小小的开头，日后会变成满满当当的大车装载，挤得车上再腾不出妻子孩子的座位，到头来，却好给他们换一辆出行的马车来坐。”

默顿留下来守卫，看护我们那些宝贝似的、正在成熟的果子。

我们走后，他便开始小心翼翼地在庄上巡行，自觉像个留守的哨兵。他后来告诉我，太阳快落山时，他正穿过树莓地，仿佛瞥见一顶旧草帽往树丛后面一缩。他一个箭步朝那地方蹿过去，转眼便同三个提着白铁桶的孩子迎面撞上。两个小的，正是温妮那两个叫人头疼的相识——我早吩咐过他们，不许再踏上这块地方；顶大的一个是男孩，年纪同默顿相差无几，在这一带早已名不虚传地博得了“顶坏的孩子”的名声。三个孩子，全是琼斯先生提醒我提防的那个危险人家的儿女。

===Original===
The boy at first regarded Merton with a sullen, defiant look, while his brother and sister coolly continued to steal the fruit.

“Clear out,” cried Merton. “We’ll have you put in jail if you come here again.”

“You shut up and clear out yerself,” said the boy, threateningly, “or I’ll break yer head. Yer pap’s away, and we ain’t afraid of you. What’s more, we’re goin’ ter have some cherries before—”

Now Merton had a quick temper, and at this moment sprang at the fellow who was adding insult to injury, so quickly that he got in a blow that blackened one of the thief’s eyes.

===Chinese===
那男孩起先阴沉着脸，挑衅地瞪着默顿，他的弟弟妹妹却若无其事，照旧偷他们的果子。

“滚出去！”默顿喝道。“再敢上这儿来，就把你们送进牢里去。”

“你给我闭上嘴，自己滚吧，”那男孩气势汹汹地说，“不然我就砸烂你的脑袋。你爸不在家，我们才不怕你呢。还有，这樱桃我们说什么也要弄几个再——”

默顿本是个急性子，此刻见这家伙偷了东西还要逞凶骂人，登时扑了上去，出手快极，一拳打得那贼乌了一只眼。

===Original===
Then they clinched, and, although his antagonist was the heavier, Merton thinks he could have whipped him had not the two younger marauders attacked him, tooth and nail, like cats. Finding himself getting the worst of it, he instinctively sent out a cry for his stanch friend Junior.

Fortunately, this ally was coming along the road toward our house, and he gave an answering halloo.

The vagrants, apparently, had a wholesome fear of John Jones, junior, for, on hearing his voice, they beat a hurried retreat; but knowing that no one was at the house, and in the spirit of revengeful mischief, they took their flight in that direction. Seeing Mousie’s flowerbed, they ran and jumped upon that, breaking down half the plants, then dashed off through the coops, releasing the hens, and scattering the broods of chickens. Merton and Junior, who for a few moments had lost sight of the invaders in the thick raspberry bushes, were now in hot pursuit, and would have caught them again, had they not seen a man coming up the lane, accompanied by a big dog. Junior laid a hand on headlong Merton, whose blood was now at boiling heat, and said, “Stop.”

===Chinese===
接着两人扭作一团。他的对手虽然更沉些，默顿自忖本来打得赢他，怎奈那两个小强盗像猫一样连抓带咬，一齐扑到他身上。眼见自己渐渐吃了亏，他出于本能，高声呼唤他那位忠实的朋友小琼斯（Junior）。

幸好这位援军正沿着大路朝我们家走来，闻声高高地应了一声。

显然，这伙流浪儿对约翰·琼斯家的小琼斯着实有些忌惮，一听见他的喊声，便仓皇后撤；可他们知道家里没有人，又存着报复捣乱的心，便朝那个方向逃去。一看见小耗子（Mousie）的花圃，他们就跑过去从上面一路践踏，踏坏了半畦花草；跟着又冲过鸡笼，放走了母鸡，惊得一群群小鸡四散奔逃。默顿和小琼斯方才在茂密的树莓丛里一时望不见这些入侵者的踪影，此刻已紧紧追了上来，眼看又要抓住他们，却见巷口走来一个男人，还带着一条大狗。小琼斯一把按住直往前冲的默顿——他的血此刻正烧得滚沸——说道：“站住。”
