## A Deal in Ostriches / 一桩鸵鸟买卖

===Original===
"Talking of the prices of birds, I've seen an ostrich that cost three hundred pounds," said the Taxidermist, recalling his youth of travel. "Three hundred pounds!"
He looked at me over his spectacles. "I've seen another that was refused at four."

===Chinese===
"说起鸟的价钱，"那位动物标本师（the Taxidermist）回想起自己年轻时四处游历的岁月，说道，"我见过一只鸵鸟卖了三百镑。三百镑哪！"
他从眼镜框上头瞅着我。"我还见过另一只，四百镑都没人肯要。"

===Original===
"No," he said, "it wasn't any fancy points. They was just plain ostriches. A little off colour, too﻿—owing to dietary. And there wasn't any particular restriction of the demand either. You'd have thought five ostriches would have ruled cheap on an East Indiaman. But the point was, one of 'em had swallowed a diamond.

===Chinese===
"不是的，"他说，"不是什么名贵品种。就是普普通通的鸵鸟。毛色还稍许差了些——是饲料的缘故。需求上也没什么特别的限制。您原本会以为，一艘东印度商船（East Indiaman）上一下子冒出五只鸵鸟，行市该看跌才是。可妙就妙在，其中有一只吞下了一颗钻石。

===Original===
"The chap it got it off was Sir Mohini Padishah, a tremendous swell, a Piccadilly swell you might say up to the neck of him, and then an ugly black head and a whopping turban, with this diamond in it. The blessed bird pecked suddenly and had it, and when the chap made a fuss it realised it had done wrong, I suppose, and went and mixed itself with the others to preserve its incog. It all happened in a minute. I was among the first to arrive, and there was this heathen going over his gods, and two sailors and the man who had charge of the birds laughing fit to split. It was a rummy way of losing a jewel, come to think of it. The man in charge hadn’t been about just at the moment, so that he didn’t know which bird it was. Clean lost, you see. I didn’t feel half sorry, to tell you the truth. The beggar had been swaggering over his blessed diamond ever since he came aboard.

===Chinese===
"那只鸟是从莫希尼·帕迪沙爵士（Sir Mohini Padishah）那儿把钻石啄了去的。这位爵士是个了不起的大人物，可以说，脖子以下是个地道的皮卡迪利（Piccadilly）阔少，再往上呢，却是一颗又黑又丑的脑袋，缠着老大一匹头巾，那颗钻石就别在上头。那该死的鸟冷不丁一啄，就把它啄到了嘴，等人闹起来，它大约也明白自己闯了祸，便钻进别的鸟堆里去，好保住自己那点'隐姓埋名'的把戏。这一切前后不过一分钟的工夫。我算是头一批赶到的人里头的，只见这异教徒正向他的诸神逐一哀告，旁边两个水手和那个看管鸵鸟的人笑得肠子都要断了。细细想来，丢一颗宝石还有比这更稀奇古怪的法子么。看管鸟的人那一会儿工夫没在跟前，所以也不知道是哪一只鸟干的。这一下可就全丢了，您瞧。说实话，我心里头也并不怎么可惜。这小子自打上了船，就一直在那儿拿他那该死的钻石神气活现地臭显摆。

===Original===
"A thing like that goes from stem to stern of a ship in no time. Everyone was talking about it. Padishah went below to hide his feelings. At dinner﻿—he pigged at a table by himself, him and two other Hindus﻿—the captain kind of jeered at him about it, and he got very excited. He turned round and talked into my ear. He would not buy the birds; he would have his diamond. He demanded his rights as a British subject. His diamond must be found. He was firm upon that. He would appeal to the House of Lords. The man in charge of the birds was one of those wooden-headed chaps you can’t get a new idea into anyhow. He refused any proposal to interfere with the birds by way of medicine. His instructions were to feed them so-and-so and treat them so-and-so, and it was as much as his place was worth not to feed them so-and-so and treat them so-and-so. Padishah had wanted a stomach-pump﻿—though you can’t do that to a bird, you know. This Padishah was full of bad law, like most of these blessed Bengalis, and talked of having a lien on the birds, and so forth. But an old boy, who said his son was a London barrister, argued that what a bird swallowed became ipso facto part of the bird, and that Padishah’s only remedy lay in an action for damages, and even then it might be possible to show contributory negligence. He hadn’t any right of way about an ostrich that didn’t belong to him. That upset Padishah extremely, the more so as most of us expressed an opinion that that was the reasonable view. There wasn’t any lawyer aboard to settle the matter, so we all talked pretty free. At last, after Aden, it appears that he came round to the general opinion, and went privately to the man in charge and made an offer for all five ostriches.

===Chinese===
"这种事在一条船上从船头传到船尾，用不了多大工夫。人人都在谈论。帕迪沙躲到下面船舱里去藏他那副心情。吃饭的时候——他独自占了一张桌子，跟另外两个印度人一处吃——船长就拿这事讥诮了他几句，他便激动起来。他扭过身，凑到我耳边絮叨。他不愿买这些鸟；他要的是他的钻石。他要求享有英国臣民的权利。他的钻石非找回来不可。这一点上他很坚决。他说要上诉到贵族院。看管鸵鸟的那个人是那种木头脑袋的家伙，任你怎么说也塞不进一个新念头去。凡是涉及用药去动那些鸟的提议，他一概回绝。他奉的吩咐是怎样喂、怎样照料，要他偏离这套喂法、照料法，那可是要丢饭碗的事。帕迪沙想用洗胃器——可您也知道，对一只鸟是使不了那个的。这位帕迪沙跟大多数这些该死的孟加拉人（Bengalis）一样，肚子里装着一堆半吊子法律，说什么他对这些鸟享有留置权（lien）啦，等等。可有一位老先生——他说他儿子是伦敦的大律师——则辩称：一只鸟吞下什么，便依此事实（ipso facto）成为这只鸟身体的一部分，帕迪沙唯一的救济途径是提起损害赔偿之诉，就算到了那一步，对方还可能举出他自己的共同过失。对一只根本不属于他的鸵鸟，他谈不上有什么权利。这话叫帕迪沙大为光火，更糟的是，我们当中多数人都表示这才是讲得通的看法。船上又没个律师来给此案定案，所以大家说话都挺放得开。最后，过了亚丁（Aden），他似乎总算回心转意，采纳了众人的意见，私下里去找那看管鸟的人，为全部五只鸵鸟开了个价。

===Original===
"The next morning there was a fine shindy at breakfast. The man hadn’t any authority to deal with the birds, and nothing on Earth would induce him to sell; but it seems he told Padishah that a Eurasian named Potter had already made him an offer, and on that Padishah denounced Potter before us all. But I think the most of us thought it rather smart of Potter, and I know that when Potter said that he’d wired at Aden to London to buy the birds, and would have an answer at Suez, I cursed pretty richly at a lost opportunity.

===Chinese===
"第二天早饭桌上可就闹腾开了。那人没有处置这些鸟的权限，便是天大的事也休想劝他卖；不过据说他告诉帕迪沙，一个名叫波特（Potter）的欧亚混血儿（Eurasian）已经向他开过价了，于是帕迪沙便当着众人的面把波特痛骂了一顿。可我想我们大多数人倒觉得波特这手挺精明；而且我知道，当波特说他在亚丁已经拍了电报到伦敦要买下这些鸟、到了苏伊士（Suez）就能收到回电时，我自己也为这错失的机会狠狠地咒骂了一通。

===Original===
"At Suez, Padishah gave way to tears﻿—actual wet tears﻿—when Potter became the owner of the birds, and offered him two hundred and fifty right off for the five, being more than two hundred percent on what Potter had given. Potter said he’d be hanged if he parted with a feather of them﻿—that he meant to kill them off one by one and find the diamond; but afterwards, thinking it over, he relented a little. He was a gambling hound, was this Potter, a little queer at cards, and this kind of prize-packet business must have suited him down to the ground. Anyhow, he offered, for a lark, to sell the birds separately to separate people by auction at a starting price of £80 for a bird. But one of them, he said, he meant to keep for luck.

===Chinese===
"到了苏伊士，当波特正式成了这些鸟的主人时，帕迪沙竟掉下了眼泪——名副其实、湿漉漉的眼泪——当场便开价£250要买下那五只，比波特当初付出的价钱高出了两倍还多。波特说他见鬼才会松一根羽毛——他打算把鸟一只一只地宰掉，找出那颗钻石；可后来再一盘算，心又稍稍软了下来。这个波特是个嗜赌的无赖，玩牌手脚也有点不干不净，这种抽奖式的买卖简直合他心意到了家。总之，他半是闹着玩地提议：把鸟分头拍卖给不同的人，每只起价£80。不过其中有一只，他说，他要留着讨个彩头。

===Original===
"You must understand this diamond was a valuable one﻿—a little Jew chap, a diamond merchant, who was with us, had put it at three or four thousand when Padishah had shown it to him﻿—and this idea of an ostrich gamble caught on. Now it happened that I’d been having a few talks on general subjects with the man who looked after these ostriches, and quite incidentally he’d said one of the birds was ailing, and he fancied it had indigestion. It had one feather in its tail almost all white, by which I knew it, and so when, next day, the auction started with it, I capped Padishah’s eighty-five by ninety. I fancy I was a bit too sure and eager with my bid, and some of the others spotted the fact that I was in the know. And Padishah went for that particular bird like an irresponsible lunatic. At last the Jew diamond merchant got it for £175, and Padishah said £180 just after the hammer came down﻿—so Potter declared. At any rate the Jew merchant secured it, and there and then he got a gun and shot it. Potter made a Hades of a fuss because he said it would injure the sale of the other three, and Padishah, of course, behaved like an idiot; but all of us were very much excited. I can tell you I was precious glad when that dissection was over, and no diamond had turned up﻿—precious glad. I’d gone to one-forty on that particular bird myself.

===Chinese===
"您得先明白，这颗钻石可是值钱的——船上有一位身材矮小的犹太人，是做钻石生意的，帕迪沙拿给他看过，他估了三四千镑——这么一来，拿鸵鸟赌博这个主意便风行起来。偏巧，我跟那位照管鸵鸟的人先前就闲聊过几次，他无意中说起有一只鸟不大精神，他估摸是消化不良。那只鸟尾巴上有一根羽毛几乎全白，我就凭这个认出它来。所以第二天拍卖先从它开张时，我便在帕迪沙的£85之上加到£90。我想我喊价时未免太有把握、太急切了些，旁人当中有几个便瞧出我肚子里有事。至于帕迪沙，咬住这一只鸟不放，活像个不知死活的疯子。最后那犹太钻石商以£175中标，落槌之后帕迪沙才喊£180——据波特说。无论如何，那犹太商人把它拿下，当场取了一杆枪，把鸟给毙了。波特闹了个鸡飞狗跳，说这样会影响另外三只的销路；帕迪沙嘛，当然像个白痴一样闹腾一通；不过我们一个个也都激动得很。我可以告诉您，等那场开膛破肚结束，没见钻石的影子，我心里那叫一个庆幸——真叫一个庆幸。我自己在那只鸟上出到过£140呢。

===Original===
"The little Jew was like most Jews﻿—he didn’t make any great fuss over bad luck; but Potter declined to go on with the auction until it was understood that the goods could not be delivered until the sale was over. The little Jew wanted to argue that the case was exceptional, and as the discussion ran pretty even, the thing was postponed until the next morning. We had a lively dinner-table that evening, I can tell you, but in the end Potter got his way, since it would stand to reason he would be safer if he stuck to all the birds, and that we owed him some consideration for his sportsmanlike behaviour. And the old gentleman whose son was a lawyer said he’d been thinking the thing over and that it was very doubtful if, when a bird had been opened and the diamond recovered, it ought not to be handed back to the proper owner. I remember I suggested it came under the laws of treasure-trove﻿—which was really the truth of the matter. There was a hot argument, and we settled it was certainly foolish to kill the bird on board the ship. Then the old gentleman, going at large through his legal talk, tried to make out the sale was a lottery and illegal, and appealed to the captain; but Potter said he sold the birds as ostriches. He didn’t want to sell any diamonds, he said, and didn’t offer that as an inducement. The three birds he put up, to the best of his knowledge and belief, did not contain a diamond. It was in the one he kept﻿—so he hoped.

===Chinese===
"那位矮小的犹太人跟大多数犹太人一样——倒了霉也不大惊小怪；可波特却拒绝把拍卖继续下去，除非大家讲明白：货要等整场拍卖结束才能交割。那矮小的犹太人想争辩说本案情况特殊，由于双方议论不相上下，事情便搁到第二天早上再说。那天晚饭桌上可热闹了，我告诉您吧，但最后还是波特说了算，因为照理说，他要把所有鸵鸟都攥在手里才稳妥些，再说他这番够意思的做法，我们也该有所表示才是。那位儿子当律师的老先生又说他把这事反复琢磨了一番，觉得大可怀疑：要是鸟被剖开、钻石找了回来，是不是就该物归原主呢。我记得我提议说，这事该归"埋藏物法"（treasure-trove）管辖——这其实才是此事的真相。众人争论得面红耳赤，最后议定一条：在船上把鸟宰掉确属愚蠢之举。那位老先生又信口把他那套法律言论扯开去，硬说这场买卖实质上是一种抽彩，是非法的，还去搬请船长做主；可波特说，他卖的是鸵鸟，鸵鸟而已（as ostriches）。他可没打算卖什么钻石，也并没有拿它来招揽生意。他拿出来拍卖的那三只，据他所知所信，并不含有钻石。钻石在他留下的那一只里——他是这样盼着的。
