## The Moth / 飞蛾

===Original===
Probably you have heard of Hapley—not W. T. Hapley, the son, but the celebrated Hapley, the Hapley of *Periplaneta Hapliia*, Hapley the entomologist.

If so you know at least of the great feud between Hapley and Professor Pawkins, though certain of its consequences may be new to you. For those who have not, a word or two of explanation is necessary, which the idle reader may go over with a glancing eye, if his indolence so incline him.

===Chinese===
你大概听说过哈普利（Hapley）吧——不是那个儿子 W·T·哈普利，而是那位大名鼎鼎的哈普利，那位写出了《哈普利蜚蠊》（*Periplaneta Hapliia*）的哈普利，那位昆虫学家哈普利。

要是听说过，那你至少也知道哈普利和帕金斯教授（Professor Pawkins）之间那场大恩怨，尽管这恩怨的某些后果你也许还是头一回听说。至于没听说过的，这里就必要稍作解释，懒散的读者若天性如此，大可走马观花地跳过去。

===Original===
It is amazing how very widely diffused is the ignorance of such really important matters as this Hapley-Pawkins feud. Those epoch-making controversies, again, that have convulsed the Geological Society are, I verily believe, almost entirely unknown outside the fellowship of that body. I have heard men of fair general education even refer to the great scenes at these meetings as vestry-meeting squabbles. Yet the great hate of the English and Scotch geologists has lasted now half a century, and has “left deep and abundant marks upon the body of the science.” And this Hapley-Pawkins business, though perhaps a more personal affair, stirred passions as profound, if not profounder. Your common man has no conception of the zeal that animates a scientific investigator, the fury of contradiction you can arouse in him. It is the *odium theologicum* in a new form. There are men, for instance, who would gladly burn Professor Ray Lankester at Smithfield for his treatment of the Mollusca in the Encyclopaedia. That fantastic extension of the Cephalopods to cover the Pteropods… But I wander from Hapley and Pawkins.

===Chinese===
像哈普利与帕金斯之争这样真正重大的事情，世人居然如此孤陋寡闻，实在叫人称奇。而那些惊心动魄、搅得地质学会（Geological Society）天翻地覆的划时代论战，我敢说，除了该学会的会员之外几乎无人知晓。我曾听见受过相当教育的人，竟把这些会议上的重大场面说成教区开会时的拌嘴。然而英格兰与苏格兰地质学家之间那场大怨恨，至今已延续了半个世纪，并且已"在科学的躯体上留下了深刻而繁多的印记"。而这场哈普利与帕金斯的风波，虽说更偏于私人意气，所激起的激情却同样深沉，甚至有过之而无不及。普通人根本想象不到，一位科学研究者胸中那股热忱能有多旺，也想象不到你能在他身上激起怎样狂暴的相争之怒。这便是新形式的"神学家的怨恨"（*odium theologicum*）。比方说，就有这样的人，恨不得把雷·兰克斯特教授（Professor Ray Lankester）在史密斯菲尔德（Smithfield）的火刑柱上烧死，只因他在那部百科全书里对软体动物门（Mollusca）的处理。他那异想天开地把头足纲（Cephalopods）扩张开来、将翼足类（Pteropods）也囊括进去的做法……可我这又扯离哈普利和帕金斯了。

===Original===
It began years and years ago, with a revision of the Microlepidoptera (whatever these may be) by Pawkins, in which he extinguished a new species created by Hapley. Hapley, who was always quarrelsome, replied by a stinging impeachment of the entire classification of Pawkins. Pawkins in his “Rejoinder” suggested that Hapley’s microscope was as defective as his power of observation, and called him an “irresponsible meddler”—Hapley was not a professor at that time. Hapley in his retort, spoke of “blundering collectors,” and described, as if inadvertently, Pawkins’ revision as a “miracle of ineptitude.” It was war to the knife. However, it would scarcely interest the reader to detail how these two great men quarrelled, and how the split between them widened until from the Microlepidoptera they were at war upon every open question in entomology. There were memorable occasions. At times the Royal Entomological Society meetings resembled nothing so much as the Chamber of Deputies. On the whole, I fancy Pawkins was nearer the truth than Hapley. But Hapley was skilful with his rhetoric, had a turn for ridicule rare in a scientific man, was endowed with vast energy, and had a fine sense of injury in the matter of the extinguished species; while Pawkins was a man of dull presence, prosy of speech, in shape not unlike a water-barrel, over conscientious with testimonials, and suspected of jobbing museum appointments. So the young men gathered round Hapley and applauded him. It was a long struggle, vicious from the beginning and growing at last to pitiless antagonism. The successive turns of fortune, now an advantage to one side and now another—now Hapley tormented by some success of Pawkins, and now Pawkins outshone by Hapley, belong rather to the history of entomology than to this story.

===Chinese===
这事起于好多年前，缘由是帕金斯对鳞翅目之微蛾类（Microlepidoptera，管它是什么吧）所作的一次修订，他在其中一笔抹杀了哈普利所建立的一个新种。哈普利这人向来好斗，便回敬以对帕金斯整个分类体系的尖刻弹劾。帕金斯在他的《答辩》中暗示，哈普利的显微镜和他观察的能力一样差劲，还说他是个"不负责任的捣乱分子"——那时哈普利还没当上教授。哈普利在反击中谈起"毛手毛脚的采集者"，又仿佛漫不经心地把帕金斯的修订形容为"无能的奇迹"。这便是你死我活的鏖战了。不过， 这两位大人物如何争吵、他们之间的裂痕如何一步步扩大，直到从微蛾类蔓延到昆虫学中每一个悬而未决的问题——细述起来，读者怕也没什么兴致。那中间有过不少令人难忘的场面。有时候，皇家昆虫学会（Royal Entomological Society）的会议简直和下议院的议会没什么两样。总的来说，我想帕金斯比哈普利更接近真相。可哈普利善于辞令，有一种科学工作者中罕见的嘲讽天赋，精力充沛之极，对那个被抹杀的物种怀有一份绝妙的委屈感；而帕金斯则是个形容沉闷、说话啰嗦的人，身形活像一只水桶，写推荐信过于拘泥良心，还有暗中操纵博物馆职位任命的嫌疑。于是年轻人们便聚集到哈普利身边，为他喝彩。这是一场漫长的争斗，从一开始便夹枪带棒，到最后竟演成毫不留情的相倾。其间运势的屡次转换，忽而这一方占先、忽而那一方得手——忽而哈普利为帕金斯的某一成功所苦，忽而帕金斯为哈普利所盖过——这些与其说属于本篇故事，不如说属于昆虫学史。

===Original===
But in 1891 Pawkins, whose health had been bad for some time, published some work upon the “mesoblast” of the Death’s Head Moth. What the mesoblast of the Death’s Head Moth may be does not matter a rap in this story. But the work was far below his usual standard, and gave Hapley an opening he had coveted for years. He must have worked night and day to make the most of his advantage.

In an elaborate critique he rent Pawkins to tatters—one can fancy the man’s disordered black hair, and his queer dark eyes flashing as he went for his antagonist—and Pawkins made a reply, halting, ineffectual, with painful gaps of silence, and yet malignant. There was no mistaking his will to wound Hapley, nor his incapacity to do it. But few of those who heard him—I was absent from that meeting—realised how ill the man was.

===Chinese===
可到了 1891 年，健康状况早已不佳的帕金斯发表了一篇关于鬼脸天蛾（Death's Head Moth）"中胚层"（mesoblast）的著作。鬼脸天蛾的中胚层究竟是什么，在这篇故事里压根儿不要紧。但这篇东西远低于他平日的水平，给了哈普利一个他垂涎多年的破绽。他必是日以继夜地赶工，好把这一优势用到极致。

在一篇精心结撰的批评里，他把帕金斯撕成了碎片——你简直可以想见那人一头乱蓬蓬的黑发，那双古怪的深色眼睛在扑向对手时闪闪放光——而帕金斯的回答吞吞吐吐、软弱无力，夹杂着令人难受的沉默空当，却又满怀恶意。他想刺伤哈普利的用意不会叫人看错，他无力做到这一点也同样不会叫人看错。可听见他发言的人当中——那次会议我没有到场——鲜少有人觉察到这人病已有多重。

===Original===
Hapley got his opponent down, and meant to finish him. He followed with a simply brutal attack upon Pawkins, in the form of a paper upon the development of moths in general, a paper showing evidence of a most extraordinary amount of mental labour, and yet couched in a violently controversial tone. Violent as it was, an editorial note witnesses that it was modified. It must have covered Pawkins with shame and confusion of face. It left no loophole; it was murderous in argument, and utterly contemptuous in tone; an awful thing for the declining years of a man’s career.

The world of entomologists waited breathlessly for the rejoinder from Pawkins. He would try one, for Pawkins had always been game. But when it came it surprised them. For the rejoinder of Pawkins was to catch influenza, proceed to pneumonia, and die.

===Chinese===
哈普利把对手按倒在地，便打算结果他。他随之对帕金斯发动了一场简直堪称暴虐的攻击，形式是一篇论述蛾类一般发育过程的论文，这篇文章显出极其惊人的脑力劳动的痕迹，措辞却带着火药味极浓的论战腔调。饶是如此激烈，一则编者按注明它已被删改过。它必定叫帕金斯羞惭得无地自容。它没有留下任何空子可钻；论点上咄咄逼人、致人死命，口吻上不屑一顾、刻薄到顶；这落到一个人事业晚景里，实在是可怕的东西。

昆虫学家们这一个圈子屏息等候帕金斯的回击。他是会试着回击的，因为帕金斯向来是个不服输的人。可回击真来的时候，却把他们吓了一跳。因为帕金斯的回击，竟是染上流感，进而转为肺炎，然后死了。

===Original===
It was perhaps as effectual a reply as he could make under the circumstances, and largely turned the current of feeling against Hapley. The very people who had most gleefully cheered on those gladiators became serious at the consequence. There could be no reasonable doubt the fret of the defeat had contributed to the death of Pawkins. There was a limit even to scientific controversy, said serious people. Another crushing attack was already in the press and appeared on the day before the funeral. I don’t think Hapley exerted himself to stop it. People remembered how Hapley had hounded down his rival, and forgot that rival’s defects. Scathing satire reads ill over fresh mould. The thing provoked comment in the daily papers. This it was that made me think that you had probably heard of Hapley and this controversy. But, as I have already remarked, scientific workers live very much in a world of their own; half the people, I dare say, who go along Piccadilly to the Academy every year, could not tell you where the learned societies abide. Many even think that research is a kind of happy-family cage in which all kinds of men lie down together in peace.

===Chinese===
在当时的情形下，这也许是他所能作出的最有效的答复了，并在很大程度上把舆论的倾向扭转到不利于哈普利的一边。当初最兴高采烈地为那些角斗士喝彩的人，如今面对这一结局也都敛容起来。败北的焦躁促成了帕金斯的死，这点没有什么可合理怀疑的。即便科学论战也该有个限度，严肃的人这样说。又一篇毁灭性的攻击文章已在排印中，并于葬礼前一日见报。我想哈普利并未费什么力气去阻止它。人们只记得哈普利是怎样把对手追逼穷追，却忘了那位对手的种种不是。尖刻的讽刺登在新坟之上读来未免刺目。这件事在日报上引来了评论。也就是这件事，让我想到你大概听说过哈普利和这场论战。可正如我已经说过的，科学工作者们大半活在一个属于自己的世界里；我敢说，每年顺着皮卡迪利大街（Piccadilly）去皇家美术院（the Academy）的人里头，有一半根本说不出那些学术团体的会所在何处。许多人甚至以为，研究工作是一只"快乐一家亲"的笼子，各色人等在里面和和气气地并肩俯卧。

===Original===
In his private thoughts Hapley could not forgive Pawkins for dying. In the first place, it was a mean dodge to escape the absolute pulverisation Hapley had in hand for him, and in the second, it left Hapley’s mind with a queer gap in it. For twenty years he had worked hard, sometimes far into the night, and seven days a week, with microscope, scalpel, collecting-net, and pen, and almost entirely with reference to Pawkins. The European reputation he had won had come as an incident in that great antipathy. He had gradually worked up to a climax in this last controversy. It had killed Pawkins, but it had also thrown Hapley out of gear, so to speak, and his doctor advised him to give up work for a time, and rest. So Hapley went down into a quiet village in Kent, and thought day and night of Pawkins, and good things it was now impossible to say about him.

===Chinese===
在私下的念头里，哈普利始终不能原谅帕金斯竟然死了。其一，这是逃避哈普利正要加诸他身上的彻底粉饰的下作伎俩；其二，这也在哈普利的心里留下了一个古怪的缺口。二十年来他辛勤工作，有时一直干到深夜，一周七天不歇，与显微镜、解剖刀、捕虫网和钢笔为伴，而几乎完全是以帕金斯为参照的。他赢得的那份欧洲声誉，不过是那场大厌恶中的一段小插曲罢了。在最近这一次论战中，他一步步地把势头推到了顶点。它结果了帕金斯，可也同样地——可以这么说——把哈普利自己弄得脱了榫，他的医生劝他暂时放下工作，歇上一阵。于是哈普利下到肯特（Kent）一处清静的村庄里，日夜想着帕金斯，想着那些如今再也没法对他说出口的漂亮话。

===Original===
At last Hapley began to realise in what direction the preoccupation tended. He determined to make a fight for it, and started by trying to read novels. But he could not get his mind off Pawkins, white in the face and making his last speech—every sentence a beautiful opening for Hapley. He turned to fiction—and found it had no grip on him. He read the *Island Nights’ Entertainments* until his “sense of causation” was shocked beyond endurance by the Bottle Imp. Then he went to Kipling, and found he “proved nothing,” besides being irreverent and vulgar. These scientific people have their limitations. Then unhappily, he tried Besant’s *Inner House*, and the opening chapter set his mind upon learned societies and Pawkins at once.

So Hapley turned to chess, and found it a little more soothing. He soon mastered the moves and the chief gambits and commoner closing positions, and began to beat the Vicar. But then the cylindrical contours of the opposite king began to resemble Pawkins standing up and gasping ineffectually against checkmate, and Hapley decided to give up chess.

===Chinese===
哈普利终于开始意识到这份萦绕在心的念想究竟朝什么方向倾了。他决心要为摆脱它搏一搏，开头的一招是试着读小说。可他怎么也没法把心从帕金斯身上移开——那张苍白的脸，他临终前的那番发言——每一句都为哈普利留下了多么漂亮的破绽可攻。他转向虚构作品——发觉它抓不住自己。他读《海岛夜谭》（*Island Nights' Entertainments*），直读到自己的"因果感"被那个"瓶中妖"（Bottle Imp）冲击得再也无法忍受。于是他改读吉卜林（Kipling），发现他"什么也证明不了"，而且既不虔敬又粗俗。这些搞科学的人自有他们的局限。不幸的是，他接着又读了贝赞特（Besant）的《内室》（*Inner House*），开篇第一章便立刻把他的心思引到了学术团体和帕金斯身上。

于是哈普利转而下棋，发觉这多少让人安定了些。他不久便掌握了各种走法、主要的开局弃兵以及较常见的收官定式，开始能胜过牧师（the Vicar）了。可随即对方那枚国王的圆柱形轮廓开始像极了帕金斯——站起身、被将死时徒劳地喘着气——哈普利便决定不再下棋了。

===Original===
Perhaps the study of some new branch of science would after all be better diversion. The best rest is change of occupation. Hapley determined to plunge at diatoms, and had one of his smaller microscopes and Halibut’s monograph sent down from London. He thought that perhaps if he could get up a vigorous quarrel with Halibut, he might be able to begin life afresh and forget Pawkins. And very soon he was hard at work in his habitual strenuous fashion, at these microscopic denizens of the wayside pool.

It was on the third day of the diatoms that Hapley became aware of a novel addition to the local fauna. He was working late at the microscope, and the only light in the room was the brilliant little lamp with the special form of green shade. Like all experienced microscopists, he kept both eyes open. It is the only way to avoid excessive fatigue. One eye was over the instrument, and bright and distinct before that was the circular field of the microscope, across which a brown diatom was slowly moving. With the other eye Hapley saw, as it were, without seeing. He was only dimly conscious of the brass side of the instrument, the illuminated part of the tablecloth, a sheet of notepaper, the foot of the lamp, and the darkened room beyond.

===Chinese===
也许钻研一门新的科学分支终究会是更好的消遣。最好的休息便是调换营生。哈普利决心一头扎进硅藻（diatoms）里去，叫人从伦敦把他一架较小的显微镜和哈利巴特（Halibut）的专著送下来。他想，要是自己能挑起一场和哈利巴特的激烈争辩，或许就能重新开始过日子，把帕金斯忘了。很快他便以自己一贯的劲头，埋头于路旁水洼里这些微小的居民之中了。

正是在钻研硅藻的第三天，哈普利察觉当地动物群中多了一个新奇的成员。他在显微镜前工作到很晚，屋里唯一的亮光便是那盏带特殊绿罩子的小小强光灯。和所有经验丰富的显微镜观察者一样，他双眼都睁着。这是避免过度疲劳的唯一法门。一只眼睛对着目镜，在它之前明亮而清晰地展开着显微镜的圆形视野，一枚褐色硅藻正缓缓地从那上面移过。另一只眼睛，哈普利可谓视而不见。他只是朦朦胧胧地觉察到仪器的黄铜侧壁、桌布被照亮的那个区域、一张信纸、灯座，以及再远处一片昏暗的房间。

===Original===
Suddenly his attention drifted from one eye to the other. The tablecloth was of the material called tapestry by shopmen, and rather brightly coloured. The pattern was in gold, with a small amount of crimson and pale blue upon a greyish ground. At one point the pattern seemed displaced, and there was a vibrating movement of the colours at this point.

Hapley suddenly moved his head back and looked with both eyes. His mouth fell open with astonishment.

It was a large moth or butterfly; its wings spread in butterfly fashion!

It was strange it should be in the room at all, for the windows were closed. Strange that it should not have attracted his attention when fluttering to its present position. Strange that it should match the tablecloth. Stranger far that to him, Hapley, the great entomologist, it was altogether unknown. There was no delusion. It was crawling slowly towards the foot of the lamp.

===Chinese===
忽然间，他的注意力从一只眼睛挪到了另一只眼睛上。那块桌布用的是店伙计所谓的"挂毯"料子，色泽颇为鲜亮。花纹是金色的，带一点深红和淡蓝，衬在灰扑扑的底子上。在某一个位置，花纹似乎错开了，那里的色彩还有一种颤动的样子。

哈普利猛地把头往后一移，用双眼一起看。他惊讶得张大了嘴。

那是一只大飞蛾，或者说蝴蝶；它的翅膀像蝴蝶那样张开着！

窗户都关着，它竟会出现在屋里，这本身就古怪。它一路扑腾到如今这位置时竟没能引起他的注意，也古怪。它竟与桌布的花色相合，也古怪。而更叫人称奇的，是对于他哈普利——这位大昆虫学家——而言，它竟全然是陌生的品种。这不是幻觉。它正慢慢朝灯座爬去。

===Original===
“New Genus, by heavens! And in England!” said Hapley, staring.

Then he suddenly thought of Pawkins. Nothing would have maddened Pawkins more… And Pawkins was dead!

Something about the head and body of the insect became singularly suggestive of Pawkins, just as the chess king had been.

“Confound Pawkins!” said Hapley. “But I must catch this.” And looking round him for some means of capturing the moth, he rose slowly out of his chair. Suddenly the insect rose, struck the edge of the lampshade—Hapley heard the “ping”—and vanished into the shadow.

===Chinese===
"天哪，新属！而且在英格兰！"哈普利瞪着眼睛说。

接着他忽然想起了帕金斯。再没有什么比这更能叫帕金斯抓狂的了……而帕金斯已经死了！

这昆虫的头和身子，有某种东西竟格外地叫人联想到帕金斯，正像那枚棋里的国王一样。

"该死的帕金斯！"哈普利说，"可这一只我非捉住不可。"他四下里找捉飞蛾的法子，慢慢地从椅子里站起身来。忽然那昆虫腾起，撞在灯罩的边上——哈普利听见"叮"的一声——便隐入了暗影里。

===Original===
In a moment Hapley had whipped off the shade, so that the whole room was illuminated. The thing had disappeared, but soon his practised eye detected it upon the wallpaper near the door.
