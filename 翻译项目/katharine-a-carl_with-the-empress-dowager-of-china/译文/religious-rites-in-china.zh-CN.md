## XXIX Religious Rites in China / 二十九 中国的宗教祭祀

===Original===
There are three great religions in China﻿—Buddhism, Taoism, and the worship of Nature. The worship of Nature, in which is embodied their highest idea of an Invisible Deity, is the purest form of religion in China. Its Temples are situated in a magnificent Park in the Chinese City of Peking. The Temple of Heaven, the most imposing of the group, spherical and triple-domed, rears its proud height here and is visible from afar. Its triple dome tiled, without, in the sacred green of Nature and vaulted within in Heaven’s own blue, is surrounded by groves of century-old arborvitae. In other parts of the great Park are the scarcely less splendid Temples to the Earth, to the Sun and Moon and to Agriculture, and grandest, most unique of these Temples, is that to the Invisible Deity. Its foundations are the Earth, its walls are limitless Ether, its dome Heaven’s own vault! On its great open altar this Nature worship has its culmination and reaches its highest fulfilment. This altar is the Holy of Holies, the tabernacle of the group of Temples consecrated to the worship of Nature.

===Chinese===
中国有三大宗教——佛教、道教，以及自然崇拜。自然崇拜寄托着中国人对无形之皇天上帝（the Invisible Deity）的最高观念，是中国最纯粹的宗教形式。其坛庙坐落于北京（Peking）外城一片宏伟的园苑之中。其中最为巍峨的天坛（Temple of Heaven）呈球形，三重圆顶，于园中昂然矗立，远远便可望见。那三重圆顶，外面覆以神圣的自然之绿的琉璃瓦，穹顶之内则是一片上苍本身的青蓝，四周环绕着百年古柏的苍翠林海。在这片大园苑的其他各处，还有几座几乎同样华美的坛庙，分祭地、日、月与先农；而其中最宏大、最为独特的，乃是那祭祀无形之皇天上帝的坛庙。它的根基是大地，它的四壁是无垠的太空，它的圆顶便是苍穹本身！在它那宏大的露天祭坛之上，这自然崇拜臻于极至，达到其最高的圆满。这座祭坛便是至圣所，是这一组奉献于自然崇拜的坛庙中的圣幕。

===Original===
It is built in the center of a great marble-paved space with the secular arborvitae radiating therefrom in long concentric avenues. It is of pure white marble, round as is the Earth. The Trinity in Nature and its Infinity are symbolized in its three superposed circles. Each of the circular platforms is surrounded by an exquisitely carved balustrade and approached by flights of nine steps each, to the north, south, east, and west. The central point of the great upper circle thus represents the center of the Universe, accessible from every point of the compass.

===Chinese===
它坐落在一大片以大理石铺就的广场正中，岁月悠久的古柏自此向四面八方作同心圆式辐射，化作一道道长长的林荫甬道。它由纯白大理石砌成，圆如大地。自然界的三一与其无垠，正象征于这三层相叠的圆台之上。每一层圆台四周都环以精雕细刻的汉白玉栏杆，南北东西四面各设一段九级台阶，拾级而上。于是乎，最上层大圆的中心便代表了宇宙之中心，自罗盘上任何一方皆可通达。

===Original===
Here in this symbolic center of the world, in this great Temple, whose walls are Space, whose towers are Infinity, on this great triple altar, canopied with Heaven itself, the Emperor of China, “Son of Heaven,” glorifies the Invisible Deity and sacrifices for the prosperity of “the Great, Pure Kingdom” and his people. This worship of the Invisible Deity has no Priestly Hierarchy. The Emperor of China is its one High Priest. He alone is worthy, as the Son of Heaven, to perform its unique ceremonies, on its one great Altar, in its single great Temple of China.

===Chinese===
就在这象征性的世界中心，在这座以太空为四壁、以无垠为塔楼的大庙之中，在这座以苍穹本身为华盖的三层大坛之上，中国皇上——「天子」——赞颂那无形的皇天上帝，并为「大清国」与天下子民的繁荣而献祭。这种对无形之上帝的崇拜，并无神职体系。中国皇上便是其唯一的大祭司。唯独他，身为天子，方配在天下唯一的大坛之上、中国唯一的大庙之中，行其独异的祭典。

===Original===
The Emperor prepares himself for the great ceremony of the semiannual celebration on this altar by a rigorous fast of three days, spending the final night before the celebration in a vigil in the Great Park of the Nature Temples, where there is a Purifying Palace set aside for his use. This glorification of the Invisible Deity at the summer and winter solstices is the most solemn act performed by the Emperor in his quality of Son of Heaven.

===Chinese===
为这坛上岁中两次的大祭之礼，皇上（the Emperor）须先严格斋戒三日；及至祭前最后一夜，便在自然诸坛的大园苑中守夜不寐——园中特设有一座斋宫（Purifying Palace），供他斋宿之用。这于夏至、冬至二日对无形之上帝所行的赞礼，乃是皇上以天子之身所行的最为庄严肃穆的大典。

===Original===
Temple of Heaven﻿—Peking

===Chinese===
天坛——北京

===Original===
The Emperor is not only the one High Priest worthy to sacrifice on the great altars to the Invisible Deity, the Priestly Hierarchy of the whole of this Cult of Nature is vested in his Sacred Person. He alone offers sacrifices in the Temples of Heaven and the other Great Temples, at times set aside in the Book of Rites, and on special occasions. When famine devastates the land, when drought or any other National Calamity is visited upon the Empire, the Emperor prays in these Temples for its cessation, for he is not only the High Priest, but as “Son of Heaven” is the expiator for the afflictions visited upon his people by Heaven, and he publicly holds himself responsible for the misfortunes of the Empire. According to the Book of Rites, he says in time of trouble, “I will purify myself by sacrifice that these calamities may be lifted from the Empire and the people. I alone am responsible.”

===Chinese===
皇上不仅是那唯一配在大坛之上向无形之上帝献祭的大祭司，整个自然崇拜的神职体系，也都寄于他圣躬之一身。唯独他，于《礼记》（Book of Rites）所定的时日、以及特殊的场合，在天坛及其他诸大坛庙中献祭。当饥荒遍野、当旱魃或任何其他国之大灾降于帝国之上，皇上便于此诸坛庙之中祈祷灾祸止息；盖因他不仅为大祭司，更以「天子」之身，为上天降于其子民的灾厄承当赎罪之责，并公开自承对帝国种种不幸负责。据《礼记》所载，逢国有灾厄之时，他如此说道：「我当以献祭洁净己身，使此等灾厄从帝国与百姓身上除去。其责惟我一人承担。」

===Original===
The great semiannual celebration to the Invisible Deity is not only the most solemn of the religious rites the Emperor performs; it is at the same time the most formal of his official acts as ruler of the Great Empire. He prepares himself, by fasting and subduing the body, for the religious rite; for the official ceremony as Emperor of China, he is accompanied by all his ministers and the highest nobles of the land and surrounded by splendid pomp and Imperial pageantry.

===Chinese===
每年两次对无形之皇天上帝所行的大祭，不仅是皇上所行诸般宗教祭典中最为庄严肃穆者，同时也是他作为大帝国之君所行的最为隆重的官方典礼。为这宗教祭典，他以斋戒和克己修身来自我预备；至于作为中国皇上的官方典礼，则由全体大臣与国中最高贵的王公贵族扈从，前后左右尽是堂皇的盛况与皇家的仪仗。

===Original===
Though in his triple quality of Emperor, High Priest, and Expiator, he personally sacrifices only in the great Temples to Nature, the Emperor has all the religions of his Empire under his protection and is their nominal Head. He assists indiscriminately at Buddhist or Taoist ceremonies, and encourages with impartiality both cults. But these religions have priestly hierarchies, and complicated Rituals, and the Emperor is only the Ex-officio Head and High Priest. All the festivals and fasts of both are celebrated in the Palace.

===Chinese===
皇上虽以皇帝、大祭司、承罪者这三重身份，仅在祭自然诸大坛庙中亲自献祭，但帝国之内的所有宗教，都置于他的庇护之下，而他为名义上的首领。他无分彼此地出席佛教或道教的仪式，对两教一视同仁地予以扶持。但这两种宗教自有其神职体系与繁复的仪轨，皇上不过是因职而兼任的首领与大祭司罢了。两教一切节庆与斋期，皆在宫中举行。

===Original===
The Chinese are not a religious people, though so moral a race. They are rather followers of a philosophy than members of religious bodies. The two most popular religions of China, Buddhism and Taoism, have become more or less outward forms. They are empty shells which may once have contained the Spirit, but have now become mere conventional representations of ancient rites. The Chinese people are really Confucians, and Confucianism is a system of ethics, a philosophy rather than a religion. Whether they be Buddhists or Taoists, they are all followers of Confucius, and live by the rules the Great Sage has laid down for them. The doctrines of Buddha and Laotze have become so encrusted with error in China as to afford no moral or ideal help to their followers. The Chinese participate indiscriminately in either of these religious rites, many of which have become mere outward spectacular ceremonies, where there is a great deal of display and much form, but very little real worship. They get all their moral support from the writings of Confucius and all their ideals from communion with Nature. They are really philosophers and worshipers of Nature, and the Emperor’s semiannual sacrifice on the altar of Heaven and those at the Temples of Nature typify the real worship of the people.

===Chinese===
中国人虽是如此重道德的民族，却并非一个宗教性的民族。与其说他们是宗教团体的成员，不如说是一种哲学的信徒。中国最盛行的两大宗教——佛教与道教——多已沦为外在的形式。它们是空壳，或许曾盛装过灵性，如今却不过成了古老祭典的惯常摆设。中国人实在是儒者，而儒学是一套伦理体系，与其说是宗教，不如说是哲学。无论信佛信道，他们都是孔子的门徒，都遵这位至圣先师为他们所立下的规矩而行。佛祖与老子的学说，在中国已为谬误所重重包裹，再不能给予其信众任何道德上或理想上的助益。中国人不分彼此地参与这些宗教祭典中的任何一种，其中许多早已沦为徒有其表的盛大仪仗——场面极大，礼数极繁，唯独少有真正的虔敬。他们的道德支柱，皆得自孔子的著述；他们的理想，皆得自与自然的契合。他们实在是哲学家，是自然的崇拜者；而皇上岁中两次在天坛之上、以及在祭自然诸坛上所行的献祭，正象征着百姓真正的崇拜。

===Original===
All the religious rites in China have their origin in, or are in celebration of, some natural phenomenon or some periodical event in Nature. They celebrate the summer and winter solstices, the equinoxes, the New Year, the awakening of spring, when the sap (life-giving element) begins to mount. The Harvest Moon is the time of the going to rest of this life-giving element. Their complicated ceremonial is but the crystallization of some simple observance of Nature’s fundamental laws. This ceremonial has been kept alive all these centuries, because of the vivifying spark of Nature which enkindled them. These rites are now observed without a thought of their origin, but Nature still remains their creative force. In spite of their conventions, the Chinese have kept very near to Nature, and I believe this is the secret of their wonderful vitality. They have been overrun and conquered by many different races, and their assimilation of these conquerors is one of the most astonishing things in the ethnic study of this wonderful race.

===Chinese===
中国所有的宗教祭祀，无不源于、或为纪念某种自然现象、某种自然界中周期性的事件。他们庆贺夏至与冬至、春分与秋分、新年、春之苏醒——是时，生气（即赋予生命之物）开始上升。丰收月，则是这赋予生机之物去安息的时节。他们繁复的仪注，不过是对自然根本法则某种简单奉行方式的结晶。这套仪注之所以历数百年而不衰，全赖最初点燃它们的、那一缕自然的生机之火。如今人们奉行这些祭典时，已不再思及其由来，然而自然仍是其创造的源泉。中国人虽为种种成规所囿，却始终与自然相去不远；我相信，这正是他们那种惊人活力之奥秘所在。他们曾为许多不同的民族所侵入、所征服，而他们将这些征服者一一同化，在研究这个奇特民族的人种学上，乃是最令人惊异的现象之一。

===Original===
No conquering race has ever changed the Chinese. Tartars, Mongols, Manchus have all passed and become amalgamated with them. Their conquerors have adopted the Chinese philosophy and religion, their customs and habits, and even their system of government. And they have never been able to impose any really new system of government upon the Chinese.

===Chinese===
从无任何一个征服民族曾改变过中国人。鞑靼、蒙古、满族（Manchus），都一一经过，并与他们融为一体。那些征服者反倒采纳了中国的哲学与宗教、中国的风俗与习惯，甚至中国的政体。他们却从未能将任何真正全新的政体强加于中国人之上。

===Original===
These founders of Dynasties in China have all been “warriors bold” and reckless marauders with little philosophy and no literature to speak of. When the Manchus, the last of these conquerors, founders of the present Dynasty, established themselves in Peking, in 1646, they were a wild and warlike race. They, like all the other conquerors of China, conciliated their vanquished foes by all sorts of concessions, and they now rule by Chinese laws, and today are hardly distinguishable from the Chinese. They have never made, any more than the other conquerors, the slightest impress upon this calm and passive race; and they have become Chinese. “Today the Emperor Kwang-Hsu is recognized as one of the best Chinamen in China.” The Manchu men today wear Chinese dress. The Emperor, himself, shaves his head and wears the queue, the one visible sign of degradation that is said to have been imposed upon the Chinese at the time of the conquest. Oh! Irony of fate!

===Chinese===
这些在中国的开朝之主，无一不是「勇猛的武夫」，是肆无忌惮的劫掠者，谈不上有什么哲学，更无文学可言。当满族——这些征服者中的最后一族，亦即当今这一朝的开基之主——于 1646 年在北京立足之时，他们还是一个野蛮好战的民族。他们一如所有其他征服中国之民族，以种种让步来安抚手下败将；如今他们按中国的法律来治理，到了今日，几乎已与中国人无从分辨。他们与别的征服者一样，从未在这个沉静而柔顺的民族身上留下丝毫印记；他们已变成了中国人。「如今皇上（光绪帝）已被公认为中国最优秀的中国人之一。」如今满族男人都穿中国衣裳。皇上自己也剃发留辫——那是在当初征服之时，据说强加于中国人的唯一可见的屈辱标志。噢！命运何其嘲弄！

===Original===
The Manchus are now as quiet and peaceful a race as the Chinese themselves. They dread war. They live by the laws of Confucius. Though not a race of thinkers or philosophers, they have come to have the same ideals as the Chinese, and this, without the natural amalgamation brought about by intermarriage, for it is only within the last few years that the Throne has issued an edict, allowing intermarriage between the Chinese and Manchu, and even, with this edict, up to this time there has been very little mixture, by marriage, of the races. The Chinese seem easily led and conquered, but their national vitality is very vigorous, and has kept them pure in racial characteristics after their thousands of years of national existence.

===Chinese===
如今满族人和中国人一样，是个沉静而爱好和平的民族。他们畏惧战争，按孔子的准则度日。虽然他们并非一个思想家或哲学家的民族，却渐渐有了与中国人相同的理想；而且这种趋同并非出于通婚所带来的自然融合——因为朝廷允许满汉通婚的诏书，不过是近几年内才颁发的；即便有了这道诏书，时至今日，两族借婚姻而造成的混血仍极少。中国人看似易于引导、易于征服，但其民族元气极为旺盛，在数千年的国族存在之后，仍使其种族特征保持纯粹。

Altar to the Invisible Deity

===Chinese===

===Original===
The Festival of the Harvest Moon, which typifies the season when the life-giving element in Nature goes to rest for the Winter, I have already described. It is intermixed with legends and practices that destroy its original meaning; but the ceremony to the awakening of Spring has not departed from its original intention, and is simpler and nearer Nature. The awakening of Spring, the day when the sap is supposed to stir from its long sleep and to feel the first throes of renewed life, is commemorated in a pretty, homely ceremony at the Palace. The radish and young shoots of lettuce, the first vegetables to receive the benefit of the rising sap, are presented on a silver salver to Her Majesty by a kneeling eunuch. She partakes of them, and then gives them to the young Empress and Ladies to taste of. When Her Majesty raises the first radish to her lips, the young Empress, Princesses, and Ladies assembled in her Throne-room, repeat the wish for Imperial happiness, synonymous with “National prosperity.” This wish is echoed by the high attendants in the antechamber, and reechoed by the eunuchs kneeling in the courts without, and still echoed and reechoed by every inmate of the Palace, until the waves of sound reach to the outer walls. Then Her Majesty makes a wish that the sap may rise in such abundance as to produce a fruitful season, that all the people of the Great Empire may enjoy peace and plenty.

===Chinese===
丰收月之节（Festival of the Harvest Moon），象征着自然界中那赋予生机之物入冬安息的时令，我已于前文记述过了。它与种种传说和习俗相掺杂，已使其本意丧失；但迎春的祭典却未曾背离其初意，更为简朴，也更贴近自然。立春之日，视为生气自长眠中苏醒、感受到新生之初阵悸动的时辰，宫中以一种别致而素朴的小仪来纪念。萝卜与生菜的嫩苗，乃是最先受到上升生气之惠的菜蔬，由一名跪着的太监（eunuch）盛在银盘之上，呈给皇太后（Her Majesty）。太后尝过，再赐与皇后（the Young Empress）与诸位女官尝食。当皇太后将头一片萝卜送到唇边时，聚集在宝座殿中的皇后、福晋格格与女官们，便齐声祝颂皇家安康——这与「国泰民安」本是同义。这祝颂之声在前厅的高级侍从间回响，又在院中跪着的太监们口中再起回响，如此一应一答，由宫中每一个人层层传响，直到声浪传至外墙之下。然后皇太后许下一愿：愿此一年生气勃发，以致五谷丰登，愿大帝国的全体子民，皆得享太平与丰足。

===Original===
Thus are these first fruits of the awakening Spring partaken of with a simple ceremony of praise and thanksgiving. Thus are these homely plants consecrated with wishes made for the good of the country and the happiness of its rulers. It was to me a beautiful ceremony, so simple that it brought these people with all their conventions and all their forms very near the heart itself of Nature.

===Chinese===
春醒时的这初熟之物，便是这样以一场简朴的赞美与感恩之仪来品尝的。这些朴素的菜蔬，便是这样在为国之福祉、为君上之安乐的祝愿之中得以祝圣。在我看来，这是一场极美的典礼，简朴得竟使这些人——纵然为种种成规与礼数所裹挟——直直地贴近了自然本身的心脏。

===Original===
The annual plowing of a furrow and sowing of the first seeds of the year by the Emperor, the planting of a mulberry tree (to nourish the silkworms) by the Empress, are other touches of Nature which show how near the Chinese are to the heart of things. One of the honorary offices which is considered a great mark of Imperial favor, and which the highest ladies of the land receive with reverent gratitude, is to be appointed “Guardian of the Cocoons”; for the silk industry is one of the great sources of National prosperity in China. These ladies of high degree, guardians of the cocoons, go in annual pilgrimage to the mulberry groves, where the cocoons flourish, to make sacrifices and prayers for the health and growth of the cocoon. Being so near to Nature, the Chinese are naturally a pastoral people, a race of agriculturists; and agriculture, being thus honored by the Sacred Persons of Their Majesties, becomes a lofty ideal. Labor, which the Emperor publicly performs, loses all taint and grows into an Inspiration.

===Chinese===
皇上每年亲犁一垄、亲播一年之第一种，皇后亲植一株桑树（以饲养蚕儿），亦是另一些自然的笔触，可见中国人何其贴近万物之本。其中有一项被视为皇恩隆重之标志的荣誉差事，国中最尊贵的夫人们无不怀着感戴之心来承领——便是被指为「蚕茧之守护」（Guardian of the Cocoons）；盖因蚕桑之业乃中国国富民强的一大源泉。这些贵妇——蚕茧的守护者们——每年都要往桑林茂盛、蚕茧丰美之处作一次朝香，在那里为蚕茧的康健与生长献祭祈祷。中国人既如此贴近自然，自然便是一个田园之族，一个农耕之民；而农事既蒙二位陛下（Their Majesties）圣躬如此尊崇，便成了一种崇高的理想。劳作，因有皇上亲自行之于众，便洗尽了一切低贱之嫌，升华为一种启迪。
