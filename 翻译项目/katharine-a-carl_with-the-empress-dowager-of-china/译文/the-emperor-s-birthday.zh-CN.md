## IX The Emperor's Birthday / 第九章 皇上的万寿节

===Original===
We went to the Palace early the day of His Majesty's Birthday, and were in the Empress Dowager's Throne-room at six o'clock in the morning; but long before that time, the outer court was filled with the red and yellow chairs and carts of the visiting members of the Imperial Family, who had come in from Peking and from the neighboring Palaces for the day. The high eunuchs were in gala costume, wearing silken gowns of great beauty, embroidered in the Double Dragon. The eunuchs of lower rank were more simply gowned, as the representation of the Double Dragon on the Court gown is only allowed to those of a certain rank. Our chair-bearers were clad in the festive red, with brocaded figures, representing the characters for Longevity.

===Chinese===
皇上万寿节（Emperor's Birthday）这一日，我们一早就进了宫，早晨六点钟便已到了皇太后的宝座殿里；然而远在此时辰之前，外朝诸庭院已挤满了红黄两色的轿子和车驾——都是皇室宗亲的，他们或自北京（Peking）城中，或自附近各处宫殿赶来，专赴这一日的盛典。那品级高的太监（eunuch）们皆着礼服，身穿绣工绝美的绸袍，袍上绣的是双龙（Double Dragon）。品级低的太监所穿之袍较为素简，因朝袍之上能否绣双龙，是有品级限制的：唯具有一定品级者方准如此。我们这一抬轿的轿夫，皆穿一身喜庆的红，衣上是织锦的图案，作「寿」字花样。

===Original===
We passed through the beautifully decorated courts, past the gaily decked Palaces to the Throne-room of Her Majesty, where the Emperor had come to receive the private congratulations of the Princesses of the blood and the Ladies of the Court. It would have been against the laws of Chinese etiquette for these Ladies to go into the Emperor's Palace to congratulate him, even on such an occasion as his Birthday. When we entered the Throne-room, the Emperor was seated, or rather, reclining upon a lounge in the most informal manner. He was not averse, as was Her Majesty, to the reclining position when on the Throne. His greater Orientalism was evidenced here, for the Oriental proverb says, "'Tis better to be sitting than standing, to be lying than sitting," etc. He sat up a little straighter on our entrance, and the Ladies made the formal Chinese bow, which he returned by a friendly nod and kindly smile. I made the European reverence as usual.

===Chinese===
我们穿过张灯结彩的庭院，经过装饰得花团锦簇的宫殿，来到皇太后的宝座殿。皇上已先到此，接受宗室福晋格格与宫中命妇们的私下行礼祝贺。按中国的礼法规矩，纵是万寿节这等大日子，这些夫人也断不能径到皇上的寝宫去向他贺寿——那是有违礼数的。我们进宝座殿时，只见皇上坐着——或者不如说是斜倚在卧榻之上，神态最为随便。在宝座之上，皇太后素不取这半倚半卧的姿势，皇上却不然。他这股东方气派，于此可见一斑——东方有句谚语道：「坐胜于立，卧胜于坐」，云云。我们进来时，他略坐直了些；诸位命妇行了正式的中式之礼，皇上则点头答礼，含笑甚是和悦。我则照例行西式礼。

===Original===
His Majesty was dressed a little more elaborately than usual, in a yellow gown, tightly belted in around his slender waist with a handsome belt buckle of jade. At this morning salutation by the Ladies of his family, his hat lay beside him on the couch, which showed it was unceremonious, for ceremonies are carried on by the Emperor and all Chinese with their hats on. The great Imperial Pearl, one of the most precious of the Imperial jewels, formed the button of his hat on his Birthday. The seven official ranks of Mandarins are shown by the different colors of the buttons worn on their hats. The color of these buttons denotes the rank acquired by their wearers, those of the Manchu Princes, alone, being hereditary. The buttons of these latter are generally of jewels or semiprecious stones. The Emperor, the most simply dressed man I saw in China, wears, as a rule, a plain red silk button, but the Pearl, which can only be worn by a reigning Emperor, is used on state occasions.

===Chinese===
皇上这一日装束较平常稍为讲究，身着一袭黄袍，纤腰之间束着一条华美的玉带钩，束得紧紧的。今晨自家女眷行礼之时，他的帽子便放在身边的榻上——这便是非正式场合的明证，因凡有典礼，皇上乃至全体中国人，都是戴帽而行的。皇上万寿节的这顶帽子，帽顶所嵌的纽扣，便是那颗硕大的御用珍珠——御用珍宝中数一数二的宝物。朝廷命官分七品，品级之分，便在帽顶所嵌之「顶子」颜色上见出。这顶子的颜色，是戴者所获品级的标识；唯有满族（Manchu）王公的顶子乃是世袭。王公的顶子，多系宝石或半宝石制成。皇上是在中国我所见到的衣着最朴素的人，平日所戴不过一颗素红绸的顶子；但那颗御珠——唯在位之皇上方得佩戴——则用于大典礼之时。

===Original===
After we had greeted His Majesty, we moved further into the Throne-room to await the *lever* of the Empress Dowager. When she came out of her sleeping apartments, the Ladies fell upon their knees and simultaneously repeated the words of greeting used every morning to Her Majesty, "*Lao-Tzu-Tzung Chee-Siang*" (Great Ancestress, be happy). After acknowledging their salutations, she advanced and held out her hand to me, and I took it and, as was now my custom, raised the tips of her fingers to my lips. I, of course, never made any but a European salutation to either Her Majesty or the Emperor. She was very gracious and said I would be the first foreigner who had ever seen the birthday celebration of any of the Sons of Heaven, and she hoped I would enjoy it! She then commented on my dress and ornaments, examining the few jewels I wore. After this she turned to the Ladies and, with a quick glance, took in all the details of their Court costumes, calling their attention to the way their official beads hung and signaling any little deviation from traditional forms that she noticed in their attire. She was extremely rigid as to all the details of Court dress.

===Chinese===
我们向皇上行礼既毕，便往宝座殿更深处去，等候皇太后（Her Majesty the Empress Dowager）起身。太后从寝宫里出来时，诸位命妇一齐跪下，齐声念诵每日早晨对太后所用的那一套请安之语：「老祖宗吉祥」（*Lao-Tzu-Tzung Chee-Siang*，意即「老祖宗，请多珍重」）。太后受了她们的礼，便走上前来向我伸出手，我接过她的手，按我如今已成习惯的礼数，将她的指尖轻轻举至唇边。当然，我无论对皇上（the Emperor）或皇太后，从不曾行过别的礼，只行西式之礼。太后和颜悦色，说我是头一个得见任何一位天子（Sons of Heaven）万寿庆典的外国人，盼我看得开心！随后她又品评我的衣裙与首饰，端详了我所戴的几件珠宝。这之后，她转向诸位命妇，目光一扫，便将她们朝服上的一切细节尽收眼底：见她们的朝珠挂得有何不妥，便指点出来；衣饰上有何细处不合传统规矩，也一一指出。她对朝服的一切细节，规矩都极其森严。

===Original===
The Court costume of the Ladies is magnificent. That worn at the Emperor's Birthday (the summer costume) was of the stiff transparent silk I have described in the gown worn by Her Majesty for the portrait. The Court costume of the married ladies is of dark red, embroidered in golden dragons. The widows wear blue; the unmarried girls, bright red—all with the Double Dragon embroidered thereon. The married ladies and widows, when in Court attire, wear a magnificent court headdress with jeweled crown. The young girls, even in Court dress, wear the ordinary Manchu coiffure, with the long red silk tassels falling to their shoulders. The young Empress was charming on the Birthday. Her headdress was of golden filigree, thickly set with jewels. Across the front, nine beautifully chased golden phoenix, with jeweled tails outspread, held in their bills strings of pearls that fell to her shoulders and veiled her forehead. Square, conventionalized bunches of flowers projected from either side of this curiously and elaborately wrought headdress. Her gown was of the Imperial yellow, embroidered with the golden Double Dragon. She had, around her neck, a solid piece of chased gold, like a huge open ring, with balls at the ends; and she wore the official beads that are always worn in Court dress by Princes and Officials and their wives. The Emperor and Empress Dowager are the only members of the Court who wear, neither the Double Dragon on their Court dress, nor the official beads. Suspended from the Empress's neck was a magnificently embroidered stole, about four inches wide, which reached to the hem of her gown. This stole is only worn by the wives of Emperors, during their husband's lifetime. The young Empress seemed unusually happy today, and this was the first time I had ever seen her and the Emperor in conversation. Next to the young Empress came the only secondary wife of the Emperor. She was dressed exactly as the young Empress was; the same gown, the same headdress, the same embroidered stole, only her jewels were not so handsome, and her dress, instead of being of the Imperial yellow, was of orange. Yellow can only be worn by the first wife of an Emperor!

===Chinese===
命妇们的朝服甚是华美。万寿节所着的（夏朝服）正是那种硬挺的薄如蝉翼的丝绸——我前文形容过皇太后画像时所穿的那一件便是。已嫁妇人的朝服用暗红色，上绣金线龙。寡居的夫人穿蓝色；未出阁的姑娘则穿鲜红色——袍上无不绣有双龙。已嫁夫人和寡居之人在朝服之上，皆戴一顶华美的朝冠，冠上饰满珠宝。年轻姑娘即便着朝服，也只梳寻常的满族发髻，长长的红丝流苏垂至肩头。万寿节这一日，皇后（the Young Empress）风姿动人。她的朝冠为金丝累丝所制，密密嵌着珠宝。冠前横列九只雕工绝美的金凤，凤尾作扇形展开，尾上镶珠宝；金凤口衔珠串，垂至她的肩头，覆在她的额前。这顶工艺奇巧繁复的朝冠两侧，各伸出一方块扇形的花簇。她的袍子是御用明黄色，绣金线双龙。她颈间戴着一件雕花纯金的饰物，形如一只硕大的开口指环，两端各有一颗圆球；她身上还挂着朝珠——王公大臣及其命妇着朝服时无不挂此。皇上与皇太后是宫中唯独二位朝服之上既不绣双龙、也不挂朝珠的人。皇后颈间还悬着一条绣工绝美的披肩，约四英寸宽，一直垂到袍子的下摆。这一条披肩，唯有皇上之妻于夫君在世之时方得披用。皇后今日似格外欢悦，这也是我头一次见她与皇上说话。皇后之下，便是皇上唯一的那位侧室（secondary wife）。她的衣饰与皇后分毫不差：同一式袍子，同一式朝冠，同一条绣花披肩；只是珠宝稍逊一筹；衣裳的颜色不是御用明黄，而是橘黄。原来明黄色，唯皇上正妻方得穿！

===Original===
After the salutations to the Emperor and Empress Dowager in Her Majesty's private Throne-room, Her Majesty went out into the court and took her place in her yellow chair of State, the Emperor following, on foot, as was his custom. The cymbals clashed. The flutes sounded and all the instruments of the Imperial Band played the curious minor air, with its tragic undertone of sound, its rhythm like a Gregorian chant, which is only played at the passing of Their Majesties for some great ceremony or official function, and which I soon called the "Imperial Hymn." This is the only approach to a National air that I ever heard in China.

===Chinese===
在皇太后私用的宝座殿里向皇上、皇太后行礼既毕，太后便出到院中，登上她那乘明黄色的仪轿；皇上照例步行随后。铜钹铿锵。长笛悠扬，宫廷乐队的全部乐器齐奏那一支奇特的小调曲子——曲调里有一种悲怆的低音，节奏如格雷果圣咏（Gregorian chant）一般。此乐唯在二位陛下为某大典或官式仪仗出行之时方才奏起，不久我便称它作「国歌」（Imperial Hymn）。这是我在中国所听到的、唯一近似国歌的曲子。

===Original===
Their Majesties went in ceremonious procession to the Great Audience Hall, where the Princes, Nobles, and high Officials privileged to enter Precincts, were to present their homage and congratulations to the Son of Heaven on the happy occasion of his Birthday. Besides these privileged visitors, there were a number of officials whose rank was not high enough to allow them to enter the Great Hall of Ceremonies. These kneel and make the prostrations in the outer courts.

===Chinese===
二位陛下排齐全副仪仗，前往大朝殿（Great Audience Hall）。此殿之中，王公贵胄与够品级入苑的高官，要在皇上这一吉庆的万寿之日，向这位天子（Son of Heaven）恭行朝贺之礼。除了这些有幸入苑的宾客之外，还有许多品级不够、不准入这大朝殿行礼的官员。这些人只在外朝诸庭院里跪下行叩拜礼。

===Original===
The young Empress and Ladies of the Court did not follow Their Majesties to the Great Hall, but stopped at the Palace of the young Empress, to await there their turn for the official congratulations, which were not to be made until after those of the Princes and Nobles. The young Empress is a charming hostess, and her eunuchs and women handed us tea and cigarettes while we were waiting. She also had her dogs brought in for me to see. Her apartments opened on a sunny court, full of flowering shrubs and fruit trees. Around the other three sides of the court were built the pavilions for the use of her attendants and ladies. We spent half an hour in her pavilion, waiting for the congratulations of the Princes and Nobles to be finished.

===Chinese===
皇后与宫中命妇们并未随二位陛下前往大朝殿，而在皇后的宫里停下，候在那里轮到自己行正式的贺礼——她们的贺礼须排在诸王公贵胄之后。皇后是一位可亲的女主人，我们等候之时，她的太监和女侍为我们奉上茶和香烟。她又命人把她的狗抱来给我看。她的寝宫开向一处向阳的庭院，院里满栽着花木果树。庭院其余三面，建着她随侍女官女侍们所用的阁子。我们在她的阁子里坐了半个钟头，等候王公贵胄们的贺礼行完。

===Original===
The Emperor, for these official congratulations, was seated upon the Dynastic Throne, erect and stiff as an archaic figure; no longer the shy boy, but the Monarch clothed in all his power, and, for today, alone upon his great ancestral Throne. He was attended by his Master of Ceremonies, gorgeously attired, who stood in the rigid attitude prescribed for this ceremony.

===Chinese===
皇上受此正式朝贺之时，端坐于本朝历代相传的御座之上，腰背挺直，凝然不动，宛若一尊古旧的造像。他不再是那个羞怯的少年，而是手握全权的君主，唯此一日，独自端坐于他那伟大的祖传宝座之上。在他身侧侍立的，是他的鸿胪官（Master of Ceremonies），装束极尽华美，按此礼所定的姿势，僵直而立。

===Original===
Each splendidly garbed Prince and Noble knelt and made the prostration prescribed by the Book of Rites, and each presented His Majesty with a jade emblem, called by the Chinese *ruyie*,3 erroneously supposed to be a scepter by most foreigners; but the *ruyie* is simply an emblem of Good Luck, and may be presented on festive occasions to anyone whom the givers wish to honor, and is not an emblem of Imperial authority. The Emperor held each of these *ruyie* in his hands for a few seconds after their presentation, bowed profoundly to the kneeling Prince, and then handed the emblem to an attendant eunuch, who placed it on a Dragon table at the left of the Emperor. When the Princes and Nobles had congratulated His Majesty and left the Throne-room, the young Empress and secondary wife, followed by the Princesses and Ladies, went in to make their official congratulations. The greeting in Her Majesty's Throne-room in the morning had been but a friendly salutation, without any official signification. The young Empress knelt and made her bow first and presented—as did each of the Ladies—a *ruyie*. She made the same official salutation as did the others, but her *ruyie* was of a much richer style than those presented by the other Ladies.

===Chinese===
每一位衣饰华美的王公贵胄，依次跪下，行《礼记》（Book of Rites）所规定的叩拜之礼，并各向皇上呈献一件玉质的吉祥之物，中国称之为「如意」（*ruyie*）——多数外国人误以为这是一种权杖；其实如意不过是一件吉祥之物，凡喜庆之日，献者欲向何人致敬，便可献与何人，并非皇权的象征。皇上接过每一柄如意，于手中把玩数秒，向那位跪着的王公深深一揖，再将此物交与身侧侍立的太监，太监便将它放在皇上左侧的龙几上。诸王公贵胄既向皇上贺毕，退出了宝座殿，皇后与那位侧室，随后由诸王妃格格与命妇们簇拥着，进去行正式的朝贺之礼。清晨在皇太后宝座殿里那一番行礼，不过是私下致意，并无官式礼数的意义。皇后先跪下行礼，并献上一柄如意——一如诸位命妇所献。她所行的官礼与众人相同，只是她的如意，较之其他命妇所献的，更为华贵。

===Original===
After the ceremony of formal congratulations was over, Her Majesty, the Emperor, and Empress, followed by the Ladies and attendants, went in state to the Theater, with the same ceremonial and pomp with which they had gone into the Hall of Ceremonies. The Empress Dowager, who was always the most gorgeously attired person at Court, was, on His Majesty's Birthday, dressed with an extreme simplicity that amounted almost to plainness, and she wore no jewels. This plainness of attire was not an accident, but had been arranged with her usual forethought. She wished the Emperor and Empress to be the central figures of this day's festivities, and did not wish to vie with the Empress even in her attire.

===Chinese===
正式朝贺之礼既毕，皇太后、皇上、皇后，由命妇和随从们簇拥着，排齐全副仪仗，前往戏楼——所行仪注排场，与方才进大朝殿之时一般无二。皇太后素来是宫中衣饰最华丽的人，然皇上万寿节这一日，她却穿得极其素淡，几近于简朴，身上也不戴一件珠宝。这素朴的装束并非出于偶然，而是她照例先期安排好的。她要叫皇上和皇后做这一日庆贺的中心，连衣着上也不愿与皇后争辉。

===Original===
The Princes and Nobles, who had come to the Palace for the official congratulations, were invited to the theatrical performance. They occupied the boxes that ran at right angles to the Imperial loge, which I have already described as forming the other two sides of the court of the Theater. A huge screen of painted silk, twelve feet high, was stretched from the last of the boxes occupied by the Princes to the stage—allowing the latter to be perfectly seen by the occupants of the boxes, but cutting off their view of the Imperial loge, whence Their Majesties, the Empress, and Ladies viewed the play. These invited guests are thus neither seen by the Imperial party, nor can they see the latter.

===Chinese===
进宫行正式朝贺的王公贵胄们，也蒙恩被请来看戏。他们坐在与御用看阁成直角的那几间隔厢里——前文我已说过，这些厢座构成戏楼庭院其余的两面。从王公们所坐的最后一间隔厢起，直至戏台，张起了一道十二英尺高的彩绘大绢屏——这屏风使隔厢里的人看戏台一览无遗，却将他们望向御用看阁的视线尽数遮断。御用的看阁，正是二位陛下、皇后与命妇们观戏之处。如此，这些被邀的客人既不能为御前的人所见，也看不见御前的人。

===Original===
When Their Majesties and the Empress were seated in their loge, the principal actors came to the front of the stage, knelt, and kowtowed to the Imperial box. Then the play began. There was first a noisy burst of weird music, then the chief actor recited a laudatory, congratulatory poem in honor of the Birthday of the Emperor, wishing His Majesty "ten thousand years" of happiness and all the blessings possible. The poem was intoned like a chant by the actor, dressed in the gorgeous historic costume of an Imperial Herald of the time of Kublai Khan. This poem was most impressive. One of the verses ran thus:

> "The vast merits of His Imperial Majesty's August Ancestors have been handed down to Him from generation to generation.

"To the wisdom of His whole Dynasty we owe it, that we have lived in happiness,

"Ever ready to comply with the lofty teaching of our Rulers, leading us unto Good.… "

The poem went on to recite His Majesty's merits as a son, his respect for his August Mother, his filial piety, and ended with a wish that Great China might flourish and prosper—grow strong outwardly and inwardly, through the blessings of his reign and his desire for Progress.

===Chinese===
二位陛下与皇后在看阁里坐定，主要伶人们便走到台前跪下，向御座叩头。然后戏便开场。先是一阵喧天古怪的乐声，紧接着那正生朗诵一首颂扬皇上万寿的贺诗，祝皇上享「万岁」之福，凡百吉祥，无所不备。这诗由那伶人如吟如诵地唱出，他身着忽必烈汗（Kublai Khan）时代御前宣旨官那般华美的古装。这一首诗气派甚是庄严。其中一节道：

> 「皇上巍巍先祖之大德，世世代代，传之于陛下。

> 「吾等得以安乐而居，皆赖本朝列圣之明，

> 「吾等恭循吾主之崇高教化，引吾侪以归于善……」

诗中又诵念皇上为人之子的美德、他对圣母的敬重、他的孝道，末了祝愿大清国兴隆昌盛——内修外攘，日益强盛，皆赖皇上当朝之福与其求新之志。

===Original===
After this poem had been intoned by the chief actor, with the whole company of players grouped around on the lower, as well as on the two superposed stages, all in splendid historic costumes, there was another noisy clash of weird music and the play itself began. The Chinese theater, which goes on from morning to night with a series of plays, generally begins with a short one, a curtain-raiser of a quarter to half an hour's length. Today it began at once, after the poem was intoned, with a great historic drama. The exploits and high deeds of former Emperors were shown, and the actors were magnificently costumed in superb historic gowns which had been handed down from antiquity and were absolutely authentic.

===Chinese===
