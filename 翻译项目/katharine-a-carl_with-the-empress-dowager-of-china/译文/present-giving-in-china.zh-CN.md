## XXVII Present-Giving in China / 二十七 中国的馈赠之风

===Original===
Present-giving is really carried to great excess all over China, and whatever obtains in China obtains at the Palace. The Palace is spoken of in Peking as the “Inside,” that is, the heart of the Empire. From this “Inside,” customs and habits flow and pulse over the rest of China, as the blood does from the heart, by a thousand arteries reaching to the very confines of the Empire, and it also receives the impress of what passes on outside among the people. Whether it be, in the instance of present-giving, that the custom has grown from the “Inside” to the “Outside” or vice versa, I know not, but it is universal in China. However, it probably reaches its greatest excess in the Palace.

===Chinese===
馈赠之风，在中国确是盛行到近乎泛滥的地步；凡是中国所通行的，宫中亦无不通行。在北京（Peking），人们把宫廷称为「里头」，意即帝国的核心。种种礼俗习尚，便由这「里头」流出，如血液之自心脏流出一般，沿着千百条脉络，一路流注、搏动，直达帝国的极边；而外间民间所行之事，亦同样在这「里头」留下印记。至于馈赠一节，究竟是此风由「里头」蔓延至「外头」，还是由「外头」传入「里头」，我不得而知；但在中国，它确是无处不在。不过，此风最盛之处，恐怕仍要数宫廷。

===Original===
Births, marriages, and deaths are all marked by presents, and there is a very riot of present-giving at the New Year! Everyone then exchanges them, from the lowest to the highest. Next comes the anniversary of the birth. This is celebrated with an unheard-of pomp in China. The more exalted the rank and the greater the age, the more splendid is the celebration and the more magnificent the presents. The Emperor’s Birthday was the first I saw celebrated, and I was astonished at the number and elegance of the presents that flowed into the Palace on this occasion. But at the Empress Dowager’s Birthday all this was far surpassed; and her presents exceeded in number and elegance His Majesty’s, for she was celebrating more years than the Emperor, and the number and value of birthday presents increase in proportion to the years. Their elegance and number are also regulated by the rank. The presents the Grand Secretary and the Prime Minister receive on such occasions would quite astonish a Westerner, and, of course, far surpass in number and magnificence what would be offered to the president of a department, as his would exceed, in elegance, those offered to secretaries of the Board.

===Chinese===
降生、婚嫁、丧葬，无不以礼物为记；而过年之时，馈赠之风更是闹腾得沸反盈天！届时人人相互馈赠，自最微贱者直至最尊贵者，无一例外。其次便是生辰之庆。在中国，生辰的庆贺排场之大，简直前所未闻。地位越高、年岁越长，庆典便越铺张，所受的礼物也越豪奢。皇上（the Emperor）的万寿节，是我头一回见到的庆典；那日宫廷中所收进的礼物，其数量之多、品物之雅，已令我惊叹不已。然而待到皇太后（Her Majesty the Empress Dowager）的万寿节，这一切又远过之；太后所受的礼物，无论数量还是雅致，都超过了皇上——一则太后所贺的年岁多于皇上，而寿礼之数与值，本就随年岁而增；再则礼物之精与多，亦因品级而有别。此等场合，大学士与首相所收的礼物，足以叫一个西洋人惊骇；自然，其数量与豪奢，又远非各部尚书所得者可比——正如尚书所得者，其精美亦胜于各部侍郎所得一般。

===Original===
Every festival, every ceremony, and all anniversaries are marked by presents, in the Palace. There is scarcely a day that presents are not sent into the Palace, that some are not sent out, and rarely a day when some presents are not exchanged by those “Inside.” The Empress Dowager and the Emperor receive the greatest number, and, of course, they give the greatest number. This seemed to me the greatest extravagance of the Empress Dowager. At every change of season, she presents the young Empress, the Princesses, and Ladies, without reference to her favorites, with silks, dresses, shoes, and ornaments appropriate to the season, and not only do the Ladies receive these articles of wearing apparel at the changes of the season: she gives them many presents at each festival. Besides this, she gives nearly all the expensive Court dresses that are worn at the Palace, which cost, with their embroideries, from three to six hundred dollars each. She presents the Ladies with coiffures and many jeweled ornaments as well. On the occasion of a wedding among the Manchu nobility, which must be announced to the Court before it takes place, Her Majesty presents the bride handsome rolls of silk, embroideries, and jewels. On the occasion of births among the courtiers, she sends handsome ornaments to be worn by the newborn child. Even on the death of certain people, she sends handsome presents to the family, or something to be worn by the departed, if it should be a widow, who had led a long and exemplary life of widowhood, and had devoted herself to charity and good works.

===Chinese===
宫廷之中，每逢节令、典礼、周年忌辰，无不以礼物为记。几乎日日都有礼物送进宫来，也日日有礼物从宫中送出，至于「里头」的人彼此之间交换礼物，更是极少有一日间断。皇太后与皇上所得最多，自然，所赠出的也最多。依我看来，这便是皇太后最为挥霍之处。每逢换季，她便按节令赏赐皇后（the Young Empress）、公主与女官们绸缎、衣裳、鞋履与首饰，并不独厚于自己所钟爱的人；而这些女眷于换季之时所得的，也远不止四季衣饰：每逢节令，她都另有厚赏。此外，宫廷里穿的那些华贵的礼服，也几乎全由她一手赏下——这些礼服，连同其上绣工，每件所值，自三百元至六百元不等。她也赏赐女官们首饰与种种珠宝头面。满族（Manchu）贵族嫁女，须于成婚前先报知宫廷，届时皇太后必赏新娘上好的整匹绸缎、绣品与珠宝。宫中人有得子之喜，她便遣人送去新生儿可佩戴的精美饰物。便是在某些人故世之时，她也要送一份厚礼给其家中——若是那亡者乃一位孀居多年、守节尽礼、一生行善积德的寡妇，她还要赏些可由逝者带去的饰物。

===Original===
When the ladies of the Legation were first received at the Palace, the Empress Dowager naturally followed the Chinese Imperial custom of giving each lady a present. This precedent having been established and seeming to have given pleasure, when the ladies were received the next time, which was after the Boxer rebellion, she gave them presents again. Unfortunately, this act was construed into a desire on her part to wheedle the foreigners, and curry favor, so that she might receive better treatment at the hands of the Powers. The truth is, she loves to play the Lady Bountiful, and she never mixes up the social with the political, and I am sure she had no arrière-pensée but was simply indulging her usual bent. After the first few Audiences (when the presents were really of value), Her Majesty gave small and unimportant presents at the garden parties, which were made the subject of ridicule. Her Majesty had heard that the ladies did not wish to receive such handsome presents as she had first given, and she hence gave inexpensive souvenirs. Finally, the Ministers asked the Chinese Foreign Office to request the Empress Dowager to give no more presents at the Audiences, and the custom was abolished; though Her Majesty continued to give presents in private, and she still sends, on the four great Chinese festivals, flowers, fruits, and confectionery to all the ladies of the Legation, as well as to every lady who has ever been received at the garden parties, and on the departure of any Minister from Peking, she sends his wife some parting presents.

===Chinese===
公使馆的夫人（ladies of the Legation）们初次蒙召入宫觐见（Audience）之时，皇太后自是依中国朝廷的规矩，赏每位夫人一份礼物。此例一开，似乎又颇令诸位夫人欢欣；待到她们第二次蒙召——那是在拳匪之乱以后——太后便又各赏了一份。不幸的是，此举竟被外界曲解为太后有意巴结洋人、讨好列强，以便在列国手中得到更好的对待。其实呢，太后素来爱做那慷慨施与的女主人（Lady Bountiful），从不把社交之事与政治相混；我敢断言，她绝无别样心思（arrière-pensée），不过是顺着自己一向的脾性罢了。头几次觐见（那时所赠的礼物确是贵重之物）之后，皇太后在园会上所赐的，便只是些微不足道的小物，因而惹来了讥笑。原来太后听说，诸位夫人并不愿收受她初次所赠那样贵重的礼物，故而改赠些不值几文的小纪念品。末了，各国公使竟请中国外务部（Wai-Wu-Pu）转请皇太后，勿在觐见时再行赏赐，此例遂废；然而太后私下照旧馈赠不绝，仍于中国四大节令之时，遣人把鲜花、果品、糖果送与公使馆全体夫人，以及每一位曾蒙召赴园会的女眷；每逢哪位公使离任出京，她也要送其夫人一份程仪。

===Original===
But though present-giving has been stopped at the Audiences of the foreign ladies, it goes on with the same excess in the Palace and among officials in China. At each of Their Majesties’ Birthdays, in spite of their protests and edicts to prevent it, presents pour into the Palace! Every official who has ever been presented in Audience, or who has the right, by his official position, to send anything, does so. Edicts from the Throne to prevent it will remain as ineffectual as those with reference to the binding of the feet of the Chinese women (which Her Majesty has for years been “recommending” in edicts to be abolished), for it has become so thoroughly a part of Chinese life as to be almost indispensable. Present-giving in China is one of those “unwritten laws” whose tyranny is hardest to break away from. Though the system of present-giving is a great tax on the officials, as well as their subordinates, in this instance the change must come from the people.

===Chinese===
然而，外国夫人们觐见时的赏赐虽已停止，宫廷之内、中国官场之中，馈赠之风却照旧泛滥如故。每逢二位陛下（Their Majesties）的万寿节，任凭太后皇上再三申诫、屡降谕旨禁止，礼物仍源源涌进宫来！但凡曾经觐见过、或按其官位有资格进献之物者，无不照进不误。朝廷所下的禁令谕旨，其无效亦正与禁缠足之谕旨无异——缠足一事，太后多年来一直在谕旨中「劝谕」废除，然而终归徒然——盖因此风已深入中国人生活之中，几乎到了不可或缺的地步。中国的馈赠之风，正属那种「不成文法」之一种，其专横最难摆脱。虽说馈赠之习于官员及其属下都是一大重负，但这一风气之改易，仍须出自民间。

===Original===
As I was an inmate of the Palace for so long, of course I came in for my share of presents from the Empress Dowager. At every festival I was remembered, as well as the Princesses and Ladies of the Court, and when presents were sent to the ladies of the Legation, she sent similar ones to me. Many of the presents she made me showed a real consideration for my comfort and displayed much forethought. When the weather became cool, and the Ladies of the Court put on wadded dresses, Her Majesty sent one of her maids to my apartments to get one of my tailor-made dresses. She had the Palace tailors copy this in wadded silk. It was wonderful how well they did it, too, for, as I knew nothing about it, I could give no advice. She ordered a few changes made in the severity of the tailor-costume, thinking it was too hard in its lines. She had a long, soft sash to tie at the side, which, she decided, made it look more graceful. When the Princesses put on furs, Her Majesty, herself, designed for me a long fur-lined garment which she thought would be comfortable to paint in. She had some trouble in arriving at a result which pleased her, which would be warm enough, and which, at the same time, would not interfere with the freedom of movement necessary for me to work with ease. At the time of the Chinese New Year, she sent me two curiously fashioned fur-lined dresses. She had the skirts copied from old pictures. They were not unlike our pleated skirts, with an embroidered panel down the center of the front. The jackets were a sort of compromise between European and Chinese, and the costumes were not only pretty but very comfortable.7

===Chinese===
我既在宫中长住，自然也分得一份皇太后所赐的礼物。每逢节令，我都不曾被忘却，与宫廷的公主、女官们一体受赏；逢到公使馆的夫人们得了赏赐，她也照样送我一份。太后赏我的许多物件，都可见出她对我起居冷暖的真切体贴，煞费了一番苦心。天时转凉、宫廷女官们都换上夹衣之时，皇太后便遣一名宫女到我房中，取去我一件量身定做的洋式衣裙。她命宫中裁缝照式样用絮绸仿制了一件。他们仿得竟十分妥帖，实在可惊——因为我对此道一窍不通，竟无从指授半句。太后以为那洋装线条过于硬朗，便吩咐在上面略作改动：添一条长长的软腰带，系于一侧，她以为如此方显得婀娜。公主们穿上皮衣之时，皇太后更亲自为我设计了一件长身的皮袄，以为穿着作画必甚相宜。她费了好一番心思，才得一个令她满意的结果：既须暖和，又不能掣肘，免得妨碍我作画时所需的挥洒自如。到了中国人过年之时，她又赏我两件式样奇特的皮袄。那裙子是照着旧画仿制的，与我们西洋的褶裙颇为相仿，正中一道绣花裙门。上衣则介于欧式与中式之间，可谓中西参半；这套衣裙不仅好看，而且穿着甚是舒适。7

===Original===
For wearing with these she ordered a sable hat, for the Chinese ladies wear some sort of coiffure on the head, winter and summer. This had an embroidered crown of pale lavender satin, with long satin streamers embroidered in gold with good-luck emblems. The brim could be worn either turned off the face or pulled over the ears and tied under the chin with lavender strings. She said she had some trouble in finding a design which she thought would suit me. This hat she had also had copied from old prints. I learned later she had tried three sorts of sables before she got a color which she thought would be becoming to my unfortunate blonde hair! On the front of the brim she placed a Princess Button. This is worn only by Ladies of the Court, and represents the Flaming Pearl of the Dynasty. It was established by the founders of the Dynasty and is the distinguishing jewel of the members of the Imperial family. It consists of a large pearl, surrounded by three alternating rows of seed-pearls and corals, which are supposed to represent flames! This Flaming Pearl, symbol of the “Unattainable,” is the eternal quest of the double dragon!

===Chinese===
为配这身衣裳，她又命人做了一顶紫貂皮帽——盖因中国女子无论冬夏，头上总要戴些什么的。帽顶是淡紫色的缎子，上面绣着花；两条长长的缎带自帽后垂下，上面用金线绣着种种吉祥图样。帽檐可翻起露面，也可拉下来遮住双耳，用淡紫色的带子在颌下系住。太后说，为寻一个她以为合我面目的式样，倒费了她一些功夫。这帽子也是从旧画谱里仿制来的。后来我才听说，为着我那一头不幸的金发，太后前后试过三种貂皮，才觅得一种她以为衬我的颜色！她在帽檐正中安了一颗「公主钮」（Princess Button）。此物唯宫廷女官方得佩戴，所表示的，乃是大清国的「火珠」（Flaming Pearl）。这是开国之初所定的规矩，是皇族宗亲所独有的辨识之宝。其制：以一颗大珠居中，外环三圈，米珠与珊瑚相间排列，据说是象征火焰！这火珠，乃「不可得之物」的象征，是双龙永恒的追逐！

===Original===
Her Majesty also presented me with a number of other charming things that I shall always treasure as coming from her, and as evidence of her consideration for “the stranger within her gates,” or as spontaneous offerings from her naturally generous nature—ever desirous of giving pleasure. I wish I might have preserved the flowers and curious grasses which she, herself, gathered and gave me on our many promenades around the beautiful grounds of the Summer Palace, but which, alas! are withered and gone!

===Chinese===
此外，皇太后还赏了我许多别的可爱之物，我皆将永远珍存——一来因为这是太后所赐，二来也因这是她善待那「寄身门下的异乡客」的一份心意，又或是她那生性慷慨、唯愿与人同乐的性情，自然而然生出的馈赠。只可惜那些她在颐和园（Summer Palace）秀美的园苑之中、与我们一次次散步之时，亲手采来赠我的花草，我竟不能一并留存——哀哉！它们都已枯萎消逝了！
