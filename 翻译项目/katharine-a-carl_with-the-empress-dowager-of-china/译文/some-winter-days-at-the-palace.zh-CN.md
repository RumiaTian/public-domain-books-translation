## XXVIII Some Winter Days at the Palace / 二十八 宫中的几个冬日

===Original===
The big, official portrait for St. Louis was advancing. I was able to accomplish much more now that I had a place where I could work uninterruptedly, and quietly study the painting when I was not working. Her Majesty came, with her usual retinue, to pose, but it was not at fixed times, and was often when I did not expect her. She was looking more and more anxious these days; but she came to pose whenever it was necessary, and was very particular as to all the details in the portrait. She often had the jewels and ornaments changed, and her pearl mantle was made over, after she saw it in the first sketch, as she did not like its form.

The throne, about which there had been a question when I began the portrait, and which had been a present to Her Majesty from the late Emperor Tong Chih, her son, had been “lost” during the Boxer troubles, but Her Majesty thought it might be reproduced from descriptions and from sketches by the Palace painters who had seen it; but I could not consent to work either from memory or other painters’ sketches, and I was finally obliged to paint, faute de mieux, one of the carved teakwood thrones of which Her Majesty is so fond. This throne did not suit the straight lines of the composition so well as almost any other in the Palace would have done, but Her Majesty wished it.

I found the representation of the nine life-size phoenix, in vigorous colors, on a blue cloisonné screen placed almost touching the throne, very difficult to represent, so that they did not seem to be real birds flying around her head. The vases of flowers and ornaments were also placed at exactly equal distances on either side of the throne, but it was necessary to paint them this way. It would not have been “proper” otherwise. The figure was in the exact center of the threefold screen, and so near it, it was impossible to get any atmosphere in the background. There was not a fold in either gown or sleeves; but I had now resigned myself to convention and tradition, and I copied mechanically what was placed before me, and made no more efforts at artistic arrangements, nor tried any experiments in execution. I worked like a good artisan, finishing so many inches a day.

===Chinese===
那幅送往圣路易斯博览会（St. Louis Exposition）的大幅御容正像，渐次推进。如今我总算有了一处可以不受打扰地作画的所在，不挥毫之时亦可静静揣摩那画，于是所成便多了许多。皇太后照例带着她的随从前来坐班，但时辰并不固定，又往往在我意料之外。这几日她神色愈见焦灼；但凡有必要，她仍来坐班，对御容上的一切细节亦十分讲究。她常常把珠宝首饰换来换去；那件珍珠氅衣，自她在初稿中瞧见之后，因不喜其式样，也命人重新做过。

宝座一事，自起稿之时便有争议。此座原是先帝同治（Tong Chih）——太后之子——所赐，庚子拳乱那一年「遗失」了。太后原以为，可凭口述与曾见过此座的宫中画师所绘的草图，再仿制一座出来；然我断不能凭记忆或他人草稿作画，到末了，退而求其次（faute de mieux），也只好绘了一座雕花柚木宝座——正是皇太后素来钟爱的那一种。这座宝座于画面那笔直的线条而言，并不如宫中几乎任何一座宝座那般相宜，但皇太后属意于它。

那座蓝色景泰蓝屏风几乎贴近宝座，上绘九只与真鸟等大的凤凰，色彩浓烈鲜明，要把它们画下来、又不至于像真鸟绕着她的头颅飞翔，却颇费我一番工夫。宝座两侧那些花瓶与陈设，也都摆放得丝毫不差地等距，但又不得不照此画来。若不如此，便「不合规矩」了。人像位于三扇屏风正中，与之相距甚近，背景里全然透不出一丝空濛的气韵。袍服与衣袖竟无一褶皱；然我如今既已向成规与旧传统低头，便也机械地照着眼前所陈一一描摹下来，再不作任何艺术布局上的努力，笔下亦不另作新样的尝试。我像一个本分的匠人那样作画，每日完工若干英寸便罢。

===Original===
The weather was now too cold for anything but the short constitutional, and, besides, there was no place in the Winter Palace to tempt one to promenades—only the walled-in courts and the shut-in walks, between high walls. Even Her Majesty’s promenades were confined to going to the Audience Hall in the morning, and walking through the courts, from one Throne-room to another.

Every day we saw the Empress Dowager for some moments in her Throne-room before I went to my work. On Theater days, I made her my morning salutation in her loge at the Theater, and when the light faded and I could paint no more, I would go into the young Empress’s and the Ladies’ loge for the last play and the spectacular finale, when there were always some good illuminations and pretty effects. Her Majesty and the young Empress seemed now to perfectly understand that I wanted to work, and must work, in order to finish the large portrait for the St. Louis Exposition. They saw I appreciated the amusements and ceremonies, etc, but that I did not wish them to interfere with my work. When there was a special festival, or some fine ceremony, I was always called in, but otherwise I might go or not, as I wished.

===Chinese===
此时天气已冷得厉害，除了短短地散一回步以舒筋骨，再不能做什么；况且紫禁城（Winter Palace）之内，亦无可诱人游赏之处——不过是被高墙围起来的几重院落，以及夹在几道高墙之间的逼仄游廊。便是皇太后散步，也只限于清晨往觐见殿（Audience Hall）去这一程，再就是由一座宝座殿（Throne-room）穿过庭院，走到另一座宝座殿而已。

每日我去作画之前，先到太后的宝座殿中见她片刻。遇着唱戏之日，我便到她在戏台（Theater）上的看阁（loge）里请晨安；待到日色渐暗、不能再画之时，我便到皇后（the Young Empress）与女官（Ladies）们的看阁里去，看最后一出戏与那排场浩大的煞尾——届时总有些好看的灯彩与精巧的景致。皇太后与皇后如今似已完全明白，我想要作画，也必须作画，方能将圣路易斯博览会的那幅大御容赶制完工。她们看出我虽欣赏这些游乐与典礼等等，却不愿它们扰了我的活计。每逢特别的节庆或盛大的典礼，总会来叫我；除此之外，去不去皆由我自便。

===Original===
I lunched generally with the Ladies, with the charming young Empress as gracious hostess, and dined at night at Her Majesty’s table. Two huge copper braziers had now been placed in the Throne-room, and though so picturesque with the blue flames curling above their openings in the top, they made but little impression upon the temperature of this lofty room. The curtains over the immense doors that opened on the courts were constantly being raised for the passage of some eunuch, and it was very drafty. But one could at least warm one’s hands by the braziers, and they were so beautiful and picturesque, I was reconciled to being a little cold; besides, I soon became accustomed to the temperature. The Chinese Ladies wear heavy fur-lined dresses in the house, and cannot stand the rooms very warm.

At dinner, a large carpet was now placed under the table, which was an improvement over the cold marble floors. This was done for my comfort, for the Chinese Ladies wear two-inch-thick cork soles to their fur-lined shoes. Down the center of the table, during the winter, there were several silver chafing-dishes, with burning charcoal beneath their steaming contents. Soups, vegetables, and meat stews were thus kept boiling hot on the table. One night I suggested to one of the eunuchs to place the claret-bottle near the fire before serving it, that the chill might be taken off. One very cold day, soon after, the eunuch brought in a large teapot, and began pouring the boiling claret out of this! The Chinese drink their wines hot, and he thought he would improve on my suggestion of “taking off the chill,” and he naively remarked “it was better for me to drink it thus on such a cold day!”

===Chinese===
午饭我多与女官们同用，由那位落落大方、妩媚动人的皇后做殷勤的主人；晚饭则在皇太后桌上用。宝座殿里如今添了两只大铜火盆，盆口的火苗幽蓝，卷曲而上，煞是好看，然对于这间高旷殿宇的温度，却几乎无济于事。那些开向庭院的硕大门扇上所挂的门帘，因有太监不停出入，须臾便要掀起一回，穿堂风甚是凛冽。好在火盆旁至少还能烘一烘手；它们又是那样华美、那样入画，我便也甘愿稍稍受些冷；况且不久我也就习惯了这温度。中国女眷在屋中皆着厚重的皮袍，受不得太暖的房间。

用晚饭时，桌下如今也铺上了一大幅地毯，比之冰凉的大理石地面要舒服许多。这是为我的舒适而设的——因中国女眷所穿的毛皮鞋，底下是两英寸厚的软木底。冬季里，餐桌正中要摆上几只银暖锅，底下烧着炭，锅里腾着热气。汤、菜蔬与炖肉，便这样在桌上保得滚热。一日傍晚，我向一名太监提议，可将红酒（claret）瓶先放在火旁温一温再上，以去其寒气。未几，遇着一个极冷的日子，那太监竟捧来一只大茶壶，将滚沸的红酒从壶里往杯中斟！中国人本就喝热酒，他自以为在我那「去去寒气」的话上又加了一层心思；还天真地说：「这样冷的天，我喝这滚热的，才相宜呢！」

===Original===
When there was no Theater, and it became too dark to paint, I would join the young Empress and Ladies in their sitting-room at the left of Her Majesty’s Throne-room and there await dinner. The young Empress would then teach me Chinese. She was very particular about my accent and seemed to take a real interest in my progress. The Chinese language is very difficult for a beginner, even for one who has a good ear, for the “tone” or inflection with which you pronounce the word may change its meaning. Sometimes one after the other of the Princesses would repeat the same word in different tones and make me repeat it and then give the meaning of each tone. They would sometimes make puns on words, or give me a string of difficult words for the accent and to improve my enunciation, as the French teach the children, “Trois gros rats dans trois gros trous.” When I would finally get quite tangled up with these words I would retaliate with “Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.” This would end the lesson for that day, for they would all try to say it and get so hilarious that there was no effort at further study, and dinner would be announced in the midst of the fun.

Sometimes the young Empress and the Ladies would play cards in the evenings. Her Majesty seemed only to like her fairy game! The cards were narrow slips of pasteboard with curious devices on each, but little more than an inch wide, and there were one hundred and fifty in a pack. I never succeeded in getting into the merits of the game. Sometimes when the Ladies felt industriously inclined, they would weave a kind of braid. The threads, gold, silver, or silk, were attached to the center of a wooden table and were weighted at the ends. They would weave these in and out into cunningly fashioned braids and ribbons. The Princesses did a great deal of beautiful embroidery, making their own shoes, which are of exquisitely embroidered satin, but they could not do this at night, for only candles are used in the two Peking Palaces, the Summer Palace being the only one in China lighted by electricity.

===Chinese===
逢到不唱戏、天又暗得不能再画的日子，我便到皇太后宝座殿左侧皇后与女官们的起居室里去，与她们同候开晚饭。其时皇后便教我中文。她对我的口音十分讲究，对我进益与否，似乎真心关切。中文于初学者而言极为艰难，即便耳朵灵光的人也不容易——因一个字你用什么「声调」、什么抑扬去念，意思便随之而变。有时公主（Princesses）们便一个挨一个，用不同的声调把同一个字念出来，叫我跟着念，再逐一告诉我每一种声调各是什么意思。她们有时拿字做双关戏谑，有时则抛给我一连串难念的字，练我的重音、正我的吐字，恰如法国人教孩童那般：「三只大老鼠，藏在三个大洞里（Trois gros rats dans trois gros trous）。」等我终于被这些字缠得舌头打结之时，我便回敬一句：「彼得·派珀拣了一撮腌胡椒（Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers）。」这一日功课便到此为止了——因她们皆要跟着念这一句，念着念着便笑得前仰后合，再无心向学，晚饭也在这一片欢腾之中报了上来。

晚间，皇后与女官们有时也打牌。皇太后似乎只喜欢她那「神仙戏」！那牌是窄窄一条硬纸片，上绘稀奇的花样，宽不过一寸有余，一副共一百五十张。我始终未能摸出这牌的奥妙来。女官们若来了兴致，有时也编织一种绦子。金银丝线或丝线，系于一张木桌正中，线头各悬重物。她们把线穿插交织，编成种种精巧别致的绦带。公主们做着大量精美的绣活，自己所穿的鞋亦亲手而制——皆是绣工绝妙的缎面——但这等细活夜里却做不得，因北京两处宫殿里只点蜡烛，全中国唯有颐和园（Summer Palace）一处通了电灯。

===Original===
One night at dinner the young Empress asked me to come earlier than usual the next morning, as there was something she wished me to see. Several eunuchs were waiting at the gate of the Palace to conduct us to the young Empress when we arrived at nine o’clock the next morning, and I then learned this was her Birthday. I hurried in and found the Imperial Princess and all the Ladies of the Palace, besides a number of visitors, standing in front of the young Empress’s pavilion. They told me she had asked them to wait to present their congratulations until I came, and said that I was to go in first. I did so, and there, on a throne, sat the young Empress in full Court dress, wearing the Court coiffure, with its veil of pearls, which was most becoming to her narrow patrician face. She was looking very sweet and gracious and held out her tiny hand to me on my entrance. I bowed low over it and kissed it, and wished her from the bottom of my heart “ten thousand” years of happiness and all kinds of “felicitous omens.” I then started to move out, but she told me to remain in the room at one side and watch the Princesses and Ladies as they came in. Each made the prostrations before her and presented a jade ruyie,8 which she received with due ceremony—the same ceremony as for the Emperor’s and Empress Dowager’s Birthday!

一日傍晚用饭时，皇后嘱我次日清晨比往日早些到，说有一样东西要我瞧。次日早晨九点我们到时，已有几名太监在宫门候着，引我们去见皇后。我这才知道，这一日原来是皇后的千秋（Birthday）。我急忙进去，只见大公主（Imperial Princess）和宫中一应女官，还有若干来客，都已站在皇后的阁前。她们告诉我，皇后吩咐她们先候着，待我到了再一同拜贺，又说我该先进去。我便依言先进去，只见皇后端坐于宝座之上，着全副朝服，戴着朝冠，冠上覆着珍珠面帘——这一身装束，最衬她那一张清秀狭长的贵胄之脸。她神情和悦可亲，我进去时便向我伸出她那只小小的手来。我深深俯下身去，吻了她的手，诚心诚意祝她「万年」福祉，并种种「吉祥佳兆」。我随即要退出去，她却叫我留在一旁，看公主与女官们依次进来。每一个人在她面前行跪拜之礼，献上一柄玉如意（jade ruyie），她便依足礼数接过——所行之礼，与皇上、皇太后万寿之时一般无二！

===Original===
But these winter days were not all given up to the Theater and festivals. There were some days of sadder import. Days of mourning were often celebrated at the Palace. The anniversary of the deaths of some Emperor or Ancestor was of frequent occurrence. It seemed to me they celebrated the anniversary of the death of every Emperor of the Dynasty! On these days there would be sacrifices at the ancestral tablets and religious ceremonies early in the mornings. The Empress Dowager and the whole Court would wear mourning for the day and there was never any sort of amusement. White, which is full mourning, is not worn on these anniversaries after the third, but violet and blue (second mourning) is put on. The flowers worn in the coiffure were also in violet, white or blue, the mourning colors. One night at dinner the young Empress, who acts as Mistress of Ceremonies in the Palace, told me the following was a day of mourning. She asked me if I would wear one of the mourning colors, as it was the anniversary of the death of the Emperor Tung-Chih (the Empress Dowager’s son).

The next day I put on a black dress, our mourning, and wore violet flowers in my hair. When we entered, Her Majesty was sacrificing at the small shrine in her sitting-room. She was dressed in dark violet, heavily trimmed with black, and had not a flower of any kind in her hair—only a few pearls. She looked very sad and was more earnest and reverent at the sacrifice than usual, but when she had finished her sacrifice, she bade us “Good morning” and inquired after our health, with her usual consideration. We soon left the Throne-room for my working-hall, and I did not see her again until after our dinner with the Empress and Ladies, when we went into the Throne-room to make our adieus. As I had not been wearing black for some time (as Her Majesty said she didn’t like it), she now noticed that I had it on and she asked Lady Yu-Keng, in an aside, “why.” She was told that when I knew what anniversary it was, I had put it on on that account. She seemed much touched, took my hand in both hers, and said, “You have a good heart to think of my grief and to have wished to sympathize,” and tears fell from her eyes on my hand, which she held in hers.

然而这些冬日，并不全交付给了戏台与节庆。也有几个意绪悲凉的日子。宫中常常过忌辰。某位先帝或某位祖先的冥诞忌日，时有举行。我瞧着，简直本朝每一位先帝的忌日他们都要过！每逢此等日子，清晨一早便要在神主牌位前献祭，行种种祭礼。皇太后与全宫廷皆着素服一日，绝无任何娱乐。素白乃大丧之服，第三次以后的忌辰便不再穿，改穿紫与蓝（即期服之色）。头饰上所戴的花，也用紫、白、蓝这几种素色。一日傍晚用饭时，皇后——她在宫中司礼——告诉我次日是个忌辰。她问我可愿着一种素色，因那一日是同治皇帝（Emperor Tung-Chih，即皇太后之子）的忌日。

次日我穿了一件黑衣——我们的素服——发间簪了几朵紫花。我们进去时，皇太后正在她起居室里的小神龛前上祭。她身穿深紫，缘边重重镶着黑，发间不簪一朵花——只缀着几颗珍珠。她神情甚是哀戚，献祭之时比往日更肃穆更虔诚；待祭毕，她照旧与我们道「早安」，又照素日的体贴问我们安。我们不久便离开宝座殿，到我的画室去。直到与皇后、女官们用罢晚饭，再到宝座殿辞驾之时，我才又见到她。因我已有一阵子不着黑（皇太后说过不喜欢），此时她留意到我身上穿黑，便低声问裕庚夫人（Lady Yu-Keng）「这是为何」。裕庚夫人告诉她：我知道了今日是什么忌辰，特地为此穿的。她似大为感动，双手将我一只手握住，说道：「你能念着我的伤心，又肯与我分忧，你这心肠真好。」说着，泪珠便从她眼中落下，滴在我那只被她握着的手上。

===Original===
Poor lady! Private sorrows and sad memories were not all she had to grieve her now. I had noticed her growing anxiety for many days! She seemed to feel all the gravity of the political situation of China. As the rumors of war between Russia and Japan grew, her anxiety increased and she was looking sad and careworn. She seemed to be full of doubt and fear, and quite unlike her usual self. I fancy she thought of the unprepared state of her country and feared that it might be drawn into this struggle. She seemed to be in doubt as to the course that was best to be taken. Even should the Empire not be drawn into the conflict, two hostile nations were to meet within its borders. The struggle was to take place in Manchuria, the cradle of the Dynasty. That beautiful, smiling country would be ravished by war, and the awful possibility of the ancestral tombs being desecrated, loomed up before her. The desecration of the tombs of one’s ancestors in China is supposed to bring dire consequences upon the family, and a pious Chinaman would face any material loss rather than run the risk of these tombs being desecrated. She felt it all, and was sad indeed.

===Chinese===
可怜的太后！她此刻所忧所伤的，远不止一己之悲与旧日之痛。许多日来，我已留意到她那与日俱增的忧色！她似乎深切地感到中国时局之严峻。日俄之间将开战的传言愈甚，她便愈发忧心忡忡，形容愁苦。她似满怀犹疑与恐惧，全不似素日的那副模样。我揣想她心中所念，乃是中国那未及筹备的国力，所惧者，是怕中国亦将被卷入这一场争斗。她似乎举棋不定，不知何去何从才是上策。即令帝国不致被卷入战火，两个敌对的国度终要在中国的疆土之内交锋。战事将在满洲（Manchuria）展开——那可是本朝龙兴之地。那一片秀美丰饶、面目和悦的河山，将遭兵燹蹂躏；而列祖列山陵或有被发掘亵渎的可怖之虞，更沉沉地横亘在她眼前。在中国，祖宗山陵一旦被人惊扰，便要给这一家子招来凶险的报应；一个虔敬的中国人，宁可承当任何财帛上的折损，也不肯冒祖宗陵寝遭毁之险。这一切她皆感同身受，心中之痛，实非言语可状。
