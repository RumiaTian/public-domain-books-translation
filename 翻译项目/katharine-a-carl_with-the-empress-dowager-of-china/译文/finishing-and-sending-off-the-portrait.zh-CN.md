## XXXIV Finishing and Sending Off the Portrait / 第三十四章 画像的完工与启程

===Original===
The nineteenth day of April was approaching, and the portrait steadily advancing. As it neared completion Her Majesty’s interest in it seemed to grow. She spent a great deal of time in my pavilion watching its progress, and expressed herself as much delighted with it. A few days before the nineteenth, I asked Her Majesty to allow Mrs. Conger to come and see it on that day. She immediately consented, and invitations were sent through the Foreign Office, not only to Mrs. Conger, but to the wives of the Ministers and First Secretaries of Legations to come to the Palace on the nineteenth day of April, for the purpose of “seeing the portrait of Her Imperial Majesty, the Empress Dowager, painted by the American artist.”

===Chinese===
四月十九日渐渐临近，画像也一寸寸趋于完工。临近收笔，皇太后（Her Majesty）对它的兴致似乎愈发浓了。她常常在我的画阁里待上好半天，看我作画的进展，言谈间不胜欢喜。十九日之前数日，我请皇太后恩准康格夫人（Mrs. Conger）于那日来观画。太后当即允诺，并由外务部（Foreign Office）发出请帖——不止请了康格夫人，更遍请各国公使及公使馆首席参赞的夫人，邀她们于四月十九日入宫，「瞻仰美国画师所绘皇太后（Her Imperial Majesty, the Empress Dowager）圣容」。

===Original===
The ladies of the Legation, of course, responded to the invitation, and on the morning of the nineteenth the portrait was placed in the splendid frame. Her Majesty decided she would receive the ladies first in her Throne-room, after which they were to come to my studio to see the portrait. As I was still working until the “fateful hour,” I did not go up to the Throne-room but awaited the ladies in my own place. Her Majesty did not accompany the ladies when they came to see the portrait, but she sent the young Empress and Princesses to my pavilion to assist me in receiving and to lend a proper dignity to the occasion. The portrait, in a Chinese milieu, and seen in the light in which it was painted, made a better effect than it could in any other surroundings. The ladies were, of course, much interested in seeing this long-talked-of picture—the first ever painted of Her Majesty—and the novelty of the precedent, as well as the interest of a visit to the Palace, favorably predisposed them, and they expressed themselves as most interested in the work, finding it a good likeness. The admiration it received from the young Empress and the Ladies of the Court was almost embarrassing, and the eunuchs said it was so lifelike when they passed the windows that it inspired the same awe Her Majesty’s own presence did.

After the ladies had duly looked at and commented upon the portrait, they repaired to one of the halls in connection with my studio, where a repast had been prepared by the orders of Her Majesty. Here, for the first and only time, while I was in the Palace, the young Empress sat down at the table with the foreign ladies, and acted as hostess, and very gracefully she filled her role.

===Chinese===
公使馆的夫人们自然欣然应邀。十九日清晨，画像被装入那架华美的画框之中。皇太后决定先在正殿接见这些夫人，随后再请她们到我的画室里来看画像。因我仍要一直画到「那决定性的一刻」，便不曾上正殿去，只在自家画阁里迎候。夫人们前来观画之时，皇太后并未亲自陪同，而是派了皇后（the Young Empress）与众位公主到我画阁里来助我款待，并为这场合添一份应有的端严。这幅画像置于中国的陈设之中，又在作画时的同一种光线之下观看，所收的效果，自非任何别处所能及。这些夫人久闻此画——皇太后的头一幅画像——如今终得亲见，兴致之高自不待言；而此番破天荒之举的新奇，再加上入宫一游本身的意趣，已使她们先自心生好感。她们皆言对此画极感兴趣，称道其酷肖其人。皇后与宫中女眷对画像的称赏，几乎令我受宠若惊；太监们则说，他们从窗下经过时，画上之人栩栩如生，竟与皇太后亲临一般，令人顿生敬畏之心。

夫人们依礼将画像细细看过、议论一番之后，便移步到我画阁相连的一间厅里——皇太后早命人在此备下筵席。在我居留宫中的那些日子里，唯有此番是头一遭，也是唯一的一遭，皇后与外国夫人同坐一席，充作主人款待宾客，举止之间，将这一主位承应得从容优雅。

===Original===
After the visit of the ladies of the Legation, Her Majesty informed me that the Princes and nobles, whose rank entitled them to enter the Palace Enclosure, were to come to see it the following day. As it would not have been “according to the Proprieties” for gentlemen to enter the quarters reserved for ladies, or the buildings where even a foreign lady worked, the portrait was, for their visit, carried out into the open court of my pavilion.

To place the portrait in its carved pedestal, it was necessary to erect a scaffolding by which the framed picture was raised into the air, and then lowered into its stand. When all was finally arranged, the scaffolding was removed, the debris cleared away, and the Princes and nobles, in full dress, came into the court to see the portrait. Each one approached the picture and closely examined it, even touching the canvas. Unfortunately, I could not hear their comments, as I only saw the ceremony discreetly ensconced behind a curtain, but I could watch their faces and study their expressions, though I must confess that they revealed very little.

A young Manchu, who had been attached to a Legation abroad and had learned photography in an amateur way, had been ordered by Her Majesty to make a photograph of the portrait. This was done while the Princes and nobles were still in the court. When it was photographed, and the Princes had retired, the scaffolding was again put up, the picture was raised out of its carved wood pedestal and was replaced in my studio. All this took the greater part of the day.

===Chinese===
公使馆夫人们去后，皇太后告知我：凡品级够得入宫禁的王公大臣，明日也要来瞻仰圣容。因男子若进入女眷所居之处，或哪怕是一位外国女画家作画的殿宇，皆「于礼不合」，故为接待他们起见，画像被搬到了我画阁前的露庭之中。

要把画像安到那座雕花座子之上，须先搭起一座脚手架，将装了框的画悬空吊起，再徐徐落入座内。待一切布置妥当，脚手架拆去，零碎清理干净，王公贵胄们穿着朝服，鱼贯入庭瞻仰圣容。每人都走到画像跟前细细端详，甚至伸手抚触画布。可惜他们的议论我听不见，我只是躲在一幅帘子背后远远地窥看这一番礼数；不过我尚可端详他们的面容，揣摩他们的神情——虽则我须承认，那些面孔上所能看出的，实在寥寥。

有一位年轻的满族（Manchu）人，曾在国外公使馆里当过差，业余学过照相之术，皇太后命他为这幅画像拍一张照片。这便是在王公大臣尚在庭中之时拍就的。拍照既毕，王公们也散去之后，脚手架又重新搭起，画像从那雕花木座中吊出，仍安回我的画室里。这一切已耗去大半日工夫。

===Original===
Her Majesty was so pleased with the comments she heard upon the portrait (of course no unfavorable ones were made to her), that she decided to accede to the prayers of several of the high officials, and allow the Sacred Picture to be viewed by a number of other high functionaries. For this purpose, the portrait was removed to the Wai-Wu-Pu (Foreign Office); for many of the highest Officials are not permitted to enter the Palace Enclosure.

At the Foreign Office, not only the high Chinese Officials, but the foreign Ministers and their staffs were invited to see it. Many of the foreigners went in full dress uniform for this visit, in deference to Chinese prejudices. After it had been duly viewed by all in Peking of sufficient rank to have that honor, it was enclosed in a satin-lined camphor-wood box, covered with satin of Imperial yellow, and the box was closed with great solemnity. The pedestal was placed in a similar box. Each had splendid bronze handles and huge circular locks. These boxes were enclosed in others, also lined with the Imperial color, and were finally ready for shipment. The packing-cases, containing the framed picture and its carved pedestal, were placed upon a flat freight car, which had been elaborately decorated with red and yellow festoons of silk. The boxes were covered with yellow cloth, painted with the Double Dragon. A special railway had been laid from the Wai-Wu-Pu to the station outside the Chien-Mên, for it was not considered fitting that ordinary bearers transport the picture of Her Majesty.

===Chinese===
皇太后所闻关于画像的称赏（自然，凡到她耳里的，无不赞颂），使她满心欢喜，遂决意俯允几位朝廷重臣之请，准这幅圣容再由若干其他大员瞻仰一回。为此，画像被移送至外务部（Wai-Wu-Pu）——因为许多品级极高的大臣，本是无缘踏入宫禁的。

在外务部，不仅中国的大员，连各国公使及其随员也受邀前来瞻仰。许多外国人特意穿上大礼服赴会，以迁就中国人的成见。北京（Peking）凡品级够得此殊荣之人，一一依礼瞻仰既毕，画像便被装入一只内衬缎子的樟木箱，箱外蒙以御用明黄色的缎子，箱子以极隆重的礼数封合。画框的雕花座子则装入一只同样的箱子。两箱各有华美的铜环把手与硕大的圆锁。这两只箱子又各装入一只外箱，外箱内也衬着御用之色，至此才算备妥，可以启运。装着画框与雕花座子的两只箱笼，被安置在一辆平板货车上，车身上绕满了红黄两色的绸彩，装点得极尽华美。箱笼上覆着黄布，黄布上绘着双龙。一条专修的小铁路，从外务部直通前门（Chien-Mên）外的车站——盖因皇太后的圣容，断不可由寻常夫役来抬送，那是不合宜的。

===Original===
The Officials of the Wai-Wu-Pu, as well as many other of the high Officials in Peking, dressed in full dress, accompanied it to the station, and stood to watch the Sacred Picture start off on its long journey to St. Louis. The special train carrying it was met at Tientsin by the Viceroy of the Province, surrounded by all his official staff. It was there placed with great ceremony upon the steamer on which it was to make the journey to Shanghai, and was accompanied from Peking to Shanghai by an official specially appointed for the purpose.

At Shanghai it was received in the same formal state and with the same official pomp as at Tientsin. It was met at the steamer by the Governor of the Province and all his staff and transhipped with great ceremony to one of the Pacific Mail Steamers for San Francisco. The Sacred Picture was accompanied on its journey from Shanghai to St. Louis by a high Official and his suite. A special car conveyed it from San Francisco to St. Louis.

===Chinese===
外务部的官员，并北京其余许多大员，皆着朝服，亲送画像至车站，肃立目送这幅圣容踏上远赴圣路易斯（St. Louis）的漫漫旅途。载像的专车行至天津（Tientsin），本省总督率全体幕僚亲迎。在那里，画像以极隆重的礼数被移上轮船——它将由此船载往上海（Shanghai）；并有一名特派的官员，一路随行，自北京护送至上海。

至上海，画像所受的礼数与排场，与在天津时一般无二。总督率全体幕僚亲到码头迎候，复以隆重礼数将之转载至一艘开往旧金山（San Francisco）的太平洋邮船（Pacific Mail Steamer）之上。这幅圣容自上海启程赴圣路易斯的一路，有一位大员携其随从同行护送。到了旧金山，又有一节专车将之载往圣路易斯。

===Original===
His Imperial Highness Prince Pu L’un, Imperial Commissioner and personal representative of Their Majesties at the Exposition of St. Louis, awaited the arrival of the portrait there, delaying his departure for several days in order to be able himself to assist at the reception and placing of the portrait. At four o’clock on the afternoon of the 19th of June, His Imperial Highness and the Imperial Chinese Commission repaired to the Art Gallery, where the cases containing the portrait and pedestal were awaiting their presence to be opened. The Director of the Art Gallery, the Assistant Director, and several other members of the Board of Fine Arts, were also present.

The cases containing the portrait, one within the other, were opened, and finally within the last, lined with yellow silk, lay the “Sacred Picture,” covered with a screen of brocaded satin of Imperial hue. This satin cover was ceremoniously removed, and the picture was unveiled. The Prince proposed the health of Her Majesty and the Prosperity of China, which the assistants drank in sparkling champagne. This opening of the cases and unveiling of the picture lasted from four o’clock to nine p.m. A few days later, when the Gallery where it was placed was opened to the public, it lost, for the first time since its inception, its semi-sacred qualities. Only then did it stand upon its own merits and become as other portraits. Then, for the first time, it could be seen by the ordinary individual—then only it became the subject of comment as any other picture at the Fair. Then it was open to the gaze of the vulgar and the comment of the scoffer.

===Chinese===
溥伦（Prince Pu L’un）殿下，身任钦差大臣、两宫陛下派赴圣路易斯博览会的全权代表，早在那里迎候画像的到来——他将启程之期延缓了数日，为的就是要亲自主持画像的迎入与安放。六月十九日下午四时，溥伦殿下偕中国钦差展览委员会来到美术馆，装着画像与画座的几只箱笼正等着他们亲临开箱。美术馆馆长、副馆长，及美术委员会的几位其他委员，也都在场。

装画的箱子，层层相套，一一开启；及至最里一层，内衬黄绸，那「圣容」便安卧其中，上面覆着一幅御用色泽的织锦缎幔。这缎幔依礼郑重揭去，画像方始展露于众。溥伦殿下举杯，为皇太后的圣寿康宁、为中国之隆盛繁荣，率在场的诸位相助之人共饮起泡香槟。这一番开箱揭幔的典礼，自下午四时一直延续到九时方毕。数日之后，画像所在的展厅向公众开放，自此它才头一遭失却了自绘制以来那半神圣的色彩。要到此时，它方才独立地凭其本身的价值而立，与别的肖像画并无二致。要到此时，凡夫俗子方得一睹其真容——也唯有到此时，它才如博览会中任何一幅画作一般，任人品评。到此，它便任凭庸人凝视、任凭轻薄者哂笑了。

===Original===
At the close of the Exposition, a delegate was sent from the Chinese Legation in Washington to arrange for the transportation of the picture to the latter place. The portrait and its carved support were again placed in their satin-lined cases, and it began the journey to Washington. Her Majesty had decided when the portrait was completed to her satisfaction that it would be a suitable present for her to make to the United States. She thought this would be particularly appropriate, as the painting of the portrait for the St. Louis Exposition had been thought of by the wife of the American Minister to Peking, and as it had been executed by an American artist. Thus the United States received the gift of the first portrait ever painted of a Chinese Ruler.

When the portrait arrived in Washington, His Excellency Sir Chentung Liang Cheng, the Chinese Minister to Washington, attended by his Secretaries, made a formal presentation of the portrait to the President, which Mr. Roosevelt received on behalf of the United States Government.

===Chinese===
博览会闭幕之时，华盛顿的中国公使馆派来一名代表，安排将画像转运至华盛顿事宜。画像连同其雕花座子，又被装入那内衬缎子的箱笼之中，启程赴华盛顿。画像完工、合乎圣意之时，皇太后便已定夺，要以此作为一份相宜之礼，赠与合众国。她以为此举尤为妥帖：盖因此幅赴圣路易斯博览会陈列的画像，其发想原出北京美国公使夫人之意，而绘画之人又是一位美国画家。由此，合众国便得了一件旷世的厚礼——有史以来所绘的中国君主的第一幅肖像。

画像抵达华盛顿之后，中国驻华盛顿公使梁诚爵士阁下（His Excellency Sir Chentung Liang Cheng），由其诸位秘书随行，将画像正式呈献于总统之前，由罗斯福先生（Mr. Roosevelt）代表合众国政府祗领。
