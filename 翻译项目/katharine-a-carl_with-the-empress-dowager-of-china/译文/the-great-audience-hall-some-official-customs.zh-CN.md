## XVI The Great Audience Hall—Some Official Customs / 十六 大朝殿——若干官场礼制

===Original===
When His Majesty the Emperor reigned alone, he was in the habit of holding his Audiences as early as three o'clock a.m. It is said, however, that this custom was owing as much to his personal shyness as to his love of early rising, for at these Audiences he would allow but two candles on the Throne-table in front of him, and the Great Hall was lighted elsewhere only by the beautiful Chinese lanterns, which shine with but a dim brilliancy and are not very effective as lights. Thus his face could not be seen if an official should so forget the Proprieties as to raise his eyes to the Imperial Person.

===Chinese===
皇上独自秉政之时，惯于清晨三点钟便开觐见。然而据说这一惯例，既因其喜早起，也因其天性腼腆——盖每逢此类觐见，御案上他面前只许燃两支蜡烛，大殿其余各处则只悬那些华美的中国宫灯照明；宫灯虽美，光辉却甚幽暗，作为照明实在无甚效用。如此一来，若有官员竟忘了礼数，抬眼偷觑天颜，也看不清圣容。

===Original===
Their Majesties' Audiences are held in the Great Audience Hall, a detached building apart and quite distinct from all the other buildings of the Palace enclosure. The inscription over its great doors points out that it is the "Hall where Industry is to be applied to State Affairs." In all the Palaces the Audience Hall is nearest the outside walls and entrances, so that the officials who are privileged to have Audiences must only pass through the outer courts to reach the hall—Their Majesties' Palaces with their private apartments being at some distance beyond. At the Winter Palace, where there are so many walls within walls, each of Their Majesties' Palaces is surrounded with walls, and the Audience Hall is also in a walled-in enclosure near one of the Great Gates, but at the Summer Palace there are no walls except the exterior ones!

===Chinese===
二位陛下的觐见，在大朝殿中举行。这是一座独立的殿宇，与宫苑之内其余所有殿宇截然分开，判然有别。其大门之上的匾额，点明此乃「勤政亲贤之殿」——即以勤勉之心料理国政的殿宇。在所有宫苑里，觐见殿都最靠近外墙和宫门，使有资格觐见的官员只须穿过外朝的几重院落便可到达——二位陛下的寝宫及内苑则在更深处，相隔尚有一段距离。紫禁城里墙内有墙，重重叠叠，二位陛下的寝宫各为一圈围墙所绕，而大朝殿亦在一处围合的院落之中，靠近一座大门；唯独颐和园，除了外墙之外，再无别的围墙！

===Original===
The interior of the Audience Hall, at the Summer Palace, is not by any means bare or austere. It is furnished in the same style as the Throne-rooms, with splendid ornaments, curios, tea-tables and chairs, and, curious anachronism, there are here three pianos! The walls are hung with ornamental scrolls, as well as with those bearing some gigantic character traced by an Emperor's hand or some condensed bit of philosophy of the Sages. One of these scrolls has an admonition to the Emperor to remember that "he is responsible to Heaven for the happiness and prosperity of his people."

===Chinese===
颐和园里这座觐见殿的内里陈设，绝非素朴简陋。它与宝座殿的陈设同一气派，满是华美的摆设、古玩、茶几和坐椅——还有一处古怪的错位：殿中竟摆着三架钢琴！四壁悬着各色挂轴，既有供赏玩的画轴，也有写着一个巨大字样的御笔之书，或是圣贤精炼的格言。其中一帧便是告诫皇上，须切记「他须为万民的安乐与福祉，向皇天负责」。

===Original===
There is a great dais in the center of the hall, on which stands the Throne, with its table, behind which is the three-, five-, or seven-leaved screen. The ancient dais was lower than those now used, and the antique Throne, with its capacious size and cushions, was more like a lounge than the modern Throne. This seems to indicate that the administration of justice by the Emperor was in ancient times less formal and more patriarchal than today. In former times the Emperor could lounge upon his Throne at his ease when seeing his Ministers, and they could approach nearer the Sacred Person, as the dais was not so large nor so high as that in use today.

===Chinese===
大殿正中设一座高大的宝座台基，台上便是宝座，前面摆着御案，后面立着一架三扇、五扇或七扇的屏风。古时的台基比今日所用的为低，古时的宝座，以其宽大的形制和厚厚的软垫，更近乎一张卧榻，而不像今日的宝座。这似乎表明：古时天子临朝听政，礼数不似今日这般繁文缛节，而更近于家长之风。从前皇上接见大臣时，可自在地斜倚在宝座之上；而大臣们也可更接近圣驾，因为那时的台基并不如今日这般高大。

===Original===
Heads of departments and Princes with honorary official positions have Audiences on certain days of the month, to report upon affairs of their Boards or to pay their respects to His Majesty. Every day Their Majesties hold Audience and see the Prime Minister and Grand Secretary, and there are frequent meetings of the Grand Council. The Prime Minister, Prince Ching, has the last Audience of the day, and business reported on during the other Audiences is then discussed.

===Chinese===
各部堂官及有荣誉官职的王公，于每月指定的日子觐见，奏报本部事务，或向皇上请安。二位陛下每日皆御觐见，接见首相与大学士，军机处亦频繁开会。庆亲王（Prince Ching）身任首相，每日最后一次觐见便是他的，其余各次觐见所奏之事，皆在此次一并面议。

===Original===
All telegrams and despatches go to their respective Boards, and are, except in cases of extreme gravity, only reported to Their Majesties at the Audiences. After eleven all State business is supposed to be finished by Their Majesties. They are then free from State worries and cares until the following day. During the times of the rebellion in the Province of Kwang-Si, when the Russian evacuation of Manchuria was expected, and at the time of outbreak of hostilities in Manchuria (the three grave events occurring during my stay in the Palace), telegrams and despatches were constantly being sent to Her Majesty out of Audience hours. They were brought to her Throne-room, and sometimes even during her walks in the gardens they would be handed her. These despatches were sent over to the Palace from the Wai-Wu-Pu on their arrival. Of course, it was by Her Majesty's express command that her privacy was thus infringed upon. No official would otherwise have dared transgress the prescribed rules. The despatches were received at the entrance of the Palace by the eunuch whose province it was. He placed them in the yellow-covered, silken-lined box, in which they were presented to Her Majesty on bended knees.

===Chinese===
一切电报公文，皆送往各部衙门；除非事关极其重大，否则只在觐见之时奏闻二位陛下。过了十一点钟，二位陛下的国事便算全数办毕。此后直至次日，方才再理国政之忧。然而广西叛乱之际、俄国将从满洲撤兵之说纷传之时，以及满洲战事爆发之时（这三桩大事皆发生在我留居宫中的那段时间里），电报与公文常常在觐见时辰之外，接连送呈皇太后。它们被送到她的宝座殿，有时甚至在她园中散步时，也直接呈到她手中。这些公文一到，便由外务部（Wai-Wu-Pu）送进宫来。自然，皇太后的私憩如此被扰，全出于她本人的特旨。若非如此，无一名官员敢于逾越既定的规矩。这些公文由专司此事的太监在宫门接收，放进一只黄绫裹面、丝缎为里的匣子里，再以这匣子盛着，跪呈皇太后。

===Original===
In front of the Throne dais, during the hours of Audience, there are five cushions placed on the floor for the members of the Grand Council to kneel upon when they are memorializing Their Majesties. The Prime Minister's cushion is nearest the Throne. A cushion to kneel upon is a privilege only granted members of the Grand Council. Any other official, when making communications to Their Majesties, must kneel upon the bare marble floor, and must kneel beyond the space occupied by these five cushions. He is thus placed at a disadvantage. The distance at which he is from Their Majesties may prevent his hearing some of their words, especially the Emperor's, whose voice is very low and without any carrying quality. The official may overcome this difficulty and shorten the distance by paying the eunuch who conducts him to the Audience Hall, to remove some of the cushions, so that he may kneel nearer the dais. The Prime Minister's and Grand Secretary's cushions may on no condition be removed, but the other three are subject to the will of the introducing eunuch. If this latter be sufficiently paid, and there is a fixed price for each cushion, he will remove the three of the lower members of the Cabinet.

===Chinese===
觐见之时，宝座台基前的地上铺着五只拜垫，是供军机大臣们向二位陛下面奏时跪用的。首相的拜垫离宝座最近。跪用拜垫，是唯有军机大臣方得享的特权。其余任何官员，向二位陛下奏事时，都必须跪在光秃秃的大理石地面上，且必须跪在这五只拜垫所占的位置之外。如此一来，他便处于劣势。他与二位陛下之间的距离，可能使他听不清某些话，尤其是皇上的话——皇上声音极低，毫无传声之力。官员可以设法克服这一不便、缩短这段距离：给那引他入觐见殿的太监一笔贿赂，请他撤去几只拜垫，他便可跪得更近台基。首相与大学士的拜垫无论如何不可撤去，其余三只则由那引见的太监做主。只要给的银钱够数——每撤一只拜垫都有定价——他便肯把那三位较低军机大臣的拜垫撤掉。

===Original===
When the official who has been granted an Audience is conducted to the Audience Hall by the eunuch appointed for the purpose, the latter throws open the great doors, falls upon his knees at the threshold, and announces the name and position of the official, gives the hour and minute of his arrival at the Palace, and, before he rises, he has deftly removed the cushions for which he has received the required sum. After his name has been announced, the official enters and kneels as near the dais as is consistent with his rank and the sum paid the eunuch. When the eunuch has introduced the official, he turns from the door and must run away as fast as he can. Officials and eunuchs stationed at some distance watch his departure. Should he linger or transgress this law, capital punishment is the result. This is to avoid eavesdropping and the possible transmission of State secrets.

===Chinese===
蒙恩觐见的官员，由专职太监引至觐见殿时，那太监便推开大门，在门槛处跪下，唱出该官员的姓名和官职，并报出他到宫中的时辰与分秒；而未及起身之前，他已灵巧地撤去了那几只收过钱的拜垫。名姓唱过之后，官员便入内，按其品级和所付太监的银数，跪到尽可能接近台基之处。太监把官员引见既毕，便须转身，并以最快的速度奔离。远处驻守的官员和太监们都注视着他离去。倘若他踟蹰逗留、违了这条规矩，便要处以极刑。此举乃为防偷听，亦防泄露国家机密。

===Original===
When the official granted an Audience hears the last echo of the steps of the departing eunuch, he falls upon his knees and begins the relation of his business. Their Majesties question him, if necessary, to elicit further explanations. When the Audience is finished, the official rises and walks out. The Chinese never back out of the Presence, and it is not considered a breach of etiquette to turn their backs upon Royalty!

===Chinese===
蒙恩觐见的官员，听见那离去太监脚步声的最后一缕余响，便跪下，开始奏陈己事。若有必要，二位陛下便向他问话，以引出进一步的说明。觐见既毕，官员起身，步行退出。中国人从不行倒退之礼退出御前；在君前转身以背相向，并不算失仪！

===Original===
The officials who are obliged to go often to Audiences resort to an amusing subterfuge to protect their knees from the marble floor. They strap heavily wadded cushions around their knees before they go in, and they can thus kneel in comfort. The long Chinese gown worn by the men, of course, hides these knee cushions.

===Chinese===
那些不得不常常入觐的官员，为护住自己的膝盖免受大理石地面之苦，想出了一个颇有趣味的权宜之计：入觐之前，他们在膝上缠缚厚厚的棉垫，如此跪着便不觉难受。男子所穿的长袍，自然把这些护膝遮得严严实实。

## The Official Audience of Their Majesties / 二位陛下的正式觐见

===Original===
His Majesty assumed the cares of State at an early age, when he was still filled with boyish spirit. Many of the heads of departments are old men, and some of them doubtless most tiresome in reiterating facts and dwelling upon details. When the young Emperor first took over the direction of affairs and held his Audiences alone, he would get very impatient at hearing several of these old men go over tiresome details. As it is not "according to the laws of propriety" for the official to raise his eyes to the Sacred Person, while the old man rambled on, with prosy detail, the young Emperor would slip off the Throne and quietly descend from the dais, and when the poor official raised his eyes to make his obeisance to the Emperor, he would see only the vacant Throne! His Majesty had been in the rear of the hall behind the screen for perhaps five minutes smoking a cigarette or otherwise diverting himself!

===Chinese===
皇上年纪尚轻便已肩负起国政，那时他还满怀少年心气。各部堂官多有年迈之人，其中几位想必翻来覆去地陈述、絮絮叨叨地讲求细节，实在叫人厌烦。当初年轻的皇上初秉国政、独自御觐见之时，听见这几位老臣一味地讲那些乏味的细枝末节，心中便十分不耐。因官员「按礼」不得抬眼仰视圣驾，那老臣还在絮絮叨叨、噜苏不休之际，年轻的皇上便从宝座上溜下来，悄然下了台基；待那可怜的官员抬起眼来，要向皇上行礼时，只见一座空空的宝座！皇上却已在大殿后方的屏风背后，也许已待了五分钟之久，正在那里吸一支纸烟，或以别的法子自娱！

===Original===
I noticed a curious fact as to the quality of the sacredness of the persons of Their Celestial Majesties. This sacredness seems to belong to them as rulers and not as individuals. In the Audience Hall when administering justice, they are not approached nor addressed, except upon bended knee. In the Palace, in their own privacy, when they give an order or any command touching upon official affairs, this order is received by the attendant, be he courtier, high official or great prince, on his knees. When any official communication is made to Their Majesties, in private or elsewhere, it is made kneeling, but when Their Majesties are in their private capacity and spoken to on ordinary affairs, they are addressed almost familiarly, and the courtier or simple attendant stands while speaking to them. If, however, in the midst of a familiar conversation an order is given, the attendant immediately drops upon his knees to receive it.

===Chinese===
关于二位陛下圣体的那种神圣不可侵犯之气，我注意到了一桩奇特之事：这种神圣，似乎是属于他们作为君主的一面，而非属于他们作为个人的一面。在大朝殿临朝决事之时，除非跪着，无人可近其前、无人敢与之语。在宫中，当他们身处私室，一旦下一道涉及公务的谕旨或命令，奉命之人——不论是近臣、高官还是显贵的王公——都须跪着接旨。凡向二位陛下奏报公事，无论在私室还是他处，皆须跪奏；然而二位陛下若以私人身份而谈寻常琐事，旁人则几乎可与之不拘礼数地交谈，近臣或寻常侍者与他们说话时，是站着的。不过，倘若在这种不拘礼数的闲谈之中，忽然降下一道旨意，那侍者便须立即跪下接旨。

===Original===
The kowtow (pronounced ker-toe and meaning literally to bow the head) is used as a form of thanks, and is not a manner of greeting. The actors kowtow to Their Majesties at the beginning and end of each performance at the Theater, first to thank for the honor they are to receive in being allowed to act before them, and at the end to thank for the privilege granted. The officials "bow the head" to thank for an Audience or any favor or gift they have received or are to receive from Their Majesties. The kowtow is not only made by people at the Palace and at Imperial Audiences: it is sometimes used by equals to each other as a proper manner of thanking for some great favor. To make the kowtow, the person kneels three times and each time bows his head three times, touching the ground with it. The kowtow could not be made by a foreigner without looking most awkward and appearing most servile, but the Chinese do it with dignity, and it is neither ungraceful nor degrading-looking. It is a time-honored manner of giving thanks, a Chinese tradition surviving from a time when the courtiers were perhaps like slaves, but at present it does not imply any slave-like inferiority on the part of him who performs it.

===Chinese===
「叩首」（kowtow，读音近「克-托」，字面义即「以首叩地」）乃是一种致谢之礼，并非问候之礼。戏园里伶人每演一戏，开场与收场都要向二位陛下叩首：开场时，是谢恩准他们在御前献技的荣宠；收场时，是谢所赐的这一番恩典。官员们「叩首」，是为一次觐见、或为已受或将要受自二位陛下的任何恩宠与赏赐而致谢。叩首之礼不仅行于宫中及觐见御前：地位相等之人之间，有时为某桩大恩而郑重致谢，亦用此礼。行叩首之礼时，其人跪三次，每跪一次便以首叩地三次。外国人行此礼，难免显得笨拙、一副奴态；然中国人行之，则自有其庄重，既不难看，亦无卑微之态。这是一种由来已久的致谢之仪，是中国的一项古传之礼，可溯至近臣尚如奴仆的时代；然在今日，行此礼之人并不因此含带半分奴性的卑下。
