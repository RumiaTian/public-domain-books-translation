## III The Palace of the Emperor’s Father (Prince Ch’un, the Seventh Prince) / 第三章 皇上生父的府邸（醇亲王，即七王爷）

===Original===
The Palace of the Emperor’s Father, which the Empress Dowager had set aside for me to live in while I was at work on her portrait, was a splendid demesne, with a noble park and spacious buildings. It had been much injured by the foreign troops in 1900 and had been unoccupied since, until Her Majesty decided it would be a suitable dwelling-place for her “Portrait Painter.” She had it hastily restored and refurnished for our occupation, but many of the pavilions and summerhouses in the grounds were in ruins, and the stables but partly rebuilt. Except the grounds immediately surrounding the buildings in which the Yu-Kengs and I lived, which were well kept and garnished, the greater part of the extensive park was in a fascinating state of natural wildness. The Palace, like all others in China, consisted of a network of verandahed pavilions built around spacious courts. There was a small Theater with the Prince’s loge and stalls for his guests, and numerous tea and summer houses were scattered over different parts of the grounds.

===Chinese===
皇太后将这座皇上生父的府邸拨出来给我作绘像时居住之所，这是一处辉煌的宅邸，有气派的园林和宽敞的屋宇。1900 年它曾被外国军队大肆毁坏，此后一直无人居住，直到皇太后觉得这里对她这位「御用画像师」来说是个合适的居所。她命人匆匆将其修缮、重新布置供我们居住，但园中的亭台别业多有坍圮，马厩也只是部分修复。除了裕庚一家和我所住屋宇周围紧邻的园地修整得齐整、点缀得宜之外，那大片园林大多处于一种迷人的天然荒野之态。这座府邸与中国所有的府第一样，由一连串带有游廊的亭阁组成，环抱在宽敞的庭院四周。园中有一座小戏台，设有王爷的专座与宾客席，还有许多茶室和凉亭散落在园中各处。

===Original===
I selected, as my abiding-place, a charming group of buildings in a walled-in garden, fronting on a lotus-covered lake, with a winding stream at the back, spanned by a picturesque bridge. The principal pavilion of this group had a lofty central hall, out of which opened, on one side, bedrooms and dressing-rooms, and on the other dining-room and dependencies. Great doors in the center of the hall, which I had decided to use as my living-room, opened on a wide verandah which ran the whole length of the building. Marble steps led from this into a court filled with flowering shrubs. Two sides of the charming court had smaller pavilions similar to the central hall, and opposite this latter was a quaint stone wall, the upper part of tiled latticework, with curiously shaped openings at irregular intervals. In the center of this wall, massive wooden doors opened out on a beautiful terrace, shaded by fine old elms, over the lake. It was a charming dwelling-place, and this group of buildings soon came to be known as the “Ker-Gunia Fu,” “Ker-Gunia” being “Miss Carl” rendered into Chinese, and “Fu” meaning “Palace,” for the Chinese are very fond of nicknames. I learned later that these pavilions had been the dwelling-place of the Seventh Prince’s son, the present Emperor Kwang-Hsu, after he had been chosen as Heir to the Throne and until he went to live regularly at the Imperial Palace.

===Chinese===
我选作居所的，是围墙花园内一组宜人的屋宇，前临一方荷花掩映的湖泊，背后一道蜿蜒溪流，上架一座如画的石桥。这组建筑的主亭阁有一间高敞的正厅，一侧通向卧室和梳妆间，另一侧通向饭厅和附属各房。我决定把正厅用作起居室，厅中央的大门通向一道宽阔的游廊，游廊贯穿整座建筑。由游廊而下，几级大理石台阶引入一座繁花烂漫的庭院。这可爱庭院的两侧，各有一组与正厅相仿的较小亭阁，正厅对面则是一堵古雅的石墙，上半部是瓦砌的花格，其间错落开着形状各异的窗洞。墙的中央，两扇厚重的木门开启，通向一方秀美的台榭——台下便是湖，台上则有古榆遮阴。这真是一处宜人的居所，不久之后，这组建筑便被人称作「克姑娘府」——「克姑娘」是「Miss Carl」的中文译音，「府」即「府邸」之意，因为中国人极爱给人取绰号。后来我才得知，这几座亭阁曾是七王爷之子——即当今皇上光绪——被选为储君之后、正式入居大内之前所住的所在。

===Original===
As Her Majesty gave me my morning sittings after the Audience was finished (which lasted from eight a.m. to ten or eleven), I had plenty of time, after my cup of tea, to explore the grounds of our Palace, and I discovered new beauties each day. The Park was enclosed by high walls, for the Chinese are jealous of their privacy. Parts of the grounds were gently undulating, and all the eminences, where views could be had, were surmounted by charming summerhouses and belvederes. In one of these, where I loved to go in the early morning to refresh myself by the contemplation of the calm and peaceful lake beneath, and drink in the faint perfume of the stately lotus flowers, which grew in rich profusion on its bosom, I found an inscription on a large flat stone at the left of the entrance. I had seen enough of Chinese characters to know the inscription looked like a “poem.” The Chinese poem is rarely more than a phrase: the expression, in elegant and concise form, of some dainty fancy, some bit of philosophy, and is more properly a “verse” than a poem.

===Chinese===
皇太后给我安排的晨间坐班都在觐见结束后进行（觐见自上午八时持续至十时或十一时），因此喝过茶之后，我有充裕的时间去探访府中园地，每日都能发现新的景致。园外围以高墙，因为中国人最重私密。园中有些地方地势微微起伏，凡可凭眺之处，山顶上都建有雅致的凉亭和眺望台。其中有一处，我尤爱在清晨前往——在那里静观下方那片宁静的湖水，吮吸湖上盛开的端庄荷花所散发的淡淡幽香，身心都为之一爽。就在这处亭子入口左侧，我发现一块大平石上刻着铭文。中国字我也见过不少，一看便知这铭文是一首「诗」。中国人的诗，往往不过片言只语：以典雅简洁的形式，抒发一点精致的想象、一缕哲思，与其称作「诗」，不如称作「短句」更为恰当。

===Original===
I found, later, the inscription on the stone at the entrance of the summerhouse was really a “poem,” and had been written by no less a personage than the Seventh Prince himself! This had been his favorite place for rest and contemplation, and one day, as he reclined upon a cushion at the entrance, he had written this poem on the flat stone which lay conveniently near. The Chinese write with a brush well charged with liquid India ink, and their writing accommodates itself to almost any surface. Their characters, one for each word, take up less space than our combination of letters, and are infinitely more picturesque! Chinese gentlemen, or some attendant, generally carry about with them tablets of writing-ink and a brush, and they thus have the means at hand for jotting down a thought as it comes to them.

===Chinese===
后来我才得知，那亭子入口处石上的铭文，果真是一首「诗」，而且出自七王爷之手！这里原是他最爱的休憩与冥想之所，某日，他倚着入口处的坐垫闲坐，便在身旁这块平石上挥就了这首诗。中国人写字用毛笔，蘸饱了墨汁，几乎无论何处都能落笔。他们的字，一字即一义，比我们以字母拼合的字占的地方更小，却要生动好看得多！中国的文人——或其随从——身边常带着墨锭和毛笔，因此一有所感，便可随时记下。

===Original===
This little poem had been written with a brush, and some of the Prince’s followers had afterward cut the characters in the stone, so that it became a permanent record of a fleeting thought. It had evidently been inspired by the lotus flowers growing beneath; so gloriously beautiful today, and tomorrow shorn of their splendor. It was a plaint on the transience of worldly glory—

> … Which today, like the lotus fair,

 Lifts its head in pride;

 But tomorrow lies low,

 Bathed in the stagnant waters of oblivion.

===Chinese===
这首小诗是用毛笔写的，后来王爷的随从将字刻入石中，于是这一缕稍纵即逝的思绪便成了永久的纪念。诗显然是受了下方那片荷花的启发而作——今日这般灿烂夺目，明日便香消玉殒。全诗乃是叹人世荣华之无常——

> ……今日，宛如那娇美的莲花，

 昂首怒放，意气洋洋；

 明日却已凋零委地，

 沉沐于湮没无闻的死水之中。

===Original===
One day I came upon a number of small tombstones, in a beautiful shady corner, near the stables. I learned that these marked the last resting-places of the Prince’s favorite dogs and horses. Each stone had an inscription with the name, and extolled the virtues of the favorite, whose bones lay beneath it. The Prince was a great lover of animals, and is said to have had the best kennels and stables of any of the Imperial Princes.

===Chinese===
一日，我在马厩附近一处秀美的阴凉角落里，看到若干小小的墓碑。后来得知，这些墓碑所标的，是王爷生前的爱犬爱马的埋骨之所。每块石上都刻着它们的名字，并颂扬其长处——它们的骸骨便埋在碑下。王爷一生酷爱禽兽，据说在诸王爷之中，他的犬舍马厩是最好的。

===Original===
In my morning rambles, I also often came upon stones engraved with some character or a phrase from the classics. The ideographic Chinese characters, always picturesque, are doubly so when deeply engraved, or standing out in high relief on some rugged stone in a charming spot in the landscape. The picturesque form of the characters is sometimes heightened by being painted in vermilion or gilded; and the glowing color makes a delightful contrast with the cool gray of the stone. Even though I could not decipher the characters, nor read the phrases, I loved to come upon them in my morning walks. How much more interesting they must have been to the scholarly Chinese who understood them! How fine, when out for rest and contemplation, to come upon some thought of their great Sages cut in the living rock, or to see some character meaning “Peace” or “Prosperity” standing out, in bold relief or glowing color, from some shady nook, as if to bless him!

===Chinese===
晨间漫步之时，我也常会遇见刻着某个字或某句经典文句的石头。中国字本就生动如画，若是深深镌刻，或以高浮雕凸现于山野胜处某块粗粝的石头上，便更觉栩栩如生。字形之美，有时因涂以朱红或贴以金箔而更显鲜明；那温暖的颜色与石头清冷的灰调相映成趣，煞是悦目。即便我并不识得这些字，也读不懂这些句子，清晨散步时遇见它们，我总觉欢喜。对于那些能够读懂它们、博学的中国人而言，这些刻字想必更加意味深长罢！出外休憩冥想之时，忽然看见某位先圣先贤的哲思刻在嶙峋的山石之上；或是在某处幽阴的角落，看见一个意为「平安」或「福寿」的字，以雄浑的浮雕或鲜艳的色彩凸现其上，仿佛在为他祈福——这是何等风雅之事！

===Original===
From another of the summerhouses in the Park I could see the stone-paved highway leading from the Capital to the Summer Palace. During Their Majesties’ residence at the Summer Palace, this is a busy thoroughfare. When I did not care for peaceful contemplation or quiet rambles over the grounds, I would go to this summerhouse, whence I could see the carts and “chairs” of the officials, with their outriders, going to and from the Palace; messengers galloping past, bearing despatches; all sorts of itinerant venders, with their wares; heavily laden wagons, with small yellow banners flying, which showed they carried supplies to the Palace. Sometimes a group of horsemen would dash gaily past, the retainers of some splendidly attired young Prince, who rode in their midst on a red-saddled, handsomely caparisoned horse with silver trappings. Anon, the cumbersome, red, fringe-bedecked cart of some Princess, preceded and followed by from fifteen to thirty outriders, according to her rank in the Princely hierarchy, the black carts of her women bringing up the rear.

===Chinese===
从园中另一座凉亭望出去，可以看见那条从京城通往颐和园的石板大路。皇上和皇太后驻跸颐和园时，这条路便是一条热闹的要道。当我不愿静静沉思或安步园中之时，便会登上这座凉亭，从这里可以看见大小官员的车马和轿子，前后带着骑马的扈从，往来于颐和园；传递公文的信使策马飞驰而过；还有形形色色的流动小贩挑着货品；以及载重的货车，车上插着小黄旗，表明是为宫中运送物资。有时一队骑者会欢快地疾驰而过——那是某位服饰华美的年轻王爷的扈从，王爷本人骑着一匹配着红鞍、饰以银质辔头、鞍韂讲究的骏马，居于队伍之中。不一会儿，又会驶过某位王妃那辆笨重的红车——车周缘饰以流苏——按她在王爷品级中的位次，前后有十五至三十名骑马的扈从开道和殿后，最后面是她女眷们乘坐的黑车。

===Original===
One can tell the rank of the Chinese from the outsides of their chairs or carts. Only a reigning Emperor and Empress can go abroad in yellow chairs. The Emperor’s secondary wives ride in orange-colored chairs. The relicts of an Emperor, first or secondary, go in yellow or orange-colored carts. Princesses go abroad in red carts. Mandarins of the first and second degrees ride in green chairs; those of the third and fourth in blue chairs; and there is still another shape and style of chair for the ordinary individual, who may prefer a chair to a cart. The rank and file go in carts. These carts, peculiar to Peking, curious two-wheeled vehicles with heavy, iron-studded wheels, are uniformly covered in blue cloth. The wealth and standing of their occupants are discernible from the quality of the cloth and its trimmings, and the richness of the harness and trappings of the mule which is always used in the Peking carts. The mule in North China is a magnificent animal, much finer than the Chinese horse, which is only a pony.

===Chinese===
从轿子和车的外表，便可看出中国人的品级。唯有在位的皇上和皇后才能坐明黄轿出行。皇上的侧室乘橘黄轿。先帝——无论正配或侧室——的遗孀则乘明黄或橘黄色的车。王妃格格乘红车出行。一品、二品大员乘绿轿；三品、四品乘蓝轿；此外还有一种形制和式样不同的轿，是给寻常人乘坐的——他们也许更爱坐轿而非坐车。寻常百姓则乘车。这种车为北京所特有，是一种奇特的两轮车，轮子笨重，钉着铁钉，外面一律蒙着蓝布。乘车之人的富足与身分，可从布料的质地与缘饰，以及骡子——北京的车一律用骡子——的辔头鞍韂的讲究程度辨认出来。中国北方的骡子是极壮健的牲口，比中国的马（不过是一种小马）要好得多。

===Original===
The Seventh Prince (Prince Ch’un) must have been a most interesting personality. He was brother to the Emperor Hsien-Feng, the husband of the present Empress Dowager; and his wife, the mother of the present Emperor, was Her Majesty’s sister. This Prince was a valued friend of the two Empresses, the present Empress Dowager and She of the Eastern Palace, while they were Co-Regents during the minority of the late and a part of that of the present Emperor, and he remained, up to the time of his death, one of the most trusted advisers of the Regency. He was recognized by foreigners, as well as by the Chinese, to be an enlightened Prince as well as a man of fine character. The esteem in which he was held may have had something to do with the choice of his second son as the Successor of the late Emperor Tung-Chih, who died childless. The Chinese Emperors and their Council may choose the Successor to the Throne. If there be but one son, he is chosen as the next Heir; if there be a number, a selection may be made from them of the one seeming to be most suited for the exalted position. If there be no sons, the Successor is chosen from the nephews without reference to their age or to their being the sons of an elder or younger brother. The present Emperor’s Father, Prince Ch’un, was the seventh brother of the Emperor Hsien-Feng, hence his Chinese name of “Seventh Prince.”

===Chinese===
七王爷（醇亲王）想必是个极有趣的人物。他是咸丰帝的兄弟——咸丰帝即当今皇太后的丈夫；而他的妻子，也就是当今皇上的生母，则是皇太后的妹妹。在先帝以及当今皇上幼年的部分时日里，两宫太后——即当今皇太后与东宫太后——共同垂帘听政，这位王爷是两位太后极为倚重的挚友；直至他临终之前，他仍是摄政体制中最受信任的谋臣之一。无论外国人还是中国人，都公认他是一位开明的王爷，品行端方。他所享有的声望，或许与他的次子被选为先帝同治——同治帝无嗣而崩——的继嗣有些关联。中国的皇上及其枢臣可以择定皇储。若仅有一子，便以此子为嗣；若有数子，则从中择一最宜此大位者。若无子嗣，则从侄辈中选立储君，不拘年齿长幼，亦不论是兄长之子还是幼弟之子。当今皇上之父醇亲王，是咸丰帝的第七个兄弟，因此中国人称他为「七王爷」。
