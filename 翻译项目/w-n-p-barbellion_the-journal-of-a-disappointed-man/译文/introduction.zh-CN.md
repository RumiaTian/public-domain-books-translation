## Introduction / 导言
===Original===

Your egoist, like the solitary beasts, lives only for himself; your altruist declares that he lives only for others; for either there may be success or failure but for neither can there be tragedy. For even if the altruist meets nothing but ingratitude, what has he to complain of? His premises abolish his grounds of complaint. But both egoist and altruist are philosophical abstractions. The human being by nature and necessity is neither egoist nor altruist; he trims a difficult course between the two; for the most part we are, within the limits of our powers of expression, *egotists*, and our desire is to think and if possible talk and write about this marvellous experiment of ourselves, with all the world﻿—or as much as we can conveniently assemble﻿—for audience. There is variety in our styles. Some drape the central figure; some let it rather appear than call attention to it; some affect a needless frankness: “*I* am an egotist, mind you, and I pretend nothing else”; some by adopting a pose with accessories do at least develop so great and passionate an interest in the accessories as to generalise and escape more or less completely from self. An egotism like an eggshell is a thing from which to escape; the art of life is that escape. The fundamental art of life is to recover the sense of that great self-forgetful continuous life from which we have individually budded off. Many people have done this through religion, which begins with a tremendous clamour to some saviour god or other to recognise us and ends in our recognition of him; or through science, when your egotist begins with: “Behold me! I, I your humble servant, am a scientific man, devoted to the clear statement of truth,” and ends with so passionate a statement of truth that self is forgotten altogether.

你的自我主义者，如同独居的野兽，只为自己而活；你的利他主义者宣称自己只为他人而活；两者都可能成功或失败，但谁也不可能遭遇悲剧。因为利他主义者即便碰到的全是忘恩负义，他又有什么可抱怨的呢？他的前提本身就取消了他抱怨的根据。可是自我主义者和利他主义者都是哲学上的抽象物。人出于天性和必然，既非自我主义者，也非利他主义者；他在两者之间勉力折中而行；在多数情况下，在我们表达能力的限度之内，我们是*自我中心者*，我们渴望思考——如果可能，还要谈论、书写——这场名为“我们自己”的奇妙实验，并且以全世界——或以我们方便召集来的尽可能多的听众——为观众。风格因人而异。有人给主角披上帷幔；有人宁让它自行显现，而不去点破；有人故意摆出一副多余的坦白：“请注意，*我*是个自我中心者，别的我什么都不装”；有人则采用一个带着行头的姿态，至少当真对那些行头培养起了浓烈而火热的兴趣，以致把自己普遍化，或多或少整个儿从自我里逃了出去。自我中心主义像蛋壳一样，是一件需要从中逃出的东西；生活的艺术就是那场逃亡。生活的根本艺术，是重新找回那种忘我的、绵延不绝的大生命的感觉——我们每个人都是从它身上萌芽分离出来的。许多人经由宗教做到了这一点：宗教以向某位救主神明大声呼求“认下我们”开始，以我们认下他告终；或者经由科学：你的自我主义者开头说：“看哪我！我，我，您的谦卑的仆人，是个科学人，献身于真理的明晰陈述”，而结尾时对真理的陈述如此热烈，自我被忘得一千二净。

In this diary of an intensely egotistical young naturalist, tragically caught by the creeping approach of death, we have one of the most moving records of the youthful aspects of our universal struggle. We begin with one of those bright schoolboys that most of us like to fancy we once were, that many of us have come to love as sons or nephews or younger brothers, and this youngster is attracted by natural science, by the employments of the naturalist and by the thought of being himself some day a naturalist. From the very beginning we find in this diary the three qualities, from the narrowest to broadest. “Observe me,” he says to himself, “I am observing nature.” There is the self-conscious, self-centred boy. But he also says “I am observing nature!” And at moments comes the clear light. He forgets himself in the twilight cave with the bats or watching the starlings in the evening sky, he becomes just you and I and the mind of mankind gathering knowledge. And the diary, as the keen edge of untimely fate cuts down into the sensitive tissue, shows us presently, after outcries and sorrow and darkness of spirit, the habits of the observer rising to the occasion. Not for him, he realises, are the long life, the honours of science, the Croonian lecture, the listening Royal Society, one’s memory embalmed in specific or generic names, the sure place in the temple of fame, that once filled his boyish dreams. But here is something close at hand to go on observing manfully to the end, in which self may be forgotten, and that is his own tormented self, with desire still great and power and hope receding. “I will go on with this diary,” I read between the lines. “You shall have at least one specimen, carefully displayed and labelled. Here is a recorded unhappiness. When you talk about life and the rewards of life and the justice of life and its penalties, what you say must square with this.”

在这部日记里，一位自我中心得无以复加的年轻博物学家，被死亡悄悄的进逼悲剧性地攫住；我们由此得到的，是关于我们共同挣扎的青春面目的最动人的记录之一。开卷是那样一个明亮的学童——我们多数人都乐于想象自己曾经是那样，我们许多人已经作为儿子、侄儿或弟弟爱上过那样的人——这个少年为自然科学所吸引，为博物学家的营生所吸引，为有朝一日自己也成为博物学家这一念头所吸引。从最初起，我们就在这部日记里发现三种品质，从最窄到最宽。“看着我，”他对自己说，“我正在观察自然。”这是那个自觉的、以自我为中心的男孩。但他也在说：“我正在观察自然啊！”而有些时刻，清澈的光降临了。他在暮色的洞穴里与蝙蝠相处时，在仰望傍晚天空中的椋鸟时忘记了自己，他变成了纯粹的你、我和人类采集知识的心灵。而当命运之刃过早的锋刃切进这敏感的组织，这部日记随即向我们表明，经过呼号、悲伤与心灵的黑暗之后，观察者的习性挺身而起，应付了这一局面。他明白，长寿、科学的荣衔、克罗尼安讲演、侧耳倾听的皇家学会、自己的名字化为种名属名而不朽、名声殿堂里那个牢靠的席位——那一切曾经填满他少年梦境的东西，都不属于他了。但眼前就有一样东西，可供他坚毅地观察到底，在其中自我可以被忘却——那就是他自己那个受折磨的自我，欲望依然盛大，而力量与希望在节节退却。“我要把这部日记继续下去，”我在字里行间读到，“你们至少将得到一件标本，经精心陈列，贴好标签。这里记录着一场不幸。当你们谈论生命、生命的报偿、生命的公道与它的刑罚时，你们所说的一切，必须与这份记录对得上。”

===Original===

Such is what we have here. It will be going beyond the necessities of this preface to expatiate upon a certain thread of unpremeditated and exquisite beauty that runs through the story this diary tells. To all sensitive readers it will be plain enough, and those who cannot see it plain do not deserve to have it underlined for them, that, still unseeing, they may pretend to see. Nor need we dilate upon the development of the quality of this diary from the rather fussy egotism of the earlier half. But it may be well to add a few explanatory facts that the opening chapters rather take for granted. Barbellion began life at a material as well as a physical disadvantage; neither of his parents were sturdy people, his mother died at last of constitutional heart weakness, and his father belonged to that most unfortunate class, the poor educated, who live lives of worry in straitened circumstances. Barbellion’s father was a newspaper reporter in a west country town, his income rarely exceeded a couple of hundred pounds a year; the educational facilities of the place were poor, and young Barbellion had to get such learning as he could as a day boy at a small private school, his father supplementing this meagre training and presently taking him on as an apprentice reporter. How the passion for natural science arose does not appear in this diary; we already find the naturalist formed in the first schoolboy entries. An uncle, a chemist, seems to have encouraged the tendency, and to have given him textbooks and other help. Somehow at any rate he acquired a considerable amount of knowledge; by the time he was eighteen he was already publishing quite excellent observations of his own in such periodicals as the *Zoologist*, and by the time he was twenty he could secure an appointment as assistant naturalist to the director of a well-known marine biological station. It was a success, as the reader will learn, gained only to be renounced. His father was ill and he had to stand by his family; our economical country cannot afford to make biologists out of men who can earn a living as hack reporters. Poverty and science are sisters wherever the flag of Britain waves; for how could the rich live if we wasted money on that sort of thing?

我们手头的便是这样一件东西。要在这篇序言的必要篇幅之外，再铺陈那条贯穿这部日记所述故事的、不期然而至的、精美绝伦的线，未免多余。对一切敏感的读者，它都足够显豁；看不出它的人，也不配让人替他们画线，好让他们视而不见地假装看见。也不必细述这部日记品质的演进，如何从前半部那种忙忙叨叨的自我主义里长出来。但补几件开卷各章默认了的事实，或许不无益处。巴贝利恩（Barbellion）起步时，物质上和身体上都处于劣势；他的双亲都不是健旺的人，母亲最终死于体质性心力衰竭，父亲则属于最不幸的一类人——贫穷的读书人——在拮据的境况里过着忧心的日子。巴贝利恩的父亲是西部一座小镇的报纸记者，年收入很少超过两百英镑；当地的教育条件很差，少年巴贝利恩只能在一所小小的私立走读学校里学到什么算什么，父亲再给这点贫乏的训练加加餐，随后把他收作见习记者。对自然科学的热情是怎样发生的，这部日记里并无交代；在最早的学童条目里，博物学家已经成形了。他的一位当药剂师的舅舅，似乎曾鼓励这一倾向，送他教科书，还帮过别的忙。无论如何，他毕竟攒下了可观的知识；到十八岁，他已经在《动物学家》（*Zoologist*）这类刊物上发表相当出色的自采观察；到二十岁，他谋得了一个职位，做一家知名海洋生物站站长的助理博物学家。读者将会知道，这是一场成功，成功到手，只是为了放弃。父亲病了，他必须站在家人一边；我们节俭的祖国，舍不得把能靠苦力记者糊口的人造成生物学家。凡是英国国旗飘扬之处，贫穷与科学都是姐妹；那种事上要是浪费钱，富人还怎么活呢？

But the dream was not altogether abandoned, and in 1911 Barbellion got a post, one of the dozen or so of rare and coveted opportunities to toil in a scientific atmosphere that our Empire affords; he secured an assistantship at the Natural History Museum, South Kensington, to which a living wage was attached, a fair equivalent to a reporter’s earnings. The rest of the story needs no helping out. Let me only add that since 1911 Barbellion, in spite of his steadily diminishing strength, has published articles in both British and American periodicals, that entirely justify the statement that in him biological science loses one of the most promising of its recent recruits. His scientific work is not only full and exact but it has those literary qualities, the grace, the power of handling, the breadth of reference, which have always distinguished the best English biological work, and which mark off at once the true scientific man from the mere collector and recorder of items. With this much introduction Barbellion may be left to tell the tragedy of his hopes and of the dark, unforeseen, unforeseeable, and inexplicable fate that has overtaken him.

H. G. Wells.

但梦想并未全然放弃。1911年，巴贝利恩得到了一个职位——大英帝国所能提供的、寥寥十来个在科学氛围里劳作的稀罕而令人艳羡的机会之一；他谋得了南肯辛顿（South Kensington）自然历史博物馆（the Natural History Museum）的一个助理职位，附有一份能糊口的薪水，与记者的报酬大致相当。故事的其余部分无需赘言。我只想补一句：自1911年以来，巴贝利恩尽管体力持续衰减，仍在英美两国的刊物上发表了若干文章，足证这句话毫不过分：生物科学在他身上失去了它近年最有前途的新人之一。他的科学工作不仅充实、精确，而且具备那些文学品质——那份风致、那种驾驭的功力、那种援引的广度——它们历来为最优秀的英国生物学著作所独具，并且立此存照，把真正的科学人与单纯的条目采集者、记录者区分开来。有了这点引言，就可以让巴贝利恩自己来讲述他希望的悲剧，讲述那个攫住了他的、黑暗的、未及预见、无从预见、也无法解释的命运了。

H. G. 威尔斯（H. G. Wells）
