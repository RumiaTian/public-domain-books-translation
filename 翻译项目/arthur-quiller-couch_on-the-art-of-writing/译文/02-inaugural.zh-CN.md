## Lecture I Inaugural / I 就职讲演

===Original===
Wednesday, January 29, 1913

===Chinese===
1913 年 1 月 29 日，星期三

===Original===
In all the long quarrel set between philosophy and poetry I know of nothing finer, as of nothing more pathetically hopeless, than Plato's return upon himself in his last dialogue "The Laws." There are who find that dialogue (left unrevised) insufferably dull, as no doubt it is without form and garrulous. But I think they will read it with a new tolerance, maybe even with a touch of feeling, if upon second thoughts they recognise in its twisting and turnings, its prolixities and repetitions, the scruples of an old man who, knowing that his time in this world is short, would not go out of it pretending to know more than he does, and even in matters concerning which he was once very sure has come to divine that, after all, as Renan says, "*La Verité consiste dans les nuances.*" Certainly "the mind's dark cottage battered and decayed" does in that last dialogue admit some wonderful flashes,

> From Heaven descended to the low-roofed house
 

 Of Socrates,

or rather to that noble "banquet-hall deserted" which aforetime had entertained Socrates.

===Chinese===
在哲学与诗之间那场旷日持久的争执中，我还不知道有什么比柏拉图在其最后一部对话录《法律篇》（The Laws）中的返身自省更精彩，也不知道有什么比它更令人怅惘地无望。有人觉得那部对话录（未经修订）沉闷得难以忍受；诚然，它无疑结构松散、絮絮叨叨。但我以为，倘若他们经过再思，能在它的迂回曲折、它的冗沓重复之中，辨认出一位老人的审慎——他自知在世的时日无多，不愿在离开这个世界时佯装懂得比实际更多，甚至对那些他曾经十分有把握的事情，也已渐渐悟到：毕竟，正如勒南（Renan）所言：「*La Verité consiste dans les nuances.*」（真理存在于精微的差异之中。）——那么，他们读它时便会带着一种新的宽容，或许还带着一丝感动。诚然，「心灵幽暗的小屋，饱经摧折而颓败」，在那最后的一部对话录里也确实接纳过几道奇妙的光：

> 自天而降，降临于低檐的屋宇，
 

 苏格拉底的居所，

或者不如说，降临到那座高贵的「空落的宴会之厅」——往昔它曾款待过苏格拉底。

===Original===
Suffer me, Mr. Vice-Chancellor and Gentlemen, before reaching my text, to remind you of the characteristically beautiful setting. The place is Crete, and the three interlocutors—Cleinias a Cretan, Megillus a Lacedaemonian, and an Athenian stranger—have joined company on a pilgrimage to the cave and shrine of Zeus, from whom Minos, first lawgiver of the island, had reputedly derived not only his parentage but much parental instruction. Now the day being hot, even scorching, and the road from Knossos to the Sacred Cave a long one, our three pilgrims, who have foregathered as elderly men, take it at their leisure, and propose to beguile it with talk upon Minos and his laws. "Yes, and on the way," promises the Cretan, "we shall come to cypress-groves exceedingly tall and fair, and to green meadows, where we may repose ourselves and converse." "Good," assents the Athenian. "Ay, very good indeed, and better still when we arrive at them. Let us push on."

===Chinese===
副校长先生、诸位先生，容我在进入正题之前，先提醒诸位回顾它那一段特有的优美场景。地点在克里特岛；三位对话者——克里特人克莱尼亚斯（Cleinias）、拉栖代蒙人墨吉洛斯（Megillus），以及一位雅典异乡人——结伴同行，踏上朝圣之路，前往宙斯（Zeus）的洞窟与神庙；相传岛上第一位立法者弥诺斯（Minos）不仅从宙斯那里得出血脉，还受过他许多父亲般的教导。时值天气炎热，甚至灼热逼人，从克诺索斯（Knossos）到圣窟的路又长，我们三位以暮年相逢的朝圣者便从容而行，打算用谈论弥诺斯及其法律来消磨旅途。「好啊，而且一路上，」那位克里特人许诺道，「我们还会走过高得出奇、秀美非常的柏树林，来到绿茵的草地上，可以在那里歇息、交谈。」「好，」雅典人应道，「是啊，实在好得很，等真到了那儿还要更好。我们往前赶吧。」

===Original===
So they proceed. I have said that all three are elderly men; that is, men who have had their opportunities, earned their wages, and so nearly earned their discharge that now, looking back on life, they can afford to see Man for what he really is—at his best a noble plaything for the gods. Yet they look forward, too, a little wistfully. They are of the world, after all, and nowise so tired of it, albeit disillusioned, as to have lost interest in the game or in the young who will carry it on. So Minos and his laws soon get left behind, and the talk (as so often befalls with Plato) is of the perfect citizen and how to train him—of education, in short; and so, as ever with Plato, we are back at length upon the old question which he could never get out of his way—What to do with the poets?

===Chinese===
于是他们继续前行。我说过，三人都已届暮年；也就是说，都是曾经有过自己的机会、挣得了自己的报酬、几乎也挣得了解职的人，如今回望一生，他们能够坦然按人的本来面目看待人——人至好也只是诸神的一件高贵玩物。然而他们也仍怀着一点眷恋向前看。他们毕竟身在尘世，虽已幻灭，却远没有倦怠到对这场游戏、对将把它继续下去的年轻人失去兴趣的地步。于是弥诺斯和他的法律很快被抛在脑后，话题（这在柏拉图是常有的事）转到了完美的公民以及如何培养他——简言之，转到了教育；于是，一如既往，柏拉图终于又回到了那个他始终绕不开的老问题上——拿诗人怎么办？

===Original===
It scarcely needs to be said that the Athenian has taken hold of the conversation, and that the others are as wax in his hands. "O Athenian stranger," Cleinias addresses him—"inhabitant of Attica I will not call you, for you seem to deserve rather the name of Athene herself, because you go back to first principles." Thus complimented, the stranger lets himself go. Yet somehow he would seem to have lost speculative nerve.

===Chinese===
几乎不必说，自然是那位雅典人执掌了谈话，其余两位在他手中如蜡一般柔软。「雅典的异乡人啊，」克莱尼亚斯这样称呼他——「我不愿只称你为阿提卡的居民，因为你实在更配得上雅典娜本人的名号，因为你事事追本溯源。」受了这样的恭维，异乡人放言而谈。然而不知怎的，他似乎已经失去了思辨的锐气。

===Original===
It was all very well in the *Republic*, the ideal State, to be bold and declare for banishing poetry altogether. But elderly men have given up pursuing ideals; they have "seen too many leaders of revolt." Our Athenian is driving now at practice (as we say), at a well-governed State realisable on earth; and after all it is hard to chase out the poets, especially if you yourself happen to be something of a poet at heart. Hear, then, the terms on which, after allowing that comedies may be performed, but only by slaves and hirelings, he proceeds to allow serious poetry.

===Chinese===
在《理想国》（*The Republic*）里，在那个理想的邦国里，尽可以放言无忌，主张把诗整个驱逐出境。但老年人已经放弃了追逐理想；他们「见过太多揭竿而起的领袖」。我们这位雅典人如今追求的是实践（如我们常说的），是一个在尘世间可以实现的良好治理的邦国；况且诗人终究难以驱逐净尽，尤其当你自己心底里恰好也多少是个诗人的时候。那么，且听他开出什么条件：在允许喜剧可以上演——但只能由奴隶和受雇者搬演——之后，他又如何进而允许严肃的诗。

===Original===
> And if any of the serious poets, as they are termed, who write tragedy, come to us and say—"O strangers, may we go to your city and country, or may we not, and shall we bring with us our poetry? What is your will about these matters?"—how shall we answer the divine men? I think that our answer should be as follows:—
> "Best of strangers," we will say to them, "we also, according to our ability, are tragic poets, and our tragedy is the best and noblest: for our whole state is an imitation of the best and noblest life. … You are poets and we are poets, both makers of the same strains, rivals and antagonists in the noblest of dramas, which true law alone can perfect, as our hope is. Do not then suppose that we shall all in a moment allow you to erect your stage in the Agora, and introduce the fair voices of your actors, speaking above our own, and permit you to harangue our women and children and the common people in language other than our own, and very often the opposite of our own. For a State would be mad which gave you this license, until the magistrates had determined whether your poetry might be recited and was fit for publication or not. Wherefore, O ye sons and scions of the softer Muses! first of all show your songs to the Magistrates and let them compare them with our own, and if they are the same or better, we will give you a chorus; but if not, then, my friends, we cannot."

===Chinese===
> 倘若那些所谓的严肃诗人，即写作悲剧的诗人，来到我们面前说——『外乡人啊，我们可不可以到你们的城邦和国土来？可不可以？我们要不要把我们的诗带来？这些事你们意下如何？』——我们该怎样回答这些神明一般的人呢？我想我们的回答应当如下：——
> 「最好的外乡人，」我们要对他们说，「我们也各尽其能，是悲剧诗人，而我们的悲剧是最美好、最高贵的：因为我们整个城邦就是对最美好、最高贵生活的模仿。……你们是诗人，我们也是诗人，都是同一批曲调的制作者，在这出至高的戏剧里互为对手与竞敌——我们指望唯有真正的法律能使这出戏臻于完美。所以，不要以为我们会转眼之间就允许你们在市场（Agora）里搭起戏台，领进你们演员的美妙嗓音，使之盖过我们自己的声音，允许你们用不同于我们、而且往往与我们相反的语言，向我们的妇女、儿童和平民高谈阔论。因为一个城邦若在官长们判定你们的诗可否朗诵、是否宜于发表之前就给你们这份许可，那它准是疯了。是以，柔媚的缪斯（Muses）之子嗣啊！首先把你们的歌拿给官长们看，让他们拿来同我们的歌比较；倘若它们不相上下或者更好，我们就给你们一支歌队；倘若不然，那么，朋友们，我们就不能给了。」

===Original===
Lame conclusion! Impotent compromise! How little applicable, at all events, to our Commonwealth! though, to be sure (you may say) we possess a relic of it in His Majesty's Licenser of Plays. As you know, there has been so much heated talk of late over the composition of the County Magistracy; yet I give you a countryman's word, Sir, that I have heard many names proposed for the Commission of the Peace, and on many grounds, but never one on the ground that its owner had a conservative taste in verse!

===Chinese===
多么跛弱的结论！多么无能的妥协！无论如何，它对我们的国家是多么不适用！尽管（你们也许会说）我们毕竟还保有它的一件遗存，那就是国王陛下的戏剧许可官。诸位知道，近来关于郡治安法官团的人选已有过许多激烈的议论；但先生，我以一个乡下人的名义向您担保：我听过许多名字被提名进入治安委员会，理由也各式各样，却从来没有任何一条理由是：此君趣味保守，恪守诗的正统！

===Original===
Nevertheless, as Plato saw, we must deal with these poets somehow. It is possible (though not, I think, likely) that in the ideal State there would be no Literature, as it is certain there would be no professors of it; but since its invention men have never been able to rid themselves of it for any length of time. *Tamen usque recurrit.* They may forbid Apollo, but still he comes leading his choir, the Nine:—

> Ἂχλητος μἐν ἔγωγε μἑνοιμἱ χεν' ἐς δἑ χαλευ'ντων
 

 Θαρσἠσας Μοἱσαιοι σὺν ἁμετἑραισιν ἱχοἱμαν.

===Chinese===
然而，正如柏拉图所见，这些诗人无论如何总得设法对付。在理想邦国里，可能不会有文学——尽管我认为这不太可能——正如可以肯定那里不会有文学教授一样；但自文学被发明以来，人们从来没能长久地摆脱它。*Tamen usque recurrit.*（然而它总是卷土重来。）人们尽可以禁绝阿波罗，但他依然会率领他的歌队、那九位缪斯前来：——

> Ἂχλητος μἐν ἔγωγε μἑνοιμἱ χεν' ἐς δἑ χαλευ'ντων
 

 Θαρσἠσας Μοἱσαιοι σὺν ἁμετἑραισιν ἱχοἱμαν.

===Original===
And he may challenge us English boldly! For since Chaucer, at any rate, he and his train have never been *ἂχλητοι* to us—least of all here in Cambridge.

===Chinese===
而且他大可以向英国人公然挑战！因为至少自乔叟（Chaucer）以来，他和他的随从对我们从来不是 *ἂχλητοι*（不受欢迎的）——在剑桥这里尤其不是。

===Original===
Nay, we know that he should be welcome. Cardinal Newman, proposing the idea of a University to the Roman Catholics of Dublin, lamented that the English language had not, like the Greek, "some definite words to express, simply and generally, intellectual proficiency or perfection, such as 'health,' as used with reference to the animal frame, and 'virtue,' with reference to our moral nature." Well, it is a reproach to us that we do not possess the term: and perhaps again a reproach to us that our attempts at it—the word "culture" for instance—have been apt to take on some soil of controversy, some connotative damage from over-preaching on the one hand and impatience on the other. But we do earnestly desire the thing. We do prize that grace of intellect which sets So-and-so in our view as "a scholar and a gentleman." We do wish as many sons of this University as may be to carry forth that lifelong stamp from her precincts; and—this is my point—from our notion of such a man the touch of literary grace cannot be excluded. I put to you for a test Lucian's description of his friend Demonax—

> His way was like other people's; he mounted no high horse; he was just a man and a citizen. He indulged in no Socratic irony. But his discourse was full of Attic grace; those who heard it went away neither disgusted by servility, nor repelled by ill-tempered censure, but on the contrary lifted out of themselves by charity, and encouraged to more orderly, contented, hopeful lives.

===Chinese===
不，我们知道他理应受到欢迎。纽曼枢机（Cardinal Newman）向都柏林的罗马天主教徒倡言大学理念时，曾感叹英语不像希腊语那样，拥有「一些明确的字眼，可以简单而概括地表达智识上的造诣或完善，正如『健康』一词用于动物的肌体，『德性』一词用于我们的道德本性」。唉，我们缺少这样一个词，这是我们的一个耻辱；而或许同样可耻的是，我们朝这个方向所作的尝试——比如「文化」（culture）一词——总是容易沾染争论的尘垢，因一方的过度说教与另一方的不耐烦而在词义上蒙受损害。但我们确实真心渴求这个东西。我们确实珍视那种智识的优雅，它使某君在我们眼中成为「一位学者，一位绅士」。我们确实希望这所大学尽可能多的学子，从她的庭园里带走这枚终身不磨的印记；而且——这正是我的要点——在这种人的观念里，文学优雅的那一笔是排除不掉的。我请诸位拿一段话来作检验：琉善（Lucian）对他朋友德谟纳克斯（Demonax）的描写——

> 他的举止与常人无异；他不装腔作势；他就是一个普通的人、一个公民。他不行苏格拉底式的反讽。但他的谈吐充满阿提卡式的优雅；听过他谈话的人离开时，既不因奴态而心生厌恶，也不因恶声恶气的指摘而感到反感，反而被仁爱提升到超乎自身的境界，受到鼓舞去过更有秩序、更知足、更有希望的生活。

===Original===
I put it to you, Sir, that Lucian needs not to say another word, but we know that Demonax had loved letters, and partly by aid of them had arrived at being such a man. No; by consent of all, Literature is a nurse of noble natures, and right reading makes a full man in a sense even better than Bacon's; not replete, but complete rather, to the pattern for which Heaven designed him. In this conviction, in this hope, public spirited men endow Chairs in our Universities, sure that Literature is a good thing if only we can bring it to operate on young minds.

===Chinese===
先生，我要向诸位指出：琉善无须再多说一个字，我们就知道德谟纳克斯热爱过文学，而且部分正是借助于文学，他才达到了如此的人格。是的；众所周知，文学是高贵天性的乳母，而正当的阅读造就一个「充实的人」，这层意思甚至比培根（Bacon）所说的更好——不是填得饱足（replete），而是臻于完整（complete），成为上天为他设计的那个样子。正是怀着这样的信念、这样的希望，热心公益的人们在我们的大学里捐设讲席，深信文学是件好东西，只要我们能使它作用于年轻的心灵。

===Original===
That he has in him some power to guide such operation a man must believe before accepting such a Chair as this. And now, Sir, the terrible moment is come when your *ζἑνος* must render some account—I will not say of himself, for that cannot be attempted—but of his business here. Well, first let me plead that while you have been infinitely kind to the stranger, feasting him and casting a gown over him, one thing not all your kindness has been able to do. With precedents, with traditions such as other professors enjoy, you could not furnish him. The Chair is a new one, or almost new, and for the present would seem to float in the void, like Muhammad's coffin. Wherefore, being one who (in my Lord Chief Justice Crewe's phrase) would "take hold of a twig or twine-thread to uphold it"; being also prone (with Bacon) to believe that "the counsels to which Time hath not been called, Time will not ratify"; I do assure you that, had any legacy of guidance been discovered among the papers left by my predecessor, it would have been eagerly welcomed and as piously honoured. O, trust me, Sir!—if any design for this Chair of English Literature had been left by Dr. Verrall, it is not I who would be setting up any new stage in your agora! But in his papers—most kindly searched for me by Mrs. Verrall—no such design can be found. He was, in truth, a stricken man when he came to the Chair, and of what he would have built we can only be sure that, had it been this or had it been that, it would infallibly have borne the impress of one of the most beautiful minds of our generation. The gods saw otherwise; and for me, following him, I came to a trench and stretched my hands to a shade.

===Chinese===
一个人在接受这样一个讲席之前，必须先相信自己身上多少有点引导这种作用的力量。而现在，先生，可怕的时刻到了，您的这位 *ζἑνος*（异乡人）必须有所交代——我不说交代他自己，那是无从尝试的——只交代他在这里的职守。那么，首先请容我申明：诸位虽然对这位异乡人恩礼无比，既设宴款待他，又为他披上学袍，但有一件事是诸位的厚爱所无能为力的。你们无法为他提供其他教授所享有的先例与传统。这个讲席是新设的，或者说几乎是新的，眼下仿佛悬在虚空之中，就像穆罕默德的棺椁。因此，作为一个（用克鲁首席大法官大人的话说）愿意「抓住一根细枝或麻线来支撑它」的人；又作为一个倾向于（同培根一起）相信「未曾请时间与闻的谋划，时间不会予以批准」的人；我向诸位保证：倘若在我前任留下的文件中找得出任何指路的遗泽，它必定会受到热切的欢迎和同样虔敬的尊重。啊，请相信我，先生！——倘若弗拉尔博士（Dr. Verrall）曾为这个英国文学讲席留下过任何规划，那么在你们的市场上另搭一座新戏台的，断不会是我！但在他的文件里——弗拉尔夫人（Mrs. Verrall）好心替我翻检过了——找不到任何这样的规划。事实上，他就任讲席时已是一位身罹重病的人；至于他本会建起什么，我们只能确定一点：无论建的是这个还是那个，它都必然会带着我们这一代最优美的心灵之一的印记。诸神另有安排；至于我，跟在他身后，来到一道墓壕旁，向一个幽魂伸出了双手。

===Original===
For me, then, if you put questions concerning the work of this Chair, I must take example from the artist in Don Quixote, who being asked what he was painting, answered modestly, "That is as it may turn out." The course is uncharted, and for sailing directions I have but these words of your Ordinance:

> It shall be the duty of the professor to deliver courses of lectures on English Literature from the age of Chaucer onwards, and otherwise to promote, so far as may be in his power, the study in the University of the subject of English Literature.

===Chinese===
那么，至于我：倘若诸位就这个讲席的工作提出问题，我只得效法《堂吉诃德》（Don Quixote）里那位画家——有人问他画的是什么，他谦逊地答道：「画出来是什么就是什么。」这门课业没有海图可循，至于航行的指令，我只有诸位《条例》（Ordinance）中的这几句话：

> 教授之职责，在于就自乔叟时代以来的英国文学开设系列讲座，并在其力所能及的范围内，以其他方式促进该大学内英国文学学科之研究。

===Original===
And I never even knew that English Literature had a "subject"; or, rather, supposed it to have several! To resume:

> The professor shall treat this subject on literary and critical rather than on philological and linguistic lines:

===Chinese===
而我从来根本不知道英国文学还有一个「学科」；或者毋宁说，我以为它有好几个呢！言归正传：

> 教授讲授此学科，应循文学与批评的路径，而非语文学与语言学的路径：

===Original===
