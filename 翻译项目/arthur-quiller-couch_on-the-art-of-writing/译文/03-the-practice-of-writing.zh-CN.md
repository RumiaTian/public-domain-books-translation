## Lecture II The Practice of Writing / II 写作的实践

===Original===
Wednesday, February 12

===Chinese===
2 月 12 日，星期三

===Original===
We found, Gentlemen, towards the close of our first lecture, that the argument had drawn us, as by a double chain, up to the edge of a bold leap, over which I deferred asking you to take the plunge with me. Yet the plunge must be taken, and today I see nothing for it but to harden our hearts.

===Chinese===
诸位先生，我们在第一讲临近结束时发现，论证已仿佛被一条双重锁链牵引着，把我们一路拖到了一处大胆跳跃的崖边；当时我暂且没有请诸位随我一同纵身跃下。然而这一跃终究必须完成，今天我看不到别的办法，唯有横下心来。

===Original===
Well, then, I propose to you that, English Literature being (as we agreed) an Art, with a living and therefore improvable language for its medium or vehicle, a part—and no small part—of our business is *to practise it*. Yes, I seriously propose to you that here in Cambridge we *practise writing*: that we practise it not only for our own improvement, but to make, or at least try to make, appropriate, perspicuous, accurate, persuasive writing a recognisable hallmark of anything turned out by our English School. By all means let us study the great writers of the past for their own sakes; but let us study them for our guidance; that we, in our turn, having (it is to be hoped) something to say in our span of time, say it worthily, not dwindling out the large utterance of Shakespeare or of Burke. Portraits of other great ones look down on you in your college halls: but while you are young and sit at the brief feast, what avails their serene gaze if it do not lift up your hearts and movingly persuade you to match your manhood to its inheritance?

===Chinese===
那么，我向诸位提议：英国文学既（如我们已同意的）是一门艺术，以一种活着的、因而可改进的语言为其媒介或载体，那么我们职责的一部分——而且不是小的一部分——便是*实践它*。是的，我郑重其事地向诸位提议：就在这剑桥，我们*练习写作*：练习它不仅是为了我们自身的精进，更是为了使——或者至少努力使——得体、明晰、准确、有说服力的写作，成为我们英文学科（English School）一切产出可资辨认的标志。让我们尽可以为往昔伟大作家自身之故去研读他们；但也要为求指引而研读他们；好让我们轮到自己时，（但愿如此）在我们这段时限里有话可说，并且说得有价值，而不是让莎士比亚（Shakespeare）或伯克（Burke）的宏声巨响在我们口中渐弱渐灭。别的伟人画像在你们学院大厅里俯视着你们：但趁你们还年轻、还坐在这短促的宴席上，倘若他们安详的凝视不能振拔你们的心志、不能动人地劝服你们以大丈夫气概去承当那份遗产，它又有何益处？

===Original===
I protest, Gentlemen, that if our eyes had not been sealed, as with wax, by the pedagogues of whom I spoke a fortnight ago, this one habit of regarding our own literature as a *hortus siccus*, this our neglect to practise good writing as the constant auxiliary of an Englishman’s liberal education, would be amazing to you seated here today as it will be starkly incredible to the future historian of our times. Tell me, pray; if it concerned *Painting*—an art in which Englishmen boast a record far briefer, far less distinguished—what would you think of a similar acquiescence in the past, a like haste to presume the dissolution of aptitude and to close accounts, a like precipitancy to divorce us from the past, to rob the future of hope and even the present of lively interest? Consider, for reproof of these null men, the Discourses addressed (in a pedantic age, too) by Sir Joshua Reynolds to the members and students of the Royal Academy. He has (as you might expect) enough to say of Tintoretto, of Titian, of Caracci, and of the duty of studying their work with patience, with humility. But why does he exhort his hearers to con them?—Why, because he is all the time *driving at practice*. Hear how he opens his second Discourse (his first to the students). After congratulating the prize-winners of 1769, he desires “to lead them into such a course of study as may render their future progress answerable to their past improvement”; and the great man goes on:—

===Chinese===
诸位先生，我敢断言：倘若我们的眼睛不曾被我两周前提到的那批教书匠用蜡封住，那么，把我们自己的文学看作一座*蜡叶标本园*（*hortus siccus*）的这一个习惯，我们这种不肯把好好写作的实践当作英国人博雅教育恒久辅翼的疏忽，对今日坐在此地的你们将是骇人听闻之事，对未来的我们时代的历史学家也将全然难以置信。请告诉我：倘若事情关乎*绘画*——一门英国人引以自夸的历史远为更短、光彩远为更逊的艺术——对于类似的对往昔的默许、类似的急于断定才能已经消解并就此结账、类似的急切要使我们与往昔离异、夺走未来的希望乃至当下活泼的兴趣，你们会作何感想？为了斥责这些空洞之人，请看乔舒亚·雷诺兹爵士（Sir Joshua Reynolds）向皇家艺术学院（Royal Academy）院士与学员们发表的《讲演录》（Discourses）——而且那还是在一个迂腐的时代。他（如你们所能料想）对丁托列托（Tintoretto）、提香（Titian）、卡拉奇（Caracci）自有一番话好讲，也讲到以耐心、以谦卑研习他们作品的本分。可他为什么劝勉听众去揣摩他们？——为什么？因为他自始至终*着眼的都是实践*。听听他怎样开启第二篇讲演（他向学员发表的第一篇）。在祝贺过 1769 年的获奖者之后，他表示希望「引导他们进入这样一条学习之路，使日后的进步无愧于已往的精进」；这位伟人接着说道：——

===Original===
> I flatter myself that from the long experience I have had, and the necessary assiduity with which I have pursued these studies in which like you I have been engaged, I shall be acquitted of vanity in offering some hints to your consideration. They are indeed in a great degree founded upon my own mistakes in the same pursuit.…

===Chinese===
> 我自忖，凭着我多年的阅历，也凭着我与诸位一样投身这些研究以来所下的那份必不可少的苦功，在此提出几点建言供诸位斟酌，当可免于自负之讥。这些建言，确实大半建立在我同一追求中犯过的错误之上。……

===Original===
Mark the noble modesty of that! To resume—

===Chinese===
请留意这话里高贵的谦逊！言归正传——

===Original===
> In speaking to you of the Theory of the Art, I shall only consider it as it has relation to the method of your studies.

===Chinese===
> 在向诸位谈及这门艺术的理论时，我只打算就其与诸位学习方法的关系来考虑。

===Original===
And then he proceeds to preach the Old Masters.—But how?—why?—to what end? Does he recite lists of names, dates, with formulae concerning styles? He does nothing of the sort. Does he recommend his old masters for copying, then?—for mere imitation? Not a bit of it!—he comes down like a hammer on copying. Then for what, in fine, will he have them studied? Listen:—

===Chinese===
接着他就开始宣讲古代大师。——可是怎么讲？——为什么讲？——为的什么目标？他背诵一连串姓名、年代，再配上关于风格的公式套语吗？他根本没有这么做。那么，他是推荐临摹那些老大师了？——单纯模仿？一点也不！——他对临摹是抡起大锤狠狠地砸。那么归根结底，他到底要人研究他们为了什么？请听：——

===Original===
> The more extensive your acquaintance is with the works of those who have excelled, the more extensive will be your powers of invention.

===Chinese===
> 你们对卓越者作品的了解越广博，你们创造（invention）的才能便越广阔。

===Original===
Yes, of *invention*, your power to make something new:

===Chinese===
是的，说的是*创造*（invention），是做出新事物的能力：

===Original===
> —and what may appear still more like a paradox, *the more original will be your conceptions*.

===Chinese===
> ——而更近乎悖论的是，你们的构想（conceptions）就越富于原创性。

===Original===
There spake Sir Joshua Reynolds: and I call that the voice of a true Elder Brother. He, standing face to face with the young, thought of the old masters mainly as spiritual begetters of practice. And will anyone in this room tell me that what Reynolds said of painting is not today, for us, applicable to writing?

===Chinese===
乔舒亚·雷诺兹爵士如是说：我称之为一位真正的兄长（Elder Brother）的声音。他站在青年面前，主要把古代大师看作实践的精神生父。这间大厅里，可有谁愿意告诉我：雷诺兹论绘画的这番话，今天在我们身上、用在写作上，竟会不适用？

===Original===
We accept it of Greek and Latin. An old Sixth Form master once said to me, “You may give up Latin Verse for this term, if you will: but I warn you, no one can be a real scholar who does not constantly practise verse.” He was mistaken, belike. I hold, for my part, that in our Public Schools, we give up a quite disproportionate amount of time to “composition” (of Latin Prose especially) and starve the boys’ reading thereby. But at any rate we do give up a large share of the time to it. Then if we insist on this way with the tongues of Homer and Virgil, why do we avoid it with the tongue of Shakespeare, our own living tongue? I answer by quoting one of the simplest wisest sayings of Don Quixote (Gentlemen, you will easily, as time goes on, and we better our acquaintance, discover my favourite authors):—

===Chinese===
对希腊文与拉丁文，我们早已接受了这条道理。从前有一位教六年级（Sixth Form）的老教师对我说过：「你这一学期尽可以放弃拉丁诗（Latin Verse），如果你愿意的话：但我警告你，不经常练诗（verse）的人，成不了真正的学者。」他这话兴许错了。就我个人而言，我认为在我们的公学（Public Schools）里，我们花在「作文」（尤其是拉丁散文作文）上的时间比例失当，以致饿坏了男童们的阅读。但无论如何，我们确实把大量时间花在了这上面。那么，对荷马（Homer）与维吉尔（Virgil）的语言我们坚持以这种方式训练，为什么对自己的语言、莎士比亚的活语言，却回避这种方式呢？我引《堂吉诃德》（Don Quixote）里最朴实也最智慧的一句话来回答（诸位先生，随着时日推移、我们相知日深，你们会很容易发现我心爱的作者是哪些）：——

===Original===
> The great Homer wrote not in Latin, for he was a Greek; and Virgil wrote not in Greek, because he was a Latin. In brief, all the ancient poets wrote in the tongue which they sucked in with their mother’s milk, nor did they go forth to seek for strange ones to express the greatness of their conceptions: and, this being so, it should be a reason for the fashion to extend to all nations.

===Chinese===
> 伟大的荷马不用拉丁文写作，因为他是希腊人；维吉尔不用希腊文写作，因为他是拉丁人。简而言之，一切古代诗人都用他们随母乳一同吮吸而来的语言写作，并不另寻陌生的语言来表达他们构想的宏大；既然如此，这一风尚便理当扩及一切民族。

===Original===
Does the difference, then, perchance lie in ourselves? Will you tell me, “Oh, painting is a special art, whereas anyone can write prose passably well”? Can he, indeed?… Can *you*, sir? Nay, believe me, you are either an archangel or a very bourgeois gentleman indeed if you admit to having spoken English prose all your life without knowing it.

===Chinese===
那么，这差别莫非出在我们自己身上？你们会对我说：「哦，绘画是一门特殊技艺，而散文嘛，谁都能写得像样？」当真吗？……您呢，先生，您能吗？不，相信我，您若承认自己一生都在说英语散文而不自知，那您要么是位天使长，要么就是一位不折不扣的「贵人迷」（bourgeois gentleman）了。

===Original===
Indeed, when we try to speak prose without having practised it the result is apt to be worse than our own vernacular. How often have I heard some worthy fellow addressing a public audience!—say a Parliamentary candidate who believes himself a Liberal Home Ruler, and for the moment is addressing himself to meet some criticism of the financial proposals of a Home Rule Bill. His own vernacular would be somewhat as follows:—

===Chinese===
的确，未经练习便去讲散文，结果往往比我们自己的日常口语还要糟。我听过多少正派人物向公众演讲啊！——比如一位议会候选人，自认是自由党的地方自治派，此刻正打起精神，要去应对针对某《地方自治法案》财政条款的批评。他自己的日常口语大致会是这样的：——

===Original===
> Oh, rot! Give the Irish their heads and they’ll run straight enough. Look at the Boers, don’t you know. Not half such a decent sort as the Irish. Look at Irish horses, too. Eh? What?

===Chinese===
> 哦，扯淡！撒手让爱尔兰人自己跑，他们跑得够直溜了。瞧瞧布尔人（Boers），你是知道的。他们可远不如爱尔兰人体面。再瞧瞧爱尔兰的马。嗯？什么？

===Original===
But this, he is conscious, would hardly suit the occasion. He therefore amends it thus:—

===Chinese===
但他心里明白，这话不太合乎场合。于是他把它修改成这样：——

===Original===
> Mr. Chairman—er—as regards the financial proposals of His Majesty’s Government, I am of the deliberate—er—opinion that our national security—I may say, our Imperial security—our security as—er—a governing people—lies in trusting the Irish as we did in the—er—case of the Boers—H’m Mr. Gladstone, Mr. Chairman—Mr. Chairman, Mr. Gladstone—

===Chinese===
> 主席先生——呃——关于国王陛下政府的财政条款，我持有一个深思熟虑的——呃——意见：我国的国家安全——我可以说，我们的帝国安全——我们作为一个——呃——统治民族的安全——系于信任爱尔兰人，正如我们当年在——呃——布尔人一事上那样——哼，格莱斯顿先生（Mr. Gladstone），主席先生——主席先生，格莱斯顿先生——

===Original===
and so on. You perceive that the style is actually worse than in the sample quoted before; it has become flabby whereas that other was at any rate nervous? But now suppose that, having practised it, our candidate was able to speak like this:—

===Chinese===
如此等等。你们察觉到，这风格其实比前面引的那份样本更糟：那一段至少还筋肉遒健，这一段却已绵软疲沓，可不是吗？但现在且设想，我们的候选人经过练习，能够这样讲：——

===Original===
> “But what (says the Financier) is peace to us without money? Your plan gives us no revenue.” No? But it does—for it secures to the subject the power of Refusal, the first of all Revenues. Experience is a cheat, and fact is a liar, if this power in the subject of proportioning his grant, or of not granting at all, has not been found the richest mine of Revenue ever discovered by the skill or by the fortune of man. It does not indeed vote you £152,750 11s. 2¾d., nor any other paltry limited sum—but it gives you the strong box itself, the fund, the bank, from whence only revenues can arise among a people sensible of freedom: *Positâ luditur arcâ*.… Is this principle to be true in England, and false everywhere else? Is it not true in Ireland? Has it not hitherto been true in the Colonies? Why should you presume that in any country a body duly constituted for any function will neglect to perform its duty and abdicate its trust? Such a presumption would go against all Governments in all nations. But in truth this dread of penury of supply, from a free assembly, has no foundation in nature. For first, observe that, besides the desire which all men have naturally of supporting the honour of their own Government, that sense of dignity, and that security to property, which ever attend freedom, have a tendency to increase the stock of a free community. Most may be taken where most is accumulated. And what is the soil or climate where experience has not uniformly proved that the voluntary flow of heaped-up plenty, bursting from the weight of its own luxuriance, has ever run with a more copious stream of revenue than could be squeezed from the dry husks of oppressed indigence by the straining of all the politic machinery in the world?

===Chinese===
> 「可是（那位理财家说），没有钱，和平于我们何益？『你们的方案给我们带不来岁入。』不能吗？恰恰能——因为它保障了臣民拒绝的权力，这是一切岁入之首。倘若臣民这种按比例拨款、乃至根本不拨款的权力，未曾被证明为人类的智巧或人类的机运所发现过的最丰富的岁入矿藏，那么经验便是骗子，事实便是谎言之徒。它固然不会径直表决拨给你 152,750 镑 11 先令 2¾ 便士，也不会拨给任何寒酸的限定数额——但它给你的是钱柜本身，是基金，是银行，在珍视自由的民族那里，唯有由此才能生出岁入：*Positâ luditur arcâ*（拉丁文，意为『钱柜既安，余事从容』）。……这条原理难道在英国为真，在其余任何地方为假？它在爱尔兰不真吗？它在各殖民地迄今不真吗？你们凭什么臆断：在任何国家，一个为某种职能依法设立的机构会疏于履行其职责、背弃其信托？这样的臆断将违背一切国家的一切政府。但事实上，这种唯恐自由议会造成财源匮乏的恐慌，在自然中毫无根据。因为首先请看：除了人人自然怀有的维护自家政府荣誉的愿望之外，那始终伴随自由而来的尊严感与财产安全感，本有一种使自由共同体财富增值的倾向。积聚最多之处，可取者也最多。而在哪种土壤、哪种气候之下，经验不曾一致地证明：堆积的丰饶因自身繁盛之重而迸裂、自愿倾泻的流淌，其所涌出的岁入之流，总比全世界全部政治机器从受压迫的贫困之干瘪外壳中硬榨出的更为丰沛？

===Original===
That, whether you agree or disagree with its doctrine, is great prose. That is Burke. “O Athenian stranger,” said the Cretan I quoted in my first lecture—“inhabitant of Attica I will not call you, since you deserve the name of Athene herself, because you go back to first principles!”

===Chinese===
诸位无论赞同还是反对其中的学说，这都是伟大的散文。这就是伯克。「哦，雅典的异乡人，」我在第一讲里引过的那位克里特人说——「我不愿仅称你为阿提卡的居民，因为你当得起雅典娜本人的名字，因为你追溯到了第一原理！」

===Original===
But, you may object, “Burke is talking like a book, and I have no wish to talk like a book.” Well, as a fact, Burke is here at the culmen of a long sustained argument, and his language has soared with it, as his way was—logic and emotion lifting him together as upon two balanced majestic wings. But you are shy of such heights? Very well again, and all credit to your modesty! Yet at least (I appeal to that same modesty) when you talk or write, you would wish to *observe the occasion*; to say what you have to say without impertinence or ill-timed excess. You would not harangue a drawing-room or a subcommittee, or be facetious at a funeral, or play the skeleton at a banquet: for in all such conduct you would be mixing up things that differ. Be cheerful, then: for this desire of yours to be appropriate is really the root of the matter. Nor do I ask you to accept this on my sole word, but will cite you the most respectable witnesses. Take, for instance, a critic who should be old enough to impress you—Dionysius of Halicarnassus. After enumerating the qualities which lend charm and nobility to style, he closes the list with “appropriateness, which all these need”:—

===Chinese===
但你们或许会反对：「伯克这是在照书本讲话，我可不想照书本讲话。」好吧，事实上，伯克此刻正处于一段绵长论证的顶点，他的语言也随之高扬，这本是他的本色——逻辑与情感如两翼均衡而庄严，一同将他托举而起。可是你们畏惧这样的高度？那也很好，而且你们的谦逊可嘉！但至少（我正是诉诸这份谦逊）你们开口或下笔时，总愿意*切合场合*；把要说的话说得不失礼数、不乖时过度。你们总不至于在客厅里或小组委员会上发表长篇演说，不至于在葬礼上打趣逗笑，也不至于在宴席上抬出一具骷髅来：因为这类行径都是把不同的东西混为一谈。那么宽心吧：你们这份求其得体的愿望，实在正是此事的根本。我也不要求你们单凭我一家之言就接受它，还要给你们传唤最可敬的证人。比如，引一位年头足以镇得住你们的批评家——哈利卡纳苏斯的狄奥尼修斯（Dionysius of Halicarnassus）。他在历数了赋予风格魅力与高贵的诸品质之后，以「得体——这一切都少不了它」来收束这张清单：——

===Original===
> As there is a charming diction, so there is another that is noble; as there is a polished rhythm, so there is another that is dignified; as variety adds grace in one passage, so in another it adds fullness; *and as for appropriation, it will prove the chief source of beauty, or else of nothing at all.*

===Chinese===
> 既有迷人的措辞，也有高贵的措辞；既有打磨光润的节奏，也有庄严威重的节奏；变化在此一处增添风致，在彼一处则增添饱满；*至于得体（appropriation），它将被证明是美的主要源泉，否则便一切皆无。*

===Original===
Or listen to Cicero, how he sets appropriateness in the very heart of his teaching, as the master secret:—

===Chinese===
或者听听西塞罗（Cicero），看他把得体安放在其学说的正中心，奉为首要秘诀：——

===Original===
> Is erit eloquens qui poterit parva summisse, modica temperate, magna graviter dicere.… Qui ad id quodcunque decebit poterit accommodare orationem. Quod quum statuerit, tum, ut quidque erit dicendum, ita dicet, nec satura jejune, nec grandia minute, nec item contra, sed erit rebus ipsis par et aequalis oratio.

===Chinese===
> Is erit eloquens qui poterit parva summisse, modica temperate, magna graviter dicere. … Qui ad id quodcunque decebit poterit accommodare orationem. Quod quum statuerit, tum, ut quidque erit dicendum, ita dicet, nec satura jejune, nec grandia minute, nec item contra, sed erit rebus ipsis par et aequalis oratio.（西塞罗拉丁原文，其大意见下段）

===Original===
—“Whatever his theme he will speak as becomes it; neither meagrely where it is copious, nor meanly where it is ample, nor in *this* way where it demands *that*; but keeping his speech level with the actual subject and adequate to it.”

===Chinese===

===Original===

===Original===
