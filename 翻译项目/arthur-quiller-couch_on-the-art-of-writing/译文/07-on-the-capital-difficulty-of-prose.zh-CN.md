## Lecture VI On the Capital Difficulty of Prose / VI 论散文之大难点

===Original===
Thursday, May 15

===Chinese===
5 月 15 日，星期四

===Original===
Today, Gentlemen, leaving the Vanity Fair of Jargon behind us, we have to essay a difficult country; of which, though fairly confident of his compass-bearings, your guide confesses, that wide tracts lie outside his knowledge—outside of anything that can properly be called his knowledge. I feel indeed somewhat as Gideon must have felt when he divided his host on the slopes of Mount Gilead, warning back all who were afraid. In asking the remnant to follow as attentively as they can, I promise only that, if Heaven carry us safely across, we shall have “broken the back” of the desert.

===Chinese===
诸位先生，今天，把套话（Jargon）的名利场（Vanity Fair）抛在身后，我们要尝试进入一片艰难的国度；关于这片国度，你们的向导虽自认对罗盘方位把握得还算可靠，却必须承认，其中有大片大片的疆域在他知识之外——在任何配称他知识的东西之外。我此刻的心情，实在有些像基甸（Gideon）当年在基列山（Mount Gilead）山坡上分选兵员时必有的心情，他把一切惧怕的人都劝了回去。既请余下的人尽其所能地凝神相随，我所能允诺的只是：若上苍保佑我们平安渡过，我们便算是「折断了这片荒漠的脊梁」——闯过了它最艰难的一段。

===Original===
In my last lecture but one, then—and before our small interlude with Jargon—the argument had carried us, more or less neatly, up to this point: that the capital difficulty of verse consisted in saying ordinary unemotional things, of bridging the flat intervals between high moments. This point, I believe, we made effectively enough.

===Chinese===
那么，在上上次的讲座里——也就是我们就套话作那次小小插叙之前——论证已经多少利落地把我们带到了这样一点：诗的大难点，在于说平常的、不动情感的事物，在于架桥跨越高昂时刻（high moments）之间的平淡间隔。这一点，我相信我们已阐发得足够有力。

===Original===
Now, for logical neatness, we should be able to oppose a corresponding point, that the capital difficulty of prose consists in saying extraordinary things, in running it up from its proper level to these high emotional, musical, moments. And mightily convenient that would be, Gentlemen, if I were here to help you to answer scientific questions about prose and verse instead of helping you, in what small degree I can, to write. But in Literature (which, let me remind you yet once again, is an art) you cannot classify as in a science.

===Chinese===
而如今，为求逻辑上的齐整，我们本应能摆出一个对偶的论点：散文的大难点在于说不平凡的事物，在于驱策它从自身固有的水位攀升到那些情绪高昂、乐音悠扬的时刻。诸位先生，倘若我站在这里是为了帮你们解答关于散文与诗的科学问题，而不是在我力所能及的些微之处帮助你们写作，那可就万分便利了。但在文学里（请容我再次提醒你们，文学是一门艺术），你们不能像在科学里那样分类。

===Original===
Pray attend while I impress on you this most necessary warning. In studying literature, and still more in studying to write it, distrust all classification! All classifying of literature intrudes “science” upon an art, and is artificially “scientific”; a trick of pedants, that they may make it the easier to examine you on things with which no man should have any earthly concern, as I am sure he will never have a heavenly one. Beetles, minerals, gases, may be classified; and to have them classified is not only convenient but a genuine advance of knowledge. But if you had to *make* a beetle, as men are making poetry, how much would classification help? To classify in a science is necessary for the purpose of that science: to classify when you come to art is at the best an expedient, useful to some critics and to a multitude of examiners. It serves the art-critic to talk about Tuscan, Flemish, Pre-Raphaelite, schools of painting. The expressions are handy, and we know more or less what they intend. Just so handily it may serve us to talk about “Renaissance poets,” “the Elizabethans,” “the Augustan age.” But such terms at best cannot be scientific, precise, determinate, as for examples the terms “inorganic,” “mammal,” “univalve,” “Old Red Sandstone” are scientific, precise, determinate. An animal is either a mammal or it is not: you cannot say as assuredly that a man is or is not an Elizabethan. We call Shakespeare an Elizabethan and the greatest of Elizabethans, though as a fact he wrote his most famous plays when Elizabeth was dead. Shirley was but seven years old when Elizabeth died; yet (if “Elizabethan” have any meaning but a chronological one) Shirley belongs to the Elizabethan firmament, albeit but as a pale star low on the horizon: whereas Donne—a post-Elizabethan if ever there was one—had by 1603 reached his thirtieth year and written almost every line of those wonderful lyrics which for a good sixty years gave the dominant note to Jacobean and Caroline poetry.

===Chinese===
请留神听我把这条最必要的警告印进你们心里。在研究文学时，尤其是在研习写作文学时，要提防一切分类！凡对文学的分类，都是把「科学」硬闯进一门艺术，都是人为地「科学化」；那是学究们的把戏，好让他们更容易就一些世人本不该有半点尘俗牵累的事物考问你们——正如我确信，他们休想对这些事物有半点天上的挂怀。甲虫、矿物、气体，尽可以分类；给它们分了类，不仅方便，而且是知识货真价实的进步。但倘若你们必须去*制造*一只甲虫，就像人们正在制造诗歌那样，分类又能帮上多少忙？在科学中分类，为的是那门科学自身的目的：而到了艺术跟前还要分类，充其量只是一种权宜之计，对某些批评家和一大群考官有用。艺术批评家谈托斯卡纳派、弗拉芒派、前拉斐尔派的绘画流派，原也无妨。这些说法很顺手，我们也大致知道它们指的是什么。同样顺手地，我们谈「文艺复兴诗人」「伊丽莎白时代派」「奥古斯都时代」，或许也无妨。但这类名目充其量做不到科学、精确、界说分明，不像「无机体」「哺乳动物」「单壳类」「老红砂岩」这些术语那样是科学的、精确的、界说分明的。一只动物要么是哺乳动物，要么不是：你们却不能同样有把握地说，一个人是或不是伊丽莎白派。我们把莎士比亚（Shakespeare）称作伊丽莎白派，而且是伊丽莎白派中最伟大的一位，尽管事实上，他那些最著名的剧作写成之时，伊丽莎白已经死了。伊丽莎白驾崩时，雪莉（Shirley）年仅七岁；然而（倘若「伊丽莎白派」除了纪年之外还有什么别的意义）雪莉仍属于伊丽莎白的那片星空，尽管只是低悬在地平线上的一颗暗星：而多恩（Donne）——若说有谁够格称后伊丽莎白派，那就数他——到 1603 年已届三十，那些精妙的抒情诗他几乎已行行写就，而在日后足足六十年间，正是这些抒情诗为詹姆士时代与查理时代的诗歌定下了主导的音调。

===Original===
In treating of an art we classify for handiness, not for purposes of exact knowledge; and man (*improbus homo*) with his wicked inventions is forever making fools of our formulae. Be consoled—and, if you are wise, thank Heaven—that genius uses our best-laid logic to explode it.

===Chinese===
谈论一门艺术时，我们分类是为了顺手，不是为了求精确的知识；而人——*improbus homo*（那顽劣不餍的人）——凭着他种种刁钻的发明，永远在愚弄我们的公式。且宽心吧——你们若明智，还当感谢上苍——天才正拿我们布置得最周密的逻辑去把它炸个粉碎。

===Original===
Be consoled, at any rate, on finding that after deciding the capital difficulty of prose to lie in saying extraordinary things, in running up to the high emotional moments, the prose-writers explode and blow our admirable conclusions to ruins.

===Chinese===
无论如何，且在下面这件事里宽心吧：我们既已判定，散文的大难点在于说不平凡的事物、在于一路攀升到情绪高昂的时刻，散文作家们却轰然爆炸，把我们那套可钦可佩的结论炸成了废墟。

===Original===
You see, we gave them the chance to astonish us when we defined prose as “a record of human thought, dispensing with metre and using rhythm laxly.” When you give genius leave to use something laxly, at its will, genius will pretty surely get the better of you.

===Chinese===
你们看，当我们把散文定义为「人类思想的记录，不用格律（metre），而对节奏（rhythm）的运用从宽」时，我们就已经亲手给了他们让我们惊异的机会。你们一旦容许天才把某样东西用得从宽、任凭己意，天才十有八九要占你们的上风。

===Original===
Observe, now, following the story of English prose, what has happened. Its difficulty—the inherent, the native disability of prose—is to handle the high emotional moments which more properly belong to verse. Well, we strike into the line of our prose-writers, say as early as Malory. We come on this; of the Passing of Arthur:—

===Chinese===
现在请看，循着英语散文的历程，都发生了些什么。散文的困难——散文与生俱来的、天然的缺陷——在于驾驭那些本该更恰当地归属诗的高昂时刻。好，让我们切入散文作家们的队列，早可早至马洛礼（Malory）。我们撞见了这样一段，讲亚瑟之死（the Passing of Arthur）：——

===Original===
> “My time hieth fast,” said the king. Therefore said Arthur unto Sir Bedivere, “Take thou Excalibur my good sword, and go with it to yonder water side; and when thou comest there I charge thee throw my sword in that water and come again and tell me what there thou seest.” “My lord,” said Bedivere, “your commandment shall be done; and lightly bring you word again.” So Sir Bedivere departed, and by the way he beheld that noble sword, that the pommel and the haft was all of precious stones, and then he said to himself, “If I throw this rich sword in the water, thereof shall never come good, but harm and loss.” And then Sir Bedivere hid Excaliber under a tree. And so, as soon as he might, he came again unto the king, and said he had been at the water and had thrown the sword into the water, “What saw thou there?” said the king, “Sir,” he said, “I saw nothing but waves and winds.”

===Chinese===
> 「我的时日飞逝，」国王说。于是亚瑟对贝迪维尔爵士（Sir Bedivere）说道：「把我那把好剑王者之剑（Excalibur）拿去，携它到那边水畔；你到了那里，我命你把我的剑掷入水中，然后回来告诉我，你在那里看见了什么。」「主上，」贝迪维尔说，「您的命令必当遵行；我很快便回来向您复命。」于是贝迪维尔爵士动身而去，一路端详着那柄名贵的宝剑——只见剑的柄头与剑柄通体皆是宝石——他随即自忖道：「我若把这柄贵重的剑掷入水里，非但永远生不出好处，反倒生出祸害与损失。」于是贝迪维尔爵士把王者之剑藏在一棵树下。随后他尽快回到国王那里，说自己已到过水边，已把剑掷入水中。「你在那里看见了什么？」国王问。「主上，」他说，「我什么也没看见，只有波浪和风。」

===Original===
Now I might say a dozen things of this and of the whole passage that follows, down to Arthur’s last words. Specially might I speak to you of the music of its monosyllables—“‘What sawest you there?’ said the king… ‘Do as well as thou mayest; for in me is no trust for to trust in. For I will into the Vale of Avilion, to heal me of my grievous wound. And if thou hear never more of me, pray for my soul.’” But, before making comment at all, I shall quote you another passage; this from Lord Berners’ translation of Froissart, of the death of Robert Bruce:—

===Chinese===
关于这一段，以及随后直到亚瑟临终遗言的整段文字，我本可以讲上十来层意思。我尤其可以向你们讲讲它单音节词的音乐——「『你在那里看见了什么？』国王说……『你尽力而为吧；因为我身上已无可倚恃之处可供倚恃。因为我要前往阿维隆之谷（Vale of Avilion），疗治我沉重的创伤。你若再也听不到我的消息，就请为我的灵魂祈祷。』」但在妄加评论之前，我还要给你们引一段文字；这段出自伯纳斯勋爵（Lord Berners）所译的傅华萨（Froissart），讲罗伯特·布鲁斯（Robert Bruce）之死：——

===Original===
> It fortuned that King Robert of Scotland was right sore aged and feeble: for he was greatly charged with the great sickness, so that there was no way for him but death. And when he felt that his end drew near, he sent for such barons and lords of his realm as he trusted best, and showed them how there was no remedy with him, but he must needs leave this transitory life.… Then he called to him the gentle knight, Sir William Douglas, and said before all the lords, “Sir William, my dear friend, ye know well that I have had much ado in my days to uphold and sustain the right of this realm; and when I had most ado I made a solemn vow, the which as yet I have not accomplished, whereof I am right sorry; the which was, if I might achieve and make an end of all my wars, so that I might once have brought this realm in rest and peace, then I promised in my mind to have gone and warred on Christ’s enemies, adversaries to our holy Christian faith. To this purpose mine heart hath ever intended, but our Lord would not consent thereto… And sith it is so that my body can not go, nor achieve that my heart desireth, I will send the heart instead of the body, to accomplish mine avow… I will, that as soon as I am trespassed out of this world, that ye take my heart out of my body, and embalm it, and take of my treasure as ye shall think sufficient for that enterprise, both for yourself and such company as ye will take with you, and present my heart to the Holy Sepulchre, whereas our Lord lay, seeing my body can not come there. And take with you such company and purveyance as shall be appertaining to your estate. And, wheresoever ye come, let it be known how ye carry with you the heart of King Robert of Scotland, at his instance and desire to be presented to the Holy Sepulchre.” Then all the lords, that heard these words, wept for pity.

===Chinese===
> 且说苏格兰国王罗伯特，年事已高，衰弱不堪：他为大症所困，沉疴压身，除死之外已无路可走。他自觉大限将近，便召来国中他最信得过的那些男爵与贵族，告知他们：自己已无药可救，必须撇下这易逝的生命了。……随后他把那位温厚的骑士威廉·道格拉斯爵士（Sir William Douglas）唤到跟前，当着众贵族的面说道：「威廉爵士，我亲爱的朋友，你深知我平生费了多少周折，来维护并撑持这王国的正统；而在最艰难的时候，我曾立下一道庄重的誓愿，至今尚未了结，为此我深感憾恨；那誓愿是：倘若我能征伐到底、平息一切战事，终能使这王国得享安宁太平，我便在心中许诺，要亲身出征，讨伐基督的仇敌、我们神圣基督教信仰的敌对者。我的心愿一直在此，只是我主不肯应允……既然我这身体去不成，无法达成心之所愿，我便要遣心代身，以了此誓……我愿在我一旦离世之后，你便将我的心自遗体中取出，以香料防腐，并从我的财宝中取用你以为足以成事之数，为你自己也为你愿携同的随行人等；然后携我的心前往圣墓（Holy Sepulchre）——我主曾经安卧之处——呈献于彼，既然我的身体不能亲到。你也要携带与你身份相称的随从与行装。无论你行至何处，都要让人知晓：你随身携着苏格兰国王罗伯特的心，是奉他的恳请与心愿，前来呈献于圣墓。」众贵族听闻此言，无不恻然泣下。

===Original===
There, in the fifteenth century and early in the sixteenth, you have Malory and Berners writing beautiful English prose; prose the emotion of which (I dare to say) you must recognise if you have ears to hear. So you see that already our English prose not only achieves the “high moment,” but seems to obey it rather and be lifted by it, until we ask ourselves, “Who could help writing nobly, having to tell how King Arthur died or how the Bruce?” Yes, but I bid you observe that Malory and Berners are both relating what, however noble, is quite simple, quite straightforward. It is when prose attempts to *philosophise*, to *express thoughts* as well as to relate simple sayings and doings—it is then that the trouble begins. When Malory has to philosophise death, to *think* about it, this is as far as he attains:—

===Chinese===
看哪，在十五世纪与十六世纪初叶，你们已见到马洛礼与伯纳斯写出的美丽英语散文；这散文的情感（我敢说）你们只消有耳朵就必能听出。于是你们看到，那时我们的英语散文不仅已经达到「高昂时刻」，而且简直像是服从它、被它托举而起，直到我们不禁自问：「既要讲述亚瑟王怎样死去、布鲁斯王怎样死去，谁能写得不高贵？」是这么回事，不过我请你们注意：马洛礼与伯纳斯所讲述的东西，无论何等高贵，都极其简单、极其直截。而当散文试图*作哲思*、试图不仅记叙简单的言行、还要*表达思想*时——麻烦便由此开始。当马洛礼不得不对死亡作哲思、不得不*思考*死亡时，他所能达到的不过如此：——

===Original===
> “Ah, Sir Lancelot,” said he, “thou wert head of all Christian Knights! And now I dare say,” said Sir Ector, “that, Sir Lancelot, there thou liest, thou were never matched of none earthly hands; and thou were the curtiest knight that ever bare shield: and thou were the truest friend to thy lover that ever strood horse, and thou were the truest lover of a sinful man that ever loved woman; and thou were the kindest man that ever strooke with sword; and thou were the goodliest person that ever came among press of knights; and thou were the meekest man and gentlest that ever sat in hall among ladies; and thou were the sternest Knight to thy mortal foe that ever put spear in the rest.”

===Chinese===
> 「啊，兰斯洛特爵士（Sir Lancelot），」他说，「你曾是全体基督徒骑士之首！而今我敢说，」埃克托爵士（Sir Ector）说，「兰斯洛特爵士啊，你躺在那里了：论武艺，尘世之手从未有人与你匹敌；你是历来执盾骑士中最谦恭知礼的：你是历来跨鞍者当中待心上人最忠实的朋友，是历来有罪凡人爱恋女子者当中最忠贞的情人；你是历来挥剑者当中最仁厚的；你是历来置身骑士丛中最堂堂的；你是历来坐于厅堂仕女之间最温良最谦和的；你是历来置长矛于架上者当中对付死敌最凛然的骑士。」

===Original===
Beautiful again, I grant! But note you that, eloquent as he can be on the virtues of his dead friend, when Sir Ector comes to the thought of death itself all he can accomplish is, “And now I dare say that, Sir Lancelot, there thou liest.”

===Chinese===
我承认，这又是美的！但请你们注意：埃克托爵士纵然能就亡友的美德说得这般雄辩，可一碰到死亡本身这个念头，他能成就的，不过是「而今我敢说，兰斯洛特爵士啊，你躺在那里了」一句而已。

===Original===
Let us make a leap in time and contrast this with Tyndale and the translators of our Bible, how they are able to make St. Paul speak of death:—

===Chinese===
让我们在时间上向前一跃，拿这个对照一下廷代尔（Tyndale）与我们圣经的译者们，看他们能叫圣保罗（St. Paul）把死亡说得怎样：——

===Original===
> So when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written, Death is swallowed up in victory. O death, where is thy sting? O grave, where is thy victory?

===Chinese===
> 这必朽坏的既穿上不朽，这必死的既穿上不死，那时经上所记「死被得胜吞灭」的话就应验了。死啊，你的毒钩在哪里？坟墓啊，你的胜利在哪里？

===Original===
There you have something clean beyond what Malory or Berners could compass: there you have a different kind of high moment—a high moment of philosophising: there you have emotion impregnated with thought. It was necessary that our English verse even after Chaucer, our English prose after Malory and Berners, should overcome this most difficult gap (which stands for a real intellectual difference) if it aspired to be what today it is—a language of the first class, comparable with Greek and certainly no whit inferior to Latin or French.

===Chinese===
这里你们所见的，已全然超出马洛礼或伯纳斯所能达到的范围：这是一种不同种类的高昂时刻——一种哲思的高昂时刻：这里是浸透了思想的情感。我们的英语诗即便在乔叟（Chaucer）之后、我们的英语散文在马洛礼与伯纳斯之后，都必须跨越这道最艰难的鸿沟（它代表着一道实实在在的智性差异），才能有望成为它今日的模样——一等的语言，可与希腊语比肩，而且绝不比拉丁语或法语逊色半分。

---

===Original===
Let us leave prose for a moment, and see how Verse threw its bridge over the gap. If you would hear the note of Chaucer at its deepest, you will find it in the famous exquisite lines of the Prioress’ Prologue:—

===Chinese===
让我们暂且撇开散文，看看诗是怎样把桥架过这道鸿沟的。你们若要听乔叟最深沉的音调，会在《女修道院院长的序幕》（Prioress' Prologue）那著名而精美的诗行里找到：——

===Original===
> O moder mayde! O maydë moder fre!

> O bush unbrent, brenning in Moyses’ sight!

===Chinese===
> 啊，母亲而贞女！啊，贞女而自由的母亲！

> 啊，未尝焚毁的荆棘，在摩西眼前燃烧！

===Original===
in the complaint of Troilus, in the rapture of Griselda restored to her children:—

===Chinese===
这音调也回响在特洛伊罗斯（Troilus）的哀诉里，回响在格丽泽尔达（Griselda）重见孩子们时的狂喜里：——

===Original===
> O tendre, O dere, O yongë children myne,

> Your woful moder wendë stedfastly

> That cruel houndës or some foul vermyne

> Hadde eten you; but God of his mercy

> And your benignë fader tendrely

> Hath doon you kept…

===Chinese===
