## Lecture XII On Style / XII 论风格

===Original===
Wednesday, January 28, 1914

===Chinese===
1914 年 1 月 28 日，星期三

===Original===
Should Providence, Gentlemen, destine any one of you to write books for his living, he will find experimentally true what I here promise him, that few pleasures sooner cloy than reading what the reviewers say. This promise I hand on with the better confidence since it was endorsed for me once in conversation by that eminently good man the late Henry Sidgwick; who added, however, “Perhaps I ought to make a single exception. There was a critic who called one of my books ‘epoch-making.’ Being anonymous, he would have been hard to find and thank, perhaps; but I ought to have made the effort.”

===Chinese===
诸位先生，倘若天意注定你们中间有哪一位要以写书为生，他便会从经验里证实我在此向他许下的话：诸般快乐之中，很少有几样比阅读评论家们的高论更快令人腻味。这句许诺我转赠给你们时格外有信心，因为已故的那位大好人亨利·西奇威克（Henry Sidgwick）曾在一席谈话中亲口替我作过保；只是他补充道：「也许我该承认一个例外。曾有一位批评家，称我的一本书『划时代』。他既是匿名，要寻着他道谢，或许本非易事；但这番努力，我原是应当做的。」

===Original===
May I follow up this experience of his with one of my own, as a preface or brief apology for this lecture? Short-lived as is the author’s joy in his critics, far-spent as may be his hope of fame, mournful his consent with Sir Thomas Browne that “there is nothing immortal but immortality,” he cannot hide from certain sanguine men of business, who in England call themselves “Press-Cutting Agencies,” in America “Press-Clipping Bureaux,” and, as each successive child of his invention comes to birth, unbecomingly presume in him an almost virginal trepidation. “Your book,” they write falsely, “is exciting much comment. May we collect and send you notices of it appearing in the World’s Press? We submit a specimen cutting with our terms; and are, dear Sir,” etc.

===Chinese===
可否容我拿我自己的经历续上他的这段经历，权作本次讲座的引子或简短的辩白？作者得自批评家的快乐纵然短促，成名的指望纵然将尽，纵然他凄然附和托马斯·布朗爵士（Sir Thomas Browne）所言「除不朽之外，无物不朽」，他却躲不开某几家血气旺盛的生意人——在英格兰自称「剪报代理行」（Press-Cutting Agencies），在美国称「剪报局」（Press-Clipping Bureaux）——每逢他心智所出的新生儿一一坠地，他们便不甚得体地认定他又怀着一份近乎处女般的惶恐。「您的大作，」他们虚言相欺道，「正激起广泛评论。可否由我们收集世界各地报刊上的评介，寄奉尊览？随函谨呈剪报样品一纸暨价目章程；敬爱的先生，敝公司」云云。

===Original===
Now, although steadily unresponsive to this wile, I am sometimes guilty of taking the enclosed specimen review and thrusting it for preservation among the scarcely less deciduous leaves of the book it was written to appraise. So it happened that having this vacation, to dust﻿—not to read﻿—a line of obsolete or obsolescent works on a shelf, I happened on a review signed by no smaller a man than Mr. Gilbert Chesterton and informing the world that the author of my obsolete book was full of good stories as a kindly uncle, but had a careless or impatient way of stopping short and leaving his readers to guess what they most wanted to know: that, reaching the last chapter, or what he chose to make the last chapter, instead of winding up and telling “how everybody lived ever after,” he (so to speak) slid you off his avuncular knee with a blessing and the remark that nine o’clock was striking and all good children should be in their beds.

===Chinese===
如今，我虽对这套伎俩始终置之不理，有时却也难免有过：把随函附来的样张评论抽出来，塞进它所评的那本书里存着——那书页凋落的频繁，其实也不比叶子逊色多少。于是便有了这一桩事：这个假期，我去拂拭（并非去读）架上一排已废或将废的著作，偶然撞见一篇书评，署名者绝非小人物，正是吉尔伯特·切斯特顿先生（Mr. Gilbert Chesterton）；他向世人宣告：我那本已废之书的作者，满肚子好故事，像一位慈祥的叔叔，只是有个漫不经心或不耐烦的毛病——话到半途便戛然而止，撇下读者去猜他们最想知道的事：那就是，写到最后一章——或者说，写到他乐意当作最后一章的地方——他非但不把故事收拢，告诉人家「后来每个人一辈子过得怎样」，反而（不妨这么说）为你祝个福，道一声九点钟正在敲、好孩子们都该上床了，便把你从他叔叔的膝头放下地来。

===Original===
That criticism has haunted me during the vacation. Looking back on a course of lectures which I deemed to be accomplished; correcting them in print; revising them with all the nervousness of a beginner; I have seemed to hear you complain﻿—“He has exhorted us to write accurately, appropriately; to eschew Jargon; to be bold and essay Verse. He has insisted that Literature is a living art, to be practised. But just what we most needed he has not told. At the final doorway to the secret he turned his back and left us. Accuracy, propriety, perspicuity﻿—these we may achieve. But where has he helped us to write with beauty, with charm, with distinction? Where has he given us rules for what is called *Style* in short?﻿—having attained which an author may count himself set up in business.”

===Chinese===
假期里，那篇批评一直萦绕着我。回顾这一门自以为已然功德圆满的讲座；将它们付印勘校；怀着初学者的全部惶恐加以修订；我仿佛听见你们抱怨——「他劝勉我们写得准确、写得得体；叫我们避开套话（Jargon）；叫我们放大胆子去试作诗（Verse）。他坚持说文学是一门活的艺术，须得实践。可是我们最需要的东西，他偏偏没有讲。到了通向秘密的最后一道门跟前，他却转过身去，把我们撇下了。准确、得体、明晰——这些我们或许做得到。可是，他又在何处帮助我们写得美、写得迷人、写得卓然出众呢？总而言之，对那所谓风格（Style），他何曾给过我们一条规则？——得了它，一个作者便可自认为开业挂牌了。」

===Original===
Thus, Gentlemen, with my mind’s ear I heard you reproaching me. I beg you to accept what follows for my apology.

===Chinese===
诸位先生，我便是这样，用心灵的耳朵听见了你们的责备。以下的话，请权当我的辩白，望你们收纳。

===Original===
To begin with, let me plead that you have been told of one or two things which Style is *not*; which have little or nothing to do with Style, though sometimes vulgarly mistaken for it. Style, for example, is not﻿—can never be﻿—extraneous Ornament. You remember, may be, the Persian lover whom I quoted to you out of Newman: how to convey his passion he sought a professional letter-writer and purchased a vocabulary charged with ornament, wherewith to attract the fair one as with a basket of jewels. Well, in this extraneous, professional, purchased ornamentation, you have something which Style is not: and if you here require a practical rule of me, I will present you with this: “Whenever you feel an impulse to perpetrate a piece of exceptionally fine writing, obey it﻿—wholeheartedly﻿—and delete it before sending your manuscript to press. *Murder your darlings.*”

===Chinese===
首先，请容我申明：有一两样东西是风格（Style）所不是的，你们已经被告知过了；它们与风格关系甚微或全无关系，只是有时被庸俗地误认作风格。譬如，风格不是——永远也不可能是——外在的藻饰（Ornament）。你们或许还记得，我曾从纽曼（Newman）著作里给你们引过那个波斯情郎：为了传达他的热情，他去寻了一位职业代书人，购置了一整套满载藻饰的词汇，好用来吸引那位美人，仿佛提着一篮珠宝前去。好了，在这种外在的、职业的、花钱买来的装点之中，你们看到的正是风格所不是的东西：倘若你们此刻要我给一条实用的规则，我便奉上这一条：「每当你感到一阵冲动，要炮制一段格外漂亮的文字，那就服从它——全心全意地服从——然后在把稿子付印之前，把它删掉。杀死你的心头爱（*Murder your darlings*）。」

===Original===
But let me plead further that you have not been left altogether without clue to the secret of what Style is. That you must master the secret for yourselves lay implicit in our bargain, and you were never promised that a writer’s training would be easy. Yet a clue was certainly put in your hands when, having insisted that Literature is a living art, I added that therefore it must be personal and of its essence personal.

===Chinese===
但请容我进一步申明：风格是什么，这个秘密的线索，你们并非全然未曾到手。你们必须亲自参透这个秘密，这一点本已暗含在我们的约定之中；而且从来没有人应许过你们，一个作家的训练会是容易的。然而线索确曾交到你们手里——当我坚持说文学是一门活的艺术之后，我又补了一句：因此它必须是个人的，而且就其本质而言，是个人的。

===Original===
This goes very deep: it conditions all our criticism of art. Yet it conceals no mystery. You may see its meaning most easily and clearly, perhaps, by contrasting Science and Art at their two extremes﻿—say Pure Mathematics with Acting. Science as a rule deals with things, Art with man’s thought and emotion about things. In Pure Mathematics things are rarefied into ideas, numbers, concepts, but still farther and farther away from the individual man. Two and two make four, and fourpence is not ninepence (or at any rate four is not nine) whether Alcibiades or Cleon keep the tally. In Acting on the other hand almost everything depends on personal interpretation﻿—on the gesture, the walk, the gaze, the tone of a Siddons, the *rusé* smile of a Coquelin, the exquisite, vibrant intonation of a Bernhardt. “English Art?” exclaimed Whistler, “there is no such thing! Art is art and mathematics is mathematics.” Whistler erred. Precisely because Art is Art, and Mathematics is Mathematics and a Science, Art being Art can be English or French; and, more than this, must be the personal expression of an Englishman or a Frenchman, as a “Constable” differs from a “Corot” and a “Whistler” from both. Surely I need not labour this. But what is true of the extremes of Art and Science is true also, though sometimes less recognisably true, of the mean: and where they meet and seem to conflict (as in History) the impact is that of the personal or individual mind upon universal truth, and the question becomes whether what happened in the Sicilian Expedition, or at the trial of Charles I, can be set forth naked as an alegebraical sum, serene in its certainty, indifferent to opinion, uncoloured in the telling as in the hearing by sympathy or dislike, by passion or by character. I doubt, while we should strive in history as in all things to be fair, if history can be written in that colourless way, to interest men in human doings. I am sure that nothing which lies further towards imaginative, creative, Art can be written in that way.

===Chinese===
这一层涵义极深：它规定着我们全部的艺术批评。然而其中并无玄秘。把科学与艺术各推到其极端加以对照——譬如拿纯粹数学对照表演——你们或许最能轻易而清楚地看出它的意思。科学照例处理事物，艺术处理人对事物的思想与情感。在纯粹数学中，事物被提纯为观念、数字、概念，却仍旧一步步远离个体的人。二加二等于四，四便士不是九便士（或者无论如何，四不是九），管账的是亚西比德（Alcibiades）还是克里昂（Cleon），全不相干。反过来，在表演中，几乎一切都系于个人的诠释——系于一位西登斯（Siddons）的身段、步态、顾盼与声口，系于科克兰（Coquelin）那 *rusé*（狡黠）的微笑，系于贝尔纳（Bernhardt）那精妙而颤动的行腔。「英国艺术？」惠斯勒（Whistler）呼道，「没有这回事！艺术就是艺术，数学就是数学。」惠斯勒错了。恰恰因为艺术是艺术，而数学是数学、是一门科学，艺术之为艺术才可以是英国的或法国的；更进一层——它必定是一个英国人或一个法国人的个人表达，正如一幅「康斯太勃」（Constable）之异于一幅「柯罗」（Corot），而一幅「惠斯勒」又与此二者皆异。这一点想必无须我多费唇舌。但艺术与科学两端成立的道理，于其中间地带同样成立，尽管有时不那么容易辨认；而在二者相遇、似相冲突之处（如在历史学中），相撞击的乃是个人的、个体的心智与普遍的真理，问题于是变成：西西里远征中发生的事、查理一世（Charles I）受审时发生的事，能否像一道代数和那样赤裸裸地铺陈出来——确然安详，无动于议，讲述也罢、听闻也罢，一概不因同情或厌恶、激情或性格而着色。我们固然应当在历史中一如在万事中力求公正，我却怀疑，历史若用那种不染颜色的写法写出，还能不能叫人对人类的行事生出兴味。至于更靠近想象的、创造的艺术一侧的东西，我确信断不能用那种写法写成。

===Original===
It follows then that Literature, being by its nature personal, must be by its nature almost infinitely various. “Two persons cannot be the authors of the sounds which strike our ear; and as they cannot be speaking one and the same speech, neither can they be writing one and the same lecture or discourse.” *Quot homines tot sententiae.* You may translate that, if you will, “Every man of us constructs his sentence differently”; and if there be indeed any quarrel between Literature and Science (as I never can see why there should be), I for one will readily grant Science all her cold superiority, her ease in Zion with universal facts, so it be mine to serve among the multifarious race who have to adjust, as best they may, Science’s cold conclusions (and much else) to the brotherly give-and-take of human life.

===Chinese===
由此推论，文学既以个人为本性，也就必以近乎无限的多样为本性。「两个人不可能同为那敲击我们耳鼓的声音的作者；正如他们不可能说着同一篇话，他们也不可能写着同一篇讲演或论说。」*Quot homines tot sententiae*（有多少人，便有多少种见解）。你们若愿意，不妨把它译作「我们每个人构句的方式各不相同」；而倘若文学与科学之间当真有什么龃龉（我始终看不出为什么会有），我个人乐于把科学那全部冷峻的优越、她在普遍事实之间安坐锡安的自在，悉数拱手奉让，只消容我在那形形色色的族类中间效力——他们不得不把科学的冰冷结论（还有其余许多东西）尽力调停，安放进人类生活兄弟般的往来互济之中。

===Original===
*Quicquid agunt homines, votum, timor, ira, voluptas﻿ ﻿…* Is it possible, Gentlemen, that you can have read one, two, three or more of the acknowledged masterpieces of literature without having it borne in on you that they are great because they are alive, and traffic not with cold celestial certainties, but with men’s hopes, aspirations, doubts, loves, hates, breakings of the heart; the glory and vanity of human endeavour, the transience of beauty, the capricious uncertain lease on which you and I hold life, the dark coast to which we inevitably steer; all that amuses or vexes, all that gladdens, saddens, maddens us men and women on this brief and mutable traject which yet must be home for a while, the anchorage of our hearts? For an instance:﻿—

===Chinese===
*Quicquid agunt homines, votum, timor, ira, voluptas…*（人们的一切所为——祈愿、恐惧、愤怒、欢乐……）诸位先生，你们可曾读过一部、两部、三部乃至更多的公认文学杰作，却始终不曾被这样一个念头穿透：它们伟大，是因为它们活着；它们打交道的不是天界冷冰冰的确定性，而是人的希望与抱负、疑惑与爱憎、心碎肠断；是人类努力的荣耀与虚妄，是美的易逝，是你我持有生命的那纸任性而无定的租约，是我们无可回避地驶向的那片黑暗海岸；是在这短暂而变幻的航程上——它仍须充当我们的家宅一时、我们心灵的锚地——使你我这些男女或欢愉或烦恼、或喜或悲、或至于癫狂的一切？且举一例：——

===Original===
> Here lies a most beautiful lady,
 

 Light of step and heart was she:
 

 I think she was the most beautiful lady
 

 That ever was in the West Country.
> But beauty vanishes, beauty passes,
 

 However rare, rare it be;

===Chinese===

> 她的脚步轻盈，心地也轻盈：
> 我想，在西部之乡曾有过的美人里，

===Original===
Or take a critic﻿—a literary critic﻿—such as Samuel Johnson, of whom we are used to think as of a man artificial in phrase and pedantic in judgment. He lives, and why? Because, if you test his criticism, he never saw literature but as a part of life, nor would allow in literature what was false to life, as he saw it. He could be wrongheaded, perverse; could damn Milton because he hated Milton’s politics; on any question of passion or prejudice could make injustice his daily food. But he could not, even in a friend’s epitaph, let pass a phrase (however well turned) which struck him as empty of life or false to it. All Boswell testifies to this: and this is why Samuel Johnson survives.

===Chinese===
或者举一位批评家——一位文学批评家——比如塞缪尔·约翰逊（Samuel Johnson）：我们惯于把他想成一个措辞造作、判断迂腐的人。他至今活着，为什么？因为你们一旦检验他的批评，便会发现他从来不曾把文学看作别的，只看作生命的一部分；凡是他眼中虚假失真的东西，他一概不容其存在于文学之中。他可以执拗，可以乖张；可以因为憎恶弥尔顿（Milton）的政治而贬斥弥尔顿；在任何一个牵动激情或偏见的问题上，可以把不公当作日常的食粮。但即便是在一位朋友的墓志铭里，他也无法放过一个在他看来空洞无生命或虚假失真的句子——无论那句子雕琢得多么漂亮。鲍斯韦尔（Boswell）的全部记载都可为此作证：而这，正是塞缪尔·约翰逊至今犹存的原因。

===Original===
Now let me carry this contention﻿—that all Literature is personal and therefore various﻿—into a field much exploited by the pedant, and fenced about with many notice-boards and public warnings. “*Neologisms not allowed here*,” “*All persons using slang, or trespassing in pursuit of originality.﻿ ﻿…*”

===Chinese===
现在，让我把这一论旨——一切文学都是个人的，因而是多样的——带进一片被学究大力垦殖过的园地；那园子四周围着许多告示牌，张贴着公家的告诫：「*Neologisms not allowed here*」（此处禁用新词），「*All persons using slang, or trespassing in pursuit of originality.…*」（凡使用俚语、或为猎取新异而擅入者……）

===Original===
Well, I answer these notice-boards by saying that, literature being personal, and men various﻿—and even the Oxford English Dictionary being no canonical book﻿—man’s use or defiance of the dictionary depends for its justification on nothing but his success: adding that, since it takes all kinds to make a world, or a literature, his success will probably depend on the occasion. A few months ago I found myself seated at a bump-supper next to a cheerful youth who, towards the close, suggested thoughtfully, as I arose to make a speech, that, the bonfire (which of course he called the “bonner”) being due at nine-thirty o’clock, there was little more than bare time left for “langers and godders.” It cost me, who think slowly, some seconds to interpret that by “langers” he meant “Auld Lang Syne” and by “godders” “God Save the King.” I thought at the time, and still think, and will maintain against any schoolmaster, that the neologisms of my young neighbour, though not to be recommended for essays or sermons, did admirably suit the time, place, and occasion.

===Chinese===
对于这些告示牌，我的回答是：文学既是个人的，人是多样的——就连《牛津英语词典》（Oxford English Dictionary）也并非什么钦定经典——一个人使用词典也好、违抗词典也好，其正当与否，全然只系于他的成败：我还要补一句，既然要各色人等才凑得起一个世界、或一门文学，他的成败多半还要看场合。几个月前，我在一次碰杯庆功晚宴（bump-supper）上，恰与一位快活的青年邻座；席近尾声，我起身正要致辞，他体贴地提醒道：篝火（他当然管篝火叫「bonner」）定于九点半点燃，留给「langers and godders」的时间，已不过刚刚够用了。我这人思维迟缓，花了好几秒才参透：他所谓「langers」，指的是《友谊地久天长》（Auld Lang Syne），所谓「godders」，指的是《天佑国王》（God Save the King）。我当时便想，如今仍这样想，并且愿在任何一位校长面前坚持：我这位年轻邻座的新造词，虽说不宜举荐给论文或布道文，用在那时、那地、那场合，却是妙不可言地合宜。

===Original===
Seeing that in human discourse, infinitely varied as it is, so much must ever depend on *who* speaks, and to *whom*, in what mood and upon what occasion; and seeing that Literature must needs take account of all manner of writers, audiences, moods, occasions; I hold it a sin against the light to put up a warning against any word that comes to us in the fair way of use and wont (as “wire,” for instance, for a telegram), even as surely as we should warn off hybrids or deliberately pedantic impostors, such as “antibody” and “picture-drome”; and that, generally, it is better to err on the side of liberty than on the side of the censor: since by the manumitting of new words we infuse new blood into a tongue of which (or we have learnt nothing from Shakespeare’s audacity) our first pride should be that it is flexible, alive, capable of responding to new demands of man’s untiring quest after knowledge and experience. Not because it was an ugly thing did I denounce Jargon to you, the other day: but because it was a dead thing, leading no-whither, meaning naught. There is *wickedness* in human speech, sometimes.
