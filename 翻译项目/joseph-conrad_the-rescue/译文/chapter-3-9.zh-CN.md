The letter went on to enlarge on the intrigues of Tengga, the wavering conduct of Belarab, and the state of the public mind. It noted every gust of opinion and every event, with an earnestness of belief in their importance befitting the chronicle of a crisis in the history of an empire. The shade of Jörgenson had, indeed, stepped back into the life of men. The old adventurer looked on with a perfect understanding of the value of trifles, using his eyes for that other man whose conscience would have the task to unravel the tangle. Lingard lived through those days in the Settlement and was thankful to Jörgenson; only as he lived not from day to day but from sentence to sentence of the writing, there was an effect of bewildering rapidity in the succession of events that made him grunt with surprise sometimes or growl﻿—“What?” to himself angrily and turn back several lines or a whole page more than once. The last page fluttered on the table out of Lingard’s fingers. He sat very still for a moment looking straight before him, then went on deck.

“Our boats back yet?” he asked Shaw, whom he saw prowling on the quarterdeck.

“No, sir, I wish they were. I am waiting for them to go and turn in,” answered the mate in an aggrieved manner.

“Lower that lantern forward there,” cried Lingard, suddenly, in Malay.

“This trade isn’t fit for a decent man,” muttered Shaw to himself, and he moved away to lean on the rail, looking moodily to seaward. After a while: “There seems to be commotion on board that yacht,” he said. “I see a lot of lights moving about her decks. Anything wrong, do you think, sir?”

“No, I know what it is,” said Lingard in a tone of elation. She has done it! he thought.

林加德简单地向他保证会有公道。他说，这正是他要游艇船员上船来的缘故。随后他安静而凝重地问，那支手枪是否还在卡特口袋里。

“那个别提了。”年轻人急忙说。“想想是谁开的头。挨一枪倒不至于让我这么憋气——受人桐吓，您明白吗，这才是压在我胸口的石头。不过昨儿夜里已经很远了——我把它从钉子上摘下来的时候到底存的什么心，我要是说得清才怪呢。罢了。事情不落定，我再多一个字也不说。这样行吗？”

他臊得满脸通红，悬置了他的判断，也收住了他的手，带着年轻人的大度。

——

林加德似乎正乐意接受这形式的缓刑。他缓缓低下了头。这样行。把自己的性命交给这个毛头小子的懵懂，仿佛使他心里那架天平对着许多隐秘的意图重新摆平了。这滋味又苦又涩，正该是赎罪的滋味。他手里也握着一条命；一条命，外加许多条死——可这些在他良心的秤上不过一根羽毛。他这样感觉是在所难免的，因为他的力量无论如何不肯被白白作践。不肯——就这样定了。他能做的，只是往风险的汪洋里再抛进一重风险。这样他才得以认识到，一滴水落进海洋，也大不相同。他那未被征服、却被放逐的欲望，已经离开了那个他可以时时听见它声音的地方。他看见它，看见他自己，看见过去和未来，看见这一切，飘忽而模糊，如同夜色面上被游子竭尽目力的眼睛用更深的笔画勾出的那些形状。
