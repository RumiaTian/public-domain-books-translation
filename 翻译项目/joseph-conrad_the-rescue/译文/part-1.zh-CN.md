## Part / 第一部
===Original===

I

The Man and the Brig

The shallow sea that foams and murmurs on the shores of the thousand islands, big and little, which make up the Malay Archipelago has been for centuries the scene of adventurous undertakings. The vices and the virtues of four nations have been displayed in the conquest of that region that even to this day has not been robbed of all the mystery and romance of its past﻿—and the race of men who had fought against the Portuguese, the Spaniards, the Dutch and the English, has not been changed by the unavoidable defeat. They have kept to this day their love of liberty, their fanatical devotion to their chiefs, their blind fidelity in friendship and hate﻿—all their lawful and unlawful instincts. Their country of land and water﻿—for the sea was as much their country as the earth of their islands﻿—has fallen a prey to the western race﻿—the reward of superior strength if not of superior virtue. Tomorrow the advancing civilization will obliterate the marks of a long struggle in the accomplishment of its inevitable victory.

The adventurers who began that struggle have left no descendants. The ideas of the world changed too quickly for that. But even far into the present century they have had successors. Almost in our own day we have seen one of them﻿—a true adventurer in his devotion to his impulse﻿—a man of high mind and of pure heart, lay the foundation of a flourishing state on the ideas of pity and justice. He recognized chivalrously the claims of the conquered; he was a disinterested adventurer, and the reward of his noble instincts is in the veneration with which a strange and faithful race cherish his memory.

Misunderstood and traduced in life, the glory of his achievement has vindicated the purity of his motives. He belongs to history. But there were others﻿—obscure adventurers who had not his advantages of birth, position, and intelligence; who had only his sympathy with the people of forests and sea he understood and loved so well. They can not be said to be forgotten since they have not been known at all. They were lost in the common crowd of seamen-traders of the Archipelago, and if they emerged from their obscurity it was only to be condemned as lawbreakers. Their lives were thrown away for a cause that had no right to exist in the face of an irresistible and orderly progress﻿—their thoughtless lives guided by a simple feeling.

第一部

人与双桅船

在构成马来群岛（Malay Archipelago）的千百个大大小小的岛屿岸边泛起泡沫、低声喧响的浅海上，几百年来一直上演着种种冒险的事业。在征服那片至今仍未被夺去往昔全部神秘与浪漫色彩的地区的过程中，四个民族的美德与恶行都曾展露无遗——而那个曾与葡萄牙人、西班牙人、荷兰人和英国人作战的种族，并未因不可避免的失败而改变。时至今日，他们仍保持着对自由的热爱、对首领的狂热效忠、友情与仇恨中的盲目信义——他们一切合法与不合法的本能。他们那水陆兼有的国度——因为海洋和岛上的陆地一样都是他们的国土——已落入西方种族之手——那是强者之酬报，即便不是美德之酬报。明天，节节推进的文明将在完成其必然的胜利之时，抹去长期斗争留下的痕迹。

开始那场斗争的冒险家们没有留下后裔。世界的观念变化得太快，不容他们有后裔。但直到本世纪很晚的时候，他们还不乏继承者。几乎就在我们自己的时代，我们还亲眼见过他们中的一人——一个真正凭冲动行事的冒险家——一个心灵高尚、心地纯洁的人，在怜悯与正义的理念之上奠定了一个繁荣国度的根基。他以骑士风度承认被征服者的权利；他是一个不谋私利的冒险家，他那高尚本能的报偿，便是一个奇异而忠实的种族至今对他的缅怀与敬仰。

他生前遭受误解与诽谤，而他事业的荣耀已为他动机的纯洁作了辩白。他已载入史册。但还有另一些人——些默默无闻的冒险家，他们没有他那种出身、地位与才智上的优势，所有的只是他那份对林海与海洋之民的同情，那些他深知且深爱的人民。不能说他们已被遗忘，因为他们根本不曾为人所知。他们湮没在群岛中以海为业的商人群体之中，即便从湮没无闻中露头，也只会被当作违法者而受到谴责。他们的生命被抛掷在一个面对不可抗拒的有序进步本无权存在的事业上——他们那不计后果的一生，只受一种单纯的情感所引导。
