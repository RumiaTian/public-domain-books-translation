## Author’s Note / 作者附记
===Original===

Of the three long novels of mine which suffered an interruption, *The Rescue* was the one that had to wait the longest for the good pleasure of the Fates. I am betraying no secret when I state here that it had to wait precisely for twenty years. I laid it aside at the end of the summer of 1898 and it was about the end of the summer of 1918 that I took it up again with the firm determination to see the end of it and helped by the sudden feeling that I might be equal to the task.

This does not mean that I turned to it with elation. I was well aware and perhaps even too much aware of the dangers of such an adventure. The amazingly sympathetic kindness which men of various temperaments, diverse views and different literary tastes have been for years displaying towards my work has done much for me, has done all﻿—except giving me that overweening self-confidence which may assist an adventurer sometimes but in the long run ends by leading him to the gallows.

As the characteristic I want most to impress upon these short Author’s Notes prepared for my first Collected Edition is that of absolute frankness, I hasten to declare that I founded my hopes not on my supposed merits but on the continued goodwill of my readers. I may say at once that my hopes have been justified out of all proportion to my deserts. I met with the most considerate, most delicately expressed criticism free from all antagonism and in its conclusions showing an insight which in itself could not fail to move me deeply, but was associated also with enough commendation to make me feel rich beyond the dreams of avarice﻿—I mean an artist’s avarice which seeks its treasure in the hearts of men and women.

No! Whatever the preliminary anxieties might have been this adventure was not to end in sorrow. Once more Fortune favoured audacity; and yet I have never forgotten the jocular translation of *Audaces fortuna juvat* offered to me by my tutor when I was a small boy: “The Audacious get bitten.” However he took care to mention that there were various kinds of audacity. Oh, there are, there are!﻿ ﻿… There is, for instance, the kind of audacity almost indistinguishable from impudence.﻿ ﻿… I must believe that in this case I have not been impudent for I am not conscious of having been bitten.

在我那三部曾遭中断的长篇小说中，《救援》是最不得不久久等候命运之神青睐的一部。我在此披露它恰恰等了二十年，并非泄露什么天机。1898年夏末我把它搁置一旁，直到1918年夏末前后才重新拿起，那时我怀着一定要把它写完的坚定决心，又得到一股突然涌上的自信，觉得自己或许胜任这项任务。

这并不意味着我是怀着欢欣鼓舞的心情转向它的。我十分清楚——甚至可能过分清楚——这样一场冒险的危险所在。多年来，性情各异、见解不同、文学趣味也互有差别的人们对我的作品表现出了惊人而温厚的善意，这善意为我做了许多，做了一切——唯独没有给我那种自负的自信。那种自信有时固然能助冒险家一臂之力，到头来却终究会把他送上绞架。

既然我最希望这些为初版文集准备的短小作者附记给人留下的印象是绝对的坦率，我便赶紧申明：我的希望并非寄托在自己想象中的优点上，而是寄托在读者们持续的好意上。我可以立刻说，这希望得到了远超我应得的回报。我得到的是最体贴、措辞最委婉的批评，不带丝毫敌意，其结论所显示的洞察力本身就足以令我深深动容；其中又夹带着足够的赞誉，使我自觉富有得超出了贪婪者的梦想——我说的是艺术家的贪婪，它到男男女女的心中去寻觅自己的财宝。

不！无论当初有过怎样的忐忑，这场冒险终究没有以忧伤告终。命运之神又一次眷顾了胆大的人；然而我从未忘记小时候我的家庭教师给我的一句戏谑的翻译：Audaces fortuna juvat——“胆大的人挨咬。”不过他特意说明，胆大也分好几种。哦，确实如此，确实如此！……比如，有一种胆大几乎与厚颜无耻难以区分。……我必须相信，这一次我并非厚颜无耻，因为我不觉得自己挨了咬。

The truth is that when *The Rescue* was laid aside it was not laid aside in despair. Several reasons contributed to this abandonment and, no doubt, the first of them was the growing sense of general difficulty in the handling of the subject. The contents and the course of the story I had clearly in my mind. But as to the way of presenting the facts, and perhaps in a certain measure as to the nature of the facts themselves, I had many doubts. I mean the telling, representative facts, helpful to carry on the idea, and, at the same time, of such a nature as not to demand an elaborate creation of the atmosphere to the detriment of the action. I did not see how I could avoid becoming wearisome in the presentation of detail and in the pursuit of clearness. I saw the action plainly enough. What I had lost for the moment was the sense of the proper formula of expression, the only formula that would suit. This, of course, weakened my confidence in the intrinsic worth and in the possible interest of the story﻿—that is in my invention. But I suspect that all the trouble was, in reality, the doubt of my prose, the doubt of its adequacy, of its power to master both the colours and the shades.

实情是，《救援》被搁置时，并非在绝望中被搁置的。有好几个原因促成了这次放弃，而其中头一个，无疑是对处理这一题材的普遍困难日益加重的感觉。故事的内容和走向我都了然于心。但对于呈现这些事实的方式，或许也在某种程度上对于这些事实本身的性质，我怀有许多疑虑。我指的是那些叙事性的、有代表性的、有助于推进主题的事实，它们同时又具备这样的性质：不致要求对氛围进行繁复的营造而损及行动本身。我看不出怎样才能在细节的呈现和对明晰的追求中不变得令人生厌。行动本身我看得够清楚了。我一时失去的，是对恰当的表达程式的感觉，是那唯一合适的程式。这自然削弱了我对故事内在价值和可能引起的兴趣的信心——也就是对我自己的构思创造的信心。但我怀疑，一切麻烦说到底，其实是对我的散文的怀疑，怀疑它是否够用，怀疑它驾驭色彩与明暗的能力。

It is difficult to describe, exactly as I remember it, the complex state of my feelings; but those of my readers who take an interest in artistic perplexities will understand me best when I point out that I dropped *The Rescue* not to give myself up to idleness, regrets, or dreaming, but to begin *The Nigger of the Narcissus* and to go on with it without hesitation and without a pause. A comparison of any page of *The Rescue* with any page of *The Nigger* will furnish an ocular demonstration of the nature and the inward meaning of this first crisis of my writing life. For it was a crisis undoubtedly. The laying aside of a work so far advanced was a very awful decision to take. It was wrung from me by a sudden conviction that *there* only was the road of salvation, the clear way out for an uneasy conscience. The finishing of *The Nigger* brought to my troubled mind the comforting sense of an accomplished task, and the first consciousness of a certain sort of mastery which could accomplish something with the aid of propitious stars. Why I did not return to *The Rescue* at once then, was not for the reason that I had grown afraid of it. Being able now to assume a firm attitude I said to myself deliberately: “That thing can wait.” At the same time I was just as certain in my mind that *Youth*, a story which I had then, so to speak, on the tip of my pen, could *not* wait. Neither could *Heart of Darkness* be put off; for the practical reason that Mr. Wm. Blackwood having requested me to write something for the No. M of his magazine I had to stir up at once the subject of that tale which had been long lying quiescent in my mind, because, obviously, the venerable Maga at her patriarchal age of 1000 numbers could not be kept waiting. Then *Lord Jim*, with about seventeen pages already written at odd times, put in his claim which was irresistible. Thus every stroke of the pen was taking me further away from the abandoned *The Rescue*, not without some compunction on my part but with a gradually diminishing resistance; till at last I let myself go as if recognising a superior influence against which it was useless to contend.

要把我记忆中那复杂的情绪状态原原本本描摹出来是困难的；但读者中那些对艺术上的困顿感兴趣的人，最能理解我下面这句话：我放下《救援》，不是为了沉溺于懒散、悔恨或空想，而是为了开始写《“水仙号”上的黑水手》，并且毫不犹豫、一气呵成地把它写了下去。把《救援》的任何一页与《“水仙号”》的任何一页对照一下，就可以直观地展示我写作生涯第一场危机的性质与内在含义。因为那无疑是一场危机。把一部已经写到如此地步的作品搁置起来，是一个极可怕的决断。它是被一种突然的信念从我身上强夺去的：唯有那条路是得救之路，是一条让不安的良心得以脱身的明路。写完《“水仙号”》之后，我纷乱的心里有了完成任务的安慰感，第一次意识到自己有了一种凭借顺遂的星宿便能有所成就的本领。那时我为什么没有立刻回到《救援》去，并不是因为我怕了它。既然如今能够采取坚定的态度，我便从容地对自己说：“那东西可以等。”与此同时，我心里也同样确信，《青春》——那个当时可谓已到了我笔尖的故事——是等不得的。《黑暗的心》也无法推迟，原因很实际：布莱克伍德先生请我为他的杂志第一千期写点东西，我只好立刻动手去搅动那个在我心中蛰伏已久的故事题材，因为显然，可敬的《玛迦》在她一千期的耄耋高龄是不容久候的。接着，《吉姆爷》带着已在零星时间里写就的约十七页书稿，提出了无法抗拒的要求。就这样，我的笔每一划都在把我带离那被弃的《救援》，并非没有几分愧疚，只是抗拒在渐渐减弱；直到最后我索性顺其自然，仿佛承认了一种无法与之抗衡的更高力量。

The years passed and the pages grew in number, and the long reveries of which they were the outcome stretched wide between me and the deserted *Rescue* like the smooth hazy spaces of a dreamy sea. Yet I never actually lost sight of that dark speck in the misty distance. It had grown very small but it asserted itself with the appeal of old associations. It seemed to me that it would be a base thing for me to slip out of the world leaving it out there all alone, waiting for its fate﻿—that would never come?

Sentiment, pure sentiment as you see, prompted me in the last instance to face the pains and hazards of that return. As I moved slowly towards the abandoned body of the tale it loomed up big amongst the glittering shallows of the coast, lonely but not forbidding. There was nothing about it of a grim derelict. It had an air of expectant life. One after another I made out the familiar faces watching my approach with faint smiles of amused recognition. They had known well enough that I was bound to come back to them. But their eyes met mine seriously as was only to be expected since I, myself, felt very serious as I stood amongst them again after years of absence. At once, without wasting words, we went to work together on our renewed life; and every moment I felt more strongly that They Who had Waited bore no grudge to the man who however widely he may have wandered at times had played truant only once in his life.

J. C.

1920

===Chinese===

年复一年，稿页越积越多，而由这些稿页孕育出的漫长冥想，像梦幻之海上平滑朦胧的片片水域，在我与那被遗弃的《救援》之间铺展开来。然而我从未真正看不见那雾霭深处的黑点。它已变得很小，却凭着旧日情谊的召唤固执地立在那里。我觉得，倘若自己悄悄溜出这个世界，把它孤零零地撇在外面，让它等候那永远不会到来的命运，那未免太卑怯了。

你们看，归根到底是情感，纯粹的情感，促使我甘冒重返的艰辛与风险。当我缓缓走向那被弃的故事之躯，它在那海岸波光粼粼的浅滩间巍然耸现，孤独却不狰狞。它身上绝没有阴森的残骸气，倒有一种期待生命的神态。我一张接一张地认出了那些熟悉的面孔，他们含着恍然重逢的浅笑望我走近。他们早就深知我注定要回到他们身边。但他们的目光与我的目光相遇时是郑重的，这也理所应当，因为多年阔别之后重新站到他们中间，我自己心里也极其郑重。我们没有浪费一言半语，立刻就一起开始了我们重续的生活；我时时刻刻都更强烈地感到，那些等待过的人们并不怨恨这个人——他虽然时有远游，一生却只逃过这一次学。

康拉德

1920年
