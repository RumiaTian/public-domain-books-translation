### XI / 第十一篇

===Original===
Indeed, it is less than nothing, and I have seen, when the great soul of the world turned over with a heavy sigh, a perfectly new, extra-stout foresail vanish like a bit of some airy stuff much lighter than gossamer. Then was the time for the tall spars to stand fast in the great uproar. The machinery must do its work even if the soul of the world has gone mad.

===Chinese===
诚然，那不过是蚊蚋之力，而我曾亲见，当世界之魂沉重地叹息着翻了个身，一面崭新的、特厚的前帆便像一缕比蛛丝还轻的飘渺之物，倏然消失在天地之间。那才是高耸的桅桁在惊天喧嚣中岿然屹立的时刻。即便世界之魂已经癫狂，机器也必须完成它的使命。

===Original===
The modern steamship advances upon a still and overshadowed sea with a pulsating tremor of her frame, an occasional clang in her depths, as if she had an iron heart in her iron body; with a thudding rhythm in her progress and the regular beat of her propeller, heard afar in the night with an august and plodding sound as of the march of an inevitable future. But in a gale, the silent machinery of a sailing-ship would catch not only the power, but the wild and exulting voice of the world's soul. Whether she ran with her tall spars swinging, or breasted it with her tall spars lying over, there was always that wild song, deep like a chant, for a bass to the shrill pipe of the wind played on the sea-tops, with a punctuating crash, now and then, of a breaking wave. At times the weird effects of that invisible orchestra would get upon a man's nerves till he wished himself deaf.

===Chinese===
现代汽船行驶在静谧而阴翳的海面上，船体微微震颤，深处偶发铿然之声，仿佛铁铸的躯壳里跳动着一颗铁打的心脏；它行进的节奏沉闷而有规律，螺旋桨的拍击声在夜里远远传来，庄严而迟缓，如同一种不可阻挡的未来在行进。但在风暴中，帆船那无声的机械所捕获的，不仅仅是力量，更是世界之魂那狂野而欢腾的歌喉。无论她高桅摇荡着飞驰，还是高桅倾倒着迎风搏击，那狂野的歌声始终如一——深沉如诵经，为风在浪尖上奏出的尖利笛音充当低音，间或以碎浪的轰然巨响为节拍。有时，那支隐形管弦乐队的诡异效果会侵入人的神经，令人恨不得自己是个聋子。

===Original===
And this recollection of a personal wish, experienced upon several oceans, where the soul of the world has plenty of room to turn over with a mighty sigh, brings me to the remark that in order to take a proper care of a ship's spars it is just as well for a seaman to have nothing the matter with his ears. Such is the intimacy with which a seaman had to live with his ship of yesterday that his senses were like her senses, that the stress upon his body made him judge of the strain upon the ship's masts.

===Chinese===
这段在几大洋上——世界之魂有足够空间施展其雄浑叹息的地方——所生出的私人愿望的回忆，引出了我的一个看法：要想妥善照料一艘船的桅桁，水手最好别让耳朵出毛病。昨日的水手与他的船之间的亲密关系便是如此：他的感官如同船的感官，他身上所承受的压力使他能判断船桅所承受的张力。

===Original===
I had been some time at sea before I became aware of the fact that hearing plays a perceptible part in gauging the force of the wind. It was at night. The ship was one of those iron wool-clippers that the Clyde had floated out in swarms upon the world during the seventh decade of the last century. It was a fine period in shipbuilding, and also, I might say, a period of over-masting. The spars rigged up on the narrow hulls were indeed tall then, and the ship of which I think, with her coloured-glass skylight ends bearing the motto, "Let Glasgow Flourish," was certainly one of the most heavily-sparred specimens. She was built for hard driving, and unquestionably she got all the driving she could stand.

===Chinese===
我出海已有相当一段时日，才意识到听觉在估测风力时所起的显著作用。那是一个夜晚。那条船是克莱德河（the Clyde）在上世纪七十年代成群结队地漂向世界的铁质运羊毛快帆船（iron wool-clipper）之一。那是一个造船的黄金时代，同时也可以说是一个桅杆过剩的时代。架在狭窄船体上的桅桁确实高得惊人，而我此刻想起的那条船，其彩色玻璃天窗端部镌刻着格言"愿格拉斯哥繁荣"（Let Glasgow Flourish），无疑是桅杆最为繁密的标本之一。她是为苦干而生的，而她也确实吃尽了一切她能承受的苦干。

===Original===
Our captain was a man famous for the quick passages he had been used to make in the old *Tweed*, a ship famous the world over for her speed. The *Tweed* had been a wooden vessel, and he brought the tradition of quick passages with him into the iron clipper. I was the junior in her, a third mate, keeping watch with the chief officer; and it was just during one of the night watches in a strong, freshening breeze that I overheard two men in a sheltered nook of the main deck exchanging these informing remarks. Said one:

===Chinese===
我们的船长以在老特威德号（Tweed）上创造快捷航速而闻名——那是一条全世界都因其速度而闻名的船。特威德号是一条木质船，而他把快捷航速的传统带进了这条铁质快帆船。我是船上的末等官——三副，与大副一起值更；就在一个风力渐强的夜间值更中，我在主甲板一个避风的角落里无意中听到了两个水手交换的如下高论。一个说：

===Original===
"Should think 'twas time some of them light sails were coming off her."

===Chinese===
"我看是时候把那些轻帆收几面下来了。"

===Original===
And the other, an older man, uttered grumpily:

===Chinese===
另一个年纪大些的，没好气地说：

===Original===
"No fear! not while the chief mate's on deck. He's that deaf he can't tell how much wind there is."

===Chinese===
"想都别想！只要大副还在甲板上。他聋得根本不知道风有多大。"

===Original===
And, indeed, poor P——, quite young, and a smart seaman, was very hard of hearing. At the same time, he had the name of being the very devil of a fellow for carrying on sail on a ship. He was wonderfully clever at concealing his deafness, and, as to carrying on heavily, though he was a fearless man, I don't think that he ever meant to take undue risks. I can never forget his naive sort of astonishment when remonstrated with for what appeared a most daredevil performance. The only person, of course, that could remonstrate with telling effect was our captain, himself a man of daredevil tradition; and really, for me, who knew under whom I was serving, those were impressive scenes. Captain S—— had a great name for sailor-like qualities—the sort of name that compelled my youthful admiration. To this day I preserve his memory, for, indeed, it was he in a sense who completed my training. It was often a stormy process, but let that pass. I am sure he meant well, and I am certain that never, not even at the time, could I bear him malice for his extraordinary gift of incisive criticism. And to hear *him* make a fuss about too much sail on the ship seemed one of those incredible experiences that take place only in one's dreams.

===Chinese===
确实，可怜的P先生（Mr. P——）年纪虽轻，却是个出色的水手，只是耳朵很背。与此同时，他又以猛撑帆的"亡命徒"名声著称。他隐藏耳聋的本事令人叹服，而关于猛撑帆——虽然他确实是个无所畏惧的人——我倒不认为他有意冒不必要的风险。我永远忘不了他那种天真的惊愕：有人对他那看似玩命的行径提出抗议时，他竟一脸莫名其妙。当然，唯一能有效劝阻他的人便是我们的船长（Captain S——）——他自己也是一个有着亡命徒传统的人；而对我来说，知道自己在谁手下当差，那些场面实在令人刻骨铭心。S船长以水手本色著称——那是一种令我年轻时肃然起敬的名声。时至今日我仍铭记着他，因为在某种意义上，正是他完成了我的训练。那常常是一个暴风骤雨般的过程，但暂且不提。我相信他本意是好的，我也确信，即便在当时，我也从未因他那令人叹服的犀利批评天赋而对他怀恨在心。而听到*他*因为船上帆挂得太多而大动肝火，简直就像是只在梦中才会发生的不可思议的经历。

===Original===
It generally happened in this way: Night clouds racing overhead, wind howling, royals set, and the ship rushing on in the dark, an immense white sheet of foam level with the lee rail. Mr. P——, in charge of the deck, hooked on to the windward mizzen rigging in a state of perfect serenity; myself, the third mate, also hooked on somewhere to windward of the slanting poop, in a state of the utmost preparedness to jump at the very first hint of some sort of order, but otherwise in a perfectly acquiescent state of mind. Suddenly, out of the companion would appear a tall, dark figure, bareheaded, with a short white beard of a perpendicular cut, very visible in the dark—Captain S——, disturbed in his reading down below by the frightful bounding and lurching of the ship. Leaning very much against the precipitous incline of the deck, he would take a turn or two, perfectly silent, hang on by the compass for a while, take another couple of turns, and suddenly burst out:

===Chinese===
事情通常是这样的：夜云在头顶疾驰，风在号叫，皇桅帆（royal）已经挂好，船在黑暗中向前猛冲，一大片白花花的浪沫与下风舷齐平。P大副（Mr. P——）负责甲板，神态自若地钩在上风舷后桅索具上；我，三副，也钩在倾斜的艉楼甲板上风的某个位置，随时准备一听到某种命令的暗示便一跃而起，除此之外则心如止水。突然，升降口里闪出一个高大黝黑的身影，没戴帽子，留着一绺垂直修剪的白色短须，在黑暗中清晰可辨——正是S船长，被船可怕的颠簸和摇晃从下面的读书中惊扰了。他斜倚在陡峭倾斜的甲板上，默默无声地踱了一两圈，抓住罗盘柜待一会儿，又踱了两圈，然后突然爆发：

===Original===
"What are you trying to do with the ship?"

===Chinese===
"你到底想把这条船怎么样？"

===Original===
And Mr. P——, who was not good at catching what was shouted in the wind, would say interrogatively:

===Chinese===
P大副——他不擅长在风中听清喊话——会试探性地问：

===Original===
"Yes, sir?"

===Chinese===
"什么事，先生？"

===Original===
Then in the increasing gale of the sea there would be a little private ship's storm going on in which you could detect strong language, pronounced in a tone of passion and exculpatory protestations uttered with every possible inflection of injured innocence.

===Chinese===
于是，在越来越猛烈的海风中，一场属于这条船的私人风暴便上演了，你能捕捉到暴烈的言辞，以激昂的语调喷出，伴随着无辜受伤的千般腔调的辩白。

===Original===
"By Heavens, Mr. P-! I used to carry on sail in my time, but—"

===Chinese===
"天哪，P先生！我当年也是猛撑帆的主儿，可——"

===Original===
And the rest would be lost to me in a stormy gust of wind.

===Chinese===
剩下的话便淹没在一阵暴风骤雨中了。

===Original===
Then, in a lull, P——'s protesting innocence would become audible:

===Chinese===
然后，在一个间隙里，P——那委屈的申辩变得可闻：

===Original===
"She seems to stand it very well."

===Chinese===
"她似乎扛得很好。"

===Original===
And then another burst of an indignant voice:

===Chinese===
接着又是一声愤怒的爆发：

===Original===
"Any fool can carry sail on a ship—"

===Chinese===
"随便哪个傻瓜都能往船上挂帆——"

===Original===
And so on and so on, the ship meanwhile rushing on her way with a heavier list, a noisier splutter, a more threatening hiss of the white, almost blinding, sheet of foam to leeward. For the best of it was that Captain S—— seemed constitutionally incapable of giving his officers a definite order to shorten sail; and so that extraordinarily vague row would go on till at last it dawned upon them both, in some particularly alarming gust, that it was time to do something. There is nothing like the fearful inclination of your tall spars overloaded with canvas to bring a deaf man and an angry one to their senses.

===Chinese===
就这样你来我往，与此同时船以更大的倾斜、更喧闹的飞沫、下风舷那白得几乎刺目的泡沫之帘发出更具威胁性的嘶嘶声向前疾驰。妙就妙在S船长似乎天生就没有给手下下达明确减帆命令的本事；于是那场格外含混不清的争吵便一直持续下去，直到某个特别令人惊骇的阵风终于让他们二位都醒悟过来：该做点什么了。要说有什么能一下子让一个聋子和一个怒汉清醒过来，没有什么比超负荷的高桅在帆重下那可怕的倾斜更有效的了。
