### XXVII / 第二十七篇

===Original===
Heralded by the increasing fierceness of the squalls, sometimes by a faint flash of lightning like the signal of a lighted torch waved far away behind the clouds, the shift of wind comes at last, the crucial moment of the change from the brooding and veiled violence of the southwest gale to the sparkling, flashing, cutting, clear-eyed anger of the King's northwesterly mood. You behold another phase of his passion, a fury bejewelled with stars, mayhap bearing the crescent of the moon on its brow, shaking the last vestiges of its torn cloud-mantle in inky-black squalls, with hail and sleet descending like showers of crystals and pearls, bounding off the spars, drumming on the sails, pattering on the oilskin coats, whitening the decks of homeward-bound ships.

===Chinese===
风向的转变终于来了——先是飑越来越猛烈，有时伴随一道隐约的闪光，像远处云层后面一支燃着的火炬在挥舞的信号。这一刻是关键的转折，从西南风暴那沉郁而遮蔽的暴虐，转向西北风凌厉、明澈、切肤的怒意。你目睹了这位君主激情的另一面：一腔镶满星辰的狂怒，也许眉间还悬着一弯月牙，在漆黑如墨的飑中抖落破烂云氅的最后残片；冰雹与雨雪如水晶和珍珠般倾泻而下，弹跳在帆桁上，鼓击着船帆，噼啪敲打着油布雨衣，将归航之船的甲板染成一片白色。

===Original===
Faint, ruddy flashes of lightning flicker in the starlight upon her mastheads. A chilly blast hums in the taut rigging, causing the ship to tremble to her very keel, and the soaked men on her decks to shiver in their wet clothes to the very marrow of their bones. Before one squall has flown over to sink in the eastern board, the edge of another peeps up already above the western horizon, racing up swift, shapeless, like a black bag full of frozen water ready to burst over your devoted head.

===Chinese===
微弱的、泛红的闪电在星光中明灭不定，映照在她的桅顶。一股寒风在绷紧的索具间嗡嗡作响，令船身从龙骨开始颤抖，甲板上浑身湿透的水手们裹着湿漉漉的衣服，冷到骨髓里去。一个飑还没飞过东边的天际沉落下去，另一个飑的边缘已从西边的海平线上探出头来，疾速攀升，没有形状，像一只装满冰水的黑袋子，即将在你头顶爆裂开来。

===Original===
The temper of the ruler of the ocean has changed. Each gust of the clouded mood that seemed warmed by the heat of a heart flaming with anger has its counterpart in the chilly blasts that seem blown from a breast turned to ice with a sudden revulsion of feeling. Instead of blinding your eyes and crushing your soul with a terrible apparatus of cloud and mists and seas and rain, the King of the West turns his power to contemptuous pelting of your back with icicles, to making your weary eyes water as if in grief, and your worn-out carcass quake pitifully. But each mood of the great autocrat has its own greatness, and each is hard to bear. Only the northwest phase of that mighty display is not demoralizing to the same extent, because between the hail and sleet squalls of a northwesterly gale one can see a long way ahead.

===Chinese===
海洋统治者的性情变了。那阴沉云层中每一阵似乎被愤怒之心的烈焰加热过的疾风，都对应着另一阵仿佛从一颗因骤然反悔而冻成冰的胸膛里吹出的寒冽之风。西风之王不再用云、雾、海与雨的恐怖装置来蒙蔽你的眼睛、碾碎你的灵魂，而是转而以冰雹轻蔑地敲打你的脊背，让你疲惫的双眼仿佛因悲伤而淌泪，让你筋疲力尽的可怜躯壳瑟瑟发抖。但这位大独裁者的每一种心绪都有其各自的壮阔，每一种都难以承受。只是在西北风的怒意中，那种骇人的景象不至于让人完全崩溃，因为在西北风的冰雹和雨雪飑之间，人还能望见前方很远。

===Original===
To see! to see!—this is the craving of the sailor, as of the rest of blind humanity. To have his path made clear for him is the aspiration of every human being in our beclouded and tempestuous existence. I have heard a reserved, silent man, with no nerves to speak of, after three days of hard running in thick southwesterly weather, burst out passionately: "I wish to God we could get sight of something!"

===Chinese===
看见！看见！——这是水手的渴望，也是所有盲目的人类共同的渴望。让前方的道路变得清晰可见，是每一个身处我们这阴云密布、风暴肆虐的存在中的人的夙愿。我曾听过一个沉默寡言、几乎没有神经可言的人，在浓重的西南天气中连续苦跑了三天之后，突然爆发出一声激烈的呐喊："老天保佑，哪怕让我们看见什么东西也好！"

===Original===
We had just gone down below for a moment to commune in a battened-down cabin, with a large white chart lying limp and damp upon a cold and clammy table under the light of a smoky lamp. Sprawling over that seaman's silent and trusted adviser, with one elbow upon the coast of Africa and the other planted in the neighbourhood of Cape Hatteras (it was a general track-chart of the North Atlantic), my skipper lifted his rugged, hairy face, and glared at me in a half-exasperated, half-appealing way. We had seen no sun, moon, or stars for something like seven days. By the effect of the West Wind's wrath the celestial bodies had gone into hiding for a week or more, and the last three days had seen the force of a southwest gale grow from fresh, through strong, to heavy, as the entries in my logbook could testify.

===Chinese===
我们刚刚下到舱里，在一间密封的舱室中待了片刻，一张大幅白色海图松垮而潮湿地铺在一张冰冷粘腻的桌子上，上方一盏冒烟的灯照着。我的船长——他粗糙多毛的脸庞半是恼怒半是恳求地瞪着我——整个人趴在那张水手们默然信赖的顾问之上，一只肘撑在非洲海岸附近，另一只搁在哈特拉斯角（Cape Hatteras）一带（那是一张北大西洋总航迹图）。我们大约有七天没有见过太阳、月亮或星辰了。在西风之怒的影响下，天体隐匿了整整一周有余，而最近三天的航程日志可以证明，西南风的威力从清劲一路飙升到猛烈再到狂暴。

===Original===
Then we separated, he to go on deck again, in obedience to that mysterious call that seems to sound forever in a shipmaster's ears, I to stagger into my cabin with some vague notion of putting down the words "Very heavy weather" in a logbook not quite written up-to-date. But I gave it up, and crawled into my bunk instead, boots and hat on, all standing (it did not matter; everything was soaking wet, a heavy sea having burst the poop skylights the night before), to remain in a nightmarish state between waking and sleeping for a couple of hours of so-called rest.

===Chinese===
然后我们分开了。他又回到甲板上去，听从那似乎永远在船长耳中回响的神秘召唤；而我则摇摇晃晃走进自己的舱室，模糊地想在尚未写完的航海日志上记下"天气极恶劣"几个字。但我放弃了，转而爬进了铺位，靴子、帽子什么都没脱——也没关系，一切都湿透了，前一天夜里一排巨浪冲破了艉楼的天窗。我就这样在似醒似睡的噩梦状态中躺了大约两个小时，权当所谓的休息。

===Original===
The southwesterly mood of the West Wind is an enemy of sleep, and even of a recumbent position, in the responsible officers of a ship. After two hours of futile, lightheaded, inconsequent thinking upon all things under heaven in that dark, dank, wet and devastated cabin, I arose suddenly and staggered up on deck. The autocrat of the North Atlantic was still oppressing his kingdom and its outlying dependencies, even as far as the Bay of Biscay, in the dismal secrecy of thick, very thick, weather.

===Chinese===
西风的西南心绪，对船上负有责任的军官们来说，是睡眠乃至躺卧的敌人。在那间黑暗、潮湿、被海水蹂躏过的舱室里，我徒劳地、晕乎乎地、漫无边际地胡思乱想了两个小时天地间的一切，然后突然起身，踉踉跄跄走上甲板。北大西洋的独裁者依然在压迫着他的王国及其偏远的属地，一直延伸到比斯开湾（Bay of Biscay），笼罩在浓密、极浓密的阴霾那令人沮丧的隐秘之中。

===Original===
The force of the wind, though we were running before it at the rate of some ten knots an hour, was so great that it drove me with a steady push to the front of the poop, where my commander was holding on.
"What do you think of it?" he addressed me in an interrogative yell.

===Chinese===
风力之猛——尽管我们正以大约每小时十节的速度顺风疾驶——把我一路稳稳地推到了艉楼前端，我的指挥官正紧紧扶着那里。
"你觉得怎么样？"他以一种质问般的喊声向我发话。

===Original===
What I really thought was that we both had had just about enough of it. The manner in which the great West Wind chooses at times to administer his possessions does not commend itself to a person of peaceful and law-abiding disposition, inclined to draw distinctions between right and wrong in the face of natural forces, whose standard, naturally, is that of might alone. But, of course, I said nothing.

===Chinese===
我真正想说的是，我们俩都差不多受够了。那位伟大的西风有时选择用来治理其领地的方式，对于一个性情平和、安分守己、倾向于在自然力量面前区分是非的人来说，实在不怎么令人欣赏——而自然力量的标准，说到底，不过是力量本身罢了。不过，我当然什么都没说。

===Original===
For a man caught, as it were, between his skipper and the great West Wind silence is the safest sort of diplomacy. Moreover, I knew my skipper. He did not want to know what I thought. Shipmasters hanging on a breath before the thrones of the winds ruling the seas have their psychology, whose workings are as important to the ship and those on board of her as the changing moods of the weather.

===Chinese===
对于一个夹在船长与伟大西风之间的人来说，沉默是最安全的外交。况且，我了解我的船长。他并不想知道我的想法。那些在主宰海洋的风的王座前屏息以待的船长们，自有他们的心理运作——这种运作对船只和船上的人的影响，与天气的变幻莫测不相上下。

===Original===
The man, as a matter of fact, under no circumstances, ever cared a brass farthing for what I or anybody else in his ship thought. He had had just about enough of it, I guessed, and what he was at really was a process of fishing for a suggestion. It was the pride of his life that he had never wasted a chance, no matter how boisterous, threatening, and dangerous, of a fair wind. Like men racing blindfold for a gap in a hedge, we were finishing a splendidly quick passage from the Antipodes, with a tremendous rush for the Channel in as thick a weather as any I can remember, but his psychology did not permit him to bring the ship to with a fair wind blowing—at least not on his own initiative. And yet he felt that very soon indeed something would have to be done. He wanted the suggestion to come from me, so that later on, when the trouble was over, he could argue this point with his own uncompromising spirit, laying the blame upon my shoulders.

===Chinese===
事实上，这个人无论在何种情形下，都从未在乎过我或船上其他人哪怕一丁点儿的想法。我猜他也差不多受够了，而他实际上做的，是一个套取建议的过程。他一生引以为傲的是，无论风多么凶猛、多么具有威胁性、多么危险，他从不浪费任何利用顺风的机会。像蒙着眼睛冲向篱笆缺口的人一样，我们正在完成一段从对跖地（the Antipodes）出发的极其快捷的航程，在我记忆中最浓重的天气里一路疾冲向海峡（the Channel），但他的心理不允许他在顺风正劲时停下船来——至少不能由他主动提出。然而，他确实感觉到很快不得不做点什么了。他想让建议从我口中说出，这样日后等麻烦过去了，他可以跟自己那绝不妥协的精神争辩这一点，把责任推到我肩上。

===Original===
I must render him the justice that this sort of pride was his only weakness.
But he got no suggestion from me. I understood his psychology. Besides, I had my own stock of weaknesses at the time (it is a different one now), and amongst them was the conceit of being remarkably well up in the psychology of the Westerly Weather. I believed—not to mince matters—that I had a genius for reading the mind of the great ruler of high latitudes. I fancied I could discern already the coming of a change in his royal mood. And all I said was:
"The weather's bound to clear up with the shift of wind."
"Anybody knows that much!" he snapped at me, at the highest pitch of his voice.

===Chinese===
我必须为他说句公道话：这种骄傲是他唯一的弱点。
但他从我这里得不到建议。我理解他的心理。况且，我那时也有自己的一堆弱点（如今换成了另一堆），其中之一便是自以为对西风天气的心理了如指掌。我不怕说得直白——我相信自己有解读那位高纬度大统治者心思的天赋。我自以为已经能察觉到他皇家心绪即将发生的变化。而我说的不过是：
"风一转，天气必定会放晴。"
"这谁都知道！"他以最高的嗓门对我厉声说道。

===Original===
"I mean before dark!" I cried.
This was all the opening he ever got from me. The eagerness with which he seized upon it gave me the measure of the anxiety he had been labouring under.

===Chinese===
"我是说天黑之前！"我喊道。
这就是他从我这里得到的全部暗示。他急切地抓住这个暗示的劲头，让我掂量出了他一直在承受的焦虑有多深。

===Original===
"Very well," he shouted, with an affectation of impatience, as if giving way to long entreaties. "All right. If we don't get a shift by then we'll take that foresail off her and put her head under her wing for the night."

===Chinese===
"好吧，"他喊道，装出一副不耐烦的样子，仿佛是在长久的恳求之后终于让步。"行。到时候要是风还没转，我们就收掉前帆，让她把头埋在翅膀底下过夜。"

===Original===
I was struck by the picturesque character of the phrase as applied to a ship brought-to in order to ride out a gale with wave after wave passing under her breast. I could see her resting in the tumult of the elements like a seabird sleeping in wild weather upon the raging waters with its head tucked under its wing. In imaginative precision, in true feeling, this is one of the most expressive sentences I have ever heard on human lips.

===Chinese===
这番话用在一艘顶风停船以度过风暴——巨浪一波又一波从她胸膛下涌过——的船上，其生动的画面感令我为之一震。我能看见她在元素的喧嚣中栖息，像一只在狂风暴雨中栖于怒涛之上的海鸟，把头埋在翅膀下。在想象的精确和真实的情感上，这是我听过的最具表现力的句子之一。

===Original===
But as to taking the foresail off that ship before we put her head under her wing, I had my grave doubts. They were justified. That long enduring piece of canvas was confiscated by the arbitrary decree of the West Wind, to whom belong the lives of men and the contrivances of their hands within the limits of his kingdom. With the sound of a faint explosion it vanished into the thick weather bodily, leaving behind of its stout substance not so much as one solitary strip big enough to be picked into a handful of lint for, say, a wounded elephant. Torn out of its bolt-ropes, it faded like a whiff of smoke in the smoky drift of clouds shattered and torn by the shift of wind.

===Chinese===
但说到在我们让船把头埋进翅膀之前先收掉前帆，我心中满是疑虑。而这些疑虑是有道理的。那块饱经风霜的帆布被西风的一道独断专令没收了——在他的王国范围内，人的生命和人手的造物都归他所有。伴随着一声微弱的爆裂声，它整个地消失在浓重的天气里，它那结实的躯体连一条能捡起来搓成一小把敷料——比方说给一头受伤的大象用的——的碎布条都没留下。它被从帆边绳上撕下来，在被风向突变撕碎的云层的烟雾般的飘流中，像一缕烟般消散了。

===Original===
For the shift of wind had come. The unveiled, low sun glared angrily from a chaotic sky upon a confused and tremendous sea dashing itself upon a coast. We recognized the headland, and looked at each other in the silence of dumb wonder. Without knowing it in the least, we had run up alongside the Isle of Wight, and that tower, tinged a faint evening red in the salt wind-haze, was the lighthouse on St. Catherine's Point.

===Chinese===
因为风向已经转了。云翳散开的低垂太阳，从混乱的天空中愤怒地照耀着一片混乱的、滔天巨浪拍击海岸的汪洋。我们认出了那个海岬，在无言的惊愕中面面相觑。我们丝毫不知道自己已经不知不觉地驶到了怀特岛（Isle of Wight）旁边——那座在咸涩风雾中泛着淡淡暮红色的塔楼，正是圣凯瑟琳角（St. Catherine's Point）的灯塔。

===Original===
My skipper recovered first from his astonishment. His bulging eyes sank back gradually into their orbits. His psychology, taking it all round, was really very creditable for an average sailor. He had been spared the humiliation of laying his ship to with a fair wind; and at once that man, of an open and truthful nature, spoke up in perfect good faith, rubbing together his brown, hairy hands—the hands of a master-craftsman upon the sea:
"Humph! that's just about where I reckoned we had got to."
The transparency and ingenuousness, in a way, of that delusion, the airy tone, the hint of already growing pride, were perfectly delicious. But, in truth, this was one of the greatest surprises ever sprung by the clearing up mood of the West Wind upon one of the most accomplished of his courtiers.

===Chinese===
我的船长最先从惊愕中回过神来。他那双鼓起的眼睛慢慢缩回了眼眶。总体而言，他的心理表现对于一个普通水手来说确实相当不错。他免于在顺风中停船的屈辱；于是这个性格开朗、诚实的人立刻以十足的诚意开口说道，一边搓着他那双棕色的、毛茸茸的手——那是一位海上匠人的手：
"哼！我估摸着咱们大概就在这一带。"
那种自欺的透明与天真、那轻飘飘的语调、那隐隐已经冒出来的骄傲，实在令人忍俊不禁。但说实话，这是西风的放晴心绪有史以来向他最出色的朝臣发动的最大突袭之一。
