### XXII / 第二十二篇

===Original===
It seems to me that no man born and truthful to himself could declare that he ever saw the sea looking young as the earth looks young in spring. But some of us, regarding the ocean with understanding and affection, have seen it looking old, as if the immemorial ages had been stirred up from the undisturbed bottom of ooze. For it is a gale of wind that makes the sea look old.

From a distance of years, looking at the remembered aspects of the storms lived through, it is that impression which disengages itself clearly from the great body of impressions left by many years of intimate contact.

If you would know the age of the earth, look upon the sea in a storm. The greyness of the whole immense surface, the wind furrows upon the faces of the waves, the great masses of foam, tossed about and waving, like matted white locks, give to the sea in a gale an appearance of hoary age, lustreless, dull, without gleams, as though it had been created before light itself.

===Chinese===
在我看来，一个生而忠实于自我的人，恐怕说不出他曾见过大海如春天的大地那般青春焕发。但我们中有些人，怀着理解与深情凝望大海，却见过它苍老的模样，仿佛那亘古的岁月已从未受惊扰的淤泥深处被搅动起来。因为，是狂风让大海显出老态。

从多年以后回望，审视那些历经风暴的记忆留下的种种印象，唯有这种苍老之感，从多年亲密接触留下的庞杂印象中清晰地剥离出来。

若想知晓大地的年岁，就去看风暴中的大海。那整个无边无际的灰色水面，那风在浪尖上犁出的沟壑，那翻腾摇曳的巨大泡沫团，如同缠结的白发，赋予了风暴中的大海一种苍老的模样，灰暗、沉闷、毫无光泽，仿佛它诞生于光本身之前。

===Original===
Looking back after much love and much trouble, the instinct of primitive man, who seeks to personify the forces of Nature for his affection and for his fear, is awakened again in the breast of one civilized beyond that stage even in his infancy. One seems to have known gales as enemies, and even as enemies one embraces them in that affectionate regret which clings to the past.

Gales have their personalities, and, after all, perhaps it is not strange; for, when all is said and done, they are adversaries whose wiles you must defeat, whose violence you must resist, and yet with whom you must live in the intimacies of nights and days.

Here speaks the man of masts and sails, to whom the sea is not a navigable element, but an intimate companion. The length of passages, the growing sense of solitude, the close dependence upon the very forces that, friendly today, without changing their nature, by the mere putting forth of their might, becomes dangerous tomorrow, make for that sense of fellowship which modern seamen, good men as they are, cannot hope to know. And, besides, your modern ship which is a steamship makes her passages on other principles than yielding to the weather and humouring the sea. She receives smashing blows, but she advances; it is a slogging fight, and not a scientific campaign. The machinery, the steel, the fire, the steam, have stepped in between the man and the sea. A modern fleet of ships does not so much make use of the sea as exploit a highway. The modern ship is not the sport of the waves. Let us say that each of her voyages is a triumphant progress; and yet it is a question whether it is not a more subtle and more human triumph to be the sport of the waves and yet survive, achieving your end.

===Chinese===
在历经许多爱与许多磨难之后回首往事，那种原始人的本能——为了寄托爱与恐惧而将自然力拟人化的本能——在一个人的心中再度苏醒，尽管他在幼年时便已超越了那一阶段。人们似乎曾将狂风视为敌人，而即便作为敌人，也带着那份萦绕着过去的眷恋之情去拥抱它们。

狂风自有其个性，而这，归根结底，或许并不奇怪；因为，说到底，它们是你必须智胜其诡计、抵抗其暴虐，却又不得不与之共度日夜、亲密相处的对手。

此刻发言的，是一位与桅杆和帆为伴的人，在他眼中，大海并非可通航的介质，而是亲密的伴侣。漫长的航程，日益滋长的孤寂感，以及对那今日友善、明日仅凭施展其伟力便可骤然变得危险的自然力的深切依赖，共同营造出一种同袍情谊——这是现代水手，无论他们多么优秀，都无法企及的。况且，你们的现代轮船，其航行原则已非顺应天气、取悦大海。它承受着猛烈的撞击，却依然前行；这是一场肉搏战，而非一场科学战役。机器、钢铁、火焰与蒸汽，已介入人与大海之间。一支现代船队与其说是利用大海，不如说是开发一条通途。现代轮船不是浪花的玩物。不妨说，它的每一次航行都是一次凯旋的进军；然而，被浪花玩弄却依然存活下来，最终达成目的——这是否是一种更微妙、更具人性的胜利，倒是个值得思索的问题。

===Original===
In his own time a man is always very modern. Whether the seamen of three hundred years hence will have the faculty of sympathy it is impossible to say. An incorrigible mankind hardens its heart in the progress of its own perfectability. How will they feel on seeing the illustrations to the sea novels of our day, or of our yesterday? It is impossible to guess. But the seaman of the last generation, brought into sympathy with the caravels of ancient time by his sailing-ship, their lineal descendant, cannot look upon those lumbering forms navigating the naive seas of ancient woodcuts without a feeling of surprise, of affectionate derision, envy, and admiration. For those things, whose unmanageableness, even when represented on paper, makes one gasp with a sort of amused horror, were manned by men who are his direct professional ancestors.

No; the seamen of three hundred years hence will probably be neither touched nor moved to derision, affection, or admiration. They will glance at the photogravures of our nearly defunct sailing-ships with a cold, inquisitive and indifferent eye. Our ships of yesterday will stand to their ships as no lineal ancestors, but as mere predecessors whose course will have been run and the race extinct. Whatever craft he handles with skill, the seaman of the future shall be, not our descendant, but only our successor.

===Chinese===
一个人身处其时代时，总是觉得自己非常现代。至于三百年后的水手是否还具备共情的能力，谁也说不准。人类那不可救药的本性，在追求自身完美的进程中，渐渐硬化了心肠。他们看到我们这个时代，或我们昨日时代的航海小说插图时，会作何感想？这无从猜测。但上一代水手，因其帆船——那些古老卡拉维尔帆船（caravels）的直系后裔——而对古代产生了共情，他们看到那些笨拙的船形在古木刻版画中描绘的稚拙海洋里航行，不可能不感到惊讶、带着爱怜的嘲弄、羡慕与钦佩。因为那些家伙，即便只是在纸面上呈现其难以驾驭的模样，也足以让人带着一种有趣的惊骇倒吸一口凉气，而驾驭它们的，正是他职业上直接的先辈。

不；三百年后的水手，很可能既不会动容，也不会生出嘲弄、爱怜或钦佩之情。他们会用冷漠、好奇而无动于衷的目光，扫视我们那些几乎消亡的帆船的照片。我们的昨日之船，对他们的船而言，并非直系祖先，而仅仅是先行者，其航程已尽，血脉已绝。无论未来水手娴熟地操弄何种船只，他都不是我们的后代，而仅仅是我们的继任者。
