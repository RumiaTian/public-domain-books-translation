### XLVIII / 第四十八篇

===Original===
And yet the fact remains that, had the wind failed and the fleet lost steerage way, or, worse still, had it been taken aback from the eastward, with its leaders within short range of the enemy's guns, nothing, it seems, could have saved the headmost ships from capture or destruction. No skill of a great sea officer would have availed in such a contingency. Lord Nelson was more than that, and his genius would have remained undiminished by defeat. But obviously tactics, which are so much at the mercy of irremediable accident, must seem to a modern seaman a poor matter of study. The Commander-in-Chief in the great fleet action that will take its place next to the Battle of Trafalgar in the history of the British navy will have no such anxiety, and will feel the weight of no such dependence. For a hundred years now no British fleet has engaged the enemy in line of battle. A hundred years is a long time, but the difference of modern conditions is enormous. The gulf is great. Had the last great fight of the English navy been that of the First of June, for instance, had there been no Nelson's victories, it would have been well-nigh impassable. The great Admiral's slight and passion-worn figure stands at the parting of the ways. He had the audacity of genius, and a prophetic inspiration.

===Chinese===
然而事实依然如此：倘若风力不济，舰队失去舵效——或者更糟，倘若舰队被东风逼得逆风掉戗，先导舰距敌舰炮火射程之内——那么，看起来没有什么能够挽救最前面的舰船免于被俘或被毁。再伟大的海军将领的才能，在这样的紧要关头也无济于事。纳尔逊勋爵（Lord Nelson）超越了将领的范畴，他的天才不会因失败而有丝毫减损。但战术这门学问如此受制于不可挽回的意外，对现代海员来说显然不值一研。在英国海军史上，紧随特拉法尔加海战（Battle of Trafalgar）之后的伟大舰队决战中，总司令将不再有那样的焦虑，也不必承受那样的依赖之重。一百年来，英国舰队再没有以战列线迎击过敌人。一百年是很长的时间，但现代条件的变化更是巨大。这道鸿沟是深广的。倘若英国海军的最后一场大海战是一七九四年六月一日（First of June）海战，倘若没有纳尔逊的胜利，这道鸿沟几乎不可逾越。这位伟大海军上将瘦削而饱经激情磨损的身影，矗立在分叉路口。他有着天才的胆魄，以及先知般的灵感。

===Original===
The modern naval man must feel that the time has come for the tactical practice of the great sea officers of the past to be laid by in the temple of august memories. The fleet tactics of the sailing days have been governed by two points: the deadly nature of a raking fire, and the dread, natural to a commander dependent upon the winds, to find at some crucial moment part of his fleet thrown hopelessly to leeward. These two points were of the very essence of sailing tactics, and these two points have been eliminated from the modern tactical problem by the changes of propulsion and armament. Lord Nelson was the first to disregard them with conviction and audacity sustained by an unbounded trust in the men he led. This conviction, this audacity and this trust stand out from amongst the lines of the celebrated memorandum, which is but a declaration of his faith in a crushing superiority of fire as the only means of victory and the only aim of sound tactics. Under the difficulties of the then existing conditions he strove for that, and for that alone, putting his faith into practice against every risk. And in that exclusive faith Lord Nelson appears to us as the first of the moderns.

Against every risk, I have said; and the men of today, born and bred to the use of steam, can hardly realize how much of that risk was in the weather. Except at the Nile, where the conditions were ideal for engaging a fleet moored in shallow water, Lord Nelson was not lucky in his weather. Practically it was nothing but a quite unusual failure of the wind which cost him his arm during the Teneriffe expedition. On Trafalgar Day the weather was not so much unfavourable as extremely dangerous.

===Chinese===
现代海军人必定感到，将过往伟大海军将领的战术实践供奉于庄严记忆的殿堂之中的时刻已经到来。帆船时代的舰队战术受制于两点：舷侧纵射火力的致命性，以及一个依赖风力的指挥官在某个关键时刻发现部分舰队被无望地抛到下风处的恐惧。这两点是帆船战术的精髓，而动力与武器的革新已将这两点从现代战术问题中彻底消除。纳尔逊勋爵是第一个以信念和胆魄——以及对他麾下将士的无限信任——将这两点弃之不顾的人。这种信念、这种胆魄和这种信任，在那份著名的备忘录的字里行间格外醒目；那份备忘录不过是他在宣告自己的信仰——压倒性的火力优势是胜利的唯一途径，也是健全战术的唯一目标。在当时艰难的条件下，他为此而奋斗，仅此而已，冒着一切风险将信念付诸实践。在这份专一的信仰中，纳尔逊勋爵在我们眼中成了现代人的先驱。

我说过"冒着一切风险"；而今天这些生于蒸汽、长于蒸汽的人，几乎无法想象那风险中有多少来自天气。除了在尼罗河海战（the Nile）——在那里，条件理想，敌舰队锚泊在浅水中——之外，纳尔逊勋爵在天气上并不走运。事实上，特内里费（Teneriffe）远征中令他失去一条手臂的，不过是一次相当罕见的风力中断。特拉法尔加日当天的天气与其说是不利，不如说是极度危险。

It was one of these covered days of fitful sunshine, of light, unsteady winds, with a swell from the westward, and hazy in general, but with the land about the Cape at times distinctly visible. It has been my lot to look with reverence upon the very spot more than once, and for many hours together. All but thirty years ago, certain exceptional circumstances made me very familiar for a time with that bight in the Spanish coast which would be enclosed within a straight line drawn from Faro to Spartel. My well-remembered experience has convinced me that, in that corner of the ocean, once the wind has got to the northward of west (as it did on the 20th, taking the British fleet aback), appearances of westerly weather go for nothing, and that it is infinitely more likely to veer right round to the east than to shift back again. It was in those conditions that, at seven on the morning of the 21st, the signal for the fleet to bear up and steer east was made. Holding a clear recollection of these languid easterly sighs rippling unexpectedly against the run of the smooth swell, with no other warning than a ten-minutes' calm and a queer darkening of the coastline, I cannot think, without a gasp of professional awe, of that fateful moment. Perhaps personal experience, at a time of life when responsibility had a special freshness and importance, has induced me to exaggerate to myself the danger of the weather. The great Admiral and good seaman could read aright the signs of sea and sky, as his order to prepare to anchor at the end of the day sufficiently proves; but, all the same, the mere idea of these baffling easterly airs, coming on at any time within half an hour or so, after the firing of the first shot, is enough to take one's breath away, with the image of the rearmost ships of both divisions falling off, unmanageable, broadside on to the westerly swell, and of two British Admirals in desperate jeopardy. To this day I cannot free myself from the impression that, for some forty minutes, the fate of the great battle hung upon a breath of wind such as I have felt stealing from behind, as it were, upon my cheek while engaged in looking to the westward for the signs of the true weather.

那是阴云密布的一天，阳光时隐时现，微风轻拂不定，西面涌来长浪，普遍朦胧，但海角周围的陆地有时清晰可见。我此生有幸不止一次怀着敬畏之心凝望过那个地方，一望就是好几个小时。将近三十年前，某些特殊的际遇使我一度对西班牙海岸上法罗角（Faro）至斯帕特尔角（Spartel）之间那片海湾非常熟悉。我记忆犹新的经验使我相信，在海洋的那个角落，一旦风向转到正西以北（正如二十日那天，将英国舰队逼得逆风掉戗），一切西风天气的表象都不足为凭，风向更可能直接转到正东，而非移回西面。正是在这样的条件下，二十一日早晨七时，舰队转向正东航行的信号发出了。我清楚地记得那些慵懒的东风叹息——出人意料地吹皱了平滑涌浪的流势——除了十分钟的短暂静风和海岸线诡异地变暗之外别无预警。每思及那个决定命运的时刻，我便不由自主地倒吸一口充满职业敬畏的冷气。也许在那个年纪——责任感带着格外鲜活的分量——的个人经历，使我在心中放大了天气的危险。那位伟大的海军上将、优秀的海员能够正确解读海天的征兆，他下令在当天结束时准备锚泊便足以证明；但即便如此，光是想到这些令人困惑的东风——在第一声炮响之后的半小时内随时可能吹来——就足以令人窒息，脑海里浮现两支纵队最末尾的舰船失控偏转、侧面对着西风涌浪的画面，以及两位英国海军上将身陷绝境的情景。直到今天，我仍然无法摆脱这样的印象：在大约四十分钟里，这场伟大战役的命运悬于一缕微风——正如我在望向西方辨认真正天象时，感受过从身后悄然拂过面颊的那缕微风。

===Original===
Never more shall British seamen going into action have to trust the success of their valour to a breath of wind. The God of gales and battles favouring her arms to the last, has let the sun of England's sailing-fleet and of its greatest master set in unclouded glory. And now the old ships and their men are gone; the new ships and the new men, many of them bearing the old, auspicious names, have taken up their watch on the stern and impartial sea, which offers no opportunities but to those who know how to grasp them with a ready hand and an undaunted heart.

===Chinese===
从此以后，英国海员投入战斗，再也不必把勇气的成败托付给一缕风了。大风与海战之神眷顾英吉利的武装至最后一刻，让英格兰帆船舰队及其最伟大的统帅在无云的荣光中落下太阳。如今，旧船与旧人已逝；新船与新人——其中许多承继了那些古老而吉祥的名字——已在严厉而公正的大海上接替了他们的守望。大海不会给任何人以机会，除非你以敏捷的双手和无畏的心灵去把握它。
