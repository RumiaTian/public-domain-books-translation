### XXVI / 第二十六篇

===Original===
The West Wind reigns over the seas surrounding the coasts of these kingdoms; and from the gateways of the channels, from promontories as if from watchtowers, from estuaries of rivers as if from postern gates, from passageways, inlets, straits, firths, the garrison of the Isle and the crews of the ships going and returning look to the westward to judge by the varied splendours of his sunset mantle the mood of that arbitrary ruler. The end of the day is the time to gaze at the kingly face of the Westerly Weather, who is the arbiter of ships’ destinies. Benignant and splendid, or splendid and sinister, the western sky reflects the hidden purposes of the royal mind. Clothed in a mantle of dazzling gold or draped in rags of black clouds like a beggar, the might of the Westerly Wind sits enthroned upon the western horizon with the whole North Atlantic as a footstool for his feet and the first twinkling stars making a diadem for his brow. Then the seamen, attentive courtiers of the weather, think of regulating the conduct of their ships by the mood of the master. The West Wind is too great a king to be a dissembler: he is no calculator plotting deep schemes in a sombre heart; he is too strong for small artifices; there is passion in all his moods, even in the soft mood of his serene days, in the grace of his blue sky whose immense and unfathomable tenderness reflected in the mirror of the sea embraces, possesses, lulls to sleep the ships with white sails. He is all things to all oceans; he is like a poet seated upon a throne—magnificent, simple, barbarous, pensive, generous, impulsive, changeable, unfathomable—but when you understand him, always the same. Some of his sunsets are like pageants devised for the delight of the multitude, when all the gems of the royal treasure-house are displayed above the sea. Others are like the opening of his royal confidence, tinged with thoughts of sadness and compassion in a melancholy splendour meditating upon the short-lived peace of the waters. And I have seen him put the pent-up anger of his heart into the aspect of the inaccessible sun, and cause it to glare fiercely like the eye of an implacable autocrat out of a pale and frightened sky.

西风统治着环绕这些王国海岸的海洋；从海峡的门户，从仿佛瞭望塔般的岬角，从仿佛侧门般的河口，从航道、小湾、海峡、河口湾，岛上的守军与来往船只的船员们，都向西眺望，试图从西风那变幻的落日华服中，判断这位专横统治者的心境。一日之末，正是凝望西风那王者面容的时刻——他是船舶命运的仲裁者。温和而辉煌，或辉煌而险恶，西方的天空映照出那位王者心中不可告人的意图。身披耀眼金袍，或如乞丐般披着褴褛的黑云，西风的伟力端坐于西方地平线的宝座之上，整个北大西洋化作他脚下的脚凳，初现的闪烁星辰在他额前缀成冠冕。此时，水手们——天气那恭谨的朝臣——便想着依照主子的心情来调适自己船只的航向。西风是如此伟大的王者，不屑于伪装：他不是那种在阴暗心底盘算深谋的阴谋家；他过于强大，无需雕虫小技；他的一切情绪都饱含激情，即便在他宁静日子的柔和心境中，在他蓝天的优雅里——那浩瀚而深不可测的温柔，倒映在大海的镜中，拥抱、俘获、催眠着白帆的船只。他对所有海洋都一视同仁；他宛如一位端坐王座的诗人——雄浑、质朴、野蛮、沉思、慷慨、冲动、善变、深不可测——但当你理解了他，他始终如一。他的一些落日，宛若为取悦大众而设计的盛典，将王家宝库中的全部珍宝陈列于海面之上。另一些则像王内心的倾诉，带着哀思与怜悯的忧郁华彩，沉思着水面那短暂的和平。我曾见过他将胸中郁结的怒火，注入那遥不可及的太阳的面容，使它从苍白而惊恐的天空中，如一位无情专制者的眼睛般凶狠地怒视。

===Original===
He is the warlord who sends his battalions of Atlantic rollers to the assault of our seaboard. The compelling voice of the West Wind musters up to his service all the might of the ocean. At the bidding of the West Wind there arises a great commotion in the sky above these Islands, and a great rush of waters falls upon our shores. The sky of the Westerly Weather is full of flying clouds, of great big white clouds coming thicker and thicker till they seem to stand welded into a solid canopy, upon whose grey face the lower wrack of the gale, thin, black and angry-looking, flies past with vertiginous speed. Denser and denser grows this dome of vapours, descending lower and lower upon the sea, narrowing the horizon around the ship. And the characteristic aspect of Westerly Weather, the thick, grey, smoky, and sinister tone sets in, circumscribing the view of the men, drenching their bodies, oppressing their souls, taking their breath away with booming gusts, deafening, blinding, driving, rushing them onwards in a swaying ship towards our coasts lost in mists and rain.

The caprice of the winds, like the wilfulness of men, is fraught with the disastrous consequences of self-indulgence. Long anger, the sense of his uncontrolled power, spoils the frank and generous nature of the West Wind. It is as if his heart were corrupted by a malevolent and brooding rancour. He devastates his own kingdom in the wantonness of his force. Southwest is the quarter of the heavens where he presents his darkened brow. He breathes his rage in terrific squalls, and overwhelms his realm with an inexhaustible welter of clouds. He strews the seeds of anxiety upon the decks of scudding ships, makes the foam-stripped ocean look old, and sprinkles with grey hairs the heads of shipmasters in the homeward-bound ships running for the Channel. The Westerly Wind asserting his sway from the southwest quarter is often like a monarch gone mad, driving forth with wild imprecations the most faithful of his courtiers to shipwreck, disaster, and death.

他是那位派遣大西洋涌浪军团袭击我们海岸的军阀。西风那威严的号令，召唤起海洋的全部伟力为其效劳。应西风之令，这些岛屿上空的天空便掀起巨大的骚动，汹涌的潮水扑向我们的海岸。西风天气的天空满是飞驰的云朵，巨大的白云越来越密，直至仿佛焊结成一个坚实的穹顶，而风暴下层那稀薄、黝黑、狰狞的云絮，在其灰色的表面上，以令人目眩的速度飞掠而过。这个蒸汽的穹顶变得越来越浓密，越压越低地逼近海面，不断收窄船只周围的地平线。西风天气的典型面貌——那浓重、灰暗、烟熏般、险恶的色调——开始降临，遮蔽了人们的视野，淋湿他们的身体，压抑他们的灵魂，用轰鸣的狂风夺走他们的呼吸，令人耳聋、目盲，裹挟着他们在摇晃的船中疾驰，冲向我们那迷失在雾雨中的海岸。

风的任性，如同人的固执，往往带来放纵自身的灾难性后果。长久的愤怒，对其不受约束之力的自觉，败坏了西风坦率而慷慨的本性。仿佛他的心已被恶毒而郁结的怨恨所腐蚀。他在力量的恣意妄为中，蹂躏着自己的王国。西南方向是他展现那阴沉眉宇的天空方位。他在可怕的飑风中呼出怒火，用无穷无尽翻腾的云层淹没他的疆域。他将焦虑的种子播撒在疾驰的船只甲板上，让被浪花剥去装饰的海洋显出苍老，让驶向海峡的返航船只的船长们头上平添几缕灰发。从西南方位施展其权威的西风，常似一位发狂的君主，用狂乱的诅咒，将他最忠诚的朝臣驱向海难、灾祸与死亡。

The Southwesterly Weather is the thick weather *par excellence*. It is not the thickness of the fog; it is rather a contraction of the horizon, a mysterious veiling of the shores with clouds that seem to make a low-vaulted dungeon around the running ship. It is not blindness; it is a shortening of the sight. The West Wind does not say to the seaman, “You shall be blind”; it restricts merely the range of his vision and raises the dread of land within his breast. It makes of him a man robbed of half his force, of half his efficiency. Many times in my life, standing in long sea-boots and streaming oilskins at the elbow of my commander on the poop of a homeward-bound ship making for the Channel, and gazing ahead into the grey and tormented waste, I have heard a weary sigh shape itself into a studiously casual comment:

“Can’t see very far in this weather.”

And have made answer in the same low, perfunctory tone:

“No, sir.”

It would be merely the instinctive voicing of an ever-present thought associated closely with the consciousness of the land somewhere ahead and of the great speed of the ship. Fair wind, fair wind! Who would dare to grumble at a fair wind? It was a favour of the Western King, who rules masterfully the North Atlantic from the latitude of the Azores to the latitude of Cape Farewell. A famous shove this to end a good passage with; and yet, somehow, one could not muster upon one’s lips the smile of a courtier’s gratitude. This favour was dispensed to you from under an overbearing scowl, which is the true expression of the great autocrat when he has made up his mind to give a battering to some ships and to hunt certain others home in one breath of cruelty and benevolence, equally distracting.

“No, sir. Can’t see very far.”

Thus would the mate’s voice repeat the thought of the master, both gazing ahead, while under their feet the ship rushes at some twelve knots in the direction of the lee shore; and only a couple of miles in front of her swinging and dripping jibboom, carried naked with an upward slant like a spear, a grey horizon closes the view with a multitude of waves surging upwards violently as if to strike at the stooping clouds.

西南天气是“坏天气”的典范。它并非雾的浓重；而更像是地平线的收缩，云层神秘地遮蔽了海岸，仿佛在疾驰的船只四周筑起一个低矮的穹顶地牢。它不是失明；而是视线的缩短。西风并未对水手说：“你将失明”；它仅仅是限制了他的视野范围，却在他心中唤起了对陆地的恐惧。它使人丧失了一半的力量，一半的效能。我一生中许多次，穿着长筒海靴和滴水的油布雨衣，站在一艘驶向海峡的返航船只的艉楼甲板上，在指挥官身旁，凝望着前方那灰暗而翻腾的荒原，曾听到一声疲惫的叹息，化作一句刻意装作随意的评论：

“这天气看不远。”

而我也总是以同样低沉、敷衍的语调回答：

“是的，先生。”

这不过是将一种时刻萦绕的思绪——与前方某处陆地的意识以及船只的高速航行紧密相连——本能地表达出来。顺风，顺风！谁敢抱怨顺风呢？这是西风王的恩典，他从亚速尔群岛（Azores）到法韦尔角（Cape Farewell）的纬度间，威严地统治着北大西洋。这是一记著名的推动，足以圆满结束一次良好的航程；然而，不知为何，人们却无法在唇边挤出朝臣般的感激微笑。这份恩典，是在一副傲慢的怒容下赐予你的——那才是这位伟大专制者的真实面目，当他决定给予某些船只一顿痛击，或用同一种残忍与仁慈的气息，将另一些船只驱赶回家时，那种令人迷惑的神情。

“是的，先生。看不远。”

于是，大副的声音会重复船长的心思，两人都凝望着前方，而他们脚下的船正以约十二节的速度冲向背风岸；就在她那摇荡滴水的艏斜桁——光秃秃地斜指向上方，如一柄长矛——前方仅两英里处，灰色的地平线便封住了视野，无数海浪汹涌上涌，仿佛要击打那低垂的云层。

Awful and threatening scowls darken the face of the West Wind in his clouded, southwest mood; and from the King’s throne-hall in the western board stronger gusts reach you, like the fierce shouts of raving fury to which only the gloomy grandeur of the scene imparts a saving dignity. A shower pelts the deck and the sails of the ship as if flung with a scream by an angry hand; and when the night closes in, the night of a southwesterly gale, it seems more hopeless than the shade of Hades. The southwesterly mood of the great West Wind is a lightless mood, without sun, moon, or stars, with no gleam of light but the phosphorescent flashes of the great sheets of foam that, boiling up on each side of the ship, fling bluish gleams upon her dark and narrow hull, rolling as she runs, chased by enormous seas, distracted in the tumult. There are some bad nights in the kingdom of the West Wind for homeward-bound ships making for the Channel; and the days of wrath dawn upon them colourless and vague like the timid turning up of invisible lights upon the scene of a tyrannical and passionate outbreak, awful in the monotony of its method and the increasing strength of its violence. It is the same wind, the same clouds, the same wildly racing seas, the same thick horizon around the ship. Only the wind is stronger, the clouds seem denser and more overwhelming, the waves appear to have grown bigger and more threatening during the night. The hours, whose minutes are marked by the crash of the breaking seas, slip by with the screaming, pelting squalls overtaking the ship as she runs on and on with darkened canvas, with streaming spars and dripping ropes. The downpours thicken. Preceding each shower a mysterious gloom, like the passage of a shadow above the firmament of grey clouds, filters down upon the ship. Now and then the rain pours upon your head in streams as if from spouts. It seems as if your ship were going to be drowned before she sank, as if all atmosphere had turned to water. You gasp, you splutter, you are blinded and deafened, you are submerged, obliterated, dissolved, annihilated, streaming all over as if your limbs, too, had turned to water. And every nerve on the alert you watch for the clearing-up mood of the Western King, that shall come with a shift of wind as likely as not to whip all the three masts out of your ship in the twinkling of an eye.

在西南方位的阴郁心境中，西风的面容被可怖而威胁性的怒容所笼罩；从西方那王者的殿堂，更猛烈的阵风向你袭来，如同疯狂的怒吼，唯有这场景的阴沉壮丽，才赋予其一丝体面的尊严。一阵骤雨如愤怒的手抛掷般，尖叫着抽打船只的甲板和船帆；当黑夜降临，这西南风暴之夜，似乎比冥府的阴影更令人绝望。伟大的西风那西南心境，是一个无光的心境，没有日、月、星辰，没有任何光亮，唯有船只两侧沸腾而起的巨大浪沫，将幽蓝的微光投射在她那黑暗而狭窄的船体上——她滚动着前行，被巨浪追逐，在混乱中惊惶失措。在西风王国里，对驶向海峡的返航船只而言，有一些可怕的夜晚；而愤怒的白昼在它们面前展开，苍白而模糊，如同在一个暴虐而激烈的爆发场景中，怯生生地亮起了无形的灯光——其单调的手段和不断增强的暴力，都令人敬畏。是同样的风，同样的云，同样疯狂竞逐的海浪，船只周围同样浓重的地平线。只是风更猛了，云似乎更浓、更具压迫感，海浪在夜间似乎变得更大、更具威胁。那些小时，其分钟由崩浪的撞击声标记，在尖叫的飑风追击下溜走——船只在风帆紧缩、桅桁淌水、绳索滴水的状态下，继续疾驰。暴雨愈发密集。每阵雨前，一团神秘的幽暗，如同影子掠过灰色云层的天穹，笼罩在船只上空。雨水不时如管道般从头顶倾泻而下。仿佛你的船在沉没之前就要被淹死，仿佛整个大气都化为了水。你喘息，你呛咳，你目盲耳聋，你被吞没、抹除、溶解、消灭，浑身湿透，仿佛你的四肢也化为了水。你每一根神经都处于警觉状态，等待着西风王那转晴的心境——它或许会随风向的转变而来，但更可能在一眨眼间，将你船上的三根桅杆尽数扫走。
