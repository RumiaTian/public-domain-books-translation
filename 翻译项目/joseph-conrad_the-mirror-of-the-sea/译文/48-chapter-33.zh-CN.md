### XXXIII / 第三十三篇

===Original===
A ship in dock, surrounded by quays and the walls of warehouses, has the appearance of a prisoner meditating upon freedom in the sadness of a free spirit put under restraint. Chain cables and stout ropes keep her bound to stone posts at the edge of a paved shore, and a berthing-master, with brass buttons on his coat, walks about like a weather-beaten and ruddy gaoler, casting jealous, watchful glances upon the moorings that fetter a ship lying passive and still and safe, as if lost in deep regrets of her days of liberty and danger on the sea.

===Chinese===
一艘停泊在船坞里的船，四周是码头和仓库的围墙，看上去像一个被束缚的自由灵魂，在忧伤中冥想着自由。锚链和粗缆将她牢牢系在铺砌岸壁边缘的石桩上，一个衣襟上钉着黄铜纽扣的带缆工（berthing-master），像个风吹日晒、面色红润的狱卒一样踱来踱去，用嫉妒而警觉的目光审视着那些束缚住这条船的系泊缆——她被动地、静止地、安全地躺着，仿佛深深陷入对她往昔自由与海上历险的追悔之中。

===Original===
The swarm of renegades—dockmasters, berthing-masters, gatemen, and suchlike—appear to nurse an immense distrust of the captive ship's resignation. There never seem chains and ropes enough to satisfy their minds concerned with the safe binding of free ships to the strong, muddy, enslaved earth. "You had better put another bight of a hawser astern, Mr. Mate," is the usual phrase in their mouth. I brand them for renegades, because most of them have been sailors in their time. As if the infirmities of old age—the grey hair, the wrinkles at the corners of the eyes, and the knotted veins of the hands—were the symptoms of moral poison, they prowl about the quays with an underhand air of gloating over the broken spirit of noble captives.

===Chinese===
那群叛徒——船坞长、带缆工、门卫之流——似乎对被囚之船的顺从心怀莫大的不信任。他们总觉得铁链和缆绳还不够多，不够满足他们要把自由的船只牢牢绑缚在坚实、泥泞、被奴役的大地上的念头。"大副先生，您最好在船尾再加一道粗缆圈，"是他们嘴里的口头禅。我称他们为叛徒，因为他们中的大多数当年都是水手。仿佛衰老的征兆——灰白的头发、眼角的皱纹、手上暴突的青筋——是道德中毒的症状，他们在码头上四处游荡，带着一种幸灾乐祸的阴郁气，觊觎着高贵囚徒那已被折服的灵魂。

===Original===
They want more fenders, more breasting-ropes; they want more springs, more shackles, more fetters; they want to make ships with volatile souls as motionless as square blocks of stone. They stand on the mud of pavements, these degraded sea-dogs, with long lines of railway-trucks clanking their couplings behind their backs, and run malevolent glances over your ship from headgear to taffrail, only wishing to tyrannize over the poor creature under the hypocritical cloak of benevolence and care.

===Chinese===
他们要更多的碰垫、更多的护舷缆；他们要更多的弹簧缆、更多的锁链、更多的脚镣；他们要把那些灵魂不安分的船变成纹丝不动的方石块。这些堕落的老水手站在泥泞的铺砌地面上，身后是哐当作响的铁路货车长龙，用恶意的目光从船首到船尾扫视你的船，一心只想披着仁慈关怀的伪善外衣，对这可怜的生灵施行暴政。

===Original===
Here and there cargo cranes looking like instruments of torture for ships swing cruel hooks at the end of long chains. Gangs of dock-labourers swarm with muddy feet over the gangways. It is a moving sight this, of so many men of the earth, earthy, who never cared anything for a ship, trampling unconcerned, brutal and hobnailed upon her helpless body.

===Chinese===
各处可见货运起重机——看上去像专门折磨船只的刑具——在长链的末端晃荡着残酷的吊钩。成群的码头工人，泥脚踩在舷梯上蜂拥而过。这是一幅令人动容的景象：如此众多尘世之人，满身尘土，对船从无半分关切，却毫不在意地、粗暴地、穿着钉靴践踏在她无助的躯体之上。

===Original===
Fortunately, nothing can deface the beauty of a ship. That sense of a dungeon, that sense of a horrible and degrading misfortune overtaking a creature fair to see and safe to trust, attaches only to ships moored in the docks of great European ports. You feel that they are dishonestly locked up, to be hunted about from wharf to wharf on a dark, greasy, square pool of black water as a brutal reward at the end of a faithful voyage.

===Chinese===
所幸，没有什么能够损毁一艘船的美丽。那种地牢的感觉，那种可怕的、有辱尊严的不幸降临在一个外观美丽、值得信赖的生灵身上的感觉，只属于那些停泊在欧洲大港船坞里的船。你会觉得它们是被不公正地锁起来的，在一方油腻发黑的方形水塘里被从一个码头驱赶到另一个码头，作为对一次忠诚航行的残酷奖赏。

===Original===
A ship anchored in an open roadstead, with cargo-lighters alongside and her own tackle swinging the burden over the rail, is accomplishing in freedom a function of her life. There is no restraint; there is space: clear water around her, and a clear sky above her mastheads, with a landscape of green hills and charming bays opening around her anchorage. She is not abandoned by her own men to the tender mercies of shore people. She still shelters, and is looked after by her own little devoted band, and you feel that presently she will glide between the headlands and disappear.

===Chinese===
一艘在开阔锚地抛锚的船，身旁紧靠着货驳（cargo-lighter），用自己的吊索将货物从舷侧吊起，是在自由中完成她生命的一项功能。没有束缚；有的是空间：四周是清澈的海水，桅顶上方是明净的天空，锚地周围展开着青山与迷人海湾的风景。她没有被自己的人抛弃，交给岸上人的"温柔"处置。她仍然有庇护，仍然由她自己那支忠心耿耿的小队伍照看着，你会觉得她不久便会滑过两座海岬之间，消失在远方。

===Original===
It is only at home, in dock, that she lies abandoned, shut off from freedom by all the artifices of men that think of quick despatch and profitable freights. It is only then that the odious, rectangular shadows of walls and roofs fall upon her decks, with showers of soot.

===Chinese===
只有在故乡——在船坞里——她才被遗弃，被那些只想着快速装卸和有利可图的运费的人们所设计的一切机关，隔绝于自由之外。也只有在那时，那些可憎的、方形的墙壁与屋顶的阴影才会投落在她的甲板上，伴随着阵阵煤灰飘落。

===Original===
To a man who has never seen the extraordinary nobility, strength, and grace that the devoted generations of shipbuilders have evolved from some pure nooks of their simple souls, the sight that could be seen five-and-twenty years ago of a large fleet of clippers moored along the north side of the New South Dock was an inspiring spectacle. Then there was a quarter of a mile of them, from the iron dockyard-gates guarded by policemen, in a long, forest-like perspective of masts, moored two and two to many stout wooden jetties. Their spars dwarfed with their loftiness the corrugated-iron sheds, their jibbooms extended far over the shore, their white-and-gold figureheads, almost dazzling in their purity, overhung the straight, long quay above the mud and dirt of the wharfside, with the busy figures of groups and single men moving to and fro, restless and grimy under their soaring immobility.

===Chinese===
对于一个从未见过世代献身的造船匠从他们淳朴灵魂的某个纯净角落里锤炼出的那种非凡高贵、力量与优雅的人来说，二十五年前在新南码头（New South Dock）北侧所见的那支庞大的快帆船（clipper）船队，是一幅令人振奋的景象。从警察守卫的铁质船坞大门望去，足足有四分之一英里的船只，在一道森林般绵延的桅杆透视线中，两两系泊在许多坚固的木质突堤码头上。它们高耸的桅桁使波纹铁皮棚屋相形见绌，它们的艏斜桅（jibboom）远远伸向岸上，它们白底描金的船首像——纯净得几乎令人目眩——悬在码头边上泥泞污垢之上的笔直长堤上方，忙碌的人影三五成群或孑然独行，在它们高耸的静止之下穿梭不息，满身污垢。

===Original===
At tide-time you would see one of the loaded ships with battened-down hatches drop out of the ranks and float in the clear space of the dock, held by lines dark and slender, like the first threads of a spider's web, extending from her bows and her quarters to the mooring-posts on shore. There, graceful and still, like a bird ready to spread its wings, she waited till, at the opening of the gates, a tug or two would hurry in noisily, hovering round her with an air of fuss and solicitude, and take her out into the river, tending, shepherding her through open bridges, through dam-like gates between the flat pier-heads, with a bit of green lawn surrounded by gravel and a white signal-mast with yard and gaff, flying a couple of dingy blue, red, or white flags.

===Chinese===
涨潮时分，你会看到一艘满载的、舱盖压条封紧的船从队列中悄然脱离，漂浮在船坞的开阔水域中，被几根细长黝黑的缆绳牵引着——如同蛛网最初的几根丝线——从船首和船尾延伸到岸上的系泊柱。在那里，她优雅而静止，像一只准备展翅的鸟儿，等待着大门开启，一两艘拖轮喧闹地驶入，带着大惊小怪和殷勤关切的样子在她身边盘旋，将她领入河中，护送她穿过开启的桥梁，穿过平坦码头之间的坝式闸门——闸门旁是一小片砾石环绕的绿地，一根白色的信号桅杆上挂着桁和斜桁（gaff），飘着两三面灰暗的蓝旗、红旗或白旗。

===Original===
This New South Dock (it was its official name), round which my earlier professional memories are centred, belongs to the group of West India Docks, together with two smaller and much older basins called Import and Export respectively, both with the greatness of their trade departed from them already. Picturesque and clean as docks go, these twin basins spread side by side the dark lustre of their glassy water sparsely peopled by a few ships laid up on buoys or tucked far away from each other at the end of sheds in the corners of empty quays, where they seemed to slumber quietly remote, untouched by the bustle of men's affairs—in retreat rather than in captivity.

===Chinese===
这新南码头（New South Dock）——它的正式名称——是我早年职业记忆的核心所在，属于西印度码头（West India Docks）群，另外还有两个更小、更古老的船坞，分别叫作进口码头和出口码头，两者的贸易辉煌都已成往事。作为船坞来说，这两座孪生的池塘算得上风景如画、干净整洁，并排铺展着幽暗而如镜的水面，零星停泊着几条系泊在浮筒上的船，或者远远地藏在空旷码头角落的棚屋尽头，彼此相隔甚远，仿佛在远离尘嚣的静谧中沉睡——与其说是囚禁，不如说是隐遁。

===Original===
They were quaint and sympathetic, those two homely basins, unfurnished and silent, with no aggressive display of cranes, no apparatus of hurry and work on their narrow shores. No railway-lines cumbered them. The knots of labourers trooping in clumsily round the corners of cargo-sheds to eat their food in peace out of red cotton handkerchiefs had the air of picnicking by the side of a lonely mountain pool. They were restful (and I should say very unprofitable), those basins, where the chief officer of one of the ships involved in the harassing, strenuous, noisy activity of the New South Dock only a few yards away could escape in the dinner-hour to stroll, unhampered by men and affairs, meditating (if he chose) on the vanity of all things human.

===Chinese===
那两座质朴的池塘是古雅而令人亲近的，没有任何摆设，寂静无声，没有咄咄逼人的起重机阵列，狭窄的岸上没有忙碌工作的器械。没有铁路线在那里盘踞。几群工人笨拙地绕过货棚的角落，在那里安安静静地从红色棉手帕里取出食物来吃，那样子像是在一座孤寂的山塘边野餐。那些池塘是令人安心的（我得说也非常无利可图），在那儿，新南码头那令人疲惫、繁重、喧闹的活动就在几码之外，而某条船的大副可以在午饭时间逃到这里来，不受人事干扰地散步，（如果他愿意的话）冥想人世间一切事物的虚妄。

===Original===
At one time they must have been full of good old slow West Indiamen of the square-stern type, that took their captivity, one imagines, as stolidly as they had faced the buffeting of the waves with their blunt, honest bows, and disgorged sugar, rum, molasses, coffee, or logwood sedately with their own winch and tackle. But when I knew them, of exports there was never a sign that one could detect; and all the imports I have ever seen were some rare cargoes of tropical timber, enormous baulks roughed out of iron trunks grown in the woods about the Gulf of Mexico. They lay piled up in stacks of mighty boles, and it was hard to believe that all this mass of dead and stripped trees had come out of the flanks of a slender, innocent-looking little barque with, as likely as not, a homely woman's name—Ellen this or Annie that—upon her fine bows. But this is generally the case with a discharged cargo. Once spread at large over the quay, it looks the most impossible bulk to have all come there out of that ship alongside.

===Chinese===
曾几何时，这里一定停满了那些缓慢老实的方尾西印度商船（West Indiamen），它们面对囚禁想必和它们当年以钝直诚实的船首迎击浪涛一样木然，用自带的绞盘和索具从容不迫地卸下蔗糖、朗姆酒、糖蜜、咖啡或苏木。但当我认识它们的时候，出口货物已经连一点迹象都看不到了；而我所见过的进口货物，不过是几批稀有的热带木材——从墨西哥湾周围森林中铁树干上粗粗劈出的巨大方木。它们堆叠在一起，原木粗壮如柱，令人难以相信，这整堆死去了的、剥了皮的树木，竟全是从一条外观纤弱无辜的小三桅帆船（barque）的船腹里吐出来的——那船上很可能还漆着某个家常女人的名字——这个艾伦、那个安妮。但这对卸下的货物来说是常态。一旦在码头上铺展开来，看上去就完全不可能是从旁边那条船里出来的了。

===Original===
They were quiet, serene nooks in the busy world of docks, these basins where it has never been my good luck to get a berth after some more or less arduous passage. But one could see at a glance that men and ships were never hustled there. They were so quiet that, remembering them well, one comes to doubt that they ever existed—places of repose for tired ships to dream in, places of meditation rather than work, where wicked ships—the cranky, the lazy, the wet, the bad sea boats, the wild steerers, the capricious, the pigheaded, the generally ungovernable—would have full leisure to take count and repent of their sins, sorrowful and naked, with their rent garments of sailcloth stripped off them, and with the dust and ashes of the London atmosphere upon their mastheads. For that the worst of ships would repent if she were ever given time I make no doubt. I have known too many of them. No ship is wholly bad; and now that their bodies that had braved so many tempests have been blown off the face of the sea by a puff of steam, the evil and the good together into the limbo of things that have served their time, there can be no harm in affirming that in these vanished generations of willing servants there never has been one utterly unredeemable soul.

===Chinese===
在繁忙的船坞世界里，它们是安静、宁静的角落，这些池塘——我从未有过在某次或苦或累的航行之后有幸在这里泊位的运气。但一眼就能看出，人和船在那里从不受催逼。它们安静得如此彻底，以至于当你清晰地回忆起它们时，反而开始怀疑它们是否真的存在过——那是疲惫的船做梦的休憩之所，是冥想而非劳作的所在，在那里，那些邪恶的船——易倾覆的、懒惰的、漏水的、航海性能差的、难以操纵的、反复无常的、固执的、一般而言无法管束的——将有充分的闲暇来清点并忏悔它们的罪孽，悲伤而赤裸，身上撕裂的帆布衣裳已被剥去，桅顶上蒙着伦敦大气的灰尘与灰烬。我毫不怀疑，即使是最糟糕的船，只要给她时间，她也会忏悔。我认识的船太多了。没有一条船是全然邪恶的；而如今，它们那曾经历过无数风暴的躯体，已被一缕蒸汽吹离了海面，善与恶一同坠入了那些已尽其使命的事物的虚无之境，在此声明，这些消逝了的世代忠仆之中，从未有过一个全然无可救赎的灵魂。

===Original===
In the New South Dock there was certainly no time for remorse, introspection, repentance, or any phenomena of inner life either for the captive ships or for their officers. From six in the morning till six at night the hard labour of the prison-house, which rewards the valiance of ships that win the harbour, went on steadily, great slings of general cargo swinging over the rail, to drop plumb into the hatchways at the sign of the gangway-tender's hand. The New South Dock was especially a loading dock for the Colonies in those great (and last) days of smart wool-clippers, good to look at and—well—exciting to handle.

===Chinese===
在新南码头，无论是被囚的船还是它们的船员，确实都没有时间来反省、内省、忏悔或进行任何内心生活的现象。从早晨六点到夜里六点，监狱般的苦役——那是对赢得港口的船只英勇航行的奖赏——稳步进行，巨大的杂货吊索从舷侧摆过，随着舷梯看守的手势笔直落入舱口。在那些运羊毛快帆船（wool-clipper）的伟大（也是最后的）岁月里，新南码头尤其是一个为殖民地装货的码头，那些船看上去漂亮极了——嗯——驾驭起来也令人兴奋。

===Original===
Some of them were more fair to see than the others; many were (to put it mildly) somewhat overmasted; all were expected to make good passages; and of all that line of ships, whose rigging made a thick, enormous network against the sky, whose brasses flashed almost as far as the eye of the policeman at the gates could reach, there was hardly one that knew of any other port amongst all the ports on the wide earth but London and Sydney, or London and Melbourne, or London and Adelaide, perhaps with Hobart Town added for those of smaller tonnage. One could almost have believed, as her grey-whiskered second mate used to say of the old *Duke of S——*, that they knew the road to the Antipodes better than their own skippers, who, year in, year out, took them from London—the place of captivity—to some Australian port where, twenty-five years ago, though moored well and tight enough to the wooden wharves, they felt themselves no captives, but honoured guests.

===Chinese===
其中有些比其他的更漂亮；许多——说得委婉些——桅杆略嫌过多；所有的船都被期望创造良好的航速；在那整排船中，索具在天空下织成浓密巨大的网络，黄铜配件的闪光几乎能被大门处警察的目光触及，而它们之中几乎没有一条知道广阔地球上除了伦敦和悉尼、或伦敦和墨尔本、或伦敦和阿德莱德之外还有什么别的港口——也许吨位较小的还会加上霍巴特（Hobart Town）。你几乎可以相信，正如那条老船"萨——公爵号"（Duke of S——）的灰胡子二副常说的那样，它们认识通往对跖地（Antipodes）的航路比它们自己的船长更清楚——那些船长年复一年地将它们从伦敦——囚禁之地——带到某个澳大利亚港口，而在二十五年前，虽然同样紧紧系泊在木质码头上，它们却觉得自己不是囚徒，而是尊贵的客人。
