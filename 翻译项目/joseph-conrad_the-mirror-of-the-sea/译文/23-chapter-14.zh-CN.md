## XIV / 第十四篇

===Original===
The sailing-ship, when I knew her in her days of perfection, was a sensible creature. When I say her days of perfection, I mean perfection of build, gear, seaworthy qualities and ease of handling, not the perfection of speed. That quality has departed with the change of building material. No iron ship of yesterday ever attained the marvels of speed which the seamanship of men famous in their time had obtained from their wooden, copper-sheeted predecessors. Everything had been done to make the iron ship perfect, but no wit of man had managed to devise an efficient coating composition to keep her bottom clean with the smooth cleanness of yellow metal sheeting. After a spell of a few weeks at sea, an iron ship begins to lag as if she had grown tired too soon. It is only her bottom that is getting foul. A very little affects the speed of an iron ship which is not driven on by a merciless propeller. Often it is impossible to tell what inconsiderate trifle puts her off her stride. A certain mysteriousness hangs around the quality of speed as it was displayed by the old sailing-ships commanded by a competent seaman. In those days the speed depended upon the seaman; therefore, apart from the laws, rules, and regulations for the good preservation of his cargo, he was careful of his loading——or what is technically called the trim of his ship. Some ships sailed fast on an even keel, others had to be trimmed quite one foot by the stern, and I have heard of a ship that gave her best speed on a wind when so loaded as to float a couple of inches by the head.

===Chinese===
我所认识的帆船，在她全盛时代，是一种通情达理的生灵。我说她的全盛时代，是指建造工艺、索具装备、适航性能与操驾便捷都臻于完美的时代，而非速度的完美。那种品质随造船材料的更迭而消逝了。昨日的铁壳船，从未达到那个时代以精湛船艺闻名的水手们从覆铜木帆船上榨取的惊人航速。一切手段都用上了，为的是让铁壳船趋于完美，然而人类的才智始终未能发明出一种有效的涂层配方，能让船底保持如铜皮包裹那般光洁。在海上漂泊几个星期后，铁壳船便开始迟滞，仿佛过早地疲倦了。不过是她的船底变脏了而已。对于一艘不靠无情螺旋桨驱动的铁壳船，微乎其微的污损便足以影响航速。究竟是哪桩不经意的微末小事打乱了她的节奏，往往无从断定。由称职的水手统率的老式帆船所展现出的那种速度品质，始终笼罩着一层神秘色彩。在那个年代，航速取决于水手；因此，除了那些旨在妥善保管货物的法规条例之外，水手还十分在意装船——即航海术语所说的"配载"。有些船平吃水便跑得快，另一些则必须尾倾整整一英尺，我还听说过一条船，当货物装到船首吃水深出几英寸时，反而在戗风中达到了最佳航速。

===Original===
I call to mind a winter landscape in Amsterdam——a flat foreground of waste land, with here and there stacks of timber, like the huts of a camp of some very miserable tribe; the long stretch of the Handelskade; cold, stone-faced quays, with the snow-sprinkled ground and the hard, frozen water of the canal, in which were set ships one behind another with their frosty mooring-ropes hanging slack and their decks idle and deserted, because, as the master stevedore (a gentle, pale person, with a few golden hairs on his chin and a reddened nose) informed me, their cargoes were frozen-in upcountry on barges and schuyts. In the distance, beyond the waste ground, and running parallel with the line of ships, a line of brown, warm-toned houses seemed bowed under snow-laden roofs. From afar at the end of Tsar Peter Straat, issued in the frosty air the tinkle of bells of the horse tramcars, appearing and disappearing in the opening between the buildings, like little toy carriages harnessed with toy horses and played with by people that appeared no bigger than children.

===Chinese===
我忆起阿姆斯特丹的一个冬日景象——一片平坦的荒地前景，四处散落着木材堆，宛如某个极贫困部落营地的棚屋；汉德尔斯卡德（Handelskade）长长的堤岸伸展出去；冰冷的石砌码头上，地面覆着薄雪，运河的水面坚硬如冻，一条条船首尾相接地停泊其间，覆霜的系泊缆松弛地垂悬，甲板空寂无人——正如那位工头（一个性情温和、面色苍白的人，下巴上几根金色胡须，鼻子冻得通红）告诉我的，货物在上游的驳船和平底船上冻住了。远处，越过荒地，与船队平行排列着一排棕色调的暖色房屋，在积雪压覆的屋顶下显得低垂。从沙皇彼得街（Tsar Peter Straat）尽头远处，马车铃铛的清响在寒冷空气中传来，马车在建筑间的开口处时隐时现，仿佛套着玩具马的小玩具车，被看上去不过孩童般大小的人们驾驭着。

===Original===
I was, as the French say, biting my fists with impatience for that cargo frozen upcountry; with rage at that canal set fast, at the wintry and deserted aspect of all those ships that seemed to decay in grim depression for want of the open water. I was chief mate, and very much alone. Directly I had joined I received from my owners instructions to send all the ship's apprentices away on leave together, because in such weather there was nothing for anybody to do, unless to keep up a fire in the cabin stove. That was attended to by a snuffy and mop-headed, inconceivably dirty, and weirdly toothless Dutch ship-keeper, who could hardly speak three words of English, but who must have had some considerable knowledge of the language, since he managed invariably to interpret in the contrary sense everything that was said to him.

===Chinese===
我正像法国人所说的那样，咬着拳头干着急，为那些冻在上游的货物而焦躁；对那条冻封的运河满腔怒火，对所有那些船萧索凄寒、了无生气的冬日景象满腔怒火——它们仿佛因失去了开阔水面而颓然衰朽，一片死寂。我是大副，而且孤身一人。我一上任，便接到船东的指令，把所有见习水手一律放假遣走，因为这种天气谁也无事可做，除非在船长室的炉子里维持一炉火。这炉火由一个邋遢的荷兰看船人照管——他满头乱发，鼻烟渍斑斑，脏得不可理喻，牙齿奇异地掉光了，几乎说不出三个英文词来，但想必对这门语言有相当的了解，因为他说什么都会被理解成相反的意思。

===Original===
Notwithstanding the little iron stove, the ink froze on the swing-table in the cabin, and I found it more convenient to go ashore stumbling over the arctic wasteland and shivering in glazed tramcars in order to write my evening letter to my owners in a gorgeous café in the centre of the town. It was an immense place, lofty and gilt, upholstered in red plush, full of electric lights and so thoroughly warmed that even the marble tables felt tepid to the touch. The waiter who brought me my cup of coffee bore, by comparison with my utter isolation, the dear aspect of an intimate friend. There, alone in a noisy crowd, I would write slowly a letter addressed to Glasgow, of which the gist would be: There is no cargo, and no prospect of any coming till late spring apparently. And all the time I sat there the necessity of getting back to the ship bore heavily on my already half-congealed spirits——the shivering in glazed tramcars, the stumbling over the snow-sprinkled waste ground, the visions of ships frozen in a row, appearing vaguely like corpses of black vessels in a white world, so silent, so lifeless, so soulless they seemed to be.

===Chinese===
尽管有那小小的铁皮炉子，舱室摇桌上的墨水还是冻住了。于是我发觉，与其在舱里挨冻，不如蹒跚走过北极般的荒原，瑟缩在有玻璃窗的电车里，去镇中心一家豪华咖啡馆给船东写晚间信件更方便些。那地方十分宏大，穹顶高耸，金碧辉煌，红色绒布铺陈，电灯通明，暖气烧得极足，连大理石桌面摸上去都是温热的。端来咖啡的侍者，比起我绝对的孤寂，简直宛如一位亲密友人的可亲面孔。在那嘈杂人群里独自坐着，我缓缓写下一封寄往格拉斯哥的信，大意是：没有货物，看来直到暮春也无望到货。而我坐在那里每一刻，返回船上的迫切感都沉沉压在我已经半冻僵的精神之上——有玻璃窗的电车里瑟瑟发抖，雪覆的荒地上跌跌撞撞，那一列列冻住的船只的幻影隐约浮现，如同白色世界里一具具黑色船舶的尸体，那般沉寂，那般了无生气，那般失魂落魄。

===Original===
With precaution I would go up the side of my own particular corpse, and would feel her as cold as ice itself and as slippery under my feet. My cold berth would swallow up like a chilly burial niche my bodily shivers and my mental excitement. It was a cruel winter. The very air seemed as hard and trenchant as steel; but it would have taken much more than this to extinguish my sacred fire for the exercise of my craft. No young man of twenty-four appointed chief mate for the first time in his life would have let that Dutch tenacious winter penetrate into his heart. I think that in those days I never forgot the fact of my elevation for five consecutive minutes. I fancy it kept me warm, even in my slumbers, better than the high pile of blankets, which positively crackled with frost as I threw them off in the morning. And I would get up early for no reason whatever except that I was in sole charge. The new captain had not been appointed yet.

===Chinese===
我会小心翼翼地攀上我那条专属尸骸的船舷，发觉她浑身冰冷如冰，脚下打滑。我那冰冷的铺位会像一处寒彻的墓龛，吞没我身体的颤抖与精神的亢奋。那是一个酷烈的冬天。空气本身都硬朗锋锐如钢铁；但要浇灭我那为操持船艺而燃烧的神圣之火，单凭这些还远远不够。一个二十四岁的年轻人，有生以来头一回被任命为大副，断不会让那荷兰式的绵绵寒意渗入他的心房。我想，在那些日子里，我从未连续五分钟忘记过自己晋升的事实。我猜想，它比那厚厚一摞毯子更能为我保暖，甚至在睡梦中也是如此——那些毯子早晨掀开时确确实实嘎吱作响，结满了霜花。而我总是没有任何理由便早早起身，唯一的缘由就是我独自当家。新船长尚未任命。

===Original===
Almost each morning a letter from my owners would arrive, directing me to go to the charterers and clamour for the ship's cargo; to threaten them with the heaviest penalties of demurrage; to demand that this assortment of varied merchandise, set fast in a landscape of ice and windmills somewhere upcountry, should be put on rail instantly, and fed up to the ship in regular quantities every day. After drinking some hot coffee, like an Arctic explorer setting off on a sledge journey towards the North Pole, I would go ashore and roll shivering in a tramcar into the very heart of the town, past clean-faced houses, past thousands of brass knockers upon a thousand painted doors glimmering behind rows of trees of the pavement species, leafless, gaunt, seemingly dead forever.

===Chinese===
几乎每天早上都会收到船东的来信，指示我去找租船方，为船货大声疾呼；以最严厉的滞期罚金威胁他们；要求那批冻在上游某处冰雪与风车之间的各色杂货立刻装上铁路，每天按量稳稳地送抵船上。喝下几杯热咖啡后，我便像一个准备乘雪橇前往北极的探险家，上岸钻进电车，瑟缩着一路摇晃到镇中心，经过一栋栋面容整洁的房屋，经过上千扇漆色门扉上数千个黄铜门环在行道树——那些光秃、枯瘦、仿佛永远死去了的行道树——间闪烁而过。

===Original===
That part of the expedition was easy enough, though the horses were painfully glistening with icicles, and the aspect of the tram-conductors' faces presented a repulsive blending of crimson and purple. But as to frightening or bullying, or even wheedling some sort of answer out of Mr. Hudig, that was another matter altogether. He was a big, swarthy Netherlander, with black moustaches and a bold glance. He always began by shoving me into a chair before I had time to open my mouth, gave me cordially a large cigar, and in excellent English would start to talk everlastingly about the phenomenal severity of the weather. It was impossible to threaten a man who, though he possessed the language perfectly, seemed incapable of understanding any phrase pronounced in a tone of remonstrance or discontent. As to quarrelling with him, it would have been stupid. The weather was too bitter for that. His office was so warm, his fire so bright, his sides shook so heartily with laughter, that I experienced always a great difficulty in making up my mind to reach for my hat.

===Chinese===
那一段远征还算轻松，尽管马匹身上挂满了亮晶晶的冰凌，令人心疼，电车售票员的脸色也呈现出一种令人厌恶的深红与绛紫的混合。但要吓唬、威逼，甚至哄骗胡迪格先生（Mr. Hudig）给出一个答复，那就是另一码事了。他是个高大黝黑的荷兰人，留着黑髭须，目光大胆。每次我还没来得及张嘴，他便一把将我摁进椅子里，热诚地递上一支大雪茄，然后用地道的英语滔滔不绝地谈论天气反常的严酷。你没法威胁一个英语说得分明、却偏偏对任何带抗议或不满语气的话充耳不闻的人。至于和他争吵，那未免太蠢了。天气已经够冷的了。他的办公室那样暖和，炉火那样明亮，他的身躯笑起来那样爽朗地抖动，我每次都很难下定决心伸手去拿帽子。

===Original===
At last the cargo did come. At first it came dribbling in by rail in trucks, till the thaw set in; and then fast, in a multitude of barges, with a great rush of unbound waters. The gentle master stevedore had his hands very full at last; and the chief mate became worried in his mind as to the proper distribution of the weight of his first cargo in a ship he did not personally know before.

===Chinese===
货物终于来了。起初只是零零星星由铁路货车运来，直到化冻开始；然后便是大潮般涌入的驳船队，伴随着奔涌的春潮浩浩荡荡地倾泻而至。那位温和的工头终于忙得不可开交；而大副则开始为如何在一条自己尚不熟悉的船上妥善分配这第一票货的重量而忧心忡忡。

===Original===
Ships do want humouring. They want humouring in handling; and if you mean to handle them well, they must have been humoured in the distribution of the weight which you ask them to carry through the good and evil fortune of a passage. Your ship is a tender creature, whose idiosyncrasies must be attended to if you mean her to come with credit to herself and you through the rough-and-tumble of her life.

===Chinese===
船确实需要哄着来。操驾上需要哄着；而若你想把她们操驾好，在分配你要求她们承载的重量——无论航途是吉是凶——时就必须哄着她们。你的船是一个娇嫩的生灵，若要让她在颠沛流离的一生中为你也为她自己赢得体面，就必须顾及她的种种脾性。
