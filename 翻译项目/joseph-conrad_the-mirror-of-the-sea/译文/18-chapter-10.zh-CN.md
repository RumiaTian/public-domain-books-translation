### X / 第十篇

===Original===
From the main truck of the average tall ship the horizon describes a circle of many miles, in which you can see another ship right down to her waterline; and these very eyes which follow this writing have counted in their time over a hundred sail becalmed, as if within a magic ring, not very far from the Azores—ships more or less tall. There were hardly two of them heading exactly the same way, as if each had meditated breaking out of the enchanted circle at a different point of the compass. But the spell of the calm is a strong magic. The following day still saw them scattered within sight of each other and heading different ways; but when, at the last, the breeze came with the darkling ripple that ran very blue on a pale sea, they all went in the same direction together. For this was the homeward-bound fleet from the far-off ends of the earth, and a Falmouth fruit-schooner, the smallest of them all, was heading the flight. One could have imagined her very fair, if not divinely tall, leaving a scent of lemons and oranges in her wake.

===Chinese===
从一般高桅大船的主桅顶望去，地平线画出一个方圆数英里的圆圈，另一艘船的水线都尽收眼底；而此刻写下这些字句的这双眼睛，当年曾在亚速尔群岛（Azores）附近数过上百艘帆船——高低不等——静静地停泊着，仿佛被一个魔环困住了。它们几乎没有两艘的航向完全一致，仿佛每艘都在盘算从罗盘的不同方位冲出这道魔咒。但无风的咒力是强大的魔法。第二天望去，它们仍然各自散落、互在视线之内，航向依旧各异；然而当微风终于吹来，在苍白的海面上荡起幽暗的蓝色涟漪时，它们便齐齐地朝同一个方向驶去。因为这是一支从天涯海角返航的船队，而法尔茅斯（Falmouth）的一条水果纵帆船，其中最小的一条，正引领着这场归航。你几乎可以想象她秀美而不失威仪，身后拖着柠檬与橙子的芬芳。

===Original===
The next day there were very few ships in sight from our mastheads—seven at most, perhaps, with a few more distant specks, hull down, beyond the magic ring of the horizon. The spell of the fair wind has a subtle power to scatter a white-winged company of ships looking all the same way, each with its white fillet of tumbling foam under the bow. It is the calm that brings ships mysteriously together; it is your wind that is the great separator.

===Chinese===
第二天从桅顶望去，视野里已寥寥无几——顶多七艘，还有几个更远的黑点，船身隐没在地平线魔环之外。顺风有着一种微妙的力量，能将一群白翼船舶——它们原先都朝同一个方向，船首各自压出一道翻滚的白沫——驱散得七零八落。是无风把船神秘地聚拢在一起，是风，才是伟大的离散者。

===Original===
The taller the ship, the further she can be seen; and her white tallness breathed upon by the wind first proclaims her size. The tall masts holding aloft the white canvas, spread out like a snare for catching the invisible power of the air, emerge gradually from the water, sail after sail, yard after yard, growing big, till, under the towering structure of her machinery, you perceive the insignificant, tiny speck of her hull.

===Chinese===
船越高，便能看得越远；而被风轻拂的白色高耸船身，最先昭示她的体量。高高的桅杆撑起白色的帆布——像一张张罗网，捕捉那无形的空气之力——自水面渐次显现，一帆接一帆，一桁接一桁，越长越大，直到你透过那高耸的帆装结构，看见下方微不足道的一小点——那便是船体。

===Original===
The tall masts are the pillars supporting the balanced planes that, motionless and silent, catch from the air the ship's motive-power, as it were a gift from Heaven vouchsafed to the audacity of man; and it is the ship's tall spars, stripped and shorn of their white glory, that incline themselves before the anger of the clouded heaven.

===Chinese===
高桅是支撑平衡翼面的柱子，那翼面纹丝不动、默然无声，从空气中攫取船舶的驱动力——仿佛上天赐予人之胆魄的一份馈赠；而正是船的高大圆材，在剥尽白色荣光之后，向阴云密布的天穹的怒意俯首。

===Original===
When they yield to a squall in a gaunt and naked submission, their tallness is brought best home even to the mind of a seaman. The man who has looked upon his ship going over too far is made aware of the preposterous tallness of a ship's spars. It seems impossible but that those gilt trucks which one had to tilt one's head back to see, now falling into the lower plane of vision, must perforce hit the very edge of the horizon. Such an experience gives you a better impression of the loftiness of your spars than any amount of running aloft could do. And yet in my time the royal yards of an average profitable ship were a good way up above her decks.

===Chinese===
当它们在飑风面前以嶙峋而赤裸的姿态屈服时，那种高耸便深深印入了水手的心智。亲眼目睹自己的船倾斜得过分的人，会深切意识到船的圆材是何等荒唐地高大。那些要仰起头才能看到的鎏金桅顶——如今却跌入视线的低位——似乎不可能不径直撞上地平线的边缘。这样一次经历，比你攀爬无数次桅梯所获得的对桅杆高耸的感受，都要深刻得多。而在我那个年头，一条中等盈利的船的皇桅帆桁（royal yards），离甲板也还是高得很。

===Original===
No doubt a fair amount of climbing up iron ladders can be achieved by an active man in a ship's engine-room, but I remember moments when even to my supple limbs and pride of nimbleness the sailing-ship's machinery seemed to reach up to the very stars.

===Chinese===
诚然，在机舱里一个灵活的人可以攀爬相当多的铁梯，但我记得有些时刻，即便以我柔软的四肢和敏捷的自负，帆船的那套"机械装置"仿佛直抵星辰。

===Original===
For machinery it is, doing its work in perfect silence and with a motionless grace, that seems to hide a capricious and not always governable power, taking nothing away from the material stores of the earth. Not for it the unerring precision of steel moved by white steam and living by red fire and fed with black coal. The other seems to draw its strength from the very soul of the world, its formidable ally, held to obedience by the frailest bonds, like a fierce ghost captured in a snare of something even finer than spun spun. For what is the array of the strongest ropes, the tallest spars and the stoutest canvas against the mighty breath of the infinite, but thistle stalks, cobwebs and gossamer?

===Chinese===
因为这正是那套机械装置，以完美的寂静和凝然不动的优雅工作着，却似乎暗藏着一股反复无常、并非总能驯服的力量，而且不耗取大地的任何物质储备。它不像那钢铁，被白色的蒸汽驱动，靠红色的火焰维生，以黑色的煤炭喂养，精准而从无差池。这一位——帆装——仿佛从世界的灵魂中汲取力量，它那令人生畏的盟友，只被最纤弱的纽带束缚着，宛如一头凶猛的幽灵，被一张比丝线更细的罗网捕获。因为面对那无穷巨力的呼吸，最坚韧的绳索、最高的圆材、最厚实的帆布——又算什么？不过是蓟草的茎、蛛网和游丝罢了。
