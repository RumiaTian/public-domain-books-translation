===Original===
So it came about that Adelais went into France with the great-grandson of Tiburce d’Arnaye: and Fulke, they say, made her a very fair husband. But he had not, of course, much time for lovemaking.

For in France there was sterner work awaiting Fulke d’Arnaye, and he set about it: through seven dreary years he and Rougemont and Dunois managed, somehow, to bolster up the cause of the fat-witted King of Bourges (as the English then called him), who afterward became King Charles VII of France. But in the February of 1429—four days before the Maid of Domremy set forth from her voice-haunted Bois Chenu to bring about a certain coronation in Rheims Church and in Rouen Square a flamy martyrdom—four days before the coming of the good Lorrainer, Fulke d’Arnaye was slain at Rouvray-en-Beausse in that encounter between the French and the English which history has commemorated as the Battle of the Herrings.

因为在法兰西，有更严酷的功业等着富尔克·达尔奈（Fulke d'Arnaye），他便着手做去：整整七个黯淡的年头里，他同鲁热蒙（Rougemont）与迪努瓦（Dunois）勉力支撑着那位蠢钝的「布尔日国王」——当年英国人这样称呼他——的大业；此人后来成了法兰西国王查理七世。然而在1429年2月——早于栋雷米的少女（the Maid of Domremy）从她那萦回着人声的舍努林（Bois Chenu）启程四天，去兰斯大教堂里促成一场加冕、在鲁昂广场上促成一场烈焰殉道——早于那位善良的洛林姑娘驾临四天，富尔克·达尔奈已战死在博斯的鲁夫赖（Rouvray-en-Beausse），死于英法两军的那场遭遇战；史册纪念它，名之曰「鲱鱼之战」（the Battle of the Herrings）。

===Original===

She left two sons: Noël, born in 1425, and Raymond, born in 1426, who were reared by their uncle, Olivier d’Arnaye. It was said of them that Noël was the handsomest man of his times, and Raymond the most shrewd, concerning that you will judge hereafter. Both of these d’Arnayes, on reaching manhood, were identified with the Dauphin’s party in the unending squabbles between Charles VII and the future Louis XI.

Now you may learn how Noël d’Arnaye came to be immortalized by a legacy of two hundred and twenty blows from an osierwhip—since (as the testator piously affirms), “*chastoy est une belle aulmosne.*”

如今诸位可以领教，诺埃尔·达尔奈（Noël d'Arnaye）如何因一笔遗产而不朽——遗产是柳条鞭抽打的二百二十下：因为（正如立遗嘱人虔敬地申言），「chastoy est une belle aulmosne」（惩戒是一桩美好的布施）。

### I / I

“Bon Bec de Paris” / 「巴黎巧嘴」

===Original===
There went about the Rue Saint Jacques a notable shaking of heads on the day that Catherine de Vaucelles was betrothed to François de Montcorbier.

“Holy Virgin!” said the Rue Saint Jacques; “the girl is a fool. Why has she not taken Noël d’Arnaye—Noël the Handsome? I grant you Noël is an ass, but, then, look you, he is of the nobility. He has the Dauphin’s favor. Noël will be a great man when our exiled Dauphin comes back from Geneppe to be King of France. Then, too, she might have had Philippe Sermaise. Sermaise is a priest, of course, and one may not marry a priest, but Sermaise has money, and Sermaise is mad for love of her. She might have done worse. But François! Ho, death of my life, what is François? Perhaps—he, he!—perhaps Ysabeau de Montigny might inform us, you say? Doubtless Ysabeau knows more of him than she would care to confess, but I measure the lad by other standards. François is inoffensive enough, I dare assert, but what does Catherine see in him? He is a scholar?—well, the College of Navarre has furnished food for the gallows before this. A poet?—rhyming will not fill the pot. Rhymes are a thin diet for two lusty young folk like these. And who knows if Guillaume de Villon, his foster-father, has one *sou* to rub against another? He is canon at Saint Benoit-le-Bétourné yonder, but canons are not Midases. The girl will have a hard life of it, neighbor, a hard life, I tell you, if—but, yes!—if Ysabeau de Montigny does not knife her some day. Oh, beyond doubt, Catherine has played the fool.”

卡特琳·德·沃塞勒（Catherine de Vaucelles）与弗朗索瓦·德·蒙科尔比耶（François de Montcorbier）订婚那天，圣雅克街（Rue Saint Jacques）上下起了一场可观的大摇头。

「圣处女呀！」圣雅克街说道；「这姑娘是个傻子。她怎么不嫁给诺埃尔·达尔奈——美男子诺埃尔？我承认诺埃尔是头蠢驴，可是，你瞧，他到底是贵族出身。他蒙王储的恩宠。等我们流亡的王储从热纳普（Geneppe）回来做了法兰西国王，诺埃尔便是大人物了。再者，她原可以得到菲利普·塞尔莫瓦斯（Philippe Sermaise）。塞尔莫瓦斯当然是教士，教士嫁不得，可塞尔莫瓦斯有钱，而且塞尔莫瓦斯为她爱得发疯。她原可以嫁得更糟。可偏偏是弗朗索瓦！嗬，我的命呀，弗朗索瓦算个什么东西？兴许——嘻，嘻！——兴许伊莎博·德·蒙蒂尼（Ysabeau de Montigny）肯赐教，你说是不是？伊莎博对他的底细，无疑比她乐意招认的知道得多，可我衡量这小子另有尺子。弗朗索瓦本人倒无伤大雅，我敢说，可卡特琳看中他哪一点呢？他是个学子？——得了吧，纳瓦尔学院（College of Navarre）从前也不是没给绞架供过货。是个诗人？——押韵可填不饱锅。对他们这样两个血气方刚的年轻人，韵是太稀薄的口粮。再说，谁知道他养父纪尧姆·德·维永（Guillaume de Villon）兜里有没有一个铜子儿（sou）好跟另一个碰一碰？他是那边圣伯努瓦-勒贝图尔内（Saint Benoit-le-Bétourné）的议事司铎，可司铎又不是弥达斯。这姑娘往后有苦日子呢，街坊，苦日子，我告诉你，要是——哦，可不！——要是伊莎博·德·蒙蒂尼哪天不给她一刀的话。哦，毫无疑问，卡特琳干了件天大的蠢事。」

===Original===
Thus far the Rue Saint Jacques.

This was on the day of the *Fête-Dieu*. It was on this day that Noël d’Arnaye blasphemed for a matter of a half-hour and then went to the Crowned Ox, where he drank himself into a contented insensibility; that Ysabeau de Montigny, having wept a little, sent for Gilles Raguyer, a priest and aforetime a rival of François de Montcorbier for her favors; and that Philippe Sermaise grinned and said nothing. ===Chinese===
这一席话，便是圣雅克街的全部高见。

这一天是圣体节（Fête-Dieu）。正是在这一天，诺埃尔·达尔奈亵渎神明达半个钟头之久，随后进了加冕牛酒店（the Crowned Ox），喝到心满意足、不省人事；伊莎博·德·蒙蒂尼洒了几滴泪，便差人去请吉尔·拉居耶（Gilles Raguyer）——一个神父，从前也曾与弗朗索瓦·德·蒙科尔比耶争夺过她的芳心；而菲利普·塞尔莫瓦斯咧嘴笑笑，一言不发。可后来，塞尔莫瓦斯四处寻访弗朗索瓦·德·蒙科尔比耶时，像疯子一般咬噬着自己的下唇。

### II / II

“Deux estions, et n’avions qu’ung Cueur” / 「我们本是两个人，却只共一颗心」

===Original===
It verged upon nine in the evening—a late hour in those days—when François climbed the wall of Jehan de Vaucelles’ garden.

A wall!—and what is a wall to your true lover? What bones, pray, did the Sieur Pyramus, that ill-starred Babylonish knight, make of a wall? did not his protestations slip through a chink, mocking at implacable granite and more implacable fathers? Most assuredly they did; and Pyramus was a pattern to all lovers. Thus ran the meditations of Master François as he leapt down into the garden.

===Chinese===
晚间将近九点——在那个年月已是很晚的时辰——弗朗索瓦翻上了让·德·沃塞勒（Jehan de Vaucelles）家的花园墙头。

一堵墙！——在一颗真正的情人心里，墙算得了什么？请问，那位倒霉的巴比伦骑士皮拉摩斯大人（Sieur Pyramus），几曾被一堵墙难倒过？他的山盟海誓不也曾从墙缝里溜过去，嘲笑着铁面无情的花岗岩和更铁面无情的父亲们？千真万确，正是如此；而皮拉摩斯是一切情人的楷模。弗朗索瓦大师跳进花园时，心里盘旋的便是这般高见。

===Original===
He had not, you must understand, seen Catherine for three hours. Three hours! three eternities rather, and each one of them spent in Malebolge. Coming to a patch of moonlight, François paused there and cut an agile caper, as he thought of that approaching time when he might see Catherine every day.

“Madame François de Montcorbier,” he said, tasting each syllable with gusto. “Catherine de Montcorbier. Was there ever a sweeter juxtaposition of sounds? It is a name for an angel. And an angel shall bear it—eh, yes, an angel, no less. O saints in Paradise, envy me! Envy me,” he cried, with a heroical gesture toward the stars, “for François would change places with none of you.”

===Chinese===

「弗朗索瓦·德·蒙科尔比耶夫人，」他说，有滋有味地品咂着每一个音节。「卡特琳·德·蒙科尔比耶。世上可有过比这更悦耳的音节毗邻么？这是给天使起的名字。而顶着这名字的，也必是一位天使——嗯，是的，一位天使，如假包换。天堂里的众圣徒啊，羡慕我吧！羡慕我吧，」他朝繁星打出一个豪迈的手势，喊道，「因为弗朗索瓦同你们哪一位换位置都不干。」

===Original===
He crept through ordered rows of chestnuts and acacias to a window wherein burned a dim light. He unslung a lute from his shoulder and began to sing, secure in the knowledge that deaf old Jehan de Vaucelles was not likely to be disturbed by sound of any nature till that time when it should please high God that the last trump be noised about the tumbling heavens.

It was good to breathe the mingled odor of roses and mignonette that was thick about him. It was good to sing to her a wailing song of unrequited love and know that she loved him. François dallied with his bliss, parodied his bliss, and—as he complacently reflected—lamented in the moonlight with as tuneful a dolor as Messire Orpheus may have evinced when he carolled in Hades.

Sang François:

她的脸告诉了他。他看见的是憎恶。

「对不起，」卡特琳木然说。「对不起。哦，看在至高上帝的份上！走吧，走吧！你要钱么？只要你肯走，我什么都给。哦，畜生！哦，猪，猪，猪！」

他转身走了，像醉汉一样踉踉跄跄。

===Original===
Once in the garden he fell prone upon his face in the wet grass. About him the mingled odor of roses and mignonette was sweet and heavy; the fountain plashed interminably in the night, and above him the chestnuts and acacias rustled and lisped as they had done seven years ago. Only he was changed.

“O Mother of God,” the thief prayed, “grant that Noël may be kind to her! Mother of God, grant that she may be happy! Mother of God, grant that I may not live long!”

And straightway he perceived that triple invocation could be, rather neatly, worked out in ballade form. Yes, with a separate prayer to each verse. So, dismissing for the while his misery, he fell to considering, with undried cheeks, what rhymes he needed.

随即他便察觉，这三重祈愿大可相当巧妙地铺排成一首谣曲体。是的，每节各配一段祷词。于是，他暂且把悲苦撇在一旁，带着未干的脸颊，琢磨起自己需要哪些韵脚来。
