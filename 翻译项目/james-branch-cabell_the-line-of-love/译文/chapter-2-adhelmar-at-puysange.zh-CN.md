Which reminds me that it was an uncomfortable number of years ago, in an out-of-the-way corner of the library at Allonby Shaw, that I first came upon *Les Aventures d’Adhelmar de Nointel*. ===Original===
The story, told in decasyllabic couplets, interspersed after a rather unusual fashion with innumerable lyrics, seems in the main authentic. Sir Adhelmar de Nointel, born about 1332, was once a real and stalwart personage, a younger brother to that Henri de Nointel, the fighting Bishop of Mantes, whose unsavory part in the murder of Jacques van Arteveldt history has recorded at length; and it is with the exploits of this Adhelmar that the romance deals, not, it may be, without exaggeration.

===Chinese===

April-Magic

When Adhelmar had ended the tale of Dame Venus and the love which she bore the knight Tannhäuser (here one overtakes Nicolas midcourse in narrative), Adhelmar put away the book and sighed. The Demoiselle Mélite laughed a little﻿—her laughter, as I have told you, was high and delicate, with the resonance of thin glass﻿—and demanded the reason of his sudden grief.

四月的魔法

当阿代尔玛讲完维纳斯夫人（Dame Venus）与她钟爱骑士唐怀瑟（Tannhäuser）的那段故事时（此处我们追上了叙事中途的尼古拉），他把书搁在一边，叹了口气。梅莉特小姐（Demoiselle Mélite）轻轻笑了——她的笑声，如我先前已告诉过您的，清越高雅，带一点薄玻璃的鸣响——便问他骤然生悲，是何缘故。

===Original===
“I sigh,” he answered, “for sorrow that this Dame Venus is dead.”

“Surely,” said she, wondering at his glum face, “that is no great matter.”

“By Saint Vulfran, yes!” Adhelmar protested; “for the same Lady Venus was the fairest of women, as all learned clerks avow; and she is dead these many years, and now there is no woman left alive so beautiful as she﻿—saving one alone, and she will have none of me. And therefore,” he added, very slowly, “I sigh for desire of Dame Venus and for envy of the knight Tannhäuser.”

===Chinese===
“我叹息，”他答道，“是伤怀这位维纳斯夫人已经死了。”

“说真的，”她说，对他那副阴沉面孔颇为诧异，“这可算不得什么了不得的大事。”

“凭圣伍尔弗兰起誓，正是了不得的大事！”阿代尔玛抗辩道；“因为这位维纳斯夫人是绝世的美人，凡有学问的书记官都这般作证；而她已死去多年，如今世上再没有一个活着的女人及得上她的美——只除了一位，而她偏不要我。所以，”他极慢地补上一句，“我为渴慕维纳斯夫人而叹息，为嫉羡骑士唐怀瑟而叹息。”

===Original===
Again Mélite laughed, but she forbore﻿—discreetly enough﻿—to question him concerning the lady who was of equal beauty with Dame Venus.

It was an April morning, and they set in the hedged garden of Puysange. Adhelmar read to her of diverse ancient queens and of the love-business wherein each took part, relating the histories of the Lady Heleine and of her sweethearting with Duke Paris, the Emperor of Troy’s son, and of the Lady Melior that loved Parthénopex of Blois, and of the Lady Aude, for love of whom Sieur Roland slew the pagan Angoulaffre, and of the Lady Cresseide that betrayed love, and of the Lady Morgaine la Fée, whose Danish lover should yet come from Avalon to save France in her black hour of need. All these he read aloud, suavely, with bland modulations, for he was a man of letters, as letters went in those days. Originally, he had been bred for the Church; but this vocation he had happily forsaken long since, protesting with some show of reason that France at this particular time had a greater need of spears than of *ave*’s.

===Chinese===
梅莉特又笑了，但她忍住了——忍得够审慎——没有去追问那位与维纳斯夫人同样美丽的女子究竟是谁。

那是个四月的早晨，他们坐在皮桑日围篱环抱的园中。阿代尔玛给她诵读各代古代王后的故事，以及每一位牵涉其中的情爱纠葛：他讲海莱娜夫人（Lady Heleine）与她同特洛伊皇帝之子帕里斯公爵（Duke Paris）的私情密约，讲爱上了布卢瓦的帕尔特诺佩克斯（Parthénopex of Blois）的梅利奥夫人（Lady Melior），讲奥德夫人（Lady Aude）——罗兰大人（Sieur Roland）为爱她手刃了异教徒安古拉弗尔——讲背弃了爱情的克蕾西德夫人（Lady Cresseide），又讲仙姬摩根（Lady Morgaine la Fée）——她的丹麦情郎终将从阿瓦隆（Avalon）降临，在法兰西至暗的危时拯救法兰西。这一切他都朗声诵读，音调温文，抑扬圆熟，因为他是位文人——以那个年代的文人标准而论。他起初本是被养来侍奉教会的；但这份前程他久已欣然抛却，还振振有词地分辩说，值此非常之世，法兰西更需要长矛，而非一遍遍的《圣母经》（*ave*）。

===Original===
For the rest, Sir Adhelmar de Nointel was known as a valiant knight, who had won glory in the wars with the English. He had lodged for a fortnight at Puysange, of which castle the master, Sire Reinault (son to the late Vicomte Florian) was Adhelmar’s cousin: and on the next day Adhelmar proposed to set forth for Paris, where the French King﻿—Jehan the Luckless﻿—was gathering his lieges about him to withstand his kinsman, Edward of England.

Now, as I have said, Adhelmar was cousin to Reinault, and, in consequence, to Reinault’s sister, the Demoiselle Mélite; and the latter Adhelmar loved, at least, as much as a cousin should. That was well known; and Reinault de Puysange had sworn very heartily that this was a great pity when he affianced her to Hugues d’Arques. Both Hugues and Adhelmar had loved Mélite since boyhood﻿—so far their claims ran equally. But while Adhelmar had busied himself in the acquisition of some scant fame and a vast number of scars, Hugues had sensibly inherited the fief of Arques, a snug property with fertile lands and a stout fortress. How, then, should Reinault hesitate between them?

===Chinese===
除去这些不谈，阿代尔玛·德·努安泰尔爵士号称骁勇的骑士，在对英格兰人的战事中挣得过荣耀。他在皮桑日已下榻半月；这座城堡的主人雷诺大人（Sire Reinault，故子爵弗洛里安之子）是阿代尔玛的表亲：次日阿代尔玛便打算启程赴巴黎，法兰西国王——“倒霉的约翰”（Jehan the Luckless）——正在那里聚拢封臣，以抵御他的亲戚、英格兰的爱德华。

前已说过，阿代尔玛是雷诺的表亲，因而也是雷诺之妹梅莉特小姐的表亲；而这一位，阿代尔玛爱她——至少，爱到一个表亲应有的分量。这是人尽皆知的事；所以雷诺·德·皮桑日把她许给于格·达尔克（Hugues d’Arques）的时候，曾痛心疾首地赌咒说这真是一大憾事。于格与阿代尔玛两人自幼便都爱着梅莉特——到此为止，两人资格相当。只是当阿代尔玛忙着挣取薄薄一点名声和满身伤疤的时候，于格却清醒务实地继承了达尔克的封地，一份膏腴遍野、坚堡矗立的可观产业。如此，雷诺在两人之间还有什么可犹豫的呢？

===Original===
He did not. For the Château d’Arques, you must understand, was builded in Lower Normandy, on the fringe of the hill-country, just where the peninsula of Cotentin juts out into the sea; Puysange stood not far north, among the level lands of Upper Normandy: and these two being the strongest castles in those parts, what more natural and desirable than that the families should be united by marriage? Reinault informed his sister of his decision; she wept a little, but did not refuse to comply.

So Adhelmar, come again to Puysange after five years’ absence, found Mélite troth-plighted, fast and safe, to Hugues. Reinault told him. Adhelmar grumbled and bit his nails in a corner, for a time; then laughed shortly.

“I have loved Mélite,” he said. “It may be that I love her still. Hah, Saint Vulfran! why should I not? Why should a man not love his cousin?”

Adhelmar grinned, while the vicomte twitched his beard and wished Adhelmar at the devil.

===Chinese===
他并没有犹豫。因为您得明白，达尔克城堡（Château d’Arques）坐落在下诺曼底，正筑在丘陵地带的边沿、科唐坦半岛（Cotentin）探入海中的那一角；皮桑日则矗立在北面不远的上诺曼底平川之上：这两座是该地带最坚固的城堡，两姓联姻，还有比这更自然、更相宜的事么？雷诺把他的决定告知妹妹；她哭了一小会儿，却没有拒绝依从。

于是，阔别五年重回皮桑日的阿代尔玛，发现梅莉特已经同于格订下婚约，钉是钉、铆是铆，稳稳当当。是雷诺告诉他的。阿代尔玛缩在角落里咕哝、咬了一阵指甲；随即短促地笑了一声。

“我爱过梅莉特，”他说。“如今兴许还爱着。哈，圣伍尔弗兰！我为什么不该爱？一个男人为什么不该爱自己的表妹？”

But the young knight stuck fast at Puysange, for all that, and he and Mélite were much together. Daily they made parties to dance, and to hunt the deer, and to fish, but most often to rehearse songs. For Adhelmar made good songs.1

Today, the summer already stirring in the womb of the year, they sat, as I have said, in the hedged garden; and about them the birds piped and wrangled over their nest-building, and daffodils danced in spring’s honor with lively saltations, and overhead the sky was colored like a robin’s egg. It was very perilous weather for young folk. By reason of this, when he had ended his reading about the lady of the hollow hill, Sir Adhelmar sighed again, and stared at his companion with hungry eyes, wherein desire strained like a hound at the leash.

Mélite bent over him; for as he spoke he had lain back in a tall carven chair by the east window. She was past speech. But now, for a moment, her lips clung to his, and her warm tears fell upon his face. What better death for a lover? thought Adhelmar.

Yet he murmured somewhat. “Pity, always pity!” he said, wearily. “I shall never win aught else of you, Mélite. For before this you have kissed me, pitying me because you could not love me. And you have kissed me now, pitying me because I may not live.”

But Mélite, clasping her arms about his neck, whispered into his ear the meaning of this last kiss, and at the honeyed sound of her whispering his strength came back for a moment, and he strove to rise. The level sunlight through the open window smote full upon his face, which was very glad. Mélite was conscious of her nobility in causing him such delight at the last.
