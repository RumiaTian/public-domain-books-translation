## Act II / 第二幕

===Original===
Scene I. Bevil Jr.’s Lodgings.
*Enter **Bevil Jr.** and **Tom**.*
Tom
Sir, Mr. Myrtle.
Bevil Jr.
Very well, do you step again, and wait for an answer to my letter.
*Exit **Tom**.*
*Enter **Myrtle**.*
Bevil Jr.
Well, Charles, why so much care in thy countenance? Is there anything in this world deserves it? You, who used to be so gay, so open, so vacant!
Myrtle
I think we have of late changed complexions. You, who used to be much the graver man, are now all air in your behaviour.—But the cause of my concern may, for aught I know, be the same object that gives you all this satisfaction. In a word, I am told that you are this very day—and your dress confirms me in it—to be married to Lucinda.
Bevil Jr.
You are not misinformed.—Nay, put not on the terrors of a rival till you hear me out. I shall disoblige the best of fathers if I don’t seem ready to marry Lucinda; and you know I have ever told you you might make use of my secret resolution never to marry her for your own service as you please; but I am now driven to the extremity of immediately refusing or complying unless you help me to escape the match.
Myrtle
Escape? Sir, neither her merit or her fortune are below your acceptance—Escaping do you call it?

第一场　小贝维尔寓所。
*小贝维尔（Bevil Jr.）与汤姆（Tom）上。*
汤姆
先生，默特尔先生到。
小贝维尔
很好，你再跑一趟，候我信件的回音。
*汤姆下。*
*默特尔（Myrtle）上。*
小贝维尔
好了，查尔斯，怎么满脸色都是心事？这世上有什么事当得起你这副神情？你，从前那样轻快、那样爽朗、那样无忧无虑的人！
默特尔
我看咱们近来是互换了脸色。你，从前稳重得多的一位，如今举手投足全是轻飘飘。——可我愁的缘由，说不定恰是叫你这般称心的那同一个对象。一句话，人家告诉我，就在今天——你这身衣裳也坐实了我的想法——你要同露辛达（Lucinda）成婚了。
小贝维尔
你听来的消息不假。——别忙，先别摆出一副情敌的惊惶，且听我把话说完。我若不显出随时愿意迎娶露辛达的样子，就要开罪天下最好的父亲；你也知道，我一向同你说过：我私下打定主意永不娶她，这个秘密你尽管随意拿去为你自己效力；可如今我已被逼到绝地，不当场回绝、便当场依从——除非你帮我脱出这门亲事。
默特尔
脱身？先生，论人品、论家财，她都没有一样配你嫌弃——这在你也配叫“脱身”？

===Original===
Bevil Jr.
Dear sir, do you wish I should desire the match?
Myrtle
No; but such is my humorous and sickly state of mind since it has been able to relish nothing but Lucinda, that though I must owe my happiness to your aversion to this marriage, I can’t bear to hear her spoken of with levity or unconcern.
Bevil Jr.
Pardon me, sir, I shall transgress that way no more. She has understanding, beauty, shape, complexion, wit—
Myrtle
Nay, dear Bevil, don’t speak of her as if you loved her neither.
Bevil Jr.
Why, then, to give you ease at once, though I allow Lucinda to have good sense, wit, beauty, and virtue, I know another in whom these qualities appear to me more amiable than in her.
Myrtle
There you spoke like a reasonable and good-natured friend. When you acknowledge her merit, and own your prepossession for another, at once you gratify my fondness and cure my jealousy.

===Chinese===
小贝维尔
亲爱的先生，难道你倒盼着我自己巴望这门亲事不成？
默特尔
不；可是自从我这颗心除了露辛达再尝不出别的滋味，它便落下这样一副古怪多病的脾性：虽说我的幸福全得仰仗你对这桩婚事的嫌恶，可听见有人拿她轻嘴薄舌、不当回事地议论，我又受不了。
小贝维尔
饶恕我，先生，我再也不往那上头失言了。她有头脑，有美貌，有身段，有容色，有机锋——
默特尔
嗐，亲爱的贝维尔，也别说她说得倒像你爱着她似的。
小贝维尔
那么，好叫你一下子宽心：尽管我认露辛达有见识、有机锋、有美貌、有德行，可我还认得一个人，这些品质在她身上，依我看，比在露辛达身上更可爱动人。
默特尔
这才像一位入情入理、厚道为人的朋友说的话。你既称许她的人品，又招认自己心有所属，一句话，既满足了我的痴心，又医好了我的醋意。

===Original===
Bevil Jr.
But all this while you take no notice, you have no apprehension, of another man that has twice the fortune of either of us.
Myrtle
Cimberton!25 hang him, a formal, philosophical, pedantic coxcomb; for the sot, with all these crude notions of diverse things, under the direction of great vanity and very little judgment, shows his strongest bias is avarice; which is so predominant in him that he will examine the limbs of his mistress with the caution of a jockey, and pays no more compliment to her personal charms than if she were a mere breeding animal.
Bevil Jr.
Are you sure that is not affected? I have known some women sooner set on fire by that sort of negligence than by—
Myrtle
No, no; hang him, the rogue has no art; it is pure, simple insolence and stupidity.
Bevil Jr.
Yet, with all this, I don’t take him for a fool.
Myrtle
I own the man is not a natural; he has a very quick sense, though very slow understanding. He says, indeed, many things that want only the circumstances of time and place to be very just and agreeable.

===Chinese===
小贝维尔
可这一路你都没有留意、都没有提防另一个人——他的家财抵得上你我两人加起来翻一倍。
默特尔
辛伯顿（Cimberton）！25 去他的，一个拘谨刻板、满口哲理、迂腐学究气的花花公子；因为那蠢货，肚里装着一堆生吞活剥的杂驳见识，由着极大的虚荣和极小的判断力领路，处处露出他最硬的偏向是贪财；这毛病在他身上占了绝对的上风，以致他打量他情妇的四肢，会像马贩子相马一样仔细掂量，对她的姿色魅力半点不讲情面，只当她是头配种的牲口。
小贝维尔
你拿得准那不是装出来的？我见过有的女人，偏是这一路怠慢，比旁的什么——更容易叫她们着起火来。
默特尔
不，不；去他的，那小子没有半点心机；那是纯纯粹粹、不掺假的傲慢与愚蠢。
小贝维尔
话虽如此，我却不把他当傻瓜。
默特尔
我认账，那人不是天生的傻子；他感官极灵敏，悟性却极迟钝。说实话，他的许多话，单欠一个时地相宜，本来会很中肯、很入耳。

===Original===
Bevil Jr.
Well, you may be sure of me if you can disappoint him; but my intelligence says the mother has actually sent for the conveyancer to draw articles for his marriage with Lucinda, though those for mine with her are, by her father’s orders, ready for signing; but it seems she has not thought fit to consult either him or his daughter in the matter.
Myrtle
Pshaw! a poor troublesome woman. Neither Lucinda nor her father will ever be brought to comply with it. Besides, I am sure Cimberton can make no settlement upon her without the concurrence of his great uncle, Sir Geoffry, in the west.
Bevil Jr.
Well, sir, and I can tell you that’s the very point that is now laid before her counsel, to know whether a firm settlement can be made without his uncle’s actual joining in it. Now, pray consider, sir, when my affair with Lucinda comes, as it soon must, to an open rupture, how are you sure that Cimberton’s fortune may not then tempt her father, too, to hear his proposals?
Myrtle
There you are right, indeed; that must be provided against. Do you know who are her counsel?
Bevil Jr.
Yes, for your service I have found out that, too. They are Serjeant Bramble and Old Target—by the way, they are neither of them known in the family. Now, I was thinking why you might not put a couple of false counsel upon her to delay and confound matters a little; besides, it may probably let you into the bottom of her whole design against you.

===Chinese===
小贝维尔
那么，只要你能搅黄他，我这边你只管放心；可我得的线报说，那位母亲已经当真遣人去请产权转让律师，来草拟他同露辛达的婚书条款了——虽然我同她的那一份，奉她父亲的吩咐，早已备好待签；只是这桩事，她似乎觉得犯不着去请教她丈夫，也犯不着请教他女儿。
默特尔
呸！一个可怜又难缠的女人。露辛达和她父亲都断断不会被她逼得点头。再说，我拿得稳，辛伯顿没有他那位住在西部的叔祖杰弗里爵士（Sir Geoffry）点头应承，一分产业也立不到她名下。
小贝维尔
是么，先生，我正可以告诉你：眼下摆到她那几位律师面前的，恰恰就是这一条——不经他叔祖先自到场连署，立不立得起一份牢靠的婚产契约。现在请你想一想，先生，待我同露辛达的亲事不久必然闹到公开决裂，你怎保得准到那时，辛伯顿的家财不会也勾得她父亲肯去听听他的提亲？
默特尔
这话你说得实在对；这一点不可不防。你知道她的律师是哪几位？
小贝维尔
知道，为着你的事，我连这个也打听出来了。是布兰布尔高级律师（Serjeant Bramble）和老塔吉特（Old Target）——顺便说一句，这两位，那府上谁都没见过。我正在想，你何不安排两个假律师到她那边去，把事情拖一拖、搅一搅；再者，这说不定还能叫你摸透她整盘对付你的算计。

===Original===
Myrtle
As how, pray?
Bevil Jr.
Why, can’t you slip on a black wig and a gown, and be Old Bramble yourself?
Myrtle
Ha! I don’t dislike it.—But what shall I do for a brother in the case?
Bevil Jr.
What think you of my fellow, Tom? The rogue’s intelligent, and is a good mimic. All his part will be but to stutter heartily, for that’s old Target’s case. Nay, it would be an immoral thing to mock him were it not that his impertinence is the occasion of its breaking out to that degree. The conduct of the scene will chiefly lie upon you.
Myrtle
I like it of all things. If you’ll send Tom to my chambers, I will give him full instructions. This will certainly give me occasion to raise difficulties, to puzzle or confound her project for a while at least.
Bevil Jr.
I’ll warrant you success.—So far we are right, then. And now, Charles, your apprehension of my marrying her is all you have to get over.

哈！这主意我不讨厌。——可这出戏里，我上哪儿再寻一位同行的弟兄？

默特尔
我简直再喜欢不过。你若肯把汤姆差到我住处来，我会把他面授周全。这一来我必有由头横生枝节，至少好一阵子，叫她的盘算晕头转向。
小贝维尔
包你成功。——这一关算是过了。那么查尔斯，如今你只剩一桩心事要打发：怕我当真娶了她。

Myrtle
Dear Bevil, though I know you are my friend, yet when I abstract myself from my own interest in the thing, I know no objection she can make to you, or you to her, and therefore hope—
Bevil Jr.
Dear Myrtle, I am as much obliged to you for the cause of your suspicion, as I am offended at the effect; but, be assured, I am taking measures for your certain security, and that all things with regard to me will end in your entire satisfaction.
Myrtle

Myrtle
Nay, no ceremony, you know I must be going.

Bevil Jr.
Well, this is another instance of the perplexities which arise, too, in faithful friendship. We must often in this life go on in our good offices, even under the displeasure of those to whom we do them, in compassion to their weaknesses and mistakes.—But all this while poor Indiana is tortured with the doubt of me. She has no support or comfort but in my fidelity, yet sees me daily pressed to marriage with another. How painful, in such a crisis, must be every hour she thinks on me! I’ll let her see at least my conduct to her is not changed. I’ll take this opportunity to visit her; for though the religious vow I have made to my father restrains me from ever marrying without his approbation, yet that confines me not from seeing a virtuous woman that is the pure delight of my eyes and the guiltless joy of my heart. But the best condition of human life is but a gentler misery—
To hope for perfect happiness is vain,
And love has ever its allays of pain.
