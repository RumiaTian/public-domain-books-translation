## II / 二

===Original===
He pursued his leisurely way to the Bellingham Library, one of the institutions of London that is known only to a select few. No novel or volume of sparkling reminiscence has a place upon the shelves of this institution, founded a hundred years ago to provide scientists and littérateurs with an opportunity of consulting volumes which were unprocurable save at the British Museum. On the four floors which constituted the building, fat volumes of German philosophy, learned and, to the layman, unintelligible books on scientific phenomena, obscure treatises on almost every kind of uninteresting subject, stood shoulder to shoulder upon their sedate shelves.

===Chinese===
他不紧不慢地一路走到了贝灵厄姆图书馆——伦敦的机构里，只有寥寥少数人才知道的一处。这家机构创建于一百年前，为的是让科学家和文人们有机会查阅那些除了大英博物馆之外无处可觅的书籍；馆里的书架上没有一本小说，也没有一部妙语连珠的回忆录。在构成这座楼的四层楼面上，大部头的德国哲学著作、学究气十足而在外行看来不知所云的科学现象论著，以及几乎每一门枯燥学科的冷僻专论，都在静穆的书架上比肩而立。

===Original===
John Bellingham, who in the eighteenth century had founded this exchange of learning, had provided in the trust deeds that “two intelligent females, preferably in indigent circumstances,” should form part of the staff, and it was to one of these that Dick was conducted.

===Chinese===
十八世纪创建这座学术交流之所的约翰·贝灵厄姆（John Bellingham），在信托契据中规定馆内职员应包括“两名聪慧的女性，以家境清寒者为宜”，迪克被领去见的正是其中一位。

===Original===
In a small, high-ceilinged room, redolent of old leather, a girl sat at a table, engaged in filing index cards.

===Chinese===
在一间天花板很高、弥漫着旧皮革气味的小屋里，一位姑娘坐在桌前，正在给索引卡片归档。

===Original===
“I am from Scotland Yard,” Dick introduced himself. “I understand that some of your books have been stolen?”

===Chinese===
“我是苏格兰场来的。”迪克自我介绍道，“听说贵馆有些书被人偷了？”

===Original===
He was looking at the packed shelves as he spoke, for he was not interested in females, intelligent or stupid, indigent or wealthy. The only thing he noticed about her was that she wore black and that her hair was a golden-brown and was brushed into a fringe over her forehead. In a vague way he supposed that most girls had hair of golden-brown, and he had a dim idea that fringes were popular among working-class ladies.

===Chinese===
说话的当儿他望着塞得满满的书架，因为他对女性不感兴趣，聪明的也好，愚笨的也好，清寒的也好，阔绰的也好。他注意到她的只有一点：她一身黑衣，头发是金褐色的，在额前梳成一排刘海。他恍恍惚惚地以为大多数姑娘的头发都是金褐色，又模模糊糊地觉得刘海在劳动阶层的女士当中正时兴。

===Original===
“Yes,” she said quietly, “a book was stolen from this room whilst I was at luncheon. It was not very valuable﻿—a German volume written by Haeckel called *Generelle Morphologie*.”

===Chinese===
“是的。”她平静地说，“我吃午饭那会儿，这屋里丢了一本书。那书不算太贵重——是海克尔（Haeckel）写的一部德文书，叫《普通形态学》（*Generelle Morphologie*）。”

===Original===
She opened a drawer and took out an index card and laid it before him, and he read the words without being greatly enlightened.

===Chinese===
她拉开一个抽屉，取出一张索引卡，摆在他面前；他读了读上面的字，没有受到多少启发。

===Original===
“Who was here in your absence?” he asked.

“My assistant, a girl named Helder.”

===Chinese===
“你不在的时候，谁在这屋里？”他问。

“我的助手，一个叫赫尔德（Helder）的姑娘。

===Original===
“Did any of your subscribers come into this room during that time?”

“Several,” she replied. “I have their names, but most of them are above suspicion. The only visitor we had who is not a subscriber of the library was a gentleman named Stalletti, an Italian doctor, who called to make inquiries as to subscription.”

===Chinese===
“那段时间里，有没有哪位订户进过这间屋？”

“有好几位。”她答道，“名字我这里都有，不过他们大多无可怀疑。当天来访的人里，只有一位不是本馆的订户——一位名叫斯塔莱蒂（Stalletti）的先生，意大利医生，是来打听入订事宜的。”

===Original===
“He gave his name?” asked Dick.

“No,” said the girl to his surprise; “but Miss Helder recognized him; she had seen his portrait somewhere. I should have thought you would have remembered his name.”

===Chinese===
“他报了姓名？”迪克问。

“没有。”姑娘的答话让他颇为意外，“不过赫尔德小姐认出了他；她在哪儿见过他的画像。我原以为你会记得他的名字。”

===Original===
“Why on earth should I remember his name, my good girl?” asked Dick a little irritably.

“Why on earth shouldn’t you, my good man?” she demanded coolly, and at that moment Dick Martin was aware of her, in the sense that she emerged from the background against which his life moved and became a personality.

===Chinese===
“我到底凭什么该记得他的名字，我的好姑娘？”迪克有点恼火地问。

“你到底凭什么不该记得呢，我的好先生？”她冷冷地诘问。就在这一刻，迪克·马丁才真正留意到她——也就是说，她从他生活赖以活动的那幅背景中显现出来，成了一个活生生的人物。

===Original===
Her eyes were grey and set wide apart; her nose straight and small; the mouth was a little wide﻿—and she certainly had golden-brown hair.

===Chinese===
她的眼睛是灰色的，两眼分得很开；鼻子又直又小巧；嘴巴略宽了些——而那头发，确确实实是金褐色的。

===Original===
“I beg your pardon!” he laughed. “As a matter of fact”﻿—he had a trick of confidence which could be very deceptive﻿—“I’m not at all interested in this infernal robbery. I’m leaving the police force tomorrow.”

===Chinese===
“请您原谅！”他笑道。“说实在的”——他有种对人推心置腹的脾气，很能迷惑人——“这桩可恶的窃案我一点不感兴趣。我明天就离开警队了。”

===Original===
“There will be great joy amongst the criminal classes,” she said politely, and when he saw the light of laughter in her eyes, his heart went out to her.

===Chinese===
“犯罪阶层这下要皆大欢喜了。”她彬彬有礼地说。待他看见她眼中闪动的笑光，他的心便向她倾了过去。

===Original===
“You have a sense of humour,” he smiled.

“You mean by that, that I’ve a sense of *your* humour,” she answered quickly. “I have, or I should very much object to being called ‘my good girl’ even by an officer of the law”﻿—she looked at his card again﻿—“even with the rank of sub-inspector.”

===Chinese===
“你有幽默感。”他微笑道。

“你是想说，我领会得了*你*的那种幽默。”她伶俐地答道，“我确实领会得了，不然的话，哪怕对方是执法官员，人家唤我一声‘我的好姑娘’，我也是要大大见怪的”——她又看了一眼他的名片——“哪怕人家挂着副督察的衔。”

===Original===
There was a chair at his hand. Dick drew it out and sat down unbidden.

===Chinese===
他手边正有一把椅子。迪克把它拉出来，不等相让就坐下了。

===Original===
“I abase myself for my rudeness, and humbly beg information on the subject of Signor Stalletti. The name means no more to me than John Smith﻿—the favourite pseudonym of all gentlemen caught in the act of breaking through the pantry window in the middle of the night.”

===Chinese===
“我为方才的无礼自贬谢罪，并谦卑地恳请赐教斯塔莱蒂先生的种种。这名字于我，与约翰·史密斯并无二致——那是所有半夜里正撬开食品间窗户时被当场拿获的绅士们最钟爱的化名。”

===Original===
For a second she surveyed him gravely, her red lips pursed.

“And you’re a detective?” she said, in a hushed voice. “One of those almost human beings who protect us while we sleep!”

===Chinese===
她红唇微抿，正色打量了他一秒钟。

“而你就是个侦探？”她压低嗓音说，“就是那种趁我们酣睡时保护我们的、差一点儿就算得上人的生物之一！”

===Original===
He was helpless with laughter.

===Chinese===
他笑得没有还手之力。

===Original===
“I surrender!” He put up his hands. “And now, having put me in my place, which I admit is a pretty lowly one, perhaps you will pass across a little information about the purloined literature.”

===Chinese===
“我投降！”他举起双手。“如今你既已把我贬到了我该待的位置——我承认那位置相当低微——那么，关于那部被窃的文献，可否请你赏传一点儿情报过来。”

===Original===
“I’ve no information to pass across.” She leaned back in her chair, looking at him interestedly. “The book was here at two o’clock; it was not here at half past two﻿—there may be fingerprints on the shelf, but I doubt it, because we keep three charladies for the sole purpose of cleaning up fingerprints.”

===Chinese===
“我没有什么情报可赏传。”她往椅背上一靠，饶有兴味地打量着他。“两点钟的时候书还在；两点半就不见了——书架上也许留有指纹，不过我存疑，因为我们专门养着三个清洁女工，唯一的职责便是清除指纹。”

===Original===
“But who is Stalletti?”

She nodded slowly.

===Chinese===
“可斯塔莱蒂是什么人？”

她缓缓点了点头。

===Original===
“That was why I expressed a little wonder about your being a detective,” she said. “My assistant tells me that he is known to the police. Would you like to see his book?”

“Has he written a book?” he asked in genuine surprise.

===Chinese===
“方才我之所以对你这位侦探的身份表示过一点儿诧异，原因就在这里。”她说，“我的助手告诉我，此人是警方挂了号的人物。你想不想看看他的书？”

“他还写过书？”他问，当真吃了一惊。

===Original===
She got up, went out of the room and returned with a thin volume, plainly bound. He took the volume in his hand and read the title.

===Chinese===
她站起身，走出房去，回来时拿着一本薄薄的、装帧朴素的书。他把书拿在手里，念出书名。

===Original===
“*New Thoughts on Constructive Biology*, by Antonio Stalletti.”

===Chinese===
“《建设性生物学新论》（*New Thoughts on Constructive Biology*），安东尼奥·斯塔莱蒂（Antonio Stalletti）著。”

===Original===
Turning the closely printed leaves, broken almost at every page by diagrams and statistical tables, he asked:

“Why did he get into trouble with the police? I didn’t know that it was a criminal offence to write a book.”

===Chinese===
他翻动着排印得密密麻麻的书页——几乎每页都被图解和统计表打断——一边问：

“他怎么会跟警方结的梁子？我可不知道写书竟是刑事犯罪。”

===Original===
“It is,” she said emphatically; “but not invariably punished as such. I understand that the law took no exception to Mr. Stalletti being an author; and that his offence was in connection with vivisection or something equally horrid.”

===Chinese===
“写书确实是犯罪。”她斩钉截铁地说，“只是未必回回都照此惩办。据我所知，法律并不反对斯塔莱蒂先生当作家；他的罪过是在活体解剖上，或是别的什么同样骇人的事由。”

===Original===
“What is all this about?” He handed the book back to her.

“It is about human beings,” she said solemnly, “like you and me; and how much better and happier they would be if, instead of being mollycoddled﻿—I think that is the scientific term﻿—they were allowed to run wild in a wood and fed on a generous diet of nuts.”

===Chinese===
“这书究竟讲些什么？”他把书递还给她。

“讲的是人类，”她郑重其事地说，“就像你和我这样的人；讲的是倘若不由着他们娇生惯养——我想这是个科学术语——而是放他们到树林里撒野，喂他们分量充足的坚果，他们将会变得多么更好、多么幸福。”

===Original===
“Oh, vegetarian stuff!” said Slick contemptuously.

“Not exactly vegetarian. But perhaps you would like to become a subscriber and read it for yourself?”

===Chinese===
“哦，吃素那一套！”“滑头”轻蔑地说。

“倒不全是吃素。不过，你愿不愿意入个订户，亲自读上一读？”

===Original===
And then she dropped her tone of banter.

随后她收起了打趣的腔调。
