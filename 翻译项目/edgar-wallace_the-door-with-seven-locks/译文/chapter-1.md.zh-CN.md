## I / 一



===Original===
Dick Martin’s last official job (as he believed) was to pull in Lew Pheeney, who was wanted in connection with the Helborough bank robbery. He found Lew in a little Soho café, just as he was finishing his coffee.

===Chinese===
迪克·马丁（Dick Martin）最后一桩公务（他自己也这么认为），就是逮捕卢·菲尼（Lew Pheeney），此人因涉嫌赫尔堡罗（Helborough）银行劫案而被通缉。他在苏荷区（Soho）一家小咖啡馆里找到了卢，那人正喝完咖啡。

===Original===
“What’s the idea, colonel?” asked Lew, almost genially, as he got his hat.
“The inspector wants to talk to you about that Helborough job,” said Dick.

===Chinese===
“这是什么路数，‘上校’？”卢取帽子的时候问道，语气近乎亲热。
“督察想找你谈谈赫尔堡罗那桩案子。”迪克说。

===Original===
Lew’s nose wrinkled in contempt.
“Helborough grandmothers!” he said scornfully. “I’m out of that bank business﻿—thought you knew it. What are you doing in the force, Martin? They told me that you’d run into money and had quit.”

===Chinese===
卢的鼻子轻蔑地皱了起来。
“去他的赫尔堡罗！”他不屑地说，“银行那档子事我早就不沾了——还当你知道呢。你怎么还在警队当差，马丁？他们说你发了一笔财，已经不干了。”

===Original===
“I’m quitting. You’re my last bit of business.”
“Too bad you’re falling down on the last lap!” grinned Lew. “I’ve got forty-five well-oiled alibis. I’m surprised at you, Martin. You know I don’t ‘blow’ banks; locks are my speciality﻿—”

===Chinese===
“我这就不干了。你是我经手的最后一桩事。”
“可惜你在最后一棒上栽了跟头！”卢咧嘴笑道，“我有四十五条滑溜顺当的不在场证明。你真让我吃惊，马丁。你知道我从不‘炸’银行；开锁才是我的本行——”

===Original===
“What were you doing at ten o’clock on Tuesday night?”
A broad smile illuminated the homely face of the burglar.

===Chinese===
“星期二晚上十点钟，你在干什么？”
窃贼那张其貌不扬的脸上绽开了一个灿烂的笑容。

===Original===
“If I told you, you’d think I was lying.”
“Give me a chance,” pleaded Dick, his blue eyes twinkling.

===Chinese===
“我要是说了，你会当我是在撒谎。”
“给我个机会嘛。”迪克央告道，蓝眼睛里闪着光。

===Original===
Lew did not reply at once. He seemed to be pondering the dangers of too great frankness. But when he had seen all sides of the matter, he spoke the truth.

===Chinese===
卢没有立刻回答。他似乎在掂量过分坦白的风险。等他把这件事的方方面面都盘算遍了，才说出了实话。

===Original===
“I was doing a private job﻿—a job I don’t want to talk about. It was dirty, but honest.”

===Chinese===
“我在干一桩私活——一桩我不愿多谈的活儿。那活儿肮脏，倒不犯法。”

===Original===
“And were you well paid?” asked his captor, polite but incredulous.

===Chinese===
“报酬优厚吗？”抓他的这一位问道，客气里透着不信。

===Original===
“I was﻿—I got one hundred and fifty pounds on account. That makes you jump, but it is the truth. I was picking locks, certainly the toughest locks I’ve ever struck, and it was a kind of horrible job I wouldn’t do again for a carload of money. You don’t believe me, but I can prove that I spent the night at the Royal Arms, Chichester, that I was there at eight o’clock to dinner and at eleven o’clock to sleep. So you can forget all that Helborough bank stuff. I know the gang that did it, and you know ’em too, and we don’t change cards.”

===Chinese===
“优厚——我先到手了一百五十镑。这话吓你一跳，可千真万确。我在开锁，那绝对是我这辈子碰上过的最难啃的锁，那活儿邪门得很，给我一汽车的钱我也不再干第二回。你不信，可我能证明那晚我是在奇切斯特（Chichester）的皇家纹章旅馆（Royal Arms）过的夜，八点钟在那儿吃的晚饭，十一点钟上的床。所以，赫尔堡罗银行那摊子事你就省省吧。我知道是哪一伙人干的，你也知道，咱们谁也不亮牌。”

===Original===
They kept Lew in the cells all night whilst inquiries were pursued. Remarkably enough, he had not only stayed at the Royal Arms at Chichester, but had stayed in his own name; and it was true that at a quarter to eleven, before the Helborough bank robbers had left the premises, he was taking a drink in his room, sixty miles away. So authority released Lew in the morning and Dick went into breakfast with him, because, between the professional thief-taker and the professional burglar there is no real ill-feeling, and Sub-Inspector Richard Martin was almost as popular with the criminal classes as he was at police headquarters.

===Chinese===
卢在拘留室里被关了一夜，期间警方四下查证。说来也怪，他不仅确曾在奇切斯特的皇家纹章旅馆投宿，而且登记的还是本名；同样确凿的是，十点三刻，赫尔堡罗银行的劫匪们还没离开现场，他已经在六十英里外自己的房间里喝上了酒。于是当局一早就把卢放了，迪克还同他一道去吃了早餐，因为在职业捕快和职业窃贼之间并没有真正的恶感，而副督察理查德·马丁在匪类当中的人缘，几乎不亚于他在警察总部的声望。

===Original===
“No, Mr. Martin, I’m not going to tell you anything more than I’ve already told you,” said Lew good-humouredly. “And when you call me a liar, I’m not so much as hurt in my feelings. I got a hundred an’ fifty pounds, and I’d have got a thousand if I’d pulled it off. You can guess all round it, but you’ll never guess right.”

===Chinese===
“不，马丁先生，除了我已经告诉你的，我什么也不会再多说。”卢和颜悦色地说，“你当我是撒谎，我心里也谈不上多委屈。我拿到了一百五十镑，要是那事儿办成了，还能拿一千。你尽管围着这事猜，可你永远猜不中。”

===Original===
Dick Martin was eyeing him keenly.
“You’ve got a good story in your mind﻿—spill it,” he said.

===Chinese===
迪克·马丁正锐利地打量着他。
“你肚子里揣着个好听的故事——倒出来吧。”他说。

===Original===
He waited suggestively, but Lew Pheeney shook his head.
“I’m not telling. The story would give away a man who’s not a good fellow, and not one I admire; but I can’t let my personal feelings get the better of me, and you’ll have to go on guessing. And I’m not lying. I’ll tell you how it happened.”

===Chinese===
他满怀期待地等着，可卢·菲尼直摇头。
“我不说。这故事会把一个家伙给抖搂出去，那家伙不是什么好东西，我也不佩服他；可我不能让私人感情占了上风，你只好接着猜。我没有撒谎。我可以把事情的经过讲给你听。”

===Original===
He gulped down a cup of hot coffee and pushed cup and saucer away from him.

===Chinese===
他一口气灌下一杯热咖啡，把杯碟推到一旁。

===Original===
“I don’t know this fellow who asked me to do the work﻿—not personally. He’s been in trouble for something or other, but that’s no business of mine. One night he met me, introduced himself, and I went to his house﻿—br-r!” he shivered. “Martin, a crook is a pretty clean man﻿—at least, all the crooks I know; and thieving’s just a game with two players; me and the police. If they snooker me, good luck to ’em! If I can beat them, good luck to me! But there’s some dirt that makes me sick, just makes my stomach turn over. When he told me the job he wanted me for, I thought he was joking, and my first idea was to turn it in right away. But I’m just the most curious creature that ever lived, and it was a new experience, so, after a lot of think, I said ‘Yes.’ Mind you, there was nothing dishonest in it. All he wanted to do was to take a peep at something. What was behind it I don’t know. I don’t want to talk about it, but the locks beat me.”

===Chinese===
“托我干这活儿的家伙我不认识——素不相识。他为这样那样的事吃过官司，可那与我无关。一天夜里他找上我，自报了家门，我就去了他家——嚯！”他打了个寒战。“马丁，贼是挺干净的人——至少我认得的贼都这样；偷东西不过是一场两个人的游戏：我和警察。他们设局算计了我，算他们走运！我要是赢了他们，算我走运！可有些肮脏事真让我恶心，直叫我反胃。他跟我说要我干的那活儿时，我还当他开玩笑，头一个念头就是当场回绝。可我偏偏是天底下头一号好奇的人，那又是桩新鲜经历，于是左思右想之后，我说了声‘好’。提醒你一句，那活儿没有任何不清不白的地方。他不过是想看一眼什么东西。背后藏着什么名堂，我不知道。我不愿多谈，反正那几把锁把我难住了。”

===Original===
“A lawyer’s safe?” suggested the interested detective.

===Chinese===
“律师的保险柜？”听得来了兴致的侦探提示道。

===Original===
The other shook his head. He turned the subject abruptly; spoke of his plans﻿—he was leaving for the United States, to join his brother, who was an honest builder.

===Chinese===
对方摇了摇头。他陡然岔开话题，谈起自己的打算——他要动身去美国投奔兄弟，他兄弟是个本本分分的营造商。

===Original===
“We’re both going out of the game together, Martin,” he smiled. “You’re too good a man for a policeman, and I’m too much of a gentleman to be on the crook. I shouldn’t be surprised if we met one of these days.”

===Chinese===
“咱们俩这是要一块儿退出这一行喽，马丁。”他笑道，“你当警察，人太好了；我做贼，绅士气又太重。哪天咱们再碰上面，我也不会奇怪。”

===Original===
Dick went back to the Yard to make, as he thought, a final report to his immediate chief.

===Chinese===
迪克回到苏格兰场（Scotland Yard），打算向直属上司作他自认为的最后一次汇报。

===Original===
Captain Sneed sniffed.

===Chinese===
斯尼德上尉（Captain Sneed）嗤了嗤鼻子。

===Original===
“That Lew Pheeney couldn’t fall straight,” he said; “if you dropped him down a well, he’d wear away the brickwork. Honest robber! He’s got that out of a book. You think you’ve finished work, I suppose?”

===Chinese===
“那个卢·菲尼，天生就歪，想直着掉下去都难。”他说，“你把他扔进井里，他能把井壁的砖都磨穿。老实的劫匪！这词儿他准是从哪本书里学来的。我猜，你以为自己已经交差了事？”

===Original===
Dick nodded.
“Going to buy a country house and be a gentleman. Ride to hounds and take duchesses into dinner﻿—what a hell of a life for a grown man!”

===Chinese===
迪克点了点头。
“打算买座乡间宅邸，当个绅士。骑马猎狐，挽着公爵夫人入席——一个大男人竟要过这种日子，真是活见鬼！”

===Original===
Dick Martin grinned at the sneer. He wanted very little persuasion to withdraw his resignation; already he was repenting﻿—and, despite the attraction of authorship which beckoned ahead, he would have given a lot of money to recall the letter he had sent to the commissioner.

===Chinese===
迪克·马丁对这番讥诮咧嘴一笑。要劝他收回辞呈，几乎用不着费什么口舌；他本来就在后悔——虽说前方招手的是当作家的诱人前景，他还是情愿花上一大笔钱，追回寄给总监的那封辞呈。

===Original===
“It’s a queer thing how money ruins a man,” said Captain Sneed sadly. “Now if I had a six-figure legacy I should want to do nothing.”

===Chinese===
“钱这东西毁人，真是桩怪事。”斯尼德上尉悲伤地说，“我要是得了一笔六位数的遗产，就什么都不想干了。”

===Original===
His assistant might sneer in turn.
“You want to do nothing, anyway,” he said; “you’re lazy, Sneed﻿—the laziest man who ever filled a chair at Scotland Yard.”

===Chinese===
他的助手便反唇相讥。
“你本来就什么都不想干。”他说，“你懒，斯尼德——在苏格兰场坐过班的人里，数你顶懒。”

===Original===
The fat man, who literally filled and overflowed the padded office chair in which he half sat and half lay, a picture of inertia, raised his reproachful eyes to his companion.

===Chinese===
胖子半坐半躺在一把蒙着软垫的办公椅里，实实在在把椅子填满还往外溢，活脱脱一幅怠惰图。他抬起一双责备的眼睛望着同伴。

===Original===
“Insubordination,” he murmured. “You’re not out of the force till tomorrow﻿—call me ‘sir’ and be respectful. I hate reminding you that you’re a paltry sub-inspector and that I’m as near being a superintendent as makes no difference. It would sound snobbish. I’m not lazy, I’m lethargic. It’s a sort of disease.”

===Chinese===
“以下犯上。”他喃喃道，“到明天你才算脱离警队——先叫一声‘长官’，放规矩点。我真不愿意提醒你：你不过是个区区副督察，而我嘛，跟警司也只差一步，几乎没分别。这话听着像摆架子。我不懒，我是倦怠。这是一种病。”

===Original===
“You’re fat because you’re lazy, and you’re lazy because you’re fat,” insisted the lean-faced young man. “It’s a sort of vicious circle. Besides, you’re rich enough to retire if you wanted.”

===Chinese===
“你胖是因为你懒，你懒是因为你胖。”瘦脸的年轻人坚持道，“这是个恶性循环。再说，你富得都够退休了，只要你想退。”

===Original===
Captain Sneed stroked his chin reflectively. He was a giant of a man, with shoulders of an ox and the height of a Grenadier, but he was admittedly inert. He sighed heavily, and, groping in a desk basket, produced a blue paper.

===Chinese===
斯尼德上尉若有所思地摸着下巴。他是个巨人般的汉子，生着公牛般的肩膀、掷弹卫兵般的个头，无奈公认地不爱动弹。他沉沉地叹了口气，在公文筐里摸索了一阵，抽出一张蓝纸。

===Original===
“You’re a common civilian tomorrow﻿—but my slave today. Go along to Bellingham Library; there has been a complaint about stolen books.”

===Chinese===
“明天你就是平头百姓了——今天还得给我当苦力。跑一趟贝灵厄姆图书馆（Bellingham Library）吧；有人投诉丢了书。”

===Original===
Sub-Inspector Dick Martin groaned.

===Chinese===
副督察迪克·马丁哀叹一声。

===Original===
“It’s not romantic, I admit,” said his superior with a slow, broad smile; “kleptomania belongs to the dust and debris of detective work, but it is good for your soul. It will remind you, whilst you’re loafing on the money you didn’t earn, that there are a few thousand of your poor comrades wearin’ their feet into ankles with fool inquiries like this!”

===Chinese===
“我承认，这不浪漫。”他的上司慢慢咧开一个大大的笑容，“盗窃癖属于侦探行当里的尘土碎屑，可它对你的灵魂有好处。它会提醒你：当你靠着不是自己挣来的钱游手好闲时，还有几千个可怜的同僚，正为这类愚蠢的查访把脚板磨得只剩脚踝！”

===Original===
Dick (or “Slick” as he was called for certain reasons) wondered as he walked slowly down the long corridor whether he was glad or sorry that police work lay behind him and that on the morrow he might pass the most exalted official without saluting. He was a “larceny man,” the cleverest taker of thieves the Yard had known. Sneed often said that he had the mind of a thief, and meant this as a compliment. He certainly had the skill. There was a memorable night when, urged thereto by the highest police official in London, he had picked the pocket of a Secretary of State, taken his watch, his pocketbook and his private papers, and not even the expert watchers saw him perform the fell deed.

===Chinese===
迪克——出于某些缘故，人家都管他叫“滑头”（Slick）——沿着长长的走廊缓缓走着，心里琢磨着：警界生涯已成过去，明天起哪怕迎面遇上最高长官也可以不敬礼，这究竟是该高兴还是该惋惜。他是个“窃案侦探”，苏格兰场有史以来最高明的捉贼高手。斯尼德常说，他长着一颗贼的头脑，而这是句恭维话。他的确有这份本事。有个令人难忘的夜晚，伦敦警界最高的长官撺掇他露一手，他扒了一位国务大臣的口袋，取走了人家的怀表、皮夹和私人文件，连那些行家监视都没瞧见他如何干下这桩大胆的勾当。

===Original===
Dick Martin came to the Yard from Canada, where his father had been governor of a prison. He was neither a good guardian of criminals or youth. Dick had the run of the prison, and could take a stick pin from a man’s cravat before he had mastered the mysteries of algebra. Peter du Bois, a lifer, taught him to open almost any kind of door with a bent hairpin; Lew Andrevski, a frequent visitor to Fort Stuart, made a specially small pack of cards out of the covers of the chapel prayer books, in order that the lad should be taught to conceal three cards in each tiny palm. If he had not been innately honest, the tuition might easily have ruined him.

===Chinese===
迪克·马丁是从加拿大进的苏格兰场，他父亲在那边当过监狱典狱长。这位典狱长看罪犯看不住，看孩子也看不住。迪克在监狱里跑进跑出随意得很，还没弄明白代数的奥秘，就已经能从人家的领巾上摘下饰针了。彼得·杜布瓦（Peter du Bois），一个无期徒刑犯，教会他用一根弯发卡打开几乎任何一种门；卢·安德列夫斯基（Lew Andrevski），斯图尔特堡（Fort Stuart）的常客，用教堂祈祷书的封面做了一副特别小的纸牌，好教这孩子学会在每只小手心里各藏三张牌。若不是天性正直，这番调教十有八九会毁了他。

===Original===
“Dicky’s all right﻿—he can’t know too much of that crook stuff,” said the indolent Captain Martin, when his horrified relatives expostulated at the corruption of the motherless boy. “The boy’s like him﻿—he’s going into the police and the education’s worth a million!”

===Chinese===
“迪基（Dicky）没事的——那些歪门邪道，他懂得再多也不碍事。”懒散的马丁上尉（Captain Martin）说。那时，吓坏了的亲戚们纷纷抗议，说这个没了娘的孩子要被教坏了。“这孩子随他——将来是要进警察界的，这门教育值一百万！”

===Original===
Straight of body, clear-eyed, immensely sane, Dick Martin came happily through a unique period of test to the office. The war brought him to England, a stripling with a record of good work behind him. Scotland Yard claimed him, and he had the distinction of being the only member of the Criminal Investigation Department who had been appointed without going through a probationary period of patrol work.

===Chinese===
迪克·马丁身板挺直、目光清明、头脑无比健全，顺顺当当地通过了一段独一无二的考验期，走马上任。大战把他带到英国，虽是少年郎，身后已有一串出色的业绩。苏格兰场把他要了去，他还享有一项殊荣：刑事调查部（Criminal Investigation Department）里，唯有他是没经过巡逻见习期就直接获任的成员。

===Original===
As he went down the stone stairs, he was overtaken by the third commissioner.
“Hello, Martin! You’re leaving us tomorrow? Bad luck! It is a thousand pities you have money. We’re losing a good man. What are you going to do?”

===Chinese===
他走下石头楼梯时，第三总监（third commissioner）从后面赶了上来。
“你好啊，马丁！明天就要离开我们了？真不走运！你偏偏有钱，万分可惜。我们损失了一员好手。你打算去做什么？”

===Original===
Dick smiled ruefully.
“I don’t know﻿—I’m beginning to think I’ve made a mistake in leaving at all.”

===Chinese===
迪克苦笑了一下。
“不知道——我已经开始觉得，辞职这事儿本来就是走错了棋。”

===Original===
The “old man” nodded.
“Do anything except lecture,” he said, “and, for the Lord’s sake, don’t start a private agency! In America detective agencies do wonderful things﻿—in England their work is restricted to thinking up evidence for divorces. A man asked me only today if I could recommend﻿—”

===Chinese===
“老头子”点了点头。
“干什么都行，就是别去讲课。”他说，“千万看在老天的份上，别开私家侦探社！在美国，侦探公司能干出惊天动地的大事——在英国，它们的业务只限于给离婚案编造证据。就在今天，还有个人问我能不能推荐——”

===Original===
He stopped suddenly at the foot of the stairs and viewed Dick with a new interest.
“By Jove! I wonder﻿—! Do you know Havelock, the lawyer?”

===Chinese===
他在楼梯脚下突然停住，带着新的兴味打量起迪克来。
“哎呀！我琢磨——！你认识律师哈夫洛克（Havelock）吗？”

===Original===
Dick shook his head.
“He’s a pretty good man. His office is somewhere in Lincoln’s Inn Fields. You’ll find its exact position in the telephone directory. I met him at lunch and he asked me﻿—”

===Chinese===
迪克摇了摇头。
“他这人相当不错。他的事务所在林肯律师学院广场（Lincoln’s Inn Fields）一带。确切位置你在电话簿里查得到。我午饭时碰到他，他问我——”

===Original===
He paused, examining the younger man with a speculative eye.
“You’re the very man﻿—it is curious I did not think of you. He asked me if I could find him a reliable private detective, and I told him that such things did not exist outside the pages of fiction.”
