## XI / 第十一章

===Original===

Several days after Norman of Torn’s visit to the castle of Leicester, a young knight appeared before the Earl’s gates demanding admittance to have speech with Simon de Montfort. The Earl received him, and as the young man entered his presence, Simon de Montfort sprang to his feet in astonishment.

“My Lord Prince,” he cried. “What do ye here, and alone?”

The young man smiled.

“I be no prince, My Lord,” he said, “though some have said that I favor the King’s son. I be Roger de Condé, whom it may have pleased your gracious daughter to mention. I have come to pay homage to Bertrade de Montfort.”

===Chinese===

托恩的诺曼造访莱斯特城堡数日之后，一名年轻骑士来到伯爵府门前，请求入内与西蒙·德·孟福尔一叙。伯爵接见了他；年轻人进得屋来行至他面前，西蒙·德·孟福尔惊得霍地站起了身。

「王子殿下，」他失声道，「您怎么会在此地？还是只身一人？」

年轻人微微一笑。

「我不是王子，大人，」他说，「虽说有人讲过我生得像国王的儿子。我是罗杰·德·孔代——或许您那位贤淑的女儿曾赏脸提起过这个名字。我此来，是要拜望贝翠德·德·孟福尔。」

===Original===

“Ah,” said De Montfort, rising to greet the young knight cordially, “an you be that Roger de Condé who rescued my daughter from the fellows of Peter of Colfax, the arms of the De Montforts are open to you.

“Bertrade has had your name upon her tongue many times since her return. She will be glad indeed to receive you, as is her father. She has told us of your valiant espousal of her cause, and the thanks of her brothers and mother await you, Roger de Condé.

“She also told us of your strange likeness to Prince Edward, but until I saw you, I could not believe two men could be born of different mothers and yet be so identical. Come, we will seek out my daughter and her mother.”

===Chinese===

「啊，」德·孟福尔起身，热络地迎向年轻骑士，「你若正是那位从科尔法克斯的彼得一伙手里救出我女儿的罗杰·德·孔代，德·孟福尔家的双臂便向你敞开。

「贝翠德自打回来，你的名字不知上了她多少回舌尖。她必定欢喜接待你，她父亲也一样。你挺身仗义、勇护她的义举，她都讲给我们听了；她兄弟们和她母亲的谢意，正候着向你致意呢，罗杰·德·孔代。

「她还说起你同爱德华王子生得何等离奇的相像；可在亲眼见你之前，我总不信两个母亲生下的两个人，竟能相像到这般一模一样的地步。来，我们这就去找我女儿和她的母亲。」

===Original===

De Montfort led the young man to a small chamber where they were greeted by Princess Eleanor, his wife, and by Bertrade de Montfort. The girl was frankly glad to see him once more and laughingly chide him because he had allowed another to usurp his prerogative and rescue her from Peter of Colfax.

“And to think,” she cried, “that it should have been Norman of Torn who fulfilled your duties for you. But he did not capture Sir Peter’s head, my friend; that is still at large to be brought to me upon a golden dish.”

“I have not forgotten, Lady Bertrade,” said Roger de Condé. “Peter of Colfax will return.”

===Chinese===

德·孟福尔领着年轻人来到一间小屋，他的妻子埃莉诺公主与贝翠德·德·孟福尔在那里迎候。姑娘再次见到他，那份欢喜毫不掩饰，又笑着嗔怪他，竟由得旁人僭了他分内的职分，从科尔法克斯的彼得手里把她救了出去。

「想想看，」她嚷道，「替你尽这份职责的，竟会是托恩的诺曼。可他没能取下彼得爵士的头颅，我的朋友；那颗头还逍遥在外，等着用金盘子盛了给我送来呢。」

「我没有忘，贝翠德小姐，」罗杰·德·孔代说，「科尔法克斯的彼得会回来的。」

===Original===

The girl glanced at him quickly.

“The very words of the Outlaw of Torn,” she said. “How many men be ye, Roger de Condé? With raised visor, you could pass in the King’s court for the King’s son; and in manner, and form, and swordsmanship, and your visor lowered, you might easily be hanged for Norman of Torn.”

“And which would it please ye most that I be?” he laughed.

“Neither,” she answered, “I be satisfied with my friend, Roger de Condé.”

“So ye like not the Devil of Torn?” he asked.

===Chinese===

姑娘飞快地瞥了他一眼。

「这可正是托恩的亡命之徒的原话，」她说，「你到底是一个人还是几个人，罗杰·德·孔代？掀起面罩，你在国王的宫廷里尽可冒充国王的儿子；论做派、身形、剑术，再压下面罩，人家把你错当成托恩的诺曼送去绞死，也是轻而易举的事。」

「那我做哪一个，最讨你的欢心呢？」他朗声笑道。

「哪个都不做，」她答道，「有我的朋友罗杰·德·孔代，我便心满意足了。」

「这么说，你不喜欢托恩的魔鬼？」他问。

===Original===

“He has done me a great service, and I be under monstrous obligations to him, but he be, nathless, the Outlaw of Torn and I the daughter of an earl and a king’s sister.”

“A most unbridgeable gulf indeed,” commented Roger de Condé, drily. “Not even gratitude could lead a king’s niece to receive Norman of Torn on a footing of equality.”

“He has my friendship, always,” said the girl, “but I doubt me if Norman of Torn be the man to impose upon it.”

“One can never tell,” said Roger de Condé, “what manner of fool a man may be. When a man’s head be filled with a pretty face, what room be there for reason?”

===Chinese===

「他于我有大恩，我欠他的情分重得很；可他终归是托恩的亡命之徒，而我，是伯爵的女儿、国王妹妹的女儿。」

「好一条万难逾越的鸿沟，」罗杰·德·孔代干巴巴地评了一句，「便是感恩戴德，也断引不来国王的侄女，以平等之礼接待托恩的诺曼。」

「我的友谊永远是他的，」姑娘说，「只是我拿不准，托恩的诺曼究竟是不是那等仗着友谊便得寸进尺的人。」

「谁也说不准，」罗杰·德·孔代道，「一个男人会当哪一号傻子。一颗脑袋里既塞满了一张俏脸，哪里还腾得出地方盛理智？」

===Original===

“Soon thou wilt be a courtier, if thou keep long at this turning of pretty compliments,” said the girl coldly; “and I like not courtiers, nor their empty, hypocritical chatter.”

The man laughed.

“If I turned a compliment, I did not know it,” he said. “What I think, I say. It may not be a courtly speech or it may. I know nothing of courts and care less, but be it man or maid to whom I speak, I say what is in my mind or I say nothing. I did not, in so many words, say that you are beautiful, but I think it nevertheless, and ye cannot be angry with my poor eyes if they deceive me into believing that no fairer woman breathes the air of England. Nor can you chide my sinful brain that it gladly believes what mine eyes tell it. No, you may not be angry so long as I do not tell you all this.”

Bertrade de Montfort did not know how to answer so ridiculous a sophistry; and, truth to tell, she was more than pleased to hear from the lips of Roger de Condé what bored her on the tongues of other men.

===Chinese===

「你这套漂亮恭维话再磨下去，眼看就要磨成个廷臣了，」姑娘冷冷地说，「我可不喜欢廷臣，也不喜欢他们那套空洞虚伪的嚼舌。」

男人笑了。

「我若真转了一句恭维话，那也是无心，」他说，「我心里想什么，嘴上便说什么。这话或许算不得宫廷辞令，或许也算得。宫廷的事我一无所知，更懒得理会；可不论同我说话的是男是女，我要么说出心里所想，要么索性不开口。我并没有一字一句说你美丽，可我心里确是这么想的；我这双可怜的眼睛若骗了我，教我信了英格兰的空气里再没有哪个女人生得比你更美，你总不能为这个生它们的气。我这颗作孽的头脑乐得相信眼睛告诉它的一切，你也怪它不得。不——只要我不把这番话明明白白说与你听，你便没有道理动怒。」

这般荒唐的诡辩，贝翠德·德·孟福尔不知如何对答才好；而说句实话，同样的话换了别的男人嘴上说出来只叫她厌烦，如今打罗杰·德·孔代唇间吐出，她非但不厌，反倒欢喜得很。

===Original===

De Condé was the guest of the Earl of Leicester for several days, and before his visit was terminated, the young man had so won his way into the good graces of the family that they were loath to see him leave.

Although denied the society of such as these throughout his entire life, yet it seemed that he fell as naturally into the ways of their kind as though he had always been among them. His starved soul, groping through the darkness of the empty past, yearned toward the feasting and the light of friendship, and urged him to turn his back upon the old life, and remain ever with these people, for Simon de Montfort had offered the young man a position of trust and honor in his retinue.

“Why refused you the offer of my father?” said Bertrade to him as he was come to bid her farewell. “Simon de Montfort is as great a man in England as the King himself, and your future were assured did you attach yourself to his person. But what am I saying! Did Roger de Condé not wish to be elsewhere, he had accepted and, as he did not accept, it is proof positive that he does not wish to bide among the De Montforts.”

“I would give my soul to the devil,” said Norman of Torn, “would it buy me the right to remain ever at the feet of Bertrade Montfort.”

===Chinese===

德·孔代在莱斯特伯爵府上做客数日；做客将尽之时，这年轻人已然讨得全家人欢心，一家人都舍不得他走了。

虽说有生以来，他从无缘与这样的人家相交，可他落入这一家人的门风做派里，竟自然得仿佛自幼便长在他们中间。他那饥渴的灵魂在空茫往昔的黑暗中摸索，渴慕着友谊的盛宴与光亮，敦促他背弃旧日的生活，永远留在这些人身边——因为西蒙·德·孟福尔已许这年轻人在自己麾下任一个受信任而又尊荣的职位。

「你为什么回绝了我父亲的抬举？」他前来辞行时，贝翠德对他这样说。「西蒙·德·孟福尔在英格兰是同国王本人一样了不起的大人物，你若依附于他，前程便稳稳有了保障。可瞧我都在说些什么！罗杰·德·孔代若不是另有想去的地方，早便应下了；他既没有应下，便是铁证——他是不愿在德·孟福尔家人中间久留的。」

「只消能买下永远留在贝翠德·孟福尔脚边的权利，」托恩的诺曼说，「我情愿把灵魂卖给魔鬼。」

===Original===

He raised her hand to his lips in farewell as he started to speak, but something﻿—was it an almost imperceptible pressure of her little fingers, a quickening of her breath or a swaying of her body toward him?﻿—caused him to pause and raise his eyes to hers.

For an instant they stood thus, the eyes of the man sinking deep into the eyes of the maid, and then hers closed and with a little sigh that was half gasp, she swayed toward him, and the Devil of Torn folded the King’s niece in his mighty arms and his lips placed the seal of a great love upon those that were upturned to him.

The touch of those pure lips brought the man to himself.

“Ah, Bertrade, my Bertrade,” he cried, “what is this thing that I have done! Forgive me, and let the greatness and the purity of my love for you plead in extenuation of my act.”

他正要开口，先捧起她的手送到唇边作别；可有一点什么——是她纤纤十指几不可察的一按，是她呼吸骤然的一促，还是她身子朝他微微一倾？——叫他顿住，抬眼望进她的眼睛。

“Take this, Roger de Condé,” she whispered, dropping a tiny parcel to him, “and wear it ever, for my sake. We may never meet again, for the Earl my father, is a mighty man, not easily turned from his decisions; therefore I shall say to you, Roger de Condé, what you forbid my saying. I love you, and be ye prince or scullion, you may have me, if you can find the means to take me.”

“Wait, my lady, until I return, then shall you decide, and if ye be of the same mind as today, never fear but that I shall take ye. Again, farewell.” And with a brave smile that hid a sad heart, Norman of Torn passed out of the castle yard.

When he undid the parcel which Bertrade had tossed to him, he found that it contained a beautifully wrought ring set with a single opal.

The Outlaw of Torn raised the little circlet to his lips, and then slipped it upon the third finger of his left hand.

他行经城堡墙面一扇窗户底下时，上头一个声音唤他；他勒住坐骑，抬头望进贝翠德·德·孟福尔的眼睛。

「接住这个，罗杰·德·孔代，」她轻声道，把一个小小的包裹丢了下来，「看在我的份上，永远戴着它。你我或许再无相见之日，因为我父亲伯爵是个权势赫赫的人物，拿定了主意便轻易不肯更改；所以，罗杰·德·孔代，你不许我说的话，我偏要现在说给你听。我爱你——不论你是王子还是灶下打杂的粗役，只要你想得出法子来取我，你便可以得到我。」

「等着，小姐，等我回来，到那时你再来定夺；你若还是今天这个心思，放心，我必来带你走。再会了。」脸上挂起一抹掩住愁怀的英气笑容，托恩的诺曼出了城堡的院子。

待他解开贝翠德抛给他的包裹，发现里面是一枚做工精美的戒指，镶着独粒蛋白石。

托恩的亡命之徒把那小小的指环举到唇边一吻，随后套上了左手无名指。
