## Foreword / 序
===Original===

To A. F., Esq.

I was much surprised, sir, when (some time ago) you so importunately desired my thoughts upon these questions:

-

Is there really any such thing as *natural religion*, properly and truly so called?

-

If there is, what is it?

-

How may a man qualify himself, so as to be able to judge for himself of the other religions professed in the world, to settle his own opinions in disputable matters, and then to enjoy tranquility of mind, neither disturbing others, nor being disturbed at what passes among them?

With what view you did this﻿—whether in expectation of some little degree of satisfaction, or merely to try my abilities, or (which I rather think) out of kindness to amuse me at a time when I wanted something to divert melancholy reflections﻿—I shall not venture to guess. I shall only say that, could I have foreseen in due time that such a task was to be imposed upon me, I might have been better prepared for it. I might have marked what was suitable to my purpose in those books which I have read but shall scarce ever return to read any more; many more I might have read too, which, not wanting them for my own conviction, I have neglected, and now have neither leisure nor patience to peruse. I might have noted what the various occurrences and cases that happen in life suggested, and, in general, I might have placed more of my time on such parts of learning as would have been directly serviceable to me on the present occasion.

However, as I have not spent my days without thinking and reflecting seriously within myself upon the articles and duties of natural religion, and they are *my thoughts* which you require, I have attempted, by recollecting old meditations, and consulting a few scattered papers in which I had formerly, for my own use, set down some of them (briefly, and almost solecistically), to give an answer to the two first of your questions, together: though I must own, not without trouble in adjusting and compacting loose sentiments, filling up vacuities, and bringing the chaos into the shape of something like a system.

致 A. F. 先生

先生，前些时候您如此恳切地要求我就这些问题谈谈想法，我当时颇为惊讶：

-

是否真有严格而名副其实的*自然宗教*这回事？

-

倘若有，它是什么？

-

人如何使自己有资格独立评判世上所信奉的其他宗教，在有争议的事情上确立自己的见解，进而享受心灵的安宁——既不打扰他人，也不因他们中间发生的事而心绪不宁？

您这样做是出于什么用意——是期待得到些许满足，还是只想试试我的能力，或是（我更愿意这样想）出于好意，想在我正需要排遣忧郁思绪的时候给我一点消遣——我不敢妄加猜测。我只想说，倘若我能及时预见这样一桩任务会落在我头上，我或许能作更好的准备。我本可以在我读过而几乎不会再读的那些书里，标出合于我目的的东西；还可以再读许多书——当初不须借它们坚定己见，便怠慢了，如今既无闲暇也无耐心去翻阅。我本可以记下人生中种种际遇与事例所提示的东西；而且总的说，我本可以把更多时间用在那些对本事直接有用的学问上。

然而，我度过的这些日子并非没有认真地在心里思索过自然宗教的条款与本分；而您要的又是*我的思想*，于是我便尝试着，追忆旧的沉思，并翻检几篇散乱的旧稿——其中一些我曾为自己的用处简要地、几乎文理不通地记下——把您前两个问题合并作一回答；尽管我必须承认，把散漫的见解加以调整与凝聚、填补空缺、把混沌理出几分系统的模样，颇费了一番周折。

Notwithstanding what I have said, in a treatise of natural religion, a subject so beaten and exhausted in all its parts by all degrees of writers, in which many notions will inevitably occur that are no one’s property, and so many things require to be proved which can scarce be proved by any other but the old arguments (or not so well), you must not expect to find *much* that is new. Yet *something* perhaps you may. That which is advanced in the following papers, concerning the nature of moral good and evil, and is the prevailing thought that runs through them all, I never met with anywhere. And even as to those matters in which I have been prevented by others, and which perhaps may be common, you have them, not as I took them from anybody, but as they used to appear to me in my walks and solitudes. So that they are indeed *my thoughts*, such as have been *long mine*, which I send you; without any regard to what others have or have not said: as I persuade myself you will easily perceive. It is not hard to discern whether a work of this kind be all of a piece, and to distinguish the genuine hand of an author from the false wares and patchwork of a plagiary. Though after all, it would be madness in a man to go out of his right way, only because it has been frequented by others, or perhaps is the high road.

Sensible how unfinished this performance is, I call it only a delineation, or rude draft. Where I am defective, or trip, I hope you will excuse a friend who has now passed the threshold of old age, and is, upon that and other accounts, not able to bear much study or application. And thus I commit to your candor what follows: which, for the sake of order and perspicuity, I have divided into sections and propositions.

尽管我说了这些，但在一篇论自然宗教的著作里——这个题目已被各式各样的作者在其各部分反复耕耘、搜刮殆尽，其中必然会出现许多并非谁家独有的观念，也有许多事情需要证明，而能证明它们的，除旧论证外几乎没有别的（或不及旧论证）——您不可指望找到*许多*新东西。然而或许*有一些*。以下各篇中关于道德善恶之本性的论述，也就是贯穿全部篇幅的那条主导思想，我在别处从未见过。即便在我已被他人先着、或许已属常见的那些问题上，您所读到的，也不是我从什么人那里取来的，而是它们在我散步与独处时惯常呈现于我心中的样子。所以，我寄给您的确实*是我的思想*，*久已属于我*的思想，并不顾他人说过或没说过什么；我相信您会轻易看出这一点。辨明这样一部作品是否浑然一体、把作者的真笔从剽窃者的假货与拼缀中分别出来，并不难。不过说到底，倘若一个人只因自己走的正路已被他人踏惯、或者竟是通衢大道，便偏要离开它，那真是疯狂了。

自知这份作品何等未完成，我只称它为一篇概略，或粗陋的草稿。在我有欠缺或失足之处，望您宽宥一位已跨过老年门槛的朋友——正因这个以及其他缘故，他已承受不了多少研读与劳心了。这样，我把以下的文字信托给您的宽厚：为求条理与明晰，我把它分成了节与命题。
