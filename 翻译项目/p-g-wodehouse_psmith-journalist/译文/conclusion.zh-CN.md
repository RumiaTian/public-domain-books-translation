## Conclusion / 尾声
===Original===
It was a drizzly November evening. The streets of Cambridge were a compound of mud, mist, and melancholy. But in Psmith’s rooms the fire burned brightly, the kettle droned, and all, as the proprietor had just observed, was joy, jollity, and song. Psmith, in pyjamas and a college blazer, was lying on the sofa. Mike, who had been playing football, was reclining in a comatose state in an armchair by the fire.

“How pleasant it would be,” said Psmith dreamily, “if all our friends on the other side of the Atlantic could share this very peaceful moment with us! Or perhaps not quite all. Let us say, Comrade Windsor in the chair over there, Comrades Brady and Maloney on the table, and our old pal Wilberfloss sharing the floor with B. Henderson Asher, Bat Jarvis, and the cats. By the way, I think it would be a graceful act if you were to write to Comrade Jarvis from time to time telling him how your Angoras are getting on. He regards you as the World’s Most Prominent Citizen. A line from you every now and then would sweeten the lad’s existence.”

===Chinese===
那是一个细雨蒙蒙的十一月黄昏。剑桥的街道是泥浆、雾气与愁闷的混合物。可是在普史密斯的房间里，炉火烧得正旺，水壶嗡嗡低鸣，而且，正如房主刚刚所说的那样，一切尽是欢乐、快活与歌唱。普史密斯穿着睡衣裤、罩学院运动上衣，躺在沙发上。踢完足球的迈克，正瘫在炉边扶手椅里，处于昏迷状态。

「那该多么惬意啊，」普史密斯悠悠地说，「倘若大西洋彼岸咱们所有的朋友，都能与咱们共享这极为安宁的一刻！或者也不必是全部。就这么说吧：温莎同志坐那边那把椅子，布雷迪同志和马洛尼同志上桌子，咱们的老伙计威尔伯弗洛斯，同 B·亨德森·阿舍、巴特·贾维斯和众猫一起分地板。对了，我想你要是时不时写封信给贾维斯同志，告诉他你的安哥拉猫们近况如何，那将是一桩风雅之举。他把你视为全世界最显赫的公民。你偶尔来上几行，会让那小伙子的人生变甜。」

===Original===
Mike stirred sleepily in his chair.

“What?” he said drowsily.

“Never mind, Comrade Jackson. Let us pass lightly on. I am filled with a strange content tonight. I may be wrong, but it seems to me that all is singularly to de good, as Comrade Maloney would put it. Advices from Comrade Windsor inform me that that prince of blighters, Waring, was rejected by an intelligent electorate. Those keen, clear-sighted citizens refused to vote for him to an extent that you could notice without a microscope. Still, he has one consolation. He owns what, when the improvements are completed, will be the finest and most commodious tenement houses in New York. Millionaires will stop at them instead of going to the Plaza. Are you asleep, Comrade Jackson?”

===Chinese===
迈克在椅子里睡意朦胧地动了动。

「什么？」他迷迷糊糊地说。

「不碍事，杰克逊同志。咱们轻轻翻过这一页。今晚我心里充满一种奇异的满足。我也许错了，不过依我看，一切都好得妥妥的——马洛尼同志会这么说。温莎同志的通报告诉我：活宝王子沃林，已被一群有头脑的选民断然拒绝。那些敏锐、目光清澈的公民不投他的票，投到了不用显微镜也看得出的程度。不过，他也有一桩安慰：等改造完工，他将拥有纽约最精美、最宽敞的廉租公寓。百万富翁们将不去广场饭店，改到他家下榻。你睡着了吗，杰克逊同志？」

===Original===
“Um—m,” said Mike.

“That is excellent. You could not be better employed. Keep listening. Comrade Windsor also stated—as indeed did the sporting papers—that Comrade Brady put it all over friend Eddie Wood, administering the sleep-producer in the eighth round. My authorities are silent as to whether or not the lethal blow was a half-scissor hook, but I presume such to have been the case. The Kid is now definitely matched against Comrade Garvin for the championship, and the experts seem to think that he should win. He is a stout fellow, is Comrade Brady, and I hope he wins through. He will probably come to England later on. When he does, we must show him round. I don’t think you ever met him, did you, Comrade Jackson?”

===Chinese===
「唔——嗯。」迈克说。

「好极了。你再没有比这更有出息的用法了。接着听。温莎同志还说——体育报纸上也登了——布雷迪同志把埃迪·伍德（Eddie Wood）那朋友全面料理了，第八回合就给灌下了催眠丸。至于那记致命一击是不是一记半剪式勾拳，我的情报来源们保持沉默，但我推定正是如此。小子如今正式敲定与加文同志对阵争冠军，专家们似乎认为他该赢。布雷迪同志是条硬汉，我盼着他一路赢下去。他晚些时候多半会来英国。他真来的时候，咱们得领他四处转转。我想你一直没见过他，对吧，杰克逊同志？」

===Original===
“Ur—r,” said Mike.

“Say no more,” said Psmith. “I take you.”

He reached out for a cigarette.

“These,” he said, comfortably, “are the moments in life to which we look back with that wistful pleasure. What of my boyhood at Eton? Do I remember with the keenest joy the brain-tourneys in the old form-room, and the bally rot which used to take place on the Fourth of June? No. Burned deeply into my memory is a certain hot bath I took after one of the foulest cross-country runs that ever occurred outside Dante’s Inferno. So with the present moment. This peaceful scene, Comrade Jackson, will remain with me when I have forgotten that such a person as Comrade Repetto ever existed. These are the real *Cosy Moments*. And while on that subject you will be glad to hear that the little sheet is going strong. The man Wilberfloss is a marvel in his way. He appears to have gathered in the majority of the old subscribers again. Hopping mad but a brief while ago, they now eat out of his hand. You’ve really no notion what a feeling of quiet pride it gives you owning a paper. I try not to show it, but I seem to myself to be looking down on the world from some lofty peak. Yesterday night, when I was looking down from the peak without a cap and gown, a proctor slid up. Today I had to dig down into my jeans for a matter of two plunks. But what of it? Life must inevitably be dotted with these minor tragedies. I do not repine. The whisper goes round, ‘Psmith bites the bullet, and wears a brave smile.’ Comrade Jackson—”

===Chinese===
「呃——啊。」迈克说。

「不必多言，」普史密斯说，「我懂你。」

他伸手够了一支烟。

「这样的时刻，」他舒舒服服地说，「正是我们日后带着怅然的欢愉回首的时刻。我在伊顿（Eton）的少年时代呢？我最欢快地记得的，是旧教室里的脑力比武、还有六月四日那天的种种胡闹吗？不。深深烙进我记忆的，是在一场越野跑之后洗的那个热水澡——那场越野跑之惨烈，但丁《地狱篇》（Dante’s Inferno）以外无出其右。眼下这一刻也是如此。这幅安宁的图景，杰克逊同志，将伴我长存，即使我忘了世上还有雷佩托同志这么一号人。这些才是真正的《舒适时光》。顺便说个相关的喜讯：这份小刊如今势头正劲。威尔伯弗洛斯这个人，自有他的一手奇迹。他似乎又把大多数老订户笼络回来了。前不久还气得直蹦的他们，如今对他言听计从。你可真想象不到，拥有一份报纸会给你一种怎样的、悄然的骄傲。我尽量不露出来，可我总觉得自己正从某座高峰上俯瞰世界。昨天夜里，我没戴方帽、没穿学袍就从峰顶俯瞰，一位学监悄悄滑了过来。今天我只得掏腰包，掏出了两块大洋。可那又如何？人生注定要点点滴滴洒满这类小型悲剧。我不怨叹。低语四处流传：『普史密斯咬紧牙关，面带勇敢的微笑。』杰克逊同志——」

===Original===
A snore came from the chair.

Psmith sighed. But he did not repine. He bit the bullet. His eyes closed.

Five minutes later a slight snore came from the sofa, too. The man behind *Cosy Moments* slept.

===Chinese===
椅子里传来鼾声。

普史密斯叹了口气。但他不怨叹。他咬紧了牙关。他的眼睛合上了。

五分钟后，沙发上也传来了轻微的鼾声。《舒适时光》背后的男人睡着了。
