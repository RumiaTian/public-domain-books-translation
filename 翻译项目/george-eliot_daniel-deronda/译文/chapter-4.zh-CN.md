### IV / 第四章
===Original===

It would be a little hard to blame the rector of Pennicote that in the course of looking at things from every point of view, he looked at Gwendolen as a girl likely to make a brilliant marriage. Why should he be expected to differ from his contemporaries in this matter, and wish his niece a worse end of her charming maidenhood than they would approve as the best possible? It is rather to be set down to his credit that his feelings on the subject were entirely good-natured. And in considering the relation of means to ends, it would have been mere folly to have been guided by the exceptional and idyllic﻿—to have recommended that Gwendolen should wear a gown as shabby as Griselda’s in order that a marquis might fall in love with her, or to have insisted that since a fair maiden was to be sought, she should keep herself out of the way. Mr. Gascoigne’s calculations were of the kind called rational, and he did not even think of getting a too frisky horse in order that Gwendolen might be threatened with an accident and be rescued by a man of property. He wished his niece well, and he meant her to be seen to advantage in the best society of the neighborhood.
Her uncle’s intention fell in perfectly with Gwendolen’s own wishes. But let no one suppose that she also contemplated a brilliant marriage as the direct end of her witching the world with her grace on horseback, or with any other accomplishment. That she was to be married some time or other she would have felt obliged to admit; and that her marriage would not be of a middling kind, such as most girls were contented with, she felt quietly, unargumentatively sure. But her thoughts never dwelt on marriage as the fulfillment of her ambition; the dramas in which she imagined herself a heroine were not wrought up to that close. To be very much sued or hopelessly sighed for as a bride was indeed an indispensable and agreeable guarantee of womanly power; but to become a wife and wear all the domestic fetters of that condition, was on the whole a vexatious necessity. Her observation of matrimony had inclined her to think it rather a dreary state in which a woman could not do what she liked, had more children than were desirable, was consequently dull, and became irrevocably immersed in humdrum.

要怪佩尼科特的教区长从各个角度看待事物时，也把格温多琳看作一个可能结下一门显赫姻缘的姑娘，未免有点苛求。在这件事上，为什么非得指望他不同于同时代的人，盼着自己的侄女以比他们认可的最佳结局更糟的方式，来结束她可爱的少女时代呢？倒不如说，他在这件事上的感情全是善意的，这该记在他的功劳簿上。而且在权衡手段与目的的关系时，若以例外的田园诗式的情形为准绳，那纯粹是愚蠢——譬如建议格温多琳穿一件像格里泽尔达那样寒酸的袍子，好让一位侯爵爱上她；或者坚持说，既然是要寻访佳人，她就该深藏不露。加斯科因先生（Mr. Gascoigne）的盘算属于所谓理性的那一类，他甚至没有想过要弄一匹过分烈性的马，好让格温多琳遭遇险情、被一位有产绅士所搭救。他希望侄女过得好，而且打算让她在本地最好的社交圈里尽显风采。

舅父的这一意图与格温多琳自己的心愿一拍即合。但别以为她把一门显赫的婚事当作自己施展魅力迷倒世人的直接目的——无论凭马上的风姿还是别的什么本领。有朝一日总得嫁人，这一点她不得不承认；而她的婚姻绝不会是大多数姑娘便心满意足的那种不上不下的婚姻，对此她安安静静、不加论证地深信不疑。但她的思绪从不把婚姻当作抱负的顶点来萦怀；她想象自己充当女主角的那些戏剧，并没有以终成眷属作结。作为新娘被人苦苦追求或无可挽回地叹息，确实是女人魅力不可或缺又令人愉快的保证；但去为人妻，戴上那种身份的一切家庭枷锁，总的说来是一种恼人的必要。她对婚姻生活的观察使她倾向于认为，那是一种相当沉闷的状态：女人不能随心所欲，孩子生得比想要的更多，于是变得迟钝，最终不可挽回地沉溺于平淡无奇之中。

Of course marriage was social promotion; she could not look forward to a single life; but promotions have sometimes to be taken with bitter herbs﻿—a peerage will not quite do instead of leadership to the man who meant to lead; and this delicate-limbed sylph of twenty meant to lead. For such passions dwell in feminine breasts also. In Gwendolen’s, however, they dwelt among strictly feminine furniture, and had no disturbing reference to the advancement of learning or the balance of the constitution; her knowledge being such as with no sort of standing-room or length of lever could have been expected to move the world. She meant to do what was pleasant to herself in a striking manner; or rather, whatever she could do so as to strike others with admiration and get in that reflected way a more ardent sense of living, seemed pleasant to her fancy.
“Gwendolen will not rest without having the world at her feet,” said Miss Merry, the meek governess: hyperbolical words which have long come to carry the most moderate meanings; for who has not heard of private persons having the world at their feet in the shape of some half-dozen items of flattering regard generally known in a genteel suburb? And words could hardly be too wide or vague to indicate the prospect that made a hazy largeness about poor Gwendolen on the heights of her young self-exultation. Other people allowed themselves to be made slaves of, and to have their lives blown hither and thither like empty ships in which no will was present. It was not to be so with her; she would no longer be sacrificed to creatures worth less than herself, but would make the very best of the chances that life offered her, and conquer circumstances by her exceptional cleverness. Certainly, to be settled at Offendene, with the notice of Lady Brackenshaw, the archery club, and invitations to dine with the Arrowpoints, as the highest lights in her scenery, was not a position that seemed to offer remarkable chances; but Gwendolen’s confidence lay chiefly in herself. She felt well equipped for the mastery of life. With regard to much in her lot hitherto, she held herself rather hardly dealt with, but as to her “education,” she would have admitted that it had left her under no disadvantages.

婚姻当然是社会性的高升；她无法设想独身的前景；但高升有时不得不伴着苦药咽下——对于一个志在领袖群伦的人，一个贵族头衔并不能替代领袖的地位；而这个二十岁、四肢纤巧的精灵正志在领袖群伦。这样的激情同样栖居于女性的胸中。只不过在格温多琳胸中，它们栖身于严格女性化的陈设之间，不牵扯学问的进步或宪制的平衡这类扰人的参照；她的学识属于那种既无立足点、也无足够长的杠杆、休想撬动世界的一类。她要以引人瞩目的方式做让自己愉快的事；或者不如说，凡她能借以令他人叹服、并从这反照中获得更炽烈的生存感的事，在她看来都是称心的。

「格温多琳不把世界踩在脚下是不会安分的，」温顺的家庭女教师梅丽小姐说；这夸张的字眼，早已只承载最平淡的含义了；谁没听说过私家人物把世界踩在脚下呢——所谓世界，不过是某个体面郊区里人人皆知的那五六项谄媚的关切罢了。而文字几乎再宽泛、再含糊，也不足以形容那笼罩在年轻的自我陶醉高处可怜的格温多琳周身的朦胧浩大。别人甘愿被当作奴隶，任凭生命像没有意志的空船被吹来吹去。她可不这样；她再也不愿被牺牲给那些价值不如自己的人，而要充分利用生活提供的每一次机会，凭她出众的聪明征服环境。当然，定居在奥芬迪恩（Offendene），以布雷肯肖夫人的垂青、射箭俱乐部和阿罗波因特家的晚餐请柬为景致中最亮的光点，这算不上一个似乎大有可为的位置；但格温多琳的信心主要在她自己身上。她觉得自己装备精良，足以驾驭人生。对于命运迄今待她的许多方面，她认为自己受了不小的亏待；至于她的「教养」，她会承认它没有给她留下任何劣势。

In the schoolroom her quick mind had taken readily that strong starch of unexplained rules and disconnected facts which saves ignorance from any painful sense of limpness; and what remained of all things knowable, she was conscious of being sufficiently acquainted with through novels, plays and poems. About her French and music, the two justifying accomplishments of a young lady, she felt no ground for uneasiness; and when to all these qualifications, negative and positive, we add the spontaneous sense of capability some happy persons are born with, so that any subject they turn their attention to impresses them with their own power of forming a correct judgment on it, who can wonder if Gwendolen felt ready to manage her own destiny?
There were many subjects in the world﻿—perhaps the majority﻿—in which she felt no interest, because they were stupid; for subjects are apt to appear stupid to the young as light seems dull to the old; but she would not have felt at all helpless in relation to them if they had turned up in conversation. It must be remembered that no one had disputed her power or her general superiority. As on the arrival at Offendene, so always, the first thought of those about her had been, what will Gwendolen think?﻿—if the footman trod heavily in creaking boots, or if the laundress’s work was unsatisfactory, the maid said, “This will never do for Miss Harleth”; if the wood smoked in the bedroom fireplace, Mrs. Davilow, whose own weak eyes suffered much from this inconvenience, spoke apologetically of it to Gwendolen. If, when they were under the stress of traveling, she did not appear at the breakfast table till everyone else had finished, the only question was, how Gwendolen’s coffee and toast should still be of the hottest and crispest; and when she appeared with her freshly-brushed light-brown hair streaming backward and awaiting her mamma’s hand to coil it up, her large brown eyes glancing bright as a wave-washed onyx from under their long lashes, it was always she herself who had to be tolerant﻿—to beg that Alice who sat waiting on her would not stick up her shoulders in that frightful manner, and that Isabel, instead of pushing up to her and asking questions, would go away to Miss Merry.

在教室里，她敏捷的头脑很容易就吸收了那种强硬的浆糊——不加解释的规则和互不连贯的事实——它使无知免于任何瘫软的痛感；至于一切可知事物中剩下的部分，她自觉通过小说、戏剧和诗歌已相当熟稔。关于她的法语和音乐——一位小姐的两项立身本领——她更觉毫无可虑之处；在这一切消极与积极的资格之上，再加上某些幸运儿与生俱来的那种自发的胜任感——无论把注意力转向什么课题，那课题都令他们深信自己有能力对它作出正确的判断——那么，格温多琳自觉准备就绪、要亲手掌管自己的命运，谁还能感到奇怪呢？

世上有许多事情——也许是大多数——她毫无兴趣，因为它们无聊；课题在年轻人眼里往往显得无聊，正如光线在老人眼里显得暗淡；但倘若这些事情在谈话中冒出来，她绝不至于束手无策。要知道，从来没有人质疑过她的能力或她普遍的优越地位。正如初到奥芬迪恩时一样，一贯地，她身边每一个人的第一个念头都是：格温多琳会怎么看？——若是男仆穿着吱嘎作响的靴子脚步沉重，或是洗衣妇的活计不济，女仆便会说：「这可万万不能让哈利斯小姐看见」；若是卧室壁炉里柴火冒烟，本身那双弱眼深受其苦的戴维洛太太，便会对格温多琳满怀歉意地解释一番。若是旅途劳顿之际，她直到别人都用完餐还不露面，那么唯一的问题便是：怎样才能让格温多琳的咖啡和吐司还是最热最脆的；等她出场了，新刷过的浅棕色头发向后披散着，等着妈妈的手把它盘起来，棕色的大眼睛在长长的睫毛下顾盼生辉，如被浪冲洗过的缟玛瑙——这时总是她本人宽宏大量——恳求侍立的艾丽斯别那样可怕地耸起肩膀，恳求伊莎贝尔别凑上来问东问西，到梅丽小姐那儿去。

Always she was the princess in exile, who in time of famine was to have her breakfast-roll made of the finest-bolted flour from the seven thin ears of wheat, and in a general decampment was to have her silver fork kept out of the baggage. How was this to be accounted for? The answer may seem to lie quite on the surface:﻿—in her beauty, a certain unusualness about her, a decision of will which made itself felt in her graceful movements and clear unhesitating tones, so that if she came into the room on a rainy day when everybody else was flaccid and the use of things in general was not apparent to them, there seemed to be a sudden, sufficient reason for keeping up the forms of life; and even the waiters at hotels showed the more alacrity in doing away with crumbs and creases and dregs with struggling flies in them. This potent charm, added to the fact that she was the eldest daughter, toward whom her mamma had always been in an apologetic state of mind for the evils brought on her by a stepfather, may seem so full a reason for Gwendolen’s domestic empire, that to look for any other would be to ask the reason of daylight when the sun is shining. But beware of arriving at conclusions without comparison. I remember having seen the same assiduous, apologetic attention awarded to persons who were not at all beautiful or unusual, whose firmness showed itself in no very graceful or euphonious way, and who were not eldest daughters with a tender, timid mother, compunctious at having subjected them to inconveniences. Some of them were a very common sort of men. And the only point of resemblance among them all was a strong determination to have what was pleasant, with a total fearlessness in making themselves disagreeable or dangerous when they did not get it. Who is so much cajoled and served with trembling by the weak females of a household as the unscrupulous male﻿—capable, if he has not free way at home, of going and doing worse elsewhere? Hence I am forced to doubt whether even without her potent charm and peculiar filial position Gwendolen might not still have played the queen in exile, if only she had kept her inborn energy of egoistic desire, and her power of inspiring fear as to what she might say or do.

她始终是一位流亡中的公主：饥荒时节，她的早餐卷也必须用七枝瘦麦穗上筛得最细的精粉做成；大撤离的时候，她的银叉也必须从行李里单独留出。这该作何解释呢？答案似乎就浮在表面：——在于她的美貌，她身上某种不同寻常的气韵，一种决断的意志，它在她优雅的举止和清亮果断的语调中让人感觉得到；于是，哪怕在雨天她走进屋来，当时人人蔫头耷脑、看不出事物大体上还有什么用处，她的到来也仿佛突然给了维持生活体面一个充分的理由；连旅馆的侍者们也更加起劲地抹去桌布的碎屑和折痕，撤走浮着垂死苍蝇的残羹。这强有力的魅力，加上她是长女、妈妈对继父带给她种种苦处始终心怀歉疚这一事实，似乎已充分解释了格温多琳的家庭霸权；再要寻找别的原因，无异于太阳高照时还问白昼为何明亮。但且慢下结论，不作比较的结论是靠不住的。我记得也见过同样殷勤、同样歉疚的照料加在一些人身上——他们毫不美丽，毫无异常之处，他们的坚定也并非以优雅悦耳的方式表现出来，他们也不是有着温柔胆怯、愧疚于心的母亲的长女。他们当中有些人是极其普通的男人。而他们之间唯一的共同点，是一种要到手的称心事的强烈决心，以及一旦得不到，便毫无顾忌地让自己变得讨厌或危险的那股狠劲。家里软弱的女眷们，谁像那个不择手段的男人那样被百般哄着、战战兢兢地伺候着呢？——他在家里若不能为所欲为，可是会到别处干出更坏的事来的。因此我不得不怀疑：即便没有那强有力的魅力和特殊的母女地位，只要格温多琳保持着与生俱来的利己欲望的劲头，以及她那种「谁知她会说出什么、做出什么」的令人惧怕的力量，她照样能扮演流亡中的女王。

However, she had the charm, and those who feared her were also fond of her; the fear and the fondness being perhaps both heightened by what may be called the iridescence of her character﻿—the play of various, nay, contrary tendencies. For Macbeth’s rhetoric about the impossibility of being many opposite things in the same moment, referred to the clumsy necessities of action and not to the subtler possibilities of feeling. We cannot speak a loyal word and be meanly silent; we cannot kill and not kill in the same moment; but a moment is wide enough for the loyal and mean desire, for the outlash of a murderous thought and the sharp backward stroke of repentance.
