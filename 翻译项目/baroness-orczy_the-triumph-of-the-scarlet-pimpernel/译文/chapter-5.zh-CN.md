## V. Rascality Rejoices / V. 无赖们的欢庆

### I / I

===Original===
The Fraternal Suppers were a great success. They were the invention of Robespierre, and the unusual warmth of these early spring evenings lent the support of their balmy atmosphere to the scheme.

Whole Paris is out in the streets on these mild April nights. Families out on a holiday, after the daily spectacle of the death-cart taking the enemies of the people, the conspirators against their liberty, to the guillotine.

And *maman* brings a basket filled with whatever scanty provisions she can save from the maximum per day allowed for the provisioning of her family. Beside her, papa comes along, dragging his youngest by the hand﻿—the latter no longer chubby and rosy, as were his prototypes in the days gone by, because food is scarce and dear, and milk unobtainable; but looking a man for all that, though barefooted and bare-kneed, with the red cap upon his lank, unwashed locks, and hugging against his meagre chest a tiny toy guillotine, the latest popular fancy, all complete with miniature knife and pulleys, and frame artistically painted a vivid crimson.

===Chinese===
兄弟晚餐会大获成功。它是罗伯斯庇尔的发明，而今岁早春夜晚少见的和暖，又以温煦宜人的气象，替这项创举帮了腔。

在这四月里温和的夜晚，整个巴黎都涌到了街上。一家家出门过节——白天刚看完每日一度的热闹：死囚车载着人民的敌人、戕害他们自由的阴谋分子，驶向断头台。

妈妈（maman）挎着一只篮子，里面装着按每日最高限价（maximum）配给一家人的口粮中、她能省下来的那一点点可怜吃食。她身边，爸爸一路走来，手里拽着最小的孩子——这孩子早已不像从前的娃娃那样白白胖胖、红扑扑粉嫩嫩了，因为食物又缺又贵，牛奶更是弄不到；可他瞧着到底有股大人模样，虽说光着脚、露着膝盖，稀黄没洗的乱发上扣着红便帽，瘦瘪的胸口还搂着一具小小的玩具断头台——时下最风行的新玩意儿，迷你刀片、滑轮一应俱全，架子还颇具匠心地漆成一片猩红。

===Original===
The Rue St. Honoré is a typical example of what goes on all over the city. Though it is very narrow and therefore peculiarly inconvenient for the holding of outdoor entertainments, the Fraternal Suppers there are extensively patronised, because the street itself is consecrated as holding the house wherein lives Robespierre.

Here, as elsewhere, huge braziers are lit at intervals, so that materfamilias may cook the few herrings she has brought with her if she be so minded, and all down the narrow street tables are set, innocent of cloths or even of that cleanliness which is next to the equally neglected virtue of godliness. But the tables have an air of cheeriness nevertheless, with resin torches, tallow candles, or old stable lanterns set here and there, the flames flickering in the gentle breeze, adding picturesqueness to the scene which might otherwise have seemed sordid, with those pewter mugs and tin plates, the horn-handled knives and iron spoons.

The scanty light does little more than accentuate the darkness around, the deep shadows under projecting balconies or lintels of portes-cochères carefully closed and barred for the night; but it glints with weird willo’-the-wisp-like fitfulness on crimson caps and tricolour cockades, on drawn and begrimed faces, bony arms, or lean, brown hands.

===Chinese===
圣奥诺雷街（Rue St. Honoré）是全城这番景象的一个典型。街道很窄，办露天宴会本该格外不便，可这里的兄弟晚餐会却宾客盈门，因为这条街本身，便因坐落着罗伯斯庇尔的寓所而被奉为了圣地。

这里同别处一样，沿街隔一段便生起大火盆，好让各家主妇高兴时烤一烤随身带来的那几条鲱鱼；窄窄的街道两旁一路摆开桌子，桌上既没有桌布，也谈不上干净——须知「清洁仅次于虔诚」，而虔诚这门德行，如今也同样乏人问津。不过桌子倒自有几分喜气：松脂火把、牛油蜡烛、旧马厩灯，东一盏西一盏地点着，火苗在微风中摇曳，给这幅景象平添了几分画意——否则，那白镴大杯、锡盘、角柄刀、铁汤匙，本要显得寒碜的。

稀疏的灯光照不出什么名堂，反倒把四周的黑夜衬得更深：挑出的阳台底下、那些入夜便仔细关严上闩的车马大门的门楣底下，一片片浓影。但这光也带着鬼火似的明灭，闪闪烁烁，落在红便帽和三色帽徽上，落在一张张枯槁污黑的脸上、一条条嶙峋的胳膊上、一只只干瘦蜡黄的手上。

A motley throng, in truth! The workers of Paris, its proletariat, all conscripted servants of the State﻿—slaves, we might call them, though they deem themselves free men﻿—all driven into hard manual labour, partly by starvation and wholly by the decree of the Committees, who decide how and when and in what form the nation requires the arms or hands﻿—not the brains, mind you!﻿—of its citizens. For brains the nation has no use, only in the heads of those who sit in Convention or on Committees. “The State hath no use for science,” was grimly said to Lavoisier, the great chemist, when he begged for a few days’ surcease from death in order to complete some important experiments.

===Chinese===

===Original===

不过，运煤工倒算国家有用的公民；铁匠、军械匠、造枪匠也一样，还有那些会缝会织袜子的，凡能给国家军队——法兰西神圣国土的保卫者——供衣供食的行当，都有用处。为这些人，为那些工人——诚实的、勤勉的、清醒的——兄弟晚餐会才被发明出来；但也不光是为他们。还有那些「编织妇」（tricotteuses），不男不女的老妖婆，奉国家之命坐在断头台脚下，自家儿女围作一圈，一边织啊，织啊，一边嘲骂——同样奉国家之命——那些走向断头台的死囚：老翁、少妇，连孩童也不放过。还有「公共辱骂妇」（insulteuses publiques），职业辱骂手——这称呼于她们真是抬举——多为女人，受雇在死囚车辚辚驶过时嚎叫咒骂。还有「打手」（tappe-durs），一手灌铅的棍棒，围着罗伯斯庇尔神圣的尊躯组成卫队。再有，革命社（Société Révolutionnaire）的社员，从这座大城的苦难与堕落的渣滓里招募而来；还有——哦，这一切想想都叫人胆寒！——「红色孩童」（Enfants Rouges），一帮红孩子，喊起「处死」和「吊上灯杆！」（à la lanterne）来不输任何好汉——新共和国早熟的小苗苗。为他们，兄弟晚餐会也同样设立：为一切无赖卑贱之徒，为人类渣滓的全数扫拢。因为这些货色也须得有人逗乐取悦，免得他们成堆坐在一处，你一言我一语说动了心，真相信自己比在君主专制的年头更凄惨、更穷困、更下贱。

### II / II

===Original===
And so, on these balmy evenings of mid-April, family parties are gathered in the open air, around meagre suppers that are “fraternal” by order of the State. Family parties which make for camaraderie between the honest man and the thief, the sober citizen and the homeless vagabond, and help one to forget awhile the misery, the starvation, the slavery, the daily struggle for bare existence, in anticipation of the belated Millennium.

There is even laughter around the festive boards, fun and frolic. Jokes are cracked, mostly of a grim order. There is intoxication in the air: spring has got into the heads of the young. And there is even kissing under the shadows, lovemaking, sentiment; and here and there perhaps a shred of real happiness.

The provisions are scanty. Every family brings its own. Two or three herrings, sprinkled with shredded onions and wetted with a little vinegar, or else a few boiled prunes or a pottage of lentils and beans.

===Chinese===

节日的餐桌四周甚至还有笑声，有打趣，有嬉闹。笑话讲了一箩筐，多半阴森森的。空气里浮着一股醉意：春天钻进了年轻人的脑袋。暗影里甚至还有人接吻，谈情，说爱；这里那里，说不定还真有一星半点实实在在的幸福。

His senses reeled, there was a mighty buzzing in his ears, which effectually drowned the cries of execration or of approval that greeted his tirade, as well as a new and deafening tumult which filled the whole narrow street with its weird and hideous sounds.

Whence the blow had come, Bertrand had no notion. It had all been so swift. He had expected to be torn limb from limb, to be dragged to the nearest Commissariat: he courted condemnation, envisaged the guillotine; ’stead of which, he was prosily knocked down by a bow which would have felled an ox.

===Chinese===
他又被打断了，这一回却来得更结实。当头狠狠一击，打得他登时丧失了言语和视觉。他的知觉天旋地转，耳朵里嗡的一声巨响，把那番话招来的咒骂也好、喝彩也好，统统盖了下去，也盖住了另一阵新的、震耳欲聋的骚动——那阵怪诞而狰狞的声响，塞满了整条窄街。

这一拳是打哪儿来的，贝特朗全无概念。一切来得太快。他原想着要被撕成碎块，要被拖到最近的警务分署去：他求的就是被判罪，想的就是断头台；不想人家却平平常常一拳把他撂倒——这一拳，连头公牛也打得翻。

===Original===
Just for a second, his fast-fading perceptions struggled back into consciousness. He had a swift vision of a giant form towering over him, with grimy fist uplifted and toothless mouth grinning hideously, and of the crowd, rising from their seats, turning their backs upon him, waving their arms and caps frantically, and shouting, shouting, with vociferous lustiness. He also had an equally swift pang of remorse as the faces of his companions﻿—of Régine and Mme. de Serval, of Joséphine and Jacques﻿—whom he dragged with him into this mad and purposeless outburst, rose prophetically before him fro out the gloom, with wide-eyed, scared faces and arms uplifted to ward off vengeful blows.

But the next moment these lightning-like visions faded into complete oblivion. He felt something hard and heavy hitting him in the back. All the lights, the faces, the outstretched hands, danced wildly before his eyes, and he sank like a log on the greasy pavement, dragging pewter plates, mugs and bottles down with him in his fall.

===Chinese===
只一秒钟，他迅速消散的知觉又挣扎着回到了清醒。他飞快地瞥见一个巨大的身影耸立在他头顶，扬着污黑的拳头，没牙的嘴狞笑着；又瞥见人群纷纷离座，背转身去对着他，发狂似的挥着手臂和帽子，扯开嗓子喊啊，喊啊，喊得中气十足。他也同样飞快地感到一阵悔恨的刺痛——他的同伴们的脸——雷吉娜和德·塞尔瓦尔夫人（Mme. de Serval），约瑟芬和雅克，那些被他拖进这场疯狂而无谓的发作里的人——一张张从幽暗中预兆似的在他眼前升起：瞪着惊恐的眼睛，高举着手臂，招架着复仇的老拳。

可下一刻，这些闪电似的幻象便消隐于全然的遗忘。他觉出一件又硬又重的东西砸在他背上。所有的灯火、脸庞、伸出的手，都在他眼前狂乱地飞舞，他像根木头似的栽倒在油腻的街石上，白镴盘子、大杯、酒瓶，都叫他一带，跟着他一齐栽了下去。
