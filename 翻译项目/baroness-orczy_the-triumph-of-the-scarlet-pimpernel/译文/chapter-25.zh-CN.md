## XXV. Four Days / XXV. 四天

===Original===

What occurred during the next few seconds Chauvelin himself would have been least able to say. Whether he stepped of his own accord into the antechamber of Catherine Théot’s apartment, or whether an unseen hand pushed him in, he could not have told you. Certain it is that, when he returned to the full realisation of things, he was sitting on one of the benches, his back against the wall, whilst immediately in front of him, looking down on him through half-closed, lazy eyes, débonnair, well groomed, unperturbed, stood his arch-enemy, Sir Percy Blakeney.

The antechamber was gloomy in the extreme. Someone in the interval had lighted the tallow candles in the centre chandelier, and these shed a feeble, flickering light on the dank, bare walls, the carpetless floor, the shuttered windows; whilst a thin spiral of evil-smelling smoke wound its way to the blackened ceiling above.

===Chinese===

接下来那几秒钟里到底发生了什么，只怕最说不清楚的，正是肖夫兰（Chauvelin）本人。他是自己迈步走进卡特琳·泰奥（Catherine Théot）寓所的前厅呢，还是有一只看不见的手把他推了进去，他也说不上来。可以断定的只是：等他回过神来、把周遭看个分明的时候，他正坐在一条长凳上，背抵着墙；而紧贴在他面前，一双慵懒的半阖眼睛正俯视着他——潇洒从容，衣冠楚楚，镇定自若——站着的，正是他的死对头珀西·布莱克尼爵士（Sir Percy Blakeney）。

前厅阴沉到了极点。这当口不知是谁点亮了中央枝形吊灯上的牛油蜡烛，烛光昏弱摇曳，照着潮乎乎的光墙、不铺地毯的地板、板条紧闭的窗户；一缕细细的、气味难闻的烟，袅袅盘旋着，升向熏得乌黑的天花板。

===Original===

Of Theresia Cabarrus there was not a sign. Chauvelin looked about him, feeling like a goaded animal shut up in a narrow space with its tormentor. He was making desperate efforts to regain his composure, above all he made appeal to that courage which was wont never to desert him. In truth, Chauvelin had never been a physical coward, nor was he afraid of death or outrage at the hands of the man whom he had so deeply wronged, and whom he had pursued with a veritable lust of hate. No! he did not fear death at the hands of the Scarlet Pimpernel. What he feared was ridicule, humiliation, those schemes—bold, adventurous, seemingly impossible—which he knew were already seething behind the smooth, unruffled brow of his arch-enemy, behind those lazy, supercilious eyes, which had the power to irritate his nerves to the verge of dementia.

This impudent adventurer—no better than a spy, despite his aristocratic mien and air of lofty scorn—this meddlesome English brigand, was the one man in the world who had, when he measured his prowess against him, invariably brought him to ignominy and derision, made him a laughingstock before those whom he had been wont to dominate; and at this moment, when once again he was being forced to look into those strangely provoking eyes, he appraised their glance as he would the sword of a proved adversary, and felt as he did so just that same unaccountable dread of them which had so often paralysed his limbs and atrophied his brain whenever mischance flung him into the presence of his enemy.

===Chinese===

特蕾莎·卡巴吕斯（Theresia Cabarrus）连个影儿都不见了。肖夫兰环顾四周，只觉得自己像一头被逼进窄笼、与折磨它的人关在一处的受戳牲口。他拼命想找回镇定，尤其想唤起那份素来不曾抛下他的勇气。说实在的，肖夫兰从来不是个贪生怕死的懦夫，他也不怕死在那人手里、受那人凌辱——那人正是他深深亏负过的，也正是他怀着如痴如狂的仇恨穷追猛打过的。不！他不怕死在猩红石榴红（the Scarlet Pimpernel）手里。他怕的是嘲弄，是羞辱，是那些计谋——大胆、冒险、看似绝无可能——他深知它们此刻正在死对头那平滑无波的额头后面、在那双慵懒倨傲的眼睛后面翻腾鼎沸；而那双眼睛，是有本领把他的神经激到疯癫边缘的。

这个狂妄的冒险家——纵有一副贵胄气派、一身居高临下的轻蔑，说到底不过是个间谍——这个爱管闲事的英国强盗，是天底下唯一一个在与他较量身手时回回都叫他蒙羞受辱的人，害他在他素来凌驾其上的人们面前沦为笑柄；而此刻，当他再一次被迫望进那双古怪地挑衅的眼睛时，他掂量那目光，就像掂量一位验证过的敌手的剑锋，并且一边掂量，一边感到对它们那种说不清道不明的忌惮——每逢厄运把他掷到那仇人面前，正是这忌惮，一次次叫他四肢瘫软、脑髓枯竭。

===Original===

He could not understand why Theresia Cabarrus had deserted him. Even a woman, if she happened to be a friend, would by her presence have afforded him moral support.

===Chinese===

他不明白，特蕾莎·卡巴吕斯为什么抛下了他。哪怕是个女人，只要碰巧算得上朋友，单凭在场，也能给他一分道义上的撑持。

===Original===

“You are looking for Mme. de Fontenay, I believe, dear M. Chambertin,” Sir Percy said lightly, as if divining his thoughts. “The ladies—ah, the ladies! They add charm, piquancy, eh? to the driest conversations. Alas!” he went on with mock affectation, “that Mme. de Fontenay should have fled at first sound of my voice! Now she hath sought refuge in the old witch’s lair, there to consult the spirits as to how best she can get out again, seeing that the door is now locked… Deemed awkward, a locked door, when a pretty woman wants to be on the other side. What think you, M. Chambertin?”

===Chinese===

“我想，您是在找德·丰特奈夫人（Mme. de Fontenay）吧，亲爱的尚贝丹先生（M. Chambertin，勃艮第名酒之名，爵士存心错叫他的姓氏）？”珀西爵士轻飘飘地说道，仿佛看穿了他的心思。“女人们哪——啊，女人们！再枯燥乏味的谈话，她们也能添上几分风韵、几分妙趣，是吧？唉！”他装腔作势地接着说，“德·丰特奈夫人竟一听到我的声音就逃之夭夭了！这会儿她躲进那老巫婆的巢穴里去了，正在那儿请教众位神灵：这门既然已经上了锁，怎样才能最好地再出去呢……门上了锁，偏偏一个漂亮女人想溜到门那边去——您说这滥不尴尬？您怎么看，尚贝丹先生？”

===Original===

“I only think, Sir Percy,” Chauvelin contrived to retort, calling all his wits and all his courage to aid him in his humiliating position, “I only think of another pretty woman, who is in the room just above our heads and who would also be mightily glad to find herself the other side of a locked door.”

“Your thoughts,” Sir Percy retorted with a light laugh, “are always so ingenuous, my dear M. Chambertin. Strangely enough, mine just at this moment run on the possibility—not a very unlikely one, you will admit—of shaking the breath out of your ugly little body, as I would that of a rat.”

===Chinese===

“我只是想，珀西爵士，”肖夫兰身处屈辱之境，只得把全副机智、全副胆气都唤来帮腔，好歹挤出了还嘴的话，“我只是想着另一个漂亮女人：她就在咱们头顶上那间屋子里，倘若能发现自己到了一扇上锁的门的另一边，只怕她也要求之不得哩。”

“您的念头，”珀西爵士轻轻一笑，回敬道，“永远这样天真，我亲爱的尚贝丹先生。说来也怪，我此刻盘算的，是一种可能——您也得承认，这可能性并不算小——把您这丑陋的小身子里那口气抖搂出来，就像抖搂一只耗子。”

===Original===

“Shake, my dear Sir Percy, shake!” Chauvelin riposted with well-simulated calm. “I grant you that I am a puny rat and you are the most magnificent of lions; but even if I lie mangled and breathless on this stone floor at your feet, Lady Blakeney will still be a prisoner in our hands.”

“And you will still be wearing the worst-cut pair of breeches it has ever been my bad fortune to behold,” Sir Percy retorted, quite unruffled. “Lud love you, man! Have you guillotined all the good tailors in Paris?”

===Chinese===

“抖吧，亲爱的珀西爵士，尽管抖吧！”肖夫兰拿捏出一副镇定，反唇相讥。“我认了：我是只孱弱的耗子，您是头最神武的雄狮；可纵然我筋断骨折、气绝身亡，躺在您脚边这石板地上，布莱克尼夫人（Lady Blakeney）也依然是我们的阶下囚。”

“而您呢，也依然穿着那条剪裁最糟的马裤——我有生以来最倒霉才有幸见到的那条，”珀西爵士全然不动声色地回敬。“老天爷疼你哟，伙计！你们把巴黎的好裁缝全都送上断头台了吗？”

===Original===

“You choose to be flippant, Sir Percy,” Chauvelin rejoined dryly. “But, though you have chosen for the past few years to play the role of a brainless nincompoop, I have cause to know that behind your affectations there lurks an amount of sound common sense.”

“Lud, how you flatter me, my dear sir!” quoth Sir Percy airily. “I vow you had not so high an opinion of me last time I had the honour of conversing with you. It was at Nantes; do you remember?”

===Chinese===

“您存心耍贫嘴，珀西爵士，”肖夫兰干巴巴地应道。“不过，您虽然这些年存心扮一个没脑子的蠢货，我却有缘知道：您那副做派的背后，藏着的是相当扎实的见识。”

“老天，您可真抬举我，亲爱的先生！”珀西爵士飘飘然道。“我敢打赌，上回我有幸与您交谈时，您对我的评价可没这么高。那是在南特（Nantes）；您还记得么？”

===Original===

“There, as elsewhere, you succeeded in circumventing me, Sir Percy.”

“No, no!” he protested. “Not in circumventing you. Only in making you look a demmed fool!”

===Chinese===

“在那里，也同在别处一样，您把我耍弄成功了，珀西爵士。”

“不，不！”他抗议道。“不是把您耍弄了。不过是叫您看上去活像个怪得要命的大傻瓜罢了！”

===Original===

“Call it that, if you like, sir,” Chauvelin admitted, with an indifferent shrug of the shoulders. “Luck has favoured you many a time. As I had the honour to tell you, you have had the laugh of us in the past, and no doubt you are under the impression that you will have it again this time.”

“I am such a believer in impressions, my dear sir. The impression now that I have of your charming personality is indelibly graven upon my memory.”

===Chinese===

“您愿意这么叫，就这么叫吧，先生，”肖夫兰认了，漠然耸了耸肩。“运气照拂过您许多回了。正如我曾有幸奉告过您的，过去您是笑赢了我们；毫无疑问，您这会儿正想着，这一回您还要再笑一次。”

“我最信印象了，亲爱的先生。此刻我对您迷人品格的印象，已经不可磨灭地镌刻在我的记忆里了。”

===Original===

“Sir Percy Blakeney counts a good memory as one of his many accomplishments. Another is his adventurous spirit, and the gallantry which must inevitably bring him into the net which we have been at pains to spread for him. Lady Blakeney—”

===Chinese===

“珀西·布莱克尼爵士把好记性算作他诸多才艺中的一样。另一项，是他那冒险的天性，还有那份豪侠心肠——正是它们，必不可免地要把他引进我们煞费苦心为他张好的网里。布莱克尼夫人——”

===Original===

“Name her not, man!” Sir Percy broke in with affected deliberation; “or I verily believe that within sixty seconds you would be a dead man!”

“I am not worthy to speak her name, *c’est entendu*,” Chauvelin retorted with mock humility. “Nevertheless, Sir Percy, it is around the person of that gracious lady that the Fates will spin their web during the next few days. You may kill me. Of course, I am at this moment entirely at your mercy. But before you embark on such a perilous undertaking, will you allow me to place the position a little more clearly before you?”

===Chinese===

“不许提她的名字，伙计！”珀西爵士故意一字一顿地打断道；“不然我实实在在相信，不出六十秒，您就是条死尸了！”

“我是不配提她的名字，那是说定了的（c’est entendu），”肖夫兰拿谦卑来打趣，回敬道。“然而，珀西爵士，往后这几天，命运诸女神要纺的那张网，恰恰是绕着那位雍容夫人的身子织的。您可以杀了我。当然，此刻我全凭您发落。只是，在您着手这样一桩凶险的事业之前，可否容我把眼下的局势，向您摆得更明白些？”

===Original===

“Lud, man!” quoth Sir Percy with a quaint laugh. “That’s what I’m here for! Think you that I have sought your agreeable company for the mere pleasure of gazing at your amiable countenance?”

“I only desired to explain to you, Sir Percy, the dangers to which you expose Lady Blakeney, if you laid violent hands upon me. ’Tis you, remember, who sought this interview—not I.”

“You are right, my dear sir, always right; and I’ll not interrupt again. I pray you to proceed.”

===Chinese===

“哎呀，伙计！”珀西爵士发出一声古怪的笑。“我正是为这个来的！您莫非以为，我来寻您作伴，就为了瞻仰您那张和蔼可亲的脸？”

“我不过是想向您说明，珀西爵士：您若对我下了毒手，会把布莱克尼夫人陷进怎样的险地。别忘了，是您求的这次会面——可不是我。”

“您说得对，亲爱的先生，您永远是对的；我不再打岔了。请您往下讲。”

===Original===

“Allow me then to make my point clear. There are at this moment a score of men of the National Guard in the room above your head. Every one of them goes to the guillotine if they allow their prisoner to escape; every one of them receives a reward of ten thousand livres the day they capture the Scarlet Pimpernel. A good spur for vigilance, what? But that is not all,” Chauvelin went on quite steadily, seeing that Sir Percy had apparently become thoughtful and absorbed. “The men are under the command of Captain Boyer, and he understands that every day at a certain hour—seven in the evening, to be precise—I will be with him and interrogate him as to the welfare of the prisoner. If—mark me, Sir Percy!—if on any one day I do not appear before him at that hour, his orders are to shoot the prisoner on sight…”

===Chinese===

“那么请容我把话说明白。此刻，您头顶上那间屋子里，有二十名国民自卫军（National Guard）的兵士。放走了囚徒，他们人人都要上断头台；擒获猩红石榴红之日，他们人人又都有一万里弗尔（livres）的赏金。督促警醒的好马刺，是吧？可还不止这些，”肖夫兰见珀西爵士显然已沉入思索，便平平稳稳地接下去说。“那些人归布瓦耶上尉（Captain Boyer）统带，而他心里明白：每天一到某个钟点——说得确切些，是晚上七点——我就会到他那里，查问囚徒的起居安好。如果——您听着，珀西爵士！——如果哪一天我没在那个钟点到他面前，他奉到的命令是：一见囚徒，当即枪毙……”

===Original===

The word was scarce out of his mouth; it broke in a hoarse spasm. Sir Percy had him by the throat, shook him indeed as he would a rat.

“You cur!” he said in an ominous whisper, his face quite close now to that of his enemy, his jaw set, his eyes no longer good-humoured and mildly scornful, but burning with the fire of a mighty, unbridled wrath. “You damned—insolent—miserable cur! As there is a Heaven above us—”

===Chinese===

话音几乎还没出口；它就在一阵嘶哑的痉挛里折断了。珀西爵士已经掐住了他的喉咙，当真像抖一只耗子似的抖着他。

“你这贱狗！”他用不祥的耳语说道，脸紧逼到仇人的脸前，牙关紧咬，眼睛里不再是那种和气而略带轻蔑的神色，而是燃着一股狂放不羁的熊熊怒火。“你这该死的——无礼的——卑贱的贱狗！头顶上自有苍天——”

===Original===

Then suddenly his grip relaxed, the whole face changed as if an unseen hand has swept away the fierce lines of anger and hate. The eyes softened beneath their heavy lids, the set lips broke into a mocking smile. He let go his hold of the Terrorist’s throat; and the unfortunate man, panting and breathless, fell heavily against the wall. He tried to steady himself as best he could, but his knees were shaking, and faint and helpless, he finally collapsed upon the nearest bench, the while Sir Percy straightened out his tall figure, with unruffled composure rubbed his slender hands one against the other, as if to free them from dust, and said, with gentle, good-humoured sarcasm:

===Chinese===

===Original===

===Chinese===

“而您当真以为，”珀西·布莱克尼爵士缓缓地、一字一顿地说道，“您有本领把您那些卑劣的阴谋做成？以为我——不错，是我！——会容您挨到它们得逞的边儿上？呸！我亲爱的朋友。您从过往的经历里什么也没学会——连这一点也没学会：当您斗胆把您那双肮脏的手按到布莱克尼夫人身上时，您，连同您那整整一帮把这片美丽河山恐怖蹂躏得太久的刺客，便已敲响了你们万劫不复的丧钟。您竟敢以一桩在我眼中穷凶极恶的暴行，来同我较量臂力；为了惩罚您，我——正是我！——要把您从地面上扫除干净，打发您去与那帮帮着您作恶的不洁食尸鬼为伍。此后——感谢上苍！——大地涤除了您，自会重新散发出芬芳。”

肖夫兰徒然想笑一笑，想耸一耸肩，想摆出他对手信手拈来的那几副倨傲做派。毫无疑问，同仇敌这场漫长交锋的重压，把他的神经压垮了。可以断定的只是：此刻，他神志尚清，足能在心底痛骂自己的怯懦，却全然动弹不得，也回不上半句嘴。他四肢沉得像灌了铅，一股冰冷的寒战顺着脊骨往下淌。真仿佛有个阴森的食尸鬼，钻进了这凄凉潮湿的寓所，正用一只枯瘦看不见的手，敲着一口无声的丧钟——他全部野心、全部希望的丧钟。他阖上了眼睛，因为他觉得头晕目眩、阵阵欲呕。待他再睁开眼时，屋里只剩他一个人了。
