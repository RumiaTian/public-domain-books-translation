## XIII Very Cold / 第十三章 冷若冰霜
===Original===

When Dennis entered Mr. Ludolph’s store Christine was absent on a visit to New York. On her return she resumed her old routine. At this time she and her father were occupying a suite of rooms at a fashionable hotel. Her schooldays were over, Mr. Ludolph preferring to complete her education himself in accordance with his peculiar views and tastes. She was just passing into her twentieth year, and looked upon the world from the vantage points of health, beauty, wealth, accomplishments of the highest order, and the best social standing. Assurance of a long and brilliant career possessed her mind, while pride and beauty were like a coronet upon her brow. She was the world’s ideal of a queen.
And yet she was not truly happy. There was ever a vague sense of unrest and dissatisfaction at heart. She saw that her father was proud and ambitious in regard to her, but she instinctively felt that he neither loved nor trusted her to any great extent. She seemed to be living in a palace of ice, and at times felt that she was turning into ice herself; but her very humanity and womanhood, deadened and warped though they were, cried out against the *cold* of a life without God or love. In the depths of her soul she felt that something was wrong, but what, she could not understand. It seemed that she had everything that heart could wish, and that she ought to be satisfied.
She had at last concluded that her restlessness was the prompting of a lofty ambition, and that if she chose she could win worldwide celebrity as an artist. This, with the whole force of her strong nature, she had determined to do, and for over two years had worked with an energy akin to enthusiasm. She had resolved that painting should be the solid structure of her success, and music its ornament.
Nor were her dreams altogether chimerical, for she had remarkable talent in her chosen field of effort, and had been taught to use the brush and pencil from childhood. She could imitate with skill and taste, and express with great accuracy the musical thought of the composer; but she could not create new effects, and this had already begun to trouble her. She worked hard and patiently, determined to succeed. So great had been her application that her father saw the need of rest and change, and therefore her visit to New York.

丹尼斯初进卢多尔夫先生画店那阵子，克里斯蒂娜恰巧出门去纽约游历未归。如今回来之后，她又恢复了往日那套起居作息。彼时她与父亲在一座时髦奢华的旅馆里包下了一整套房间。她的学校课业早已修毕，而卢多尔夫先生更乐意亲自按照自己独到的见解与品位，亲手替她完成余下的深造教育。她正值即将步入二十岁芳华的妙龄，站在集健康、美貌、财富、顶级才艺与最优越社会地位之巅俯瞰着这个世界。对于漫长而辉煌的前程，她早已胸有成竹、笃信不疑，而骄傲与美貌宛如一顶璀璨的华冠戴在她额前。在她所处的那个世界里，她正是人们心目中理想女王的化身。

然而，她却并不真正快乐。在她心底深处，始终潜藏着一丝隐约的不安与不满。她看得出来，父亲为她感到骄傲，也在她身上寄托着极大的野心，可她凭直觉却敏锐地感觉到，父亲其实并不怎么爱她，也并不怎么信任她。她仿佛置身于一座冰晶筑成的宫殿之中，有时甚至觉得自己也正在一点点变成一块寒冰；然而她天性里与生俱来的人性与女儿本色——即便已被麻木、扭曲得不成样子——却仍在暗中呐喊着，抗拒这种没有上帝、也没有爱的*冰冷*人生。在她灵魂的最深处，她隐约觉得哪儿出了大差错，可究竟错在哪里，她却百思不得其解。按理说，世间凡是人心所能希冀的一切她似乎都已拥有，理应心满意足才是。

思来想去，她终于给自己得出一个结论：心头那股焦躁不安，不过是源于自己胸怀大志、雄心万丈的驱策——她认定，只要她愿意，自己完全能够以大画家的身份扬名立万、享誉世界。凭着她那刚烈倔强的全部天性，她打定了主意非做到不可，并且两年多来一直以近乎狂热的干劲埋头苦学。她立志要把绘画铸成自己功成名就的坚实大厦，而以音乐作为点缀其间的华美装饰。

她的梦想也并非全是镜花水月，因为她在自己选定的这门行当里确实天赋异禀，更何况自幼便受过素描与油画的严格调教。她能够极其灵巧而有品味地临摹前人，也能丝毫不差地精准传达出作曲家的乐思；然而她却无法独创出崭新的艺术意境，而这一局限早已开始令她苦恼不已。她夜以继日、忍耐坚毅地苦练，铁了心非闯出一番名堂不可。她用功之狠、耗神之巨，连她父亲都看出她必须歇一歇、换换环境，这才有了她前一阵子的纽约之行。

===Original===

She had now returned strengthened, and eager for her former studies, and resumed them with tenfold zest.
The plan of rearranging the store on artistic principles daily grew in favor with her. It was just the exercise of taste she delighted in, and she hoped some day to indulge it on palace walls that would be her own. Her father’s pride caused him to hesitate for some time, but she said: “Why, Chicago is not our home; we shall soon be thousands of miles away. You know how little we really care for the opinions of the people here: it is only our own pride and opinion that we need consult. I see nothing lowering or unfeminine in the work. I shall scarcely touch a thing myself, merely direct; for surely among all in your employ there must be one or two pairs of hands not so utterly awkward but that they can follow plain instructions. My taste shall do it all. We are both early risers, and the whole change can be made before the store is opened. Moreover,” she added (with an expression indicating that she would have little difficulty in ruling her future German castle, and its lord also), “this is an affair of our own. Those you employ ought to understand by this time that it is neither wise nor safe to talk of our business outside.”
After a moment’s thought she concluded: “I really think that the proper arrangement of everything in the store as to light, display, and effect, so that people of taste will be pleased when they enter, would add thousands of dollars to your sales; and this rigid system of old Schwartz’s, which annoys us both beyond endurance, will be broken up.”
Won over by arguments that accorded with his inclinations, Mr. Ludolph gave his daughter permission to carry out the plan in her own way.
She usually accompanied her father to the store in the morning. He, after a brief glance around, would go to his private office and attend to correspondence. She would do whatever her mood prompted. Sometimes she would sit down for a half-hour before one picture; again she would examine most critically a statue or a statuette. Whenever new music was received, she looked it over and carried off such pieces as pleased her fancy.
She evidently was a privileged character, and no one save her father exercised the slightest control over her movements.

如今她养足了精神归来，对往日的功课渴盼不已，立刻以十倍的热忱重新投入了研习。

将整间画店照着艺术原则彻底重新布置的念头，一天比一天更得她心意。这恰恰是她平生最酷爱的展示品位之道，她一直盼着有朝一日能在属于自己的贵族府邸四壁之上尽情施展这番才华。起初，她父亲因自尊心作祟而犹豫了颇久，克里斯蒂娜却劝说道：“哎呀，芝加哥又不是我们的家乡；用不了多久，我们就要远隔重洋、去到万里之外了。您明知道，我们心里根本不大在乎这儿老百姓的看法：我们只需顾全自己的体面与主见就够了。这活儿我看既不掉价，也无损大家闺秀的仪态。我几乎不必亲自动手碰任何东西，只需站在旁边发号施令罢了；在您雇的那么多人里头，总该能挑出一两双不算太笨手笨脚的手，能照着简单明白的吩咐去干活吧。一切全凭我的眼光调遣就行。咱们父女俩都是早起的人，赶在每天画店开门营业之前，整套改头换面就能大功告成。况且，”她补充道（说话时脸上露出的那副神情，分明在说将来统御那座德国古堡连同城堡的主人，于她而言也绝非难事），“这是咱们自家的体己事。您手下雇的那些人，到如今也该明白在外面嚼咱们生意的舌根既不明智、也不安全。”

略微思忖了片刻，她又一锤定音地总结道：“我真心认为，把店里每一样物件的光线、陈列与整体效果都调理得当，好让有品味的人一跨进门便赏心悦目，单这一招就能给您的营业额平白添上好几千美元；顺带也能把老施瓦茨那套死板僵化、教咱们俩都忍无可忍的臭规矩彻底打破。”

这番说辞句句正中卢多尔夫先生下怀，他欣然首肯，准许女儿放手照自己的主意大刀阔斧去办。

自此，她早晨常常随父亲一同来到画店。父亲草草巡视一圈，便走进自己的私人办公室处理往来信函；她则任凭兴致所至，随心所欲地打发时光。有时她会在某幅油画前坐上足足半个钟头；有时又会拿无比挑剔的眼光细细端详一尊大雕像或小塑像。每逢新一批乐谱到货，她便一一翻检，挑出合自己心意的曲谱带回家去。

显而易见，她在店里是个享有特权的人物，除了她父亲之外，谁也管束不住她半分举止。

She treated all the clerks, save old Schwartz, as if they were animated machines; and by a quiet order, as if she had touched a spring, would set them in motion to do her bidding. The young men in the store were of German descent, and rather heavy and undemonstrative. Mr. Schwartz’s system of order and repression had pretty thoroughly quenched them. They were educated to the niches they filled, and seemed to have no thought beyond; therefore they were all unruffled at Miss Ludolph’s air of absolute sovereignty. Mr. Schwartz was as obsequious as the rest, but, as second to her father in power, was permitted some slight familiarity. In fact this heavy, stolid prime-minister both amused and annoyed her, and she treated him with the caprice of a child toward an elephant﻿—at times giving him the sugarplum of a compliment, and oftener pricking him with the pin of some caustic remark. To him she was the perfection of womankind﻿—her reserved, dispassionate manner, her steady, unwearied prosecution of a purpose, being just the qualities that he most honored; and he worshipped her reverently at a distance, like an old astrologer adoring some particularly bright fixed star. No whisking comets or changing satellites for old Schwartz.
As for Dennis, she treated him as she probably had treated Pat Murphy, and for several days had no occasion to notice him at all. In fact he kept out of her way, choosing at first to observe rather than be observed. She became an artistic study to him, for her every movement was grace itself, except that there was no softness or gentleness in her manner. Her face fascinated him by its beauty, though its expression troubled him﻿—it was so unlike his mother’s, so unlike what he felt a woman’s ought to be. But her eager interest in that which was becoming so dear to him﻿—art﻿—would have covered a multitude of sins in his eyes, and with a heart abounding in faith and hope, not yet diminished by hard experience, he believed that the undeveloped angel existed within her. But he remembered her frown when she had first noticed his observation of her. The shrewd Yankee youth saw that her pride would not brook even a curious glance. But while he kept at a most respectful distance he felt that there was no such wide gulf between them as she imagined.

除了老施瓦茨以外，她看待店里所有伙计，简直如同看待一具具会走动的发条木偶；只要她轻声下一道指令，仿佛伸手按了一记弹簧开关，他们便得立刻转动起来，乖乖照办她的差遣。店里那几个年轻小伙子都是德国裔，性子颇为木讷呆板、不苟言笑。施瓦茨先生那套讲究死规矩与高压管束的做派，早已把他们调教得服服帖帖、毫无生机。他们被规训得严丝合缝地嵌在各自领受的那个位置上，仿佛脑子里压根儿不敢越雷池半步；因此对于卢多尔夫小姐那副唯我独尊、发号施令的霸王派头，大伙儿全都习以为常、波澜不惊。施瓦茨先生自己也跟其余人一般卑躬屈膝、唯命是从，不过因他是仅次于她父亲的二把手，倒被允许略微多一点亲近随和的余地。事实上，这位笨重呆板的首席大臣既教她觉得好笑，又常教她心烦，因此她摆弄起他来，就像小孩子逗弄一头大象般随心所欲、全凭喜怒——时而赏他一颗甜丝丝的恭维糖果，更多时候却拿几句尖酸刻薄的冷箭扎他一针。而在老施瓦茨眼里，她简直是世间女子完美的极致——她那副内敛矜持、不苟喜怒的气度，她那份沉稳坚毅、百折不挠推进目标的狠劲，恰恰都是他平生最推崇的美德；于是他从远远的地方怀着虔诚敬畏之心仰慕着她，活像一位老占星家顶礼膜拜天穹中某颗格外璀璨的恒星。老施瓦茨眼中，可容不得那些轻狂乱窜的彗星或阴晴不定的卫星。

至于丹尼斯，她对他的态度大概同从前对待帕特·墨菲一般无二，一连好几天压根儿没正眼瞧过他一回。事实上，丹尼斯自己也总是主动避开她的视线，起初宁可躲在暗处静静观察，而不愿引人注目。在她身上，他寻到了一个绝佳的艺术观察标本——因为她的一举一动无一不优雅到了极致，唯独言谈举止间找不出半点柔和与温情。她那张容颜凭着绝世之美深深迷住了他，可眉宇间流露出的神情却令他心里隐隐刺痛——那神情与他母亲是何等截然不同，与他心目中一位女子理当具备的温良神韵又是何等格格不入！然而她对艺术——那门正在他心中变得无比珍贵的事业——所怀有的热切执着，在他眼里便足以遮掩千般过失；怀着一颗尚未被残酷阅历磨灭、依旧充盈着信心与希望的赤子之心，他深信在她躯壳之内必定沉睡着一位尚未唤醒的天使。不过，他依然牢牢记着她头一回察觉自己在打量她时脸上浮现出的那道蹙眉。这位精明敏感的美国佬青年看得出，她的骄傲绝不容许旁人投去哪怕一丝好奇的窥探目光。可当他恭恭敬敬地退守在极其远的距离之外时，他心里却隐隐觉得，两人之间横亘的鸿沟，其实远没有她自以为的那般天差地别。

By birth and education he was as truly entitled to her acquaintance as the young men who sometimes came into the store with her and whom she met in society. Position and wealth were alone wanting, and in spite of his hard experience and lowly work he felt that there must be some way for him, as for others, to win these.
He longed for the society of ladies, as every right-feeling young man does, and to one of his nature the grace and beauty of woman were peculiarly attractive. If, before she came, the lovely faces of the pictures had filled the place with a sort of witchery, and created about him an atmosphere in which his artist-soul was awakening into life and growth, how much more would it be true of this living vision of beauty that glided in and out every day!
“She does not notice me,” he at first said to himself, “any more than do these lovely shadows upon the canvas. But why need I care? I can study both them and her, and thus educate my eye, and I hope my hand, to imitate and perhaps surpass their perfections in time.”
But this cool, philosophic mood did not last long. It might answer very well in regard to the pictures on the walls, but there was a magnetism about this living, breathing woman that soon caused him to long for the privilege of being near her and speaking to her of that subject that interested them both so deeply. Though he had never seen any of her paintings to know them, he soon saw that she was no novice in such matters and that she looked at works of art with the eye of a connoisseur. In revery he had many a spirited conversation with her, and he trusted that some day his dreams would become real. He had the romantic hope that if she should discover his taste and strong love of art she might at first bestow upon him a patronizing interest which would gradually grow into respect and acknowledged equality.

论出身门第与学识教养，他原与那些时常陪她来画店、或在社交场上与她周旋的年轻绅士们同样有资格结识她。他所欠缺的，唯独地位与财富而已；然而尽管眼下经历艰辛、干着卑微的粗活，他坚信既然旁人能挣来这些东西，他自己也定然寻得到一条出路。

同每一位情感健全的青年男子一样，他渴望结交淑女佳人，而以他这般重情细腻的天性，女子的风姿与美貌对他更是具有一种格外动人的魅力。倘若在克里斯蒂娜到来之前，画店里那些画布上的美人容颜便已令整座店堂笼罩在一股奇异的魔力之中、替他营造出了一方足以唤醒他艺术灵魂并使其勃发生长的天地，那么对于眼前这位每日轻盈穿梭进出、活生生的绝色丽人，这种魔力又该强烈到何等地步呢！

“她根本没有留神我，”起初他暗自宽慰自己，“正如同画布上那些迷人的倩影不会留神我一样。可我又何必介怀？我大可以一边揣摩那些名画，一边细细观察她，借此磨炼我的眼光，更但愿假以时日也能磨炼出这双手的巧技，去效法乃至超越她们的完美风华。”

然而这番冷静超然的哲学心境并没有维持太久。拿这一套去对待墙上挂着的油画倒还行得通，可这位活生生、有血有肉、呼吸吐纳的女子身上散发出的强烈磁场，很快便教他心底生出一股难以遏制的渴望——渴望能获准靠近她身旁，同她聊一聊那桩令两人同样魂牵梦萦的艺术话题。尽管他从未见过她本人所绘的画作，无从知晓她的手艺究竟如何，他却很快看出来，她在这些门道上绝非外行新手，而是以一位行家鉴赏家的挑剔眼光审视着件件艺术品。在白日梦里，他曾无数次同她展开意气风发、精彩纷呈的畅谈；他笃信终有一天，他的美梦会照进现实。他甚至怀着一个浪漫天真的幻想：倘若她哪天无意中发觉了他的审美品味与对艺术的一腔炽爱，兴许起初会赏给他几分居高临下的垂青赏识，而那份赏识日后定会一点点生长为由衷的敬重，乃至彼此平等的惺惺相惜。
