## XV Promoted / 第十五章 晋升

===Original===
Late in the afternoon of the same day (which was Saturday), as Mr. Ludolph was passing out of the store on his way home, he noticed the table that he had arranged artistically some little time before as a lesson to his clerks. Gradually it had fallen back into its old straight lines and rigid appearance. He seemed greatly annoyed.

“What is the use of rearranging the store?” he muttered. “They will have it all back again on the general principle of a ramrod in a little while. But we have put our hands to this work, and it shall be carried through, even if I discharge half of these wooden-heads.”

Then calling the clerk in charge, he said, “Look here, Mr. Berder, I grouped the articles on this counter for you once, did I not?”

“Yes, sir.”

“Let me find them Monday morning just as I arranged them on that occasion.”

The young man looked as blank and dismayed as if he had been ordered to swallow them all before Monday morning.

After the duties of the store were over, Dennis wrote to his mother a warm, bright, filial letter, portraying the scene of the day in its comic light, making all manner of fun of himself, that he might hide the fact that he had suffered. 散工之后，店里的差事都忙完了，丹尼斯给母亲写了一封温暖、明快而又满含孝心的信，把白天店里的种种情形从滑稽趣致的角度描绘了一番，拿自己百般打趣，好瞒住自己所受的委屈。然而他到底没能瞒住，后来母亲的回信便是最好的明证——信里字字句句都是心疼与愤慨，只因竟有人敢那样对待*她*的儿子；但信中同时也满是对他作为基督徒那份刚毅气节与隐忍耐心的赞许。

“还有，我的儿啊，”她在信中写道，“让我告诉你，你如今虽担着微末的差事，却踏实尽责地去做，至少已结出了两样好果子。你那样按时寄来的钱，应付我们母子简朴的日用已绰绰有余。我们什么都不缺，日子安稳舒适，我还能攒下些银钱，以防日后生病遭难——在这世道上，这两样事迟早总免不了会找上门来。其次，你给了我比金钱珍贵得多的东西——那就是慰藉与力量。我越来越真切地感到，我们可以把你当作尘世的依靠，靠上去，绝不会碰上一根‘压伤的芦苇’。眼下多少儿子正伤透母亲的心，你却让我的心里盛满了盼望与喜乐。我算不得什么女先知，我的儿，但凭着上帝确凿无疑的圣言，我敢为你预言：你这般安慰并奉养守寡的母亲，日后定必大享福乐与兴旺。记住妈妈的话。上帝已经试炼了你，没有看出你的亏欠。祂很快就会赐给你更好的差事去做——一份更配得上你品格与才干的差事。”

This prediction was fulfilled before Dennis received the letter containing it, and it happened on this wise.

Early on Monday morning Mr. Berder appeared and attempted the hopeless task of grouping the articles on his table in accordance with Mr. Ludolph’s orders. After an hour’s work he exclaimed in despair, “I cannot do him to save my life.”

Dennis at a distance, with a half-amused, half-pitying face, had watched Mr. Berder’s wonderful combinations, and when Rip Van Winkle was placed between two togated Roman senators, and Ichabod Crane arranged as if making love to a Greek goddess, he came near laughing outright. But when Mr. Berder spoke he approached and said, kindly and respectfully, “Will you let me try to help you?”

“Yes,” said Mr. Berder; “you cannot make dings vorse.” Acting upon this ungracious permission, Dennis folded his arms and studied the table for five minutes.

“Come,” said Mr. Berder, “standing dere and looking so vise as an owl von’t help matters. Mr. Ludolph vill be here soon.”

“I am not losing time,” said Dennis; and a moment proved he was not, for, having formed a general plan of arrangement, he went rapidly to work, and in a quarter of an hour could challenge Mr. Ludolph or any other critic to find serious fault.

But I never trust anyone who has deceived me once,” he said, so sternly that even Christine’s cheek paled. “Mr. Schwartz will settle with you, and let me never see or hear from you again. Mr. Fleet, I promote you to Mr. Berder’s counter and pay.”

Thus this man of the world, without a thought of pity, mercy, or kindly feeling in either case, gave one of his clerks a new impetus toward the devil, and another an important lift toward better things, and then went his way, congratulating himself that all things had worked together for his good, that morning, though where he would find another Dennis Fleet to fill Pat’s place, again vacant, he did not know.

But Miss Ludolph looked at Dennis somewhat kindly, and with a little honest admiration in her face. He was very different from what she had as a matter of course supposed him to be, and had just done in a quiet, manly way a thing most pleasing to her, so she said with a smile that seemed perfectly heavenly to him, “*You* are above blacking boots, sir.”

卢多尔夫先生身上带有拿破仑式的做派，倒不止体现在他的野心与自私上；他还有着足够的精明，懂得“按功当场火线提拔”。因此，眼见丹尼斯手头的活计已近尾声，他便开口道：“行了，弗利特先生，剩下的细活儿你回头慢慢收尾便是。贝尔德先生，鉴于你欺上瞒下，从今日起你被辞退了。倘若你肯老老实实做人，你的平庸无能我尚且还能容忍；可只要有人欺骗过我一次，我就绝不再信任他。”他这番话说得冷峻逼人，连克里斯蒂娜的脸颊都随之泛白，“施瓦茨先生会同你结清账目，从今往后，叫我再也别见着你，也别再听见你的半点消息。弗利特先生，我提拔你接替贝尔德先生的柜台，薪水也按他的标准算。”

这位老于世故的商人就这样行事——无论是对哪一个，他心中都不曾生出半分怜悯、宽宥或温情——一手把一名店员朝魔鬼的深渊推近了一步，另一手却把另一名店员向着更光明的境地托举了一程；随后他便自顾自扬长而去，暗自庆幸今早真是万事顺遂、件件都叫他占了便宜——只是眼下帕特留下的那份杂役空缺又腾了出来，究竟上哪儿再找第二个丹尼斯·弗利特来顶替，他倒还拿不准主意。

不过，卢多尔夫小姐望向丹尼斯的目光倒柔和了几分，脸上也隐隐浮现出一丝真切的赞赏。他与她先前想当然以为的那副模样截然不同，方才又以一种沉静坦荡、颇具男子气概的方式，做成了一件极合她心意的事；于是她绽开一抹微笑——那笑意在丹尼斯眼里简直犹如天国降临的恩光——说道：“*您*可绝不是只配擦皮靴的人，先生。”
