## V A Hornet’s Nest / 第五章 马蜂窝

===Original===
Dennis awoke greatly refreshed and strengthened. For half an hour he lay quietly thinking over the scenes of the preceding day; something of his old anger returned, but he compressed his lips, and, with a face expressing the most resolute purpose, determined that the day before him should tell a different story. Every faculty and energy he possessed should be skilfully bent to the attainment of his objects. Wise deliberation should precede everything. He would write a few lines to his mother, decide as to a lodging-place, and then seek better success in another part of the city. He went to the bar and inquired as to his bill, and found that so far as bed and meals were concerned, such as they were, he could not find anything cheaper in the city, the house evidently not depending on these for its revenue. Disgusted as he was with his surroundings, he resolved to lose no time in looking for a new boarding-place, but, after writing to his mother, to start off at once in search of something permanent. He was in no mood to consult personal wishes, and the saving of time and money settled the question.

Where should he write? There was no place save a desk at the end of the bar. Looking askance at the half-filled, villainous-smelling bottle at his elbow, he wrote in a hand stiff and unnatural (for he had resolved to change his scrawl to a business hand at once), the following note:

> “**Dear Mother**﻿—I arrived safely, and am very well. I did not, yesterday, find a situation suited to my taste, but expect better success today. I am just on the point of starting out on my search, and when settled will write you full particulars. Many kisses for yourself and the little girls. Your affectionate son,
> Dennis.”

“There! there is nothing in that to worry mother, and soon I shall have good news for her.” (If he had seen its reception, he would have learned his mistake. The intuitions of love are keen, and this formal negative note in the constrained hand told more of his disappointment than any words could have done. While he knew it not, his mother was suffering with him. In reply she wrote a letter full of general sympathy, intending to be more specific when he gave her his confidence.)

Dennis folded the letter most carefully and mailed it﻿—for he was now doing the least thing with the utmost precision﻿—with the air of one who meant to find out the right thing to do, and then to do it to a hair-breadth. Nothing should go wrong that day. So at an early hour he again sallied forth.

===Chinese===
丹尼斯（Dennis）一觉醒来，精神大振，浑身充满了力气。他静静地躺了半个小时，回想着昨日的种种遭遇；胸中又泛起些许旧怒，但他紧抿双唇，神情毅然决绝，暗下决心：眼前的这一天，定要写出一番截然不同的光景。他要把自己所有的才干与精力都巧妙地集中起来，去实现自己的目标。凡事须先深思熟虑，而后行。他打算先给母亲写上几行家书，定好住处的事，再到城里别处碰碰运气，争取更好的结果。他走到酒吧柜台前打听账目，发现单就食宿而言——虽说简陋了些——全城也再找不出更便宜的去处；显然，这家店并非靠食宿营利。虽说对周遭环境厌恶至极，他还是打定主意不把工夫耽搁在另寻寄宿处上，而是写完家信便即刻出门，去谋一份长久的生计。眼下他无心顾及个人好恶，省时省钱才是硬道理。

在哪儿写信呢？除了酒吧尽头那张写字台，别无去处。他斜睨着肘边那只散发着恶臭的半满酒瓶，随即执笔，用一种僵硬而不自然的字体（因为他已立志立刻把原先潦草的字迹改成工整的商务字体），写下了这封短信：

> “**亲爱的母亲**——儿已平安抵达，一切安好。昨日未曾觅得合意之职，望今日能有佳音。此刻正欲出门寻访，待安顿妥当，再向您详述细情。代吻您与妹妹们。爱您的儿子，
> 丹尼斯。”

“成了！这样写母亲就不会担心，用不了多久我就能给她捎去好消息。”（倘若他亲眼见到母亲收信时的情形，便会明白自己错得多么离谱。母爱的直觉敏锐无比，这封措辞生分、一无所获、字迹又如此拘谨的短信，比千言万语更暴露了他的失意。他虽浑然不觉，母亲却已与他感同身受、一同煎熬。回信时，她只写了满纸宽慰鼓励的话，打算等他肯向自己吐露心曲时再细细开导。）

丹尼斯极为仔细地折好信件并投寄出去——因为他如今连最细微的小事都做得一丝不苟——那副神态，活像一个打定主意要找准正道、并毫厘不差地践行到底的人。今天绝不能再出任何差池。于是，一大早他又意气风发地出了门。

===Original===
Not far from the hotel there was a new grocery store about to be opened by two young men, formerly clerks, but now setting up for themselves. They stood at the door receiving a cartload of goods as Dennis approached. He had made up his mind to ask at every opportunity, and to take the first thing that promised fairly; he would also be very polite. Touching his hat to the young men﻿—a little act pleasing to them in their newly acquired dignity as heads of a firm which as yet had no subordinates﻿—Dennis asked if they would need any assistance. Graciously replying to his salutations, they answered, yes; they wanted a young man.

Dennis explained that he was from the country, and showed the ministerial letter. The young grocers looked wise over it, seemed pleased, said they wanted a young fellow from the country, that was not up to city tricks. Chicago was a hard place on young men﻿—spoiled most of them. Glad he was a member of the church. They were not, but believed a man must be mighty good to be one. As the young man they hired must sleep in the store, they wanted one they could trust, and would prefer a church member.

The salary they offered was not large, but pretty fair in view of his having so much to learn, and it was intimated, that if business was good, and he suited, it would be increased. The point uppermost in their minds was to find someone with whom they could trust their store and goods, and this young man from the country, with a letter from a minister, seemed a godsend.

They engaged him, but just as he was starting, with heart swelling with self-satisfaction and joy, one of the firm asked, carelessly, “Where are you staying?”

他们开出的薪水不算丰厚，但念及他还有许多东西要学，倒也相当公道；而且他们还暗示，只要生意兴隆、他干得称心，工钱日后还会涨。他俩眼下最挂心的，就是找一个能放心把铺子和货物托付给他的人，而眼前这个拿着牧师推荐信的乡下小伙，简直像是老天爷送来的宝贝。

两人当场就定下了他。可就在丹尼斯怀着满腔得意与欢喜正要动身之际，其中一位店主随口问了一句：“你住哪儿？”

===Original===
“At Gavin’s Hotel.”

The man turned sharply, and looked most suspiciously at him, and then at his partner, who gave a low whistle of surprise, and also eyed the young man for a moment askance. Then the men stepped aside, and there was a brief whispered consultation. Dennis’s heart sank within him. He saw that something was wrong, but what, he had not the least idea. The elder member of the embryo firm now stepped up and said, decidedly, “Good morning, young man; we shall not need your services.”

“What do you mean?” cried Dennis, in a voice of mingled dismay and indignation.

The man’s face was growing red with anger, but he said, coldly, “You had better move on. *We* understand.”

“But *I* don’t understand, your course toward me is most unjust.”

“But I tell you, gentlemen﻿—” cried Dennis, eagerly.

“*You* may tell what you please. *We* tell you that we would not believe anyone from that den under oath. Now you leave!”

The last words were loud and threatening. The attention of passersby was drawn toward them, and Dennis saw that further words were useless. In the minds of shrewd but narrow business men, not over-honest themselves, more acquainted with the trickery of the world than with its virtues, suspicion against anyone is fatal, and most assuredly so against a stranger with appearances unfavorable.

With heart well-nigh bursting with anger, disappointment, and shame, Dennis hastened away. He had been regarded as a thief, or at best a blackleg, seeking the position for some sinister purpose. This was the opening scene of the day on which he had determined that no mistakes should be made, and here at the outset he had allowed himself to be identified with a place of notorious ill-repute.

最后这几句话声音又响，又带着威吓。街上的行人纷纷侧目张望，丹尼斯明白再争辩下去也是徒劳。在这些精明却心胸狭窄的生意人眼里——他们自己也未必有多诚实，见惯了世间的狡诈伎俩，却少见几分真诚美德——一旦对谁起了疑心，那便是无可挽回的绝症；更何况对方是个来路不明、表面上又露了马脚的陌生人。

丹尼斯只觉得胸口几乎被愤怒、失望和屈辱胀裂，匆匆转身离去。人家把他当成了小偷，往好了说也不过是个骗子，疑心他是怀着什么歹意来谋这差事的。这便是他立誓绝不再犯错的那一天的开场戏，可才刚一迈步，他竟让人把自个儿和那样一个臭名昭著的腌臜去处扯在了一起。

Dennis was almost speechless from indignation. Conscious of strength, his strong impulse for a moment was to spring at the throat of the barkeeper and vent his rage on him. Dennis Fleet, the son of Ethel Fleet, brawling, fighting in a barroom, a gambling-den, and going out to seek a situation that required confidence and fair-appearing, all blackened, bruised, and bleeding!
