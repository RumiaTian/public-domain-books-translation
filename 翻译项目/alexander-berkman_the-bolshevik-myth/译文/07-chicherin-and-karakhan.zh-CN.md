## VI Chicherin and Karakhan / VI 契切林与卡拉汉
===Original===

*February 24.*﻿—It was 3 a.m. In the Foreign Office correspondents were about and visitors come by appointment with Chicherin. The People’s Commissar for Foreign Affairs has turned night into day.
I found Chicherin at a desk in a large, cold office, an old shawl wrapped around his neck. Almost his first question was “how soon the revolution could be expected in the States.” When I replied that the American workers were still too much under the influence of the reactionary leaders, he called me pessimistic. In a revolutionary time like the present, he thought, even the Federation of Labor must quickly change to a more radical attitude. He was very hopeful of revolutionary developments in England and America in the near future.
We discussed the Industrial Workers of the World, Chicherin saying that he believed I exaggerated their importance as the only revolutionary proletarian movement in America. He considered the Communist Party in that country of far greater influence and significance. He had recently seen several American Communists, he explained, and they informed him on the labor and revolutionary situation in the States.
A clerk entered with a typed sheet. Chicherin scanned it carefully, and began making corrections. His neck shawl kept sliding down on the paper, and impatiently he would throw it back over his shoulder. He read the document again, made more corrections, and looked displeased. “Terribly confused,” he muttered irritably.
“I’ll have it retyped at once,” the clerk said, picking up the paper.
Chicherin impatiently snatched it out of his hand, and without another word his lean, bent figure disappeared through the door. I heard his short, nervous step in the corridor.
“We are used to his ways,” the clerk remarked apologetically.
“I met him on the stairs without hat or coat when I came up,” I said.
“He is all the time between the second and the fourth floor,” the clerk laughed. “He insists on taking every paper to the radio room himself.”
Chicherin returned all out of breath, and took up the conversation again. Messengers and telephone kept interrupting us, Chicherin personally answering every call. He looked worried and preoccupied, with difficulty picking up the thread of our talk.
“We must bend every effort toward recognition,” be said presently, “and especially to lift the blockade.”

*2月24日。*——当时正是凌晨3点（3 a.m.）。外交人民委员部大楼里随处可见各国记者的身影，还有按约前来拜会契切林（Chicherin）的各方访客。这位外交人民委员完全把黑夜当成了白昼。
我在一间宽大而阴冷透骨的办公室里见到了契切林，他伏在一张办公桌前，脖颈上紧紧围着一条破旧的羊毛披肩。他开口向我提出的第一个问题几乎就是：“美利坚预计何时能爆发革命？”当我回答说美国工人阶级目前仍深受反动工运领袖的深重影响时，他直言我未免太过悲观。在他看来，在眼下这种急风暴雨般的革命时代，哪怕是美国劳工联合会（Federation of Labor）也必将迅速转向更为激进的立场。他对英美两国在不久的将来爆发革命的前景寄予了极大的厚望。
我们探讨了世界产业工人同盟（I.W.W.），契切林直言，他认为我把该组织视为美利坚唯一一支革命无产阶级力量，显然是夸大了其历史重要性。他认为美国共产党（Communist Party）在该国拥有大得多的影响力和历史意义。他解释说，自己最近刚刚接见过几位美共党人，他们向他全面汇报了美利坚当前的工人运动与革命态势。
一名办事员手拿一份打字机打印的文稿走了进来。契切林仔仔细细扫视着文稿，随即开始提笔修改。他脖子上的披肩三番五次滑落到稿纸上，他总是不耐烦地猛然一把将其甩回肩头。他将文件重读了一遍，又增补了数处修改，脸上的神色愈发阴沉不悦。“简直一团混乱！”他焦躁地喃喃自语道。
“我立刻让人重新打印一份，”办事员一边拿起文件一边说道。
契切林却不耐烦地一把从办事员手中夺过文稿，未再吐露半个字，便拖着瘦削而微偻的身躯快步冲出了大门。走廊里回荡起他短促而神经质的匆忙脚步声。
“我们早就习惯了他这种工作作风，”办事员略带歉意地向我解释道。
“我刚才上楼时，在楼梯上也撞见了他，连帽子和大衣都没穿，”我说道。
“他整天都在2楼到4楼（second and the fourth floor）之间来回飞奔，”办事员苦笑着说，“他坚持每一份电文都必须亲自送到无线电发报室去。”
不多时，契切林气喘吁吁地跑了回来，重新恢复了先前的交谈。然而通信员的通报与电话铃声接二连三打断我们的谈话，每一通电话契切林都坚持亲自接听。他神情焦虑、心事重重，好不容易才重新理清我们谈话的头绪。
“我们当前必须倾尽一切努力争取获得国际承认，”他顿了顿说道，“尤其是打破并解除帝国主义的经济封锁。”

He hoped much in that direction from the friendly attitude of the workers abroad, and he was pleased to hear of the growing sentiment in the United States for the recall of American troops from Siberia.
“No one wants peace so much as Russia,” he said emphatically. “If the Allies would come to their senses, we would soon enter into commerce with them. We know that business in England and America is eager for such an opportunity.”
“The trouble with the Allies,” he continued, “is that they don’t want to realize that we have the country back of us. They still cling to the hope of some White general rallying the people to his banner. A vain and stupid hope, for Russia is solidly for the Soviet Government.”
I related to Chicherin the experience of the *Buford* deportees on the Finnish border, and repeated to him the request of a certain American correspondent I had met there to be admitted to Russia.
“He is from a bourgeois newspaper,” Chicherin remarked, recalling that the man had been refused a Soviet visa. “On what ground does he apply again?”
“He asked me to tell you that his newspaper was the first in America to take a friendly attitude to the Bolsheviki.”
Chicherin became interested, and promised to consider the application.
“I also need some ‘paper’ from you,” I remarked jestingly, explaining that I was probably the only person in Soviet Russia without “documents,” as I had left Petrograd before they were issued to the *Buford* deportees. He laughed at my being “unidentified,” and recalled the mass meeting of Kronstadt sailors and workers in the Tshinizelli Circus in Petrograd, in 1917, to protest against my being “identified” with the Mooney case and extradited to California.
He ordered the clerk to prepare a “little paper” for me, and he signed it, remarking that there was much work in the Foreign Office, and that he hoped I would help with translations.
When I looked at the document I saw that it referred in very favorable terms to the “well-known American revolutionist,” but that there was no mention of my being an Anarchist. Was that term avoided purposely, I wondered? What cause would there be for it in Soviet Russia? I felt as if a veil were stealthily drawn over my personality.
---
Later in the day I visited Karakhan. Tall, good-looking, and well-groomed, he sat leisurely in a sumptuous office, his feet resting on a fine tiger skin.

===Chinese===

他将打破封锁的莫大希望寄托于海外工人阶级对苏俄的友好声援态度；获悉美利坚国内要求从西伯利亚撤回美军部队的呼声日益高涨，他感到十分欣慰。
“全世界没有任何一个国家像苏维埃俄国这样渴望和平，”他语气极其笃定地说道，“只要协约国头脑清醒过来，我们很快就能同他们开展双边经贸往来。我们深知英美两国的工商界对这种通商良机早已望眼欲穿。”
“协约国的大毛病在于，”他接着说道，“他们始终不肯正视全俄广大人民是坚决拥护我们的这一铁的事实。他们依然死抱幻想，盼着哪个白匪头子能振臂一呼、聚拢民众归顺其反动旗帜。这纯粹是痴人说梦、愚不可及，因为全俄人民是铁板一块坚决拥护苏维埃政权的。”
我向契切林详述了“布福德号”（*Buford*）遣返同胞在芬兰边境的遭遇，并向他转达了我在边境结识的一位美国记者的入境申请。
“他是资产阶级报社派来的记者，”契切林忆及此人此前曾被苏维埃拒签，随即问道，“他凭什么再次提出申请呢？”
“他托我转告您，他的报社当年在全美是第一家对布尔什维克采取友善客观立场的报纸。”
契切林顿时来了兴致，当场应允重新慎重考虑这项申请。
“另外我也急需从您这里讨一份‘公文条子’，”我半开玩笑地说道，向他解释说我大概是整个苏维埃俄国唯一一个没有合法“身份文件”的人，因为在彼得格勒（Petrograd）向“布福德号”人员统一发证之前我就已经启程离开了。听闻我是个“身份未明者”，他不禁哑然失笑，并深情忆及1917年在彼得格勒奇尼泽利马戏院（Tshinizelli Circus），喀琅施塔得（Kronstadt）的水兵与工人们举行万人群众大会、严正抗议我被无端卷入穆尼冤案并险遭引渡去加利福尼亚的往事。
他吩咐办事员当即为我草拟了一份“小证明信”，并提笔签了字，随口说道外交人民委员部公文堆积如山，十分期盼我能协助分担一些英译汉或互译工作。
接过文件一看，我发现信中以极其赞许推崇的措辞称我为“美利坚著名革命家”，却只字未提我是一名安那其主义者。我不禁暗自思忖：他们是有意回避这个词吗？在堂堂苏维埃俄国，究竟有何必要避讳这一点呢？我心中隐隐感到，仿佛有一层无形的纱幔被悄无声息地笼罩在了我的人格本色之上。
---
当天晚些时候，我前往拜会了卡拉汉（Karakhan）。他身材颀长英挺，相貌堂堂，仪表修饰得无可挑剔；此时正神态悠闲地端坐在一间极尽奢华的宽大办公室里，双脚踏在一张精美的华贵虎皮地毯上。

===Original===

His appearance justified the humorous characterization I heard of him in the anteroom. “A Bolshevik who can wear white gloves gracefully,” someone had said.
Karakhan asked me to converse in Russian. “Nature has given me no talent for languages,” he remarked. We discussed the labor situation abroad, and he expressed himself confident of the speedy bankruptcy of international capitalism. He was enthusiastic about the “growing influence of the Communist Party in England and America,” and seemed much displeased when I pointed out that his optimism was entirely unjustified by the actual state of affairs. He listened with a smile of well-bred incredulity as I spoke of the reaction following the war and the persecution of radicalism, in the States. “But the workers of England and America, inspired by the Communists, will presently force their governments to lift the blockade,” he insisted. I sought to impress him that Russia must make up her mind to rely upon herself for the reconstruction of her economic life. “Of course, of course,” he assented, but there was no conviction in his tone.
“Our hope is in the lifting of the blockade,” he said again, “and then our industries will develop quickly. At present we are handicapped by the lack of machinery and skilled labor.”
Referring to the peasantry, Karakhan asserted that the farmer profited by the Revolution more than any other part of the population. “Why,” he exclaimed, “in the villages you will find upholstered furniture, French mirrors, graphophones, and pianos, all given to them by the city in exchange for food. The luxuries of the mansion have been transferred to the hovel,” he laughed, pleased with his bon mot and gracefully stroking his well-trimmed black beard. “We have declared ‘war to the palaces, peace to the huts,’ ” he continued, “and the *muzhik* lives like a *barin*2 now. But the Russian peasant is backward and deeply imbued with the petty bourgeois spirit of ownership. The *kulaki*3 often refuse to contribute of their surplus, but the Army and the city proletariat must be fed, of course. We have therefore been compelled to resort to the *razvyorstka*4﻿—an unpleasant system, forced upon us by the Allied blockade. The peasants must do their share to sustain the soldiers and the workers who are the vanguard of the Revolution, and on the whole they do so.

===Chinese===

他的这副翩翩风度，恰好印证了我在接待室里听到的那句关于他的诙谐打趣：“这是一位戴得起、也戴得惯白手套的布尔什维克。”
卡拉汉请我用俄语同他交谈。“老天爷没赏给我学外语的天赋，”他自嘲道。我们畅谈了海外的工人运动态势，他对国际资本主义行将迅速破产充满坚如磐石的信心。他对“共产党在英美两国与日俱增的巨大影响力”赞不绝口；而当我直言不讳指出，从客观现实状况来看他的这种乐观情绪完全站不住脚时，他显得十分不悦。当我向他陈述美利坚战后反动狂潮的疯狂反扑以及对激进派的残酷政治迫害时，他只是带着一种受过良好教养的怀疑微笑静静听着。“但是英美两国的产业工人受到共产党的感召，很快就会以强硬手段迫使其反动政府解除封锁，”他固执己见地强调道。我试图让他认清一个现实：在重建自身国民经济的道路上，苏维埃俄国必须下定决心走完全依靠自身力量的自力更生之路。“那是自然，那是自然，”他随口附和道，但语调中却感受不到半点坚定的信念。
“我们的首要厚望仍寄托在封锁的彻底解除上，”他又重申道，“届时我们的现代工业必将实现一日千里的飞跃。眼下我们受制于机器设备的极度匮乏和熟练技术工人的严重短缺。”
在谈及广大农民阶级时，卡拉汉断言，农民是这场伟大革命中获益最为丰厚的人群。“何止如此！”他兴致勃勃地高声说道，“在如今的乡村农舍里，你能见到高档沙发软垫家具、法式全身穿衣镜、留声机甚至昂贵的钢琴——这一切全是城市居民为了换取糊口粮食而奉送给乡下的。昔日深宅大院里的奢华珍玩，如今全搬进了庄稼汉的低矮茅舍！”他开怀大笑起来，显然对自己的这句妙语得意洋洋，一面优雅地抚弄着修剪整齐的黑色胡须。“我们庄严宣告了‘对宫殿开战，给茅屋和平！’”他接着说道，“如今的庄稼汉/农夫（*muzhik*）日子过得赛过旧社会的老爷2（*barin*）。然而俄国农民思想落后闭塞，骨子里深受小资产阶级私有观念的毒害。富农3（*kulaki*）经常死守余粮拒不缴纳，可是前线红军部队和城市的工人阶级总得张嘴吃饭啊！因此我们迫不得已，只能推行余粮征集制4（*razvyorstka*）——这是一项令人不悦的严苛制度，完全是被协约国反动派的无情封锁硬逼出来的。农民必须承担起自己的一份历史责任，以供养作为革命先锋主力军的工农子弟兵和产业工人；总的来看，他们大体上还是遵照执行的。”

Occasionally the *muzhiki* resist requisition, and in such cases the military is called upon. Unfortunate occurrences, but not very frequent. They usually happen in the Ukraine, our richest wheat and corn region﻿—the peasants are mostly *kulaki* there.”
Karakhan lit a cigar and continued: “Of course, when requisition is made, the Government pays. That is, it gives the peasant its written obligation, as proof of its good faith. Those ‘papers’ will be honored as soon as civil war is over, and our economic life put in order.”
The conversation turned to the recent arrests in Moscow in connection with a counterrevolutionary conspiracy unearthed by the Cheka. “Oh, yes,” Karakhan smiled, “they are still plotting.” He grew thoughtful for a moment, then added: “We abolished capital punishment, but in certain cases exceptions have to be made.”
He leaned comfortably back in his armchair and continued:
“One mustn’t be sentimental. I remember how hard it was for me, way back in 1917, when I myself had to arrest my former college chums. Yes, with my own hands”﻿—he held out both hands, white and well-cared for﻿—“but what will you? The Revolution imposes stern duties upon us. We mustn’t be sentimental,” he repeated.
The subject changed to India, Karakhan remarking that a delegate had just arrived from that country. The movement there was revolutionary, though of nationalistic character, he thought, and could be exploited to keep England in check. Learning that while in California I was in touch with Hindu revolutionists and Anarchists of the Hindustan Gadar organization, he suggested the advisability of getting in communication with them. I promised to look after the matter.

“偶尔也有个别庄稼汉（*muzhiki*）暴力抗拒征粮，遇到这种恶性事态，就不得不动用军队武力镇压了。这确实令人痛心，但好在并不算十分频繁。这种流血冲突通常多发于乌克兰（Ukraine），那里是我们最富饶的小麦与玉米粮仓——当地的农户绝大多数都是顽固的富农（*kulaki*）。”
卡拉汉点燃了一支雪茄，接着说道：“当然，在依法执行征粮时，政府是如数付账的。也就是说，政府会向农民开具具有法律效力的书面凭证，以此作为苏维埃政府守信的信誉担保。一旦国内革命战争宣告胜利结束、国家经济生活恢复正轨，这些‘借据凭单’定将全额兑现补偿。”
话题随后转到了莫斯科（Moscow）最近侦破的一起反革命叛乱阴谋所引发的系列大搜捕，该案系契卡（Cheka）一手破获。“哦，可不是嘛，”卡拉汉微微一笑，“那帮家伙直到现在依然在暗中密谋暴动。”他沉吟片刻，随即补充道：“我们虽然在法律上早已废除了死刑，但在某些极端特殊的大案要案面前，有时也不得不破例处置。”
他悠然自得地往扶手大皮椅深处靠了靠，继续侃侃而谈：
“干革命绝不能感情用事。我还清晰记得早在1917年那阵子，当我自己不得不亲手下令逮捕我当年的大学同窗老友时，内心是何等的痛苦纠结。是的，正是用这双手亲手抓捕的，”——他摊开双手，十指修长洁白、保养得极尽细腻——“然而，又能如何呢？伟大革命赋予我们铁一般的冷酷使命。我们决不能妇人之仁、感情用事，”他又刻意重复了一遍。
随后话题转向了印度问题，卡拉汉提及一位来自印度的代表刚刚抵京。在他看来，那里的反帝反殖运动虽然带有强烈的民族主义色彩，但本质上依然属于革命范畴，完全可以善加利用以牵制遏制大英帝国。获悉我在加利福尼亚期间曾同印度革命党人以及“印度起义党”（Gadar）的安那其主义者有过密切往来，他当即建议不妨同他们重新建立秘密联络。我答应他会亲自跟进这项事宜。
