## XXVI My Father Reaches Home, and Dies Not Long Afterwards / 二十六、我父亲回到家中，不久之后溘然长逝

===Original===
My father could walk but slowly, for George’s boots had blistered his feet, and it seemed to him that the riverbed, of which he caught glimpses now and again, never got any nearer; but all things come to an end, and by seven o’clock on the night of Tuesday, he was on the spot which he had left on the preceding Friday morning. Three entire days had intervened, but he felt that something, he knew not what, had seized him, and that whereas before these three days life had been one thing, what little might follow them, would be another﻿—and a very different one.

He soon caught sight of his horse which had strayed a mile lower down the riverbed, and in spite of his hobbles had crossed one ugly stream that my father dared not ford on foot. Tired though he was, he went after him, bridle in hand, and when the friendly creature saw him, it recrossed the stream, and came to him of its own accord﻿—either tired of his own company, or tempted by some bread my father held out towards him. My father took off the hobbles, and rode him barebacked to the camping ground, where he rewarded him with more bread and biscuit, and then hobbled him again for the night.

===Chinese===
我父亲只能缓缓而行，因为乔治的靴子把他的脚磨起了泡；那河床他时不时瞥上一眼，却似乎永远不见近前。然而万事终有尽头，到星期二晚上七点钟，他已回到前一个星期五早晨离开的那处地方。中间整整隔了三天，可他感到有什么东西——他说不清是什么——已经攫住了他；在这三天之前，人生是一回事，此后无论还余下多少，都将是另一回事——而且是大不相同的一回事。

他很快便望见了自己的马。那马顺着河床往下游荡了一英里，尽管绊着腿，竟也蹚过了一条他自己不敢徒步涉过的险恶水流。他尽管疲惫，仍旧手里提着笼头追了上去；那友善的牲口一见他，便重新渡了回来，自己走到他跟前——要么是独自待得腻烦了，要么是受了我父亲朝它递过去的面包的引诱。我父亲解下绊马索，不备鞍子骑回宿营地，又拿面包和硬饼犒劳它，随后照旧绊住它的腿过夜。

===Original===
“It was here,” he said to me on one of the first days after his return, “that I first knew myself to be a broken man. As for meeting George again, I felt sure that it would be all I could do to meet his brother; and though George was always in my thoughts, it was for you and not him that I was now yearning. When I gave George my watch, how glad I was that I had left my gold one at home, for that is yours, and I could not have brought myself to give it him.”

“Never mind that, my dear father,” said I, “but tell me how you got down the river, and thence home again.”

“My very dear boy,” he said, “I can hardly remember, and I had no energy to make any more notes. I remember putting a scrap of paper into the box of sovereigns, merely sending George my love along with the money; I remember also dropping the box into a hole in a tree, which I blazed, and towards which I drew a line of wood-ashes. I seem to see a poor unhinged creature gazing moodily for hours into a fire which he heaps up now and again with wood. There is not a breath of air; Nature sleeps so calmly that she dares not even breathe for fear of waking; the very river has hushed his flow. Without, the starlit calm of a summer’s night in a great wilderness; within, a hurricane of wild and incoherent thoughts battling with one another in their fury to fall upon him and rend him﻿—and on the other side the great wall of mountain, thousands of children praying at their mother’s knee to this poor dazed thing. I suppose this half delirious wretch must have been myself. But I must have been more ill when I left England than I thought I was, or Erewhon would not have broken me down as it did.”

===Chinese===
「就是在这儿，」他回来之后最初几天里，有一天对我这样说，「我头一回明白自己已经是个垮掉了的人。至于再见乔治一面，我怕连见上他弟弟一面，都已是竭尽我所能了；乔治虽时时在我心头，可我如今渴念的是你，不是他。我把手表给了乔治的时候，我多么庆幸那块金表留在了家里——那是你的，我是万万舍不得给他的。」

「别把这事放在心上，亲爱的父亲，」我说，「只告诉我您是怎样沿河下来的，又怎样从那里回到家里。」

「我的好孩子，」他说，「我几乎记不得了，也没有气力再记什么笔记。我记得往那匣金镑里放进一张纸条，只说随这笔钱附上我对乔治的爱；我还记得把匣子放进一个树洞里，在那棵树上砍了记号，又撒出一线木灰指向它。我仿佛看见一个心神散乱的可怜人，郁郁地一连好几个钟头凝望着一堆火，不时往火里添些柴。四下没有一丝风；大自然睡得那样安稳，连大气也不敢喘，惟恐惊醒自己；连那条河也止住了流淌。身外，是茫茫荒野里夏夜星光下的岑寂；身内，是一场狂乱而支离的思绪的风暴，在暴怒中相互厮杀，要扑上这个人、把他撕碎——而在另一面，是那一堵大山的高墙，成千上万的孩子跪在母亲膝前，向这个可怜的、神志昏乱的生灵祈祷。我想，这个半癫狂的可怜虫必定就是我自己。可我离开英格兰时，必定已病得比自己以为的更重，否则埃瑞洪不会把我摧折成那个样子。」

===Original===
No doubt he was right. Indeed it was because Mr. Cathie and his doctor saw that he was out of health and in urgent need of change, that they left off opposing his wish to travel. There is no use, however, in talking about this now.

I never got from him how he managed to reach the shepherd’s hut, but I learned some little from the shepherd, when I stayed with him both on going towards Erewhon, and on returning.

“He did not seem to have drink in him,” said the shepherd, “when he first came here; but he must have been pretty full of it, or he must have had some bottles in his saddlebags; for he was awful when he came back. He had got them worse than any man I ever saw, only that he was not awkward. He said there was a bird flying out of a giant’s mouth and laughing at him, and he kept muttering about a blue pool, and hanky-panky of all sorts, and he said he knew it was all hanky-panky, at least I thought he said so, but it was no use trying to follow him, for it was all nothing but horrors. He said I was to stop the people from trying to worship him. Then he said the sky opened and he could see the angels going about and singing ‘Hallelujah.’ ”

===Chinese===
无疑他是说得对的。事实上，正因为凯西先生和他的医生看出他健康亏损、亟需换换环境，才不再反对他远行的愿望。不过，如今再谈这些也无益了。

他究竟怎样设法走到牧羊人的小屋，我从没能从他口中问出来；倒是我去埃瑞洪时和回来时都在牧羊人那里住过，从他嘴里零零碎碎听得了一些。

「他初来的时候，」牧羊人说，「瞧着并不像喝了酒；可他必定灌了个满腹，再不然就是鞍囊里藏了几瓶；因为他回来那阵子真是吓人。那毛病发作得比我平生所见的任何人都凶，只亏得他手脚还算安稳。他说有一只鸟从一张巨人的嘴里飞出来，冲他嘲笑；他又不住口地咕哝什么蓝池，什么汉基潘基（hanky-panky）的种种把戏，还说他知道那全是汉基潘基——至少我觉着他是这么说的；可想跟上他的话却是白费，因为他嘴里没别的，尽是吓人的光景。他嘱咐我拦住众人，别叫他们想去崇拜他。后来他又说天开了，他看得见天使们来来往往，唱着『哈利路亚』。」

===Original===
“How long did he stay with you?” I asked.

“About ten days, but the last three he was himself again, only too weak to move. He thought he was cured except for weakness.”

“Do you know how he had been spending the last two days or so before he got down to your hut?”

I said two days, because this was the time I supposed he would take to descend the river.

“I should say drinking all the time. He said he had fallen off his horse two or three times, till he took to leading him. If he had had any other horse than old Doctor he would have been a dead man. Bless you, I have known that horse ever since he was foaled, and I never saw one like him for sense. He would pick fords better than that gentleman could, I know, and if the gentleman fell off him he would just stay stock still. He was badly bruised, poor man, when he got here. I saw him through the gorge when he left me, and he gave me a sovereign; he said he had only one other left to take him down to the port, or he would have made it more.”

===Chinese===
「他在您这里住了多久？」我问。

「约莫十天；只是最后三天他神志又清明了，只是虚弱得动弹不得。他只当病已经好了，剩下没气力而已。」

「您可知道，在他下到您这小屋之前的最后两天前后，他是怎么打发的？」

我说两天，因为据我估量，他沿河下行正需要这些工夫。

「照我看，他是一路喝过来的。他说他从马背上摔下来两三回，后来索性牵着马走。他骑的若是别的什么马，而不是那匹老『医生』（Doctor），他早就没命了。我的老天，那匹马从落地起我就认得，论灵性我再没见过像它那样的。它挑涉口，比那位先生挑得还准，这我知道；那位先生若从它背上摔下来，它便一动不动地站定。他到这儿的时候，浑身乌青，怪可怜的。他走时，我望着他穿出峡谷才回身；他给了我一枚金镑，说身上只剩一枚要带他下到港口去，不然还要多给些。」

===Original===
“He was my father,” said I, “and he is dead, but before he died he told me to give you five pounds which I have brought you. I think you are wrong in saying that he had been drinking.”

“That is what they all say; but I take it very kind of him to have thought of me.”

My father’s illness for the first three weeks after his return played with him as a cat plays with a mouse; now and again it would let him have a day or two’s run, during which he was so cheerful and unclouded that his doctor was quite hopeful about him. At various times on these occasions I got from him that when he left the shepherd’s hut, he thought his illness had run itself out, and that he should now reach the port from which he was to sail for S. Francisco without misadventure. This he did, and he was able to do all he had to do at the port, though frequently attacked with passing fits of giddiness. I need not dwell upon his voyage to S. Francisco, and thence home; it is enough to say that he was able to travel by himself in spite of gradually, but continually, increasing failure.

===Chinese===
「他是我的父亲，」我说，「他如今已经故去了；但他临终前吩咐我给您送五个英镑来，我已带来了。只是您说他一直在喝酒，我想您是说错了。」

「人家个个都这么说；不过他还惦记着我，我领他这份情。」

我父亲归来后的头三个星期，病魔摆弄他，活像猫儿摆弄老鼠；时不时放他有一两天奔逃的工夫，其间他那样开朗，神志那样清明，医生对他颇为乐观。我趁着这些时候，陆陆续续从他口中得知：离开牧羊人的小屋时，他以为自己的病已经发作完了，此后去那要搭船驶往旧金山（S. Francisco）的港口，当可平安无事。他果然到了，在港口把该办的事也都办妥了，尽管时时被一阵阵忽来的眩晕侵袭。他从旧金山渡海返乡的一路，我不必细述；只说一句就够了：尽管体力在渐渐然而不休地衰竭，他终究还能只身成行。

===Original===
“When,” he said, “I reached the port, I telegraphed as you know, for more money. How puzzled you must have been. I sold my horse to the man from whom I bought it, at a loss of only about £10, and I left with him my saddle, saddlebags, small hatchet, my hobbles, and in fact everything that I had taken with me, except what they had impounded in Erewhon. Yram’s rug I dropped into the river when I knew that I should no longer need it﻿—as also her substitutes for my billy and pannikin; and I burned her basket. The shepherd would have asked me questions. You will find an order to deliver everything up to bearer. You need therefore take nothing from England.”

At another time he said, “When you go, for it is plain I cannot, and go one or other of us must, try and get the horse I had: he will be nine years old, and he knows all about the rivers: if you leave everything to him, you may shut your eyes, but do not interfere with him. Give the shepherd what I said and he will attend to you, but go a day or two too soon, for the margin of one day was not enough to allow in case of a fresh in the river; if the water is discoloured you must not cross it﻿—not even with Doctor. I could not ask George to come up three days running from Sunch’ston to the statues and back.”

Here he became exhausted. Almost the last coherent string of sentences I got from him was as follows:﻿—

===Chinese===
「我到了港口，」他说，「便如你所知，拍电报去再要钱。你准是纳闷得很。我把马卖还给当初卖马给我的那人，只亏折了约 10 英镑；又把马鞍、鞍囊、小斧、绊马索，总之一切随身携去之物，都留在了他那里——在埃瑞洪被扣下的除外。雅拉姆那条毯子，等我确知再用不着了，便丢进了河里——她用来替换我那比利壶和小铁杯的东西也一并丢了；她的篮子，我烧掉了。不然牧羊人定要问长问短。你会找到一张凭单，命人将一切物件交与持单之人。因此你不必从英格兰带任何东西去。」

另有一次他说：「你去的时候——我去不了是明摆着的，而我们两个总得去一个——务必要弄到我那匹马：到那时它九岁了，这些河道它无所不晓；你只消事事由着它，闭着眼睛也成，只是千万别去干预它。照我先前说的给牧羊人，他自会照应你；不过宁可提前一两天到，因为只留一天的富余，河水一涨便不够应付；水若是浑了，就断断不可渡河——骑着『医生』也不行。我总不能要乔治从桑奇斯顿赶到雕像那里再返回，一连三天日日如此。」

说到这里，他已气力不支。我从他口中得到的最后一段连贯通顺的话，差不多是这样的：——

“About George’s money if I send him £2,000 you will still have nearly £150,000 left, and Mr. Cathie will not let you try to make it more. I know you would give him four or five thousand, but the Mayor and I talked it over, and settled that £2,000 in gold would make him a rich man. Consult our good friend Alfred” (meaning, of course, Mr. Cathie) “about the best way of taking the money. I am afraid there is nothing for it but gold, and this will be a great weight for you to carry﻿—about, I believe 36 lbs. Can you do this? I really think that if you lead your horse you﻿ ﻿… no﻿—there will be the getting him down again﻿—”

“Don’t worry about it, my dear father,” said I, “I can do it easily if I stow the load rightly, and I will see to this. I shall have nothing else to carry, for I shall camp down below both morning and evening. But would you not like to send some present to the Mayor, Yram, their other children, and Mrs. Humdrum’s granddaughter?”

“Do what you can,” said my father. And these were the last instructions he gave me about those adventures with which alone this work is concerned.

The day before he died, he had a little flicker of intelligence, but all of a sudden his face became clouded as with great anxiety; he seemed to see some horrible chasm in front of him which he had to cross, or which he feared that I must cross, for he gasped out words, which, as near as I could catch them, were, “Look out! John! Leap! Leap! Le﻿ ﻿…” but he could not say all that he was trying to say and closed his eyes, having, as I then deemed, seen that he was on the brink of that gulf which lies between life and death; I took it that in reality he died at that moment; for there was neither struggle, nor hardly movement of any kind afterwards﻿—nothing but a pulse which for the next several hours grew fainter and fainter so gradually, that it was not till some time after it had ceased to beat that we were certain of its having done so.

===Chinese===
「关于乔治的钱：我若寄他 2,000 英镑，你手里还剩将近 150,000 英镑，凯西先生不会容你再设法增添。我知道你会想给他四五千，可我和市长商议定了，2,000 英镑金币已足以叫他成为富人。去请教我们的好朋友阿尔弗雷德」（自然是指凯西先生）「，问这笔钱怎样携带最妥。只怕除了金币别无良策，这于你将是个不小的重负——我想约莫有 36 磅。这个你办得到么？我实在想，你若牵着你的马走，你……不——还有把它再带下去这一层——」

「别为这个操心，亲爱的父亲，」我说，「只要把驮载安放停当，这事我轻易便能办到，我自会料理。此外我什么也不用驮，因为早晚我都在下面扎营。只是您不想给市长、雅拉姆、他们其余的孩子们，还有亨德伦太太的孙女，捎些礼物去么？」

「随你的力吧。」我父亲说。关于本书所专述的那番奇遇，这便是他给我的最后的嘱托了。

去世的前一天，他有过一星短暂的神智回光，可忽然间脸上阴云四合，像是怀着极大的忧惧；他似乎看见面前横着一道可怕的深渊，须得他自己跨过去，又或是他害怕须由我跨过去，因为他喘着气迸出几个字来，我勉强听出的，是：「留神！约翰！跳！跳！跳……」可他想说的话终究没能说尽，便阖上了眼；我当时以为，他已望见自己立在生死之间那道鸿沟的边沿。我把那一刻当作他真正死去的时刻；因为此后既无挣扎，也几乎没有任何动弹——只剩一缕脉搏，在随后的几个钟头里渐渐微弱下去，弱得那样缓慢，直到它停歇了好一会儿之后，我们才敢断定它已经停了。
