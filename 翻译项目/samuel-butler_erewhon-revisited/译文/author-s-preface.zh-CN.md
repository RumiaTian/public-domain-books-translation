## Author’s Preface / 作者序

===Original===
I forget when, but not very long after I had published *Erewhon* in 1872, it occurred to me to ask myself what course events in Erewhon would probably take after Mr. Higgs, as I suppose I may now call him, had made his escape in the balloon with Arowhena. Given a people in the conditions supposed to exist in Erewhon, and given the apparently miraculous ascent of a remarkable stranger into the heavens with an earthly bride﻿—what would be the effect on the people generally?

There was no use in trying to solve this problem before, say, twenty years should have given time for Erewhonian developments to assume something like permanent shape, and in 1892 I was too busy with books now published to be able to attend to Erewhon. It was not till the early winter of 1900, i.e. as nearly as may be thirty years after the date of Higgs’s escape, that I found time to deal with the question above stated, and to answer it, according to my lights, in the book which I now lay before the public.

I have concluded, I believe rightly, that the events described in Chapter XXIV of *Erewhon* would give rise to such a cataclysmic change in the old Erewhonian opinions as would result in the development of a new religion. Now the development of all new religions follows much the same general course. In all cases the times are more or less out of joint﻿—older faiths are losing their hold upon the masses. At such times, let a personality appear, strong in itself, and made to seem still stronger by association with some supposed transcendent miracle, and it will be easy to raise a Lo here! that will attract many followers. If there be a single great, and apparently well-authenticated, miracle, others will accrete round it; then, in all religions that have so originated, there will follow temples, priests, rites, sincere believers, and unscrupulous exploiters of public credulity. To chronicle the events that followed Higgs’s balloon ascent without showing that they were much as they have been under like conditions in other places, would be to hold the mirror up to something very wide of nature.

已记不清确切是什么时候了，总之在我于 1872 年出版《埃瑞洪》（*Erewhon*）之后不太久，我忽然想到要问问自己：在希格斯先生（Mr. Higgs）——我想如今总可以这样称呼他了——带着阿罗温娜（Arowhena）乘气球逃走之后，埃瑞洪的事态大概会怎样发展？且设想一个民族正处在《埃瑞洪》所假定的那种境况之中，又设想一位卓异的外乡人携着一位尘世的新娘，以显然不可思议的方式升上了天——这对一般民众会发生什么影响呢？

这个问题，非得等上——姑且说——二十年，让埃瑞洪的种种演变有时间大致定形，否则着手求解也是徒然；而 1892 年我又忙于几部如今均已出版的书，腾不出工夫来照管埃瑞洪。直到 1900 年初冬——也就是说，距希格斯逃走之日差不多正好三十年——我才得闲处理上述问题，并按照自己有限的见识，在如今呈于公众面前的这本书里作答。

我得出的结论——我相信并没有下错——是：《埃瑞洪》第二十四章所描写的那些事件，势必在埃瑞洪旧日的观念里激起一场天崩地裂的变革，结果便是一种新宗教的诞生。而一切新宗教的发育，走的都是大同小异的路。无论哪一桩，其时世总是多少「脱了节」——旧的信仰正失去对大众的把控。当此之际，只消有一位人物出场，其人本身已够坚强，再借同一桩想当然的超凡奇迹烘托，显得愈发坚强，那么要喊出一声「看哪，在这里！」（Lo here!）来招徕大批追随者，便易如反掌。只要有单独一件重大而且看来信而有征的奇迹，别的奇迹便会附着其上，滋蔓丛生；随后，凡循此例发端的宗教，都免不了殿宇、祭司、仪轨、笃信的信徒，以及利用公众轻信而肆无忌惮的牟利之徒，接踵而至。若是记述希格斯乘气球升天之后的种种事变，却不表明它们同别处类似境况之下发生过的事情大体相仿，那便无异于举起一面镜子，去照一种与真实人生相去悬远的东西。

Analogy, however, between courses of events is one thing﻿—historic parallelisms abound; analogy between the main actors in events is a very different one, and one, moreover, of which few examples can be found. The development of the new ideas in Erewhon is a familiar one, but there is no more likeness between Higgs and the founder of any other religion, than there is between Jesus Christ and Muhammad. He is a typical middle-class Englishman, deeply tainted with priggishness in his earlier years, but in great part freed from it by the sweet uses of adversity.

If I may be allowed for a moment to speak about myself, I would say that I have never ceased to profess myself a member of the more advanced wing of the English Broad Church. What those who belong to this wing believe, I believe. What they reject, I reject. No two people think absolutely alike on any subject, but when I converse with advanced Broad Churchmen I find myself in substantial harmony with them. I believe﻿—and should be very sorry if I did not believe﻿—that, mutatis mutandis, such men will find the advice given in chapters XXIV and XXV of this book much what, under the supposed circumstances, they would themselves give.

Lastly, I should express my great obligations to Mr. R. A. Streatfeild of the British Museum, who, in the absence from England of my friend Mr. H. Festing Jones, has kindly supervised the corrections of my book as it passed through the press.

然而，事变进程之间的类比是一回事——历史上这类平行先例比比皆是；事变中主要人物之间的类比却全然是另一回事，况且能举出的例子寥寥无几。新思想在埃瑞洪的这番发展是人所熟知的路数，但希格斯同其他任何宗教创始人的相似之处，并不比耶稣基督同穆罕默德的相似之处更多。他是个典型的英国中产阶级人士，早年深染自命清高的迂腐习气，所幸逆境自有它甘美的用处，使他大体上把这习气洗脱了。

倘若容我稍稍谈谈自己，那么我要说：我一向自认是英国广教会（Broad Church）中较为先进一翼的成员。这一翼的人信什么，我便信什么；他们摒弃什么，我也摒弃什么。无论对什么问题，都没有两个人想的绝对一样；但每逢我同先进的广教会人士交谈，总发觉自己与他们在大端上契合无间。我相信——倘若不信，我将引为深憾——只须作过必要的变通，这一类人便会发现，本书第二十四、二十五章所给的劝告，同他们在那种假想处境之下自己会提出的劝告，大体无异。

最后，我应当向大英博物馆的斯特雷特菲尔德先生（R. A. Streatfeild）深致谢忱：我的朋友费斯廷·琼斯先生（H. Festing Jones）离英期间，承他好意督理本书付印过程中的种种修订。
