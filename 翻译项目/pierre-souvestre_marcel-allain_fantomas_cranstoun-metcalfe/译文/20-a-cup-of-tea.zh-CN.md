## XX A Cup of Tea / 第二十章 一杯茶
===Original===

After the tragic death of her husband, Lady Beltham﻿—whose previous life had inclined to the austere﻿—withdrew into almost complete retirement. The world of gaiety and fashion knew her no more. But in the world where poverty and suffering reign, in hospital wards and squalid streets, a tall and beautiful woman might often be seen, robed all in black, with distinguished bearing and eyes serene and grave, distributing alms and consolation as she moved. It was Lady Beltham, kind, good and very pitiful, bent on the work of charity to which she had vowed her days.
Yet she had not allowed herself to be crushed by sorrow; after the tragedy which left her a widow, she had assumed the effective control of her husband’s property, and, helped by faithful friends, had carried on his interests and administered his estates, spreading a halo of kindness all around her.
To help her in the heavy correspondence entailed by all these affairs, she found three secretaries none too many. On M. Etienne Rambert’s recommendation, Thérèse Auvernois was now one of these, and the young girl was perfectly happy in her new surroundings; time was helping her to forget the tragedy which had taken her grandmother from her at Beaulieu, and she enjoyed the company of the wellborn, well-bred English gentlewomen.
Lady Beltham was reclining on a sofa in the great hall of her house at Neuilly. It was a spacious room, furnished half as a lounge and half as an office, and Lady Beltham liked to receive people there. A large glass-enclosed balcony commanded a view over the garden and the boulevard Richard Wallace beyond, with the Bois de Boulogne beyond that again. A few minutes before, a footman had brought in a table and set out tea-things, and Lady Beltham was reading while Thérèse and the two young English girls were chattering among themselves.
The telephone bell rang and Thérèse answered it.
“Hullo? Yes﻿ ﻿… yes: you want to know if you may call this evening? The Reverend﻿—oh, yes: you have just come from Scotland? Hold on a minute.” She turned to Lady Beltham. “It is Mr. William Hope, and he wants to know if you will see him tonight. He has just come from your place in Scotland.”
“The dear man!”

他呈递上傍晚送来的信件，姑娘们手脚麻利地从托盘里收拢起所有信封和报纸，着手分拣拆阅；大管家则同女主人低声回话，威廉·霍普牧师趁此契机道了晚安，起身告退。
来信大多不过是恳求金钱救济或物资援助的求助信，但利斯贝思将一封长信递到了贝尔特姆夫人手中。夫人瞥了一眼信末署名。
“啊，是埃蒂安·朗贝尔先生来信了，”她轻呼道。泰蕾兹本能地凑上前去，心知自己也能探听到这位老相识写了些什么；贝尔特姆夫人顺手将信笺塞到她手里。“亲爱的，你来读吧，读完回头再告诉我他说了些什么。”
泰蕾兹急不可耐地展信细读。埃蒂安·朗贝尔先生一周前动身离开了巴黎，踏上了一趟漫长而重要的远行。这位精神矍铄的老先生先是前去德国考察，随后打算从汉堡赶赴英格兰，在那儿代为处理贝尔特姆夫人的若干业务——他如今同夫人的信赖私交比以往任何时候都要密切。随后他打算从南安普敦扬帆启航，前往哥伦比亚度过寒冬，他在那儿有一大笔属于自己的重大利益需要亲自打理。
在泰蕾兹阅信之际，贝尔特姆夫人继续同她的大管家交谈。
“我很欣慰你今天下午去检查修缮了庄园大门，”她说，“你知道我向来胆子小、容易神经紧绷。我童年在苏格兰度过，孤苦伶仃无依无靠，从那时起我就对形单影只和无边黑夜怀有一种莫名的恐惧。”
大管家出言宽慰她，他对自己看家护院的本事向来胸有成竹。
“夫人完全无须担惊受怕；宅邸万无一失，防卫森严。门房沃尔特（Walter）是条一等一的看家好狗，夜里睡觉都睁着一只眼。还有我呢，也绝不会——”
“是的，我心里有数，西尔伯敦，”年轻的孀妇打断了他，“只要我容自己定下心来理智想想，我其实并不慌乱。多谢你；你眼下可以退下了。”
她转向三位年轻姑娘。
“我有些乏了，孩子们；咱们今晚就不熬夜了。”
利斯贝思和苏珊娜亲昵地吻了吻她，相继退去。泰蕾兹多耽搁了片刻，取来一本《圣经》，轻轻摆在贝尔特姆夫人座椅旁的几案上。贝尔特姆夫人把一只手轻抚在姑娘头顶，宛如给予赐福一般，柔声说道：
“晚安；愿上帝保佑你，亲爱的孩子！”
