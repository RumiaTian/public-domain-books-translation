## II · The Inquest / II · 验尸审讯

===Original===

When Ruth Averill awoke next morning she found that the overwhelming sense of sick horror which had weighed her down on the previous evening had lightened. She had been worn out in body from the shock and the nervous strain, but sleep had restored her physical well-being, and her mind reacted to her body. She was young, she was in perfect health, and﻿—she was in love.

===Chinese===

第二天早晨，露丝·埃弗里尔一觉醒来，发觉前一日傍晚沉沉压在心头的那股令人作呕的恐怖，已经减轻了几分。惊吓与神经的紧张本已使她身心俱疲，但一场酣睡恢复了她的体力，心境也随之跟着身体好转起来。她年轻，身子十分康健，而且——她在恋爱。

===Original===

While her feelings of compassion for the trio who had lost their lives in so terrible a way were in no whit lessened, she would have been less than human had she not begun to look upon the tragedy as it affected herself. And here at once was something exciting and a little terrifying. What would happen to her now? She had hated her life at Starvel; would the life that lay before her be better or worse? Scarcely worse, she thought; any change must surely be for the better. She had intended while at York to make some inquiries about earning her own living so that she might leave Starvel. Now this was no longer a matter of choice; in some way she must learn to support herself. Vaguely she wondered if any of her uncle’s money would come to her. But she dismissed the idea as too good to be true. Perhaps with luck there might be enough to keep her until she could train for some post, but even about this she could not be certain. However, Mr. Oxley was kind and clever. She need not worry overmuch. He would advise her.

===Chinese===

她对那三位惨遭横祸、丧生火窟之人的怜悯虽分毫未减，但若说她不曾开始从切身利害来看这场惨祸，那她便不是血肉凡人了。而这一看，立刻便有令人兴奋、又多少叫人心悸的东西。她如今会有怎样的遭遇呢？她素来憎恨斯塔维尔的生活；摆在眼前的日子，会更好些，还是更坏些？她想，再坏也坏不到哪里去；但凡变化，必定是往好里变。她在约克时本就打算打探一下自食其力的门路，好离开斯塔维尔；如今这已不是愿不愿的事，她无论如何总得学会养活自己。她也曾隐约想过，叔父的钱会不会有一部分落到她名下，可这念头好得近乎妄想，随即被她撇开了。运气好的话，或许有笔钱够她维持到学成一份差事，可就连这一点，她也拿不准。好在奥克斯利先生心地仁厚，人又精明。她不必过分忧心，他自会替她指点迷津。

===Original===

While making up her mind to rise and face what the day might bring forth, Ruth was greatly comforted by a visit from Mrs. Oxley. That lady presently knocked to inquire if her charge were awake, and she was so kind and understanding and kissed her in such a motherly way that Ruth felt a glow of warmth in her heart. Mrs. Oxley brought with her a tiny tray with the daintiest little tea service and the thinnest of bread and butter, and while Ruth enjoyed this unheard-of luxury the elder woman sat on the bed and proceeded to feed the girl’s mind with healing news. She mentioned, casually and yet with such a wealth of detail, that Mr. Whymper had called on the previous evening to inquire for Miss Averill. With really praiseworthy ingenuity she spun out the subject for nearly ten minutes, then she went on to tell something of almost﻿—though of course not quite﻿—equal importance. Mr. Oxley had wished her to say, in the strictest confidence﻿—no one at this stage was supposed to know anything about it﻿—but in order to relieve Ruth’s mind, he thought he might tell her﻿—that she was not to worry as to her future. He had drawn up old Mr. Averill’s will and there would be some money. Mr. Oxley had not said how much, but Mrs. Oxley was sure there would be enough. At all events Ruth was not to worry. And now, breakfast would be ready in half an hour and there was plenty of hot water in the bathroom.

===Chinese===

露丝正打定主意起床，去迎接这一天可能带来的一切，奥克斯利夫人前来看她，叫她大觉宽慰。夫人随即过来敲门，问她照看的姑娘醒了没有；她是那样和蔼体贴，又那样慈母般地吻她，露丝只觉一股暖流涌上心头。奥克斯利夫人随身带来一只小小的托盘，摆着一套精致之极的茶具，还有切得薄薄的黄油面包。露丝享受着这份闻所未闻的奢华时，这位年长的夫人便坐在床沿，往姑娘心里灌输起抚慰人心的消息来。她先提起温珀先生昨儿晚上来探问过埃弗里尔小姐——说得随意，细节却又那般丰富——凭着真正值得称道的机巧，把这一话题足足敷衍了将近十分钟；接着又说了一件几乎——当然也不完全是——同样要紧的事。奥克斯利先生本要她严守机密——眼下这个阶段，照理谁也不该知道半点风声——但为了让露丝宽心，他想不妨告诉她：她不必为自己的前途担忧。老埃弗里尔先生的遗嘱是他起草的，她会得到一笔钱。奥克斯利先生没说有多少，但奥克斯利夫人断定，总归是够用的。无论如何，露丝切莫发愁。好了，再过半小时早饭就好，浴室里热水管够。

===Original===

During the morning Ruth went down into the little town and engaged in the melancholy business of buying mourning. Mr. Oxley had lent her twenty pounds, explaining that she could repay him when she got her own money. This prospect of money coming to her made Ruth feel excited and important, and she could not refrain from daydreaming about all the wonderful things she would do when she received it. It was well for her indeed that she had something so absorbing to take her mind off the ghastliness of the tragedy which surrounded her. In fact, if only Pierce Whymper had come to see her again, she would have been really happy. But, as she afterwards learned, the young architect was out of town on business all that morning.

===Chinese===

上午，露丝下了小镇，去办置办丧服这门凄凉的差事。奥克斯利先生借了她二十英镑，说等她拿到自己的钱再还不迟。这笔钱财在望的前景，叫露丝又兴奋又觉得身价不同起来，忍不住做起白日梦，盘算起拿到钱之后要办的种种美事来。她周遭惨祸阴森可怖，能有这样一件叫人全神贯注的事分心，对她实在是大好的事。说真的，只消皮尔斯·温珀再来看她一趟，她就要真正快活了。可惜——她后来才知道——那位年轻的建筑师一上午都在城外办事。

===Original===

During Sergeant Kent’s call on the evening after the tragedy he had warned Ruth that she would be required to give evidence at the inquest. Now he came round to say that this was to be held in the courthouse at three o’clock that afternoon, and that she must be sure to be there in good time. The girl was naturally nervous at the prospect of giving evidence, which she had always heard was a terrible ordeal. But Mrs. Oxley reassured her in her kindly way, explaining that she had nothing to do but answer the questions she was asked, and promising that Mr. Oxley would see that nothing untoward befell her.

===Chinese===

惨祸发生后的那个傍晚，肯特警士来访时曾提醒过露丝，验尸审讯时她要出来作证。这会儿他又过来传话，说审讯定于当天下午三点在法院举行，叮嘱她务必及早到场。一想到要出庭作证，姑娘自然心里发慌——她一向听说，那可是个可怕的关口。奥克斯利夫人却和颜悦色地宽慰她，说她只消有问必答，别无他事，又答应说，奥克斯利先生自会照应，不叫她受半点委屈。

===Original===

Shortly before the hour, therefore, the little party approached the courthouse. The building was already crowded, but Mr. Oxley’s position as the leading solicitor of the town and Ruth’s as one of the most important witnesses procured them an immediate entrance and places on the seats usually reserved for counsel. As Ruth looked round the small old-fashioned building she saw many familiar faces. There, surrounded by policemen and looking weighed down with importance and responsibility, was Sergeant Kent. He was moving restlessly about, whispering to various persons and consulting at times a sheaf of papers he held in his hand. Some of the policemen she recognised also. There was the young smiling one with the light blue eyes whom she had met so many times when shopping in the town, and his companion with the long drooping nose and the hollow cheeks. In the seat behind was Mr. Snelgrove, the butcher, and Mr. Pullar, of the shoe shop. That tall very thin man with the little moustache and the bald head was Mr. Tarkington, the bank manager, and the slight, medium-sized man beside him was Mr. Bloxham, the clerk whom he used to send out to Starvel with Mr. Averill’s money. The venerable-looking old gentleman with the short white beard who was just pushing to the front was Dr. Emerson. And there﻿—how could she have failed to see him before?﻿—there, at the back of the court, was Pierce Whymper. He looked anxious and troubled, and though when she caught his eye and smiled, he smiled back, there was a something of embarrassment or reserve in his manner that seemed to her strange and disquieting. And just beside him﻿—but a sudden shuffle took place about her, and looking in front of her, she saw that a stout thickset man with a square face and a walrus moustache had entered from some invisible side door and was taking his seat in the judge’s chair.

===Chinese===

于是，将到时辰，这一小行人便朝法院走去。里面已经挤满了人，但凭着奥克斯利先生是本城首屈一指的事务律师，露丝又是最要紧的证人之一，两人当即被让了进去，还在通常留给律师的座席上得了位置。露丝环顾这座狭小旧式的屋子，望见许多熟悉的面孔。那边，被警察们簇拥着、一副身负重任、不胜其任模样的，是肯特警士。他不安地走来走去，凑近这人那人低声交代，不时翻看手里的一沓文件。有几个警察她也认得：一个是笑容满面、生着淡蓝眼睛的年轻人，她进城买东西时遇见过不知多少回；还有他那位同伴，长鼻子耷拉，双颊凹陷。后排坐的是肉铺老板斯内尔格罗夫先生（Mr. Snelgrove）和鞋店的普拉勒先生（Mr. Pullar）。那个瘦高挑、留一撮小胡子、头顶光秃的汉子，是银行经理塔金顿先生（Mr. Tarkington）；他身边那个清瘦的中等个儿，是布洛克斯汉先生（Mr. Bloxham），从前常替他带着埃弗里尔先生的钱送到斯塔维尔来的，便是这位职员。那位正往人前挤来的、仪表威严、留着白色短须的老先生，是埃默森医生。还有那边——她方才怎么会没瞧见他呢？——法庭后排，站着皮尔斯·温珀。他神色焦灼，满面忧容；待她与他目光相接、嫣然一笑，他也报之一笑，可他举止间带着几分局促或保留，她瞧着又奇怪又心慌。而就在他身旁——她近旁忽然起了一阵骚动；朝前一望，只见一个矮壮敦实、方脸盘、蓄着海象胡子的汉子，已从一道看不见的边门走进来，正在法官席上落座。

===Original===

She had expected an elaborate and picturesque ritual, but nothing of the kind took place. The coroner opened his bag, and taking out some papers, began to turn them over. Other persons sitting round the table before her also took out papers and shuffled them, while Sergeant Kent, turning round, shouted out “Robert Judd!” so suddenly and loudly that Ruth jumped. Someone at the back of the court answered “Here!” and was promptly ordered to come forward and enter the jury box. Other names were called﻿—to some of which there was no reply﻿—until all the places in the box were occupied. Then all stood up and stared vacantly at Kent while he murmured something about “justly try and true deliverance make,” after which everyone sat down again.

===Chinese===

「验尸官朗斯代尔医生（Dr. Lonsdale）。」奥克斯利先生低声道。露丝点了点头。这桩事开场竟如此平淡，她不禁暗暗纳罕。她原以为会有一套铺张而富于画意的仪式，谁知全然不是那么回事。验尸官打开提包，取出几份文件，翻检起来。坐在她前面那张桌子四周的旁人，也纷纷掏出文件翻动；肯特警士忽地转过身来，大喝一声「罗伯特·贾德（Robert Judd）！」，来得那样突兀、那样响亮，露丝吓了一跳。法庭后面有人应了声「到！」，随即被吩咐上前，进入陪审席。接着又点了几个名字——有的无人应声——直到席上座无虚席。然后众人起立，茫然望着肯特，听他咕哝了一句什么「秉公审理、如实裁断」，说完大家又都坐下了。

Spence told his story in great detail. In answer to the sergeant’s questions he explained that he drove a breadcart belonging to Messrs. 梅洛斯签罢口供回到座位后，验尸官对陪审团作了一篇短短的训示。他说，这场惨祸就发生在诸位近旁，其骇人听闻，人人心悸。警方除当日作证的埃弗里尔小姐外，未能寻得死者的任何亲属，他愿趁此机会，向她转达诸位对她丧亲之痛的诚挚慰问。他愿提醒陪审团，此番职责有三：其一，倘若证据足以令他们信服，便须认定死者的身份；其二，须分别查明各人的死因；其三，须说明依他们之见，有无何人应负其咎，若有，咎在何人。他并不以为这差事有何为难。前两项均无疑义。至于第三项——即这场大祸的责任归属——他只有一句话要说。事实已表明，男仆约翰·罗珀在出事当晚多少受了酒力。其中的暗示自然不言自明：或是罗珀某个疏忽之举酿成了大火。对于警方揭出此节，他是赞许的——事已至此，警方也别无选择——但他必须向陪审团指出：并无证据表明，罗珀先生的酒意与这场大火有丝毫干系。若说有什么倾向，证据反倒指向反面。遗体的位置表明，三个不幸的人是在床上被烧死的；倘若如此，便难免推定他们是睡梦之中被浓烟窒息的。陪审团若采纳这一看法，便知它排除了灯烛肇祸、跌入火中、引燃汽油或灯油种种意外的可能。这层道理自然算不上铁案，但他认为倾向确如所言。无论如何，除非污名果真当得，他不愿见罗珀先生——多位证人都称许他的尽忠职守与办事干练——身后蒙垢。裁决当然全在陪审团，但他提议，他们不妨认定：西蒙·拉尔夫·埃弗里尔（Simon Ralph Averill）、约翰·罗珀与弗洛拉·罗珀于九月十五日夜在斯塔维尔的一场大火中丧生，起火缘由，则无证据可资表明。
