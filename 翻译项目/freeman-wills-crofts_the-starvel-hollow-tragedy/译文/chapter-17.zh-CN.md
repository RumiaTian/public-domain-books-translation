## XVII · Concerning Wedding Rings / XVII · 谈结婚戒指

===Original===

“This morning about 10:30,” said the Chief Inspector, “we had a phone from Inspector Marshall of the Whitechapel District. He wanted to know whether we had had any recent reports of thefts of small jewellery, as he had come across some in connection with a scrap between two lightermen. It seems that about ten o’clock last night a constable on patrol heard cries coming from an entry off Cable Street, as if someone was being murdered. He ran down and found a man on the ground with another belabouring him furiously with his fists. The constable pulled the victor off, to find his opponent was little the worse. The fellow was really more frightened than hurt. The constable would have dismissed the affair with a good-humoured caution to both, had it not been that in the heat of the explanations the cause of the quarrel came out. The men had obtained some jewellery, which both claimed, and when the constable saw the stuff he didn’t wait for further discussion, but marched them both off to Divisional Headquarters. Marshall questioned them and reported their statements with his inquiry.

===Chinese===

「今天上午 10 点 30 分左右，」总督察说道，「我们接到怀特查佩尔区（Whitechapel）马歇尔探长（Marshall）的一个电话。他想了解我们近来可有小件珠宝失窃的报案，因为他在一起两名驳船船工的斗殴中撞见了一批这类东西。事情似乎是这样：昨晚 10 点来钟，一名巡逻警士听见卡布尔街（Cable Street）旁一条小巷里传出呼救声，像是有杀人案发生。他跑进去，只见一个人倒在地上，另一个正抡起拳头狠狠揍他。警士把得胜的那个拉开，才发现吃了打的那位并没受什么伤——这家伙其实与其说伤了，不如说是吓坏了。若不是在吵吵嚷嚷的解释当中吵出了打架的缘由，警士本想对两人笑呵呵训诫几句，把这事打发了的。原来这两人弄到了一些珠宝，两人都说是自己的；警士一见那批东西，便不再多话，径直把两人一并押到了区警察总部。马歇尔讯问了他们，把口供连同查问的经过一并呈报了上来。

===Original===

“The whole thing so far was purely commonplace, and if the jewellery had consisted of ordinary trinkets I should have thought no more about it. But the nature of the stuff tickled my fancy and I grew interested. You would hardly guess what they had. Wedding rings!”

===Chinese===

「到此为止，整件事平平无奇；倘若那批珠宝不过是寻常的小饰物，我本不会再多想。可那批东西的品类撩起了我的兴头，我上了心。你怕是猜不到他们弄到的是什么。结婚戒指！」

===Original===

“I certainly shouldn’t have guessed that, sir.”

===Chinese===

「这我倒确实猜不着，先生。」

===Original===

“I don’t suppose you would. Well, that’s what they had. Thirty-nine wedding rings on a cord. They were all much of the same size and value. And there was not another ring. They were searched, but nothing else was found on them.

===Chinese===

「谅你也猜不着。嗯，他们弄到的就是这个：一根细绳上串着三十九枚结婚戒指，枚枚大小价值都相去不远，此外再没有别的戒指。两人都搜了身，身上别无他物。

===Original===

“Marshall, of course, asked them where they got them, and their answer was more interesting still. It appeared that the victor, James Gray, was the skipper of a Thames lighter and the vanquished, William Fuller, was his ‘crew.’ A third man was on board who looked after the engine, but he didn’t come into the affair. Gray stated that about 8:30 that same evening they were working empty down the river. They had left a cargo of Belgian coal at an upriver works and were running down to their moorings for the night. They usually stopped about six, but trouble with their engine had delayed them on this occasion. It was rather a dirty night, raining and very dark and blowing a little. Gray, the skipper, was at the helm and Fuller was forward acting as lookout. The third man was below at the engines. Just as they began to emerge from beneath the Tower Bridge Fuller heard a smack on the deck beside him. He looked down and in the light of some of the shore lamps saw some bright objects rolling about on the planks. On picking one up he was astonished to find it was a wedding ring. He began to search and found several others, but the skipper swore at him for not minding his job, and he had to let the remainder lie. When they reached their moorings he tried again, but Gray was curious and came forward and found a ring himself. Then they had a proper look with lanterns and recovered the thirty-nine. Immediately, as might be expected, a row broke out. Both men wanted the rings. Fuller said they had fallen beside him and he had found all but one or two, but Gray held that he was skipper and that anything that came on the ship was his. They had to bury the hatchet temporarily so as not to give away the secret to their engineer, but the quarrel broke out again ashore, Fuller’s cries attracting our man. What do you think of that, French? A good story, isn’t it?”

===Chinese===

「马歇尔自然问他们东西从何而来，而两人的回答越发有意思了。原来那得胜的，詹姆斯·格雷（James Gray），是一条泰晤士河驳船的船老大；落败的，威廉·富勒（William Fuller），是他雇的『船员』。船上还有第三个人，管机器的，但此事与他无干。格雷供称，当晚 8 点 30 分前后，他们的空船正顺流而下。此前他们把一船比利时煤卸在上游一家工厂，正赶回过夜的泊位。他们通常 6 点前后就歇了，这一回是机器出了毛病才耽搁下来。那是个挺糟糕的夜晚，下着雨，天极黑，还刮着点风。船老大格雷掌舵，富勒在船头瞭望，第三个人在下面看机器。船刚要驶出伦敦塔桥（Tower Bridge）的桥洞，富勒就听见身旁甲板上啪的一声。他低头一看，借着几盏岸灯的光，瞧见一些亮闪闪的小物件在船板上滚来滚去。捡起一枚，他惊愕地发现竟是结婚戒指。他动手再找，又寻出几枚，可船老大骂他不好好当差，他也只得由其余的躺着去。到了泊位他又想再找，不料格雷起了疑心，走上前来自己也摸着一枚。两人这才点起提灯正经搜了一遍，把那三十九枚尽数捡齐。接下来可想而知，一场争吵立刻爆发。两人都想要这些戒指。富勒说戒指是落在他身边的，除了一两枚全是他的发现；格雷却咬定自己是船老大，凡落到这条船上的东西一概归他。为了不让他们的机器匠窥破这个秘密，两人只得暂且休战，可一上了岸战端重开，富勒的叫喊引来了我们那位警士。你以为如何，弗伦奇？这故事不错吧？」

===Original===

“Like a book, sir. Just a bit humorous too, if you don’t mind my saying so.”

===Chinese===

「简直像小说里编出来的，先生。恕我直言，还带几分滑稽呢。」

===Original===

There was a twinkle in Chief Inspector Mitchell’s eye as he continued:﻿—

===Chinese===

米切尔总督察眼中闪过一丝促狭的光，接着说道：——

===Original===

“Oh, you think so, do you? Well, anyhow, as I say, I was interested. The men’s mentality I found quite intriguing. I wondered how much imagination they had between them. Marshall described them as slow, unintelligent, bovine fellows. Now, such men could never have invented a tale like that. If they had been making it up they would have said they found a bag of rings in the street. The idea of wedding rings having been thrown over the parapet of the Tower Bridge just as they were passing beneath would only occur to men of imagination, and to have got all the details right would have involved a very considerable gift of invention as well. Do you see what I’m getting at, French? Their story shows too much imagination for their intelligences as described by Marshall, and therefore I am disposed to accept it.”

===Chinese===

「哦，你是这么想的？嗯，不管怎样，如我方才所说，我是上了心。这两个人的心性，我觉得颇堪玩味。我盘算他们两个加在一起能有多少想象力。据马歇尔形容，这两人迟钝、愚鲁，一副牛脾气。喏，这种人断然编不出那样一套话来。倘若他们要瞎编，只会说在街上拾到一袋戒指。‘有人恰在他们船过桥下之际，把结婚戒指从伦敦塔桥栏外抛下’——这个念头，只有富有想象力的人才想得出；而要把种种细节都编得滴水不漏，还得有相当高明的编造天分不可。你明白我的用意吧，弗伦奇？照马歇尔的形容，这两个人的脑力配不上他们故事里那许多想象力，所以我倾向于相信这故事是真的。」

===Original===

Chief Inspector Mitchell paused and looked at French as if expecting a comment.

===Chinese===

米切尔总督察顿了顿，望着弗伦奇，似乎等他置评。

===Original===

“I follow you all right, sir, and what you say sounds reasonable to me. And yet it’s not very likely that anyone would throw thirty-nine wedding rings into the Thames off the Tower Bridge, for I take it it was into the river and not on to the boat they were intended to go.”

===Chinese===

「您说的我都跟得上，先生，听来也在理。可终究不大像真有人会把三十九枚结婚戒指从伦敦塔桥上抛进泰晤士河——依我看，那些戒指是要落进河里的，本不打算落到什么船上。」

===Original===

“I should say undoubtedly.” Mitchell sat for a moment drumming with his fingers on his desk and looking thoughtfully out of the window. “You think the whole thing’s unlikely, do you? Perhaps you are right. And yet I don’t know. I think I can imagine circumstances in which a man might be very anxious to get rid of thirty-nine wedding rings. And what’s more, to throw them over the parapet of the Tower Bridge at 8:30 in the evening seems to me a jolly good way of getting rid of them. How would you have done it, French?”

===Chinese===

「要我说，这是毫无疑问的。」米切尔用手指在桌上敲了一阵，若有所思地望着窗外。「你觉得整件事不像真的，是不是？或许你是对的。不过也难说。我想我能够设想出某种情形，教一个人急欲甩脱这三十九枚结婚戒指。不但如此，依我看，晚上 8 点 30 分把它们从伦敦塔桥的栏外抛下去，就是个再好不过的甩脱法子。换了你，你会怎么处置，弗伦奇？」

===Original===

French glanced at his superior in some surprise. He could not understand the other’s interest in this commonplace story of stolen rings. Still less could he understand why he had been interrupted in his useful and important work to come and listen to it. However, he realised that it would be tactless to say so.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇颇为诧异地瞥了上司一眼。戒指失窃这样一桩平淡无奇的案子，他闹不懂对方为何如此上心；更闹不懂的是，为什么偏偏打断他手头那桩有益而要紧的差事，把他叫来听这个。不过他也明白，这话说出来未免不知趣。

===Original===

“I don’t know, sir,” he answered slowly. “I suppose to throw ’em in the river would be the best way. But he should have seen there was nothing passing underneath.”

===Chinese===

「我说不好，先生，」他慢条斯理地答道，「依我看，把它们扔进河里就是最好的法子。不过他总该先瞧瞧底下有没有船过吧。」

===Original===

“Ah, now that is an interesting point also. But first, does anything else strike you?”

===Chinese===

「啊，这一点也颇值得玩味。不过先说说，你可还看出别的什么来？」

===Original===

French looked wary.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇露出戒备的神色。

===Original===

“Just in what way, sir?”

===Chinese===

「您指的是哪方面，先生？」

===Original===

“This. Suppose you want to throw a package into the river and you want to do it absolutely unobserved. Where will you do it?”

===Chinese===

「是这样。假如你要把一件包裹抛进河里，而且要做得神不知鬼不觉，你会选在哪儿动手？」

===Original===

“I see what you mean, sir. That bridge at that time of night is about as deserted as any of the London bridges.”

===Chinese===

「我明白您的意思了，先生。夜里那个时辰，那座桥在伦敦诸桥之中，要算顶冷清的了。」

===Original===

“Exactly, that’s what I mean. There is evidence there of selection which would never strike a man like these bargees. But you say he ought to have seen the boat. Why should our unknown not have looked out for passing boats? I’ll tell you, I think. Though the bridge is *comparatively* deserted, it is *not* deserted. To look over the parapet far enough to see the water below would have attracted attention. A suicide might have been feared. Some officious person might have come forward. No, the unknown would simply chuck his parcel over without even turning his head, secure in the belief that even if by some miracle it was found, the contents would never be traced to him. Do you agree?”

===Chinese===

「正是，我说的就是这个。这里头有一种拣择的痕迹，是这两个驳船汉子之流决计想不出的。可你说他本该看见那条船。那么咱们那位无名氏，为什么没提防来往的船只？我猜是这样：那座桥虽然*比较*冷清，却*并非*无人来往。要把身子探出栏杆、一直望到桥下的水面，是会惹人注目的。人家会当是有人要投河。说不定就有好管闲事的家伙凑上来。不会的，那位无名氏只消头也不回地把包裹一丢便是，他笃信纵使有什么奇迹叫人捡着了，那包里的东西也万万追查不到他头上。你同意么？」

===Original===

“Seems quite sound, sir.”

===Chinese===

「听来很有道理，先生。」

===Original===

“It may be sound or it may not,” Mitchell returned. “All that I have been saying to you may be the merest nonsense. But it shows, I think, that the story these men told may be true. The chances of its being true are sufficiently great to warrant investigation before they are charged with theft. You agree with that?”

===Chinese===

「或许有理，或许没理，」米切尔答道，「我方才对你说的这一大篇，也可能纯属废话。但我想，它至少表明这两人讲的故事有可能是真的。这种可能性不小，足以值得在以窃贼罪名起诉他们之前，先行查究一番。这一点你同意吧？」

===Original===

This time French felt no doubt.

===Chinese===

这一回弗伦奇没有异议了。

===Original===

“Oh, yes, sir, I agree with that certainly. The men could not be convicted without going into their story.”

===Chinese===

「哦，同意，先生，这一点我当然同意。不查清他们的说法，这两人是定不了罪的。」

===Original===

The Chief Inspector nodded as if he had at last reached the goal for which he had so long been aiming.

===Chinese===

总督察点了点头，仿佛终于到达了他久久瞄准的那个目标。

===Original===

“That’s it, French. Now will you start in and do it?”

===Chinese===

「就是这话，弗伦奇。那么，由你来办，怎么样？」

===Original===

French stared.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇瞪大了眼睛。

===Original===

“Me, sir?” he exclaimed as if unable to believe his ears. “Do you wish me to take it up?”

===Chinese===

「我么，先生？」他叫了起来，像是不敢相信自己的耳朵。「您是要我来办这件案子？」

===Original===

The other smiled satirically.

===Chinese===

对方讥诮地微微一笑。

===Original===

“I don’t know anyone who could do it better.”

===Chinese===

「我想不出还有谁能办得更好。」

===Original===

“And drop my present case?”

===Chinese===

「那把我手头的案子丢下？」

===Original===

“Only temporarily,” the Chief assured him. “A day or so will make little difference to your own affair, and I have no one else to send on this one. Look into it and try and find out if anyone dropped those rings off the bridge, and if so, who he was and why he did it. When you have done that you can go ahead with the Starvel affair.”

===Chinese===

「只是暂且搁一搁，」总督察向他保证，「耽误一两天，你那桩案子出不了什么大岔子，可这一桩我另无人可派。你去查一查，看是否真有人从桥上抛下过那些戒指；若是真有，他是何人，缘何这么做。查完这个，你就可以回头去办斯塔维尔那桩案子了。」

===Original===

French was completely puzzled. This was very unlike the line the Chief usually took.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇简直摸不着头脑。这可全然不似上司平日的做派。

===Original===

“Of course, sir, it’s what you say; but do you not think it is very urgent that this banknote business be followed up while the trail is warm? Every day that passes will make it more difficult to get the truth.”

===Chinese===

「当然，先生，全凭您吩咐；只是您不觉得，这桩钞票的案子正当线索未冷，亟须穷追么？拖过一天，便多一分难查出真相。」

===Original===

“That applies even more strongly to this other affair. But it has the advantage of probably being a shorter inquiry. With luck you can finish it off tomorrow, and if so, that will delay the larger case only very slightly.”

===Chinese===

「这个道理放在眼下这桩上更加要紧。不过它有个好处：查起来多半为期不长。运气好的话，你明天就能了结；果真如此，那桩大案也耽误不了多少。」

===Original===

French saw that whatever might be the Chief’s motive, he had made up his mind.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇看得分明：不管头儿用意何在，他主意已定。

===Original===

“Very good, sir,” he returned. “I’ll go down to Whitechapel at once and get started.”

===Chinese===

「好吧，先生，」他答道，「我这就去怀特查佩尔，立刻动手。」

===Original===

“Right, I wish you would.”

===Chinese===

「好，我正希望你这就去。」

===Original===

French was conscious of not a little exasperation as he walked to Charing Cross and there took an eastward bound train. A few hours might make all the difference between success and failure in the Starvel case, and here he was turned on to this other business during the very period when it was most important he should be on his own job. He could not understand what was at the back of the Chief Inspector’s mind. Apparently he suspected a crime, though what crime he had in view French could not imagine. Marshall could have dealt with ordinary petty theft. But if Mr. Mitchell suspected a serious crime and if, as he said, no other officer was available to investigate the affair, his attitude would be explained.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇一路走到查令十字，在那里登上一列东行的火车，心头说不出的懊恼。在斯塔维尔一案上，几个钟头之差，或许就是成败的分野，偏偏在这最要他守着自己案子的当口，他被调来干这个别的。他猜不透总督察心里究竟打着什么算盘。他显然是疑心有一桩罪案，只是疑心的是什么罪案，弗伦奇无从想象。寻常的小偷小摸，马歇尔自己就办得了。但倘使米切尔先生疑心的是一桩重案，又果如他所说再无别人可派去查办，那么他这副态度也就说得通了。

===Original===

But whether it were explained or not orders were orders, and French with an effort switched his mind off John Roper and on to lightermen and wedding rings. On arrival at Divisional Headquarters he saw Inspector Marshall and heard his account of the affair, which was almost word for word that of the Chief Inspector’s.

===Chinese===

但不管说得通说不通，命令总是命令。弗伦奇费了些气力，才把心思从约翰·罗珀身上挪开，转到驳船船工和结婚戒指上来。一到区警察总部，他便见了马歇尔探长，听他讲述事情经过——与总督察所说的几乎一字不差。

===Original===

“I don’t know what the Chief’s got in his mind,” French grumbled. “Here was I on that Starvel case and on a hot scent too, and why he should switch me off on to this affair I can’t see. He’s got some bee in his bonnet about it. He believes these fellows’ yarn and he wants me to find the man who threw the rings over.”

===Chinese===

「我闹不清头儿打的什么主意，」弗伦奇嘟囔道，「我本来办着斯塔维尔那桩案子，线索正热，他凭什么把我调到这桩上来，我实在看不出。他这儿是着了什么魔。他信了这两个家伙的一套话，要我去找那个把戒指抛下河的人。」

===Original===

Marshall made noises indicative of surprise and sympathy. “I shouldn’t have thought the Chief Inspector would have stood for that dope,” he remarked. “What are you going to do about it?”

===Chinese===

马歇尔发出几声表示惊愕与同情的声响。「想不到总督察竟肯听信那套胡话，」他说道，「你打算怎么办？」

===Original===

French didn’t exactly know. He supposed he had better hear the men’s story for himself, though, of course, after his colleague had examined them his doing so would be only a matter of form to satisfy the Chief. Then he would think over the affair and try to plan his next move.

===Chinese===

弗伦奇也说不上来。他想还是亲耳听一听这两个人的说法为好——虽说同事既已审过，他再问一遍无非走个过场，好向上头交差。然后他要把这件事细细想一遍，盘算下一步怎么走。

===Original===

But rather to his own surprise, French found himself considerably impressed by the two men’s personalities and the way they told their story. Both were heavy and slow-witted and, French judged, without any imagination at all, and both seemed reasonably honest. After he had questioned them he felt very much inclined to accept his Chief’s view and to believe the tale.

===Chinese===

可是连他自己都颇感意外，这两个人的品性模样，连同他们讲述始末的那副神气，竟叫他暗暗信服。两人都憨重迟钝，依弗伦奇判断，毫无想象力可言，且瞧着都还算老实。问过话后，他颇倾向于接受上司的看法，相信这套说法不假。

===Original===

“You say you found the rings by the light of a hand lamp,” he went on presently. “Very good. Come along down with me to this boat of yours and we’ll have another look by daylight. Perhaps you missed a few.”

===Chinese===

「你们说，是借着手提灯的光找到那些戒指的，」过了一会儿他接着说，「很好。跟我一道下你们那条船去，咱们趁白天再找一遍。说不定漏了几枚。」

===Original===

The men didn’t think so, but they were very willing to do anything which got them out of the police station. They led the two inspectors to the dirtiest wharf that French had ever seen, and there hailing a man in a wherry, the four were put aboard the Thames lighter *Fickle Jane*.

===Chinese===

他接过那一串，利落解开串着戒指的绳结，把戒指一枚一枚拿起来，逐枚细看。

===Chinese===

弗伦奇做了个苦脸。「看来我这差事希望不大，」他哀叹着谢过朋友，起身告辞。

===Chinese===

他派了几个人翻查行名录，把伦敦各珠宝商的地址尽数找出，又安排人把通函如数印就分发。随后他组织起一班人手，专司回函涌到时的料理。最后，戒指一节的良心账既已交代清楚，他便回到钞票案上，从先前撂下的地方接着查起。
