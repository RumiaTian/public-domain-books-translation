## IX / 九

===Original===
There was being held another meeting the same night. Two miles from Central City, to the North, was a natural auditorium, an amphitheatre formed by three hills. In this place a meeting alfresco was in progress. Though the place was far enough from the road to be reasonably free from prying intruders, sentinels paced the narrow roads that led to the place of assemblage. Skeleton-like pine-trees formed an additional barrier to the lonely spot, making as they did a natural fringe atop the three hills.

There was no moon. Light was furnished by pine torches fastened in some instances to trees, in others borne aloft by members of the gathering. About three hundred men were ranged in a circle around a rudely carved cross stuck in the ground. Each man was garbed in a long white robe reaching to his feet. On the left breast of each hood was a cross with other strange figures. Over the head of each man was a cowl with holes for eyelets. It was a meeting of Central City Klan, Knights of the Ku Klux Klan, Realm of Georgia. The Exalted Cyclops, whose voice bore a remarkable likeness to that of Sheriff Parker, was initiating new members into the mysteries of the order. He held in his hand a sheet from which he was reading the oath which the “aliens” repeated after him with their right hands upraised. Whether through fright or excitement or because the night air was chilly, the voices of the embryo “knights” had a strange quaver in them. Around them, rank on rank, stood the Klansmen, who followed the ceremony closely.

“… will willingly conform—to all regulations, usages, and requirements—of the Knights of the Ku Klux Klan—which do now exist—or which may hereafter—be enacted—and will render—at all times—loyal respect, and steadfast support—to the Imperial Authority of same.…”

===Chinese===
同一夜，还有另一场集会正在举行。从中央城往北两英里，有一座天然的剧场，三面环山，形同一座露天圆形剧场。就在这个地方，一场露天集会正在进行。这地方离大路虽远，足以大致免遭爱打探者的闯入，可通往集会处的几条窄路上，仍有哨兵在来回踱步。骨架似的松树构成了这片僻静之地的又一道屏障，在三座山顶上连成一道天然的边饰。

这夜没有月亮。照明全靠松脂火把——有的绑在树上，有的由集会者高举着。约莫三百人围成一圈，中间地上插着一座粗雕的十字架。每个人都罩着一件垂到脚面的白色长袍。每顶兜帽的左胸上都绣着一个十字，配着些别的怪异图样。每个人头上都扣着一顶兜帽，只在眼睛处开了两个洞。这是中央城三K党（Central City Klan）[1]——三K党佐治亚州分部骑士团——的一次集会。那位崇高独眼巨人（Exalted Cyclops）——他的嗓音和帕克警长惊人地相像——正为新成员主持入会秘仪。他手里拿着一张纸，照着念那段誓词，那些「外人」举起右手，跟着他一句一句复诵。也不知是惊惧、是兴奋，还是夜风太凉，这些准「骑士」们的嗓音里都带着一种古怪的颤抖。在他们四周，一排又一排地站着三K党徒，紧跟着仪式的每一步。

「……甘愿遵从——三K党骑士团——之一切规章、惯例与要求——无论现存的——抑或日后——所订立的——并将随时随地——对三K党之帝国当局——效以忠敬——予以坚定的支持——……」

===Original===
The droning voices ended the monotonous recital. The flickering torches gave forth a weird light that was lost in the darkness cast by the trees. The pungent odour of burning resin and the thick stifling smoke were blown by vagrant breezes into the faces of the hooded figures, causing a constant accompaniment of coughs, sneezes, and curses to the mumbled words. A recent rainstorm had left the low-lying ground soggy and damp and mightily uncomfortable underfoot. The crowd shifted uneasily as their feet grew cold with the dampness. Moths, mosquitoes, and other flying insects, attracted by the flaring lights, swarmed, getting beneath the cowls and robes and adding to the discomfort of the wearers. Even the imperfect illumination showed the cheap material of which the disguises were made, exhibited the wrinkles and dirt around the hems, revealed every aspect of the ill-fitting garments. Once from a spluttering torch there fell a bit of blazing resin on the hand of the man holding the light. With a yell he dropped the torch, danced and howled with pain, a ludicrous figure, until the agony had subsided. The torch, flung hastily away, set fire to the underbrush into which it had been cast. An unlooked-for intermission in the ceremonies followed as a score of the figures, holding the skirts of their robes aloft like old maids frightened at the appearance of a mouse, stamped out the fire, circling and yelling like a band of whirling deryishes.

Stodgy, phlegmatic, stupid citizens by day, these by night went through the discomforts of so unprepared a meeting-place, and through the absurdities of the rites imposed upon them by clever rogues who extracted from them fees and donations for the privilege of being made to appear more silly than is usually apparent. Add to that gullibility a natural love of the mysterious and adventurous and an instinct towards brute action restrained only by fear of punishment, by a conjuring of bogies and other malevolent dangers, and one understands, at least in part, the presence of these three hundred “white, Gentile, Protestant” citizens of Central City at this meeting.

嗡嗡的嗓音念完了那段单调的诵文。明灭的火把发出一种诡异的光，被树影投下的黑暗吞没了大半。燃烧松脂的刺鼻气味和那浓重呛人的烟，被乱窜的风吹进那些蒙面人脸上，于是那些含混的诵词便配上了此起彼伏的咳嗽、喷嚏和咒骂。不久前的一场暴雨把这片低地泡得又湿又软，踩在脚下极不舒服。人群随着脚底受潮发凉，不安地挪动着身子。飞蛾、蚊子和别的飞虫被火光吸引，一团团地扑来，钻进兜帽和袍子底下，更增穿戴者的难受。即便是这并不明亮的火光，也照得出这些化装用料之廉价——袍边的褶皱、污垢一览无遗，把这些不合身的衣物暴露得纤毫毕现。有一回，一把噼啪作响的火把溅出一小块燃烧的松脂，正落在举火把那人的手上。他大叫一声扔下火把，又跳又叫，痛得直蹦，那模样滑稽可笑，直到痛劲儿过去了才消停。那把被匆匆甩出去的火把，点燃了它落进去的灌木丛。仪式因此有了一段意想不到的中断——只见二十来个蒙面人，像老姑娘见了耗子一样惊惶地撩起袍子的下摆，绕着圈跺脚叫喊，活像一群旋转舞动的托钵僧，这才把火踩灭。

白天那些沉闷、麻木、愚钝的市民，到了夜里却来忍受这么一个毫无准备的集会场所的种种不适，忍受那些强加在他们头上的荒唐仪式——这些仪式是一些狡猾的无赖编出来，好从他们身上榨取会费和捐献，作为一种特权，让他们得以显得比平日还要愚蠢。除了这份轻信之外，再加上他们对神秘和冒险天生的爱好，再加上一种只有靠对惩罚的恐惧、靠招魂唤鬼之类的恶毒恐吓才能约束的蛮干本能——这么一想，你便多少能明白，这三百名中央城的「白人、新教徒、纯种（Gentile, Protestant）」[2]市民，为何会出现在这场集会上。

===Original===
The initiation ended, the Exalted Cyclops ordered the Kligrapp or secretary to read several communications from the Imperial Klan Palace at Atlanta. This he did, struggling manfully through the weird and absurd verbiage that would have made any of the men present howl with laughter had he heard his children using it in their play. Instead it was listened to attentively, seriously, and solemnly.

Then followed a recital of the work to be done by the local Klan. The Kligrapp consulted a sheet of paper in his hand.

“The eye that never sleeps has been seeking out those in our city who have acted in a manner displeasing to the Invisible Empire. There is in Central City a nigger wench named Nancy Ware who has been saying evil things against our brother, George Parker. In the name of our sacred order, and in the furtherance of our supreme duty of preservation of white supremacy, she is being watched and will be treated so as to end her dangerous utterances.”

At this statement a robed figure that, even under the disguise, seemed to resemble him who had been “defamed” by Nancy Ware’s tongue nodded approvingly. The Kligrapp continued after a pause:

===Chinese===
入会仪式结束，崇高独眼巨人命纪录官（Kligrapp，即书记）[3]宣读几封来自亚特兰大帝国党宫（Imperial Klan Palace）的通函。纪录官照办，费力地一行行念着那些古怪荒唐的字眼——这些字眼，在场的任何一个人，要是听见自己的孩子在玩耍时说出来，准会笑得直不起腰。可眼下这些话，却被大家一字不漏地、严肃而庄重地听着。

接下来是本地三K党待办事务的逐项宣读。纪录官看了看手里的一张纸。

「那只永不阖眼的眼睛，一直在我们城中搜寻那些行事有忤『无形帝国（Invisible Empire）』[4]之人。中央城有一名黑鬼娘们（nigger wench）[5]，名唤南希·韦尔，一直在散布恶毒的言辞，诋毁我们的兄弟乔治·帕克。奉我们神圣结社之名，并为我等捍卫白人至上（white supremacy）[6]之至高职责计，此人正受监视，并将予以处置，以绝其危言。」

听到这话，一个蒙面人点了点头，表示赞许——那人即便隔着这身化装，看上去也很像那个被南希·韦尔的舌头「诋毁」了的人。纪录官顿了顿，又往下念：

“Word has also come to us from Brothers Ed Stewart and Taylor that there’s a young nigger named Tom Tracy out this way who’s going around among the niggers saying that they have got to stop white people from robbing them on their crops. Tracy hasn’t done anything but talk thus far, but we will keep our eye on him and stop him if he talks too much.” Cowled heads nodded approvingly.

“And then there’s a nigger doctor who came in my office I mean, he went into the office of Health Commissioner Lane—and had the gall to repo’t the death of a nigger bootlegger and say that a white man had killed him for fooling around with the nigger’s wife. This nigger’s daddy was one of the best niggers that ever lived here in this town, and this boy’s keeping away from the other trouble-making niggers, but we’ve got to watch all these niggers that’s been spoiled by goin’ to school.” He added, as an afterthought: “… up Nawth.”

「又，埃德·斯图尔特兄弟和泰勒兄弟传来消息，说我们这一带有个名叫汤姆·特雷西的年轻黑鬼，专在黑鬼们中间串来串去，鼓动他们阻止白人在庄稼账上劫掠他们。特雷西迄今还只是说说而已，没干什么别的，但我们会盯紧他，他要是再多嘴，就制止他。」一顶顶兜帽点着头，表示赞许。

「还有那么一个黑鬼医生，跑进我办公室里——我是说，他跑进了卫生专员莱恩的办公室——竟然厚着脸皮报告一个黑鬼私酒贩子的死，还说是一个白人因为跟那黑鬼的老婆鬼混而把他杀了。这黑鬼的爹是这城里住过的最好的黑鬼之一，这小子也还算没跟其余那些爱闹事的黑鬼搅在一起，但这些读了书学坏的黑鬼，我们全都得盯着。」他像想起什么似的，又补了一句：「……是在北边（Nawth）[7]读的书。」

===Original===
And so he droned on. Negroes, two Jews, three men suspected of Catholic leanings—all were condemned by the self-appointed arbiters of morals and manners. One or two men were singled out as violating the code of morals by consorting with Negro women. There was not much to report on this score, as those who were violating this rule in Central City had rushed, on formation of a Klan there, to join the order, that they might gain immunity from attack and yet continue their extralegal activities without check or interference. With the conclusion of the Kligrapp’s report, the meeting dispersed, the members silently entered the woods and there disrobing, and scattering to their various homes. Some went towards “Factoryville,” some towards the country districts, others climbed into automobiles parked near the road and drove towards the residential section of Central City where lived the more affluent merchants and other upper-class whites of the town.

The place was soon deserted. The ceremony had been a strange mixture of the impressive and the absurd. There was underneath the ridiculously worded language, the amusing childlike observance of the empty ceremonies, the queer appearance of the robes all designed alike with little regard for fatness or thinness of the prospective wearers, a seriousness which betokened a belief in the urgent need of their organizing in such a manner. They had been duped so long by demagogues, deluded generation after generation into believing their sole hope of existence depended on oppression and suppression of the Negro, that the chains of the ignorance and suppression they sought to fasten on their Negro neighbours had subtly bound them in unbreakable fashion. They opposed every move for better educational facilities for their children, for improvement of their health or economic status or welfare in general, if such improvement meant better advantages for Negroes.

他便这样嗡嗡地念下去。黑人、两个犹太人、三个被怀疑信奉天主教的人——全都被这些自封的道德礼俗仲裁者定了罪。还有一两个人被点了名，说他们违犯了道德准则，与黑人女子厮混。这一条上倒是没什么可报的，因为中央城违犯这一条的人，一听说本地成立了三K党，便赶紧入伙，好让自己免遭攻击，又能继续他们那些法外的勾当，不受掣肘、不受干扰。纪录官的报告念完，集会便散了。成员们默默地走进树林，在那里脱下袍子，四散回各自的家去。有的往「工厂镇」走，有的往乡间去，有的爬上停在大路边的汽车，朝中央城那片住着殷实商贾和城中其他上等白人的住宅区驶去。

那地方很快便空无一人。这场仪式是庄严与荒诞的一种奇异混合。在那措辞可笑的语言、那近乎儿戏的空洞礼仪的遵行、那全然不顾穿戴者高矮胖瘦一刀切的古怪袍服底下，藏着一种认真——这种认真表明他们当真相信，他们非得这样结社不可。他们被那些煽动家政客愚弄得太久了，一代又一代地被骗，相信他们唯一的活路就在于压迫和钳制黑人；以至于他们想要套在黑人邻居脖子上的那条无知与钳制的锁链，已经反过来以一种挣不开的方式，套到了他们自己身上。他们反对任何旨在为他们子女改善教育条件、改善他们的健康或经济状况或一般福利的举措——只要这种改善意味着黑人也能得到更好的好处。

Creatures of the fear they sought to inspire in others, their lives are lived in constant dread of the things of evil and terror they preached. It is a system based on stark, abject fear—fear that he whom they termed inferior might, with opportunity, prove himself not inferior. This unenlightened viewpoint rules men throughout the South like those who formed the Central City Klan—dominates their every action or thought—keeps the whites back while the Negro—in spite of what he suffers—always keeps his face towards the sun of achievement.…

In spite of the secrecy surrounding the meeting, next morning all Central City talked of what had taken place on the previous evening. In such a town, where little diversion exists, the inhabitants seize with avidity upon every morsel of news that promises entertainment. Though they had taken fearful oaths of secrecy, it was asking too much of human frailty to expect three hundred men to refrain even from mysterious hints of their doings. With the love that simple minds have of the clandestine, the midnight secrecy, the elaborately arranged peregrinations to the place of meeting, the safeguards adopted by the leaders not so much to prevent interference as to impress their followers, the “inviolable oath,” the grips and passwords—all these added to the human desire to be considered important in the eyes of family and friends and neighbours. Thus many of the three hundred dropped hints to their wives of what had been said and done. Over back fences, at the stores on Lee Street, in the numerous places where women contrived to meet and gossip, the one topic discussed was the meeting of the night before. One told her bit of information to another, who in turn contributed her mite. Each in turn told a third and a fourth. With each telling, the ball of gossip grew, and each repetition bore artistic additions of fact or fancy designed to add to the drama of the story. By noon the compounded result assumed the proportions of a feat bordering on the heroic.

他们是他们自己想在别人身上制造的那种恐惧的造物，他们的日子便是在不断的战栗中度过的——战栗于他们自己所宣扬的那些邪恶与恐怖。这是一种赤裸裸、卑怯的恐惧之上建立起来的体制——恐惧那个被他们称作低人一等的人，一旦有了机会，便会证明自己并非低人一等。正是这种蒙昧的观念，支配着南方各处像组成中央城三K党的那类人——主宰着他们的每一个行动、每一个念头——它把白人拽在原地动弹不得，而黑人——尽管吃尽苦头——却始终把脸朝着成就的那轮太阳……

尽管集会严守秘密，第二天早上整个中央城却都在谈论昨夜发生的事。在这样一个没什么消遣的小城里，但凡有一星半点可以博人一乐的消息，居民们便如饥似渴地抓住不放。这三百人虽都发过骇人的保密毒誓，可要指望他们连一个神秘兮兮的暗示都不露，未免太把人的弱点看得太高了。头脑简单的人天生偏爱这等隐秘勾当——子夜的秘密，煞费苦心安排的迂回赴会，头目们所设的那些与其说是防人干扰、不如说是要震慑部下的层层关卡，那「不可违背」的誓言，那些握手礼和口令——这一切，又都助长了人想在家人、朋友、邻居眼里显得要紧的那份人之常情。于是这三百人里便有许多人，向自己妻子漏了些口风，透露说了些什么、做了些什么。在后院的栅栏边，在李街上的一家家铺子里，在女人们变着法儿凑到一起嚼舌头的无数个场合，议论的话题只有一个，那就是昨夜的集会。这一个把她那点消息告诉另一个，另一个又添上自己那点；每一个人又转告了第三个、第四个。每传一遍，这桩闲话的雪球便滚大一分，每一次复述都添上一些出于事实或出于想象的「点睛之笔」，好叫故事更富戏剧性。到了晌午，层层叠叠累积起来的版本，已夸张到近乎一出英雄壮举了。

At the noonday meal, known as dinner, the men found themselves viewed in a new and admiring light by their spouses and offspring. They basked in the temporary glamour and sought to add to the fame of their midnight prowling by elaborate hints of deeds of dark and magnificent proportions.

In turn, to the Negro section of Central City were borne the tales by cooks and laundresses and maids, servants, with acutely developed ears, in the houses of the whites. Everywhere in the Negro section, in homes, on street corners, over back fences, the news was discussed by the dusky inhabitants of the town. In the eyes of a few, fear could be discerned. Most of the Negroes, however, discussed the news as they would have talked about the coming of the circus to town. Some talked loudly and in braggart fashion of what they would do if the “Kluxers” bothered them. Others examined for the hundredth time well-oiled revolvers. Most generally the feeling was a hope the Klan would not bother any coloured person—but if it did—!…

「怎么样，鲍勃，你怎么看？」

「有人要有麻烦了，」鲍勃肯定地说。「而且我有一种感觉，那有人就是我们，」他顿了顿，又加上一句。

「我倒说不准，」肯尼思半信半疑地应道。「这些穷白佬里头，有些是坏到了会生事的地步，可我敢说中央城有的是正派的白人，足以压下任何可能闹起来的乱子。」

===Original===
Bob said nothing, though his face showed plainly he did not share his brother’s confidence. Kenneth went on:

“Besides, they must have sense enough to know that a sheet and pillowcase won’t scare coloured folks today as they did fifty years ago. It wasn’t hard to scare Negroes then—they’d just come out of slavery, and believed in ghosts and spooks and all those other silly things. But today—”

“I think white people are right sometimes,” broke in Bob with conviction, “when they say education ruins a Negro. One of those times is when you talk like that.”

The irony in his voice was but thinly veiled. He continued:

“The Southern white man boasts he knows the Negro better than anybody else, but he knows less what the coloured man is really thinking than the man in the moon. I’ll bet anything you say, that seven out of every ten men in town believe that you and I and all the rest of us coloured folks are scared to death every time we hear the word ‘Ku Klux.’ They believe the sight of one of those fool robes’ll make us run and hide under a bed—”

===Chinese===
鲍勃没作声，但他脸上分明写着，他并不和哥哥一般有把握。肯尼思接着说：

「再说，他们也该有点脑子，知道如今一块床单、一个枕套，再不能像五十年前那样吓唬得住有色人了。那时候吓唬黑人倒是不难——他们刚从奴隶身份里出来，还信鬼信魂儿，信那些乱七八糟的玩意儿。可如今——」

「我看白人有时候说得对，」鲍勃带着确信打断他，「他们说读书毁了黑人。这话对你这种人开口的时候，倒是对得很。」

他话里的那点讥讽，几乎没怎么遮掩。他接着说：

「南方白人吹牛说自己最懂黑人，其实他连有色人心里到底在想些什么，都不比月亮上那个人懂得多。我跟你打什么赌都行，城里十个人里头有七个都相信：你、我，还有我们所有这些有色人，每回一听见『三K党』这三个字，就吓得要死。他们信只要瞧见那种蠢袍子一眼，我们就会撒腿就跑，钻到床底下去——」

===Original===
“Oh, I don’t go quite that far,” interrupted Kenneth. “I only said I thought some of the good white people.”

“You can name all your ‘good white folks’ on one hand,” replied Bob irritably. “A lot they could do if these poor white trash decide to raise hell. Why, they’d lynch Judge Stevenson or Roy Ewing or anybody else if they tried to stop ’em. Look what they did to Governor Slaton at Atlanta just because he commuted the sentence of that Jew, Leo Frank!” he added triumphantly. “A mob even went out to his house to lynch *him—the governor*!”

“But that was an extraordinary case,” replied Kenneth.

“Call it what you will, it just shows you how far they will go when they are all stirred up. And with this Ku Klux outfit to stir them up, there’s no telling what’ll happen.”

===Chinese===
「哦，那倒也不至于，」肯尼思打断他。「我只是说，我想总有些正派的白人……」

「你那些『好白人』，一只手都数得过来，」鲍勃没好气地答道。「要是这些穷白人垃圾铁了心要闹翻天，他们顶个什么用。呸，他们要是闹起来，就是把史蒂文森法官、罗伊·尤因或别的什么人判了私刑也办得到，只要谁想拦他们。你瞧瞧他们在亚特兰大对斯莱顿州长（Governor Slaton）[8]干的好事——就因为他给那个犹太人利奥·弗兰克（Leo Frank）[9]减了刑！」他得意洋洋地补上一句。「甚至有一伙暴民跑到他家，要把*他——堂堂州长*——都处私刑！」

「可那是个非同寻常的案子，」肯尼思答道。

「随你怎么叫它，这只不过说明他们一旦被煽起来，能闹到什么地步。如今再加上三K党这一套在煽风点火，谁知道会出什么事。」

===Original===
“Bob, do you really believe what you said just now about most of them really believing Negroes will be scared by the Klan? That seems so far-fetched.”

“Believe it? Of course I do. Just use your eyes and see how Negroes fool white folks all the time. Take, for instance, old Will Hutchinson who works for Mr. Baird. Will cuts all sorts of monkey-shines around Baird, laughs like an idiot, and wheedles old Baird out of anything he’s got. Baird gives it to him and then tells his friends about ‘his good nigger Will’ and boasts that Will is one ‘darky’ he really knows. Then Will goes home and laughs at the fool he’s made of Baird by acting like a fool.” Bob laughed at the memory of many occasions on which Will had bamboozled his employer. “And there are Negroes all over the South doing the same thing every day!” he ended.

“That’s true,” admitted Kenneth, “but what ought we to do about this meeting last night?”

“Do?” echoed Bob. A determined look came to his face, his teeth clenched, his eyes narrowed until they became thin slits. “Do?” he repeated. “If they ever bother me, I’m going to fight—and fight like hell!”

Long into the night Kenneth sat alone in his office, wondering how it was all going to turn out.

===Chinese===
「鲍勃，你方才说的话——说他们多半当真以为黑人都被三K党吓住了——你真信吗？那未免太离谱了。」

「信？我当然信。你只要睁开眼瞧瞧，看黑人是怎样天天把白人哄得团团转的。拿老威尔·哈钦森（Will Hutchinson）来说吧，他在贝尔德（Baird）先生手下做事。威尔在贝尔德跟前装出种种滑稽相，笑得像个傻子，把老贝尔德哄得要什么给什么。贝尔德把东西给了他，回头就跟朋友们夸耀『我那个好黑鬼（nigger）威尔』，吹嘘威尔是他真正『摸透了』的一个黑佬（darky）。然后威尔回到家，暗自嘲笑贝尔德那个被他装疯卖傻骗惨了的大傻瓜。」鲍勃想起威尔多少回把东家哄得晕头转向，不禁笑出声来。「南方各地到处都有黑人天天干着这样的事！」他总结道。

「这话不假，」肯尼思承认，「可昨晚那个会，我们该怎么办呢？」

「怎么办？」鲍勃应道。他脸上掠过一道决绝之色，牙关紧咬，两眼眯成了一条细缝。「怎么办？」他重复了一遍，「他们要是敢来招惹我，我就跟他们干——往死里干！」

直到深夜，肯尼思仍独自坐在诊所里，琢磨着这一切究竟会怎样收场。

> 译注：
>
> [1] **Central City Klan / Knights of the Ku Klux Klan（中央城三K党 / 三K党佐治亚州分部骑士团）**：三K党（Ku Klux Klan）是美国白人至上主义的秘密恐怖组织，1860 年代由南方邦联旧人创立，20 世纪 20 年代（本书成书之际）借「百分之百美国主义」之名再度猖獗，以暴力恐吓、私刑、纵火等手段迫害黑人及其他少数族裔。本书作者怀特是 NAACP 领袖、黑人平权活动家，本章以讽刺笔触揭露其集会之荒唐与本质之恐怖。下文「崇高独眼巨人（Exalted Cyclops）」「纪录官（Kligrapp）」「帝国党宫（Imperial Klan Palace）」等均为三K党内部职衔与机构，译文照录原称以存其历史语境。
>
> [2] **Gentile, Protestant（非犹太裔、新教徒）**：三K党入会资格限定为「白人、信基督新教、非犹太裔（white, Gentile, Protestant）」，即所谓「百分之百美国人」，明确排斥黑人、犹太人、天主教徒及外来移民。译文保留原文以见其排他本质。
>
> [3] **Kligrapp（纪录官）**：三K党地方支部（Klavern）之书记，源自该组织自造的术语体系，与「崇高独眼巨人（Exalted Cyclops，即支部首领）」「大巨人（Great Titan，即州级首领）」「帝国巫师（Imperial Wizard，即全国首领）」等同属其刻意营造神秘感的内部称谓。
>
> [4] **Invisible Empire（无形帝国）**：三K党自称其组织为「无形帝国」，奉「帝国巫师」为首领，对应其种种帝国式虚衔。下文「永不阖眼的眼睛（the eye that never sleeps）」亦为其内部用语。
>
> [5] **nigger wench（黑鬼娘们）**：白人种族主义对黑人女子的极度贬称，nigger（黑鬼）之外再加以 wench（旧时指低贱女仆、淫妇）双重贬抑。本章此处出自纪录官宣读三K党文书之口，作者怀特意在揭露其语言与意图之恶毒，译文保留其贬义冲击力，不软化。
>
> [6] **white supremacy（白人至上）**：三K党的核心信条，主张白种人对其他种族的绝对统治与优越地位，本书时代为南方各州种族隔离与暴行的意识形态根基。
>
> [7] **Nawth（「北边」）**：南方白人对「North（北方）」的方言读法，纪录官以此补缀「在北边读的书」一语，带轻蔑口吻，指肯尼思等人在北方受教育。译文以「北边」对应并附原文。
>
> [8] **Governor Slaton（斯莱顿州长）**：约翰·M. 斯莱顿（John M. Slaton），时任佐治亚州州长。1915 年他将被判死刑的犹太工厂主利奥·弗兰克减刑为终身监禁，引发一场暴民骚动——暴民闯入州立监狱将弗兰克劫出私刑绞死，并扬言要惩办斯莱顿本人。本书以这段历史为「白人暴民不可制」之显例。
>
> [9] **Leo Frank（利奥·弗兰克）**：纽约出生的犹太裔工厂主管，1913 年在亚特兰大被诬告谋杀一名白人女工，在排犹与种族狂热的氛围下被判死刑；1915 年斯莱顿州长复审证据后予以减刑，旋即遭暴民劫狱私刑绞死，为二十世纪美国最著名的犹太人遭私刑冤案之一，亦直接催生了反犹组织「三K党」的死灰复燃与「反诽谤联盟（ADL）」的成立。
