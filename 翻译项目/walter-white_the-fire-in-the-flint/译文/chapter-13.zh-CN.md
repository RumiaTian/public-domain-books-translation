## XIII / 十三

===Original===
The days that followed were full of interest for Kenneth and Jane. The constitution and bylaws were drafted and approved and sent to Atlanta to be printed by a coloured printing firm. Judge Stevenson prepared the articles of incorporation and did the necessary legal work, still refusing any pay for his services. Kenneth had offered to pay him out of his own pocket, but the judge told him: “Keep your money, Ken, I c’n wait. I’m gettin’ along in years now and I’ve been hopin’ that this problem that’s cursin’ the South would be settled befo’ I passed on. But what with these damn fool Kluxers kickin’ up hell ’round here, I don’t know whether I’ll see it or not. Your idea may do some good—I don’t know whether it will or not—but if I c’n help, let me know.” Kenneth thanked him and had been immeasurably encouraged by the old man’s attitude.

===Chinese===
接下来的日子，对肯尼思和简来说，过得既充实又有意思。章程和附则起草出来、议定通过，送到亚特兰大，交一家有色人（coloured）印刷厂去排印。史蒂文森法官拟好了公司组织章程，把该办的法律手续一一办妥，仍旧不肯收一文报酬。肯尼思提出要自掏腰包付给他，老法官却对他说：「钱你留着吧，肯，我等得起。我年纪也大了，一直盼着这个祸害南方的问题能在我闭眼之前有个了结。可眼下这帮该死的蠢三K党徒在这一带胡作非为、闹得天翻地覆，我能不能活到那一天，我也说不准。你这个主意也许管点用——管不管用我也不知道——可要是帮得上忙的，你只管开口。」肯尼思向他道谢，老人这份态度给了他莫大的鼓舞。

===Original===
As soon as the literature they had ordered was received, the first meeting was called by Tucker and Tracy at Ashland. Jane and her father drove out with Kenneth, who was to present the plan to the group gathered. The meeting was held at a little wooden church, whitewashed on the outside, and furnished within only with rude benches. On the walls were one or two highly coloured lithographs of religious subjects. The hall seated not more than two hundred and was crowded to capacity. Even the windows were comfortably filled by those unable to obtain a seat on the floor. The illumination was furnished by four kerosene lamps attached to the walls, two on each side.

Hiram Tucker acted as chairman, while Tom Tracy took minutes of the meeting. After a preliminary announcement of the purpose of the gathering by the chairman, Kenneth was called upon to outline the plan that had been proposed. At the outset, having had no experience as a public speaker, he stumbled and faltered and knew not what to do with his hands. After a few minutes he jammed them into the side pockets of his coat and, warming to his subject, swung into a clear, forceful, and convincing recital of the purpose and possibilities of the cooperative societies. His enthusiasm became infectious. His audience began to share his zeal. Humble and lowly folks, their vision limited by the life they led, they had the feeling, as Kenneth talked on, of having been face to face with a blank wall of immeasurable height and impenetrable thickness. Under the spell of his words they seemed to see the miraculous opening of a door in this wall. Hope, which had been crushed to earth year after year by disappointing settlements for their labour, began to mount.

===Chinese===
他们订购的宣传资料一到手，塔克和特雷西就在阿什兰召集了第一次大会。简和她父亲开车陪肯尼思一道去，由肯尼思向到会的人宣讲拟好的计划。会议在一座小木教堂里开，外头刷了白灰，里头只有些粗陋的长凳。墙上挂着那么一两幅色彩浓艳的宗教石印画。这厅堂至多坐得下两百人，此时已挤得满满当当，连窗户上都舒舒服服地趴满了人——都是地板上抢不到座位的。屋里靠墙挂着的四盏煤油灯照明，两边各两盏。

海勒姆·塔克当主席，汤姆·特雷西做记录。主席先开场宣布了此次集会的宗旨，接着便请肯尼思把拟好的计划细细说来。一开始，他没有什么当众讲演的经验，磕磕巴巴，半天才吐出半句，两只手也不知往哪儿放才好。过了几分钟，他把手往外套两边口袋里一插，话一讲顺了，便滔滔不绝地讲起合作社的宗旨和种种可能，条理分明，有力量，有说服力。他这股热忱有了传染性，底下听的人也跟着一同热起来。这些卑微、低微的人，一辈子过的日子限住了他们的眼界，听肯尼思讲下去，他们原以为自己是面对一堵不可度量其高、也不可穿透其厚的高墙，已经走到尽头。在他的话的魔力下，他们仿佛看见这墙上奇迹般地打开了一扇门。一年又一年，那因结算劳作所得而屡屡落空、被狠狠踩进土里的希望，又开始悄悄抬头。

===Original===
As for Kenneth, he had forgotten his self-imposed inhibitions and prohibitions. Gone was the hesitation and doubt. He had seen a light where he had thought there was no light. His voice rang true and firm, and there was a look of eager earnestness on his face as the pale, flickering light from the oil lamps illumined it.

He finished with a flourish so dear to the hearts of coloured audiences. It was what the old-style coloured preacher used to call “de ’rousements.”

===Chinese===
至于肯尼思，他早已忘掉自己给自己设下的那些禁制与戒律。迟疑和动摇都一扫而空。他原以为没有光的地方，他已经看见了光。他的嗓音清亮而坚定，油灯那苍白、摇曳的光映在他脸上，他一脸急切而恳挚的神情。

他这一段结尾收得慷慨激昂——这正是有色人听众最听得入心的一种收尾。也就是那种老派有色人牧师常说的「鼓动劲儿（’rousements）」。

===Original===
“You husbands and sons and brothers, three years ago you were called on to fight for liberty and justice and democracy! Are you getting it?” He was answered by a rousing “No!” “What are you going to do about it?” he demanded. “Single-handed, you can do nothing! Organized, you can strike a blow for freedom, not only for yourselves but for countless generations of coloured children yet unborn! No race in all history has ever had its liberties and rights handed to it on a silver platter—such rights can come only when men are willing to struggle and sacrifice and work and die, if need be, to obtain them! I call on you here tonight to join in this movement which shall in time strike from our hands and feet the shackles which bind them, that we may move on as a race together to that greater freedom which we have so long desired and which so long has been denied us! Only slaves and cowards whine and beg! Men and women stand true and firm and struggle onwards and upwards until they reach their goal!” He paused impressively while the audience sat mute. He looked over the assemblage for a full minute and then demanded in a ringing voice: “What do you choose to be slaves or men?”

He sat down. A salvo of applause greeted him. A Daniel had arisen to lead them! Kenneth took on a new importance and affection in the eyes and minds of his hearers. He had heard their Macedonian cry and answered it.

===Chinese===
「你们这些做丈夫的、做儿子的、做兄弟的——三年前，人家叫你们去为自由、为正义、为民主打仗！如今你们得到这些了吗？」回答他的是一声炸雷般的「没有！」「那你们打算怎么办？」他追问。「单枪匹马，你们什么也办不成！组织起来，你们就能为自由奋力一击——不光是为你们自己，更是为千千万万尚未出生的有色人孩子！有史以来，没有一个种族的自由和权利是别人用银盘子端到它面前的——这样的权利，只有当人愿意去挣扎、去牺牲、去流汗、必要时去送命，才能到手！今晚我就在这儿号召你们，加入这一运动——它迟早要从我们手脚上砸断那捆绑着我们的镣铐，好让我们作为一个种族一同前行，走向我们渴慕已久、也被剥夺了已久的那更大的自由！只有奴隶和懦夫才哼哼唧唧、伸手乞讨！真正的男人和女人，他们立得稳、站得直，一路向前、向上，挣扎奋斗，直到达到目标！」他有意顿了一顿，全场鸦雀无声。他把全场扫视了足有一分钟，然后以清亮的嗓音发问：「你们要做什么——奴隶，还是人？」

他坐下了。一阵雷鸣般的掌声向他涌来。一位但以理[1]在他们中间兴起，要引领他们了！在听众的眼里、心里，肯尼思有了新的分量，也添了新的亲厚。他听见了他们马其顿人的呼声[2]，并作出了回应。

===Original===
As he mopped his brow, Kenneth felt that he had made a good beginning, although he was a bit ashamed of having made so direct an appeal at the end to emotion instead of to reason. At the same time he knew that it had been necessary. “’Rousements” were absolutely essential to awaken the response needed to get the cooperative societies under way. Without them his humble audience might not have been aroused to the point of action that was so necessary.

Following Kenneth, Mr. Wilson made a stirring appeal to the crowd to come forward and give their names if they wanted to join the newly formed society. Those who had the money were urged to join at once. At first, only a few came forward. Then they came in numbers until around the table at which sat Secretary Tracy there was an excited, chattering, milling throng.

===Chinese===
肯尼思抹了一把额头，觉得自己有了一个不错的开端，只是结尾处他如此直白地诉诸情感而非理智，心里多少有点惭愧。但他同时也明白，这是必须的。「鼓动劲儿」是绝对少不了的——非要这样才能激发起把合作社办起来所需的反响。没有这一手，他这些质朴谦卑的听众，也许就激发不出那股非有不可的行动劲儿。

肯尼思讲完，威尔逊先生接着登台，热烈地呼吁大家上前报名入会——凡是愿意加入这个新成立的团体的人都报上名来。手头有钱的，更被劝着当场就入会。起初，只有零星几个人走上前。接着，人一拨接一拨地涌上去，到最后，秘书特雷西坐的那张桌子四周，已围成一圈又兴奋、又嘈杂、拥来挤去的人海。

===Original===
After the meeting Mr. Phillips accepted Mr. Wilson’s invitation to ride home in his car. Kenneth did not object—it enabled him to be alone with Jane. They talked of the meeting as they walked to the car. Jane gave Kenneth’s hand a faint squeeze. “Oh, Kenneth, you were splendid!” she declared.

It was a perfect night—one created for making love. A soft light filtered through the leaves of the trees, casting a lace-like shadow on the earth. The air was soft and languorous, as it can be only on a spring evening in the South, as soft and caressing as the touch of a baby’s hands. From near at hand came the mingled odour of honeysuckle and cape jasmine and magnolia blossoms and roses. The world seemed at peace. No sound disturbed the air save the chattering and singing of a mockingbird, as lovely as the sob of velvety, full-throated violins, and the voices, growing fainter and fainter, of the crowd leaving the now deserted church. It would have taken a much stronger man than Kenneth to resist the spell of so perfect an evening. He was not mawkishly sentimental—rather he detested the moon-calfish type of man who rolled his eyes and whispered empty, silly compliments in the ear of whatever girl he met. On the other hand, he was amazingly ignorant of women. As a youngster he had been exceedingly chary of the little girls of the neighbourhood, preferring to spend his time playing baseball or shooting marbles. This shyness had never entirely left him. From his youth on he had had but one strong passion in his life that passion had possessed his every thought and in it was centred his every ambition—his desire and determination to become a great surgeon. His one serious venture into the realm of lovemaking had been the affair with the girl in New York, but that had not taken a strong enough hold upon him to leave much of a mark. So rapidly had it begun and ended that he had had in it little experience in the great American sport of “petting.” It was thus easy for him to fall head over heels in love with Jane, for she was, in fact, the first girl in his life outside of his sister who had come into his life in more than a casual way.

会后，菲利普斯先生应了威尔逊先生的邀请，搭他的车回去。肯尼思并不反对——这倒让他能和简单独待在一起。他们一边朝汽车走，一边说着方才的会。简轻轻捏了捏肯尼思的手。「哦，肯尼思，你太棒了！」她说。

这是个完美的夜晚——一个天生为谈情说爱而造的夜晚。柔和的光透过树叶筛下来，在地上投下花边般的阴影。空气柔软而慵懒，只有南方春天的夜晚才能这样，柔软而爱抚，像婴儿的小手摸在脸上一样。近处飘来金银花、月橘、木兰和玫瑰混合在一起的香气。整个世界仿佛都已安睡。除了反舌鸟[3]婉转啼鸣——那声音之美，一如丝绒般嗓音饱满的小提琴的低泣——以及渐行渐远、正离开这座如今已空荡荡的教堂的人群的说话声，再没有什么惊扰这一片空气。要抵御这样一个完美夜晚的魔力，需要比肯尼思硬气得多的男人。他并非多愁善感到肉麻的那种人——相反，他讨厌那种痴情种子的男人，眼睛骨碌碌转，见着哪位姑娘都凑到人家耳边，低声说些空洞、愚蠢的奉承话。可在另一方面，他对女人简直惊人地无知。小时候，他极怕招惹邻里的小女孩，宁可把时间都花在打棒球、弹玻璃珠上。这份羞怯一辈子也没完全离开过他。从少年时起，他这一生只有过一种强烈的激情——这股激情占据了他每一念的心思，他一切的抱负都围绕着它——那就是成为一名伟大外科医生的渴望与决心。他唯一一次认真走进谈情说爱这一领域，是和纽约那个姑娘的一段情，可那段情并没在他身上扎下足够深的根，没能留下多深的印记。它来去都那么快，以至于他从中所得的，对于「亲昵（petting）」这门美国大运动而言，几乎谈不上什么经验。因此，他很容易就一头栽进对简的热恋里——因为事实上，除妹妹之外，简是他生命中第一个不只是萍水相逢走进来的姑娘。

===Original===
Jane, on the other hand, had, innocently enough, flirted as every pretty girl (and many who are not pretty) will do. She appreciated Kenneth’s fine qualities: he was capable, industrious, and handsome in a way. He annoyed her at times by his almost bovine stupidity in expressing his love. She naturally liked the idea of having the love of a man who is naive, who has not run the whole gamut of emotions in affairs with other girls; yet, also naturally enough, she did expect him to have at least some savoir faire, to be able to win her with some degree of the finesse that every girl wants and expects. She resented his businesslike matter-of-factness in seeking her—as coldly calculating, it seemed to her, as though he were operating on one of his patients. In this she was doing him an injustice. Underneath his surface placidity Kenneth’s love had become a raging flame—he cursed the shell of professional dignity which had crossed over and become a part of himself.

Thus they walked through the soft spring air, she wishing he would do that which he in his ignorance felt would be the unwisest thing he could attempt. Thus is life made up of paradoxical situations where a word, a look, an otherwise insignificant gesture, would clear away at one fell swoop mountainous clouds of doubt and misunderstanding.

简呢，在另一方面，则像每一个漂亮姑娘（许多不算漂亮的也一样）那样，多少也算清白无罪地调过情。她欣赏肯尼思的好品质：他能干、勤勉，有几分英俊。他表达爱意时那种近乎牛一般的木讷，有时也叫她恼火。她自然喜欢这样一个念头：得到一个天真男子的爱——这男子没有在别的姑娘身上跑遍情感的整个音域；然而，同样合乎情理地，她也确实指望他至少有那么一点处世的本事，能够带着每个姑娘都想要、都指望着的那一点手腕来赢得她。他追求她时那副公事公办的实在劲儿，叫她心里有气——那样子冷冷地算计着，她觉得，就像他在给哪一个病人开刀一样。在这一点上，她是冤枉了他。在肯尼思那表面平静之下，他对她的爱已烧成了一片熊熊烈火——他咒骂那层职业尊严的外壳，那外壳不知何时已反过来长进了他自己身上，成了他的一部分。

他们就这样在柔软的春夜里走着，她盼着他做那件事，而他因自己的懵懂，反而觉得那是他所能尝试的最不明智的一件事。人生便是如此，由这一桩桩自相相成的处境构成——一句话、一个眼神、一个本来微不足道的手势，便能在顷刻之间扫净堆积如山的疑虑和误解。

Jane stood, one foot on the ground, the other on the step, her hand resting on the opened door of the car. A faintly provocative smile flitted over her face. Kenneth longed to seize this elusive, seductive girl in his arms, press her close to him, and tell her of his love. She wanted him to. Instead he steeled himself against yielding to the impulse that almost overcame him, and helped her with complete decorum into the car.…

They did not say much on the way home. Jane bade him good night, he thought somewhat coldly—as though she were vexed. He told her he was leaving the next morning for Atlanta to operate on Mrs. Tucker. She made no comment. He wondered as he drove home what he had done to offend her.…

他从车上取来一只小手电筒。回到那不动的人形旁边，把电筒的光照在那身体上，看出这是一个赤身裸体的女人，浑身糊着柏油，摸上去却还是温的。在那女人背上，一团团黏糊的东西之间，是一道道长长的鞭痕，有的还在渗血，像是有人用了粗重的皮鞭，使劲抽打过。她的头发蓬乱，发丝间沾着一块一块融化的柏油。这副惨状令肯尼思一阵作呕。女人趴着。从她的口和鼻里淌出一道血，已在脸下的地上积成一小汪，和路面的尘土一混，成了血红的泥浆。肯尼思抓住她的左肩，把那身体半撑起来，把手电筒照向她的脸。是南希·韦尔——就是被乔治·帕克杀害的那个黑人之妻。肯尼思半抱半拖地把这软绵绵的人弄到几家门外南希自己的家里。门是开着的，像是南希出门一会儿去敲哪位邻居的门。前屋桌上还点着一盏灯，灯罩被风吹得熏黑了一层烟灰。灯旁放着一件南希正在缝补的衣裳。

===Original===
Kenneth placed her on the bed and hurried next door to summon help. His efforts were unsuccessful. He pounded on the door with both fists, calling out in his excitement to the occupants to open up. After what seemed an infinite delay, a window to the left of the door cautiously opened and an inquiring voice wanted to know what was the matter. Seeing who it was, the owner of the voice disappeared and a minute later opened the door. Kenneth hastily told what had happened, brushing aside a muttered excuse that the delay in answering was due to the fact that “I didn’ know but whut you might ’a’ been the p’lice.”

===Chinese===
肯尼思把她安顿到床上，又急忙跑到隔壁去求援。可这一趟却碰了壁。他用两只拳头擂门，激动地喊里头的人开门。等了好一段仿佛漫无尽头的工夫，门左边一扇窗子才小心翼翼地开了，一个犹疑的声音问出了什么事。看清是谁之后，那声音的主人缩了回去，过了一分钟才来开门。肯尼思匆匆讲了出了什么事，对方嘟囔了一句解释应门迟了的原因——「我哪晓得你不是警察呢」——被他一句带过。

===Original===
On going back to Nancy’s cottage, Kenneth gave her a restorative and endeavoured to relieve her suffering. She began to revive after a few minutes. In the meantime the neighbours called by Kenneth arrived, and they removed as much as they could of the tar from Nancy’s body. Kenneth then examined her back, finding it covered with long and ugly gashes that bled profusedly. He dressed them and Nancy was arranged as comfortably as possible. He found himself so tired after the hard work and excitement of the day and evening that he was almost ready to drop in his tracks. At the same time he had an uncontrollable desire to find out just what had happened to Nancy Ware. He was almost certain the Ku Klux Klan had done it, but he wanted to hear the story from Nancy’s own lips. The neighbours had gone, with the exception of an evil-looking, elderly woman who had volunteered to remain with Nancy until morning.

===Chinese===
回到南希的小屋，肯尼思给她用了强心药，又尽力减轻她的痛苦。过了几分钟，她开始缓过一点气来。其间肯尼思叫来的邻居也到了，她们便七手八脚地把南希身上能擦掉的柏油擦掉一些。肯尼思随后检查她的背，见上面布满了又长又丑、血如泉涌的伤口。他把这些伤一一处理好，又把南希尽可能安置得舒服一些。这一整天加上一晚上的辛劳和激动下来，他已经累得几乎当场就要倒下。与此同时，他又有一股按捺不住的念头，非要知道南希·韦尔到底出了什么事不可。他几乎可以断定是三K党（Ku Klux Klan）下的手，可他要从南希自己嘴里听见这事。邻居们都散了，只留下一个面相凶恶的老妇人，自告奋勇愿意陪南希到天亮。

===Original===
After the application of restoratives regularly for an hour, she began to show signs of returning consciousness. Kenneth watched her eagerly. Five minutes later her eyelids fluttered. She gave a low moan—almost a whimper. Suddenly she cried out in the terror of delirium: “Doan let ’em whip me no mo’! Doan let ’em whip me no mo?!” and writhed in her agony. She struggled to arise but Kenneth, sitting by the side of the bed, managed with the aid of the other woman to restrain Nancy and calm her. Afterwards she became more rational. Her eyes opened. Another wait. Then, at Kenneth’s questioning, she began to tell what had happened. For weeks he had thought but little of her and the tragedy that had taken place in this same house, other events having crowded it out of his mind.

每小时定时地用强心药，过了一个钟头，她开始显出苏醒的迹象。肯尼思紧张地盯着她。又过五分钟，她的眼皮动了动。她低低地呻吟了一声——几乎像是一声呜咽。忽然，她在谵妄的恐惧里尖叫起来：「别让他们再打我啦！别让他们再打我啦？！」痛得在床上扭动。她挣扎着想爬起来，肯尼思坐在床边，在那位老妇人的帮助下，把南希按住、安抚下来。过后她神志清楚了一些。她睁开了眼。眼里有一道认出肯尼思的光，他知道她已苏醒过来了。

又是一阵等待。后来，在肯尼思的探问下，她开始讲起出了什么事。这几个礼拜以来，他几乎没怎么想过她，也没怎么想过发生在这同一座屋子里的那场悲剧——别的事早已把它从他的脑子里挤了出去。

===Original===
“Doc, you won’t let ’em get me again, will you?” she pleaded with a whimper like a child’s. Kenneth assured her he wouldn’t.

“Doc, I ain’t done nuthin’ t’ them Kluxers. Hones’ t’ Gawd, I ain’t.” Kenneth told her soothingly that he knew she hadn’t.

「大夫，你不会让他们再把我抓走吧，会不会？」她像孩子似的呜咽着央求。肯尼思向她保证，不会的。

「大夫，我啥也没对他们三K党徒做过呀。对天发誓，真的没有。」肯尼思柔声安慰她，说他知道她没做过。

It was just as Kenneth had suspected. Good God, and these were the self-elected defenders of morals in the South! What if Nancy wasn’t all that she should have been?—whose was the greater fault—hers or George Parker’s? He could see him now in the bank—smug, a hypocritical smile on his face, talking about what the white people have got to do to stop these troublesome “niggers” from getting too cheeky—about protecting “pure” Southern womanhood from attacks by “black, burly brutes.” And the Klan with all its boasted and advertised chivalry—twenty or thirty strong men to beat up and maltreat one lone woman, because she “talked too much” about the brutal, cold-blooded murder of her husband! Nancy was resting easily Kenneth, shaken by the fury of his anger, more devastating because he knew that he could do nothing but hurl silent imprecations on the heads of those who had done this deed—impotent because his skin was black and he lived in the South—went home to roll and toss during the few hours of the night which remained before he took the train to Atlanta.

>
> [3] **反舌鸟（mockingbird）**：北美常见的模仿鸟，善仿他鸟啼鸣及环境声响，歌声婉转多变。南方春夜常见，其啼鸣在本书中是南方田园夜色的标志意象。
>
> [4] **nigger（黑鬼）**：此章多处涉及种族贬语与三K党暴行。①银行出纳乔治·帕克口中的「niggers」与「black, burly brutes」，系白人种族主义对黑人的蔑称与污名化刻板印象，作者怀特借此揭露三K党所谓「保护南方女性纯洁」之说的虚伪——乔治·帕克本人即与南希通奸、并枪杀其夫巴德（第6章），却以「黑鬼勃然兴事」「野兽侵犯白人女子」等陈词滥调为自身及白人暴行开脱；②三K党徒以「白人老爷（white gemmen）」自称、对南希施以柏油鞭刑，其「保护白人女性」之意识形态与实际暴行之荒谬反差，作者以冷峻反讽笔调直陈。译文一律保留「黑鬼（nigger）」「有色人（coloured）」之原貌与贬义冲击力不软化，与第1章以来全书处理一致（同杜波依斯项目原则）。又，南希口述遭遇一段用黑人乡村方言（de/do’/dem/mouf/ca’ied/po’ed/he’p/cullud 等），译文以中文乡土口吻传达其惊惧与质朴，不用怪异拼写（与第6章巴德临终口吻、第8章塔克/特雷西陈述、第12章塔克建议之口吻一致）。
