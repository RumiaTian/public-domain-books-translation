## XI / 十一

===Original===
Early the next morning Kenneth rose and rummaged through his books until he found his old and battered textbooks on economics.

Into these he dipped during the intervals between patients, making notes of ideas which seemed useful in the organization of the cooperative society. The more he read, the more feasible the plan seemed. Properly guided and carefully managed, there was no reason, so far as he could see, why the society should not be a success. Eighty percent of the farmers of the South, white and coloured, he estimated, suffered directly or indirectly from the present economic system. Though his interest was in the Negro tillers of the soil, success in their case would inevitably react favourably on the white just as oppression and exploitation of the Negro had done more harm to white people in the South than to Negroes. Kenneth felt the warm glow of the crusader in a righteous cause. Already he saw a new day in the South with white and coloured people free from oppression and hatred and prejudice—prosperous and contented because of that prosperity. He could see a lifting of the clouds of ignorance which hung over all the South, an awakening of the best in all the people of the South. Thus has youth dreamed since the beginning of time. Thus will youth ever dream. And in those dreams rests the hope of the world, for without them this world with all its defects would sink into the black abyss of despair, never to rise again.

===Chinese===
第二天一清早，肯尼思起了床，在书堆里翻找，直到找出了他那几本破旧的经济学（economics）老课本。

在病人看病的间隙，他便翻阅这些书，把那些似乎对组织合作社（cooperative society）有用的想法一一记下。他越读，这计划越显得行得通。只要引导得当、经营细致，就他所见，这合作社没有不成功的道理。据他估计，南方的农户——不论白人还是有色人——十之有八都直接或间接地吃这现行经济制度的苦头。他虽把心放在那些黑人（Negro）庄稼人身上，但他们的成功必然惠及白人，正像过去对黑人的压迫和盘剥，对南方白人所造成的祸害，反倒比对黑人本身更深。肯尼思感到一股十字军为正义事业而战的暖意涌上心头。他已经依稀看见南方一个新时代的到来：白人和有色人都不再受压迫、受仇恨、受偏见——因这富足而殷实、而知足。他看见笼罩整个南方的那片愚昧之云正在散去，看见南方所有人身上最美好的东西正在苏醒。自古以来，青年人就是这样做梦的。今后，青年人也永远会这样做梦。而世界的希望，就寄托在这些梦上；若没有这些梦，这个满是缺陷的世界便会沉入绝望的黑渊，再也浮不起来。

===Original===
His work finished for the day, he went as soon as he decently could to talk with Jane. She, too, had been at work. Eagerly they planned between them the infinite details of so ambitious a scheme. Confidently they discounted possible difficulties they might expect to encounter—the opposition of the whites who were profiting from the present system, the petty jealousies and suspicions of those who would gain most from the success of their scheme. They realized that the Negro had been robbed so much, both by his own people and by the whites, that he was chary of new plans and projects. They knew he was contentious and quarrelsome. These things seemed trivial, however, for with the natural expansiveness of the young they felt that difficulties like these were but trifles to be airily brushed aside.

===Chinese===
当天的事一办完，他一得着合宜的空便赶去和简商谈。她那一头也没闲着。两人热切地一起筹划着这么一个雄心勃勃的计划中那千头万绪的细节。他们满怀信心地不把可能遭遇的种种难处放在眼里——那些正从现行制度中获利的白人的反对，那些本将从计划成功中得益最多的人那点小小的嫉妒和猜疑。他们心里明白，黑人被自己的本族人、也被白人坑骗得太多次了，对新计划、新花样总是心存戒备。他们也知道他爱争爱闹。但这些都显得无足轻重，因为凭着年轻人天然的开朗，他们觉得这种种难处不过是些可轻轻拂去的小麻烦。

===Original===
Jane was not too much engrossed in their plans to notice the change in Kenneth’s manner. She had watched him closely during the times she had seen him since his return. He had been almost morose, his mind divided between his work and the effort to keep to a “middle-of-the-road” course in his relations with the whites. The inevitable conflict within himself, the lack of decisiveness in his daily life that he consciously developed and which was so diametrically opposite to that he used in his profession, had begun to create a complex personality that was far from pleasing. In a freer atmosphere Kenneth would have been a direct, straightforward character, swift to decision and quick of action. One cannot, however, compromise principle constantly and consciously without bearing the marks of such conflicts.

===Chinese===
简并未因沉浸在计划中而忽略了肯尼思神态上的变化。自从他回来之后，她每次见他，都把他看得很仔细。他几乎有些阴郁，心思一头挂在工作上，一头又用在竭力维持与白人交往时的那条「中间路线」。他内心那不可避免的冲突，他在日常生活中有意养成的优柔寡断——这与他行医时的作风恰恰相反——已开始造就出一个并不讨人喜欢的复杂人格。换一个更自由的空气，肯尼思本会是个直来直去、干脆利落的性子，决断迅速，行动敏捷。然而，一个人不能时刻、清醒地妥协原则，而不在身上留下这种种冲突的痕迹。

===Original===
His compromises were not all conscious ones, though. He believed honestly it was wisest that he observe some sort of halfway ground between rank cowardice and uncompromising opposition to the conditions which existed. In doing so, he had no sense of physical or moral timidity. He knew no Negro could yet safely advocate complete freedom for the Negro in the South. He felt there had been improvement during the past half-century in those conditions. He believed that in time all of the Negro’s present problems would be solved satisfactorily. If, by not trying to rush things, he could help in that solution, he was content. In believing thus, Kenneth was different in no way from the majority of intelligent Negroes in the South: temporizing with the truth, it may be, yet of such temporizations and compromises is the life of the Negro all over the South.

===Chinese===
不过，他的妥协并不全是清醒为之。他真心相信，在赤裸裸的懦弱和对现行状况毫不妥协的对抗之间，守某种折中之道，是最为明智的。他这样做，丝毫没有肉体上或道德上的怯懦之感。他知道，在南方，还没有哪个黑人能安然地为黑人争取完全的自由。他觉得这半个世纪以来，境况是有所改善的。他相信，黑人眼下所有的问题，终有一日会得到圆满的解决。只要他不去急于求成，就能为这解决出一分力，他便知足了。肯尼思这样想，与南方大多数有头脑的黑人并无二致：也许是对真理的一种权宜，然而南方黑人的全部生活，正是由这种种权宜与妥协构成的。

===Original===
With the evolving of a plan which enabled him to be of help and, at the same time, involved him in no danger of trouble with his white neighbours, Kenneth took on an eagerness which was at marked variance with his former manner. His eyes shone with the desire to make their plan a success. Of a tender and sympathetic nature, almost with the gentleness of a woman, he realized now that the burdens of his race had lain heavy upon him. He had suffered in their suffering, had felt almost as though he had been the victim when he read or heard of a lynching, had chafed under the bonds which bound the hands and feet and heart and soul of his people. But launched as he now was on a plan to furnish relief from one of the worst of those bonds, he had changed overnight into a determined and purposeful and ardent worker towards the goal he and Jane had set for themselves. Jane rejoiced at the changed air of Kenneth—he seemed to have emerged from the shell in which he had encased himself and, womanlike, she rejoiced that he had done so through her own work.

So absorbed had they been in discussion of their plans that the time had flown by as though on wings. Ten o’clock was announced by Mr. Phillips in the room above by the dropping of his shoes, one after the other, on the floor. Kenneth needed no second signal, he rose to go. Jane went to the door with him.

“Kenneth, you’re entirely different from the way you were yesterday. I’m so glad.…”

===Chinese===
如今有了一个既能让他出力、又不致把他卷入与白人邻居纠纷的计划，肯尼思生出一种热切，与往日那副神情截然不同。他眼里闪着要使计划成功的光。他天性温柔、富于同情，几乎带着几分女子的细腻，此刻才意识到自己本族的重担一直沉沉地压在他身上。他曾为本族的苦难而一同受苦，读到或听到一桩私刑（lynching）时，几乎像是自己也成了受害者一样感同身受，曾为本族那捆住了手脚、也捆住了心魂的枷锁而愤懑不已。可如今他既已投身到一个能松开其中最沉重一道枷锁的计划之中，他一夜之间便变成了一个坚决、有目标、满腔热忱的人，朝着他和简为自己定下的那个目标奋力向前。简为肯尼思这番变化而欣喜——他似乎已从自己为自己裹上的那层壳里钻了出来，而她，像一个女子那样，为他是经由她的一番功夫才钻出来的而更是欣喜。

他们谈计划谈得太投入，时光竟像插上了翅膀一般飞逝。十点钟是楼上房里的菲利普斯先生宣布的——他把鞋子一只接一只地丢在地板上，便是报时。肯尼思无需第二个信号，起身便要走。简陪他走到门口。

「肯尼思，你跟昨天简直判若两人。我真高兴。……」

===Original===
The next morning he called on Judge Stevenson. The Judge’s office was above the Bon Ton Store in a two-story brick building on Lee Street. Kenneth climbed the flight of dingy, dusty stairs which bore alternately on the vertical portions tin signs inscribed:

Richard P. Stevenson, Attorney-at-Law

				and

				Dr. J. C. Carpenter, Dentist.

The judge’s office was at the head of the stairs and in it Kenneth found the old lawyer seated near the window, his coat off, and in his mouth the long, thin, villainous-looking cigar without which few persons in Central City ever remembered seeing him, though none had ever seen one of them lighted. He chewed on it ruminatively when in repose. When engaged in an argument, either in or out of a courtroom, and especially when opposition caused his choleric temper to be aroused, he chewed furiously as though he would have enjoyed treating his enemy of the moment in similar fashion. He was tall and thickset, his snow-white hair brushed straight back from his forehead like the mane of a lion. Skin reddened by exposure to sun and wind, bushy eyebrows from under which gleamed fiery eyes that could shift in an instant from twinkling good humour to flashing indignation or anger, thin nose and ample mouth, his face was one that would command respect or at least attention in almost any gathering. He wore loosely fitting, baggy clothes that draped his ample figure with a gracefulness that added to his distinguished appearance. Many thought he resembled at first glance that famous Kentuckian, Henry Watterson, and indeed he did bear an unmistakable likeness to “Marse Henry.”

===Chinese===
第二天上午，他去拜访史蒂文森法官（Judge Stevenson）。法官的事务所在「好时光」百货店（Bon Ton Store）楼上，是李街（Lee Street）上一栋两层的砖楼。肯尼思爬上那道昏暗、积灰的楼梯，楼梯的竖板上交替钉着几块铁皮招牌，分别写着：

理查德·P. 史蒂文森，律师（Richard P. Stevenson, Attorney-at-Law）

　　　　及

　　　　J. C. 卡彭特医生，牙医（Dr. J. C. Carpenter, Dentist）。

法官的事务所在楼梯口，肯尼思进去时，见这位老律师正坐在窗边，外套脱了，嘴里叼着一支又长又细、看上去一脸凶相的雪茄——中央城的人几乎都想不起来几时见过他没有这支雪茄，尽管谁也没见过他把这支雪茄点着过。他闲坐时，便若有所思地嚼着它。一旦争辩起来——不论是在法庭里还是法庭外，尤其是当对手惹起了他那暴躁的性子时——他便恶狠狠地嚼，仿佛恨不得以同样的方式来处置他此刻的敌人。他身材高大敦实，一头雪白的头发从前额直直往后梳去，像一头雄狮的鬃毛。日晒风吹晒得发红的皮肤，浓密的眉毛下一双火灼灼的眼睛，能在一瞬间从闪烁的诙谐好脾气一变而为闪电般的义愤或盛怒；薄薄的鼻子、阔阔的嘴——他这张脸，几乎在任何场合都能赢得敬意，至少也能引人注目。他穿着宽大松垂的衣裳，颇为从容地裹住他那魁梧的身形，反倒为他那副气派添了几分雍容。许多人乍一看都说他像那位著名的肯塔基人亨利·沃特森（Henry Watterson），他也确实与那位「亨利老爷（Marse Henry）」有不容置疑的相似之处。

===Original===
The judge’s life had been a curious combination of contradictions. He had fought valiantly in the Confederate army as a major, serving under “Stonewall” Jackson, whose memory he worshipped second only to that of his wife, who had died some ten years before. He bore a long scar, reminder of the wound that had laid him low during the battle of Atlanta. His mode of brushing his hair back was adopted to cover the mark, but when he talked, as he loved to do, of his martial experiences, he would always, at the same time in the narrative, brush, with one sweep of his hand, the hair down over his forehead and reveal the jagged scar of which he was inordinately proud.

===Chinese===
法官的一生，是一个由矛盾凑成的奇特组合。他当年在邦联军（Confederate army）里官至少校，奋勇作战，曾在「石墙」杰克逊（“Stonewall” Jackson）麾下效力——他对这位将军的追念，仅次于对亡妻的追念，妻子大约已去世十年。他身上留着一道长长的伤疤，那是亚特兰大战役中把他放倒的那处创伤留下的。他把头发往后梳的这种做法，原本就是为遮住这道疤，可每当他像他所爱做的那样，谈起自己的戎马生涯时，总会在讲到同一处时，用手一拂，把头发披到前额上，露出那道参差的伤疤——他对此自负得了不得。

===Original===
With the end of the Civil War, he had reconciled himself to the result though it had meant the loss of most of his wealth. He harboured little bitterness towards the North, unlike most of his comrades in arms who never were willing to forgo any opportunity to vent their venomous hatred of their conquerors. Judge Stevenson had counselled against such a spirit. So vigorously had he done it, he had alienated most of those who had been his closest friends. Following a speech he had delivered at one of the reunions of Confederate veterans in which he urged his comrades at least to meet halfway the overtures of friendliness from the North, he had been denounced from the floor of the convention as a “Yankee-lover,” and threatened with violence. Judge Stevenson with flashing eye and belligerent manner had jumped to his feet, offered to fight any man, or any ten men, who thought him guilty of treachery to the cause of the Confederacy, and when none accepted the challenge, denounced them as cowards and quit the convention.

===Chinese===
内战结束后，他已对这结局心平气和了，尽管这意味着他失去了大半家财。他对北方几乎不存什么怨恨，不像他那些老战友——他们从来不肯放过任何一个机会，去发泄他们对征服者的恶毒仇恨。史蒂文森法官曾劝人不要抱这种心态。他劝得那样激烈，以至于疏远了大多数原本最亲密的朋友。有一次，在邦联老兵（Confederate veterans）的一场团聚会上，他发表演说，劝老战友们至少要对北方示好的姿态接个半步，当场便被人从会场席上痛斥为「北佬党（Yankee-lover）」，还受到了动武的威胁。史蒂文森法官目光炯炯、一副好斗的模样，霍地跳起来，邀战任何一个、或任何十个认为他对邦联事业不忠的人；见无人应战，便骂他们是懦夫，拂袖而去，退出了大会。

===Original===
He had hoped that, with the passing on, one by one, of the unreconstructed veterans of the Confederacy, a newer and less embittered generation, with no personal memories of the gall of defeat, would right things. Instead had come the rise of the poor whites with none of the culture and refinement of the old Southern aristocracy, a nation of petty minds and morals, vindictive, vicious, dishonest, and stupid. Lacking in nearly all the things that made the old South, at least the upper crust of it, the most civilized section of America at that time, he saw his friends and all they stood for inundated by this flood of crudeness and viciousness, until only a few remained left high and dry like bits of wreckage from a foundered ship cast up on the shore to rot away, while all around them raged this new regime, no longer poor in purse but eternally impoverished in culture and civilizing influences. On these the judge spat his contempt and he poured upon their unconcerned heads the vials of his venom and wrath.

===Chinese===
他曾盼望，随着那些至死不肯服输的邦联老兵一个接一个离世，一个更新、积怨更浅的一代人——他们不曾亲尝过战败的苦胆——会把事情拨正过来。可取而代之的，却是穷白人（poor whites）的得势，他们身上没有一丝旧南方贵族阶层的文化与教养，整个儿是一帮心胸狭隘、品行鄙陋、报复心重、阴毒、不老实、又愚蠢的人。旧南方——至少是旧南方的上流社会——之所以成为当时美国最文明的角落，靠的几乎全是他这一辈人所具备的种种品格；如今他们在这些方面几乎一无所有，他眼睁睁看着自己的朋友和他们所代表的一切，被这股粗鄙与毒恶的洪水吞没，直到只剩下寥寥几人，像一艘沉船漂散上岸的几片残骸，被搁在高处任其朽烂，而他们周围狂飙激荡的，却是这一套新秩序——钱袋不再瘪了，文化与教化却永远贫瘠。对这些人，法官啐之以鄙夷，把他的毒涎怒火一股脑儿倾到他们那毫不在意的头上。

===Original===
The second devastating blow he suffered was the succumbing, one by one, of his children to the new order. Nancy, his eldest daughter, had run away from home and married a merchant whose wealth had been gained through the petty thievery of padding accounts and other sharp practices on poorer whites and Negroes. Mary Ann, his other daughter, whom he loved above all others of his children, had fallen victim to an unfortunate love affair with a dashing but worthless son of their next-door neighbour. She had died in giving birth to her child, which, fortunately, the judge thought, had been born dead. His son had “gone in for politics.” He had been successful, as success was measured by the present-day South, but in his father’s eyes, judged by the uncompromising standards of that member of an older and nobler generation, he had sunk to levels of infamy from which he could never recover.

===Chinese===
他所受的第二记沉重打击，是自己的孩子们也一个接一个向这新秩序屈服了。大女儿南希离家出走，嫁给了一个商人——那人的钱财是靠在更穷的白人和黑人身上虚报账目、玩弄种种欺诈伎俩这种小偷小摸勾当捞来的。另一个女儿玛丽·安，是他在所有孩子里最爱的一个，却成了与隔壁人家那个神气活现、却一无所长的儿子一桩不幸恋情的牺牲品。她死于分娩，而她生下的那个孩子——法官以为这是万幸——是个死婴。他的儿子「搞起了政治」。以今日南方衡量成功的尺度来看，他是成功了，但在父亲眼里，用老一辈、高贵一辈那毫不通融的标准来衡量，他已堕入了永远也翻不了身的下流地步。

===Original===
The crowning misfortune dealt the judge by an unkind fate was the loss of his gentle, kindly wife. She had uncomplainingly borne their misfortunes one after another, had calmed and soothed her husband’s irascible tantrums, had been a haven to which he could come and find repose when buffeted by a world which he did not and could not understand. As long as she lived, he had been able to bear up despite the bitter disappointments life had dealt him. He had gone away to try a case in a nearby county, had returned after a two days’ absence and found her with a severe cold and fever.

For three weeks he did not leave her bedside, drove away in anger the trained nurse Dr. Bennett brought to the house, ministered gently to his wife’s every need, and held her in his arms as she breathed her last breath. Frantic at this last and most crushing blow, he cursed the doctor, though Dr. Bennett had done all he could in his bungling way, cursed God, cursed everything and everybody he could think of in his grief. He never recovered from this loss. His hair rapidly became white, he neglected his profession and sat by the hour, his eyes half closed, dreaming of his dead wife.…

===Chinese===
无情的命运给法官降下的最致命的不幸，是失去了他那位温柔善良的妻子。她毫无怨言地承受了他们一桩接一桩的不幸，平息、抚慰过丈夫那一阵阵的暴躁脾气，是他被一个他既不理解、也无法理解的世界捶打得疲了时，可以归来歇息的港湾。只要她还活着，无论生活给了他多少痛心的失望，他都还撑得住。有一次他离家去邻县出庭办一桩案子，走了两天，回来便见她染了重感冒，发起烧来。

整整三个礼拜，他没有离开过她的床边，愤怒地赶走了贝内特医生（Dr. Bennett）带到家里来的那个受过训练的护士，亲力亲为、温柔地照料妻子的一切需要，并在她咽下最后一口气时把她抱在怀里。这最后一记、也是最沉重的一击让他几乎发狂，他咒骂医生——尽管贝内特医生已用他那笨拙的方式尽了一切力量——咒骂上帝，咒骂他在悲恸中想得起来的每一件事、每一个人。这一打击他始终没有恢复过来。他的头发很快变白了，他荒疏了自己的业务，一坐就是几个钟头，两眼半阖，梦着他那亡妻……

===Original===
Had he chosen to adapt himself to the new order, he could have made money. This, however, he refused to do. He boasted proudly that never had he cheated any man or been a party to any transaction from which he emerged with any stain on his honour. Friend he was to all in his gentle, kindly manner—a relic of a day that had passed.…

He started, roused from one of his usual reveries, when Kenneth knocked on the open door. The gentle breezes of late spring stirred the mane of white hair as he brought his chair to the floor with a thump.

法官把雪茄挪到嘴的一角，从另一角喷出一股烟叶汁，每一滴都端端正正落在十英尺开外那只盛锯末的箱子里。他接着说：

「南方害的正是这个病。她为了把尼格拉（nigras）压下去，残暴、狡诈、欺瞒得太久了，想正派也正派不起来了。要是这样做到底能捞着什么，倒也还有几分道理可言，可他们偏偏闭上了眼，不肯看见像你这样的尼格拉，再也不会像你爹、像你爹那一辈人那样任他们摆布了。」

「我们该怎么办——我们能做什么呢？」肯尼思问。他从未想到，连史蒂文森法官这么好的人，竟已把事情想得像他们这场谈话所表明的那样透彻。他觉得，既然像法官这样的人也这样想、这样说，局势也就并非全然绝望。也许，他们正是那一点面酵，终将去影响南方那一整块愚昧与恶毒的死面。

===Original===
“What are we going to do?” echoed the elder man. “God knows—I don’t! Mebbe the lid will blow off some day—then there would be hell to pay! One thing’s going to help, and that’s nigras pulling up stakes and going North. When some of these white folks begin to see their fields going to seed, they’ll begin to realize how much they need the nigra—just like some of ’em are seeing already.”

“But are they seeing it in the right way?” asked Kenneth. “Instead of trying to make things better so Negroes are willing to stay in the South, they’re trying more oppressive methods than ever before. They’re beating up labour agents, charging them a thousand dollars for licences, lynching more Negroes, and robbing them more than ever.”

“Oh, they’ll be fools enough until the real pinch comes. Far’s I can see, instead of stopping nigras from going North, them things are hurrying them up. Wait till it hits their pocketbooks hard. Then the white people’ll get some sense.”

===Chinese===
「我们该怎么办？」老人跟着重复，「天知道——我可不知道！说不定哪天锅盖会被顶飞——到那时候就有大乱子好瞧了！有一桩事倒是要起作用的，那就是尼格拉卷铺盖往北走。等到这些白人有几个眼看着自家地里的庄稼荒了，他们才会开始明白他们多么离不开尼格拉（nigra）——就像他们当中有些人已经看出来了那样。」

「可他们看出来的是正路吗？」肯尼思问。「他们不去想办法把事情弄好些，让黑人乐意留在南方，反倒使出比从前更狠的压迫手段。他们殴打招工的人（labour agents），向他们索一千美元的执照费，私刑处死的黑人比以前更多，对他们的劫掠也比以前更甚。」

「噢，他们要犯傻，一直犯到真正被掐住的那一天。就我所见，这些手段非但拦不住尼格拉往北走，反倒催着他们快走。等到这事真砸了他们的钱袋，白人自然会明白过来。」

===Original===
“Let’s hope so,” was Kenneth’s rejoinder as he rose to go. “It’s been mighty comforting to talk like this with you, Judge. Things don’t seem so hopeless when we’ve got friends like you.”

“’Tain’t nothing. Nothing at all,” replied the judge. “Just like to talk with somebody’s got some sense. It’s a pity you’re coloured, Ken, you got too much sense to be a nigra.”

Kenneth laughed.

“From all we’ve been saying, a coloured man’s got to have some sense or else he’s in a mighty poor fix nowadays.”

He did not resent the old man’s remark, for he knew the judge could not understand that he was much more contented as a member of a race that was struggling upward than he would have been as one of that race that expended most of its time and thought and energy in exploiting and oppressing others. The judge followed him to the door promising to draw up the necessary legal documents for the cooperative society. When Kenneth broached the subject of payment, the old man waved his hand again in protest.

“Ain’t got long to live, so’s I got to do what little I can to help. ’Tain’t much I can do, but I’ll help all I can.”

===Chinese===
「但愿如此，」肯尼思一边起身告辞，一边应道，「能跟您这样谈一谈，真是莫大的安慰，法官。有您这样的朋友在，事情好像也没那么叫人绝望了。」

「这没什么。一点也没什么，」法官答道，「不过是喜欢跟个还有点头脑的人说说话罢了。可惜你是有色人，肯，你有这么多头脑，做尼格拉（nigra）太可惜了。」

肯尼思笑了。

「照我们方才说的这些来看，一个有色人要是不长点心眼，眼下这世道可就要吃大亏了。」

他并不怪老人这句话，因为他知道，法官不可能理解：他身为一个正奋力向上攀登的种族的一员，远比做一个把大半时间、心思和精力都花在剥削压迫他人之上的种族的一员，要心安理得得多。法官送他到门口，答应替合作社把必要的法律文书拟好。肯尼思提起付报酬的事，老人又是一摆手，连连推辞。

「我也活不了多久了，能做点小小的事帮衬帮衬，总得做。我能做的不多，可我一定尽力而为。」

===Original===
Thanking him, Kenneth started to leave, but the judge recalled him after he had reached the hallway. “Ken, just consider all I said as between us. Can’t tell what folks’d say if they knew I been running on like this.”

There was almost a note of pleading in his voice. Kenneth assured the judge their conversation would be treated as confidential. As he walked home, he reflected on the anomalous position the judge and men like him occupied, hemmed in, oppressed, afraid to call their souls their own, creatures of the Frankenstein monster their own people had created which seemed about to rise up and destroy its creators. No, he said to himself, he would much rather be a Negro with all his problems than be made a moral coward as the race problem had made the white people of the South.

===Chinese===
肯尼思道了谢，正要走，可他已走到过道里时，法官又把他叫住了。「肯，我方才说的这些，就当只是你我二人之间的话。要是让人知道我这样喋喋不休，谁知道人家会怎么说。」

他声音里几乎带上了一丝央求的味道。肯尼思向法官保证，这场谈话一定当作私话守密。他一路走回家，心里琢磨着法官以及像他那样的人所处的那个尴尬境地：被四面围住、被压迫、连自己的灵魂都不敢认作自己的——他们都是自己本族一手造出来的那个弗兰肯斯坦（Frankenstein）怪物的造物，而那怪物如今似乎就要抬起头来，反过来毁灭它的造物主了。不，他对自己说，他宁可做一个黑人，承受做黑人的所有难题，也不愿像种族问题那样，被弄得成了道德上的懦夫——它已把南方的白人弄成了这个样子。

===Original===
The judge stood at the window, dim with the dust of many months, and gazed at Kenneth’s broad back as he swung down Lee Street. Long after he had disappeared, the old man stood there, chewing on the cigar which by now was a mangled mass of wet tobacco. At last he turned away and resumed his seat in the comfortable old chair where Kenneth had found him. He shook his head slowly, doubtfully, and murmured, half to himself, half to the dusty, empty room:

“Hope this thing turns out all right. Hope he don’t get in no trouble. But even if he does, there’ll be more like him coming on—and they got too much sense to stand for what nigras been made to suffer. Lord, if we only had a few white folks who had some sense…”

It was almost a prayer.

===Chinese===
法官站在窗前——窗玻璃蒙了几个月的灰，已经昏暗不清——目送肯尼思宽阔的背影沿着李街大步流星地走远。在肯尼思消失之后许久，老人仍站在那里，嚼着那支雪茄——此时它已被嚼成一团湿漉漉、烂糟糟的烟叶。他终于转过身，回到肯尼思初来时他所坐的那张舒适旧椅上坐下。他缓缓地、满腹狐疑地摇着头，半自言自语、半对那落满灰尘的空屋子嘟囔：

「这事有个好结果就好了。盼他别惹上什么麻烦。可就算他真惹上了，后头还有更多像他这样的人要来——他们头脑够用，再也不会忍着尼格拉（nigras）受过的那些罪了。主啊，我们要是也有几个有头脑的白人就好了……」

这几乎是一句祷告。

> 译注：
>
> [1] **nigra（尼格拉）**：美国南方白人对黑人的轻蔑读法，介于标准称谓「Negro（黑人）」与种族主义极端贬语「nigger（黑鬼）」之间。原文在此处特地点明——「他既不说 Negro，也不说那不堪入耳的 nigger，而是取了个介于两者之间的 nigra」。这一语音上的细微差别，是南方白人中那种「自以为不算太失礼、实则仍含轻蔑」的典型态度的写照。译文依项目策略以「尼格拉」音译保留其介于两者之间的语气，不软化其贬义，以存其历史与批判语境。法官作为本章中相对开明的白人角色，其使用此词，正折射出即便是较开通的南方白人，也无法摆脱那一套根深蒂固的种族轻蔑——这亦是怀特批判笔锋所指之一。
