## VII / 七

===Original===
Central City was the county seat of Smith County. The morning after the murder of Bud Ware, Kenneth went down to the County Court House to file his report on the death. It was a two-story building, originally of red brick but now of a faded brownish red through the rains and sun of many years. It sat back from the street about fifty feet and was surrounded by a yard covered here and there with bits of grass but for the most part clear of all vegetation, its red soil trampled by many feet on “co’t day.” The steps were worn thin through much wear of heavy boots. On either side of the small landing at the top, there hung a bulletin board on which were pasted or tacked yellow notices of sheriff’s sales, rewards for the arrest of criminals, and other court documents. The floor of the dark and narrow hallway was stained a reddish colour by the mud and dust from the feet of those who had entered the building. Just inside the doorway, on either side, were rectangular boxes filled with sawdust for the convenience of those of a tobacco-chewing disposition, which included most of the male population. The condition of the floor around the boxes seemed to indicate that only a few of these had realized for what purpose the boxes had been placed there. Over all was a liberal coating of the dust that had blown in the door and windows.

===Chinese===
中央城是史密斯县的县城。巴德·韦尔被杀的第二天早上，肯尼思下楼到县法院去递交那份死亡报告。法院是一栋两层楼房，原本是红砖砌的，经过多年风吹日晒，如今已褪成褐红色。它从街面退后约莫五十英尺，四周是一片院子，这里那里生着零星的草，大半地方寸草不生，红色的泥土被「开庭日」上来来往往的脚踩得结结实实。台阶被沉重的靴子磨得又薄又光。顶端那块小平台两侧，各挂着一块公告板，上面糊着、钉着一张张发黄的告示——警长的拍卖、缉拿罪犯的悬赏，还有别的法庭文书。又暗又窄的门厅里，地板被进进出出的人脚带来的泥灰染成了泛红的颜色。门里两侧各放着一只长方形的木箱，里头装满锯末，是给好嚼烟草的人吐口水用的——而这里的男人几乎个个都好嚼烟草。木箱周围地板的情形看上去，似乎这群人里没几个明白这木箱放在那里究竟是干什么用的。到处都厚厚地落着一层灰，那是从门窗吹进来的。

===Original===
Entering the office of the County Health Commissioner, Kenneth found that dignitary in his shirtsleeves, feet comfortably placed on top of his desk.

“Good morning, Mr. Lane. I’ve come to make a report of a death.”

At the sound of Kenneth’s voice, County Commissioner of Health Henry Lane turned in his chair without moving his feet to see who it was that had entered. Long, lanky, a two days’ growth of red beard on his face, Mr. Lane removed the corncob pipe from his mouth with a rising and falling of a prominent Adam’s apple. Seeing that his visitor was only a Negro, he replaced his pipe in his mouth and, between several jerky puffs to get it going again, querulously replied:

“Can’t you see I’m busy? Why don’t you save up them repo’ts till you git a passel of them, and then bring ’em in? Got no time t’ be writin’ up niggers’ deaths, anyhow. Ev’ry time I turn ’round, some nigger’s gittin’ carved up or shot or somepin’.”

===Chinese===
肯尼思走进县卫生委员的办公室，只见那位大人物只穿着衬衫，两只脚舒舒服服地架在办公桌上。

「早上好，莱恩先生。我来报一桩死亡。」

听见肯尼思的声音，县卫生委员亨利·莱恩在椅子里转过身来——脚却没挪动——看看进来的是谁。他身形又长又瘦，脸上是两天没刮的红胡子，把玉米芯烟斗从嘴里取下时，突出的喉结上下滚动了一下。一看进来的不过是个黑人，他又把烟斗塞回嘴里，抽上几口断断续续的烟气好重新把它抽着，一边没好气地回话：

「你没瞅见我正忙着吗？你干嘛不把那些个报告攒着，攒上一大捆再一块儿送来？反正我哪有闲工夫去给黑鬼（nigger）[1]的死写报告。我每回一转身，准有哪个黑鬼叫人给砍了、给崩了，要不就是出了别的什么岔子。」

===Original===
“I understand it’s the law, Mr. Lane, that deaths of anybody, white or coloured, must be reported by the physician at once.”

“Drat the law. That’s fo’ white folks.”

He drew himself out of his chair with great reluctance and ambled over to the counter, drawing to him a pad and pencil as he turned towards Kenneth.

“What nigger’s dead now?” he inquired.

===Chinese===
「据我所知，莱恩先生，按规矩，无论白人还是有色人，谁死了，大夫都得立刻上报。」

「规矩见鬼去吧。规矩是给白人定的。」

他老大不情愿地从椅子里把自己拖起来，慢吞吞地踱到柜台前，一面拉过一本便笺簿和一支铅笔，转身朝向肯尼思。

「这回又是哪个黑鬼死了？」他问。

===Original===
“Bud Ware, who lived at 79 Butler Street,” replied Kenneth.

“How’d he die?” was the next question.

“Shot through the abdomen.”

“Know who shot him?”

“Yes. George Parker.”

“Th’ hell you say! And you come in here to repo’t it?”

Kenneth was somewhat startled at the ferocity of the Commissioner’s expression, which had replaced that of laziness and resentment at being disturbed. “I thought it my duty …” he began.

Lane spat disgustedly.

“Duty, Hell! You’re a Goddamned fool and one of these damned niggers that’s always causin’ trouble ’round here. I always said eddication spoiled a nigger and, by God, you prove it. Lemme tell you somepin’—you’d better remember s’long’s you stay ’round these parts. When you hear anything ’bout a white man havin’ trouble with a nigger, you’d better keep your mouth shet. They’s lots of niggers been lynched for less’n you said this mornin’. Ain’t you got sense enough t’ know you hadn’t any business comin’ in here t’ tell me ’bout Mr. Parker? Don’t you know his brother’s sheriff? If y’ aint, goin’ up No’th tuk away what li’l’ sense you might’ve had befo’ you went.”

===Chinese===
「巴德·韦尔，住巴特勒街 79 号，」肯尼思答道。

「他怎么死的？」紧跟着是下一个问题。

「腹部中枪。」

「知道是谁开的枪？」

「知道。乔治·帕克。」

「你他妈胡扯！你还跑来报告这事？」

那位委员方才还是一副懒散、嫌被打扰的神情，此刻已换成一脸凶相，肯尼思不禁有些吃惊。「我以为这是我的本分……」他开口道。

莱恩厌恶地啐了一口。

「本分，见鬼！你是个该死的大傻瓜，是这一带那种成天给这儿惹麻烦的混账黑鬼之一。我早说过，读书会把黑鬼宠坏，老天作证，你这就是明证。我告诉你件事——只要你还在这地界待着，最好给我记牢了：凡是听见白人跟黑鬼之间闹了什么事，你最好把嘴闭紧。有多少黑鬼，就因为比你今早说的还轻的事儿，就遭了私刑（lynching）。难道你连这点见识都没有——你压根儿就不该上这儿来跟我说帕克先生的事？你不知道他兄弟是警长？要是上北方那一趟把你走以前那丁点见识都给走没了的话。」

===Original===
Kenneth stood silent, a deep red flush suffusing his face, while the official continued his vituperative tirade. His fists, thrust deep into his pockets, were clenched until they hurt, but he did not feel the pain. He longed to take that long, yellow, unshaven neck in his hands and twist it until Lane’s eyes popped out and his face turned black. He knew it would be suicide if he did it. He realized now that he had done an unwise thing in telling Lane who had killed Bud Ware—he should have remembered and said that he did not know. If he was going to stay in the South, he would have to remember these things.

When Lane had paused for breath, Kenneth bade him good morning and left the room. As he went down the steps, he heard Lane shouting after him:

“You’d better not lemme hear o’you doin’ any talkin’ ’bout this. If y’ do, you’ll fin’ yo’self bein’ paid a visit one o’ these nights by the Kluxers!”

===Chinese===
肯尼思站着不作声，一脸涨得通红，任那官员一连串地骂下去。他两拳深深插在口袋里，攥得发疼，可他觉不出疼来。他恨不得双手掐住那根又长又黄、没刮过的脖子，一直拧到莱恩眼珠凸出、脸憋成黑色才罢。他心里清楚，真这么做无异于自寻死路。他这会儿才明白，自己把杀巴德·韦尔的凶手告诉莱恩，是做了一件蠢事——他本该记着，说自己不知道。既然他要在南方待下去，这些事他就得记牢。

等莱恩停下来喘气，肯尼思道了声早安，走出了屋。他下台阶时，听见莱恩在身后嚷：

「你最好别让我听见你到处瞎说这件事。你要是说了，迟早哪天夜里，会有人上门来拜访你——三K党徒（Kluxers）！」

===Original===
Hardly had Kenneth left the court house before Lane rushed as fast as his natural indolence would permit him into the office of Sheriff Robert Parker—known throughout the county as “She’f Bob.” Lane was so indignant he spluttered in trying to speak. The sheriff looked at him amusedly and counselled:

“Ca’m yo’self, Henry. What’s eatin’ you?”

“Bob, d’you know George shot and killed a nigger buck over in ‘Darktown’ yestiddy mornin’ named Ware?” Lane finally managed to get out.

“Yeh. What about it? George tol me about it las’ night,” was the sheriff’s easy reply.

“Well, that nigger doctor Harper who’s been up No’th studyin’ and come back here las’ fall, come into my office this mornin’ to repo’t it, and he had the gall t’ tell me George done it.”

“Th’ black bastard! What th’ hell’s he got to do with it?”

“Said it was his duty. You bet I tol him good an’ plenty where he got off at. Guess he won’t come in here repo’tin’ no more ‘accidents’ like George run into.”

===Chinese===
肯尼思前脚刚离开法院，莱恩后脚便以他那天生的懒散所能容许的最快速度，冲进了罗伯特·帕克警长的办公室——警长在全县都被人叫作「鲍勃警长（She’f Bob）」。莱恩气得说话都直喷唾沫。警长饶有兴味地瞧着他，劝道：

「消消气，亨利。谁招你惹你了？」

「鲍勃，你知不知道乔治昨天早上在『黑镇』（Darktown）[2]开枪打死了一个叫韦尔的年轻黑鬼（nigger buck）？」莱恩总算把话憋了出来。

「知道。怎么啦？乔治昨晚跟我说了，」警长轻描淡写地答道。

「是这样，那个上北方去读过书、去年秋天回来的黑鬼医生哈珀，今早进我办公室来报告这事，他居然胆大包天，跟我说是乔治干的。」

「这黑皮的狗杂种！这事他妈的关他屁事？」

「他说这是他的本分。你放心，我把他好生训了一顿，叫他认清自己的斤两。我估摸他下回再不敢来报告乔治碰上的这种『意外』了。」

===Original===
Sheriff Parker’s face had assumed the colour of an overripe tomato as he jumped to his feet and banged his right fist on the table with a resounding thwack.

“I’ll keep my eye on that nigger,” he promised. “His daddy was as good a nigger as ever I did see, but they ain’t no way o’tellin’ what these young bucks’ll do. Roy Ewing was saying only this mornin’ that Bob, that nigger doctor’s kid brother, was tellin’ him the other day that he’d have to stop them boys ’roun’ the sto’ from botherin’ with th’ nigger gals when they pass by. Humph! They ain’t no nigger gal that’s pure after she’s reached fo’teen years ol’. Yep, I’ll jus’ kep my eye on those boys, and the first chance I git, I’ll—!”

His eyes narrowed in malevolent fashion as he left his threat unuttered.

===Chinese===
帕克警长的脸涨得像一只熟透的番茄，他一跃而起，右拳砰地一声砸在桌上。

「我会盯着那黑鬼的，」他应承道。「他爹是我这辈子见过的最听话的黑鬼，可这些年轻气盛的家伙会干出什么，谁也说不准。就在今天早上，罗伊·尤因还说，那个黑鬼医生的小弟鲍勃，前两天居然跟他说，得管管店门口那帮小子，叫他们别在那些黑鬼丫头路过时纠缠人家。哼！黑鬼丫头过了十四岁，哪还有一个身子干净的。行，我就盯着这哥俩，一有机会，我就——！」

他眼睛恶毒地眯起来，把后半句威胁咽了下去。

===Original===
In the meantime, Kenneth had gone home. He hesitated to talk the matter over with Bob or tell him what had happened to Bud Ware or what had taken place at the court house that morning. Bob was so hotheaded and insults made him angry so easily, he was afraid of what might be the outcome if Bob knew what had occurred. He would breathe a deep sigh of relief when Bob left in the fall to go back to school. Up in Atlanta there wouldn’t be so many chances for Bob to run up against these white people and, besides, Bob’s studies would keep him busy, leaving little time to brood over the indignities he had suffered. Kenneth determined that when Bob had finished his course at Atlanta University, he would urge him to go to Columbia University or Harvard and study law, and then settle down in some Northern city. It wouldn’t do for Bob to come back as he had done to Central City. Sooner or later Bob’s fiery temper would give way.

===Chinese===
这时肯尼思已经回了家。他犹豫着要不要把这事跟鲍勃谈一谈，要不要告诉鲍勃巴德·韦尔出了什么事，以及今早法院里又发生了什么。鲍勃脾气太爆，受辱又太容易动怒，他不敢想鲍勃一旦知道了这一切，会闹出什么结果。等到秋天鲍勃回亚特兰大去上学，他才能长长地松一口气。在亚特兰大，鲍勃撞上这些白人的机会不会这么多，再说，鲍勃的功课会让他忙得没多少时间去反复咀嚼自己所受的那些屈辱。肯尼思打定主意，等鲍勃在亚特兰大大学读完书，他要劝他去哥伦比亚大学（Columbia University）或哈佛（Harvard）读法律，然后在某个北方城市安顿下来。鲍勃可不能再像他这样回中央城来了。迟早有一天，鲍勃那火爆性子会撑不住的。

===Original===
He wondered to whom he could turn to talk this thing out. He felt that if he didn’t have a chance soon to unburden his soul to somebody, he would go insane. He thought of his mother. No, that wouldn’t do. His mother had enough to worry about without taking his burdens on her shoulders.

Mamie? No, she wouldn’t do either. She had no business knowing about the sordidness of the affair of Bud Ware and Nancy and George Parker. All her life she had been sheltered and kept away, as much as is possible in a Southern town, from the viciousness and filth and brutality of the race relations of the town.

===Chinese===
他想不出能找谁把这桩事掰开来谈。他觉得，要是不尽快找个人把心里的话倾吐一番，他非疯了不可。他想到了母亲。不行，这不成的。母亲自己烦心的事已经够多，不能再让她把自己的担子也压到肩上。

玛米？不行，她也不成。巴德·韦尔、南希、乔治·帕克之间那桩肮脏事，她不该知道。她这辈子一直被呵护着，尽可能远远地隔开——在南方小城里，能隔开多少就隔开多少——不让她沾上这座城里那种族关系里的恶毒、污秽和暴戾。

===Original===
Mr. Wilson, the clergyman? He was ignorant and coarse, but he had lived in South Georgia all his life and he would know better what to do than anybody else. He determined to go and talk with Mr. Wilson that evening as soon as he was free. He had hardly made the decision when Mr. Wilson himself entered the reception room and called out to Kenneth as he sat in his office:

“Good mawnin’, Brudder Harper. It certainly has done my heart good to see you attendin’ chu’ch ev’ry Sunday with your folks. Mos’ of these young men and women, as soon’s they get some learning, thinks they’s too good to ’tend chu’ch. But, as I says to them all th’ time, th’ Lawd ain’t goin’ t’ bless none of them, even if they is educated, if they don’t keep close to Him.”

Kenneth rose and showed his visitor to a seat. He did so with an inward repugnance as the coarseness of the man repelled him. Mr. Wilson seemed always overheated even in the coldest weather, and his face shone with a greasiness that seemed to indicate that his body excreted oil instead of perspiration. Yet, perhaps this man could give him some ray of light, if there was any to be had.

He told Mr. Wilson of his experiences of the past two days. The preacher’s eyes widened with a mild surprise and the unctuous, benevolent mask which he wore most of his waking hours seemed to drop rapidly as he heard Kenneth through to the end without comment. At the same time he dropped his illiterate speech much to Kenneth’s surprise, when he finally spoke.

“Dr. Harper, I’ve been watching you since you came back here. I knew that you were trying to keep away from this trouble that’s always going on around here. That’s just why I came here today. I think it would be a good thing to talk over these things and try to find a way to avoid any trouble.”

Kenneth looked at him in surprise, not at the idea of holding a meeting, but at the language the man was using.

肯尼思惊讶地望着他——惊讶的不是开会这个提议，而是这人此刻说话用的语言。

“I remember when I first started preaching over at Valdosta. I was just out of school and was filled up with the ambition to raise my people out of their ignorance. I was determined I would free them from a religion that didn’t do anything for them but make them shout and holler on Sunday. I was going to give them some modern religion based on intelligence instead of just on feeling and emotion.”

He chuckled throatily in recollecting the spiritual and religious crusade on which he had based such exalted hopes.

“I preached to them and told them of Aristotle and Shakespeare and Socrates. One Sunday, after I’d preached what I thought was a mighty fine sermon, one old woman came up after the services and said to me: ‘Brer Wilson, dat’s a’ right tellin’ us ’bout Shakespeare and Homer and all dem other boys. But what we want is for you t’ tell us somethin’ ’bout Jesus!’ ”

===Chinese===
「我还记得我刚到瓦尔多斯塔（Valdosta）开始讲道那会儿。我刚从学校出来，满心是要把我的乡亲从愚昧里拔出来的抱负。我打定主意，要让他们摆脱掉那种除了教他们在礼拜天上大声喊叫、呼天抢地之外别无用处的宗教。我要给他们一种建立在理智之上、而不只是建立在感情和情绪之上的、新式的信仰。」

他低沉地笑了笑，回想起当年自己曾寄予厚望的那场精神与宗教的十字军。

「我跟他们讲道，给他们讲亚里士多德、莎士比亚、苏格拉底。有个礼拜天，我自以为讲了一篇极精彩的道，礼拜做完，一个老太太走到我跟前，对我说：『威尔逊兄弟啊，你给我们讲莎士比亚、讲荷马、讲那一帮子人，那是挺好的。可我们想要的是你给我们讲讲耶稣！』」

===Original===
Kenneth laughed with the preacher at the old woman’s insistence on his not straying from the religion to which they were used.

“I had to discard my high-flown theories and come down to my folks if I wanted to do any good at all.”

He continued:

“These same folks, however, don’t want you to come down too close. Like all people with little education, whether they’re black, white, or any other colour, they like to look up to their leaders. So I use a few big words now and then which have a grand and rolling sound, and they feel that I am even more wonderful because I do know how to use big words but don’t use them often.”

===Chinese===
肯尼思和牧师一起笑起来，笑那老太太执拗地不让他偏离他们所习惯的那种信仰。

「我要是真想做成点什么事，就只能丢开我那些高调的理论，回到我那些乡亲中间去。」

他接着说：

「可这些乡亲，又不希望你和他们贴得太近。凡是读书不多的人，不论黑人、白人、还是别的什么肤色，都一样——他们喜欢仰望自己的领头人。所以我有意时不时甩出几个大字眼，听着响亮气派，他们就愈发觉得我了不起——因为我知道怎么用这些大字眼，却又不常用。」

===Original===
He paused while Kenneth looked at this man and saw him in a new light. He had known that Mr. Wilson, many years before coming to Central City, had attended a theological seminary in Atlanta, and he had wondered how a man could attend a school of theology of any standing and yet use such poor English. It had never occurred to him that it might be deliberate.

“And then there’s another reason,” continued Reverend Wilson. “The white folks here are mighty suspicious of any Negro who has too much learning, according to their standards. They figure he’ll be stirring up the Negroes to fighting back when any trouble arises. I had to make a decision many years ago. I decided that somebody had to help these poor coloured folks bear their burdens, and to comfort and cheer them. I knew that if I came out and said the things I thought and felt, I would either be taken out of my house some night and lynched, or else I’d be run out of town. So I decided that I’d smile and bear it and be what the white folks think they want—what the coloured folks call a ‘white man’s nigger.’ It’s been mighty hard, but the Lord has given me the strength somehow or other to stand it this far.”

===Chinese===
他停了停，肯尼思望着这个人，对他有了新的认识。他知道威尔逊先生许多年前——在来中央城之前——曾在亚特兰大的一家神学院读过书，他一直纳闷，一个人怎么能在一家像样的神学院读过书，却说起那么蹩脚的英文来。他从未想过，这也许是故意的。

「还有另一个缘故，」威尔逊牧师接着说。「这儿的白人，对任何一个按他们的标准读书太多的黑人，都满腹狐疑。他们觉得，一有风吹草动，这种人就会去煽动黑人群起反抗。许多年前我不得不做一个抉择。我认定，总得有人帮这些可怜的有色人扛一扛他们的担子，安慰他们、给他们鼓劲。我知道，要是我把心里想的、嘴里该说的都亮出来，要么哪天夜里我会被人从家里拖出去遭了私刑，要么就是被赶出城去。所以我就决定，笑着忍下这一切，去做白人以为他们想要的那种人——也就是有色人所说的『白人的黑鬼（white man’s nigger）』[3]。这一路走来不容易，可主到底给了我力量，让我撑到了今天。」

===Original===
With his deliberately imperfect English, there had gone from the preacher’s face the subservient smile. Kenneth felt his heart warming to this man. He found his feeling of distaste and repulsion dissipating, now that the shell had been removed and he saw beneath the surface. The simile of the protective device of the chameleon came to his mind. Yes, the Negro in the South had many things in common with the chameleon—he had to be able to change his colour figuratively to suit the environment of the South in order to be allowed to stay alive. His own trouble with the Parkers and Lane seemed much more trivial now than before. He looked at Mr. Wilson and asked:

“What’s the purpose of this meeting tonight? How can I help, Reverend Wilson?”

“It’s like this. A good part of my congregation is made up of folks who live out in the country. They’ve had a lot of trouble for years getting honest settlements from the landlords on whose land they work. Within the last five years, two of my members have been lynched when they wouldn’t stand for being cheated any longer. The folks out there are in a pretty bad way, and they want us to advise with them as to the best way to act. I haven’t time to go into the details now, but it’ll all be taken up tonight. Can I count on your being there? We need a man like you, with your education.”

Kenneth deliberated several minutes before giving his answer. What Mr. Wilson wanted him to do was just exactly what he had determined not to do. But what harm could come from attending the meeting? If he didn’t want to take any part in the plans, he didn’t have to. Anyhow, it seemed that the more a man tried to keep away from the race question, the more deeply involved he became in it. Might as well do what little he could to help, if he didn’t have to take too prominent a part. He’d go anyway. He told Reverend Wilson they could look for him that night.

===Chinese===
随着他这番不再遮掩的、故意蹩脚的英语，牧师脸上那副唯唯诺诺的笑也一同消失了。肯尼思觉得自己的心向这个人暖了过去。如今这层外壳卸下了，他看穿了底下的真相，方才那一阵反感和抵触随之散去。变色龙那种自我保护的本事，作为一个比喻，浮上了他的心头。是啊，南方的黑人，与变色龙有许多相通之处——他必须能——比喻意义上地——变换自己的肤色，去迁就南方的环境，才能被允许活下去。他自己与帕克家、与莱恩那一摊子事，此刻看来比先前小得多了。他望着威尔逊先生，问道：

「今晚这个聚会有什么打算？我能帮上什么忙，威尔逊牧师？」

「是这样。我那些会众里头，一大半是住在乡下的乡亲。多年来，他们想从自己为其种地的东家那儿得到一个公道的结算，一直碰钉子。这五年里头，我手底下有两个会众，就因为不肯再忍气吞声地任人坑骗，遭了私刑。乡下的乡亲如今日子很不好过，他们想请咱们给他们出出主意，看怎样行事最妥当。这会儿我没工夫细说，今晚都会一并议一议。我能指望你到场吗？我们需要你这样的人，有你这样的学问。」

肯尼思斟酌了好几分钟才作答。威尔逊先生要他做的，恰恰是他原本打定主意不去做的事。可去参加这个聚会，又能出什么岔子呢？要是不想掺和到那些计划里，他不掺和就是了。再说，一个人越是想躲开种族问题，似乎就越深地陷进去。既然不必抛头露面，那就不妨尽一点绵薄之力。他横竖是要去的。他告诉威尔逊牧师，当晚他们会看到他。
