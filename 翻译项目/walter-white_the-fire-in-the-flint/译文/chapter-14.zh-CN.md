## XIV / 十四

===Original===
It seemed to Kenneth he had just fallen into a troubled slumber when he was aroused by the tinkling of the telephone bell at the side of his bed. It was Hiram Tucker.

“Doc, I reck’n you won’t have to go t’ Atlanty today, after all. My wife, she tol’ me to tell you she’s changed her min’ ’bout that op’ration.… What’s dat?… Naw, suh, she’s kinder skeered she won’ wake up from dat chlo’form.… Yas, suh, yas, suh, I knows ’rangements been made but, Doc, you ain’t married, so you don’t know nuthin’ ’tall ’bout wimmenfolks.… Some day you’ll learn dat when dey says dey ain’t gwine do somethin’ dey’s done sot dere minds on not doing, dey ain’t gwine t’ do it.… Hello.… Hello.… Hello!”

But Kenneth had hung up. He telephoned the local telegraph office to send a wire to the hospital in Atlanta to cancel the arrangements he had made for the operation on the following day, and tumbled back in bed to sleep like a log until late in the morning. He was awakened by Bob, who informed him that the reception room was half filled with patients who were no longer patient at being kept waiting so long. He arose reluctantly, his eyes still filled with sleep. Bob leaned against the wall, hands in pockets, and looked at his brother with a smile of amusement. Kenneth, not thoroughly awakened as yet, paid no attention to him for a time, but at last noticed Bob’s smile.

===Chinese===
肯尼思觉得自己才刚刚坠入一段烦躁的浅睡，就被床边电话铃声叮铃铃地吵醒了。是海勒姆·塔克。

「大夫，我寻思您今儿到底还是不用上亚特兰大去了。我那口子，她叫我跟您说，那手术她改主意不做了。……您说啥？......不，先生，她就是有点儿怕，怕吸了那氯仿（chlo’form）[1]醒不过来。……是，先生，是，先生，我知道都安排好了，可大夫，您没成过家，您一点儿也不懂女人家的事。……赶明儿您就明白了，她们要是说自个儿不做一件事，那就是铁了心不做了，再也不会去做的......喂......喂......喂！」

可肯尼思已经把电话挂了。他给本地电报局打电话，让他们给亚特兰大的医院拍份电报，取消他为次日手术所做的全部安排，然后一头栽回床上，睡得像个死人，一直睡到日头老高。是鲍勃把他叫醒的，告诉他候诊室里已经坐了半屋子病人，等了这么久，都等得不耐烦了。他不情愿地爬起来，眼睛里还满是睡意。鲍勃靠在墙上，两手插在口袋里，带着一脸看好戏的笑望着哥哥。肯尼思还没彻底醒透，有一阵子没理会他，可到底还是注意到了鲍勃的笑。

===Original===
“Why this early morning humour? I’ve seen many possums with a more engaging smile then the one that distorts your face now!” he half-grumblingly, half-cheerfully observed.

Bob but grinned the more at Kenneth’s remark.

“I was just thinking that if Jane could only get one glimpse of you in the morning before breakfast, your chances would be mighty slim with her.”

“Jane? What have my looks to do with her?” Kenneth retorted with some heat, in a vain attempt to spar for time.

Bob addressed the world in general, calling on it for some aid in understanding this brother of his.

“Jane?” he mimicked Kenneth’s tone of surprise. “You talk like a ten-year-old boy with his first love affair. Isn’t he the innocent one, though? Why, you poor maligned creature, everybody in Central City who isn’t blind knows that you are head over heels in love with Jane Phillips. And,” he added as an afterthought, “those who are blind have been told it. But to return to my original observation, if there was some means by which, with all propriety, all the girls in the world who are in love could see, and be seen by, the poor boobs with whom they are so infatuated, marriage-licence bureaus would be closed that day, never to open again.” This last with an omniscient air of worldly wisdom that caused Kenneth to burst into a roar of laughter, while Bob watched him, somewhat discomfited.

===Chinese===
「这一大清早的，你乐什么？我见过好多负鼠，笑起来都比你这会儿扭歪的脸讨人喜欢得多！」他半是嘟囔、半是快活地说。

可鲍勃听了肯尼思这话，反而笑得更欢了。

「我刚才在想，要是简能瞅见你早饭前这副早起的模样，你追她的胜算可就小得可怜喽。」

「简？我的模样跟她有什么相干？」肯尼思带点火气地反唇相讥，徒劳地想拖一拖时间。

鲍勃冲着整个天地发话，求它帮忙参一参他这位哥哥。

「简？」他学肯尼思那惊讶的腔调，「你说话就像个十岁的小男孩，碰上头一桩恋爱的事。他可真够天真的啊！哎，你这受了天大冤枉的可怜虫，中央城里只要不是瞎子，谁不知道你死心塌地地爱上了简·菲利普斯。还有呢，」他想了想又补一句，「瞎的也已经有人告诉他们了。可话又说回我方才那句话，要是有个法子，又能顾全体面，又能让全世界所有正谈着恋爱的姑娘，瞧见、也被瞧见她们爱得死去活来的那些个傻小子，那结婚登记处那天就得关门，再也别想开张了。」最后这几句，他带着一种无所不知的世故老成，惹得肯尼思哄然大笑，鲍勃望着他，多少有点下不来台。

===Original===
“What’re you laughing at?” he demanded in an aggrieved tone. Kenneth laughed all the harder. “Why, you poor little innocent, you haven’t gotten rid of your pin feathers, and yet you are talking as though you were a philosopher like Schopenhauer. You’d better wait until you finish school and see something of the world. Then you can talk a little—though only a little as you did just now. By the way, it’s about time for you to be planning for school this fall. Still thinking about going back to Atlanta?”

“I don’t know what I want to do,” was Bob’s troubled rejoinder. “I’ve seen too much of what’s going on around this town since papa died to be satisfied with school again. I’ve probably seen more of the real sordidness and meanness and deviltry of this place since I’ve been settling up papa’s affairs than you’ll see in five years. At any rate, I hope you don’t,” he finished somewhat doubtfully.

“Bob”—Kenneth walked over and put his arm around his brother’s shoulders—“the trouble with you is that you’re too darned sensitive. I know things aren’t all they ought to be around here, but we’ve got to buckle down and make them that way. And perhaps I’ve seen more of this deviltry than you think.”

He told Bob of what had happened to Nancy Ware the night before. A long whistle of surprise escaped from Bob’s lips.

===Chinese===
「你笑什么？」他愤愤地问。肯尼思反而笑得更厉害了。「哎呀，你这可怜的天真小子，你那身雏毛还没换干净呢，就说话活像个叔本华那样的哲学家了。你最好还是等念完书、见过点世面再说。到那时候你才能讲上一点——也不过像你刚才那样一点点罢了。顺便说一句，你也该筹划今年秋天开学的事了。还是想回亚特兰大去吗？」

「我不知道自己想干什么，」鲍勃忧心忡忡地答道，「自从爸爸过世，我在这镇上见到的那些事太多了，再回学校念书我安不下心。我料理爸爸后事这些日子，见到的这地方真正的肮脏、卑劣、恶毒，多半比你五年里见到的还多。无论如何，我希望你别见到，」他末了又犹疑地补了一句。

「鲍勃，」——肯尼思走过去，一只手搭在弟弟肩上——「你的毛病就是太过敏了。我知道这地方的事不像该有的样子，可咱们得沉下心来，把它扭成那个样子。再说了，这种恶毒的事，我见过的也许比你以为的多。」

他把头天夜里南希·韦尔的遭遇讲给鲍勃听。鲍勃的唇间溜出一声长长的、惊讶的口哨。

===Original===
“And this happened right here in the coloured section?” he asked in surprise. Kenneth nodded in assent.

“I felt they were planning some mischief but I didn’t think they would have the nerve to come right here in ‘Darktown’ and do it. I wonder,” he said musingly, “if that dirty little Jim Archer who said those filthy things to Minnie Baxter that day is a member of the Klan. I passed him on Lee Street this morning and he grinned at me like a cat that has just eaten a fat mouse.”

“He may be,” Kenneth replied. “Nancy Ware told me last night she recognized the voices of Sheriff Parker and Henry Lane and George Parker and two or three other prominent white people here.”

“That settles it,” Bob answered determinedly. “When you first came back here I thought you were foolish to do so after having been in France. I said I was going to get out of this country as soon as I could and live in France or Brazil or any old place where a man isn’t judged by the colour of his skin. But I’ve decided that I’d be a coward if I did run away like that. Ken,” he said in voice that showed he had passed in spite of his years from childhood into the more serious things of manhood, “I’m going to Harvard this fall. I’m going to take whatever course I need to get into the law school. I’m going to make myself the best lawyer they can turn out. And then I’m coming back here to the South like you did and give my time to fighting for my people!”

Bob’s eyes flashed. In them was a light of high resolve such a look as might have shone in the eyes of Garibaldi or of Joan of Arc.

Kenneth said nothing, but he gripped Bob’s hand in his and there passed between the two brothers a look of mutual understanding and sympathy that was more potent and meaningful than words.

===Chinese===
「这事就发生在有色人区？」他惊讶地问。肯尼思点头称是。

「我原觉着他们在打什么坏主意，可没想到他们竟敢直接跑到『黑镇』[2]来动手。我纳闷，」他若有所思地说，「那天对明妮·巴克斯特说那些下流话的那个肮脏的小吉姆·阿切尔，是不是三K党里的人。今早我在李街跟他打了个照面，他冲我咧嘴一笑，活像一只刚吞下肥老鼠的猫。」

「可能是，」肯尼思答道，「南希·韦尔昨夜告诉我，她听出了帕克警长、亨利·莱恩、乔治·帕克，还有本地两三个显赫白人的嗓子。」

「这就定了，」鲍勃毅然答道，「你刚回来那阵子，我想你在法国待过还回来，真是犯傻。我说过我要尽早离开这地方，去法国、巴西，随便哪个不拿肤色论人的旮旯都成。可如今我拿定了主意，要就这么跑了，我便是懦夫。肯，」他以一种嗓音说道——这嗓音显出他虽年纪还轻，却已从童年跨入了成年人那些更正经的事——「今年秋天我要上哈佛（Harvard）去。我要把进法学院该念的课程都念了。我要把自己造就成他们能培养出来的最好的律师。然后我要像你一样回到南方来，把我的时间献给我的人民！」

鲍勃眼睛发亮。眼里那道崇高决心的光，一如从前或许曾在加里波第、在圣女贞德眼中闪耀过的那样。

肯尼思什么也没说，只是把鲍勃的手握在自己手里。两兄弟之间交换了一个彼此心照、彼此怜惜的眼神，那比任何言语都更有力、更深长。

===Original===
Kenneth went down to attend to his patients and nothing more was said of the incident between them. Bob took on a new interest in life. His moodiness, his brooding over the constant irritations and insults he had to suffer in his dealings as a coloured man with the whites of the town, his resentment at the attitude of condescension on the part of the poor and ignorant whites who had neither his intelligence, his education, nor his wealth—all these disappeared in his eager preparations for the new life he had mapped out for himself. He already saw himself a powerful champion of his race and he gloried in that vision with all of the impetuosity and idealistic fervour of youth.

As for Kenneth, he divided his time between his practice, Jane, and the formation of more branches of the N.N.F.C.P.L.

Kenneth knew there was nothing to be done towards the punishment of the men who had so brutally beaten Nancy Ware. He knew that it would even be unwise for him to talk too much about it. If Sheriff Parker was himself a member of the Klan, reporting the outrage to him would be in effect a serving of notice that he was meddling in the affairs of the Klan which might bring disastrous results at a time when Kenneth was most anxious to avoid such a complication, certainly until the cooperative societies were well under way and actively functioning. Much as he chafed under the restraint and at his own impotence in the situation, Kenneth knew that his interference would be a useless and foolhardy butting of his head against a stone wall.

===Chinese===
肯尼思下楼去照看他的病人，他俩之间再没提起这件事。鲍勃对生活有了一番新的兴致。他那阴郁，他因身为有色人、在本镇与白人打交道时不得不忍受的层出不穷的气恼和侮辱而生的郁结，他对那些既无他的才智、又无他的教养、也无他的家产、却偏偏摆出一副居高临下架子的穷而无知的白人的愤懑——这一切，都消融在他为自己规划的新生活所作的热切准备之中了。他已经看见自己成了本族一位强有力的捍卫者，他怀着少年人全部的冲劲和理想主义的热忱，为这一幅前景欢欣鼓舞。

至于肯尼思，他把时间分作三处：行医、简，以及把 N.N.F.C.P.L.[3]的更多分部办起来。

肯尼思知道，要惩罚那些如此凶残地毒打南希·韦尔的人，是无计可施的。他甚至知道，这事自己说太多都不明智。倘若帕克警长本人就是三K党徒，那么把这场暴行去报告他，实际上等于通知对方自己正在插手三K党的事，这在肯尼思最怕节外生枝的当口——至少在各合作社尚未站稳脚跟、切实运转起来之前——可能招来灾难性的后果。尽管这重束缚和他自己在此事上的无能为力令他焦灼难耐，肯尼思心里清楚，他的干预不过是拿脑袋去撞石墙，既无用又冒失。

It occurred to him to tell what had happened to Judge. Stevenson. He could be trusted and was as much opposed to the outlawry of the Klan as Kenneth himself. The judge listened gravely to the end without comment other than a question here and there. “That looks worse than I thought,” he said half to himself. “A few mo’ cracks like that and there’ll be hell to pay ’round here. But ’twon’t do no good for you t’ meddle in it,” he observed in answer to Kenneth’s question as to what he could do. “If Nancy’s right about Bob Parker being in it, your sayin’ anything will only set them on you. You’d better go ahead and get your societies on their feet and then you’ll have somethin’ behin’ you. Then you won’t be playin’ a lone hand.”

As for the coloured people, there were several days of excited gossip over what had happened to Nancy Ware. There was not much to go on, as she had been so frightened by her terrible experience that she refused for once to talk. The only tangible effect was that mysterious parcels marked with the names of household implements began to arrive at the homes of the coloured people, but which contained firearms and ammunition. There was also a noticeable tightening of the lips and the development of a less cordial relationship between white and black. Negroes, feeling that there was no help they could expect from the law, felt that their backs were being slowly pressed against the wall. Within a few hours the old *esprit cordial* between white and black had been wiped out. Negroes who had been happy-go-lucky, carefree, and kindly in manner began to talk among themselves of “dying fighting” if forced to the limit.

他想到把事情原原本本告诉史蒂文森法官。法官靠得住，对三K党的无法无天，他和肯尼思一样深恶痛绝。法官神情凝重地听到末了，只在这里那里插问一句，没作别的评论。「这看起来比我预想的还糟，」他半是自言自语地说，「再这么来几下，这一带可就要大难临头了。不过你去插一手，没半点好处，」他是这样回答肯尼思该怎么办的问话的，「要是南希没认错人，鲍勃·帕克真在里头，那你一说出去，反倒把他们引到你身上来了。你不如先把你的那些团体办起来立住脚，到那时你身后就有了依仗。到那时你也就不是一个人单打独斗了。」

至于有色人这边，南希·韦尔的事闹得他们兴奋地议论了好几天。可也议论不出什么来，她被那场可怖的遭遇吓坏了，破天荒头一回不肯开口。唯一看得见摸得着的影响是：一个个贴着家用器皿名目的神秘包裹，开始送进有色人各家各户的门，里面装的都是枪支弹药。嘴唇也明显地抿紧了，黑白之间原本的关系也不再那么热络。黑人们（Negroes）觉得求诸法律无望，觉得自己正被人一寸一寸地把背脊往墙上摁。几个钟头之内，黑白之间旧日那种*由衷的亲睦*（*esprit cordial*）[4]便荡然无存。那些一向随遇而安、无忧无虑、待人厚道和气的黑人，开始彼此议论起：被逼到尽头，便「拼死一战」。

July came with all its heat. August passed with yet more heat. With the coming of September there had been formed in Smith County alone seven branch societies of the Cooperative and Protective League with a membership of more than twelve hundred. Kenneth worked as one inspired, one who knew neither heat nor cold, fatigue nor hunger. During the day he was busy with his practice, but it mattered not how busy he had been, he was always ready and willing to drive five, ten, fifteen miles at night to aid in establishing new branches or directing and guiding and advising those already established.

The Ashland Branch, through the hard work of Hiram Tucker and Tom Tracy, had enrolled three hundred and fifteen members. In its treasury it had $657.85, to which it was constantly adding as new members were enrolled. At a meeting held during the latter part of August the members decided that they would forgo the purchasing of their supplies in bulk that year but would use the money raised towards prosecuting one of the cases of dishonest settlements when the time came for such settlements, usually in December or January. This step was decided upon after due and lengthy deliberation, as it was felt that if they could end the cheating of the farmers through court action, then these same farmers would have more money through the settlement of their accounts for the present season and could then begin the cooperative buying and distribution the following year.

===Chinese===
七月带着它全部的酷暑来了。八月过去了，暑气更甚。到了九月，光史密斯县一地，就已经成立了合作保护同盟的七个分部，会员总数超过一千二百人。肯尼思干得像个受了感召的人，不知寒暑，不知疲倦，不知饥饿。白天他忙着看诊，可无论白天有多忙，夜里他总是欣然驱车五里、十里、十五里，去帮着建立新分部，或是去指导、指点、参谋那些已经建起来的分部。

阿什兰分部，靠海勒姆·塔克和汤姆·特雷西的苦干，已经招募了三百一十五名会员。库房里有 657.85 元的公积金，还随着新会员的加入在不断添增。八月底开的一次会上，会员们议定：这一年他们暂不集中批量采购物资，而把筹集起来的钱用在来日清算时打一桩东家欺账的官司——这种清算通常在十二月或一月。这一步是经过郑重而长久的商议才定下的，因为他们觉得，要是能借打官司了结对农户的欺蒙，那么这些农户在今季清算账目时就能多拿到些钱，第二年也就可以着手合作采买和分发了。

===Original===
News of the new society that was going to end the unsatisfactory relations sharecroppers had with their landlords spread rapidly throughout the surrounding counties. Letters, crudely and cumbersomely worded and with atrocious spelling, came to Kenneth and often individuals came in person to ask that he come to their counties to organize societies there. Kenneth was elated at this sign of interest. He had expected a great deal of opposition from the coloured farmers. Bickering and carping criticism there was aplenty, but most of them regarded him as a new Moses to lead them into the promised land of economic independence. Minor disputes over authority in the local societies there were in abundance. But none of them was hard to settle, for the members themselves were too eager to get out of bondage to tolerate much petty politics and selfishness on the part of their officers.

As a loyal ally Kenneth learned to rely on Jane more and more. Often she went with him to attend meetings and to talk to groups not yet organized. While Kenneth talked to the men, Jane circulated among the women, who were subtly flattered that one so daintily clad and well educated should spend so much of her time and energy talking to lowly ones like themselves.

Her mother’s health had not been of the best during the summer. That had been throughout the summer her only worry. In August her mother had suffered an attack of paralysis, her second one. Jane decided to remain at home instead of going to Oberlin to resume her music. Dr. Bennett had been dismissed and Kenneth was now treating Mrs. Phillips. During her more serious illness in August, Jane often sat on one side of her mother’s bed until late in the night while Kenneth sat on the other, ministering to the aged woman’s wants. There came a new and stronger feeling of companionship between the two. Often Kenneth would look up suddenly and catch in Jane’s eyes a new tenderness. Without knowing what it meant, he felt a subtly conveyed encouragement in them.
