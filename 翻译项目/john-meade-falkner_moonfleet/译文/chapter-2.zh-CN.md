## II The Floods / 二 洪水

===Original===
“Then banks came down with ruin and rout,
						

						Then beaten spray flew round about,
						

						Then all the mighty floods were out,
						

						And all the world was in the sea.”

> Jean Ingelow
===Chinese===
“于是堤岸崩摧，溃乱倾覆，
						于是激碎的浪花四下飞洒，
						于是滔滔洪水尽数泛滥，
						整个世界尽没于汪洋大海。”

> Jean Ingelow

===Original===
On the third of November, a few days after this visit to the Why Not?, the wind, which had been blowing from the southwest, began about four in the afternoon to rise in sudden strong gusts. The rooks had been pitch-falling all the morning, so we knew that bad weather was due; and when we came out from the schooling that Mr. Glennie gave us in the hall of the old almshouses, there were wisps of thatch, and even stray tiles, flying from the roofs, and the children sang:
===Chinese===
十一月三日，也就是上“Why Not?”去过没几天，原本一直从西南方向吹来的风，下午四点钟光景，忽然一阵紧似一阵地猛刮起来。整个上午，白嘴鸦都在半空里栽着跟头往下坠，所以我们知道坏天气快要来了；我们从 Glennie 先生教课的老济贫院大厅里出来时，只见一绺绺的茅草，连零星的碎瓦，都从屋顶上飞了起来，孩子们唱道：

===Original===
“Blow wind, rise storm,
					

					Ship ashore before morn.”
===Chinese===
“风儿刮，暴风起，
						船儿天亮前上岸去。”

===Original===
It is heathenish rhyme that has come down out of other and worse times; for though I do not say but that a wreck on Moonfleet beach was looked upon sometimes as little short of a godsend, yet I hope none of us were so wicked as to *wish* a vessel to be wrecked that we might share in the plunder. Indeed, I have known the men of Moonfleet risk their own lives a hundred times to save those of shipwrecked mariners, as when the *Darius*, East Indiaman, came ashore; nay, even poor nameless corpses washed up were sure of Christian burial, or perhaps of one of Master Ratsey’s headstones to set forth sex and date, as may be seen in the churchyard to this day.
===Chinese===
这是从别的、更糟的年月里传下来的一首异教歪诗；因为虽说我不敢否认，Moonfleet 海滩上出的海难，有时候简直被大家看作老天爷的赏赐，但我盼望我们中间还没有谁坏到存心*盼着*一条船失事，好去分一杯赃物。说真的，我见过 Moonfleet 的人上百次豁出性命，去救失事船上的水手，就像东印度商船“Darius”号搁浅那回一样；不但如此，就连冲上岸来的无名浮尸，也必定得到基督教的安葬，或许还能得一块 Ratsey 师傅立的墓碑，写明性别和日期——这情形直到今天还看得见，就在教堂墓园里。

===Original===
Our village lies near the centre of Moonfleet Bay, a great bight twenty miles across, and a deathtrap to up-channel sailors in a southwesterly gale. For with that wind blowing strong from south, if you cannot double the snout, you must most surely come ashore; and many a good ship failing to round that point has beat up and down the bay all day, but come to beach in the evening. And once on the beach, the sea has little mercy, for the water is deep right in, and the waves curl over full on the pebbles with a weight no timbers can withstand. Then if poor fellows try to save themselves, there is a deadly undertow or rush back of the water, which sucks them off their legs, and carries them again under the thundering waves. It is that back-suck of the pebbles that you may hear for miles inland, even at Dorchester, on still nights long after the winds that caused it have sunk, and which makes people turn in their beds, and thank God they are not fighting with the sea on Moonfleet beach.
===Chinese===
我们村坐落在 Moonfleet 湾将近正中的地方。那是个宽达二十英里的大海湾，对顶着西南大风在海峡里赶路的水手，就是个催命的陷阱。因为南边那样的大风紧刮着，你若绕不过那道海岬，就十拿九稳得上岸；多少好船没能绕过那个岬角，在湾里抢风折腾了一整天，到傍晚还是搁上了海滩。而一旦上了海滩，大海是没有什么怜悯的：岸边的海水直上直下地深，海浪整个儿卷起来，结结实实砸在卵石上，那分量是任何船骨也吃不消的。这时候可怜人若想自救，还有一股要命的回头浪——海水凶猛地倒卷回来，把他们从脚上吸倒，又裹进雷鸣般的浪头底下去。卵石上那阵倒吸的轰响，能往内陆传出好几英里，连多切斯特都听得见；在酿成它的风早已平息之后很久，静夜里还响着，叫人在床上翻来覆去，感谢上帝：自己此刻不是在 Moonfleet 海滩上同大海拼命。

===Original===
But on this third of November there was no wreck, only such a wind as I have never known before, and only once since. All night long the tempest grew fiercer, and I think no one in Moonfleet went to bed; for there was such a breaking of tiles and glass, such a banging of doors and rattling of shutters, that no sleep was possible, and we were afraid besides lest the chimneys should fall and crush us. The wind blew fiercest about five in the morning, and then some ran up the street calling out a new danger﻿—that the sea was breaking over the beach, and that all the place was like to be flooded. Some of the women were for flitting forthwith and climbing the down; but Master Ratsey, who was going round with others to comfort people, soon showed us that the upper part of the village stood so high, that if the water was to get thither, there was no knowing if it would not cover Ridgedown itself. But what with its being a spring-tide, and the sea breaking clean over the great outer beach of pebbles﻿—a thing that had not happened for fifty years﻿—there was so much water piled up in the lagoon, that it passed its bounds and flooded all the sea meadows, and even the lower end of the street. So when day broke, there was the churchyard flooded, though ’twas on rising ground, and the church itself standing up like a steep little island, and the water over the doorsill of the Why Not?, though Elzevir Block would not budge, saying he did not care if the sea swept him away. It was but a nine-hours’ wonder, for the wind fell very suddenly; the water began to go back, the sun shone bright, and before noon people came out to the doors to see the floods and talk over the storm. Most said that never had been so fierce a wind, but some of the oldest spoke of one in the second year of Queen Anne, and would have it as bad or worse. But whether worse or not, this storm was a weighty matter enough for me, and turned the course of my life, as you shall hear.
===Chinese===
可是这个十一月三日，并没有出什么海难，只是刮了一场我此前从未领教过、后来也只再领教过一回的大风。一整夜暴风越刮越猛，我想 Moonfleet 没有一个人上过床；瓦片和玻璃噼里啪啦地碎，门扇砰砰地撞，百叶窗哐啷哐啷地响，谁也别想睡着；何况我们还提心吊胆，生怕烟囱倒下来把我们砸扁。风刮得最凶是在清晨五点钟前后，这时有人沿街跑上来喊新的险情——说海浪正整个儿漫过海滩，这地方眼看就要全淹了。有些女人主张马上搬家，爬上山岗去躲；可是正挨家挨户安慰大家的 Ratsey 师傅很快就让我们明白：村子高处站得那么高，水若是都能淹到那里，天晓得会不会连 Ridgedown 本身都盖过去。无奈那天正赶上大潮，海水整个儿漫过了外面那道巨大的卵石堤——这是五十年没有过的事——潟湖里积起那么多水，漫出了界，淹掉了全部海边草甸，连街道靠下的那一头也淹了。于是天一亮，教堂墓园进了水——虽然它在高地上；教堂孤零零立着，像一座陡峭的小岛；水漫过了“Why Not?”的门槛，尽管 Elzevir Block 说什么也不肯挪窝，说哪怕大海把他卷了去，他也满不在乎。这不过是一桩热闹了九个钟头的新鲜事，因为风说停就停；水开始往回退，太阳明晃晃地照着，不到中午，人们已经走到门口看洪水、议论这场风暴了。大多数人说不曾有过这么凶的风，可几位最年长的老人说起安妮女王二年那一场，一口咬定不相上下，或者更凶。但不管凶不凶，这场风暴对我说来已是件天大的事，它扭转了我一生的道路——诸位往下听就知道了。

===Original===
I have said that the waters came up so high that the church stood out like an island; but they went back quickly, and Mr. Glennie was able to hold service on the next Sunday morning. Few enough folks came to Moonfleet Church at any time; but fewer still came that morning, for the meadows between the village and the churchyard were wet and miry from the water. There were streamers of seaweed tangled about the very tombstones, and against the outside of the churchyard wall was piled up a great bank of it, from which came a salt rancid smell like a guillemot’s egg that is always in the air after a southwesterly gale has strewn the shore with wrack.
===Chinese===
我已经说过，水涨得那么高，教堂像座岛似的立在水中央；不过水退得快，到下一个礼拜天早晨，Glennie 先生照常主持了礼拜。Moonfleet 教堂平日来的人本就不多；那天早晨来得更少，因为村子同教堂墓园之间的草甸叫水泡得又湿又烂。一绺绺的海草缠在墓碑上，教堂墓园外墙根还堆着一大垛，散出一股咸腥的哈喇味，活像海鸠蛋的气味——每逢西南大风把海草撒满海岸，空气里总少不了这一股味儿。

===Original===
This church is as large as any other I have seen, and divided into two parts with a stone screen across the middle. Perhaps Moonfleet was once a large place, and then likely enough there were people to fill such a church, but never since I knew it did anyone worship in that part called the nave. This western portion was quite empty beyond a few old tombs and a Royal Arms of Queen Anne; the pavement too was damp and mossy; and there were green patches down the white walls where the rains had got in. So the handful of people that came to church were glad enough to get the other side of the screen in the chancel, where at least the pew floors were boarded over, and the panelling of oak-work kept off the draughts.
===Chinese===
这座教堂同我见过的任何一座一样大，中间横着一道石屏，分作两半。或许 Moonfleet 从前是个大地方，那时候很可能真有人把这样一座教堂坐满；可从我记事以来，从没有人到叫作中殿的那一半去做过礼拜。靠西的这半边空空荡荡，只有几座古墓和一幅安妮女王在位时的王室纹徽；石板地面潮乎乎地生了苔，白墙上雨水渗漏过的地方淌下一条条绿斑。所以来做礼拜的那一小撮人都乐于坐到石屏那一边的圣坛里去，那儿至少座位底下铺了木板，橡木护板也挡得住穿堂风。

===Original===
Now this Sunday morning there were only three or four, I think, beside Mr. Glennie and Ratsey and the half-dozen of us boys, who crossed the swampy meadows strewn with drowned shrewmice and moles. Even my aunt was not at church, being prevented by a migraine, but a surprise waited those who did go, for there in a pew by himself sat Elzevir Block. The people stared at him as they came in, for no one had ever known him go to church before; some saying in the village that he was a Catholic, and others an infidel. However that may be, there he was this day, wishing perhaps to show a favour to the parson who had written the verses for David’s headstone. He took no notice of anyone, nor exchanged greetings with those that came in, as was the fashion in Moonfleet Church, but kept his eyes fixed on a prayerbook which he held in his hand, though he could not be following the minister, for he never turned the leaf.
===Chinese===
这个礼拜天的早晨，我想，除了 Glennie 先生、Ratsey，和我们六个跨过泥泞草甸——上面撒满了淹死的鼩鼱和鼹鼠——的男孩，来的也就三四个人了。连我的姑母都没来做礼拜，叫偏头痛绊住了。可是来的人却迎面撞上一桩意外：Elzevir Block 独自一人坐在一处座位上。人们进门时都直着眼瞧他，因为从没有人见他进过教堂；村里有人说他是天主教徒，也有人说他是个不信神的人。不管怎么说，这一天他坐在那里，也许是想对那位替 David 的墓碑写过诗句的牧师表示一点好意。他不理会任何人，也不照 Moonfleet 教堂的规矩，同进来的人互道平安，只把眼睛盯在手里的一册《公祷书》上，尽管他并没有跟着牧师念——因为他一页也没有翻过。

===Original===
The church was so damp from the floods, that Master Ratsey had put a fire in the brazier which stood at the back, but was not commonly lighted till the winter had fairly begun. We boys sat as close to the brazier as we could, for the wet cold struck up from the flags, and besides that, we were so far from the clergyman, and so well screened by the oak backs, that we could bake an apple or roast a chestnut without much fear of being caught. But that morning there was something else to take off our thoughts; for before the service was well begun, we became aware of a strange noise under the church. The first time it came was just as Mr. Glennie was finishing “Dearly Beloved,” and we heard it again before the second lesson. It was not a loud noise, but rather like that which a boat makes jostling against another at sea, only there was something deeper and more hollow about it. We boys looked at each other, for we knew what was under the church, and that the sound could only come from the Mohune vault. No one at Moonfleet had ever seen the inside of that vault; but Ratsey was told by his father, who was clerk before him, that it underlay half the chancel, and that there were more than a score of Mohunes lying there. It had not been opened for over forty years, since Gerald Mohune, who burst a blood-vessel drinking at Weymouth races, was buried there; but there was a tale that one Sunday afternoon, many years back, there had come from the vault so horrible and unearthly a cry, that parson and people got up and fled from the church, and would not worship there for weeks afterwards.
===Chinese===
教堂叫洪水浸得那么潮，Ratsey 师傅便在后头那只火盆里生起了火——往常总要等冬天真正开了头，那火盆才点。我们几个男孩都尽量紧挨着火盆坐，因为湿冷从石板地里一个劲儿往上冒；再说我们离牧师那么远，又叫橡木椅背遮得严严实实，烤个苹果、煨个栗子，不大怕叫人逮住。可是那天早晨另有一桩事分了我们的心：礼拜还没正经开始，我们就觉察出教堂底下有一种奇怪的响动。头一回响，正赶上 Glennie 先生念完“亲爱的弟兄们”那段开场；第二篇经课之前，我们又听见了一回。那声音并不响，倒有点像海上这条小船磕碰那条小船的动静，只是里头更沉、更空一些。我们几个男孩面面相觑，因为我们知道教堂底下是什么，也知道那声音只可能出自 Mohune 家的墓窖。Moonfleet 没有一个人见过那墓窖的里面；不过 Ratsey 听他父亲说过——他父亲在他之前就当这个差——墓窖占了半个圣坛的地底，里面躺着二十多位 Mohune 家的人。墓窖有四十多年不曾打开了，自从 Gerald Mohune 埋进去以后——他在韦茅斯赛马会上喝酒喝得爆了血管；可是有个传说，说许多年前一个礼拜天的下午，墓窖里发出过一声那么可怕、那么不像人间的喊叫，牧师和会众纷纷起身逃出教堂，之后连着好几个礼拜都不肯到那儿做礼拜。

===Original===
We thought of these stories, and huddled up closer to the brazier, being frightened at the noise, and uncertain whether we should not turn tail and run from the church. For it was certain that something was moving in the Mohune vault, to which there was no entrance except by a ringed stone in the chancel floor, that had not been lifted for forty years.
===Chinese===
我们想起这些传闻，愈发紧紧地朝火盆缩成一团，心里害怕那响声，拿不定主意是不是该掉头逃出教堂。因为千真万确，有什么东西正在 Mohune 家的墓窖里动——那墓窖没有别的入口，只有圣坛地面上那块带环的活石，而那块石头已经四十年不曾抬起过了。

===Original===
However, we thought better of it, and did not budge, though I could see when standing up and looking over the tops of the seats that others beside ourselves were ill at ease; for Granny Tucker gave such starts when she heard the sounds, that twice her spectacles fell off her nose into her lap, and Master Ratsey seemed to be trying to mask the one noise by making another himself, whether by shuffling with his feet or by thumping down his prayer book. But the thing that most surprised me was that even Elzevir Block, who cared, men said, for neither God nor Devil, looked unquiet, and gave a quick glance at Ratsey every time the sound came. So we sat till Mr. Glennie was well on with the sermon. His discourse interested me though I was only a boy, for he likened life to the letter Y, saying that “in each man’s life must come a point where two roads part like the arms of a Y, and that everyone must choose for himself whether he will follow the broad and sloping path on the left or the steep and narrow path on the right. For,” said he, “if you will look in your books, you will see that the letter Y is not like the Mohunes’, with both arms equal, but has the arm on the left broader and more sloping than the arm on the right; hence ancient philosophers hold that this arm on the left represents the easy downward road to destruction, and the arm on the right the narrow upward path of life.” When we heard that we all fell to searching our prayerbooks for a capital Y; and Granny Tucker, who knew not A from B, made much ado in fumbling with her book, for she would have people think that she could read. Then just at that moment came a noise from below louder than those before, hollow and grating like the cry of an old man in pain. With that up jumps Granny Tucker, calling out loud in church to Mr. Glennie﻿—
===Chinese===
不过我们转念一想，还是没有挪窝；只是每逢我站起来从椅背顶上望过去，看得出除我们之外，别人也坐不安稳：Tucker 老婆婆每回听见那声响，都惊跳个不住，有两回眼镜从鼻梁上跌下来，落进膝头；Ratsey 师傅呢，像是要自己弄出些动静来压过它——一会儿用脚蹭地板，一会儿把《公祷书》砰地往下一墩。最叫我吃惊的却是，连 Elzevir Block——人们说他天也不怕、地也不怕，上帝和魔鬼一概不放在心上——也露出一副心神不宁的样子，那声音每响一回，他都飞快地朝 Ratsey 瞟一眼。我们就这样坐着，直到 Glennie 先生的布道讲得入了港。他那篇讲道虽说我不过是个孩子，却听得津津有味，因为他把人生比作字母 Y，说“每个人的生命里必定来到一个处所，两条路在那里像 Y 的两条臂一样分开；走左边那条又宽又缓的路，还是走右边那条又陡又窄的路，人人都得自己拿主意。因为，”他说，“你们翻开书看看便知，字母 Y 不像 Mohune 家的那样两臂一般齐，它左边的臂比右边的更宽、更斜；所以古代的哲人认为，左臂代表通往毁灭的那条轻松的下坡路，右臂代表通往生命的那条狭窄的上坡路。”我们听了，都埋头在自己的《公祷书》里找起大写的 Y 来；连 A 和 B 都分不清的 Tucker 老婆婆也捧着书乱翻乱摸、忙作一团，因为她要人家当她识得字。正在这时，底下传来一声比先前都响的响动，空洞刺耳，像老人在剧痛中哀叫——
