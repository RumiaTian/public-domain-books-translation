## III A Discovery / 三 一项发现

===Original===
“Some bold adventurers disdain
						

						The limits of their little reign,
						

						And unknown regions dare descry;
						

						Still, as they run, they look behind,
						

						They hear a voice in every wind
						

						And snatch a fearful joy.”

> Gray
===Chinese===
“有些大胆的冒险家，鄙夷
						自己小小王国的疆界，
						敢把未知的疆域窥探；

===Original===
I have said that I used often in the daytime, when not at school, to go to the churchyard, because being on a little rise, there was the best view of the sea to be had from it; and on a fine day you could watch the French privateers creeping along the cliffs under the Snout, and lying in wait for an Indiaman or up-channel trader. There were at Moonfleet few boys of my own age, and none that I cared to make my companion; so I was given to muse alone, and did so for the most part in the open air, all the more because my aunt did not like to see an idle boy, with muddy boots, about her house.
===Chinese===
我已说过，白天不上学的时候，我常到教堂墓园去，因为它坐落在一片小小的隆起上，从那里望海眼界最好；天气晴好的日子，你能望见法国的私掠船贴着 Snout 海岬底下的峭壁悄悄游弋，守候着印度商船，或是溯海峡北上的商船。Moonfleet 同我年纪相仿的男孩本就没有几个，也没有一个我乐意引为同伴；于是我惯于独自想心事，而且多半是在露天里想，何况姑母本就不爱见一个靴子上带泥、闲着没事的男孩在她家里转悠。

===Original===
For a few weeks, indeed, after the day that I had surprised Elzevir and Ratsey, I kept away from the church, fearing to meet them there again; but a little later resumed my visits, and saw no more of them. Now, my favourite seat in the churchyard was the flat top of a raised stone tomb, which stands on the southeast of the church. I have heard Mr. Glennie call it an altar-tomb, and in its day it had been a fine monument, being carved round with festoons of fruit and flowers; but had suffered so much from the weather, that I never was able to read the lettering on it, or to find out who had been buried beneath. Here I chose most to sit, not only because it had a flat and convenient top, but because it was screened from the wind by a thick clump of yew-trees. These yews had once, I think, completely surrounded it, but had either died or been cut down on the south side, so that anyone sitting on the grave-top was snug from the weather, and yet possessed a fine prospect over the sea. On the other three sides, the yews grew close and thick, embowering the tomb like the high back of a fireside chair; and many times in autumn I have seen the stone slab crimson with the fallen waxy berries, and taken some home to my aunt, who liked to taste them with a glass of sloe-gin after her Sunday dinner. Others beside me, no doubt, found this tomb a comfortable seat and lookout; for there was quite a path worn to it on the south side, though all the times I had visited it I had never seen anyone there.
===Chinese===
的确，自从那天撞见了 Elzevir 和 Ratsey，我有几个礼拜躲着教堂没去，生怕再在那里碰上他们；可是过了些日子，我又照旧去了，再没见着他们的踪影。我在墓园里最爱坐的位子，是一座高基石棺的平顶，它立在教堂的东南面。我听 Glennie 先生管这种坟叫祭坛墓；想当年它也是座体面的纪念碑，四周雕着一串串果子花朵的垂花饰；只是叫风雨糟蹋得太狠，我从没能辨认出上面的刻字，也没打听出底下埋的是谁。我最爱挑这里坐，不只因为它顶面平整合用，还因为有一丛茂密的紫杉替它挡风。这些紫杉，我想，从前原是把坟四面围拢的，只是南面的要么枯死了、要么叫人砍了去，于是坐在墓顶上的人既避得开风雨，又占得一片望海的好景致。另外三面的紫杉长得密密层层，把坟墓拢在当中，好像壁炉边座椅那高高的椅背；多少个秋天，我看见石板叫落下的蜡质浆果染得通红，还拾过一些带回给姑母——她喜欢在礼拜天正餐之后，就着一杯黑刺李杜松子酒尝上几粒。不必说，除了我，别人想必也觉得这座坟是个舒坦的座处和瞭望台；因为南面分明叫人踩出了一条直达坟前的小径，虽然我去了那么些回，从没在那里遇上过一个人。

===Original===
So it came about that on a certain afternoon in the beginning of February, in the year 1758, I was sitting on this tomb looking out to sea. Though it was so early in the year, the air was soft and warm as a May day, and so still that I could hear the drumming of turnips that Gaffer George was flinging into a cart on the hillside, near half a mile away. Ever since the floods of which I have spoken, the weather had been open, but with high winds, and little or no rain. Thus as the land dried after the floods there began to open cracks in the heavy clay soil on which Moonfleet is built, such as are usually only seen with us in the height of summer. There were cracks by the side of the path in the sea-meadows between the village and the church, and cracks in the churchyard itself, and one running right up to this very tomb.
===Chinese===
就这样，到了 1758 年二月初的某个下午，我正坐在这座坟上望海。虽说年头还这么早，空气却柔和暖和得同五月天一样，又那么静谧，静得连山坡上 Gaffer George 老汉往大车里扔萝卜的咚咚声我都听得见——那地方离这里差不多半英里。自我说起过的那场洪水以来，天气一直敞亮，只是风大，雨却下得很少，或者压根儿没下。于是大水之后地面渐渐干缩，Moonfleet 村所坐落的那片黏重泥土里，开始绽开一道道裂缝——这样的裂缝，在我们这里寻常只有盛夏才见得着。村子同教堂之间的海边草甸里，小路两旁裂了缝，墓园里也裂了缝，还有一道，恰恰一直裂到这座坟跟前。

===Original===
It must have been past four o’clock in the afternoon, and I was for returning to tea at my aunt’s, when underneath the stone on which I sat I heard a rumbling and crumbling, and on jumping off saw that the crack in the ground had still further widened, just where it came up to the tomb, and that the dry earth had so shrunk and settled that there was a hole in the ground a foot or more across. Now this hole reached under the big stone that formed one side of the tomb, and falling on my hands and knees and looking down it, I perceived that there was under the monument a larger cavity, into which the hole opened. I believe there never was boy yet who saw a hole in the ground, or a cave in a hill, or much more an underground passage, but longed incontinently to be into it and discover whither it led. So it was with me; and seeing that the earth had fallen enough into the hole to open a way under the stone, I slipped myself in feet foremost, dropped down on to a heap of fallen mould, and found that I could stand upright under the monument itself.
===Chinese===
那时想必已过下午四点，我正打算回姑母家吃茶点，忽然听见身子底下的石头里发出一阵轰隆塌落的响动；跳下来一看，只见地上的裂缝恰在贴到坟墓的地方又展宽了许多，干土收缩沉陷得那么厉害，地上竟豁出一个洞，一英尺多宽。这洞正通到砌成坟墓一侧的那块大石头底下；我扑下去双手双膝着地，朝洞里一望，察觉纪念碑底下有一个更大的空腔，这个洞便通向那里头。我相信，天底下从来不曾有过一个男孩，见了地上的洞、山里的穴，更不必说地下的暗道，而不登时心痒难熬、想要钻进去探个水落石出的。我自然也是一样；眼见塌下去的土已在石头底下让出一条路，我便脚在前头，缩身钻了进去，落到一堆塌土上，发觉自己竟能在纪念碑底下直挺挺地站着。

===Original===
Now this was what I had expected, for I thought that there had been below this grave a vault, the roof of which had given way and let the earth fall in. But as soon as my eyes were used to the dimmer light, I saw that it was no such thing, but that the hole into which I had crept was only the mouth of a passage, which sloped gently down in the direction of the church. My heart fell to thumping with eagerness and surprise, for I thought I had made a wonderful discovery, and that this hidden way would certainly lead to great things, perhaps even to Blackbeard’s hoard; for ever since Mr. Glennie’s tale I had constantly before my eyes a vision of the diamond and the wealth it was to bring me. The passage was two paces broad, as high as a tall man, and cut through the soil, without bricks or any other lining; and what surprised me most was that it did not seem deserted nor mouldy and cobwebbed, as one would expect such a place to be, but rather a well-used thoroughfare; for I could see the soft clay floor was trodden with the prints of many boots, and marked with a trail as if some heavy thing had been dragged over it.
===Chinese===
这本在我意料之中，因为我想，这座坟底下该是有一个墓窖，窖顶塌了陷，土才跟着落了下去。可是等眼睛习惯了昏暗，我看出根本不是那么回事：我爬进来的这个洞，不过是一条通道的口子，那通道缓缓朝教堂的方向斜下去。我的心咚咚地急跳起来，又热切又惊奇，因为我觉得自己做成了一桩了不得的大发现，这条隐秘的路必定通向了不得的去处，说不定就通到 Blackbeard 的窖藏；因为自听了 Glennie 先生讲的那段故事，我眼前便时时浮动着那颗钻石，和它将给我带来的财富。那通道有两步宽，一个高个子男人那么高，是从泥土里掏挖出来的，不砌砖，也没有别的衬砌；最叫我惊奇的是，它不见荒废，也不像这种地方该有的那样发霉结网，倒像一条常有人走的通路；因为我看得出，松软的泥地面上踩满了许许多多靴子的印子，还有一道拖痕，仿佛有什么重家伙从上面拖运过去。

===Original===
So I set out down the passage, reaching out my hand before me lest I should run against anything in the dark, and sliding my feet slowly to avoid pitfalls in the floor. But before I had gone half a dozen paces, the darkness grew so black that I was frightened, and so far from going on was glad to turn sharp about, and see the glimmer of light that came in through the hole under the tomb. Then a horror of the darkness seized me, and before I well knew what I was about I found myself wriggling my body up under the tombstone on to the churchyard grass, and was once more in the low evening sunlight and the soft sweet air.
===Chinese===
于是我顺着通道往下走，把手伸在身前，怕在黑暗里撞上什么，两脚贴着地面慢慢挪，防着地上有陷坑。可是没走上十来步，黑暗浓得那么厉害，我怕了，哪里还谈得上前进，只巴不得猛地转过身去，好望着坟墓底下洞口透进来的那一星微光。跟着，一阵对黑暗的恐怖攫住了我，还没等我明白自己在做什么，已经扭动着身子从墓碑底下爬了上去，趴到墓园的草地上，重新回到西斜的太阳和柔软香甜的空气里。

===Original===
Home I ran to my aunt’s, for it was past teatime, and beside that I knew I must fetch a candle if I were ever to search out the passage; and to search it I had well made up my mind, no matter how much I was scared for this moment. My aunt gave me but a sorry greeting when I came into the kitchen, for I was late and hot. She never said much when displeased, but had a way of saying nothing, which was much worse; and would only reply yes or no, and that after an interval, to anything that was asked of her. So the meal was silent enough, for she had finished before I arrived, and I ate but little myself, being too much occupied with the thought of my strange discovery, and finding, besides, the tea lukewarm and the victuals not enticing.
===Chinese===
我一路跑回姑母家，因为吃茶点的时辰已经过了；再说我知道，真要探明那条通道，总得取一支蜡烛；而探明它，我是拿定了主意的，哪怕这会儿吓成这样也罢。我进厨房时，姑母给我的招待可够冷淡，因为我回来晚了，又是一身燥热。她不高兴时从来不多说话，却有一套一言不发的做派，那比骂人还难受；问她什么，她只隔上好一阵，才答一个是或不。这一顿饭于是吃得够沉闷，她在我回来前早已吃完；我自己也吃得很少，一脑子全是我那桩奇怪的发现，何况茶是温吞的，饭菜也不馋人。

===Original===
You may guess that I said nothing of what I had seen, but made up my mind that as soon as my aunt’s back was turned I would get a candle and tinderbox, and return to the churchyard. The sun was down before Aunt Jane gave thanks for what we had received, and then, turning to me, she said in a cold and measured voice:
===Chinese===
诸位猜得到，我看见的事我一个字也没有提，只打定主意，等姑母一转背，就去取一支蜡烛和火绒盒，重回墓园去。直到太阳落了山，Jane 姑妈才为领受的饮食做了谢恩祷告；随后她转向我，用一种冷冷的、字斟句酌的腔调说：

===Original===
“John, I have observed that you are often out and about of nights, sometimes as late as half past seven or eight. Now, it is not seemly for young folk to be abroad after dark, and I do not choose that my nephew should be called a gadabout. ‘What’s bred in the bone will come out in the flesh,’ and ’twas with such loafing that your father began his wild ways, and afterwards led my poor sister such a life as never was, till the mercy of Providence took him away.”
===Chinese===
“John，我留意到，你夜里时常在外头逛荡，有时候竟晚到七点半、八点钟。年轻人天黑以后还在外头跑，这可不体面；我不愿意让人管我的侄子叫游手好闲的二流子。‘骨子里生就的性子，皮肉上迟早要露出来’，你父亲当年就是从这般闲逛起步，走上了荒唐路，后来叫我那可怜的姊妹吃足了苦，那样日子世上再没有第二个女人受过，直到上天的慈悲把他收了去。”

===Original===
Aunt Jane often spoke thus of my father, whom I never remembered, but believe him to have been an honest man and good fellow to boot, if something given to roaming and to the contraband.
===Chinese===
Jane 姑妈时常这样说起我父亲；我对他毫无记忆，却相信他为人诚实，还是个好性子的人，只是有些流荡的脾性，又沾着走私的路子。

===Original===
“So understand,” she went on, “that I will not have you out again this evening, no, nor any other evening, after dusk. Bed is the place for youth when night falls, but if this seem to you too early you can sit with me for an hour in the parlour, and I will read you a discourse of Doctor Sherlock that will banish vain thoughts, and leave you in a fit frame for quiet sleep.”
===Chinese===
“所以你要明白，”她接着说，“今天晚上我不许你再出去；不但今晚，往后任凭哪个晚上，天一擦黑都不许。夜幕一落，年轻人就该上床；你若嫌这太早，可以到前厅陪我坐一个钟头，我给你念一篇 Sherlock 博士的讲道文，把那些胡思乱想统统念跑，叫你安安分分地睡个好觉。”

===Original===
So she led the way into the parlour, took the book from the shelf, put it on the table within the little circle of light cast by a shaded candle, and began. It was dull enough, though I had borne such tribulations before, and the drone of my aunt’s voice would have sent me to sleep, as it had done at other times, even in a straight-backed chair, had I not been so full of my discovery, and chafed at this delay. Thus all the time my aunt read of spiritualities and saving grace, I had my mind on diamonds and all kinds of mammon, for I never doubted that Blackbeard’s treasure would be found at the end of that secret passage. The sermon finished at last, and my aunt closed the book with a stiff “goodnight” for me. I was for giving her my formal kiss, but she made as if she did not see me and turned away; so we went upstairs each to our own room, and I never kissed Aunt Jane again.
===Chinese===
于是她领头进了前厅，从架上取下书，摊在带罩蜡烛投下的一小圈光里，念了起来。那篇东西枯燥得很，虽说这类磨难我从前也领教过；姑母那单调的嗡嗡声若在平时，早把我送进梦乡了——从前就是这样，连直背椅上也照睡不误——无奈我心里满满当当装着那桩发现，又为这场耽搁焦躁难耐。于是姑母念的尽是灵性和救恩，我心里想的却尽是钻石和各色各样的浮财，因为我从不怀疑，Blackbeard 的财宝就在那条秘道的尽头。讲道总算念完，姑母合上书，硬邦邦地赏了我一声“晚安”。我想照例吻她一下，她却装作没瞧见我，扭过脸去；于是我们上楼，各回各屋——从那以后，我再没有吻过 Jane 姑妈。

===Original===
There was a moon three-quarters full already in the sky, and on moonlight nights I was allowed no candle to show me to bed. But on that night I needed none, for I never took off my clothes, having resolved to wait till my aunt was asleep, and then, ghosts or no ghosts, to make my way back to the churchyard. I did not dare to put off that visit even till the morning, lest some chance passerby should light upon the hole, and so forestall me with Blackbeard’s treasure.
===Chinese===
天上已经升起一轮圆了四分之三的月亮；有月光的夜里，照例不许我点蜡烛照着上楼。可是那一夜我用不着，因为我压根儿没脱衣裳，只打定主意等姑母睡熟，然后——有鬼没鬼——摸回墓园去。这一趟我连推迟到第二天早晨都不敢，生怕哪个过路人碰巧撞上那个洞，抢在头里把 Blackbeard 的财宝得了去。

===Original===
Thus I lay wide awake on my bed watching the shadow of the tester-post against the whitewashed wall, and noting how it had moved, by degrees, as the moon went farther round. At last, just as it touched the picture of the Good Shepherd which hung over the mantelpiece, I heard my aunt snoring in her room, and knew that I was free. Yet I waited a few minutes so that she might get well on with her first sleep, and then took off my boots, and in stockinged feet slipped past her room and down the stairs. How stair, handrail, and landing creaked that night, and how my feet and body struck noisily against things seen quite well but misjudged in the effort not to misjudge them! And yet there was the note of safety still sounding, for the snoring never ceased, and the sleeper woke not, though her waking then might have changed all my life. So I came safely to the kitchen, and there put in my pocket one of the best winter candles and the tinderbox, and as I crept out of the room heard suddenly how loud the old clock was ticking, and looking up saw the bright brass band marking half past ten on the dial.
===Chinese===
就这样，我在床上睁着眼躺着，望着床架天盖的柱子投在刷白墙上的影子，看着它随着月亮越转越远，一分一分地挪动。末了，那影子恰好触到壁炉架上挂的那幅《好牧人》图，我听见姑母屋里打起呼噜来，知道自己自由了。可我还等了几分钟，好叫她头一觉睡沉，然后脱下靴子，穿着袜子蹑手蹑脚溜过她的房门，下了楼。那一夜，楼梯、扶手和楼梯口的地板响得多么厉害；我的脚和身子，又怎样一次次磕在那些看得清清楚楚、只因生怕碰错反而撞了上去的东西上，声响多么大！然而平安的底音始终没有断：呼噜一直没停，睡着的人也没有醒——虽然她那时若是醒了，我这一生的路数怕就要全盘改写。就这样，我平安到了厨房，往口袋里揣了一支上好的过冬蜡烛，又拿上火绒盒；正蹑着脚往外走，忽然听见那座老钟滴答得多么响，抬头一看，钟面上明晃晃的黄铜指针正指着十点半。

===Original===
Out in the street I kept in the shadow of the houses as far as I might, though all was silent as the grave; indeed, I think that when the moon is bright a great hush falls always upon Nature, as though she was taken up in wondering at her own beauty. Everyone was fast asleep in Moonfleet and there was no light in any window; only when I came opposite the Why Not? I saw from the red glow behind the curtains that the bottom room was lit up, so Elzevir was not yet gone to bed. It was strange, for the Why Not? had been shut up early for many a long night past, and I crossed over cautiously to see if I could make out what was going forward. But that was not to be done, for the panes were thickly steamed over; and this surprised me more as showing that there was a good company inside. Moreover, as I stood and listened I could hear a mutter of deep voices inside, not as of roisterers, but of sober men talking low.
===Chinese===
到了街上，我尽量拣房影里走，虽然四下静得像坟墓；说真的，我想，月色明亮的时候，大自然总要罩上一层深深的静谧，仿佛她自己也对着自己的美出了神。Moonfleet 家家睡得正沉，哪扇窗里都没有灯；只是走到“Why Not?”对面时，我看见窗帘后头透出红光，楼底下的屋子亮着——这么说，Elzevir 还没上床。这可蹊跷，因为许多个长夜以来，“Why Not?”总是早早便打了烊；我小心地穿过街去，想弄明白底下在搞什么名堂。可那没法办到，因为一块块窗玻璃都叫水汽蒙得严严实实；这更叫我吃惊，因为这表明里头坐着好些人。再者，我站着听的工夫，听得见里面透出一阵低沉的语声，不像起哄的酒客，倒像一群稳重的人压低了嗓子谈话。

===Original===
Eagerness would not let me wait long, and I was off across the meadows towards the church, though not without sad misgivings as soon as the last house was left well behind me. At the churchyard wall my courage had waned somewhat: it seemed a shameless thing to come to rifle Blackbeard’s treasure just in the very place and hour that Blackbeard loved; and as I passed the turnstile I half-expected that a tall figure, hairy and evil-eyed, would spring out from the shadow on the north side of the church. 这些不过是转瞬间的念头，说话声却更近了；我听见通道深处传来一声闷响，知道有人从墓园跳进洞里来了。于是我最后环顾了一遭，苦苦寻看有没有脱身的路；可是石头墙壁和顶子结实得能把我压成齑粉，那堆酒桶又码得密不透风，除了耗子什么也藏不下。这时已有一个人在洞底同墓园里的其余人说话；跟着，我的眼睛像叫磁石牵着，落到最高一层搁架上独自躺着的一口大木棺上——离地足足六英尺。一看见那口棺材，我就知道自己得了缓刑，因为照我估量，它同背后墙壁之间的空当，尽容得下我这副小小的身板；说时迟，那时快，我已吹熄蜡烛，攀上搁架，一头撞在窖顶上，撞得神志半昏，仍旧把身子挤进了墙壁同棺材的夹缝。我就那样侧身躺着，同死人之间只隔一层朽薄的木板，头上那一撞撞得我晕头转向，喘着粗气；火把的光顺着通道下来，在头顶的窖顶上映得通红，一闪一闪。
