## XIX On the Beach / 十九 在海滩上

===Original===

“Toll for the brave,
						

						The grave that are no more;
						

						All sunk beneath the wave
						

						Fast by their native shore.”

> Cowper

===Chinese===

“为勇者鸣钟——
他们已成坟茔，而今不存；
尽数沉没在波涛之下，
紧傍着故乡的海滨。”

> Cowper

===Original===

The night was cold, and I had nothing on me save breeches and boots, and those drenched with the sea, and had been wrestling with the surf so long that there was little left in me. Yet once I clutched the rope I clung to it for very life, and in a minute found myself in the midst of the beachmen. I heard them shout again, and felt strong hands seize me, but could not see their faces for a mist that swam before my eyes, and could not speak because my throat and tongue were cracked with the salt water, and the voice would not come. There was a crowd about me of men and some women, and I spread out my hands, blindly, to catch hold of them, but my knees failed and let me down upon the beach. And after that I remember only having coats flung over me, and being carried off out of the wind, and laid in warmest blankets before a fire. I was numb with the cold, my hair was matted with the salt, and my flesh white and shrivelled, but they forced liquor into my mouth, and so I lay in drowsy content till utter weariness bound me in sleep.

===Chinese===

夜里冷得很，我身上除了马裤和靴子一无所有，而这两样也叫海水泡得透湿；又同浪头搏斗了那么久，我身子里几乎不剩什么了。可我一抓住那条绳，便为着性命死死攥住不放，不出一分钟，就发觉自己已在这伙滩上人当中了。我听见他们又喊起来，觉出有力的手攥住了我，眼前却游着一层水雾，看不清他们的脸；喉咙和舌头都叫咸水渍得开了裂，话说不出来，声音怎么也发不出。我周围围了一圈人，有男也有女；我盲盲目目地张开两手去抓他们，膝盖却不争气，把我撂倒在了海滩上。此后我只记得一件件外衣往我身上盖，被人抬出去避风，放在炉火前最暖和的毯子里。我冻得麻木了，头发叫盐黏作一团，皮肉白得皱缩；可他们硬把酒灌进我嘴里，我便昏昏然地躺着，直到彻骨的困乏把我捆进了睡眠。

===Original===

It was a deep and dreamless sleep for hours, and when it left me, gently and as it were inch by inch, I found I was still lying wrapped in blankets by the fire. Oh, what a vast and infinite peace was that, to lie there half-asleep, yet wake enough to know that I had slipped my prison and the pains of death, and was a free man here in my native place! At last I shifted myself a little, growing more awake; and opening my eyes saw I was not alone, for two men sat at a table by me with glasses and a bottle before them.

===Chinese===

那是一场深沉无梦的酣睡，睡了好几个钟头；它离开我时那么轻缓，仿佛是一寸一寸退去的。我发觉自己仍裹着毯子躺在炉火旁。啊，那是何等浩渺无边的安宁——半睡半醒地躺在那里，却又醒得足以知道：我已逃出牢狱、躲过死亡的痛楚，如今在自己家乡做了自由人！过了许久，我稍稍挪动了一下，人越发醒了；睁开眼睛，才看见自己并非独自一人，有两个男人坐在旁边一张桌前，面前摆着酒杯和一瓶酒。

===Original===

“He is coming-to,” said one, “and may live yet to tell us who he is, and from what port his craft sailed.”

===Chinese===

“他缓过来了，”一个说，“兴许还能活下来，告诉我们他是谁，他的船是从哪个口岸开出来的。”

===Original===

“There has been many a craft,” the other said, “has sailed for many a port, and made this beach her last; and many an honest man has landed on it, and never one alive in such a sea. Nor would this one be living either, if it had not been for that other brave heart to stand by and save him. Brave heart, brave heart,” he said over to himself. “Here, pass me the bottle or I shall get the vapours. ’Tis good against these early chills, and I have not been in this place for ten years past, since poor Elzevir was cut adrift.”

===Chinese===

“开往各处口岸的船多着呢，”另一个说，“把这片海滩当成最后一站的也多着呢；多少正经人在这滩上靠了岸，可在昨夜那样的海况里，从来没有一个活人上得来。要不是另外还有一位好汉守在旁边救他，这一个也活不成。好汉，好汉，”他自言自语地念叨着。“喂，把瓶子递给我，不然我要犯郁症了。这东西最能抵挡清晨的寒气。我自己也有十年没进这地方了，自打可怜的 Elzevir 像断了缆一样漂走了。”

===Original===

I could not see the speaker’s face from where I lay upon the floor, yet seemed to know his voice; and so was fumbling in my weakened mind to put a name to it, when he spoke of Elzevir, and sent my thoughts flying elsewhere.

===Chinese===

我躺在地上，看不见说话人的脸，却似乎听得出来他的嗓音；正当我在这衰弱的脑袋里摸索着给他安一个名字时，他提起了 Elzevir，把我的心思一下子引到了别处。

===Original===

“Elzevir,” I said, “where is Elzevir?” and sat up to look round, expecting to see him lying near me, and remembering the wreck more clearly now, and how he had saved me with that last shove forward on the beach. But he was not to be seen, and so I guessed that his great strength had brought him round quicker than had my youth, and that he was gone back to the beach.

===Chinese===

“Elzevir，”我说，“Elzevir 在哪儿？”我坐起身来环顾四周，指望看见他就躺在我近旁；这时我把失事的情形想得更清楚了，想起他怎样在滩上最后把我向前一推、救了我。可他不见踪影，于是我猜，他那一身好气力准比我这年轻人恢复得快，已经回海滩上去了。

===Original===

“Hush,” said one of the men at the table, “lie down and get to sleep again;” and then he added, speaking to his comrade: “His brain is wandering yet: do you see how he has caught up my words about Elzevir?”

===Chinese===

“嘘，”桌边一个人说，“躺下，接着睡吧；”随后他又对同伴说：“他脑子还发着昏呢：你瞧，我一提 Elzevir，他就接住了话头。”

===Original===

“No,” I struck in, “my head is clear enough; I am speaking of Elzevir Block. I pray you tell me where he is. Is he well again?” They got up and stared at one another and at me, when I named Elzevir Block, and then I knew the one that spoke for Master Ratsey, only greyer than he was.

===Chinese===

“不，”我插嘴道，“我的脑子清楚得很；我说的是 Elzevir Block。求你们告诉我他在哪儿。他好些了吗？”我一说出 Elzevir Block 的名字，两人霍地站起，面面相觑，又直盯着我看；这时我也认出了方才说话的那位——正是 Ratsey 师傅，只是比从前更多了几分灰白。

===Original===

“Who are you?” he cried, “who talk of Elzevir Block?”

===Chinese===

“你是谁？”他喊道，“谁在你嘴里念叨 Elzevir Block？”

===Original===

“Do you not know me, Master Ratsey?” and I looked full in his face. “I am John Trenchard, who left you so long ago. I pray you tell me where is Master Block?”

===Chinese===

“您认不出我了吗，Ratsey 师傅？”我直望着他的脸。“我是 John Trenchard，那么久以前离开您的那个 John Trenchard。求您告诉我，Block 师傅在哪儿？”

===Original===

Master Ratsey looked as if he had seen a ghost, and was struck dumb at first: but then ran up and shook me by the hand so warmly that I fell back again on my pillow, while he poured out questions in a flood. How had I fared, where had I been, whence had I come? until I stopped him, saying: “Softly, kind friend, and I will answer; only tell me first, where is Master Elzevir?”

===Chinese===

Ratsey 师傅的神情像是见了鬼，一时说不出话来；随后他抢上前来，热热烈烈地握住我的手摇个不停，摇得我又倒回枕上，他口中的问题却像决了堤：我这十年怎么熬的，到过什么地方，从何处来？——直到我拦住他，说：“轻些，好心的朋友，我都回答；只求你先告诉我，Elzevir 师傅在哪儿？”

===Original===

“Nay, that I cannot say,” he answered, “for never a soul has set eyes on Elzevir since that summer morning we put thee and him ashore at Newport.”

===Chinese===

“唉，这个我说不上，”他答道，“自打那个夏天的早晨，我们把你和他送上 Newport 的岸，就再没有一个人见过 Elzevir。”

===Original===

“Oh, fool me not!” I cried out, chafing at his excuses; “I am not wandering now. ’Twas Elzevir that saved me in the surf last night. ’Twas he that landed with me.”

===Chinese===

“唉，你别哄我了！”我喊起来，为他的推托着恼，“我这会儿并没有发昏。昨夜在浪头里救我的就是 Elzevir。跟我一同上岸的就是他。”

===Original===

There was a look of sad amaze that came on Ratsey’s face when I said that; a look that woke in me an awful surmise. “What!” cried he, “was that Master Elzevir that dragged thee through the surf?”

===Chinese===

我这么一说，Ratsey 脸上现出一种悲凉的惊愕；那神情在我心里唤醒了一个可怕的猜疑。“什么！”他喊道，“拖着你在浪里上岸的，是 Elzevir 师傅？”

===Original===

“Ay, ’twas he landed with me, ’twas he landed with me,” I said; trying, as it were, to make true by repeating that which I feared was not the truth. There was a minute’s silence, and then Ratsey spoke very softly: “there was none landed with you; there was no soul saved from that ship alive save you.”

===Chinese===

“对，是他同我上的岸，是他同我上的岸，”我说；仿佛要把那件我唯恐不是真事的事，靠一遍遍重复说成真的。静了有一分钟，随后 Ratsey 极轻地说：“没有谁同你一道上岸；那条船上活下来的，只有你一个。”

===Original===

His words fell, one by one, upon my ear as if they were drops of molten lead. “It is not true,” I cried; “he pulled me up the beach himself, and it was he that pushed me forward to the rope.”

===Chinese===

他的话一字一字落在我耳中，像一滴滴熔化的铅。“这不是真的，”我喊道，“是他亲手把我拖上滩的，是他把我推向那条绳子的。”

===Original===

“Ay, he saved thee, and then the undertow got hold of him and swept him down under the curl. I could not see his face, but might have known there never was a man, save Elzevir, could fight the surf on Moonfleet beach like that. Yet had we known ’twas he, we could have done no more, for many risked their lives last night to save you both. We could have done no more.” Then I gave a great groan for utter anguish, to think that he had given up the safety he had won for himself, and laid down his life, there on the beach, for me; to think that he had died on the threshold of his home; that I should never get a kind look from him again, nor ever hear his kindly voice.

===Chinese===

“是啊，他救了你；随后退浪攫住了他，把他扫进了卷浪底下。我看不见他的脸，可也该想到：除了 Elzevir，从没有谁能像那样在 Moonfleet 海滩的浪头里搏斗。可就算当时我们知道是他，也无力做得更多，因为昨夜为救你们两个，多少人舍了命。我们实在无力做得更多了。”这时我一声长号，痛彻心腑——想到他竟把亲手挣来的安全抛开，在那片滩上为我舍了性命；想到他死在了自家门槛上；想到我从此再也得不到他慈爱的顾盼，再也听不见他那温厚的嗓音。

===Original===

It is wearisome to others to talk of deep grief, and beside that no words, even of the wisest man, can ever set it forth, nor even if we were able could our memory bear to tell it. So I shall not speak more of that terrible blow, only to say that sorrow, so far from casting my body down, as one might have expected, gave it strength, and I rose up from the mattress where I had been lying. They tried to stop me, and even to hold me back, but for all I was so weak, I pushed them aside and must needs fling a blanket round me and away back to the beach.

===Chinese===

深创巨痛说与旁人听，只令人生厌；何况纵是顶有智慧的人，也没有言语能将它说尽；就算说得出，我们的记忆也担不起那样的复述。所以那可怕的一击，我不愿再多说，只说一句：悲恸非但没有像人们料想的那样拖垮我的身子，反倒给了它力气。我从躺着的褥子上撑起来。他们想拦我，甚至动手拉住我，可我尽管虚弱，还是把他们推开，抓过一条毯子往身上一裹，径自回海滩去了。

===Original===

The morning was breaking as I left the Why Not?, for ’twas in no other place but that I lay, and the wind, though still high, had abated. There were light clouds crossing the heaven very swiftly, and between them patches of clear sky where the stars were growing paler before the dawn. The stars were growing paler; but there was another star, that shone out from the Manor Woods above the village, although I could not see the house, and told me Grace, like the wise virgins, kept her lamp alight all night. Yet even that light shone without lustre for me then, for my heart was too full to think of anything but of him who had laid down his life for mine, and of the strong kind heart that was stilled forever.

===Chinese===

我离开“Why Not?”的时候，天正破晓——原来我正是躺在这家客栈里——风虽然还大，已经减弱了些。轻云飞快地掠过天空，云隙间露出一块块晴空，星星在黎明前渐渐淡去。星星渐渐淡去了；可在村子上方庄园林里，还亮着另一颗星——虽然我望不见那宅子——它告诉我，Grace 像那聪明的童女一样，通宵点着她的灯。可那时连这点光在我眼里也失了辉泽，因为我心里满满当当，只装得下他——那为我舍了性命的他，那颗永远沉默了的、宽厚仁慈的心，别的什么都装不下了。

===Original===

’Twas well I knew the way, so sure of old, from Why Not? to beach; for I took no heed to path or feet, but plunged along in the morning dusk, blind with sorrow and weariness of spirit. There was a fire of driftwood burning at the back of the beach, and round it crouched a group of men in reefing jackets and sou’westers waiting for morning to save what they might from the wreck; but I gave them a wide berth and so passed in the darkness without a word, and came to the top of the beach. There was light enough to make out what was doing. The sea was running very high, but with the falling wind the waves came in more leisurely and with less of broken water, curling over in a tawny sweep and regular thunderous beat all along the bay for miles. There was no sign left of the hull of the *Aurungzebe*, but the beach was strewn with so much wreckage as one would have thought could never come from so small a ship. There were barrels and kegs, gratings and hatch-covers, booms and pieces of masts and trucks; and beside all that, the heaving water inshore was covered with a floating mask of broken matchwood, and the waves, as they curled over, carried up and dashed down on the pebble planks and beams beyond number. There were a dozen or more of men on the seaward side of the beach, with oilskins to keep the wet out, prowling up and down the pebbles to see what they could lay their hands on; and now and then they would run down almost into the white fringe, risking their lives to save a keg as they had risked them to save their fellows last night﻿—as they had risked their lives to save ours, as Elzevir had risked his life to save mine, and lost it there in the white fringe.

===Chinese===

幸而从“Why Not?”到海滩这条路，我从前熟得闭着眼也错不了；因为我一路全不顾脚下有路没路，只顾在黎明的昏暝里跌跌撞撞往前赶，两眼叫悲伤和困乏蒙得发盲。海滩后头烧着一堆浮木火，围着火蜷着一群穿收帆短褂、戴油布风帽的人，等着天亮好从残船上抢捞些什么；可我远远绕开他们，一言不发地从黑暗里走过去，来到海滩顶上。光亮已够看清那边在做什么了。海仍涨得很高，只是风势渐杀，浪头进来得从容了些，碎水也少了，沿着海湾数英里，一浪一浪卷成黄褐色的弧，轰然有节拍地砸下来。*Aurungzebe* 号的船身已无影无踪，可滩上撒满了那么多残骸，叫人想不到是那样小的一条船上能出来的。有大桶有小桶，有格栅有舱盖，有帆桁、断桅和桅顶盘；除此之外，近岸的涌水里还浮着一整层火柴杆似的碎木，浪头卷过来时，把数不清的木板和梁木托起来，又摔在卵石上。十几个或者更多的人在滩的临海一侧，穿着油布衣裤挡水，在卵石间走上走下地巡，看什么能到手；他们时不时几乎跑到白沫镶边里去，冒死去捞一只小桶——就像昨夜冒着死救他们的同伴那样；就像他们曾经冒着死救我们那样；就像 Elzevir 曾冒着死救我，而把命丢在了那道白沫镶边里。

===Original===

I sat down at the top of the beach, with elbows on knees, head between hands, and face set out to sea, not knowing well why I was there or what I sought, but only thinking that Elzevir was floating somewhere in that floating skin of wreck-wood, and that I must be at hand to meet him when he came ashore. He would surely come in time, for I had seen others come ashore that way. For when the *Bataviaman* went on the beach, I stood as near her as our rescuers had stood to us last night, and there were some aboard who took the fatal leap from off her bows and tried to battle through the surf. I was so near them I could mark their features and read the wild hope in their faces at the first, and then the undertow took hold of them, and never one that saved his life that day. And yet all came to beach at last, and I knew them by their dead faces for the men I had seen hoping against hope ’twixt ship and shore; some naked and some clothed, some bruised and sorely beaten by the pebbles and the sea, and some sound and untouched﻿—all came to beach at last.

===Chinese===

我在滩顶上坐下来，肘拄着膝，头夹在两手间，脸朝着大海，不明白自己为什么在这里、又在找什么，只想着 Elzevir 就漂在那层漂着的残木中间的什么地方，他上岸来的时候，我必须守在旁边迎他。他迟早总会来的，我见过别人那样上岸来。当年 *Bataviaman* 号在滩上失事时，我站的地方就同昨夜救我们的人站得离我们一样近；船上有几个人从船头跳下那致命的一跃，想搏过浪头游上岸。我离他们那么近，看得清他们的眉目，起初还读得出他们脸上那种疯狂的指望；随后退浪攫住了他们，那一天没有一个保住性命的。可到头来他们全都上了岸，我凭着那些死者的脸认出他们，正是我在船岸之间见过的那种明知无望仍存一线指望的人；有的赤裸，有的穿着衣裳，有的青一块紫一块、叫卵石和海水打得够呛，有的却毫发无伤——到头来全都上了岸。

===Original===

So I sat and waited for him to come; and none of the beach-walkers said anything to me, the Moonfleet men thinking I came from Ringstave, and the Langton men that I belonged to Moonfleet; and both that I had marked some cask at sea for my own and was waiting till it should come in. Only after a while Master Ratsey joined me, and sitting down by me, begged me to eat bread and meat that he had brought. Now I had little heart to eat, but took what he gave me to save myself from his importunities, and having once tasted was led by nature to eat all, and was much benefited thereby. Yet I could not talk with Ratsey, nor answer any of his questions, though another time I should have put a thousand to him myself; and he, seeing ’twas no good, sat by me in silence, using a spyglass now and again to make out the things floating at sea. As the day grew the men left the fire at the back of the beach, and came down to the seafront where the waves were continually casting up fresh spoil. And there all worked with a will, not each one for his own hand, but all to make a common hoard which should be divided afterwards.

===Chinese===

我就这样坐着等他来；巡滩的人没有一个同我搭话，Moonfleet 的人当我是 Ringstave 来的，Langton 的人当我是 Moonfleet 的；两边都只当我在海里看中了哪只桶，等它漂上来好归自己。过了一阵，只有 Ratsey 师傅过来挨着我坐下，劝我吃他带来的面包和肉。我本没什么胃口，只因拗不过他的殷勤，才接了过来；一经入口，天性便引着我吃了个精光，人也着实得了补益。可我同 Ratsey 说不成话，他问什么我也答不上——换了别的时候，倒是我有一千个问题要问他呢；他见没有用处，便默默坐在一旁，不时举起单筒望远镜，辨认海上漂浮的东西。天色渐亮，人们离开滩后的火堆，下到海边缘，那里的浪头正不断把新的漂货卷上来。到了那里，人人卖力地干，不是各捞各的，而是全归一个公堆，过后再均分。

===Original===

Among the flotsam moving outside the breakers I could see more than one dark ball, like black buoys, bobbing up and down, and lifting as the wave came by: and knew them for the heads of drowned men. Yet though I took Ratsey’s glass and scanned all carefully enough, I could make nothing of them, but saw the pinnace floating bottom up, and farther out another boat deserted and down to her gunwale in the water. ’Twas midday before the first body was cast up, when the sky was breaking a little, and a thin and watery sun trying to get through, and afterwards three other bodies followed.
