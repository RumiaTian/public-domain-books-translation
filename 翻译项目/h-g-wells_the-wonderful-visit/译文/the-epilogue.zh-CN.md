## LIII The Epilogue / 五十三 尾声

===Original===

And there the story of the Wonderful Visit ends. The Epilogue is in the mouth of Mrs. Mendham. There stand two little white crosses in the Siddermorton churchyard, near together, where the brambles come clambering over the stone wall. One is inscribed Thomas Angel and the other Delia Hardy, and the dates of the deaths are the same. Really there is nothing beneath them but the ashes of the Vicar’s stuffed ostrich. (You will remember the Vicar had his ornithological side.) I noticed them when Mrs. Mendham was showing me the new De la Beche monument. (Mendham has been Vicar since Hilyer died.) “The granite came from somewhere in Scotland,” said Mrs. Mendham, “and cost ever so much﻿—I forget how much﻿—but a wonderful lot! It’s quite the talk of the village.”

“Mother,” said Cissie Mendham, “you are stepping on a grave.”

===Chinese===

奇异的来访的故事，到这里就结束了。尾声出自门德姆太太之口。锡德莫顿的教堂墓地里立着两个小小的白色十字架，紧挨在一起，荆棘从石墙上爬过来掩着它们。一个上面刻着托马斯·安吉尔（Thomas Angel），另一个刻着迪莉娅·哈代（Delia Hardy），忌辰是同一天。其实底下什么也没有，只有教区牧师那只鸵鸟标本的灰。（你们总还记得，教区牧师有他的鸟类学的一面。）这两座碑是我陪门德姆太太看德拉贝什家（De la Beche）的新纪念碑时留意到的。（希利尔去世后，门德姆接任了教区牧师。）“这花岗岩是从苏格兰什么地方运来的，”门德姆太太说，“花了好大一笔钱——多少我忘了——反正多得了不得！全村都拿它当谈资呢。”

“妈，”西茜·门德姆说，“您踩到一座坟了。”

===Original===

“Dear me!” said Mrs. Mendham, “How heedless of me! And the cripple’s grave too. But really you’ve no idea how much this monument cost them.”

“These two people, by the by,” said Mrs. Mendham, “were killed when the old Vicarage was burnt. It’s rather a strange story. He was a curious person, a hunchbacked fiddler, who came from nobody knows where, and imposed upon the late Vicar to a frightful extent. He played in a pretentious way by ear, and we found out afterwards that he did not know a note of music﻿—not a note. He was exposed before quite a lot of people. Among other things, he seems to have been ‘carrying on,’ as people say, with one of the servants, a sly little drab.﻿ ﻿… But Mendham had better tell you all about it. The man was half-witted and curiously deformed. It’s strange the fancies girls have.”

===Chinese===

“哎呀！”门德姆太太说，“我多不小心！还是那瘸子的坟呢。不过这纪念碑花了他们多少钱，您真没法想象。”

“顺带说说，这两个人，”门德姆太太说，“是老牧师公馆失火那年烧死的。说来是桩离奇的故事。那男的是个古怪人物，一个驼背的提琴手，来历无人知晓，把已故的教区牧师蒙骗得好苦。他拉琴全凭耳朵，装腔作势，我们后来才发现，他连一个音符都不识——一个都不识。还是当着好多人揭穿的。此外呢，他还同一个小女仆‘有一手’，照人们的说法——一个贼眉鼠眼的小骚货。……不过这些还是让门德姆讲给你们听吧。那人脑子缺根弦，身子又畸得古怪。姑娘家的心思真是奇怪。”

===Original===

She looked sharply at Cissie, and Cissie blushed to the eyes.

“She was left in the house and he rushed into the flames in an attempt to save her. Quite romantic﻿—isn’t it? He was rather clever with the fiddle in his uneducated way.

“All the poor Vicar’s stuffed skins were burned at the same time. It was almost all he cared for. He never really got over the blow. He came to stop with us﻿—for there wasn’t another house available in the village. But he never seemed happy. He seemed all shaken. I never saw a man so changed. I tried to stir him up, but it was no good﻿—no good at all. He had the queerest delusions about angels and that kind of thing. It made him odd company at times. He would say he heard music, and stare quite stupidly at nothing for hours together. He got quite careless about his dress.﻿ ﻿… He died within a twelvemonth of the fire.”

===Chinese===

她锐利地瞟了西茜一眼，西茜一直红到了眼根。

“她当时被落在了屋里，他冲进火里想去救她。够罗曼蒂克的——是不是？他那没受过教育的拉法，倒还算有两下子。”

“可怜的教区牧师那些鸟类标本，也一并烧了个精光。那几乎是他全部的心头爱。这个打击他到底没有真正缓过来。他来我们家住了一段——村里再寻不出别的空房。可他总不快活，整个人像散了架。我从没见一个男人变得这样厉害。我想提提他的精神，可没用——全没用。他对天使之类的事抱着最古怪的妄想，有时弄得人同他做伴都觉得别扭。他总说听见音乐，能对着空无一物的地方呆望好几个钟头。穿衣裳也马虎起来了。……火灾过后不到一年，他就去世了。”
