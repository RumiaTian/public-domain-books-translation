## XLVII The Last Day of the Visit / 四十七 来访的最后一天

===Original===

On the morning of the next day the Angel, after he had breakfasted, went out towards the moor, and Mrs. Hinijer had an interview with the Vicar. What happened need not concern us now. The Vicar was visibly disconcerted. “He *must* go,” he said; “certainly he must go,” and straightway he forgot the particular accusation in the general trouble. He spent the morning in hazy meditation, interspersed by a spasmodic study of Skiff and Waterlow’s price list, and the catalogue of the Medical, Scholastic, and Clerical Stores. A schedule grew slowly on a sheet of paper that lay on the desk before him. He cut out a self-measurement form from the tailoring department of the Stores and pinned it to the study curtains. This was the kind of document he was making:

> “1 Black Melton Frock Coat, patts? £3, 10s.
> “? Trousers. 2 pairs or one.
> “1 Cheviot Tweed Suit (write for patterns. Self-meas.?)”

The Vicar spent some time studying a pleasing array of model gentlemen. They were all very nice-looking, but he found it hard to imagine the Angel so transfigured. For, although six days had passed, the Angel remained without any suit of his own. The Vicar had vacillated between a project of driving the Angel into Portbroddock and getting him measured for a suit, and his absolute horror of the insinuating manners of the tailor he employed. He knew that tailor would demand an exhaustive explanation. Besides which, one never knew when the Angel might leave. So the six days had passed, and the Angel had grown steadily in the wisdom of this world and shrouded his brightness still in the ample retirement of the Vicar’s newest clothes.

> “黑色麦尔登长礼服一件，要料样？3 镑 10 先令。
> “长裤？两条还是一条。
> “切维奥特粗花呢套装一套（函索料样。自助量体？）”

教区牧师花了不少工夫，研究一列讨人喜欢的模特绅士。他们个个仪表堂堂，可他很难想象天使也能如此改头换面。原来，尽管六天过去了，天使仍旧没有一套自己的衣裳。教区牧师一直在两个念头之间摇摆：一是驾车载天使去波特伯多克量体做衣，一是他一想起自己那位裁缝那种刨根问底的做派就深恶痛绝。他知道那裁缝准会逼他作一番详尽无遗的解释。再说，谁说得准天使哪天就走了呢。就这样过了六天，天使在这个世界的世事上稳步长进，那身光彩也仍旧裹在教区牧师几件最新衣裳的宽大退隐里。

And so forth﻿—meanderingly. It kept the Vicar busy until lunch time, though his heart ached.
The Angel did not return to lunch. This was not so very remarkable﻿—once before he had missed the midday meal. Yet, considering how short was the time they would have together now, he might perhaps have come back. Doubtless he had excellent reasons, though, for his absence. The Vicar made an indifferent lunch. In the afternoon he rested in his usual manner, and did a little more to the list of requirements. He did not begin to feel nervous about the Angel till teatime. He waited, perhaps, half an hour before he took tea. “Odd,” said the Vicar, feeling still more lonely as he drank his tea.
As the time for dinner crept on and no Angel appeared the Vicar’s imagination began to trouble him. “He will come in to dinner, surely,” said the Vicar, caressing his chin, and beginning to fret about the house upon inconsiderable errands, as his habit was when anything occurred to break his routine. The sun set, a gorgeous spectacle, amidst tumbled masses of purple cloud. The gold and red faded into twilight; the evening star gathered her robe of light together from out the brightness of the sky in the West. Breaking the silence of evening that crept over the outer world, a corncrake began his whirring chant. The Vicar’s face grew troubled; twice he went and stared at the darkening hillside, and then fretted back to the house again. Mrs. Hinijer served dinner. “Your dinner’s ready,” she announced for the second time, with a reproachful intonation. “Yes, yes,” said the Vicar, fussing off upstairs.
He came down and went into his study and lit his reading lamp, a patent affair with an incandescent wick, dropping the match into his wastepaper basket without stopping to see if it was extinguished. Then he fretted into the dining-room and began a desultory attack on the cooling dinner.﻿ ﻿…
(Dear Reader, the time is almost ripe to say farewell to this little Vicar of ours.)

> “皮旅行箱一只（此类最好亲看）。”

诸如此类——信马由缰。这桩事让教区牧师一直忙到午饭时分，尽管他心里作痛。天使没有回来吃午饭。这不算太稀奇——先前他也误过一顿正餐。可是，想想如今他们能在一起的日子已这么短，他总该回来的。不过，他的缺席想必有十足的理由。教区牧师马马虎虎吃了午饭。下午他照例歇了歇，又给那份需用清单添了几笔。直到喝下午茶的时辰，他才开始为天使悬心。他等了大概半个钟头才用茶。“古怪，”教区牧师说，喝茶时愈发觉得冷清。晚饭的时辰一步步挨近，天使还是不见踪影，教区牧师的想象力开始作祟了。“他总会回来吃晚饭的吧，”教区牧师说，一边摩挲着下巴，一边为些不要紧的差事在屋里团团打转——每逢日常的轨道被什么事打断，他总要这样的。太阳落山了，紫色的乱云堆里，一派壮丽的景象。金红褪成暮色；长庚星从西天的明亮里拢起她一身的光袍。外间的世界渐渐爬上夜的沉寂，一只秧鸡打破了它，开始它嗡嗡的吟唱。教区牧师的脸色不安起来；他两度走出去，望着渐渐暗下去的山坡发怔，又心事重重地折回屋里。希尼杰太太端上晚饭。“晚饭得了，”她第二次通报，语调里带着责备。“就来，就来，”教区牧师说，慌慌张张地上了楼。他下楼进了书房，点亮阅读灯——那是一盏白炽灯芯的新式玩意儿——随手把火柴丢进废纸篓，也没停下来看看它熄了没有。随后他又心神不定地踱进饭厅，对那渐渐凉下去的晚饭发起一场零星的攻势。……（亲爱的读者，同我们这位小个子牧师道别的时辰，差不多熟了。）
