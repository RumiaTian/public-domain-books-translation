## XXVI / 二十六

Everybody Happy / 皆大欢喜

===Original===

Jimmy looked at Ann. They were alone. Mr. Pett had gone back to bed, Mrs. Crocker to her hotel. Mr. Crocker was removing his makeup in his room. A silence had followed their departure.
“This is the end of a perfect day!” said Jimmy.
Ann took a step towards the door.
“Don’t go!”
Ann stopped.
“Mr. Crocker!” she said.

===Chinese===

吉米望着安。屋里只剩下他们两人。佩特先生回床上去了，克罗克太太回旅馆去了，克罗克先生正在自己房里卸妆。他们一走，随之而来的是一片寂静。
「这完美的一天，就此落幕了！」吉米说。
安朝门口迈了一步。
「别走！」
安停住了脚。
「克罗克先生！」她说。

===Original===

“Jimmy,” he corrected.
“Mr. Crocker!” repeated Ann firmly.
“Or Algernon, if you prefer it.”
“May I ask﻿—” Ann regarded him steadily. “May I ask.”
“Nearly always,” said Jimmy, “when people begin with that, they are going to say something unpleasant.”
“May I ask why you went to all this trouble to make a fool of me? Why could you not have told me who you were from the start?”

===Chinese===

「叫吉米。」他纠正道。
「克罗克先生！」安斩钉截铁地又说了一遍。
「你若更喜欢，叫阿尔杰农也行。」
「我可以请问——」安定定地注视着他。「我可以请问。」
「十之八九，」吉米说，「人们一拿这话起头，接下去准没什么中听的。」
「我可以请问：你何苦费这么大的周章来愚弄我？为什么不从一开始就告诉我你是谁？」

===Original===

“Have you forgotten all the harsh things you said to me from time to time about Jimmy Crocker? I thought that, if you knew who I was, you would have nothing more to do with me.”
“You were quite right.”
“Surely, though, you won’t let a thing that happened five years ago make so much difference?”
“I shall never forgive you!”
“And yet, a little while ago, when Willie’s bomb was about to go off, you flung yourself into my arms!”

===Chinese===

「你难道忘了，你从前时不时对我数落的那些关于吉米·克罗克的难听话？我原以为，你要是知道了我是什么人，就再也不会同我来往了。」
「你想得一点没错。」
「不过话说回来，五年前的一桩旧事，你总不至于让它起这么大的作用吧？」
「我永远不会原谅你！」
「可是方才，威利的炸弹眼看要炸的时候，你可是扑进了我怀里！」

===Original===

Ann’s face flamed.
“I lost my balance.”
“Why try to recover it?”
Ann bit her lip.
“You did a cruel, heartless thing. What does it matter how long ago it was? If you were capable of it then﻿—”

===Chinese===

安的脸腾地烧红了。
「我一时没站稳。」
「那又何必非要站稳呢？」
安咬住了嘴唇。
「你干下的是一件残酷无情的事。事情隔了多久又有什么要紧？你当时既然干得出来——」

===Original===

“Be reasonable. Don’t you admit the possibility of reformation? Take your own case. Five years ago you were a minor poetess. Now you are an amateur kidnapper﻿—a bright, lovable girl at whose approach people lock up their children and sit on the key. As for me, five years ago I was a heartless brute. Now I am a sober serious businessman, specially called in by your uncle to help jack up his tottering firm. Why not bury the dead past? Besides﻿—I don’t want to praise myself, I just want to call your attention to it﻿—think what I have done for you. You admitted yourself that it was my influence that had revolutionised your character. But for me, you would now be doing worse than write poetry. You would be writing *vers libre*. I saved you from that. And you spurn me!”
“I hate you!” said Ann.
Jimmy went to the writing-desk and took up a small book.
“Put that down!”
“I just wanted to read you ‘Love’s Funeral’! It illustrates my point. Think of yourself as you are now, and remember that it is I who am responsible for the improvement. Here we are. ‘Love’s Funeral’. ‘My heart is dead.﻿ ﻿…’ ”

===Chinese===

「讲点道理嘛。你难道不承认人有改过自新的可能？就拿你自己的情形来说：五年前，你是个无足轻重的小诗人；如今你成了业余绑匪——一个聪明可爱、人还未到家家便锁起孩子、坐上钥匙的姑娘。再说我：五年前，我是个没心肝的恶棍；如今我是个清醒严肃的生意人，还是你叔叔特意请来、给那摇摇欲坠的公司支千斤顶的。何不把死去的往事埋葬了呢？再者——我不想自吹自擂，只想请你留意这个事实——想想我为你做了多少。你自己也承认过，正是我的影响使你改头换面。倘若没有我，你如今干的事会比写诗更糟——你会去写*自由体诗*。是我把你从那深渊里救出来的。而你竟把我一脚踢开！」
「我恨你！」安说。
吉米走到写字台前，拿起一本小书。
「把书放下！」
「我不过想给你念念《爱的葬礼》！它正好印证我的论点。想想你如今这副模样，并且记住，这份长进全亏了我。找着了。《爱的葬礼》。『我心已死。……』」

===Original===

Ann snatched the book from his hands and flung it away. It soared up, clearing the gallery rails, and fell with a thud on the gallery floor. She stood facing him with sparkling eyes. Then she moved away.
“I beg your pardon,” she said stiffly. “I lost my temper.”
“It’s your hair,” said Jimmy soothingly. “You’re bound to be quick-tempered with hair of that glorious red shade. You must marry some nice, determined fellow, blue-eyed, dark-haired, clean-shaven, about five foot eleven, with a future in business. He will keep you in order.”
“Mr. Crocker!”
“Gently, of course. Kindly﻿—lovingly. The velvet thingummy rather than the iron what’s-its-name. But nevertheless firmly.”
Ann was at the door.

===Chinese===

安一把从他手里夺过书，甩了出去。那书腾空飞起，越过楼廊栏杆，啪的一声闷响落在楼廊地板上。她面对着他站定，双眼灼灼发亮。随后她走开了。
「请原谅，」她生硬地说。「我失态了。」
「这得怪你的头发，」吉米抚慰道。「顶着一头那样红得光彩夺目的头发，脾气急是在所难免的。你一定得嫁一个正派而有决断的人：蓝眼睛，黑头发，脸刮得干干净净，身高五英尺十一英寸上下，在商界大有前程。他会把你管教得服服帖帖。」
「克罗克先生！」
「当然是温柔地管。和蔼地——怜爱地。要天鹅绒的那什么，不要铁打的那玩意儿。不过仍旧要坚定不移。」
安已经到了门口。

===Original===

“To a girl with your ardent nature someone with whom you can quarrel is an absolute necessity of life. You and I are affinities. Ours will be an ideally happy marriage. You would be miserable if you had to go through life with a human doormat with ‘Welcome’ written on him. You want someone made of sterner stuff. You want, as it were, a sparring-partner, someone with whom you can quarrel happily with the certain knowledge that he will not curl up in a ball for you to kick, but will be there with the return wallop. I may have my faults﻿—” He paused expectantly. Ann remained silent. “No, no!” he went on. “But I am such a man. Brisk give-and-take is the foundation of the happy marriage. Do you remember that beautiful line of Tennyson’s﻿—‘We fell out, my wife and I’? It always conjures up for me a vision of wonderful domestic happiness. I seem to see us in our old age, you on one side of the radiator, I on the other, warming our old limbs and thinking up snappy stuff to hand to each other﻿—sweethearts still! If I were to go out of your life now, you would be miserable. You would have nobody to quarrel with. You would be in the position of the female jaguar of the Indian jungle, who, as you doubtless know, expresses her affection for her mate by biting him shrewdly in the fleshy part of the leg, if she should snap sideways one day and find nothing there.”
Of all the things which Ann had been trying to say during this discourse, only one succeeded in finding expression. To her mortification, it was the only weak one in the collection.
“Are you asking me to marry you?”
“I am.”
“I won’t!”
“You think so now, because I am not appearing at my best. You see me nervous, diffident, tongue-tied. All this will wear off, however, and you will be surprised and delighted as you begin to understand my true self. Beneath the surface﻿—I speak conservatively﻿—I am a corker!”

===Chinese===

「以你这样热烈的天性，一个能跟你吵架的人实在是生活的绝对必需品。你我二人正是天造地设的一对。我们的婚姻将是一场理想的美满婚姻。倘若你得跟一个身上印着『欢迎光临』字样的人形门垫厮守一生，那你才叫可怜。你要的是一个用更硬扎的材料造就的人。可以说，你要的是一位陪练搭档，一个能让你开开心心吵架的对象，而且你尽可放心：他绝不会蜷缩成一团任你踢，他必定原样奉还一记重拳。我或许自有种种缺点——」他满怀期待地停了停。安一声不吭。「不，不！」他继续说道。「但我正是这样一个人。爽利的你来我往，正是美满婚姻的地基。你可记得丁尼生那行美妙的诗——『我们吵翻了，我和我妻子』？它总在我心头唤起一幅妙不可言的家庭幸福图景。我仿佛看见我们到了垂暮之年，你坐在暖气片的这一边，我坐在那一边，烘着我们老迈的四肢，各自琢磨些俏皮话递给对方——依旧是一对恩爱情侣！若是我此刻从你的生活里退场，你可就要痛苦了。你将无人可吵。你的处境将等同印度丛林中的母美洲豹——想必你知道，她向配偶表达爱意的方式，是照着他腿上多肉的地方狠狠咬上一口——倘若有一天她侧头咬去，却发现那里空空如也。」
在他发表这番宏论期间，安想说的许多话里，只有一句成功地冲出了口。令她羞恼的是，那一句恰是全部存货中最不济的一件。
「你这是在向我求婚吗？」
「正是。」
「我不答应！」
「你现在这样想，是因为我未将最佳状态示人。你眼里的我紧张、怯场、张口结舌。不过这一切都会消退，等你开始领会真正的我时，你自会又惊又喜。在这层皮相底下——我说得保守些——我可是个响当当的妙人！」

===Original===

The door banged behind Ann. Jimmy found himself alone. He walked thoughtfully to Mr. Pett’s armchair and sat down. There was a feeling of desolation upon him. He lit a cigarette and began to smoke pensively. What a fool he had been to talk like that! What girl of spirit could possibly stand it? If ever there had been a time for being soothing and serious and pleading, it had been these last few minutes. And he talked like that!
Ten minutes passed. Jimmy sprang from his chair. He thought he had heard a footstep. He flung the door open. The passage was empty. He returned miserably to his chair. Of course she had not come back. Why should she?
A voice spoke.
“Jimmy!”
He leaped up again, and looked wildly round. Then he looked up. Ann was leaning over the gallery rail.

===Chinese===

门在安身后砰然关上。吉米发觉自己孤零零一人。他心事重重地踱到佩特先生的扶手椅前，坐了下来。一阵凄凉之感笼罩着他。他点燃一支香烟，开始闷闷地抽。方才那样讲话，他真是头号蠢驴！稍有几分志气的姑娘怎么受得了？倘若世上有过一个时刻，最该温存体贴、一本正经、低声下气，那便是方才那几分钟。而他偏偏那样讲话！
十分钟过去了。吉米从椅子里蹦起来。他觉得自己听见了脚步声。他猛地推开门。过道里空无一人。他灰溜溜地回到椅子上。她当然没有回来。她凭什么回来？
一个声音响了。
「吉米！」
他又一跃而起，慌乱地环顾四周。随后他抬起头来。安正俯身倚在楼廊栏杆上。

===Original===

“Jimmy, I’ve been thinking it over. There’s something I want to ask you. Do you admit that you behaved abominably five years ago?”
“Yes!” shouted Jimmy.
“And that you’ve been behaving just as badly ever since?”
“Yes!”
“And that you are really a pretty awful sort of person?”
“Yes!”

===Chinese===

「吉米，我把这事通盘想过了。有个问题要问你。你承不承认，五年前你的行为恶劣透顶？」
「承认！」吉米高声道。
「承不承认，打那以后你一直表现得同样恶劣？」
「承认！」
「承不承认，你实在是个糟糕透顶的家伙？」
「承认！」

===Original===

“Then it’s all right. You deserve it!”
“Deserve it?”
“Deserve to marry a girl like me. I was worried about it, but now I see that it’s the only punishment bad enough for you!” She raised her arm.
“Here’s the dead past, Jimmy! Go and bury it! Good night!”
A small book fell squashily at Jimmy’s feet. He regarded it dully for a moment. Then, with a wild yell which penetrated even to Mr. Pett’s bedroom and woke that sufferer just as he was dropping off to sleep for the third time that night he bounded for the gallery stairs.
At the further end of the gallery a musical laugh sounded, and a door closed. Ann had gone.

===Chinese===

「那就妥了。你罪有应得！」
「罪有应得？」
「该当娶一个我这样的姑娘。我先前还为这事担忧来着，如今可看明白了：这是唯一罚得够重、配得上你的惩罚！」她扬起手臂。
「这就是那死去的往事，吉米！拿去埋了吧！晚安！」
一本小书啪唧一声落在吉米脚边。他怔怔地望了它片刻。随后，他发出一声狂呼，朝楼廊楼梯纵身奔去——那声狂呼甚至直透佩特先生的卧室，在那位受害者当夜第三次正要朦胧入睡之际将他惊醒。
楼廊尽头响起一串音乐般的笑声，一扇门随即关上。安走了。
