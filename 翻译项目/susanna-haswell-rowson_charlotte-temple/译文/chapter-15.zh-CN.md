## XV Embarkation / XV 登船启航

===Original===

It was with the utmost difficulty that the united efforts of mademoiselle and Montraville could support Charlotte’s spirits during their short ride from Chichester7 to Portsmouth, where a boat waited to take them immediately on board the ship in which they were to embark for America.

As soon as she became tolerably composed, she entreated pen and ink to write to her parents. This she did in the most affecting, artless manner, entreating their pardon and blessing, and describing the dreadful situation of her mind, the conflict she suffered in endeavoring to conquer this unfortunate attachment, and concluded with saying her only hope of future comfort consisted in the (perhaps delusive) idea she indulged, of being once more folded in their protecting arms, and hearing the words of peace and pardon from their lips.

The tears streamed incessantly while she was writing, and she was frequently obliged to lay down her pen: but when the task was completed, and she had committed the letter to the care of Montraville, to be sent to the post office, she became more calm, and indulging the delightful hope of soon receiving an answer that would seal her pardon, she in some measure assumed her usual cheerfulness.

===Chinese===

从奇切斯特[7]到朴次茅斯这一短短的旅程中，全靠小姐和蒙特维尔合力宽解，夏洛特的精神才勉强支撑得住；朴次茅斯有一艘小艇等着，要把她们立刻送上那艘即将启航开赴美洲的船。

她刚一勉强平静下来，便恳求给她纸笔，给父母写信。她写得那样动人、那样毫无心机：恳求他们的宽恕和祝福，描画她心境的可怕，描画她努力克服这段不幸情缘所受的煎熬，末了说，她对来日安慰的惟一指望，全在于她纵容自己的那个（也许是虚妄的）念头——有朝一日重被父母护佑的双臂搂住，亲耳听他们说出和平与宽恕的话。

写信时她泪流不止，好几回不得不搁下笔来：但写完了，把信托付给蒙特维尔、让他送去邮局之后，她平静了些，纵着自己去想那很快便能得到封印宽恕的回音的甜美希望，多少恢复了往日的欢颜。

===Original===

But Montraville knew too well the consequences that must unavoidably ensue should this letter reach Mr. Temple: he, therefore, wisely resolved to walk on the deck, tear it in pieces, and commit the fragments to the care of Neptune, who might or might not, as it suited his convenience, convey them on shore.

All Charlotte’s hopes and wishes were now concentered in one, namely, that the fleet might be detained at Spithead till she could receive a letter from her friends; but in this she was disappointed, for the second morning after she went on board the signal was made, the fleet8 weighed anchor, and in a few hours (the wind being favorable) they bade adieu to the white cliffs of Albion.

In the meantime every enquiry that could be thought of was made by Mr. and Mrs. Temple; for many days did they indulge the fond hope that she was merely gone off to be married, and that when the indissoluble knot was once tied, she would return with the partner she had chosen and entreat their blessing and forgiveness.

===Chinese===

可是蒙特维尔太清楚这封信一旦落到坦普尔先生手里必然引起的后果：于是他明智地决意在甲板上散步时把信撕成碎片，交给尼普顿（Neptune）照管——这位海神方便的话，也许肯、也许不肯替他把碎片捎上岸去。

此时夏洛特的一切希望和心愿都聚成了一条：但愿舰队能在斯皮特黑德（Spithead）滞留到她收到亲友的来信；可这一点她也落了空，因为她上船后的第二个早晨，信号升起了，舰队[8]起锚了，不出几个钟头（风势顺利），他们便向阿尔比恩（Albion）的白色峭壁道了别。

与此同时，坦普尔先生和坦普尔夫人用尽了一切想得到的打探之方；许多天里，他们纵着那痴心的指望，想她不过是去成了亲，等那解不开的结一系上，她就会带着自己选中的伴侣回来，恳求他们的祝福和宽恕。

===Original===

“And shall we not forgive her?” said Mr. Temple.

“Forgive her!” exclaimed the mother. “Oh! yes; whatever be her errors, is she not our child? And though bowed to the earth even with shame and remorse, is it not our duty to raise the poor penitent and whisper peace and comfort to her desponding soul? would she but return, with rapture would I fold her to my heart and bury every remembrance of her faults in the dear embrace.”

But still, day after day passed on and Charlotte did not appear, nor were any tidings to be heard of her: yet each rising morning was welcomed by some new hope﻿—the evening brought with it disappointment. At length hope was no more; despair usurped her place, and the mansion which was once the mansion of peace became the habitation of pale, dejected melancholy.

===Chinese===

“我们还不肯饶她么？”坦普尔先生说。

“饶她！”做母亲的喊道。“哦！肯的；无论她有什么错处，她难道不是我们的孩子么？哪怕她被羞愧和悔恨压弯到尘埃里，把这可怜的忏悔者扶起来、对她绝望的灵魂低声说些安宁宽慰的话，不正是我们的本分么？只要她肯回来，我会狂喜地把她搂进怀里，把对她过错的每一分记忆，都埋进那亲爱的拥抱之中。”

可是一天又一天过去，夏洛特没有露面，也听不到她的一点音讯：然而每一个新升的早晨都带着新的希望受到欢迎——傍晚随之而来的是失望。终于，希望不复存在；绝望僭据了它的位置；那座曾是安宁之宅的宅邸，变成了苍白沮丧的忧郁之所。

===Original===

The cheerful smile that was wont to adorn the face of Mrs. Temple was fled, and had it not been for the support of unaffected piety, and a consciousness of having ever set before her child the fairest example, she must have sunk under this heavy affliction.

“Since,” said she, “the severest scrutiny can not charge me with any breach of duty, to have deserved this severe chastisement, I will bow before the Power who inflicts it with humble resignation to His will; nor shall the duty of a wife be totally absorbed in the feelings of the mother; I will endeavor to appear more cheerful, and by appearing in some measure to have conquered my own sorrow, alleviate the sufferings of my husband, and rouse him from that torpor into which this misfortune has plunged him. My father, too, demands my care and attention: I must not, by a selfish indulgence of my own grief, forget the interest those two dear objects take in my happiness or misery: I will wear a smile on my face, though the thorn rankles in my heart; and if by so doing, I in the smallest degree contribute to restore their peace of mind, I shall be amply rewarded for the pain the concealment of my own feelings may occasion.”

Thus argued this excellent woman: and in the execution of so laudable a resolution, we shall leave her to follow the fortunes of the hapless victim of imprudence and evil counselors.


===Chinese===

往日点缀着坦普尔夫人脸颊的那副欢欣的笑容不见了；若不是有质朴的虔敬支撑着，又自觉曾在女儿面前立过最端正的榜样，她早就被这沉重的打击压垮了。

“既然，”她说，“最严苛的细察也找不出我有半分亏职、该受这般严厉的惩戒，那么我愿怀着恭顺的听命，向降下这惩戒的大能俯首；为妻的本分也不该被为母的情感完全吞没；我要勉力显得欢快些，借表面上多少战胜了自己的悲伤，来减轻我丈夫的痛苦，把他从这桩不幸使他陷入的麻木中唤起来。我的父亲也需要我的照料和挂心：我决不能因放纵自己的哀伤，就忘了那两个亲爱的人对我的幸福或忧苦的牵挂：我脸上要挂着笑，哪怕心头的荆棘还在刺痛；倘若这样做能为恢复他们心境的安宁尽到一分力，那么掩饰自己的情感纵然带来痛苦，我也得到了丰厚的报偿。”

这位贤淑的妇人便是这样自持：在她践行这一可嘉决心的同时，我们且撇下她，去追随那个轻率与恶友手中的不幸牺牲者的命运。
