## XXVI What Might Be Expected / XXVI 意中之事

===Original===

In the meantime the passion Montraville had conceived for Julia Franklin daily increased, and he saw evidently how much he was beloved by that amiable girl: he was likewise strongly prepossessed with an idea of Charlotte’s perfidy. What wonder, then, if he gave himself up to the delightful sensation which pervaded his bosom; and finding no obstacle arise to oppose his happiness, he solicited and obtained the hand of Julia. A few days before his marriage, he thus addressed Belcour:

“Though Charlotte, by her abandoned conduct, has thrown herself from my protection, I still hold myself bound to support her till relieved from her present condition, and also to provide for the child. I do not intend to see her again, but I will place a sum of money in your hands which will amply supply her with every convenience; but should she require more, let her have it, and I will see it repaid. I wish I could prevail on the poor, deluded girl to return to her friends; she was an only child, and I make no doubt but that they would joyfully receive her; it would shock me greatly to see her henceforth leading a life of infamy, as I should always accuse myself of being the primary cause of all her errors. If she should choose to remain under your protection, be kind to her, Belcour, I conjure you. Let not satiety prompt you to treat her in such a manner as may drive her to actions which necessity might urge her to, while her better reason disapproves them: she shall never want a friend while I live, but I never more desire to behold her: her presence would be always painful to me, and a glance from her eye would call the blush of conscious guilt into my cheek.

“I will write a letter to her, which you may deliver when I am gone, as I shall go to St. Eustatia the day after my union with Julia, who will accompany me.”

与此同时，蒙特维尔对朱莉娅·富兰克林怀有的激情与日俱增，他又分明看得出那位可爱的姑娘多么爱他：他还固执地相信着夏洛特的背信。于是何怪他纵着自己沉入漫过胸怀的甜美感觉；眼见没有障碍起来阻挡他的幸福，他便向朱莉娅求婚，并得到了她。婚期前几天，他这样对贝尔库尔说：

“我要给她写一封信，你可以在我走后交给她，因为我同朱莉娅成婚的第二天就去圣尤斯塔歇斯（St. Eustatia），她将随我同行。”

Belcour promised to fulfil the request of his friend, though nothing was further from his intentions than the least design of delivering the letter, or making Charlotte acquainted with the provision Montraville had made for her. He was bent on the complete ruin of the unhappy girl, and supposed, by reducing her to an entire dependence upon him, to bring her by degrees to consent to gratify his ungenerous passion.

The evening before the day appointed for the nuptials of Montraville and Julia, the former retired early to his apartment, and, ruminating on the past scenes of his life, suffered the keenest remorse in the remembrance of Charlotte’s seduction. “Poor girl,” said he, “I will at least write and bid her adieu; I will, too, endeavor to awaken that love of virtue in her bosom which her unfortunate attachment to me has extinguished.” He took up the pen and began to write, but words were denied him. How could he address the woman whom he had seduced, and whom, though he thought unworthy his tenderness, he was about to bid adieu forever? How should he tell her that he was going to abjure her, to enter into the most indissoluble ties with another, and that he could not even own the infant which she bore as his child? Several letters were begun and destroyed: at length he completed the following:

贝尔库尔答应照朋友所托去办，尽管送信也好、让夏洛特知道蒙特维尔为她作的安排也好，都同他的本意相去十万八千里。他一心要彻底毁掉这不幸的姑娘，盘算着把她逼到完全仰他鼻息的地步，好一步步诱她点头，满足他卑鄙的情欲。

蒙特维尔和朱莉娅婚期前一天的晚上，蒙特维尔早早退回自己房中，回味着生平的旧事，一想起夏洛特的堕落，便受着最锋利的悔恨的咬啮。“可怜的姑娘，”他说，“我至少要写一封信，向她道别；我还要尽力唤醒她心中对美德的爱——那爱被她对我的不幸依恋扑灭了。”他提起笔来想写，却找不出话。他怎能面对这个被他诱骗的女子，这个他虽以为不配他的柔情、却即将与之永诀的女子？他怎能告诉她，他就要弃绝她，去同另一个人结成解不开的盟约，而且连她腹中怀着的婴儿，他都不能认作自己的孩子？好几封信起了头又撕掉了：最后他写成了下面这一封：

“致夏洛特。

“Montraville.”

After he had sealed this letter he threw himself on the bed and enjoyed a few hours’ repose. Early in the morning Belcour tapped at his door: he arose hastily, and prepared to meet his Julia at the altar.

“This is the letter to Charlotte,” said he, giving it to Belcour: “take it to her when we are gone to Eustatia;13 and I conjure you, my dear friend, not to use any sophilastic arguments to prevent her return to virtue; but should she incline that way, encourage her in the thought and assist her to put her design in execution.”

“蒙特维尔。”

封好这封信，他扑倒在床上，得了几个钟头的安眠。清晨，贝尔库尔来叩他的门：他急忙起身，准备去圣坛前会他的朱莉娅。

“这是给夏洛特的信，”他把信交给贝尔库尔说：“我们去了尤斯塔歇斯[13]之后再交给她；我恳求你，亲爱的朋友，别用什么诡辩的话拦她回向美德；她若有意那方面去，就鼓励她这个念头，帮她把打算付诸实行。”
