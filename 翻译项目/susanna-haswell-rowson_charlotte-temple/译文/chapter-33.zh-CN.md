## XXXIII Which People Void of Feeling Need Not Read / XXXIII 无情之人不必读这一章

===Original===

When Mrs. Beauchamp entered the apartment of the poor sufferer, she started back with horror. On a wretched bed, without hangings and but poorly supplied with covering, lay the emaciated figure of what still retained the semblance of a lovely woman, though sickness had so altered her features that Mrs. Beauchamp had not the least recollection of her person. In one corner of the room stood a woman washing, and shivering over a small fire, two healthy, but half-naked children; the infant was asleep beside its mother, and on a chair by the bedside stood a porringer and wooden spoon containing a little gruel, and a teacup with about two spoonfuls of wine in it. Mrs. Beauchamp had never before beheld such a scene of poverty; she shuddered involuntarily, and exclaiming﻿—“heaven preserve us!” leaned on the back of a chair, ready to sink to the earth. The doctor repented having so precipitately brought her into this affecting scene; but there was no time for apologies: Charlotte caught the sound of her voice, and starting almost out of bed, exclaimed﻿—“Angel of peace and mercy, art thou come to deliver me? Oh, I know you are, for whenever you was near me I felt eased of half my sorrows; but you don’t know me, nor can I, with all the recollection I am mistress of, remember your name just now; but I know that benevolent countenance and the softness of that voice, which has so often comforted the wretched Charlotte.”

Mrs. Beauchamp had, during the time Charlotte was speaking, seated herself on the bed: and taking one of her hands she looked at her attentively, and at the name of Charlotte she perfectly conceived the whole shocking affair. A faint sickness came over her. “Gracious Heaven!” said she, “is this possible?” and bursting into tears, she reclined the burning head of Charlotte on her own bosom; and folding her arms about her, wept over her in silence. “Oh,” said Charlotte, “you are very good to weep thus for me: it is a long time since I shed a tear for myself: my head and heart are both on fire; but these tears of yours seem to cool and refresh it. Oh, now I remember you said you would send a letter to my poor father: do you think he ever received it? or perhaps you have brought me an answer: why don’t you speak, madam? Does he say I may go home? Well, he is very good: I shall soon be ready.”

She then made an effort to get out of bed; but being prevented, her frenzy again returned, and she raved with the greatest wildness and incoherence. Mrs. Beauchamp, finding it was impossible for her to be removed, contented herself with ordering the apartment to be made more comfortable, and procuring a proper nurse for both mother and child; and having learned the particulars of Charlotte’s fruitless application to Mrs. Crayton from honest John, she amply rewarded him for his benevolence, and returned home with a heart oppressed with many painful sensations, but yet rendered easy by the reflection that she had performed her duty toward a distressed fellow creature.

Early the next morning she again visited Charlotte, and found her tolerably composed; she called her by name, thanked her for her goodness, and when her child was brought to her, pressed it in her arms, wept over it, and called it the offspring of disobedience. Mrs. Beauchamp was delighted to see her so much amended, and began to hope she might recover, and in spite of her former errors, become a useful and respectable member of society; but the arrival of the doctor put an end to these delusive hopes: he said nature was making her last effort, and a few hours would most probably consign the unhappy girl to her kindred dust.

博尚夫人走进那可怜的受难者的房间，惊怖得倒退了一步。一张破床上，没有床幔，盖的东西也稀薄，躺着一个瘦骨嶙峋的身形，虽还保留着一个可爱女子的模样，病魔却已把她的容貌改变得使博尚夫人全然认不出了。屋子一角，一个女人在洗衣，缩着身子守着一小盆火；两个健康却半裸的孩子；婴儿睡在母亲身旁；床头椅上放着一只粥碗和一把木匙，盛着一点麦片粥，还有一只茶杯，里面约有两匙酒。博尚夫人从未见过这样的贫穷景象；她不由自主地打了个寒颤，喊了一声——“上天保佑我们！”便扶住椅背，几乎要跌坐在地。医生懊悔不该这样仓促地带她进这伤心之地；可道歉已来不及：夏洛特听出了她的声音，几乎从床上跳起来，喊道——“和平与仁慈的天使，你是来解脱我的么？哦，我知道你是；因为你一靠近我，我的悲伤便消了一半；可你不认得我，而我呢，纵然竭力回想，此刻也想不起你的名字；可我认得那副仁厚的面容，认得那声音的温柔——它曾多少次安慰过不幸的夏洛特。”

夏洛特说话的时候，博尚夫人在床边坐下：执起她的一只手，端详着她；一听到夏洛特这个名字，她便把整个骇人的事想明白了。一阵轻微的昏眩涌上来。“慈悲的上天！”她说，“这怎么可能？”她泪如泉涌，把夏洛特烧灼的头搂在自己怀里；双臂环着她，默默流泪。“哦，”夏洛特说，“你这样为我哭，你真好：我自己已经很久不曾流泪了：我的头和心都在燃烧；可你这些泪似乎把它凉一凉、清爽清爽。哦，现在我想起来了，你说过要替我给我可怜的父亲寄一封信：你想他收到了么？或许你带来了回信：您怎么不说话，夫人？他说我可以回家了么？好，他真好：我很快就准备好了。”

随后她挣扎着要下床；被拦住了，她的狂乱又发作起来，喊叫得异常激烈而语无伦次。博尚夫人见移她不动，只得吩咐把屋子收拾得舒适些，并为母子二人雇了一名合适的看护；又从老实的约翰口中得知夏洛特向克雷顿夫人求助无果的详情，便重赏了他的仁厚，怀着满心沉重的痛楚回了家——却又因想到自己已对受苦的同胞尽了本分而心安。

第二天一早她又来看夏洛特，见她相当平静；她唤她的名字，谢她的好意；孩子抱到她跟前时，她把它搂在怀里，对着它流泪，称它为“不从的后代”。博尚夫人见她大有起色，很是欢喜，开始指望她或许能康复，抛开旧日的过失，重新做一个有用、可敬的人；可医生的到来破灭了这些虚妄的希望：他说天性在作最后的挣扎，不出几个钟头，这不幸的姑娘多半就要归于尘土了。

Being asked how she found herself, she replied﻿—“Why, better, much better, doctor. I hope now I have but little more to suffer. I had last night a few hours sleep, and when I awoke recovered the full power of recollection. I am quite sensible of my weakness; I feel I have but little longer to combat with the shafts of affliction. I have an humble confidence in the mercy of Him who died to save the world, and trust that my sufferings in this state of mortality, joined to my unfeigned repentance, through His mercy, have blotted my offences from the sight of my offended Maker. I have but one care﻿—my poor infant! Father of mercy!” continued she, raising her eyes, “of Thy infinite goodness, grant that the sins of the parent be not visited on the unoffending child. May those who taught me to despise Thy laws be forgiven; lay not my offences to their charge I beseech Thee; and oh! shower the choicest of Thy blessings on those whose pity has soothed the afflicted heart, and made easy even the bed of pain and sickness.”

She was exhausted by this fervent address to the throne of mercy, and though her lips still moved, her voice became inarticulate: she lay for some time, as it were, in a doze, and then recovering, faintly pressed Mrs. Beauchamp’s hand, and requested that a clergyman might be sent for.

On his arrival she joined fervently in the pious office, frequently mentioning her ingratitude to her parents as what lay most heavy at her heart. When she had performed the last solemn duty, and was preparing to lie down, a little bustle on the outside door occasioned Mrs. Beauchamp to open it and inquire the cause. A man, in appearance about forty, presented himself, and asked for Mrs. Beauchamp.

“That is my name, sir,” said she.

问她觉得怎样，她答道——“唔，好些了，好多了，医生。我希望如今我受的苦不多了。昨夜我睡了几个钟头，醒来时记忆完全恢复了。我很清楚自己的虚弱；我知道我与苦难的箭镞交战不了多久了。我谦卑地信托那位为拯救世人而死的者的慈悲，相信我在尘世的受难，加上我真诚的悔悟，凭着他的慈悲，已把我的过犯从我触犯的造物主眼前抹去了。我只挂着一件事——我可怜的孩子！慈悲的天父！”她举目接着说，“以你无限的良善，求你叫父母的罪不落在无辜的孩子身上。愿那些教我轻看你律法的人得蒙宽恕；求你不要把我的过犯归到他们头上；哦！把你最精选的福分，倾在那些以怜恤抚慰受苦者的心、甚至叫病痛之榻也得安舒的人身上。”

这一番向慈悲宝座发出的热切祷告使她力竭；她的嘴唇还在动，声音却不成语句了：她躺着，有一阵像是打盹，随后缓过来，轻轻握了握博尚夫人的手，请求派人去请一位牧师。

牧师到了，她热切地同他行了虔敬的礼，屡屡提到她对父母的忘恩，说那是压在她心上最重的东西。行完这最后的庄严本分，她正要躺下，外门一阵小小的骚动，使博尚夫人开门去看个究竟。一个看来约四十岁的男人站在那里，求见博尚夫人。

“那正是我的名字，先生。”她说。

“Oh, then, my dear madam,” cried he, “tell me where I may find my poor, ruined, but repentant child.”

Mrs. Beauchamp was surprised and affected; she knew not what to say; she foresaw the agony this interview would occasion Mr. Temple, who had just arrived in search of his Charlotte, and yet was sensible that the pardon and blessing of her father would soften even the agonies of death to the daughter.

She hesitated. “Tell me, madam,” cried he, wildly, “tell me, I beseech thee, does she live? shall I see my darling once again? Perhaps she is in this house. Lead, lead me to her, that I may bless her, and then lie down and die.”

The ardent manner in which he uttered these words occasioned him to raise his voice. It caught the ear of Charlotte: she knew the beloved sound: and uttering a loud shriek, she sprang forward as Mr. Temple entered the room. “My adored father.” “My long lost child.” Nature could support no more, and they both sunk lifeless into the arms of the attendants.

Charlotte was again put into bed, and a few moments restored Mr. Temple: but to describe the agony of his sufferings is past the power of anyone, who though they may readily conceive, can not delineate the dreadful scene. Every eye gave testimony of what each heart felt﻿—but all were silent.

When Charlotte recovered, she found herself supported in her father’s arms. She cast on him a most expressive look, but was unable to speak. A reviving cordial was administered. She then asked in a low voice for her child: it was brought to her: she put it in her father’s arms. “Protect her,” said she, “and bless your dying﻿—”

Unable to finish the sentence, she sunk back on her pillow: her countenance was serenely composed; she regarded her father as he pressed the infant to his breast, with a steadfast look; a sudden beam of joy passed across her languid features: she raised her eyes to heaven﻿—and then closed them forever.

夏洛特重新被放上床，片刻之后坦普尔先生缓了过来：可他受苦的剧烈，不是任何人所能描画的——尽管理解不难，那可怕的一幕却无人能够勾画。每一只眼睛都为各人的心作证——但大家都沉默着。

夏洛特苏醒过来，发觉自己被父亲的手臂扶着。她投给他一个意味深长的眼色，却说不出话。人们给她灌下一剂提神的药酒。随后她低声要她的孩子：孩子抱来了：她把他放进父亲的怀里。“保护她，”她说，“并祝福你这垂死的——”

这句话没能说完，她跌回枕上：她的面容安详平静；父亲把婴儿贴在胸前时，她以坚定的目光望着他；一道突至的喜悦掠过她憔悴的眉眼：她举目望天——然后永远地阖上了它们。
