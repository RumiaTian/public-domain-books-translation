## II Domestic Concerns / II 家事种种

===Original===

Mr. Temple was the youngest son of a nobleman, whose fortune was by no means adequate to the antiquity, grandeur, and I may add, pride of the family. He saw his elder brother made completely wretched by marrying a disagreeable woman, whose fortune helped to prop the sinking dignity of the house; and he beheld his sisters legally prostituted to old, decrepit men, whose titles gave them consequence in the eyes of the world, and whose affluence rendered them splendidly miserable. “I will not sacrifice internal happiness for outward show,” said he; “I will seek Content; and if I find her in a cottage, will embrace her with as much cordiality as I should if seated on a throne.”

Mr. Temple possessed a small estate of about five hundred pounds a year; and with that he resolved to preserve independence, to marry where the feelings of his heart should direct him, and to confine his expenses within the limits of his income. He had a heart open to every generous feeling of humanity, and a hand ready to dispense to those who wanted, part of the blessings he enjoyed himself.

As he was universally known to be the friend of the unfortunate, his advice and bounty were frequently solicited; nor was it seldom that he sought out indigent merit, and raised it from obscurity, confining his own expenses within a very narrow compass.

“You are a benevolent fellow,” said a young officer to him one day; “and I have a great mind to give you a fine subject to exercise the goodness of your heart upon.”

===Chinese===

坦普尔先生（Mr. Temple）是一位贵族的幼子，而这位贵族的家产，实在配不上这个家族的古老、显赫，还有——恕我直言——自傲。他眼见兄长娶了一个性情乖张的女人，从此完全陷入不幸，那女人的财产倒帮着撑住了这门户摇摇欲坠的体面；他又眼见自己的姐妹们借着合法的名分被嫁卖给了老朽之人，那些人的头衔使她们在世人眼中有了身价，那些人的财富则使她们过着豪华的悲惨生活。“我决不为外在的排场牺牲内心的幸福，”他说，“我要去寻觅‘知足’；即便是在一间茅舍里寻到她，我也会如同在王座上寻到她一般，满怀热忱地拥抱她。”

坦普尔先生拥有一份小小的田产，岁入约五百镑；他决意凭这份产业保持独立，依照内心情感的指引择偶成婚，并把开销限制在收入的范围之内。他的一颗心对人类一切慷慨的情感敞开着，一只手随时准备把自己所享福分的一部分，分给那些穷乏的人。

因为素来被公认为不幸者的朋友，常有人来求他的忠告与周济；他也不时主动寻访落魄的贤才，把它从埋没中扶起，而把自己的开支压缩在极窄的限度之内。

“你是个大好人，”一天，一位年轻军官对他说，“我很想给你出一个好题目，好让你那副好心肠有用武之地。”

===Original===

“You can not oblige me more,” said Temple, “than to point out any way by which I can be serviceable to my fellow creatures.”

“Come along then,” said the young man, “we will go and visit a man who is not in so good a lodging as he deserves; and were it not that he has an angel with him, who comforts and supports him, he must long since have sunk under his misfortunes.” The young man’s heart was too full to proceed; and Temple, unwilling to irritate his feelings by making further inquiries, followed him in silence, till they arrived at the Fleet Prison.2

The officer inquired for Captain Eldridge: a person led them up several pair of dirty stairs, and pointing to a door which led to a miserable, small apartment, said that was the captain’s room, and retired.

The officer, whose name was Blakeney, tapped at the door, and was bid to enter by a voice melodiously soft. He opened the door and discovered to Temple a scene which riveted him to the spot with astonishment.

===Chinese===

“你再没有什么别的法子更能让我高兴了，”坦普尔说，“只要你能指出一条途径，让我能对自己的同胞们有所助益。”

“那就来吧，”年轻人说，“我们去探望一个人，他住的屋子远配不上他的身份；若不是有位天使守在他身边安慰他、扶持他，他早就在不幸的重压下沉沦了。”年轻人心头堵塞，说不下去；坦普尔不愿再追问而刺痛他的感情，便默默跟着他走，直到两人来到弗利特监狱（Fleet Prison）[2]。

军官打听埃尔德里奇船长（Captain Eldridge）；有人领他们上了几段肮脏的楼梯，指着一扇通向一间寒碜小屋的房门说，那就是船长的房间，说完便退下了。

这位军官名叫布莱克尼（Blakeney），他敲了敲门，一个柔婉悦耳的声音请他进去。他推开门，让坦普尔看到了一幅景象，那景象使他惊愕得钉在原地。

===Original===

The apartment, though small and bearing strong marks of poverty, was neat in the extreme. In an armchair, his head reclined upon his hand, his eyes fixed on a book which lay open before him, sat an aged man in a lieutenant’s uniform, which, though threadbare, would sooner call a blush of shame into the face of those who could neglect real merit, than cause the hectic of confusion to glow on the cheeks of him who wore it.

Beside him sat a lovely creature, busied in painting a fan mount. She was fair as the lily; but sorrow had nipped the rose in her cheek before it was half blown. Her eyes were blue; and her hair, which was light brown, was slightly confined under a plain muslin cap, tied round with a black ribbon; a white linen gown and plain lawn handkerchief composed the remainder of her dress; and in this simple attire she was more irresistibly charming to such a heart as Temple’s than she would have been if adorned with all the splendor of a courtly belle.

When they entered, the old man arose from his seat, and shaking Blakeney by the hand with great cordiality, offered Temple his chair; and there being but three in the room, seated himself on the side of his little bed with evident composure.

“This is a strange place,” said he to Temple, “to receive visitors of distinction in, but we must fit our feelings to our station. While I am not ashamed to own the cause which brought me here, why should I blush at my situation? Our misfortunes are not our faults; and were it not for that poor girl﻿—”

===Chinese===

这间屋子虽然狭小，处处带着贫穷的印记，却整洁到了极点。一把扶手椅上坐着一位老人，头侧倚在手上，双眼凝视着面前摊开的一本书；他身上穿着一套中尉制服，那制服虽已磨得露了线，却只会叫那些怠慢真正功勋的人羞得脸红，而绝不会令穿着它的人双颊发烧、局促不安。

他身旁坐着一位可爱的姑娘，正忙着画一柄扇面。她白皙得如同百合；然而忧伤早在这朵蓓蕾半开之前，就掐残了她颊上的玫瑰。她的眼睛是蓝色的；淡棕色的头发松松地拢在一顶素色细布软帽里，帽上系着一圈黑缎带；一件白色亚麻长裙、一方素麻手帕，便是她余下的全部装束；这一身朴素的衣装，比起满身宫廷美人的华彩，更叫坦普尔这样的心为之倾倒，难以自持。

两人进屋时，老人从座位上站起身，热忱地握住布莱克尼的手，又把自己的椅子让给坦普尔；屋里统共只有三把椅子，他便安然自在地在自己小床的床沿坐了下来。

“这地方实在古怪，”他对坦普尔说，“本不该在这里接待贵客，可我们总得让心境迁就境遇。既然我毫不羞于承认是什么缘由把我弄到了这里，我又何必为自己的处境脸红？我们的不幸并非我们的过错；若不是为了那可怜的姑娘——”

===Original===

Here the philosopher was lost in the father. He rose hastily from his seat, and walking toward the window, wiped off a tear which he was afraid would tarnish the cheek of a sailor.

Temple cast his eye on Miss Eldridge; a pellucid drop had stolen from her eyes and fallen upon a rose she was painting. It blotted and discolored the flower. “ ’Tis emblematic,” said he mentally; “the rose of youth and health soon fades when watered by the tear of affliction.”

“My friend Blakeney,” said he, addressing the old man, “told me I could be of service to you: be so kind, then, dear sir, as to point out some way in which I can relieve the anxiety of your heart and increase the pleasures of my own.”

“My good young man,” said Eldridge, “you know not what you offer. While deprived of my liberty, I can not be free from anxiety on my own account; but that is a trifling concern; my anxious thoughts extend to one more dear a thousand times than life: I am a poor, weak old man, and must expect in a few years to sink into silence and oblivion, but when I am gone who will protect that fair bud of innocence from the blasts of adversity, or from the cruel hand of insult and dishonor.”

===Chinese===

说到这里，哲人隐没了，剩下的只是父亲。他霍地从座位上站起，走向窗边，拭去一滴眼泪——他生怕这滴泪会玷污水手的面颊。

坦普尔把目光投向埃尔德里奇小姐（Miss Eldridge）；一滴晶莹的泪珠不知何时从她眼中溜出，落在她正画着的一朵玫瑰上，把那朵花浸得斑驳失色。“这是命运的象征啊，”他心中暗想，“青春与健康的玫瑰，一经苦难的泪水浇灌，便很快凋萎。”

“我的朋友布莱克尼，”他对老人说，“告诉我说我能为你们效劳：那么，亲爱的先生，请好意指点一个法子，让我既能解除你心头的焦虑，又能增添我自己的快乐。”

“好心的年轻人，”埃尔德里奇说，“你不知道你许下的是什么。只要我还被剥夺着自由，我自己的事就不能不叫我悬心；可那不过是小事一桩；我焦虑的心思都系在一个比生命还要珍贵千倍的人身上：我是个衰弱无用的老人，不出几年就该归于沉寂、被人遗忘；可我死之后，谁来保护那朵纯洁的蓓蕾，使她免遭逆境的摧残，免受凌辱与污名的毒手呢？”

===Original===

“Oh, my father!” cried Miss Eldridge, tenderly taking his hand, “be not anxious on that account, for daily are my prayers offered to Heaven that our lives may terminate at the same instant, and one grave receive us both; for why should I live when deprived of my only friend?”

Temple was moved even to tears. “You will both live many years!” said he, “and, I hope, see much happiness. Cheerily, my friend, cheerily; these passing clouds of adversity will serve only to make the sunshine of prosperity more pleasing. But we are losing time: you might, ere this, have told me who were your creditors, what were their demands, and other particulars necessary to your liberation.”

“My story is short,” said Mr. Eldridge, “but there are some particulars which will wring my heart barely to remember; yet to one whose offers of friendship appear so open and disinterested, I will relate every circumstance that led to my present painful situation. But, my child,” continued he, addressing his daughter, “let me prevail on you to take this opportunity, while my friends are with me, to enjoy the benefit of air and exercise. Go, my love; leave me now; tomorrow, at your usual hour, I will expect you.”

Miss Eldridge impressed on his cheek the kiss of filial affection, and obeyed.


===Chinese===

“啊，我的父亲！”埃尔德里奇小姐喊道，温柔地握住他的手，“这件事请您不必忧心，因为我天天向天祈祷，愿我们的生命在同一时刻终结，让一座坟墓把我们一同收殓；因为失去惟一的朋友之后，我还活着做什么呢？”

坦普尔感动得几乎落泪。“你们两位都会活上许多年！”他说，“而且我希望，会看到许多幸福的日子。高兴起来，我的朋友，高兴起来；这些掠过的逆境乌云，只会叫日后的顺境阳光更加可喜。可我们是在浪费时间了：你本该早些告诉我，你的债主们是些什么人，他们的索款有多少，还有其他一些为解救你所必需的细节。”

“我的经历说来话短，”埃尔德里奇先生说，“可有些细节，光是回想一下就足以绞碎我的心；不过，对一位友谊之手伸得如此坦荡、如此无所求的人，我愿意把导致我今日惨境的每一个情节都和盘托出。可是，孩子，”他转向女儿，继续说道，“让我劝你趁我的朋友们在这儿，出去享受一下空气和活动的好处。去吧，亲爱的；现在就离开我；明天，在你往常来的时辰，我等着你。”

埃尔德里奇小姐在他颊上印了一个女儿孝爱的吻，依言去了。
