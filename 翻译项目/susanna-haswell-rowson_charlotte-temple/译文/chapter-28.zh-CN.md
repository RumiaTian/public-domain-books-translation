## XXVIII A Trifling Retrospect14 / XXVIII 一段闲笔回顾[14]

===Original===

“Bless my heart!” cries my young, volatile reader, “I shall never have patience to get through these volumes, there are so many ahs! and ohs! so much fainting, tears and distress, I am sick to death of the subject.” My dear, cheerful, innocent girl, for innocent I will suppose you to be, or you would acutely feel the woes of Charlotte, did conscience say, thus might it have been with me, had not Providence interposed to snatch me from destruction: therefore, my lively, innocent girl, I must request your patience; I am writing a tale of truth: I mean to write it to the heart: but, if perchance the heart is rendered impenetrable by unbounded prosperity, or a continuance in vice, I expect not my tale to please, nay, I even expect it will be thrown by with disgust. But softly, gentle fair one; I pray you throw it not aside till you have perused the whole; mayhap you may find something therein to repay you for the trouble. Methinks I see a sarcastic smile sit on your countenance﻿—“And what,” cry you, “does the conceited author suppose we can glean from these pages, if Charlotte is held up as an object of terror, to prevent us from falling into guilty errors? Does not La Rue triumph in her shame, and, by adding art to guilt, obtain the affection of a worthy man and rise to a station where she is beheld with respect, and cheerfully received into all companies. What, then, is the moral you would inculcate? Would you wish us to think that a deviation from virtue, if covered by art and hypocrisy, is not an object of detestation, but on the contrary, shall raise us to fame and honor? while the hapless girl who falls a victim to her too great sensibility, shall be loaded with ignominy and shame?” No, my fair querist, I mean no such thing. Remember the endeavors of the wicked are often suffered to prosper, that in the end their fall may be attended with more bitterness of heart, while the cup of affliction is poured out for wise and salutary ends, and they who are compelled to drain it even to the bitter dregs, often find comfort at the bottom; the tear of penitence blots their offences from the book of fate, and they rise from the heavy, painful trial, purified and fit for a mansion in the kingdom of eternity.

Yes, my young friends, the tear of compassion shall fall for the fate of Charlotte, while the name of La Rue shall be detested and despised. For Charlotte the soul melts with sympathy; for La Rue it feels nothing but horror and contempt. But perhaps your gay hearts would rather follow the fortunate Mrs. Crayton through the scenes of pleasure and dissipation in which she was engaged than listen to the complaints and miseries of Charlotte. I will for once oblige you; I will for once follow her to midnight revels, balls and scenes of gayety, for in such was she constantly engaged.

I have said her person was lovely; let us add that she was surrounded by splendor and affluence, and he must know but little of the world who can wonder (however faulty such a woman’s conduct) at her being followed by the men and her company courted by the women: in short, Mrs. Crayton was the universal favorite; she set the fashions; she was toasted by all the gentlemen, and copied by all the ladies.

Colonel Crayton was a domestic man. Could he be happy with such a woman? impossible! Remonstrance was vain: he might as well have preached to the winds as endeavor to persuade her from any action, however ridiculous, on which she had set her mind: in short, after a little ineffectual struggle, he gave up the attempt and left her to follow the bent of her own inclinations: what those were, I think the reader must have seen enough of her character to form a just idea. Among the number who paid their devotions at her shrine, she singled one, a young ensign of mean birth, indifferent education, and weak intellects. How such a man came into the army we hardly know to account for; and how he afterward rose to posts of honor is likewise strange and wonderful. But fortune is blind, and so are those, too, frequently, who have the power of dispensing her favors: else why do we see fools and knaves at the very top of the wheel, while patient merit sinks to the extreme of the opposite abyss. But we may form a thousand conjectures on this subject, and yet never hit on the right. Let us, therefore, endeavor to deserve her smiles, and whether we succeed or not, we shall feel more innate satisfaction than thousands of those who bask in the sunshine of her favor unworthily. But to return to Mrs. Crayton: this young man, whom I shall distinguish by the name of Corydon, was the reigning favorite of her heart. He escorted her to the play, danced with her at every ball, and, when indisposition prevented her going out, it was he alone who was permitted to cheer the gloomy solitude to which she was obliged to confine herself. Did she ever think of poor Charlotte?﻿—if she did, my dear miss, it was only to laugh at the poor girl’s want of spirit in consenting to be moped up in the country, while Montraville was enjoying all the pleasures of a gay, dissipated city. When she heard of his marriage, she smiling said: “So there’s an end of Madame Charlotte’s hopes. I wonder who will take her now, or what will become of the little affected prude?”

But, as you have led to the subject, I think we may as well return to the distressed Charlotte, and not, like the unfeeling Mrs. Crayton, shut our hearts to the call of humanity.

“老天保佑！”我那位年轻、心浮的读者喊道，“这些个‘啊！’和‘哦！’这么多，又是晕厥、又是眼泪、又是苦难，我永远没耐心读完这几卷，这题目真叫我烦死了。”我亲爱的、快活的、天真的姑娘——我就当你天真吧，否则你若良心说一句“若不是上天伸手把我从毁灭里拽出来，夏洛特的苦难原也可能落在我身上”，你便会真切地体会她的不幸：所以，我活泼天真的姑娘，我必须请你拿出耐心来；我写的是一篇真实的故事：我要把它写进人心：可倘若那颗心已被无边的幸运或一贯的恶行封得透不进去了，我也不指望我的故事能讨喜欢；不，我甚至料到它会被人厌恶地丢开。可是慢着，温柔的美人；我求你读完全篇再丢不迟；说不定你会发现里面有东西，值得你这一番辛苦。我想我看见你脸上挂起讥诮的笑——“那么，”你喊道，“这位自负的作者以为，从这几页书里能拾掇出什么来？倘若夏洛特被举出来当吓人的例子，好叫我们不犯有罪的过错，那又怎么样？拉吕不是靠她的耻辱得意么？她给罪恶加上手段，赢得了一个可贵男人的爱，爬到了一个受人敬重、处处受欢迎的位置。那么，你要教给我们的道德是什么？你要我们相信，偏离了美德，只要蒙上手腕和伪善，就不可憎，反倒会抬高我们的名声和荣誉？而那个做了过分敏感的牺牲品的不幸姑娘，倒要背负耻辱和羞垢？”不，我美丽的质问者，我不是这个意思。要记住：恶人的营谋常被容许得逞，好叫他们终局的跌落更苦涩；而苦杯的倾出自有智慧而有益的用意，那些被迫把它饮到苦渣的人，常在杯底找到安慰；悔悟之泪把他们的过犯从命运之书上抹去，他们从沉重痛苦的试炼中站起，得了洁净，配得上永恒之国中的一座宅邸。

是的，我的年轻朋友们，同情之泪将为夏洛特的命运落下，而拉吕的名字将受人憎恶和鄙弃。为夏洛特，灵魂因同情而融化；为拉吕，它只感到恐怖和轻蔑。可你们欢快的心，也许宁愿跟着得势的克雷顿夫人穿过她耽溺其中的行乐与喧嚣，也不愿听夏洛特的哀诉和苦难。我就依你们一回；我就随她走一回，走进午夜的狂欢、舞会和寻欢作乐的场面，因为她一向就泡在这些里面。

我说过她的容貌是可爱的；让我们再加上一句：她又身处豪富与显赫之中；若还有人奇怪（无论这女人的行为多么有亏）男人们追随着她、女人们争相与她结交，那他必定太不谙世事：一句话，克雷顿夫人是人人的宠儿；她开风气之先；绅士们举杯向她致意，女士们个个仿效她。

克雷顿上校是个恋家的人。同这样一个女人，他能幸福么？不可能！规劝是徒然：他与其去劝她放弃任何她打定主意的事——无论多么荒唐——还不如去对风说教：一句话，一番无效的挣扎之后，他放弃了尝试，随她照自己的性子去走：那些性子是什么，我想读者对她的为人已看得足够，自能形成一个公允的看法。在她神龛前献殷勤的人群里，她挑中了一个：一个出身寒微、教养平平、智力孱弱的年轻旗官。这样的人怎么进的军队，我们几乎无从解释；他后来又怎能升到荣耀的职位，也同样又怪又奇。可是命运是盲的，而那些有权分发她恩宠的人，也常常同样是盲的；否则，为什么我们眼见蠢人和恶棍高踞轮顶，而坚忍的功业沉到对面深渊的极底？但在这个题目上我们尽可以作一千种猜想，却永远猜不中那个对的。所以，让我们勉力配得上命运的微笑罢；成也罢不成也罢，我们心里的自在，也要胜过千万个不配而沐浴在她恩宠阳光里的人。可是回到克雷顿夫人：这个年轻人——我姑且用科里冬（Corydon）这个名字来称呼他——是她心中当令的宠儿。他陪她看戏，场场舞会与她共舞；她身子不适不能出门时，也只有他一人获准去为她排遣那不得不困守的阴郁的孤寂。她可曾想过可怜的夏洛特？——若是想过，我亲爱的小姐，那也不过是笑话这可怜姑娘没骨气，甘愿闷在乡下，而蒙特维尔正在浮华喧嚣的都会里享尽欢乐。她听到他结婚的消息，笑着说：“得，夏洛特夫人的指望到头了。我倒要看如今谁肯要她，那位装腔作势的小正经又会落个什么下场。”

不过，既然话头已经引到那里，我想我们还是回到苦难的夏洛特身边去吧，不要学那无情的克雷顿夫人，把心向人性的呼唤关死。
