## XXIV Mystery Developed / XXIV 谜底揭开

===Original===

Unfortunately for Charlotte, about three weeks before this unhappy rencontre, Captain Beauchamp, being ordered to Rhode Island, his lady had accompanied him, so that Charlotte was deprived of her friendly advice and consoling society. The afternoon on which Montraville had visited her she had found herself languid and fatigued, and after making a very slight dinner, had lain down to endeavor to recruit her exhausted spirits, and, contrary to her expectations, had fallen asleep. She had not long been laid down when Belcour arrived, for he took every opportunity of visiting her, and striving to awaken her resentment against Montraville. He enquired of the servant where her mistress was, and being told she was asleep, took up a book to amuse himself; having sat a few minutes, he by chance cast his eyes towards the road, and saw Montraville approaching; he instantly conceived the diabolical scheme of ruining the unhappy Charlotte in his opinion forever; he therefore stole softly upstairs, and laying himself by her side with the greatest precaution, for fear she should awake, was in that situation discovered by his credulous friend.

When Montraville spurned the weeping Charlotte from him, and left her almost distracted with terror and despair, Belcour raised her from the floor, and leading her downstairs, assumed the part of a tender, consoling friend; she listened to the arguments he advanced, with apparent composure; but this was only the calm of the moment: the remembrance of Montraville’s recent cruelty again rushed upon her mind: she pushed him from her with some violence, crying﻿—“Leave me, sir, I beseech you; leave me, for much I fear you have been the cause of my fidelity being suspected; go, leave me to the accumulated miseries my own imprudence has brought upon me.”

She then left him with precipitation, and retiring to her own apartments, threw herself on the bed, and gave vent to an agony of grief which it is impossible to describe.

It now occurred to Belcour that she might possibly write to Montraville, and endeavor to convince him of her innocence: he was well aware of her pathetic remonstrances, and sensible of the tenderness of Montraville’s heart, resolved to prevent any letters ever reaching him: he therefore called the servant, and by the powerful persuasion of a bribe, prevailed with her to promise whatever letters her mistress might write should be sent to him. He then left a polite, tender note for Charlotte, and returned to New York. His first business was to seek Montraville, and endeavor to convince him that what had happened would ultimately tend to his happiness; he found him in his apartment, solitary, pensive and wrapped in disagreeable reflections.

蒙特维尔把哭泣的夏洛特踢开、撇下她几乎被恐怖和绝望逼疯之后，贝尔库尔把她从地板上扶起来，领她下楼，扮起一个温柔的、劝慰的朋友；她听着他陈说的道理，表面上还算镇定；但这不过是一时的平静：蒙特维尔新近的残忍重新涌上心头：她用力把他推开，喊道——“离开我，先生，我求您；离开我，因为我深怕正是您害得我的忠贞蒙了嫌疑；去吧，把我丢给我自己的轻率招来的重重苦难吧。”

说罢她匆匆撇下他，退回自己的房间，扑倒在床上，任一阵无法描摹的悲恸尽情倾泻。

这时贝尔库尔想到，她或许会给蒙特维尔写信，竭力辩白自己的清白：他太清楚她那哀婉动人的辩才，也深知蒙特维尔心软，便决意堵死一切信件到他手里的路：于是他唤来女仆，凭着重贿的威力，说得她应允：女主人写的任何信，都交给他。随后他给夏洛特留下一封礼貌温存的短简，回纽约去了。他的头一件事就是去找蒙特维尔，设法让他相信，已经发生的事终究会成全他的幸福；他见他在寓所里，孤零零的，闷闷不乐，浸在不痛快的沉思里。

“Why, how now, whining, pining lover?” said he, clapping him on the shoulder. Montraville started; a momentary flush of resentment crossed his cheek, but instantly gave place to a deathlike paleness, occasioned by painful remembrance﻿—remembrance awakened by that monitor, whom, though we may in vain endeavor, we can never entirely silence.

“Belcour,” said he, “you have injured me in a tender point.”

“Prithee, Jack,”12 replied Belcour, “do not make a serious matter of it: how could I refuse the girl’s advances? and thank Heaven she is not your wife.”

“True,” said Montraville; “but she was innocent when I first knew her. It was I seduced her, Belcour. Had it not been for me, she had still been virtuous and happy in the affection and protection of her family.”

“嗳，怎么了，哭哭啼啼、唉声叹气的情人？”他拍着他的肩膀说。蒙特维尔一惊；一刹那的愤懑之红掠过他的面颊，随即化作死一般的苍白，那是痛苦的回忆唤来的——回忆被那位内心的监察唤醒，我们纵然竭力想堵住它的口，却永远不能叫它彻底沉默。

“贝尔库尔，”他说，“你在一件要害处伤了我。”

“这话不假，”蒙特维尔说；“可我初识她时，她是清白的。是我诱骗了她，贝尔库尔。若不是我，她此刻还会贞洁而幸福地活在亲人的爱护之中。”

“Pshaw,” replied Belcour, laughing, “if you had not taken advantage of her easy nature, some other would, and where is the difference, pray?”

“I wish I had never seen her,” cried he, passionately, and starting from his seat. “Oh, that cursed French woman!” added he with vehemence, “had it not been for her I might have been happy﻿—” He paused.

“With Julia Franklin,” said Belcour. The name, like a sudden spark of electric fire, seemed for a moment to suspend his faculties﻿—for a moment he was transfixed; but recovering, he caught Belcour’s hand, and cried﻿—“Stop! stop! I beseech you, name not the lovely Julia and the wretched Montraville in the same breath. I am a seducer, a mean, ungenerous seducer of unsuspecting innocence. I dare not hope that purity like hers would stoop to unite itself with black, premeditated guilt: yet, by heavens! I swear, Belcour, I thought I loved the lost abandoned Charlotte till I saw Julia﻿—I thought I never could forsake her; but the heart is deceitful, and I now can plainly discriminate between the impulse of a youthful passion, and the pure flame of disinterested affection.”

At that instant Julia Franklin passed the window, leaning on her uncle’s arm. She courtesied as she passed, and with the bewitching smile of modest cheerfulness, cried﻿—“Do you bury yourselves in the house this fine evening, gents?” There was something in the voice! the manner! the look! that was altogether irresistible. “Perhaps she wishes my company,” said Montraville, mentally, as he snatched up his hat: “if I thought she loved me, I would confess my errors, and trust to her generosity to pity and pardon me.” He soon overtook her, and offering her his arm, they sauntered to pleasant, but unfrequented walks. Belcour drew Mr. Franklin on one side, and entered into a political discourse; they walked faster than the young people, and Belcour, by some means, contrived entirely to lose sight of them. It was a fine evening in the beginning of autumn; the last remains of daylight faintly streaked the western sky, while the moon with pale and virgin luster in the room of gorgeous gold and purple, ornamented the canopy of heaven with silver, fleecy clouds, which now and then half hid her lovely face, and, by partly concealing, heightened every beauty; the zephyrs whispered softly through the trees, which now began to shed their leafy honors; a solemn silence reigned: and, to a happy mind, an evening such as this would give serenity, and calm, unruffled pleasure: but to Montraville, while it soothed the turbulence of his passions, it brought increase of melancholy reflections. Julia was leaning on his arm: he took her hand in his, and pressing it tenderly, sighed deeply, but continued silent. Julia was embarrassed: she wished to break a silence so unaccountable, but was unable; she loved Montraville; she saw he was unhappy, and wished to know the cause of his uneasiness, but that innate modesty which nature has implanted in the female breast, prevented her inquiring. “I am bad company, Miss Franklin,” said he, at last recollecting himself; “but I have met with something today that has greatly distressed me, and I can not shake off the disagreeable impression it has made on my mind.”

“但愿我从没见过她。”他狂乱地喊道，从座位上跳起来。“哦，那个可咒的法国女人！”他又激烈地说，“若不是她，我本可以幸福的——”他顿住了。

“同朱莉娅·富兰克林一起幸福。”贝尔库尔说。这名字像电光的一星火花，似乎一时缚住了他的官能——他有一瞬僵在那里；随即缓过来，一把抓住贝尔库尔的手，喊道——“打住！打住！我求你，别把可爱的朱莉娅和不幸的蒙特维尔放进同一口气里。我是个诱骗者，一个卑鄙、无耻的诱骗者，专骗不疑人的天真。我不敢指望她那样的纯洁会俯就一身蓄意的黑罪：可是，上天作证！我发誓，贝尔库尔，我一直以为我爱那个失落堕落的夏洛特，直到见了朱莉娅——我以为我永远不会抛弃她；可是心是欺人的，如今我能清清楚楚分辨出少年激情的冲动，和无私之爱的纯净火焰。”

就在此时，朱莉娅·富兰克林挽着她叔父的手臂从窗前走过。经过时她屈膝一礼，带着谦逊快活的迷人笑容喊道——“这么好的傍晚，两位先生要在屋里把自己埋起来么？”那声音！那风度！那眼神！处处叫人无从抗拒。“也许她愿意我作伴，”蒙特维尔心里想，一把抓起帽子：“倘若我能相信她爱我，我就向她认我的过错，信赖她的宽厚来怜悯我、饶恕我。”他很快追上她，递上手臂，两人信步走向几条宜人而人迹罕至的小径。贝尔库尔把富兰克林先生引到一边，谈起政事来；他们走得比年轻人快，贝尔库尔又设法让他们完全走出了视线。那是初秋一个晴美的傍晚；白昼最后的余光淡淡地划亮西天，月亮以素贞的清辉代替了华丽的金紫，用银色絮云装点天幕，云絮时而半掩她可爱的脸，半遮半露之间，更添了每一分美；微风柔柔地穿过开始落叶的树林；一片肃穆的静谧统治着一切：对一颗幸福的心，这样一个傍晚会给它安谧、平静、无波的欢愉：可对蒙特维尔，它在抚平他激情的躁动的同时，却添了忧郁的沉思。朱莉娅挽着他的手臂：他执起她的手，温柔地握着，深深叹息，却始终沉默。朱莉娅窘住了：她想打破这莫名的沉默，却不能够；她爱蒙特维尔；她看出他不快活，想知道他不安的缘由，可是自然植于女性胸怀的那份天生的羞怯，使她不能发问。“我这人真不宜作伴，富兰克林小姐，”他终于回过神来说；“可我今天遇到了一件叫我十分痛心的事，摆脱不开它留在我心上的不快印象。”

Just then they were again joined by Mr. Franklin and Belcour. It had interrupted an interesting discourse. They found it impossible to converse on indifferent subjects, and proceeded home in silence. At Mr. Franklin’s door, Montraville again pressed Julia’s hand, and, faintly articulating “good night,” retired to his lodgings, dispirited and wretched, from a consciousness that he deserved not the affection with which he plainly saw he was honored.
