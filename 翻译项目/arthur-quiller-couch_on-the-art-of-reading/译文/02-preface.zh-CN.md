## Preface / 序言

===Original===
The following twelve lectures have this much in common with a previous twelve published in 1916 under the title *On the Art of Writing*—they form no compact treatise but present their central idea as I was compelled at the time to enforce it, amid the dust of skirmishing with opponents and with practical difficulties.

They cover—and to some extent, by reflection, chronicle—a period during which a few friends, who had an idea and believed in it, were fighting to establish the present English Tripos at Cambridge. In the end we carried our proposals without a vote: but the opposition was stiff for a while; and I feared, on starting to read over these pages for press, that they might be too occasional and disputatious. I am happy to think that, on the whole, they are not; and that the reader, though he may wonder at its discursiveness, will find the argument pretty free from polemic. Anyone who has inherited a library of 17th century theology will agree with me that, of all dust, the ashes of dead controversies afford the driest.

===Chinese===
以下十二篇讲演，与 1916 年以《论写作的艺术》（*On the Art of Writing*）为题刊行的先前十二篇有以下一点相通：它们都不构成一部紧凑严整的专著，只是把其中的中心思想呈现出来，呈现为我当年在与论敌交锋、与实际困难缠斗的尘嚣之中不得不申说力争的那个模样。

这些讲演所涵盖的——在某种程度上也凭其映照而记录了——是一段时期：其间，少数怀有一种理念并笃信不移的朋友，正为在剑桥建立今日的英文荣誉学位考试（English Tripos）而奋战。最终，我们的方案未经表决便告通过；但反对的声势一度相当顽强；因此，当我着手为付印而通读这些篇章时，曾担心它们未免过于因应时势、论辩之气太重。令我欣慰的是，通观全篇，大体并非如此；读者纵或讶异于其行文的旁骛散漫，当会发现其中的论证几乎不带论战的火气。凡是继承过一批十七世纪神学藏书的人，都会同意我的看法：世间种种尘埃之中，唯有作古论战的灰烬最为枯燥。

===Original===
And after all, and though it be well worth while to strive that the study of English (of our own literature, and of the art of using our own language, in speech or in writing, to the best purpose) shall take an honourable place among the schools of a great university, that the other fair sisters of learning shall

> Ope for thee their queenly circle …,

it is not in our universities that the general redemption of English will be won; nor need a mistake here or there, at Oxford or Cambridge or London, prove fatal. We make our discoveries through our mistakes: we watch one another's success: and where there is freedom to experiment there is hope to improve. A youth who can command means to enter a university can usually command some range in choosing which university it shall be. If Cambridge cannot supply what he wants, or if our standard of training be low in comparison with that of Oxford, or of London or of Manchester, the pressure of neglect will soon recall us to our senses.

===Chinese===
然而说到底，尽管我们值得奋力争取，让英语研究（亦即研究我们自身的文学，研究把母语用于言谈或书写以求最大效用的艺术）在一所伟大大学的诸学院中赢得一个令人尊崇的位置，让学习的其他几位美丽姊妹

> 为你开启她们那女王般雍容的环舞……

但英语的普遍救赎，终究不会在我们的大学里赢得；纵使牛津、剑桥或伦敦这里或那里偶有失误，也无须视作致命。我们借错误而有所发现；我们彼此注视着对方的成功；凡有试验的自由，便有改进的希望。一个青年若有足够的资财进入大学，通常也就有相当的余地来选择进哪一所大学。倘若剑桥拿不出他所需要的东西，或者我们的训练水准与牛津、伦敦或曼彻斯特相形见绌，遭人冷落的压力自会很快令我们幡然醒悟。

===Original===
*The real battle for English lies in our elementary schools, and in the training of our elementary teachers.* It is there that the foundations of a sound national teaching in English will have to be laid, as it is there that a wrong trend will lead to incurable issues. For the poor child has no choice of schools, and the elementary teacher, whatever his individual gifts, will work under a yoke imposed upon him by Whitehall. I devoutly trust that Whitehall will make the yoke easy and adaptable while insisting that the chariot must be drawn.

I foresee, then, these lectures condemned as the utterances of a man who, occupying a Chair, has contrived to fall betwixt two stools. My thoughts have too often strayed from my audience in a university theatre away to remote rural classrooms where the hungry sheep look up and are not fed; to piteous groups of urchins standing at attention and chanting "The Wreck of the Hesperus" in unison. Yet to these, being tied to the place and the occasion, I have brought no real help.

===Chinese===
*英语的真正战场，在于我们的初等学校，在于初等师资的培养。*健全的国民英语教育必须在那里奠定根基，因为一旦趋势走偏，酿成的祸患将无可救药。因为贫穷的孩子无从挑选学校，而初等教师无论个人天资如何，都得在白厅（Whitehall）强加于他的一副轭下劳作。我诚挚地期望，白厅能在坚持「战车总得有人来拉」的同时，把这副轭打造得轻省而易于调适。

于是我可以预见，这些讲演将被人指斥为一个身居讲席者的言论——此人竟设法让自己落得两头落空。我的思绪太常常从大学讲堂内的听众身上飘走，飞向遥远的乡村教室——那里，饥饿的羊群昂首企望而无人喂饲；飞向一群群可怜的学童，他们立正肃立，齐声诵读着《赫斯珀洛斯号的失事》（The Wreck of the Hesperus）。然而对于这些，我既为地点与场合所拘束，实在不曾带来什么真正的助益。

===Original===
A man has to perform his task as it comes. But I must say this in conclusion. Could I wipe these lectures out and rewrite them in hope to benefit my countrymen in general, I should begin and end upon the text to be found in the twelfth and last—that a liberal education is not an appendage to be purchased by a few: that Humanism is, rather, a *quality* which can, and should, condition all our teaching; which can, and should, be impressed as a character upon it all, from a poor child's first lesson in reading up to a tutor's last word to his pupil on the eve of a Tripos.

Arthur Quiller-Couch

July 7, 1920

===Chinese===
人总得完成落到自己头上的任务。但在结束之际，我必须说这样一句话。倘若我能把这些讲演尽数抹去、重写一遍，以期裨益于我的国人同胞，我会自始至终扣住第十二讲亦即末一讲中的那段题旨立论——博雅教育（liberal education）并非少数人才购置得起的一件附属物；毋宁说，人文主义（Humanism）乃是一种性质，它能够且应当统摄我们的全部教学，能够且应当作为一种品格铭刻于教学的全部——从一个穷孩子的第一堂阅读课，直到导师在荣誉学位考试（Tripos）前夜对学生的临别一言。

亚瑟·奎勒-库奇（Arthur Quiller-Couch）

1920 年 7 月 7 日
