1918 年 5 月 6 日，星期一

I

===Original===
My task today, Gentlemen, is mainly practical: to choose a particular book of Scripture and show (if I can) not only that it deserves to be enjoyed, in its English rendering, as a literary masterpiece, because it abides in that dress, an indisputable classic for us, as surely as if it had first been composed in English; but that it can, for purposes of study, serve the purpose of any true literary school of English as readily, and as usefully, as the Prologue to *The Canterbury Tales* or *Hamlet* or *Paradise Lost*. I shall choose The Book of Job for several reasons, presently to be given; but beg you to understand that, while taking it for a striking illustration, I use it but to illustrate; that what may be done with Job may, in degree, be done with Ruth, with Esther, with the Psalms, The Song of Songs, Ecclesiastes; with Isaiah of Jerusalem, Ezekiel, sundry of the prophets; even with St. Luke’s Gospel or St. Paul’s letters to the Churches.

===Chinese===
诸位先生，我今天的工作主要是实际的：选定圣经中的某一卷书，来表明（倘若我能的话）它不仅配得上以其英文的形态当作文学杰作来欣赏——因为它既久居于那一衣装之中，对我们便是一位无可争议的经典，就像它当初就是用英文写成的一样确凿；而且表明，就研习而言，它能够像《坎特伯雷故事》（The Canterbury Tales）总引、《哈姆雷特》（Hamlet）或《失乐园》（Paradise Lost）一样便捷、一样有用地为任何一所真正的英文学院（School of English）效力。我将选《约伯记》，理由有数端，随即奉告；但请诸位明白：我拿它作一个醒目的例证，却只是拿它作例证；凡能施之于《约伯记》的，在相当的程度上也能施之于《路得记》、《以斯帖记》、《诗篇》、《雅歌》、《传道书》；施之于耶路撒冷的以赛亚、《以西结书》与先知中的若干卷；甚至施之于《路加福音》，或保罗致各教会的书信。

===Original===
My first reason, then, for choosing Job has already been given. It is the most striking illustration to be found. Many of the Psalms touch perfection as lyrical strains: of the ecstasy of passion in love I suppose The Song of Songs to express the very last word. There are chapters of Isaiah that snatch the very soul and ravish it aloft. In no literature known to me are short stories told with such sweet austerity of art as in the Gospel parables—I can even imagine a high and learned artist in words, after rejecting them as divine on many grounds, surrendering in the end to their divine artistry. But for high seriousness combined with architectonic treatment on a great scale; for sublimity of conception, working malleably within a structure which is simple, severe, complete, having a beginning, a middle and an end; for diction never less than adequate, constantly right and therefore not seldom superb, as theme, thought and utterance soar up together and make one miracle, I can name no single book of the Bible to compare with Job.

===Chinese===
那么，我选《约伯记》的第一个理由已经给出了：它是所能找到的最醒目的例证。《诗篇》中有许多首作为抒情曲调几近完美；论爱情中激情的狂喜，我想《雅歌》说尽了最后的言语。《以赛亚书》中有若干章能把灵魂一把攫住，携之高飞。就我所知，没有任何文学讲述短篇故事时，能像福音书的比喻那样，带着如此甜美而克制的艺术——我甚至可以设想一位高超而有学问的驭字大师，虽已从许多方面否认它们出自神意，终究拜服于它们那神圣的艺术手腕。但论到高贵的严肃与大格局的建筑式经营相结合；论到意念的崇高，在一个单纯、严整、完整——有开端、有中段、有结尾——的结构之内婉转地运化；论到措辞从不失于不足、恒常精确、因而时常臻于壮美，当主题、思想与言语一同翱翔而上、合成一个奇迹——我能指名与之比肩的圣经书卷，《约伯记》而外别无他卷。

===Original===
My second reason is that the poem, being brief, compendious and quite simple in structure, can be handily expounded; Job is what Milton precisely called it, “a brief model.” And my third reason (which I must not hide) is that two writers whom I mentioned in my last lecture Lord Latymer and Professor R. G. Moulton—have already done this for me. A man who drives at practice must use the tools other men have made, so he use them with due acknowledgment; and this acknowledgment I pay by referring you to Book II of Lord Latymer’s *The Poet’s Charter*, and to the analysis of Job with which Professor Moulton introduces his *Literary Study of the Bible*.

===Chinese===
我的第二个理由是：这首诗篇幅简短、内容扼要、结构极为单纯，可以便当地加以阐说；《约伯记》正是弥尔顿（Milton）所称的「一个简短的范型」（a brief model）。我的第三个理由（这可不能隐瞒）是：我在上一讲中提到过的两位作者——拉蒂默勋爵（Lord Latymer）与莫尔顿（R. G. II

But I have a fourth reason, out of which I might make an apparent fifth by presenting it to you in two different ways. Those elders of you who have followed certain earlier lectures “On the Art of Writing” may remember that they set very little store upon metre as a dividing line between poetry and prose, and no store at all upon rhyme. I am tempted today to go farther, and to maintain that, the larger, the sublimer, your subject is, the more impertinent rhyme becomes to it: and that this impertinence increases in a sort of geometrical progression as you advance from monosyllabic to dissyllabic and on to trisyllabic rhyme. Let me put this by a series of examples.

===Chinese===
II

但我还有第四个理由；若换一种方式向你们呈报，我还可以从中做出一个表面上的第五个理由来。你们当中年资较深的几位，如果一直追随我先前题为「论写作的艺术」（On the Art of Writing）的若干讲演，也许还记得，那些讲演很少把音律（metre）当作诗与散文的分界线看待，对韵脚更是全不理会。今天我忍不住要走得更远，主张说：你们的题目越大、越崇高，韵脚就越发与它不相干；并且这不相干的程度，随着你从单音节韵进到双音节韵、再到三音节韵，按一种几何级数递增。让我用一连串例子来说明。

Now of rhyme he were a fool who, with Lycidas, or Gray’s “Elegy,” or certain choruses of *Prometheus Unbound*, or page after page of Victor Hugo in his mind, should assert it to be in itself inimical, or a hindrance, or even less than a help, to sublimity; or who, with Dante in his mind, should assert it to be, in itself, any bar to continuous and sustained sublimity. But languages differ vastly in their wealth of rhyme, and differ out of any proportion to their wealth in words: English for instance being infinitely richer than Italian in vocabulary, yet almost ridiculously poorer in dissyllabic, or feminine rhymes. Speaking generally, I should say that in proportion to its wonderful vocabulary, English is poor even in single rhymes; that the words “love,” “truth,” “God,” for example, have lists of possible congeners so limited that the mind, hearing the word “love,” runs forward to match it with “dove” or “above” or even with “move”: and this gives it a sense of arrest, of listening, of check, of waiting, which alike impedes the flow of Pope in imitating Homer, and of Spenser in essaying a sublime and continuous story of his own. It does well enough to carry Chaucer over any gap with a “forsooth as I you say” or “forsooth as I you tell”: but it does so at a total cost of the sublime. And this (I think) was really at the back of Milton’s mind when in the preface to *Paradise Lost* he championed blank verse against “the jingling sound of like endings.”

Let me instance a poem which, planned for sublimity, keeps tumbling flat upon earth through the inherent fault of the machine—I mean Myers’s “St. Paul”—a poem which, finely conceived, pondered, worked and reworked upon in edition after edition, was from the first condemned (to my mind) by the technical bar of dissyllabic rhyme which the poet unhappily chose. 现在是另一个问题（你们记得，几年前曾争得热闹）：「有没有——能不能有——一种静态剧场、静态戏剧（Static Drama）这样的东西？」

===Original===
Most of you (I daresay) remember M. Maeterlinck’s definition of this and his demand for it. To summarise him roughly, he contends that the old drama—the traditional, the conventional drama—lives by action; that, in Aristotle’s phrase, it represents men doing, πράττοντας, and resolves itself into a struggle of human wills—whether against the gods, as in ancient tragedy, or against one another, as in modern. M. Maeterlinck tells us—

===Chinese===
你们大多数（我敢说）还记得梅特林克（Maeterlinck）先生对它的界定和他对它的吁求。粗略概括他的意思：他主张旧戏——传统的、因袭的戏剧——靠行动而活；用亚里士多德（Aristotle）的话说，它摹写行事中的人（πράττοντας），归结为人类意志的斗争——或是与神明斗，如古代悲剧；或是人与人相斗，如近代戏剧。梅特林克先生告诉我们——

===Original===
> There is a tragic element in the life of every day that is far more real, far more penetrating, far more akin to the true self that is in us, than is the tragedy that lies in great adventure. … It goes beyond the determined struggle of man against man, and desire against desire; it goes beyond the eternal conflict of duty and passion. Its province is rather to reveal to us how truly wonderful is the mere act of living, and to throw light upon the existence of the soul, self-contained in the midst of ever-restless immensities; to hush the discourse of reason and sentiment, so that above the tumult may be heard the solemn uninterrupted whisperings of man and his destiny.
> To the tragic author [he goes on, later], as to the mediocre painter who still lingers over historical pictures, it is only the violence of the anecdote that appeals, and in his representation thereof does the entire interest of his work consist. … Indeed when I go to a theatre, I feel as though I were spending a few hours with my ancestors, who conceived life as though it were something that was primitive, arid and brutal. … I am shown a deceived husband killing his wife, a woman poisoning her lover, a son avenging his father, a father slaughtering his children, murdered kings, ravished virgins, imprisoned citizens—in a word all the sublimity of tradition, but alas how superficial and material! Blood, surface-tears and death! What can I learn from creatures who have but one fixed idea, who have no time to live, for that there is a rival, a mistress, whom it behoves them to put to death?

===Chinese===
> 日常生活里自有一种悲剧因素，比起大惊险故事里包藏的悲剧来，它更真实，更深切，更贴近我们里面的那个真我。……它超出人对人、欲望对欲望的既定斗争；它超出责任与激情那永恒的冲突。它的职分，毋宁是向我们启示：单单「活着」这一桩行为，就何其真实地奇妙；是照亮灵魂的存在——那在永不止息的浩渺之中自足自处的魂；是让理性与情感的言谈静下来，好教喧嚣之上，可以听见人与他的命运之间那庄严而不曾间断的低语。
> 对于悲剧作者[他后来接着说]，一如对还在历史画前流连的平庸画家，打动他的只是轶事的暴力，而他的作品的全部趣味，也就全在摹写这轶事上。……真的，每逢走进剧院，我便觉得自己在同几位祖先消磨几个钟头——他们把人生设想成某种原始、干瘪而残忍的东西。……人们演给我看：被欺的丈夫杀死妻子，女人毒杀情人，儿子为父复仇，父亲屠戮儿女，被弑的君王，被劫的处女，被囚的公民——一句话，传统的一切崇高，可叹多么浮面、多么物质！血，浮面的泪，和死亡！从这些生灵身上，我能学到什么？他们只有一个固着的念头，无暇生活，只因有一个情敌、一个情妇，非置于死地不可？

===Original===
M. Maeterlinck does not (he says) know if the Static Drama of his craving be impossible. But may we not, out of the East—the slow, the stationary East—fetch an instance more convincing?

===Chinese===
他（据他自己说）不知道他所渴求的静态戏剧是否为不可能之物。他倾向于认为——举出《普罗米修斯》（Prometheus）、《奠酒人》（Choephori）等几部希腊悲剧为例——它已经存在。但我们何妨从东方——那迟缓的、凝定的东方——取来一个更有说服力的例证呢？

My purpose being merely to persuade you that this surpassing poem can be studied, and ought to be studied, as literature, I shall content myself with turning it (so to speak) once or twice in my hand and glancing one or two facets at you.

VI

戏剧本身，我不拟试作分析；这方面请你们参看我方才引过的那两部书。我的目的只在说服你们：这首超迈的诗可以当作文学来研读，而且应当当作文学来研读；为此我只求把它（姑且这么说）在手中转动一两回，向你们闪出一两个棱面来。

===Original===
To begin with, then, you will not have failed to notice, in the setting out of the drama, a curious resemblance between Job and the *Prometheus* of Aeschylus. The curtain in each play lifts on a figure solitary, tortured (for no reason that seems good to us) by a higher will which, we are told, is God’s. The chorus of Sea-nymphs in the opening of the Greek play bears no small resemblance in attitude of mind to Job’s three friends. When Job at length breaks the intolerable silence with

> Let the day perish wherein I was born,
> And the night which said, There is a man child conceived.

he uses just such an outburst as Prometheus: and, as he is answered by his friends, so the Nymphs at once exclaim to Prometheus

> Seest thou not that thou hast sinned?

===Chinese===
那么，先从这一点说起：你们想必没有漏看，这出戏的布局里，约伯与埃斯库罗斯（Aeschylus）的《普罗米修斯》（Prometheus）有着奇妙的相似。两出戏的幕布升起时，台上的都是个孤独的人物，受着一种更高的意志的折磨——据告诉我们，那意志是上帝的——而折磨的缘由，在我们看来并不成话。希腊戏开场时的海仙女歌队，在心灵姿态上同约伯的三个朋友相去不远。当约伯终于冲破那不堪忍受的静默，喊出

> 愿我生的那日灭没，
> 愿那夜说：怀了男胎。

他用的正是普罗米修斯那样的爆发：他的朋友怎样应他，仙女们也立刻向普罗米修斯惊呼：

> 你看不见你已犯了罪么？

===Original===
But at once, for anyone with a sense of comparative literature, is set up a comparison between the persistent West and the persistent East; between the fiery energising rebel and the patient victim. Of these two, both good, one will dare everything to release mankind from thrall; the other will submit, and justify himself—mankind too, if it may hap—by submission.

===Chinese===
但立刻，在稍具比较文学意识的人眼里，便立起了一重比较：执拗的西方对执拗的东方；火烈奋发的反叛者对坚忍的受难者。这二者都是善的：一个敢于拿出一切，去把人类从奴役中解救出来；另一个则顺服，并且以顺服证明自己有理——若命运允许，连人类也一并如此。

===Original===
At once this difference is seen to give a difference of form to the drama. Our poem is purely static. Some critics can detect little individuality in Job’s three friends, to distinguish them. For my part I find Eliphaz more of a personage than the other two; grander in the volume of his mind, securer in wisdom; as I find Zophar rather noticeably a mean-minded greybeard, and Bildad a man of the stand-no-nonsense kind. But, to tell the truth, I prefer not to search for individuality in these men: I prefer to see them as three figures with eyes of stone almost expressionless. For in truth they are the conventions, all through—the orthodox men—addressing Job, the reality; and their words come to this:

> Thou sufferest, therefore must have sinned.
> All suffering is, must be a judgment upon sin.
> Else God is not righteous.

===Chinese===
立刻可以看出，这一分别也给了戏剧形式上的分别。我们的诗是纯粹静态的。有的批评家在约伯的三个朋友身上辨不出多少个性来区分他们。在我看来，以利法比另两位更成个人物：心胸的气象更宏大，智慧上更笃定；而琐法颇为扎眼地是个心地褊狭的老朽，比勒达则是个容不得半句废话的人物。但说老实话，我并不想在这些人身上搜求什么个性：我宁愿把他们看作三尊石目无光的像，几乎毫无表情。因为说真的，他们从头到尾就是那些程式——正统的人们——向约伯，那位「真实」，说话；他们的话归结起来便是：

> 你受苦，所以必定犯了罪。
> 一切受苦都是，必定是对罪的审判。
> 否则，神就不是公义的。

===Original===
They are statuesque, as the drama is static. The speeches follow one another, rising and falling, in rise and fall magnificently and deliberately eloquent. Not a limb is seen to move, unless it be when Job half rises from the dust in sudden scorn of their conventions:

> No doubt but *ye* are the people,
> And wisdom shall die with you!

or again

> Will ye speak unrighteously for God,
> And talk deceitfully for him?
> Will *ye* respect *his* person?
> Will *ye* contend for God?

===Chinese===
他们如雕像，正如这出戏是静态的。一篇篇演说相继而来，起起伏伏，那起伏宏伟而从容，雄辩滔滔。看不见一肢一动，除非约伯从尘土里蓦地半支起身，蔑视他们的程式：

> 无怪乎*你们*是子民，
> 智慧要同*你们*一同灭亡！

或者又喊：

> 你们要为神说不义的话，
> 为他说诡诈的言语么？
> *你们*要看*他的*情面么？
> *你们*要为他争论么？

===Original===
Yet—so great is this man, who has not renounced and will not renounce God, that still and ever he clamours for more knowledge of Him. Still getting no answer, he lifts up his hands and calls the great Oath of Clearance; in effect, “If I have loved gold overmuch, hated mine enemy, refused the stranger my tent, truckled to public opinion”:

> If my land cry out against me,
> And the furrows thereof weep together;
> If I have eaten the fruits thereof without money,
> Or have caused the owners thereof to lose their life:
> Let thistles grow instead of wheat,
> And cockle instead of barley.

===Chinese===
然而——这个人何等伟大：他不曾弃掉上帝，也不肯弃掉上帝，以至他仍旧、永远呼喊着要多一点对他的认识。依旧得不到回答，他便举起双手，发起那庄严的雪冤大誓；要旨说：「倘若我爱金子过了度，倘若我恨过仇敌，倘若我拒绝客居我帐棚的外人，倘若我向公共舆论屈膝」：

> 我若这地向我喊冤，
> 它的犁沟一同哭泣；
> 我若未给价值便吃了它的出产，
> 或叫原主丧了性命：
> 愿蒺藜长出来代替麦子，
> 愿恶草长出来代替大麦。

===Original===
With a slow gesture he covers his face:

> The words of Job are ended.

===Chinese===
他缓缓作势，掩住了脸：

===Original===
VII

They are ended: even though at this point (when the debate seems to be closed) a young Aramaean Arab, Elihu, who has been loitering around and listening to the controversy, bursts in and delivers his young red-hot opinions. They are violent, and at the same time quite raw and priggish. Job troubles not to answer: the others keep a chilling silence. But while this young man rants, pointing skyward now and again, we see, we feel—it is most wonderfully conveyed—as clearly as if indicated by successive stage-directions, a terrific thunderstorm gathering; a thunderstorm with a whirlwind. It gathers; it is upon them; it darkens them with dread until even the words of Elihu dry on his lips:

===Chinese===
VII

是说完了：尽管在这当口（辩论似乎已告收束），一个年轻的亚兰阿拉伯人，以利户（Elihu），——他一直在旁边逡巡、听这场争辩——突然闯进来，迸出他那一套年轻的、烧得正红的意见。那些意见是猛烈的，同时又生涩得很，还有几分道学气。约伯不屑于费神回答：其余的人守着一片冷得瘆人的沉默。可正当这年轻人高声咆哮、不时向天指指点点的时候，我们看见，我们感到——这一点传达得奇妙之至——一场可怖的雷雨正在聚拢来，清楚得仿佛一道接一道的舞台指示标出来的一般；一场携着旋风的雷雨。它聚拢来；它已临到他们；它用惧怕罩暗了他们，直到连以利户的话也在唇上干涸：

===Original===
> If a man speak, surely he shall be swallowed up.

It breaks and blasts and confounds them; and out of it the Lord speaks.

===Chinese===
> 人若开口，就必被吞没。

雷雨炸裂，轰击他们，把他们震伏；耶和华就从雷雨之中说话。

===Original===
Now of that famous and marvellous speech, put by the poet into the mouth of God, we may say what may be said of all speeches put by man into the mouth of God. We may say, as of the speeches of the Archangel in *Paradise Lost* that it is argument, and argument, by its very nature, admits of being answered. But, if to make God talk at all be anthropomorphism, here is anthropomorphism at its very best in its effort to reach to God.

===Chinese===
至于那篇由诗人放进上帝口中的著名而奇妙的演说，凡人放进上帝口中的演说所能说的话，对它都可以说。我们可以像论《失乐园》里天使长的演说那样说：它是论辩，而论辩按其本性是可以被驳答的。但，倘若叫上帝开口本身就是拟人论（anthropomorphism），那么这里有的是拟人论的极致——它竭尽全力企及上帝。

===Chinese===

===Original===
VIII

Structurally a great poem; historically a great poem; philosophically a great poem; so rendered for us in noble English diction as to be worthy in any comparison of diction, structure, ancestry, thought! Why should we not study it in our English School, if only for purpose of comparison? I conclude with these words of Lord Latymer:

===Chinese===
VIII

就结构说是一首伟大的诗；就历史说是一首伟大的诗；就哲学说是一首伟大的诗；又以高贵的英文辞采为我们译出，在辞采、结构、血统、思想的任何比较中都当得起！我们为什么不在我们的英文学院（School of English）里研读它呢——哪怕只为比较的目的？我以拉蒂默勋爵的这些话作结：

===Original===
> There is nothing comparable with it except the *Prometheus Bound* of Aeschylus. It is eternal, illimitable … its scope is the relation between God and Man. It is a vast liberation, a great gaol-delivery of the spirit of Man; nay, rather a great Acquittal.

===Chinese===
