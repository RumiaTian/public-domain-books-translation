## Introduction / 引言
===Original===

> The’s as much human nature in some folks as th’ is in others, if not more.
> — David Harum

===Chinese===

> 有的人身上的人性，同旁人一样多——兴许还多些。

===Original===

One of the most conspicuous characteristics of our contemporary native fiction is an increasing tendency to subordinate plot or story to the bold and realistic portrayal of some of the types of American life and manners. And the reason for this is not far to seek. The extraordinary mixing of races which has been going on here for more than a century has produced an enormously diversified human result; and the products of this “hybridization” have been still further differentiated by an environment that ranges from the Everglades of Florida to the glaciers of Alaska. The existence of these conditions, and the great literary opportunities which they contain, American writers long ago perceived; and, with a generally true appreciation of artistic values, they have created from them a gallery of brilliant genre pictures which today stand for the highest we have yet attained in the art of fiction.
Thus it is that we have (to mention but a few) studies of Louisiana and her people by Mr. Cable; of Virginia and Georgia by Thomas Nelson Page and Joel Chandler Harris; of New England by Miss Jewett and Miss Wilkins; of the Middle West by Miss French (Octave Thanet); of the great Northwest by Hamlin Garland; of Canada and the land of the habitans by Gilbert Parker; and finally, though really first in point of time, the Forty-niners and their successors by Bret Harte. This list might be indefinitely extended, for it is growing daily, but it is long enough as it stands to show that every section of our country has, or soon will have, its own painter and historian, whose works will live and become a permanent part of our literature in just the degree that they are artistically true. Some of these writers have already produced many books, while others have gained general recognition and even fame by the vividness and power of a single study, like Mr. Howe with *The Story of a Country Town*. But each one, it will be noticed, has chosen for his field of work that part of our country wherein he passed the early and formative years of his life; a natural selection that is, perhaps, an unconscious affirmation of David Harum’s aphorism: “Ev’ry hoss c’n do a thing better ’n’ spryer if he’s ben broke to it as a colt.”
In the case of the present volume the conditions are identical with those just mentioned.

我们当代本土小说最显眼的特征之一，是日益把情节或故事降到次要，转而大胆、写实地描画美国生活与风俗中的某些典型人物。个中缘由并不难寻。一个多世纪以来，在这片土地上持续进行着的种族大交融，造就了千差万别的人间成品；而这场「杂交」的产品，又因环境进一步分化——环境从佛罗里达的大沼泽一直排到阿拉斯加的冰川。这些条件的存在，以及其中蕴藏的巨大的文学机遇，美国作家们早就看到了；凭着对艺术价值总体上不差的鉴别，他们从中造出了一整廊光彩夺目的风俗画，这些画如今代表着我们在小说艺术上迄今达到的最高处。
于是我们有了（略举数端）：凯布尔先生的路易斯安那和路易斯安那人；托马斯·纳尔逊·佩奇和乔尔·钱德勒·哈里斯的弗吉尼亚与佐治亚；朱厄特小姐和威尔金斯小姐的新英格兰；弗伦奇小姐（奥克塔夫·塔内）的中西部；哈姆林·加兰的大西北；吉尔伯特·帕克的加拿大和法裔庄稼人的土地；最后——其实论年头倒是头一个——布雷特·哈特的「四九人」及其后继者。这张单子可以无限延长，因为它天天在长；可就现有的一张，已足够表明：我们国家的每一角落，都已经或即将有自己的画师和史家；他们的作品将长存，并恰恰按其艺术上真实的程度，成为我们文学永久的一部分。这些作家中，有的已经著作等身；有的则凭单独一部研究的力量与鲜活，赢得了普遍的承认甚至声名——如豪先生的《一个乡村小镇的故事》。但值得注意的是：每一位都把自己工作的园地，选在了他度过人生最早、可塑性最强那几年的地方；这一天然的挑选，或许正不自觉地印证了大卫·哈勒姆的格言：「马驹子调教出来的路数，长大了走得又快又好。」
就眼下这册书而言，条件同方才说的并无二致。

Most of the scenes are laid in central New York, where the author, Edward Noyes Westcott, was born, September 24, 1847, and where he died of consumption, March 31, 1898. Nearly all his life was passed in his native city of Syracuse, and although banking and not authorship was the occupation of his active years, yet his sensitive and impressionable temperament had become so saturated with the local atmosphere, and his retentive memory so charged with facts, that when at length he took up the pen he was able to create in David Harum a character so original, so true, and so strong, yet withal so delightfully quaint and humorous, that we are at once compelled to admit that here is a new and permanent addition to the long list of American literary portraits.
The book is a novel, and throughout it runs a love story which is characterized by sympathetic treatment and a constantly increasing interest; but the title role is taken by the old country banker, David Harum: dry, quaint, somewhat illiterate, no doubt, but possessing an amazing amount of knowledge not found in printed books, and holding fast to the cheerful belief that there is nothing wholly bad or useless in this world. Or, in his own words: “A reasonable amount of fleas is good for a dog﻿—they keep him f’m broodin’ on bein’ a dog.” This horse-trading country banker and reputed Shylock, but real philanthropist, is an accurate portrayal of a type that exists in the rural districts of central New York today. Variations of him may be seen daily, driving about in their road wagons or seated in their “bank parlors,” shrewd, sharp-tongued, honest as the sunlight from most points of view, but in a horse trade much inclined to follow the rule laid down by Mr. Harum himself for such transactions: “Do unto the other feller the way he’d like to do unto you﻿—an’ do it fust.”
The genial humor and sunny atmosphere which pervade these pages are in dramatic contrast with the circumstances under which they were written. The book was finished while the author lay upon his deathbed, but, happily for the reader, no trace of his sufferings appears here. It was not granted that he should live to see his work in its present completed form, a consummation he most earnestly desired; but it seems not unreasonable to hope that the result of his labors will be appreciated, and that David Harum will endure.

故事的大半场景放在纽约州中部；作者爱德华·诺伊斯·韦斯特科特（Edward Noyes Westcott）一八四七年九月二十四日生在那里，一八九八年三月三十一日因痨病死在那里。他一生几乎都在故乡雪城度过；他盛年的营生是银行，不是写作；可他敏感易感的性情早被当地的空气浸透，善记的脑子里早塞满了实情实况——待他终于提笔，便在大卫·哈勒姆身上造出了这样一个人物：如此独创，如此真实，如此结实，却又如此可爱地古怪、幽默，我们只得立刻承认：美国文学人物的长长画廊里，添了一件新的、永久性的藏品。
这是一部小说，贯穿全书的是一个爱情故事，笔致温厚，兴味层层递进；但挑大梁的，是那位乡下老银行家大卫·哈勒姆：干巴巴，土里土气，多半也没读过多少书，却握着惊人的、印在书里找不到的学问，并且牢牢抱定一个快活的信念：这世界上没有全然坏、全然无用的东西。用他自己的话说：「跳蚤来个适量的，对狗是好事——省得它整天琢磨自己是条狗。」这位做马生意的乡下银行家，名声里的夏洛克，骨子里的慈善家，是对今日纽约州中部乡间存在的一类人物的准确写照。这一类的变体天天可见：赶着轻便马车转悠，或坐在他们的「银行客厅」里；精明，嘴快，从大多数角度看同阳光一样诚实——可一到马生意上，就多半要奉行哈勒姆先生亲自为这类交易立下的规矩：「对方想怎么对付你，你就先怎么对付他——而且抢先一步。」
充盈这些书页的温厚幽默和阳光气象，同写作它们时所处的境况，形成戏剧性的对照。这本书是作者躺在临终的病床上完成的；幸而读者看到，他的痛苦在这里没有留下一丝痕迹。他终于没能活着见到作品如今这个完整的样子——这是他最恳切盼望的一件圆满；但我们有理由希望：他劳作的成果会得到赏识，而大卫·哈勒姆将长存。
