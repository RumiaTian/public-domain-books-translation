## The Afterword / 后记

===Original===

===Original===
Thus, rather suddenly, ends our knowledge of the love-business between Perion and Melicent. For at this point, as abruptly as it began, the one existing chronicle of their adventures makes conclusion, like a bit of interrupted music, and thereby affords conjecture no inconsiderable bounds wherein to exercise itself. Yet, in view of the fact that deductions as to what befell these lovers afterward can at best result in freehanded theorising, it seems more profitable in this place to speak very briefly of the fragmentary *Roman de Lusignan*, since the history of Melicent and Perion as set forth in this book makes no pretensions to be more than a rendering into English of this manuscript, with slight additions from the earliest known printed version of 1546.

===Chinese===
于是，我们关于佩里翁与梅莉森特之间情事的知识，便这样颇为突兀地到了尽头。因为记述他们奇遇的那部硕果仅存的编年史，在此处一如它开篇时那样遽然作结，恰似一曲中道断绝的音乐，从而给臆测留出了不小的一片驰骋之域。然而，鉴于对这对恋人此后遭际的种种推究，至多不过落得一场放手悬揣的空论，在此处似以极简短地谈谈那部残缺的《吕西尼昂罗曼史》（*Roman de Lusignan*）更为有益；因为本书所载梅莉森特与佩里翁的史事，并无意自命为更多的什么——它不过是这部手稿的一个英文译本，略添少许采自知见所及最早的 1546 年印本罢了。

### II / 二

===Original===
M. Verville, in his monograph on Nicolas de Caen,2 considers it probable that the *Roman de Lusignan* was printed in Bruges by Colard Mansion at about the same time Mansion published the *Dizain des Reines*. This is possible; but until a copy of the book is discovered, our sole authority for the romance must continue to be the fragmentary MS. No. 503 in the Allonbian Collection.
Among the innumerable manuscripts in the British Museum there is perhaps none which opens a wider field for guesswork. In its entirety the *Roman de Lusignan* was, if appearances are to be trusted, a leisured and ambitious handling of the Melusina legend; but in the preserved portion Melusina figures hardly at all. We have merely the final chapters of what would seem to have been the first half, or perhaps the first third, of the complete narrative; so that this manuscript account of Melusina’s beguilements breaks off, fantastically, at a period by many years anterior to a date which those better known versions of Jean d’Arras and Thuring von Ringoltingen select as the only appropriate starting-point.
By means of a few elisions, however, the episodic story of Melicent and of the men who loved Melicent has been disembedded from what survives of the main narrative. This episode may reasonably be considered as complete in itself, in spite of its precipitous commencement; we are not told anything very definite concerning Perion’s earlier relations with Melusina, it is true, but then they are hardly of any especial importance. And speculations as to the tale’s perplexing chronology, or as to the curious treatment of the Ahasuerus legend, wherein Nicolas so strikingly differs from his precursors, Matthew Paris and Philippe Mouskes, or as to the probable course of latter incidents in the romance (which must almost inevitably have reached its climax in the foundation of the house of Lusignan by Perion’s son Raymondin and Melusina) are more profitably left to M. Verville’s ingenuity.

===Chinese===
韦尔维尔先生（M. Verville）在其论尼古拉·德·卡昂（Nicolas de Caen）的专著〔2〕里认为，《吕西尼昂罗曼史》多半是在布鲁日（Bruges）由科拉尔·芒西翁（Colard Mansion）付梓的，时间约当芒西翁刊行《王后十篇》（*Dizain des Reines*）前后。这不无可能；但在此书的一个存本被发现之前，这部罗曼史的唯一依据，仍只能继续是阿隆比安收藏（Allonbian Collection）中那部残缺的第 503 号抄本。
在大英博物馆（British Museum）汗牛充栋的抄本之中，或许没有一部能为猜想开启比它更宽阔的天地。《吕西尼昂罗曼史》在完整之时——倘若表象可信——原是对梅吕西娜（Melusina）传说一番从容不迫而气魄宏大的铺陈；可是在留存下来的部分里，梅吕西娜几乎全然不见踪影。我们仅占有全篇叙事——看来是——前一半、或者也许是前三分之一的末尾数章；于是这份手稿对梅吕西娜种种蛊惑行径的记述，竟匪夷所思地中断在一个时期，比让·德·阿拉斯（Jean d’Arras）与图林·冯·林戈尔廷根（Thuring von Ringoltingen）那两部更为人熟知的版本所择定为其唯一合宜起点的时候，还要早上许多年。
然而，借几处删削之功，梅莉森特、以及爱过梅莉森特的男子们这一段插叙，已从主叙事的劫余部分里剥离了出来。这段插曲尽管开篇突兀，却尽可视为自身完足的一个整体；诚然，关于佩里翁先前与梅吕西娜的关系，书里并未给我们什么十分确切的交代，但那些关系也说不上有何特别的重要。至于种种悬揣——关于这个故事令人困惑的编年；关于对亚哈随鲁（Ahasuerus）传说的奇特处理，尼古拉在此与他的前驱马修·帕里斯（Matthew Paris）和菲利普·穆斯克斯（Philippe Mouskes）判然悬殊；关于罗曼史后文诸事的可能进程（其高潮几乎无可避免地要落在佩里翁之子雷蒙丹（Raymondin）与梅吕西娜创建吕西尼昂家族一事之上）——还是留给韦尔维尔先生的巧智去驰骋，更为合算。

### III / 三

===Original===
One feature, though, of this romance demands particular comment. The happenings of the Melicent-episode pivot remarkably upon domnei﻿—upon chivalric love, upon the *frauendienst* of the minnesingers, or upon “woman-worship,” as we might bunglingly translate a word for which in English there is no precisely equivalent synonym. Therefore this English version of the Melicent-episode has been called *Domnei*, at whatever price of unintelligibility.
For there is really no other word or combination of words which seems quite to sum up, or even indicate this precise attitude toward life. Domnei was less a preference for one especial woman than a code of philosophy. “The complication of opinions and ideas, of affections and habits,” writes Charles Claude Fauriel,3 “which prompted the chevalier to devote himself to the service of a lady, and by which he strove to prove to her his love and to merit hers in return, was expressed by the single word *domnei*.”
And this, of course, is true enough. Yet domnei was even more than a complication of opinions and affections and habits: it was also a malady and a religion quite incommunicably blended.

===Chinese===
不过，这部罗曼史有一处特色，须得特别一说。梅莉森特插曲中的种种情事，显著地以崇慕（domnei）为枢轴——以骑士之爱为枢轴，以恋歌诗人（minnesingers）的 *frauendienst*（妇人之效劳）为枢轴，抑或以「妇人之崇拜」为枢轴——倘若我们竟用这样笨拙的译法，去对付一个在英文里找不出精确对当同义词的字眼的话。因此，梅莉森特插曲的这个英译本，便题为《多姆内伊》（*Domnei*），哪怕须以费解为代价，也在所不惜。
因为实在没有别的字眼、也没有任何字眼的组合，能把这样一种对待人生的特定态度概括净尽，甚至连把它指点出来也办不到。崇慕与其说是对某一位特定女子的偏宠，毋宁说是一套哲学的法典。「意见与观念、情感与习惯的这团纠葛，」夏尔·克洛德·福里埃尔（Charles Claude Fauriel）〔3〕写道，「驱使骑士献身于一位贵妇的侍奉，又使他力图向她证明自己的爱、并赢取她回报的爱——这一切，尽由崇慕（*domnei*）一词道出。」
这自然不错。然而崇慕还远不止是意见、情感与习惯的纠葛：它同时也是一种病症与一种宗教，两者交融到了无可言传的地步。

===Original===
Thus you will find that Dante﻿—to cite only the most readily accessible of medieval amorists﻿—enlarges as to domnei in both these last-named aspects impartially. Domnei suspends all his senses save that of sight, makes him turn pale, causes tremors in his left side, and sends him to bed “like a little beaten child, in tears”; throughout you have the manifestations of domnei described in terms befitting the symptoms of a physical disease: but as concerns the other aspect, Dante never wearies of reiterating that it is domnei which has turned his thoughts toward God; and with terrible sincerity he beholds in Beatrice de’Bardi the highest illumination which Divine Grace may permit to humankind. “This is no woman; rather it is one of heaven’s most radiant angels,” he says with terrible sincerity.

===Chinese===
于是你会发现，但丁（Dante）——姑且只举中世纪歌咏爱情者中最为易得的一位——对崇慕的铺陈，恰好不偏不倚地落在方才点出的这两个面向上。崇慕使他的一切感官尽皆失灵，唯独视觉除外；使他面无人色，使他左侧肢体震颤，使他「像挨了打的小孩子似的，噙着眼泪」上床就寝：通篇你见到的，是用一副躯体病症的症候口吻描摹出来的崇慕诸相；可是论及另一个面向，但丁又百说不厌地反复申言：正是崇慕把他的思绪引向了上帝；并且他以可怖的真诚，在贝亚特丽切·德·巴尔迪（Beatrice de’Bardi）身上，看见神恩所能垂准于人类的至高光照。「这不是女人；这是天国最光辉灿烂的天使之一。」他带着可怖的真诚如是说。

===Original===
With terrible sincerity, let it be repeated: for the service of domnei was never, as some would affect to interpret it, a modish and ordered affectation; the histories of Peire de Maënzac, of Guillaume de Caibestaing, of Geoffrey Rudel, of Ulrich von Liechtenstein, of the Monk of Pucibot, of Pons de Capdueilh, and even of Peire Vidal and Guillaume de Balaun, survive to prove it was a serious thing, a stark and life-disposing reality. *En cor gentil domnei per mort no passa*, as Nicolas himself declares. The service of domnei involved, it in fact invited, anguish; it was a martyrdom whereby the lover was uplifted to saintship and the lady to little less than, if anything less than, godhead. For it was a canon of domnei, it was the very essence of domnei, that the woman one loves is providentially set between her lover’s apprehension, and God, as the mobile and vital image and corporeal reminder of heaven, as a quick symbol of beauty and holiness, of purity and perfection. In her the lover views﻿—embodied, apparent to human sense, and even accessible to human enterprise﻿—all qualities of God which can be comprehended by merely human faculties. It is precisely as such an intermediary that Melicent figures toward Perion, and, in a somewhat different degree, toward Ahasuerus﻿—since Ahasuerus is of necessity apart in all things from the run of humanity.

===Chinese===
容我把「可怖的真诚」再说一遍：因为崇慕的侍奉，从来不曾像某些人乐于曲解的那样，是一种入时而规整的做作；佩雷·德·马恩扎克（Peire de Maënzac）、纪尧姆·德·凯贝斯坦（Guillaume de Caibestaing）、杰弗里·吕德尔（Geoffrey Rudel）、乌尔里希·冯·利希滕施泰因（Ulrich von Liechtenstein）、普西博的修士（the Monk of Pucibot）、蓬斯·德·卡普杜伊（Pons de Capdueilh），乃至佩雷·维达尔（Peire Vidal）与纪尧姆·德·巴洛恩（Guillaume de Balaun），其行状俱存于世，足证这是一件郑重其事的东西，一桩严酷而支配生命的现实。「高贵温雅的心中，崇慕不因死亡而消逝」（*En cor gentil domnei per mort no passa*），尼古拉本人如是宣称。崇慕的侍奉牵动着苦痛，实则更邀引着苦痛；那是一场殉道，情人由此被擢登圣品，贵妇则被擢登到仅仅略逊于——倘若尚有逊色的话——神格之位。因为崇慕有一条法度，崇慕的真髓正在于此：人所爱的那个女子，经天意安置在她的情人的领悟与上帝之间，作上天灵动而鲜活的形象，作天堂肉身的凭证，作美与神圣、纯洁与圆满的一个活的象征。在她身上，情人观照着——具形显体、诉诸人类感官、乃至人类营求所能企及地观照着——上帝的一切凡仅凭人类官能便可领会的品质。恰恰是作为这样一位中介者，梅莉森特现身于佩里翁面前，也以稍有不同的程度，现身于亚哈随鲁（Ahasuerus）面前——因为亚哈随鲁在一切事上，都必然超然于人类的常轨之外。

===Original===
Yet instances were not lacking in the service of domnei where worship of the symbol developed into a religion sufficing in itself, and became competitor with worship of what the symbol primarily represented﻿—such instances as have their analogues in the legend of Ritter Tannhäuser, or in Aucassin’s resolve in the romance to go down into hell with “his sweet mistress whom he so much loves,” or (here perhaps most perfectly exampled) in Arnaud de Merveil’s naive declaration that whatever portion of his heart belongs to God heaven holds in vassalage to Adelaide de Beziers. It is upon this darker and rebellious side of domnei, of a religion pathetically dragged dustward by the luxuriance and efflorescence of over-passionate service, that Nicolas has touched in depicting Demetrios.

===Chinese===
然而在崇慕的侍奉之中，亦不缺乏这样的事例：对象征的崇拜发达成一门自足的宗教，进而与对那象征本来所代表之物的崇拜分庭抗礼——这样的事例，其近似者见于骑士唐怀瑟（Ritter Tannhäuser）的传说，见于罗曼史里奥卡森（Aucassin）定要携「他如此深爱着的甜蜜恋人」同下地狱的决心，或者（此处或许示范得最为完满）见于阿尔诺·德·梅尔韦伊（Arnaud de Merveil）天真烂漫的自白：他心中凡属上帝的那一份，天国也不过是代贝济耶的阿德莱德（Adelaide de Beziers）领有的一片封邑。尼古拉在描画德米特里奥斯（Demetrios）时所触到的，正是崇慕这幽暗而悖逆的一面——一门宗教，被过度炽烈的侍奉之繁茂与绽发，可怜地拖曳向尘土。

### IV / 四

===Original===
Nicolas de Caen, himself the servitor *par amours* of Isabella of Burgundy, has elsewhere written of domnei (in his *Le Roi Amaury*) in terms such as it may not be entirely out of place to transcribe here. Beelzebub, as you may remember, has been discomfited in his endeavours to ensnare King Amaury and is withdrawing in disgust.
“A pest upon this *domnei*!”4 the fiend growls. “Nay, the match is at an end, and I may speak in perfect candour now. I swear to you that, given a man clear-eyed enough to see that a woman by ordinary is nourished much as he is nourished, and is subjected to every bodily infirmity which he endures and frets beneath, I do not often bungle matters. But when a fool begins to flounder about the world, dead-drunk with adoration of an immaculate woman﻿—a monster which, as even the man’s own judgment assures him, does not exist and never will exist﻿—why, he becomes as unmanageable as any other maniac when a frenzy is upon him. For then the idiot hungers after a life so high-pitched that his gross faculties may not so much as glimpse it; he is so rapt with impossible dreams that he becomes oblivious to the nudgings of his most petted vice; and he abhors his own innate and perfectly natural inclination to cowardice, and filth, and self-deception. He, in fine, affords me and all other rational people no available handle; and, in consequence, he very often flounders beyond the reach of my whisperings. There may be other persons who can inform you why such blatant folly should thus be the master-word of evil, but for my own part, I confess to ignorance.”

===Chinese===
尼古拉·德·卡昂本人，便是以爱相事（*par amours*）侍奉勃艮第的伊莎贝拉（Isabella of Burgundy）的仆从；他在别处——在他的《阿莫里王》（*Le Roi Amaury*）里——写过崇慕，那些言辞誊录在此，或许不至全然不合时宜。别西卜（Beelzebub），你大概还记得，在罗致阿莫里王（King Amaury）入彀的种种努力里吃了败仗，正满怀厌恶地退场。
「这该死的崇慕（*domnei*）！」〔4〕魔鬼咆哮道。「罢了，这一局棋已经下完，如今我可以句句吐实了。我向你发誓：只要一个男人眼光够亮，看得出女人平日里滋养自己的方式与他无甚两样，也承受着他所忍受、为之烦躁的一切身体上的羸弱，我做事便不常失手。可一旦有个蠢货开始踉跄着闯荡世界，对一位纯洁无玷的女人崇拜得酩酊大醉——那样一头怪物，连他自己的判断都向他担保并不存在、也永远不会存在——唉，那他一旦狂性发作，便同任何别的疯子一样无从驾驭了。因为那时这白痴渴慕着一种高亢得让他粗钝的官能连一瞥也瞥不见的人生；他为绝无可能的幻梦出神，连他最宠溺的恶习来推促，也浑然忘却；而且他憎恶自己那份与生俱来、再自然不过的趋赴怯懦、污秽与自欺的倾向。总而言之，他给我、也给其余一切讲道理的人留不下任何下手的把柄；于是乎，他往往一个趔趄，便跌出我耳语所能及的地方。或许另有高明能告诉你，为何这般昭彰的愚行竟会是邪恶的主咒；至于我自己，我承认一无所知。」

===Original===
