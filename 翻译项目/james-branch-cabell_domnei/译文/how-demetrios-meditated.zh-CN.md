## XVI How Demetrios Meditated / 十六 德米特里奥斯如何冥想

===Original===

Demetrios came without any hindrance into Narenta, a free city. He believed his Emperor must have sent galleys toward Christendom to get tidings of his generalissimo, but in this city of merchants Demetrios heard no report of them. Yet in the harbour he found a trading-ship prepared for traffic in the country of the pagans; the sail was naked to the wind, the anchor chain was already shortened at the bow. Demetrios bargained with the captain of this vessel, and in the outcome paid him 400 sequins. In exchange the man agreed to touch at the Needle of Ansignano that afternoon and take Demetrios aboard. Since the proconsul had no passport, he could not with safety endeavour to elude those officers of the Tribunal who must endorse the ship’s passage at Piaja.

Thus about sunset Demetrios waited the ship’s coming, alone upon the Needle. This promontory is like a Titan’s finger of black rock thrust out into the water. The day was perishing, and the querulous sea before Demetrios was an unresting welter of gold and blood.

He thought of how he had won safely through a horde of dangers, and the gross man chuckled. He considered that unquestioned rulership of every person near Demetrios which awaited him oversea, and chiefly he thought of Melicent whom he loved even better than he did the power to sneer at everything the world contained. And the proconsul chuckled.

===Chinese===

德米特里奥斯一路通行无阻，来到了自由城市纳伦塔。他相信他的皇帝必定已派出若干桨帆船，驶往基督教世界去打探他这位大统帅的音讯；可在这座商贾之城，德米特里奥斯不曾听到有关它们的半点消息。不过在港口里，他寻见一艘备妥了要去异教徒国度经商的商船：帆已赤条条地迎着风，锚链已在船头收短。德米特里奥斯同这条船的船长讲了一番价钱，讲到最后，付给他 400 塞奎因（sequins）。作为交换，那人答应当天下午在安西尼亚诺之针（Needle of Ansignano）略作停靠，把德米特里奥斯接上船去。只因总督没有通行文书，若想避过那些必须在皮亚雅（Piaja）为船只出港签押的裁判所（Tribunal）官吏，终究无法保得周全。

于是，日落时分，德米特里奥斯独自立在针岬上，等候那船到来。这处海岬宛如泰坦（Titan）伸入水中的一根黑岩手指。白昼正在死去，德米特里奥斯眼前那片嗔怨的海，翻腾不休，尽是金与血交搅的狂澜。

他想到自己如何从成群的危难中安然脱身，这粗蠢的汉子不由得窃笑。他思量着海外正待他领受的那份权柄——对德米特里奥斯近旁每一个人不容置喙的统治；而他心里想得最多的，还是梅莉森特：他爱她，甚至胜过爱那一份足以对世间万物嗤笑以对的权力。于是总督又窃笑了。
===Original===

He said, aloud:

“I owe very much to Messire de la Forêt. I owe far more than I can estimate. For, by this, those lackeys will have slain Messire de la Forêt or else they will have taken Messire de la Forêt to King Theodoret, who will piously make an end of this handsome idiot. Either way, I shall enjoy tranquillity and shall possess my Melicent until I die. Decidedly, I owe a deal to this self-satisfied tall fool.”

Thus he contended with his irritation. It may be that the man was never sane; it is certain that the mainspring of his least action was an inordinate pride. Now hatred quickened, spreading from a flicker of distaste; and his faculties were stupefied, as though he faced a girdling conflagration. It was not possible to hate adequately this Perion who had struck Demetrios of Anatolia and perhaps was not yet dead; nor could Demetrios think of any sufficing requital for this Perion who dared to be so tall and handsome and young-looking when Demetrios was none of these things, for this Perion whom Melicent had loved and loved today. And Demetrios of Anatolia had fought with a charmed sword against a person such as this, safe as an angler matched against a minnow; Demetrios of Anatolia, now at the last, accepted alms from what had been until today a pertinacious gnat. Demetrios was physically shaken by disgust at the situation, and in the sunset’s glare his swarthy countenance showed like that of Belial among the damned.

===Chinese===

他出声说道：

「我欠德·拉福雷爵爷的实在很多。我欠下的，远远超出我所能估量。因为照此情形，那班奴才不是已经杀了德·拉福雷爵爷，便是已把他解往泰奥多雷特王驾前，由那位君王满怀虔敬地结果了这俊美的白痴。无论哪一样，我都将安享清静，都将占有我的梅莉森特，直到我死。千真万确，我欠这位志得意满的高个子傻瓜的，着实不少。」

他便这样与自己胸中的愠怒搏斗。这个人说不定从来就算不得神志清明；可以断言的是，哪怕他最细微的举动，其主发条也是一股毫无节制的骄矜。此刻仇恨愈燃愈旺，从一星嫌恶的微焰蔓延开来；他的心神为之昏乱，仿佛面对一场四面合围的大火。对这个佩里翁——他打了安纳托利亚（Anatolia）的德米特里奥斯，而且说不定还没死——怎么恨也恨不到十足；德米特里奥斯也想不出任何足以相抵的报偿，来对付这个佩里翁——他竟敢生得那样高大、俊美、面相年轻，而德米特里奥斯这三样一样也不占；他竟是梅莉森特爱过、而且今日还爱着的佩里翁。而安纳托利亚的德米特里奥斯，曾持一柄施了魔法的剑，同这样一号人物对阵，安稳得如同垂钓人对付一尾鲦鱼；安纳托利亚的德米特里奥斯，临到这最后关头，竟接受了这么一样东西的周济——直到今天，它还不过是一只纠缠不休的蚊蚋。这处境令德米特里奥斯厌恶得周身发颤；在落日的灼光里，他那张黝黑的面孔显露出来，宛如众沉沦者之间的彼列（Belial）。
===Original===

“The life of Melicent hangs on my safe return to Nacumera.﻿ ﻿… Ey, what is that to me!” the proconsul cried aloud. “The thought of Melicent is sweeter than the thought of any god. It is not sweet enough to bribe me into living as this Perion’s debtor.”

So when the ship touched at the Needle, a half-hour later, that spur of rock was vacant. Demetrios had untethered his horse, had thrown away his sword and other armour, and had torn his garments; afterward he rolled in the first puddle he discovered. Thus he set out afoot, in grimy rags﻿—for no one marks a beggar upon the highway﻿—and thus he came again into the realm of King Theodoret, where certainly nobody looked for Demetrios to come unarmed.

With the advantage of a quiet advent, as was quickly proven, he found no check for a notorious leave-taking.

===Chinese===

「梅莉森特的性命，系于我能否平安回到纳库梅拉。……哼，这与我何干！」总督高声喊道。「对梅莉森特的思念，比对任何神明的思念都甘美。可它还不够甘美，买不动我活着去做这佩里翁的债户。」

于是，半小时后，船靠上针岬时，那道岩脊上已空无一人。德米特里奥斯解开了马缰，抛掉了剑和其余的甲胄，又撕破了衣裳，随后在撞见的第一处泥坑里打了个滚。就这样，他一身泥污褴褛，徒步上路——大路上的乞丐，是没有人会多看一眼的——就这样，他再次踏入了泰奥多雷特王的国境；在那里，断然没有谁料到德米特里奥斯竟会不带兵刃地前来。

事实很快便证明：靠着这悄然而来的一点便宜，他先前那桩声名狼藉的出走，竟没有受到任何盘查。
