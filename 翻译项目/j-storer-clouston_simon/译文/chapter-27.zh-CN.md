## XXVII / 夜奔

===Original===
On the morning after his visit from Mr. Carrington, Ned Cromarty took his keeper with him and drove over to shoot on a friend’s estate. He stayed for tea and it was well after five o’clock and quite dark when he started on his long drive home. The road passed close to a wayside station with a level crossing over the line, and when they came to this the gates were closed against them and the light of the signal of the up line had changed from red to white.

“Train’s up to time,” said Ned to the keeper. “I thought we’d have got through before she came.”

There was no moon, a fine rain hung in the air, and the night was already pitch dark. Sitting there in the dogcart before the closed gates, behind the blinding light of the gig lamps, they were quite invisible themselves; but about thirty yards to their left they saw the station platform plainly in the radiance of its lights, and, straight before them in the radiance of their own, they could see less distinctly the road beyond the line.

===Chinese===
卡林顿先生来访的次日早晨，内德·克罗玛蒂（Ned Cromarty）带上他的猎场看守（keeper），驱车到一位朋友的庄上行猎。他在那边一直待到用过茶点才动身，踏上归途的长路时，早已过了五点钟，天也全黑了。大路贴着一座路边小站经过，铁路道口就横在铁轨上；他们到了道口，栏木已经放下拦住去路，上行线的信号灯也已由红转白。

“车倒是准点的。”内德对猎场看守说。“我还当咱们能在它来之前先过去呢。”

那晚没有月亮，细雨浮在空气里，夜已漆黑。两人坐在轻便马车上，停在放下的栏木前，藏在马车灯晃眼的光芒背后，自己身上是一点也看不见的；左侧约三十码开外，站台在它那片灯光照耀下历历在目，而他们正前方自己灯照出去的、铁道那头的路面，反倒看得不甚分明。

===Original===
At first, save for the distant rumble of the southward bound train, there was no sign of life or of movement anywhere, and then all at once a figure on a bicycle appeared on the road, and in a moment dismounted beside the station. It was a girl in black, and at the sight of her, Ned bent forward suddenly in his driving seat and stared intently into the night. He saw her unstrap a small suit case from the bicycle and lead the bicycle into the station. A minute or two passed and then she emerged from the ticket office on to the platform carrying the suit case in her hand. The bicycle she had evidently left in the station, and it seemed manifest that she was going by this train.

“That’s Miss Farmond, sir, from Keldale House!” exclaimed the keeper.

His master said nothing but kept his eye intently fixed on the girl. One of the platform lamps lit her plainly, and he thought she looked the most forlorn and moving sight that had ever stirred his heart. There was something shrinking in her attitude, and when she looked once for a few moments straight towards him, there seemed to be something both sad and frightened in her face. Not another soul was on the platform, and seen in that patch of light against an immensity of dark empty country and black sky, she gave him such an impression of friendlessness that he could scarcely stay in his seat. And all the while the roar of the oncoming train was growing louder and ever louder. In a few minutes she would be gone﻿—“Where?” he asked himself.

===Chinese===
起初，除了南行列车远远的隆隆声，四下里不见一点生气，也没有一点动静；随后冷不丁地，路上出现了一个骑自行车的人影，转眼便在车站旁下了车。那是个一身黑衣的姑娘。一见她，内德在车夫座上猛然俯身向前，凝神盯住那片夜色。他看她从车上解下一只小手提箱，推着自行车进了车站。一两分钟后，她提着提箱从售票处出来，走上站台。自行车显然是留在站里了，她分明是要搭这班车走。

“那是法蒙德小姐（Miss Farmond），老爷，凯尔代尔宅邸（Keldale House）的法蒙德小姐！”猎场看守失声叫道。

主人没有作声，只把目光紧紧锁在姑娘身上。站台的一盏灯把她照得清清楚楚，他觉得她那副光景，是他平生所见最凄惶动人、最搅得他心口发紧的景象。她的姿态里有种畏畏缩缩的东西；有一回她朝他这边直望了几秒钟，那张脸上似乎又愁又怕。站台上再没有第二个活人；衬着四野无边的黑暗荒原和黑沉沉的天，她独自立在那片灯光里，那份孤苦无依的意味，逼得他几乎在座位上坐不住。而列车驶近的轰鸣，一直越滚越响。再过几分钟她就要走了——上哪儿？他问自己。

===Original===
“I’m wondering where she’ll be going at this time o’ night with nae mair luggage than yon,” said the keeper.

That decided it.

“Take the trap home and tell Miss Cromarty not to expect me tonight,” said his master, quickly. “Say I’ve gone﻿—oh, anywhere you derned well like! There’s something up and I’m going to see what it is.”

He jumped quietly on the road just as the engine thundered between the gates in front. By the time the train was at rest, he was over the gate and making his way to the platform. He stopped in the darkness by the rear end of the train till he saw the figure in black disappear into a carriage, and then he stepped into a compartment near the guard’s van.

===Chinese===
“我正纳闷呢，这么晚的天，行李就那么一星半点，她这是要奔哪儿去呀。”猎场看守说。

这一下，主意就定了。

“把车赶回去，告诉克罗玛蒂小姐（Miss Cromarty），今晚甭等我。”主人急急地说。“就说我走了——哦，见鬼的，随你爱编上哪儿都成！反正出了事，我去瞧瞧是怎么回事。”

机车正轰隆隆碾过前面两道栏门之间，他悄悄跳到路上。等列车停稳，他已翻过栏门朝站台去了。他先摸黑停在车尾，看见那黑衣身影进了车厢，这才跨进紧挨守车的一间隔间。

===Original===
“Haven’t got a ticket, but I’ll pay as I go along,” he said to the guard as he passed the window.

The guard knew Mr. Cromarty well and touched his cap, and then the train started and Mr. Cromarty was embarked upon what he confessed to himself was the blindest journey he had ever made in all his varied career.

Where was she going﻿—and why was she going? He asked himself these questions over and over again as he sat with a cigar between his teeth and his long legs stretched out on the opposite seat, and the train drove on into an ever wilder and more desolate land. It would be very many miles and a couple of hours or more before they reached any sort of conceivable destination for her, and as a matter of fact this train did not go beyond that destination. Then it struck him sharply that up till the end of last month the train had continued its southward journey. The alteration in the timetable was only a few days old. Possibly she was not aware of it and had counted on travelling to﻿—where? He knew where she had got to stop, but where had she meant to stop? Or where would she go tomorrow? And above all, why was she going at all, leaving her bicycle at a wayside station and with her sole luggage a small suit case? Ned shook his head, tried to suck life into his neglected cigar, and gave up the problem in the meanwhile.

As to the question of what business he had to be following Miss Farmond like this, he troubled his head about it not at all. If she needed him, here he was. If she didn’t, he would clear out. But very strong and very urgent was the conviction that she required a friend of some sort.

===Chinese===
“还没来得及买票，路上再补给您。”他经过车窗时对列车员说。

列车员跟克罗玛蒂先生是熟识，抬手碰了碰帽檐。随后列车开动，克罗玛蒂先生就此踏上了他自己也承认是他一生形形色色的经历里最没头没脑的一趟旅程。

她上哪儿去——又为什么要去？他嘴里叼着雪茄，两条长腿伸直了搭在对面的座位上，列车一头开进越来越荒僻凄凉的地界，他就这么一遍又一遍地问自己。照她此行想得出的目的地算，还得走上许多英里、两个来钟头；何况说实在的，这趟车本来也就到那儿为止。这时他猛地想起：直到上个月底，这趟车还照旧往南开，改时刻表不过是几天前的事。说不定她压根不知道，还打算一路坐到——坐到哪儿呢？他知道她如今不得不停下的地方，可她原打算停在哪一站？又或者，明天她再接着往哪儿去？而最要紧的是：她究竟为什么要走，自行车撂在路边小站，全部行李只有一只小手提箱？内德摇了摇头，使劲嘬了几口那支受了冷落的雪茄，暂且把这道难题搁下。

至于他自己凭什么就这样尾随法蒙德小姐，这个问题他压根没费一点心思。她若需要他，他就在这儿；不需要，他就抽身走人。不过有一点他信得又深又切，而且火烧火燎：她总归需要有个朋友帮衬。

===Original===
The stations were few and far between and most desolate, improbable places as endings for Cicely Farmond’s journey. He looked out of the window at each of them, but she never alighted.

“She’s going to find herself stuck for the night. That’s about the size of it,” he said to himself as they left the last station before the journey ended.

Though their next stop was the final stop, he did not open the carriage door when the train pulled up. He did not even put his head far out of the window, only just enough to see what passed on the platform ahead.

“I’m not going to worry her if she doesn’t need me,” he said to himself.

===Chinese===
一路上车站寥寥可数，又净是些荒凉得不可思议的所在，拿来做西塞莉·法蒙德（Cicely Farmond）旅程的终点，实在最不像是那么回事。每到一站，他都朝窗外张望，她却始终没有下车。

“她今晚要落得没处安身了。就是这么档子事。”驶过终点之前最后一站时，他自言自语道。

下一站便是终点，可列车停下时，他并没有拉开车门，连头也没怎么伸出窗去，只探到刚够看清前面站台上动静的地步。

“她要是不需要我，我就不去搅扰她。”他对自己说。

===Original===
He saw the slip of a figure in black talking to the stationmaster, and it was hardly necessary to hear that official’s last words in order to divine what had happened.

“Weel, miss,” he overheard the stationmaster say, “I’m sorry ye’re disappointed, but it’s no me that has stoppit the train. It’s aff for the winter. If ye turn to the left ye’ll fin’ the hotel.”

The girl looked round her slowly and it seemed to Ned that the way she did it epitomised disappointment and desolation, and then she hurried through the station buildings and was gone.

He was out of the carriage and after her in an instant. Beyond the station the darkness was intense and he had almost passed a road branching to the left without seeing it. He stopped and was going to turn down it when it struck him the silence was intense that way, but that there was a light sound of retreating footsteps straight ahead.

===Chinese===
他望见那纤细的黑衣身影正同站长说话；即便不听那位官员末了的那几句，也不难猜出出了什么事。

“唉，小姐，”他听见站长说，“真抱歉，叫您白失望一场，可这趟车停开，不归我管——一入冬它就不跑啦。您往左手拐，就能找着酒店。”

姑娘缓缓环顾四周。内德觉得，她这一望，简直把失望和凄凉都凝在了里头；随后她匆匆穿过站房，不见了。

眨眼间他已出了车厢追了上去。站外一片浓黑，左边岔出去的一条路，他差点走了过去都没瞧见。他停住脚，正要拐下去，忽然觉出那条路上静得出奇，倒是正前方传来一阵轻轻远去的脚步声。

===Original===
“She’s missed the turning!” he said to himself, and followed the footsteps.

In a little he could see her against the sky, a dim hurrying figure, and his own stride quickened. He had never been in this place before, but he knew it for a mere seaboard village with an utterly lonely country on every inland side. She was heading into a black wilderness, and he took his decision at once and increased his pace till he was overhauling her fast.

At the sound of his footsteps he could see that she glanced over her shoulder and made the more haste till she was almost running. And then as she heard the pursuing steps always nearer she suddenly slackened speed to let him pass.

“Miss Farmond!” said he.

===Chinese===
“她错过岔路口了！”他自语一声，循着脚步声追了下去。

不多时，他便望见她衬着天幕，一个模糊而匆促的身影，自己的步子也随之加快。这地方他从前没来过，但他知道这不过是座海边小村，朝内陆的每一面都是荒无人烟的乡野。她正一头扎进黑荒野里去。他当即拿定主意，加紧脚步，眼看就要追上她了。

听见身后的脚步声，他看出她回头瞥了一眼，越发放紧脚步，几乎小跑起来。随后，那追赶的脚步声步步逼近，她忽然放缓了脚步，要让来人超过去。

“法蒙德小姐！”他唤道。

===Original===
He could hear her gasp as she stopped short and turned sharply. She was staring hard now at the tall figure looming above her.

“It’s only me﻿—Ned Cromarty,” he said quietly.

And then he started in turn, for instead of showing relief she gave a half smothered little cry and shrank away from him. For a moment there was dead silence and then he said, still quietly, though it cost him an effort.

“I only mean to help you if you need a hand. Are you looking for the hotel?”

“Yes,” she said in a low frightened voice.

===Chinese===
她猛地收步，急急转过身来，他听得见她倒抽冷气的声音。此刻她正死死盯住自己跟前那道高高耸立的身影。

“是我——内德·克罗玛蒂。”他轻轻地说。

这一回，倒是他吃了一惊：她非但没有露出半点松快，反倒从喉咙里挤出半声闷住的低叫，直往旁边躲。一时间死一般寂静，随后他又开了口，语气依旧平平，虽说这一句说得颇为费力。

“我只是想，您要是需要人搭把手，我可以帮您。您是在找那家酒店么？”

“是。”她低低地答，声音里满是惊惶。

===Original===
“Well,” said he, “I guess you’d walk till morning before you reached an hotel along this road. You missed the turning at the station. Give me your bag. Come along!”

She let him take the suit case and she turned back with him, but it struck him painfully that her docility was like that of a frightened animal.

“Where are you bound for?” he enquired in his usual direct way.

She murmured something that he could not catch and then they fell altogether silent till they had retraced their road to the station and turned down towards a twinkling light or two which showed where the village lay.

===Chinese===
“这个嘛，”他说，“估摸着顺着这条路走下去，走到天亮您也碰不上一家酒店。您在车站把岔路口错过去了。箱子给我。走吧！”

她由着他接过提箱，随他一道折返。可她那份驯顺，活像一只受了惊的小兽，这一点叫他心里发疼。

“您这是要上哪儿去？”他照一贯直来直去的做派问道。

她咕哝了一句什么，他没有听清；随后两人便一言不发，直到原路折回车站，再朝着一两点闪闪烁烁的灯火拐下去——村子的所在，就在那儿了。

===Original===
“Now, Miss Farmond,” said he, “we are getting near this pub and as we’ve both got to spend the night there, you’ll please observe these few short and simple rules. I’m your uncle﻿—Uncle Ned. D’you see?”

There was no laugh, or even a smile from her. She gave a little start of surprise and in a very confused voice murmured:

“Yes, I see.”

“My full name is Mr. Ned Dawkins and you’re Louisa Dawkins my niece. Just call me ‘Uncle Ned’ and leave me to do the talking. We are touring this beautiful country and I’ve lost my luggage owing to the derned foolishness of the railroad officials here. And then when we’ve had a little bit of dinner you can tell me, if you like, why you’ve eloped and why you’ve got a down on me. Or if you don’t like to, well, you needn’t. Ah, here’s the pub at last.”

He threw open the door and in a loud and cheerful voice cried:

“Well, here we are, Louisa. Walk right in, my dear!”

===Chinese===
“好了，法蒙德小姐，”他说，“咱们快到那家小酒馆了。既然咱俩今晚都得在那儿落脚，那就请您务必遵守下面这几条简而又简的小规矩。我是您舅舅——内德舅舅。明白吗？”

她没有笑，连一丝笑影也没有。她小小地吃了一惊，用慌乱之极的声音喃喃道：

“明白，我明白。”

“我的大名叫内德·道金斯（Ned Dawkins）先生，您是露易莎·道金斯（Louisa Dawkins），我的外甥女。您只管叫我‘内德舅舅’，开口的事都交给我。咱俩是来游历这块美丽地方的，行李却叫这儿的铁路官员们蠢得没了影儿。等咱们用一点儿晚饭，您要是乐意，就告诉我您干吗私奔，又干吗看我不顺眼。要是不乐意呢，那不说也罢。啊，酒馆总算到了。”

他一把推开门，扬起欢畅的嗓门喊道：

“好啦，咱们到了，露易莎。大大方方往里走，亲爱的！”
