## XXV / 一封电报

===Original===

The car took Mr. Carrington straight back to the town and dropped him at the door of Mr. Rattar’s office.

“I shall want you again at two o’clock sharp,” he said to the chauffeur, and turned in to the office.

He caught the lawyer just before he went out to lunch and said at once:

“I want to see Sir Malcolm Cromarty. Can you arrange for him to run up here for a day?”

===Chinese===

汽车把卡林顿先生（Mr. Carrington）径直送回镇上，在拉塔先生（Mr. Rattar）的事务所门前放下了他。

“两点整我还要用车，”他对司机说罢，转身进了事务所。

他赶在律师出门用午饭之前截住了他，劈头便说：

“我要见马尔科姆·克罗玛蒂爵士（Sir Malcolm Cromarty）。您能不能安排他上来这儿盘桓一天？”

===Original===

Simon stared at him hard, and there seemed to be even more caution than usual in his eye; almost, indeed, a touch of suspicion. The lawyer was not looking quite as well as usual; there was a drawn look about the upper part of the face and a hint of strain both in eyes and mouth.

“Why do you want to see Sir Malcolm?” he enquired.

“Well,” said Carrington, “the fact of the matter is, Mr. Rattar, that, as you yourself said, the direct evidence is practically nil, and one is forced to go a good deal by one’s judgment of the people suspected or concerned.”

Simon grunted sceptically.

“Very misleading,” he said.

===Chinese===

西蒙（Simon）紧紧地盯住他，眼里的戒备似乎比往常更重；说实在的，几乎还带上了一丝疑心。律师的气色不大如常，脸孔上半部显着几分憔悴绷紧，眼角与嘴角也都隐隐透着强自支撑的紧张。

“您要见马尔科姆爵士做什么？”他问道。

“呃，”卡林顿说，“实情是这样的，拉塔先生：正像您自己说过的，直接证据几乎等于零，人便只得大半凭自己对嫌疑者或牵连者的判断行事。”

西蒙将信将疑地哼了一声。

“顶容易把人引上歧路。”他说。

===Original===

“That depends entirely on one’s judgment, or rather on one’s instinct for distinguishing bad eggs from good. As a matter of observation I don’t find that certain types of men and women commit certain actions, and I do find that they are apt to commit others. And contrariwise with other types.”

“Very unsafe doctrine,” said Simon emphatically.

“Extremely—in the hands of anyone who doesn’t know how to apply it. On the other hand, it can be made a short and commonsense cut to the truth in many cases. For instance, the man who suspected Mr. Bisset of committing the crime would simply be wasting his time and energy, even if there seemed to be some evidence against him.”

“Any man can commit any crime,” said Simon dogmatically.

Carrington smiled and shook his head.

===Chinese===

“那得全看各人的判断力，说得更确切些，全看各人分辨好蛋坏蛋的直觉。就我平日的观察而论，某一类的男人女人，我不曾见他们犯下某几种行径，倒分明见他们容易犯下另外几种；另一类人则恰恰相反。”

“非常不稳妥的论调。”西蒙加重语气说。

“极其不稳妥——只要落在不会运用它的人手里。反过来说，在许多案子里，它也可以成为一条直抵真相的常识捷径。譬如说，谁若疑心比塞先生（Mr. Bisset）犯下本案，那他纵然手头似乎握着几分不利于比塞的证据，也不过是白白浪费时间和气力。”

“什么人什么罪都犯得出来。”西蒙武断地说。

卡林顿含笑摇头。

===Original===

“Personally,” said he, “if you had a young and pretty wife, I am capable of running away with her, and possibly even of letting her persuade me to abscond with some of your property, but I am not capable of laying you out in cold blood and rifling that safe. And a good judge of men ought to be able to perceive this and not waste his time in trying to convict me of an offence I couldn’t commit. On the other hand, if the crime was one that my type is apt to commit he would be a fool to acquit me offhand, even if there was next to no evidence against me.”

“Then you simply go by your impressions of people?”

“Far from it. A complete absence of motive would force me to acquit even the most promising looking blackguard, unless of course there were some form of lunacy in his case. One must have motive and one must have evidence as well, but character is the shortcut—if the circumstances permit you to use it. Sometimes of course they don’t, but in this case they force me to depend on it very largely. Therefore I want to see Sir Malcolm Cromarty.”

===Chinese===

“拿我本人来说，”他说，“倘若您有一位年轻貌美的太太，我拐着她私奔，是干得出来的；甚而至于经不住她一撺掇，卷走您一笔财产一道远走高飞，也说不定。可是要我冷血地把您撂倒，再洗劫那只保险柜，我万万干不出来。一位善相人的行家理应看得出这一点，不至于把光阴浪费在给我罗织一桩我根本犯不下的罪名上。反过来，倘若罪行恰是我这一路人容易犯的，那他若不问青红皂白便还我清白，便是十足的傻子——哪怕落到我头上的证据几近于无。”

“这么说，您干脆就凭对人的印象行事？”

“绝非如此。只要动机全然阙如，纵然是那个看上去最有指望的恶棍，我也只得开脱——当然，除非他案中牵扯着某种疯癫。动机不可无，证据亦不可缺；然而品性是一条捷径——只要情势容你走这条路。当然，情势有时并不容许；可本案的情势，逼得我在很大程度上只能仰仗它。所以，我要见马尔科姆·克罗玛蒂爵士。”

===Original===

The lawyer shook his head.

“No, no, Mr. Carrington,” he said, “I can’t bring him down here on such trivial grounds.”

“But you yourself suspect him!”

For a moment the lawyer was silent.

===Chinese===

律师摇了摇头。

“不行，不行，卡林顿先生，”他说，“凭这样鸡毛蒜皮的理由，我可不能把他召到这乡下来。”

“可您自己就在疑心他呀！”

律师半晌没有作声。

===Original===

“I think suspicion points to him; but what is wanted is *evidence*. You can’t get evidence merely by bringing him here. You don’t suppose he will confess, do you?”

“Have you ever studied the French methods of getting at the truth?” enquired Carrington, and when Simon shook his head contemptuously, he added with some significance: “We can learn a good deal from our neighbours.”

“Trivial grounds!” muttered Simon. “No, no!”

===Chinese===

“我认为嫌疑的矛头确是指向他；可眼下要的是*证据*。把他召到这儿来，证据并不会凭空到手。您总不至于指望他招供吧？”

“您可曾研究过法国人探求真相的手段？”卡林顿问道。见西蒙不屑地摇了摇头，他便意味深长地补上一句：“从咱们的邻居身上，可以学到不少东西。”

“鸡毛蒜皮的理由！”西蒙喃喃道，“不行，不行！”

===Original===

Carrington became unusually serious and impressive.

“I am investigating this case, Mr. Rattar, and I want to see Sir Malcolm. Will you send for him or not?”

“He wouldn’t come.”

“It depends on the urgency of the message.”

===Chinese===

卡林顿忽然变得异乎寻常地严肃，一派郑重其事。

“我正在查办这桩案子，拉塔先生。我要见马尔科姆爵士。您到底肯不肯替我把他召来？”

“他不肯来的。”

“那得看口信报得有多急。”

===Original===

“I can’t invent bogus urgent messages to my clients.”

Carrington smiled.

“I might do the inventing for you.”

===Chinese===

“总不能捏造假急电去诓我的当事人。”

卡林顿微微一笑。

“捏造的事，也许我能代劳。”

===Original===

Again the lawyer stared at him and again there was the same extreme caution in his eye, mingled with a hint of suspicion.

“I’ll think about it,” he said.

“I want to see him immediately.”

“Call again tomorrow morning.”

Carrington’s manner altered at once into his usual easygoing air.

“Very well, then, Mr. Rattar,” said he as he rose.

===Chinese===

律师又一次死死盯住他，眼里又是那般极度的戒备，还掺着一丝疑心。

“这事我考虑考虑。”他说。

“我要立刻见到他。”

“明天上午再来吧。”

卡林顿的做派立时一变，恢复了平日那副悠然自得的神气。

“那么好吧，拉塔先生。”他说着站起身来。

===Original===

“By the way,” said Simon, “you have been out at Keldale this morning, I presume?”

“Yes,” said Carrington carelessly, “but there is really nothing new to be found.”

Simon looked at him hard.

“No fresh evidence?”

Carrington laughed.

“Not likely, after you and your sleuth hounds had been over the ground!”

===Chinese===

“顺便问一句，”西蒙说，“今天上午，您出门去凯尔代尔（Keldale）了吧？”

“是啊，”卡林顿漫不经心地应道，“不过那地方实在搜不出什么新东西了。”

西蒙狠狠地盯着他。

“没有新的证据？”

卡林顿笑了起来。

“您和您那班缉凶猎犬早把地界踏了个遍，还能指望有什么！”

===Original===

He went to the door, and there Simon again spoke.

“What are you doing next?”

“Upon my word, I am rather wondering. I must think about it. Good morning.”

For a man who was rather wondering, Mr. Carrington’s next movements were remarkably prompt.

===Chinese===

他向门口走去，西蒙却又开了口。

“您下一步做什么？”

“说句实在话，我自己还真在纳闷呢。得好好想一想。再会。”

就一个“正自纳闷”的人来说，卡林顿先生接下来的动作未免利落得出奇。

===Original===

He first went straight to the Post Office and dispatched a wire. It was addressed to Sir Malcolm Cromarty and it ran—“Come immediately urgent news don’t answer please don’t delay.” The only thing that seemed to indicate a wondering and abstracted mind was the signature to this message. Instead of “Carrington” he actually wrote “Cicely Farmond.”

He then hurried to the hotel, which he reached at one-fifty. In ten minutes he had bolted a hasty lunch and at two o’clock was sitting in the car again.

“To Stanesland Castle,” he commanded. “And be as quick as you can.”

===Chinese===

他先是一径奔到邮局，拍出一封电报。电报打给马尔科姆·克罗玛蒂爵士，电文是：“速来有急事勿复勿延”。唯一显出拍报人心思恍惚、神不守舍的，是这封电报的署名：落款写的竟不是“卡林顿”，而是“西塞莉·法蒙德（Cicely Farmond）”。

随后他匆匆赶回旅馆，进门时已是一点五十分。十分钟里，他囫囵吞下一顿匆促的午饭；两点整，人又坐回了汽车。

“去斯坦斯兰城堡（Stanesland Castle）。”他吩咐道，“能开多快就开多快。”
