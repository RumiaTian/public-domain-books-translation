## VI / 夜里

===Original===
When Simon Rattar came to his present villa, he brought from his old house in the middle of the town (which had been his father’s before him) a vast accumulation of old books and old papers. Being a man who never threw away an opportunity or anything else, and also a person of the utmost tidyness, he compromised by keeping this litter in the spare rooms at the top of the house. In fact Simon was rather pleased at discovering this use for his superfluous apartments, for he hated wasting anything.

On this same morning, just before he started for his office, he had again called his housemaid and given her particular injunctions that these rooms were not to be disturbed during the day. He added that this was essential because he expected a gentleman that evening who would be going through some of the old papers with him.

Perhaps it was the vague feeling of disquiet which possessed Mary MacLean this morning that made his injunction seem a little curious. She had been with the master three years and never presumed or dreamt of presuming to touch his papers. He might have known that, thought she, without having to tell her not to. Indeed, she felt a little aggrieved at the command, and in the course of the morning she made a discovery that seemed to her a further reflection on her discretion.

===Chinese===
西蒙·拉塔（Simon Rattar）搬进如今这座别墅时，把老宅——镇中心那幢、先前归他父亲所有的房子——里积攒如山的旧书旧纸也一并搬了过来。他这个人从不丢弃机会，也从不丢弃任何别的东西，偏偏又是个极爱整洁的人，于是折中行事，把这一堆破烂安置在宅子顶层的空房间里。说实在的，西蒙发现自己这些多余的房间竟能派上这般用场，心里颇为得意，因为他最恨浪费。

就在这天早晨，动身去事务所之前，他又一次唤来女仆，特地叮嘱这些房间白天一概不许惊动。他还补了一句，说这一层至关重要，因为今晚有位先生要来，将同他一道翻检其中一些旧文件。

也许正是这天早晨缠住玛丽·麦克莱恩（Mary MacLean）的那股莫名不安，才使得主人这道叮嘱显得有几分古怪。她伺候主人已经三年，从来不敢，连想也不曾想过，要去碰他的文件。这等事他本该心里有数，她想，何须特意叮嘱。她确实为这道命令感到有点委屈，而上午忙碌之间她又有一桩发现，在她看来，那更是对她分寸感的再次质疑。

===Original===
When she came to dust the passage in which these rooms opened her eye was at once caught by a sheet of white paper pinned to each of the three doors. On each of these sheets was written in her master’s hand the words “This room not to be entered. Papers to be undisturbed.” The result was a warning to those who take superfluous precautions. Under ordinary circumstances Mary would never have thought of touching the handles of those doors. Now, she looked at them for a few moments and then tried the handle nearest to her. The door was locked. She tried the second and the third, and they stood locked too. And the three keys had all been removed.

“To think of the master locking the doors!” said she to herself after failing at each in turn. “As if I’d have tried to open them!”

That top storey was of the semi-attic kind, with roofs that sloped and a skylight in one of them and the slates close overhead. It was a grey windy morning, and as she stood there, alone in that large house save for the cook far away in the kitchen, with a loose slate rattling in the gusts, and a glimpse of clouds driving over the skylight, she began all at once to feel uncomfortable. Those locked doors were uncanny—something was not as it should be; there was a sinister moan in the wind; the slate did not rattle quite like an ordinary slate. Tales of her childhood, tales from the superstitious western islands, rushed into her mind. And then, all at once, she heard another sound. She heard it but for one instant, and then with a pale face she fled downstairs and stood for a space in the hall trembling and wondering.

===Chinese===
等她掸扫那几间房所在的那条走廊时，目光立刻被吸引住了——三扇门上各钉着一张白纸。每张纸上都是主人的笔迹：“此室不得入内。文件不得惊动。”其结果，恰是对那些多此一举者的警告。换作平常，玛丽做梦也不会想到去碰那些门的把手。可这一回，她盯着看了半晌，随即试了试离她最近的那扇。门锁着。她又试第二扇、第三扇，也都锁得死死的。而且三把钥匙统统不在了。

“瞧瞧，主人居然把门锁上了！”一扇扇试过去都打不开之后，她暗自嘀咕，“倒像我真会去开门似的！”

那顶层是半阁楼式的，屋顶倾斜，其中一面坡上开着天窗，石板瓦低压在头顶近处。那是个灰暗多风的早晨。她站在那里，偌大一座宅子只她一个人，厨娘远远地在底下厨房里；狂风一阵阵刮得一块松动的石板咔嗒作响，天窗上有云疾驰而过。她忽然觉得浑身不对劲起来。那几扇锁着的门透着邪性——有什么地方不对头；风里含着一缕阴惨的呜咽；那石板的响动也不大像寻常石板。儿时听来的故事，那些来自迷信盛行的西部海岛的故事，一齐涌上心头。紧接着，她猛地又听见一个声响。只听得一瞬，她便脸色煞白地奔下楼梯，在大厅里站了半晌，浑身发抖，满腹疑猜。

===Original===
She wondered first whether the sound had really come from behind the locked doors, and whether it actually was someone stealthily moving. She wondered next whether she could bring herself to confide in cook and stand Janet’s cheerful scorn. She ended by saying not a word, and waiting to see what happened when the master came home.

He returned as usual in time for a cup of tea. It was pretty dark by then and Mary was upstairs lighting the gas (but she did not venture up to the top floor). She heard Mr. Rattar come into the hall, and then, quite distinctly this time, she heard overhead a dull sound, a kind of gentle thud. The next moment she heard the master running upstairs, and when he was safely past she ran even more swiftly down and burst into the kitchen.

“There’s something in yon top rooms!” she panted.

“There’s something in your top storey!” snapped cook; and poor Mary said no more.

===Chinese===
她先是琢磨，那声音究竟是不是真的从锁着的门后传出来的，里面是不是当真有人在蹑手蹑脚地走动。接着又盘算，自己是否鼓得起勇气对厨娘吐露实情，受不受得住珍妮特（Janet）那毫不留情的嘲弄。末了她一个字也没说，只等着瞧主人回来会有什么动静。

他照例赶在喝茶的钟点回来。那时天色已经相当黑了，玛丽正在楼上点煤气灯（可顶层她是无论如何不敢再上去的）。她听见拉塔先生走进大厅，紧接着，这一回听得真真切切，头顶上响起一记闷声，像是什么东西轻轻磕了一下。下一刻，她听见主人奔上楼去；等他一过，她跑得比他还快，一头冲进厨房。

“顶上那几间屋里有个什么东西！”她喘着气说。

“有东西的是你这颗脑袋！”厨娘抢白道；可怜的玛丽再没敢吭声。

===Original===
When she brought his tea in to Mr. Rattar, she seemed to read in his first glance at her the same expression that had disturbed her in the morning, and yet the next moment he was speaking in his ordinary grumpy, laconic way.

“Have you noticed rats in the house?” he asked.

“Rats, sir!” she exclaimed. “Oh, no, sir, I don’t think there are any rats.”

“I saw one just now,” he said. “If we see it again we must get some rat poison.”

So it had only been a rat! Mary felt vastly relieved; and yet not altogether easy. One could not venture to doubt the master, but it was a queer-like sound for a rat to make.

===Chinese===
她给拉塔先生端茶进去时，觉得他朝她投来的第一瞥里，带着早晨那副叫她心神不定的同样神情；可下一刻，他已经用平日那种阴沉寡言的腔调说起话来。

“屋子里你留意过老鼠没有？”他问。

“老鼠，先生！”她失声道，“哦，没有，先生，我想不会有老鼠的。”

“方才我就瞧见一只，”他说，“要是再瞧见，就得买点老鼠药了。”

原来只是只老鼠！玛丽大松了一口气；心里却仍不全然踏实。主人的话自然不敢疑，只是老鼠能弄出这样的动静，未免太怪了。

===Original===
Mr. Rattar had brought back a great many papers today, and sat engrossed in them till dinner. After dinner he fell to work again, and then about nine o’clock he rang for her and said:

“The gentleman I expect this evening will probably be late in coming. Don’t sit up. I’ll hear him and let him in myself. We shall be working late and I shall be going upstairs about those papers. If you hear anybody moving about, it will only be this gentleman and myself.”

This was rather a long speech for silent Simon, and Mary thought it considerate of him to explain any nocturnal sounds beforehand; unusually considerate, in fact, for he seldom went out of his way to explain things. And yet those few minutes in his presence made her uncomfortable afresh. She could not keep her eyes away from that red cut on his chin. It made him seem odd-like, she thought. And then as she passed through the hall she heard faintly from the upper regions that slate rattling again. At least it was either the slate or—she recalled a story of her childhood, and hurried on to the kitchen.

===Chinese===
拉塔先生今天带回来好大一摞文件，埋头看得入神，直看到晚饭。饭后他又接着用功；九点钟上下，他摇铃唤她，说道：

“今晚我候的那位先生多半会来得很迟。不用守夜等门。他来了我自己听得见，自己去开门。我们要办事办到很晚，我还得上楼去取那些文件。夜里要是听见有人走动，不过是我和这位先生罢了。”

对沉默的西蒙（Silent Simon）来说，这一番话可算够长的了。玛丽心想，他事先把夜里的动静交代清楚，可谓体贴；说实话，体贴得出奇，他素来是不肯多费口舌解释什么的。然而在他跟前待着的这几分钟，又让她重新不自在起来。她的眼睛怎么也躲不开他下巴上那道红红的伤口。那让他瞧着有几分怪样，她想。随后她穿过大厅时，又隐隐听得楼上那块石板咔嗒作响。至少，不是石板就是——她记起儿时听过的一个故事，赶紧一路小跑进了厨房。

===Original===
She and the cook shared the same bedroom. It was fairly large with two beds in it, and along with the kitchen and other back premises it was shut off from the front part of the house by a door at the end of the hall. Cook was asleep within ten minutes. Mary could hear her heavy breathing above the incessant droning and whistling of the wind, and she envied her with all her Highland heart. In her own glen people would have understood how she felt, but here she dared not confess lest she were laughed at. It was such a vague and nameless feeling, a sixth sense warning her that all was not well; that *something* was in the air. The longer she lay awake the more certain she grew that evil was afoot; and yet what could be its shape? Everything in that quiet and respectable household was going on exactly as usual; everything that anyone else would have considered material. The little things she had noticed would be considered absurd trifles by the sensible. She knew that as well as they.

She thought she had been in bed about an hour, though the time passed so slowly that it might have been less, when she heard, faintly and gently, but quite distinctly, the door from the hall into the back premises being opened. It seemed to be held open for nearly a minute, as though someone were standing there listening. She moved a little and the bed creaked; and then, as gently as it had been opened, the door was closed again.

Had the intruder come through or gone away? And could it only be the master, doing this curious thing, or was it someone—or something—else? Dreadful minutes passed, but there was not a sound of anyone moving in the back passage, or the kitchen, and then in the distance she could hear the grating noise of the front door being opened and the rush of wind that accompanied it. It was closed sharply in a moment and she could catch the sound of steps in the hall and the master’s voice making some remark. Another voice replied, gruff and muffled and indistinct, and then again the master spoke. Evidently the late caller had arrived, and a moment later she heard the library door shut, and it was plain that he and Mr. Rattar were closeted there.

===Chinese===
她和厨娘同住一间寝室。屋子相当宽敞，摆着两张床，连同厨房和其他后屋一起，靠大厅尽头的一扇门，同宅子的前半截隔离开来。不出十分钟，厨娘便睡熟了。风声不住地呜呜呼啸，玛丽却能听见她粗重的鼾息，一颗高地人的心把她羡慕得不行。要是在她自己那道山谷里，人们准能懂得她此刻的心情；可在这儿，她不敢吐露，生怕招人笑话。那是一种多么飘忽而无以名状的感觉，一种第六感，警告她事态不妙；空气里头有**某种**东西。睁着眼躺的时间越久，她越确信有祸事在暗中酝酿；可它会是什么模样呢？这座安静体面的宅子，一切都进行得同往常分毫不差；凡是旁人会看作要紧的事，无一不照常运转。她留意到的那些小节，在有见识的人眼里，不过是可笑的琐屑罢了。这一点，她同他们一样清楚。

她估摸自己上床约莫一个钟头了——虽说时间过得那样慢，兴许还不到——忽听得大厅通后屋的那扇门开了，声音轻缓，却分外清晰。那门像是敞着停了将近一分钟，仿佛有人立在那儿侧耳细听。她微微一动，床架咯吱一响；随即，那门又同开时一样轻缓地掩上了。

来的是穿过去了，还是退走了？那当真只能是主人，做着这桩古怪的事，还是别的什么人——或者别的什么东西？难熬的几分钟挨过去，后过道里、厨房里却听不见半点人声动静；接着，远处传来前门开启时干涩刺耳的声响，还夹着一股灌进来的风。片刻之间门又砰地关严，她听得见大厅里的脚步声，还有主人开口说了句什么。另一个嗓音应答着，粗嘎、发闷、含混不清，随后主人又开口。显然，那位迟来的客人到了；片刻之后，书房门一响，分明是他同拉塔先生一起关在里头了。

===Original===
They seemed to remain in the library about a quarter of an hour before the door opened again, and in a moment the stairs were creaking faintly. Evidently one or both were going up for the old papers.

All this was exactly what she had been led to expect, and ought to have reassured her, yet, for no reason at all, the conviction remained as intense and disturbing as ever, that something unspeakable was happening in this respectable house. The minutes dragged by till quite half an hour must have passed, and then she heard the steps descending. They came down very slowly this time, and very heavily. The obvious explanation was that they were bringing down one of those boxes filled with dusty papers which she had often seen in the closed rooms; yet though Mary knew perfectly that this was the common sense of the matter, a feeling of horror increased till she could scarcely refrain from crying out. If cook had not such a quick temper and such a healthy contempt for this kind of fancy, she would have rushed across to her bed; but as it was, she simply lay and trembled.

The steps sounded still heavy but more muffled on the hall carpet, though whether they were the steps of one man or two she could not feel sure. And then she heard the front door open again and then close; so that it seemed plain that the visitor had taken the box with him and gone away. And with this departure came a sense of relief, as devoid of rational foundation as the sense of horror before. She felt at last that if she could only hear the master going upstairs to bed, she might go to sleep.

两人在书房里似乎待了约莫一刻钟，门才再度打开，不多时，楼梯上传来轻微的咯吱声。显然是两人中的一个，或者两个，上楼取旧文件去了。

这一切原在她预想之中，本该让她安心才对；然而毫无来由地，那个信念依然强烈得搅人心神：这座体面宅子里正发生着一件说不出口的事。时间一寸寸挨过去，足有半个钟头了，然后她听见脚步下楼。这一回下得极慢，也极沉。显而易见的解释是，他们正抬着一只装满积尘文件的箱子下来——那样的箱子她在那几间锁着的屋里见过不止一回；尽管玛丽心里明明白白，这正是事情最寻常不过的道理，一股恐怖之感却有增无已，直到她几乎忍不住要喊出声来。倘若厨娘不是那样急性子、对这一类玄想又不是抱着那样健康的轻蔑，她早就冲过去钻进她的被窝了；可是眼下，她只能干躺着发抖。

脚步声依旧沉重，踏在大厅的地毯上，却愈发发闷；只是究竟是一个人的脚步还是两个人的，她拿捏不准。随后她听见前门又开了，又关上；看来明明白白，是客人带着箱子走了。随着这一走，一阵如释重负之感涌了上来，同先前那阵恐怖一样毫无理性的根据。她终于觉得，只要再听见主人上楼安寝，她便能睡着了。

But though she listened hard as she lay there in the oppressive dark, she heard not another sound so long as she kept awake, and that was for some time, she thought. She did get off at last and had been asleep she knew not how long when she awoke drowsily with a confused impression that the front door had been shut again. How late it was she could but guess—about three or four in the morning her instinct told her. But then came sleep again and in the morning the last part of her recollections was a little uncertain.

At breakfast the master was as silently formidable as ever and he never said a word about his visitor. When Mary went to the top floor later the papers were off the doors and the keys replaced.

早饭桌上，主人一如既往地沉默得叫人望而生畏，对那位来客只字未提。过后玛丽再上顶层时，门上的字条已经揭去，钥匙也各归了原位。
