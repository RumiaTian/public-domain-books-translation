## XXVI A Letter and Its Consequences / 二十六 一封信及其后果

===Original===
> “Squar Haukins
> “this is too Lett u no that u beter be Keerful hoo yoo an yore familly tacks cides with fer peepl wont Stan it too hev the Men wat’s sportin the wuns wat’s robin us, sported bi yor Fokes kepin kumpne with ’em, u been a ossifer ov the Lau, yor Ha wil bern as qick as to an yor Barn tu. so Tak kere. No mor ad pressent.”

===Chinese===
> 「霍金斯治安官：
> 「写这封信是告诉你，你跟你一家子人到底跟啥人站一头，顶好给我当心着点。那个跟抢俺们的人穿一条裤子的家伙，叫你家的人抬举着、厮混着，乡亲们可不答应就这么下去。你是当过执法官的人，你家干草烧起来跟麻絮一样快，连谷仓也跑不了。所以自个儿掂量。多余的话不写了。」

===Original===
This letter accomplished its purpose. The Squire’s spectacles slipped off several times while he read it. His wig had to be adjusted. If he had been threatened personally he would not have minded it so much. But the hay stacks were dearer to him than the apple of his glass eye. The barn was more precious than his wig. And those who hoped to touch Bud in a tender place through this letter knew the Squire’s weakness far better than they knew the spelling-book. To see his new red barn with its large “Mormon” hay-press inside, and the mounted Indian on the vane, consumed, was too much for the Hawkins heart to stand. Evidently the danger was on the side of his niece. But how should he influence Martha to give up Bud? Martha did not value the haystacks half so highly as she did her lover. Martha did not think the new red barn, with the great Mormon press inside and the galloping Indian on the vane, worth half so much as a moral principle or a kindhearted action. Martha, bless her! would have sacrificed anything rather than forsake the poor. But Squire Hawkins’s lips shut tight over his false teeth in a way that suggested astringent purse-strings, and Squire Hawkins could not sleep at night if the new red barn, with the galloping Indian on the vane, were in danger. Martha must be reached somehow.

===Chinese===
这封信达到了目的。治安官读信的时候，眼镜滑下来好几回，假发也只得扶了又扶。倘若威胁的是他本人，他倒不至于这么放在心上。可那些干草垛，在他眼里比那只玻璃假眼的眼珠还金贵；谷仓又比他的假发贵重得多。想借这封信往巴德的痛处戳的人，对治安官的软处可比对拼写课本熟得多。眼睁睁瞧着那座新红谷仓——里头架着大型「摩门」压草机，风向标上骑着个印第安人——付之一炬，霍金斯家的心可受不了。明摆着，危险出在他侄女这一头。可他怎么才能叫玛莎撒开巴德呢？在玛莎眼里，干草垛抵不上她心上人的一半分量；那座新红谷仓——连同仓里的大摩门压草机、风向标上飞奔的印第安人——也抵不上一条道义、一件善举的一半。玛莎，老天保佑这好姑娘！她什么都舍得牺牲，就是舍不得撇下穷人。可是霍金斯治安官的嘴唇在假牙上抿得紧紧的，那副样子活像勒紧了的荷包绳；只要新红谷仓——连同风向标上那个飞奔的印第安人——有个闪失，他夜里就睡不成觉。无论如何，总得想个法子说动玛莎。

===Original===
So, with many adjustings of that most adjustable wig, with many turnings of that reversible glass eye, the Squire managed to frighten Martha by the intimation that he had been threatened, and to make her understand, what it cost her much to understand, that she must turn the cold shoulder to chivalrous, awkward Bud, whom she loved most tenderly, partly, perhaps, because he did not remind her of anybody she had ever known at the East.

Tuesday evening was the fatal time. Spelling-school was the fatal occasion. Bud was the victim. Pete Jones had his revenge. For Bud had been all the evening trying to muster courage enough to offer himself as Martha’s escort. He was not encouraged by the fact that he had spelled even worse than usual, while Martha had distinguished herself by holding her ground against Jeems Phillips for half an hour. But he screwed his courage to the sticking place, not by quoting to himself the adage, “Faint heart never won fair lady,” which, indeed, he had never heard, but by reminding himself that “ef you don’t resk nothin’ you’ll never git nothin’.” So, when the spelling-school had adjourned, he sidled up to her, and, looking dreadfully solemn and a little foolish, he said:

“Kin I see you safe home?”

And she, with a feeling that her uncle’s life was in danger, and that his salvation depended upon her resolution—she, with a feeling that she was pronouncing sentence of death on her own great hope, answered huskily:

“No, I thank you.”

If she had only known that it was the red barn with the Indian on top that was in danger, she would probably have let the galloping brave take care of himself.

It seemed to Bud, as he walked home mortified, disgraced, disappointed, hopeless, that all the world had gone down in a whirlpool of despair.

“Might a knowed it,” he said to himself. “Of course, a smart gal like Martha a’n’t a-goin’ to take a big, blunderin’ fool that can’t spell in two syllables. What’s the use of tryin’? A Flat Cricker is a Flat Cricker. You can’t make nothin’ else of him, no more nor you can make a Chiny hog into a Berkshire.”

===Chinese===
于是，治安官把那顶顶好摆弄的假发摆弄了又摆弄，把那只两面都能转的玻璃眼转了又转，总算借着自己受了恐吓的口风吓住了玛莎，也叫她明白了——她明白这个可费了大劲——她必须给那位侠气而笨拙的巴德冷脸子。她爱巴德爱得极温柔，也许一部分缘故，正在于巴德不像她在东部认得的任何人。

星期二晚上是那个要命的时刻，拼字学校是那个要命的场合，巴德是受害的人，皮特·琼斯报了他的仇。原来巴德一整晚都在攒劲，想攒足勇气提出送玛莎回家。偏偏这一晚他拼得比平时还糟，玛莎却大出风头，跟吉姆斯·菲利普斯顶了半个钟头不落下阵——这可没法给他壮胆。可他还是把勇气拧到了螺丝拧紧的地步，不是靠默念那句格言「懦夫难赢美人心」——这话他实在压根没听过——而是靠提醒自己：「啥险都不敢冒，就啥也捞不着。」于是拼字学校散场后，他蹭到她跟前，神情庄重得吓人，又带着点傻气，说道：

「俺能送你平安到家不？」

而她呢，一心只觉得叔叔性命堪虞、他的安危全看她这会儿的决心——她，怀着一种正给自己心头大希望宣判死刑的感觉，嗓音发哑地答道：

「不，谢谢你。」

她要是早知道遭殃的不过是屋顶上立着印第安人的红谷仓，多半就由着那个飞奔的勇士自求多福了。

巴德一路回家，又臊又丢脸，又失望又绝望，只觉得整个世界都沉进了一座绝望的漩涡里。

「早该想到喽，」他自言自语，「像玛莎这么伶俐的姑娘，咋会要俺这么个大个子笨蛋，连两个音节的词都拼不来。还试个啥劲？弗拉特克里克人天生就是弗拉特克里克人，咋也变不成别的，就跟中国猪变不成巴克夏猪一个理。」
