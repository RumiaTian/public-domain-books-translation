## XV The Church of the Best Licks / 十五 最卖力气的教会

===Original===
Just as the flame on the forestick, which Ralph had watched so intensely, flickered and burned low, and just as Ralph with a heavy but not quite hopeless heart rose to leave, the latch lifted and Bud reentered.

“I wanted to say something,” he stammered, “but you know it’s hard to say it. I ha’n’t no book-larnin to speak of, and some things is hard to say when a man ha’n’t got book-words to say ’em with. And they’s some things a man can’t hardly ever say anyhow to anybody.”

Here Bud stopped. But Ralph spoke in such a matter-of-course way in reply that he felt encouraged to go on.

===Chinese===
正当拉尔夫凝神望着的那根炉口柴的火苗摇曳着、渐渐烧低，正当他怀着沉重却还不算全然绝望的心起身要走时，门闩一抬，巴德又进来了。

「俺有句话想说，」他结结巴巴地说，「可你知道，这话难说出口。俺肚里没什么书本学问好提的，一个人没攒下书本上的词儿，有些事就是说不出口。再说，有些事一个人不管咋样，都几乎没法对任何人说。」

巴德说到这儿停住了。可拉尔夫答话的口吻那么自然而然，让他受了鼓舞，又接着说了下去。

===Original===
“You gin up Hanner kase you thought she belonged to me. That’s more’n I’d a done by a long shot. Now, arter I left here jest now, I says to myself, a man what can gin up his gal on account of sech a feeling fer the rights of a Flat Cricker like me, why, dog-on it, says I, sech a man is the man as can help me do better. I don’t know whether you’re a Hardshell or a Saftshell, or a Methodist, or a Campbellite, or a New Light, or a United Brother, or a Millerite, or whatnot. But I says, the man what can do the clean thing by a ugly feller like me, and stick to it, when I was jest ready to eat him up, is a kind of a man to tie to.”

Here Bud stopped in fright at his own volubility, for he had run his words off like a piece learned by heart, as though afraid that if he stopped he would not have courage to go on.

Ralph said that he did not belong to any church, and he was afraid he couldn’t do Bud much good. But his tone was full of sympathy, and, what is better than sympathy, a yearning for sympathy.

===Chinese===
「你把汉娜让了出来，是因为你寻思她是俺的人。这可比俺能做出来的强出老鼻子了。刚才俺打这儿走了以后，俺跟自己说：一个人能凭着对俺这么个弗拉特溪佬的名分的那点儿心意，就把自己的姑娘让出来——嘿，见鬼，俺说，这样的人才是能帮俺学好的人。俺不知道你是硬壳派（Hardshell）还是软壳派，是卫理公会的，还是坎伯尔派的、新光派的、联合弟兄会的、米勒派的，还是别的什么派。可俺说，一个人能对俺这么个丑家伙干出这么地道漂亮的事，还能咬定了不松口——那会儿俺正恨不得一口吞了他——这样的人，就是可以死心塌地拴上去跟定的人。」

说到这里，巴德被自己的滔滔不绝吓了一跳，停了下来。原来他这一番话是像背书似的冲口倾泻的，仿佛生怕一停下来，便再没有勇气说下去。

拉尔夫说，他自己不属于任何教会，而且怕帮不上巴德什么忙。可他语气里满是同情，而且——比同情更好的是——满是对同情的渴求。

===Original===
“You see,” said Bud, “I wanted to git out of this low-lived, Flat Crick way of livin’. We’re a hard set down here, Mr. Hartsook. And I’m gittin’ to be one of the hardest of ’em. But I never could git no good out of Bosaw with his whisky and meanness. And I went to the Mount Tabor church concert. I heard a man discussin’ baptism, and regeneration, and so on. That didn’t seem no cure for me, I went to a revival over at Clifty. Well, ’twarn’t no use. First night they was a man that spoke about Jesus Christ in sech a way that I wanted to foller him everywhere. But I didn’t feel fit. Next night I come back with my mind made up that I’d try Jesus Christ, and see ef he’d have me. But laws! they was a big man that night that preached hell. Not that I don’t believe they’s a hell. They’s plenty not a thousand miles away as deserves it, and I don’t know as I’m too good for it myself. But he pitched it at us, and stuck it in our faces in sech a way that I got mad. And I says, Well, ef God sends me to hell he can’t make me holler ’nough nohow. You see my dander was up. And when my dander’s up, I wouldn’t gin up fer the devil his-self. The preacher was so insultin’ with his way of doin’ it. He seemed to be kind of glad that we was to be damned, and he preached somethin’ like some folks swears. It didn’t sound a bit like the Christ the little man preached about the night afore. So what does me and a lot of fellers do but slip out and cut off the big preacher’s stirrups, and hang ’em on to the rider of the fence, and then set his hoss loose! And from that day, sometimes I did, and sometimes I didn’t, want to be better. And today it seemed to me that you must know somethin’ as would help me.”

===Chinese===
「你瞧，」巴德说，「俺想跳出这种下三滥的弗拉特溪活法。俺们这一带的人都是些硬茬儿，哈特索克先生，而俺眼看要变成硬茬儿里最硬的一个。可俺从博索和他的威士忌跟那下三滥的做派里，从来榨不出半点儿好处。俺去听过他泊山教堂的礼拜。俺听一个人讨论洗礼呀、重生呀这一套。那玩意儿治不了俺的病。俺又到克利夫蒂那边赶了一场奋兴布道会。嘿，也没用。头天晚上有个人讲耶稣基督，讲得俺恨不得跟着他走遍天涯。可俺觉得自己不配。第二天晚上俺又去了，拿定了主意要试一试耶稣基督，看祂肯不肯要俺。可是老天爷！那晚来了个大人物，讲地狱。倒不是俺不信有地狱。离俺不出一千英里远的地方，有的是该下地狱的，俺也不知道俺自己是不是就金贵得下不去。可他把那话朝俺们劈头砸过来，往俺们脸上戳，戳得俺火冒三丈。俺就说：哼，上帝要是送俺下地狱，他也休想叫俺喊一声饶。你瞧，俺的火气上来了。俺火一上来，就是魔鬼本尊来了俺也不让。那传道人干这事的手法太气人。他好像巴不得俺们下地狱似的，讲起道来跟有些人骂街一个样。听着一点儿也不像头天晚上那小个子讲的基督。于是你猜俺跟一帮伙计干了啥？俺们溜了出去，把那大个子传道人的马镫割了下来，挂到栅栏顶上那根横杆上，再把他的马撒了缰！打那天起，俺有时想学好，有时又不想了。今天俺寻思，你肚里准是有点儿什么，能帮得了俺。」

===Original===
Nothing is worse than a religious experience kept ready to be exposed to the gaze of everybody, whether the time is appropriate or not. But never was a religious experience more appropriate than the account which Ralph gave to Bud of his Struggle in the Dark. The confession of his weakness and wicked selfishness was a great comfort to Bud.

“Do you think that Jesus Christ would﻿—would﻿—well, do you think he’d help a poor, unlarnt Flat Cricker like me?”

“I think he was a sort of a Flat Creeker himself,” said Ralph, slowly and very earnestly.

“You don’t say?” said Bud, almost getting off his seat.

===Chinese===
再没有比一种不论时机合不合适、都随时准备摊开来给人看的宗教经验更糟糕的了。可也从来没有比这一次更合时宜的宗教经验——拉尔夫向巴德讲述了自己在黑暗中的挣扎。他对自己软弱和邪恶自私的坦白承认，对巴德是极大的安慰。

「你看耶稣基督会不会——会不会——呃，你看祂肯不肯帮俺这么个可怜的、没学问的弗拉特溪佬？」

「依我看，他自己就有点儿像个弗拉特溪人。」拉尔夫说得很慢，极其恳切。

「不会吧？」巴德说，差一点儿从座位上出溜下来。

===Original===
“Why, you see the town he lived in was a rough place. It was called Nazareth, which meant ‘Bush-town.’”

“You don’t say?”

“And he was called a Nazarene, which was about the same as ‘backwoodsman.’”

And Ralph read the different passages which he had studied at Sunday-school, illustrating the condescension of Jesus, the stories of the publicans, the harlots, the poor, who came to him. And he read about Nathanael, who lived only six miles away, saying, “‘Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?’”

“Jus’ what Clifty folks says about Flat Crick,” broke in Bud.

===Chinese===
「你看，他住的那座镇子本就是个粗地方，叫拿撒勒，意思就是『灌木镇』。」

「不会吧？」

「人家还管他叫拿撒勒人，这跟『边地林子里长大的人』差不多是一回事。」

拉尔夫便把他主日学里研读过的那些段落一一读来，讲的尽是耶稣如何俯就卑微：那些到他跟前来的税吏、妓女和穷苦人的故事。他还读到住在仅六英里外的拿但业说的话：「『拿撒勒还能出什么好的么？』」

「可不正是克利夫蒂人讲弗拉特溪的话么。」巴德插嘴道。

===Original===
“Do you think I could begin without being baptized?” he added presently.

“Why not? Let’s begin now to do the best we can, by his help.”

“You mean, then, that I’m to begin now to put in my best licks for Jesus Christ, and that he’ll help me?”

This shocked Ralph’s veneration a little. But it was the sincere utterance of an earnest soul. It may not have been an orthodox start, but it was the one start for Bud. And there be those who have repeated with the finest aesthetic appreciation the old English liturgies who have never known religious aspiration so sincere as that of this ignorant young Hercules, whose best confession was that he meant hereafter “to put in his best licks for Jesus Christ.” And there be those who can define repentance and faith to the turning of a hair who never made so genuine a start for the kingdom of Heaven as Bud Means did.

===Chinese===
「你看俺不受洗，能开这个头么？」过了一会儿他又问。

「为什么不能？咱们这就开始，靠祂的帮助，尽力做到最好吧。」

「那你是说，俺这就开始为耶稣基督出俺最卖力气的劲儿，祂还会帮俺？」

这话让拉尔夫的敬畏之心微微受了些震动。可它是一个恳切灵魂的真诚吐露。这或许算不得一个正统的开头，但对巴德来说，这就是那唯一的一个开头。世上有些人，能把古老的英国礼拜仪式吟诵得最具审美情趣，却从来不曾见识过像这个无知的年轻大力士这般真诚的宗教渴望——他最好的忏悔，就是今后打算「为耶稣基督出自己最卖力气的劲儿」。世上也有些人，能把悔改与信心定义到毫厘不爽，却从来不曾像巴德·米恩斯这样，为天国做过如此真切的一个开头。

===Original===
Ralph said yes, that he thought that was just it. At least, he guessed if there was something more, the man that was putting in his best licks would be sure to find it out.

“Do you think he’d help a feller? Seems to me it would be number one to have God help you. Not to help you fight other folks, but to help you when it comes to fighting the devil inside. But you see I don’t belong to no church.”

“Well, let’s you and me have one right off. Two people that help one another to serve God make a church.”

I am afraid this ecclesiastical theory will not be considered orthodox. It was Ralph’s, and I write it down at the risk of bringing him into condemnation.

But other people before the days of Bud and Ralph have discussed church organization when they should have been doing Christian work. For both of them had forgotten the danger that hung over the old basket-maker, until Shocky burst into the schoolhouse, weeping. Indeed, the poor, nervous little frame was ready to go into convulsions.

“Miss Hawkins﻿—”

Bud started at mention of the name.

“Miss Hawkins has just been over to say that a crowd is going to tar and feather Mr. Pearson tonight. And﻿—” here Shocky wept again. “And he won’t run, but he’s took up the old flintlock, and he’ll die in his tracks.”

===Chinese===
拉尔夫说是的，他想正是这么回事。至少，他估摸着，若是还有什么更进一步的东西，那个正在出最卖力气劲儿的人，准保自己就能找出来。

「你看祂肯帮一个伙计么？在俺想来，有上帝帮着你，那可是头一等的好事。不是帮你去打别人，是等你跟心里头的魔鬼开打的时候帮你。可你瞧，俺什么教会也不属于。」

「那好，咱们俩这就在这儿立一个。两个互相帮着事奉上帝的人，就是一个教会。」

恐怕这个教会理论不会被人视为正统。它是拉尔夫的理论，我把它记下来，甘冒把他也牵连受责的风险。

不过在巴德和拉尔德之前的年代，也早有人在应该做基督徒工夫的时候，去高谈教会组织。因为他们俩都把悬在老编篮匠头上的危险忘了个干净，直到肖基哭着冲进校舍。确实，那可怜的、神经质的小身板儿，眼看就要抽搐起来了。

「霍金斯小姐——」

一听到这个名字，巴德浑身一激灵。

「霍金斯小姐刚到这边来说，有一伙人今晚要给皮尔逊先生涂柏油、粘羽毛。还有——」说到这儿，肖基又哭了。「还有他不肯跑，他抄起了那杆老燧发枪，说要死就死在自己的地界上。」
