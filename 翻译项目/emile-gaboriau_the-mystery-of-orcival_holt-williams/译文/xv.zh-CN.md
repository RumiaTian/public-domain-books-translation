## XV / 第十五章
===Original===

On going to bed, that night, the count was less enchanted than ever with the devotion of his friend Sauvresy. There is not a diamond on which a spot cannot be found with a microscope.
“Here he is,” thought he, “abusing his privileges as the saver of my life. Can’t a man do you a service, without continually making you feel it? It seems as though because he prevented me from blowing my brains out, I had somehow become something that belongs to him! He came very near upbraiding me for Jenny’s extravagance. Where will he stop?”
The next day at breakfast he feigned indisposition so as not to eat, and suggested to Sauvresy that he would lose the train.
Bertha, as on the evening before, crouched at the window to see them go away. Her troubles during the past eight-and-forty hours had been so great that she hardly recognized herself. She scarcely dared to reflect or to descend to the depths of her heart. What mysterious power did this man possess, to so violently affect her life? She wished that he would go, never to return, while at the same time she avowed to herself that in going he would carry with him all her thoughts. She struggled under the charm, not knowing whether she ought to rejoice or grieve at the inexpressible emotions which agitated her, being irritated to submit to an influence stronger than her own will.
She decided that today she would go down to the drawing-room. He would not fail﻿—were it only for politeness﻿—to go in there; and then, she thought, by seeing him nearer, talking with him, knowing him better, his influence over her would vanish. Doubtless he would return, and so she watched for him, ready to go down as soon as she saw him approaching. She waited with feverish shudderings, anxiously believing that this first tête-à-tête in her husband’s absence would be decisive. Time passed; it was more than two hours since he had gone out with Sauvresy, and he had not reappeared. Where could he be?
At this moment, Hector was awaiting Jenny at the Corbeil station. The train arrived, and Jenny soon appeared. Her grief, joy, emotion had not made her forget her toilet, and never had she been so rollickingly elegant and pretty. She wore a green dress with a train, a velvet mantle, and the jauntiest little hat in the world.

当天夜里上床就寝之际，伯爵对自己挚友索夫勒齐的赤诚奉献，感受到的非但不再是感激，反倒是前所未有的心烦意乱。即便世间最纯净无瑕的钻石，在显微镜下也能挑出斑驳的瑕疵。
“你们瞧瞧他，”埃克托暗自寻思，“分明是在滥用救命恩人的特权。难道一个人施了恩惠，就非得时时刻刻让受恩者刻骨铭心地感受到这份压迫吗？仿佛就因为他拦着没让我崩碎脑袋，我整个人就莫名其妙成了他的私有附属品一样！他方才甚至差点就珍妮的奢靡挥霍当面数落我了。他究竟还要得寸进尺到何等地步？”
翌日共进早餐时，他故意装出身体抱恙的模样一口未动，还旁敲侧击提醒索夫勒齐莫要误了赶火车的时辰。
贝尔塔一如前夜那般，悄悄蜷缩在窗前目送着二人远去。在过去的整整48小时里，她胸中所饱受的心神动荡是如此剧烈，以至于连她自己都快认不出自己了。她甚至不敢静下心来反躬自省、不敢探入自己灵魂的深渊。这个男人究竟拥有何等勾魂摄魄的神秘魔力，竟能如此暴烈狂乱地撼动她的整个人生？她一方面巴不得他立刻拔腿远去、此生再莫踏足此地半步，可另一方面却又在心底无可救药地向自己承认：倘若他当真离去，必将带走她所有的神思与魂魄。她在这股无形的魔咒下苦苦挣扎，面对这股撕扯胸膛的莫名心悸，竟不知究竟该喜该悲；而屈从于一股凌驾于自身意志之上的狂暴牵引，更让她心头涌起了一股无名怒火。
她暗自拿定主意，今天一定要下楼去大客厅。哪怕纯粹出于礼节，他也绝不可能不踏入客厅；她心想，只要能离得更近一些端详他、同他搭话、更深入地了解他，他施加在自己身上的无形魔力势必烟消云散。他料想很快就会折返，于是她目不转睛地守候在窗前，随时准备一瞧见他的身影便步下楼梯。她在阵阵发热的战栗中苦苦守候，满心焦灼地认定，趁着丈夫不在府上的这初次独处幽会，必将成为定夺命运的关键时刻。时间飞速流逝；距离他同索夫勒齐出门已过去了整整2个多小时，他却始终没有现身。他究竟能去往何处？
而此时此刻，埃克托正在科尔贝伊火车站等候着珍妮的到来。列车缓缓进站，珍妮的身影很快便映入了眼帘。重重悲痛、狂喜与心潮澎湃，丝毫未能让她疏忽了自己的仪容装扮，她平生似乎从未展现出如此明艳欢脱、风情万种的优雅身姿。她身着一袭拖地翠绿长裙，外披一件天鹅绒斗篷，头顶斜戴着一顶全天下最俏皮别致的小礼帽。

As soon as she saw Hector standing near the door, she uttered a cry, pushed the people aside, and rushed into his arms, laughing and crying at the same time. She spoke quite loud, with wild gestures, so that everyone could hear what she said.
“You didn’t kill yourself, after all,” said she. “Oh, how I have suffered; but what happiness I feel today!”
Trémorel struggled with her as he could, trying to calm her enthusiastic exclamations, softly repelling her, charmed and irritated at once, and exasperated at all these eyes rudely fixed on him. For none of the passengers had gone out. They were all there, staring and gazing. Hector and Jenny were surrounded by a circle of curious folks.
“Come along,” said Hector, his patience exhausted. He drew her out of the door, hoping to escape this prying curiosity; but he did not succeed. They were persistently followed. Some of the Corbeil people who were on the top of the omnibus begged the conductor to walk his horses, that this singular couple might not be lost to view, and the horses did not get into a trot until they had disappeared in the hotel.
Sauvresy’s foresight in recommending the place of meeting had thus been disconcerted by Jenny’s sensational arrival. Questions were asked; the hostess was adroitly interrogated, and it was soon known that this person, who waited for eccentric young ladies at the Corbeil station, was an intimate friend of the owner of Valfeuillu. Neither Hector nor Jenny doubted that they formed the general topic of conversation. They breakfasted gayly in the best room at the Belle Image, during which Trémorel recounted a very pretty story about his restoration to life, in which he played a part, the heroism of which was well calculated to redouble the little lady’s admiration. Then Jenny in her turn unfolded her plans for the future, which were, to do her justice, most reasonable. She had resolved more than ever to remain faithful to Hector now that he was ruined, to give up her elegant rooms, sell her furniture, and undertake some honest trade. She had found one of her old friends, who was now an accomplished dressmaker, and who was anxious to obtain a partner who had some money, while she herself furnished the experience. They would purchase an establishment in the Breda quarter, and between them could scarcely fail to prosper.

刚一瞥见立在门边的埃克托，她便失声发出一声惊呼，一把推开挡路的人群，飞扑进他的怀抱，一边又哭又笑。她扯着嗓门大声呼喊，手舞足蹈，好让在场的所有人都能听清她的满腔肺腑：
“您到底还是没自尽成，”她嚷道，“天哪，这些日子我饱受了何等煎熬；可我今天又是何等快活啊！”
特雷莫雷尔手忙脚乱地应付着她，极力试图平息她那狂热奔放的惊呼，轻手轻脚地将她推开，心头既被她的真情打动又烦躁不堪，更被四周无数双粗暴聚焦在自己身上的直勾勾目光惹得无名火起。只因车厢里的乘客竟没有一个挪步离开，所有人全挤在原地目瞪口呆地看热闹。埃克托与珍妮被这群好事之徒团团围在核心。
“快跟我走，”埃克托耐性耗尽地低喝道。他拉扯着她冲出门外，一心渴望摆脱这群窥探的好奇目光；然而一切徒劳无功，人群亦步亦趋地尾随在后。坐在公共马车站顶座席上的几位科尔贝伊本地看客，甚至央求车夫把马车放慢步速，好不至于跟丢这对离奇古怪的旷世男女，直到二人彻底消失在旅馆大门内，车夫才重新甩响马鞭让马儿小跑起来。
索夫勒齐当初出于体贴挑选这一僻静碰头地点的先见之明，就这样被珍妮这场轰轰烈烈的盛大登场彻底搅黄了。闲言碎语迅速在坊间传开；女掌柜被人旁敲侧击套出了口风，不出多久世人便获悉：在科尔贝伊火车站与这位举止出格的风流女郎私会的贵介公子，乃是瓦尔菲伊庄园主人的莫逆之交。无论是埃克托还是珍妮，都毫不怀疑自己已然沦为了全镇茶余饭后的头条谈资。两人在靓影旅馆（Belle Image）最体面的一间客房里欢畅地共进早午餐，席间特雷莫雷尔编造了一套娓娓动听的死里逃生传奇，将自己在其中的英勇壮举吹得神乎其神，狠狠成倍放大了这位小美人对他的顶礼膜拜。随后珍妮也依次描摹了自己对未来的规划打算，平心而论，她的这番打算可谓务实至极。既然埃克托如今家财散尽，她反倒比以往任何时刻都更铁了心要对他矢志不渝；她打算退掉巴黎的豪奢寓所，变卖陈设家具，转而去干一门正经本分的体面生计。她重新联系上了一位当年交好的旧姐妹，对方如今已是一名手艺拔尖的成衣裁缝，正急切寻觅一位有些许本钱的入股合伙人，由对方出资、她自个儿负责出技术操持业务。她们打算在布雷达街区盘下一处铺面，凭二人的联手打拼，生意断无不兴隆之理。

Jenny talked with a pretty, knowing, businesslike air, which made Hector laugh. These projects seemed very comic to him; yet he was touched by this unselfishness on the part of a young and pretty woman, who was willing to work in order to please him.
But, unhappily, they were forced to part. Jenny had gone to Corbeil intending to stay a week; but the count told her this was absolutely impossible. She cried bitterly at first, then got angry, and finally consoled herself with a plan to return on the following Tuesday.
“Goodbye,” said she, embracing Hector, “think of me.” She smilingly added, “I ought to be jealous; for they say your friend’s wife is perhaps the handsomest woman in France. Is it true?”
“Upon my word, I don’t know. I’ve forgotten to look at her.”
Hector told the truth. Although he did not betray it, he was still under the surprise of his chagrin at the failure of his attempt at suicide. He felt the dizziness which follows great moral crises as well as a heavy blow on the head, and which distracts the attention from exterior things. But Jenny’s words, “the handsomest woman in France,” attracted his notice, and he could, that very evening, repair his forgetfulness. When he returned to Valfeuillu, his friend had not returned; Mme. Sauvresy was alone reading, in the brilliantly lighted drawing-room. Hector seated himself opposite her, a little aside, and was thus able to observe her at his ease, while engaging her in conversation. His first impression was an unfavorable one. He found her beauty too sculptural and polished. He sought for imperfections, and finding none, was almost terrified by this lovely, motionless face, these clear, cold eyes. Little by little, however, he accustomed himself to pass the greater part of the afternoon with Bertha, while Sauvresy was away arranging his affairs﻿—selling, negotiating, using his time in cutting down interests and discussing with agents and attorneys. He soon perceived that she listened to him with pleasure, and he judged from this that she was a decidedly superior woman, much better than her husband. He had no wit, but possessed an inexhaustible fund of anecdotes and adventures. He had seen so many things and known so many people that he was as interesting as a chronicle. He had a sort of frothy fervor, not wanting in brilliancy, and a polite cynicism which, at first, surprised one.

Had Bertha been unimpassioned, she might have judged him at his value; but she had lost her power of insight. She heard him, plunged in a foolish ecstasy, as one hears a traveller who has returned from far and dangerous countries, who has visited peoples of whose language the hearer is ignorant, and lived in the midst of manners and customs incomprehensible to ourselves.
Days, weeks, months passed on, and the Count de Trémorel did not find life at Valfeuillu as dull as he had thought. He insensibly slipped along the gentle slope of material well-being, which leads directly to brutishness. A physical and moral torpor had succeeded the fever of the first days, free from disagreeable sensations, though wanting in excitement. He ate and drank much, and slept twelve round hours. The rest of the time, when he did not talk with Bertha, he wandered in the park, lounged in a rocking-chair, or took a jaunt in the saddle. He even went fishing under the willows at the foot of the garden; and grew fat. His best days were those which he spent at Corbeil with Jenny. He found in her something of his past, and she always quarrelled with him, which woke him up. Besides, she brought him the gossip of Paris and the small talk of the boulevards. She came regularly every week, and her love for Hector, far from diminishing, seemed to grow with each interview. The poor girl’s affairs were in a troubled condition. She had bought her establishment at too high a price, and her partner at the end of the first month decamped, carrying off three thousand francs. She knew nothing about the trade which she had undertaken, and she was robbed without mercy on all sides. She said nothing of these troubles to Hector, but she intended to ask him to come to her assistance. It was the least that he could do.
At first, the visitors to Valfeuillu were somewhat astonished at the constant presence there of a young man of leisure; but they got accustomed to him. Hector assumed a melancholy expression of countenance, such as a man ought to have who had undergone unheard-of misfortunes, and whose life had failed of its promise. He appeared inoffensive; people said:
“The count has a charming simplicity.”
But sometimes, when alone, he had sudden and terrible relapses. “This life cannot last,” thought he; and he was overcome with childish rage when he contrasted the past with the present.

倘若贝尔塔当时心如止水，她本能够一眼看穿他徒有其表的虚浮身价；然而她早已彻底丧失了明辨秋毫的理智洞察。她沉溺在愚蠢的如痴如醉中，聆听着他的高谈阔论，就如同一名深闺凡人聆听着自极远、极险恶异域归来的探险家，讲述着那些言语不通的蛮荒部族、以及寻常人匪夷所思的风土人情。
数日、数周乃至数月匆匆流逝，特雷莫雷尔伯爵渐渐发觉，瓦尔菲伊庄园的日子根本不像他原先预料的那般索然无味。他在物质优裕的平缓斜坡上悄无声息地滑落，而这条斜坡的尽头，直通肉体的麻木与堕落。最初几天的精神高热退去之后，取而代之的是一种肉体与灵魂上的彻底怠惰——虽无刺激狂欢，倒也无病无灾、惬意安泰。他大口啖肉大碗痛饮，每天雷打不动蒙头大睡整整12个小时。其余的大把时光，只要不陪贝尔塔闲聊，他便漫步在偌大的庄园林苑里，懒洋洋地斜倚在摇椅上发呆，或是翻身上马巡游一番。他甚至还会跑到花园尽头的柳树荫下垂钓度日；整个人渐渐发福丰满起来。他过得最快活的时光，无非是每周前往科尔贝伊同珍妮相聚的日子。他在她身上能重新觅得昔日纸醉金迷的旧影，而她成天同他拌嘴吃醋，倒也能让他昏睡的神经清醒几分。不仅如此，她还能源源不断为他带来巴黎市井的花边绯闻与林荫大道上的街谈巷议。她雷打不动每周前来赴约，而她对埃克托的爱火非但未曾冷却，反倒在每一次耳鬓厮磨中愈燃愈炽。这个可怜姑娘的生计陷入了重重泥淖。她盘下那处铺面的价格被敲了大竹杠，而她的合伙人在第一个月末便卷走了整整3000法郎逃之夭夭。她对自己经办的这门行当一窍不通，周遭所有人都在肆无忌惮地宰割盘剥她。她对埃克托将这些烦心苦楚捂得严严实实，心里却暗自盘算着找个由头向他求援。这原本就是他分内理所应当的关照。
起初，登门造访瓦尔菲伊的宾客们对庄园里常年寄居着这样一位游手好闲的名门公子颇感诧异；然而久而久之人们便习以为常了。埃克托在人前总摆出一副郁郁寡欢的忧郁面孔，俨然一个饱尝旷古未闻的横祸惨剧、半生宏图彻底付诸东流的落魄贵胄。他显得人畜无害，四邻乡绅纷纷交口称赞道：
“伯爵大人身上当真有一种令人如沐春风的纯朴谦逊。”
然而有时形单影只独处之际，他心头仍会冷不丁爆发出一阵阵可怕的心神激荡。“这种日子绝不可能长久，”他暗自咬牙切齿；每当将昔日的风光无限与当下的寄人篱下两相对照，他便被一股孩童般的暴怒所吞噬。

How could he shake off this dull existence, and rid himself of these stiffly good people who surrounded him, these friends of Sauvresy? Where should he take refuge? “I am going, Hector,” said she, after a moment’s reflection; “If you are really leaving me to get married, you shall never hear of me again.”
“Why, Jenny, I hope I shall still remain your friend.”
“Well, only if you abandon me for another reason, remember what I tell you; you will be a dead man, and she, a lost woman.”
She opened the door; he tried to take her hand; she repulsed him.
“Adieu!”
Hector ran to the window to assure himself of her departure. She was ascending the avenue leading to the station.
“Well, that’s over,” thought he, with a sigh of relief. “Jenny was a good girl.”

埃克托早已做好了迎接一场雷霆震怒的万全准备——尖声嘶嚎、撒泼发疯、歇斯底里、昏厥倒地。然而大出他意料的是，珍妮竟连半个字都没吐露。她的面庞刹那间变得同衣领一般惨白无血，原本红润的双唇褪尽了血色，一双眼珠子直勾勾地凸瞪着。
“这么说来，”她死死咬紧牙关强压着体内翻江倒海的狂澜，从齿缝间挤出话来，“这么说来，你当真是要去迎娶别人了？”
“唉，造化弄人，我别无选择，”他长吁短叹、摆出一副虚伪透顶的悲切模样答道，“你自知晓，最近这些日子我接济你的银两，全是我低声下气从好友手里拆借来的；他的钱袋子绝不可能一辈子任由我索取无度。”
珍妮一把攥住埃克托的手腕，将他径直拽到了窗前。在那儿，她将目光如利刃般死死钉在他脸上，仿佛要用这审视的烈火逼出他灵魂深处的所有真话，一字一顿、极其缓慢地吐露道：
“这当真是大实话吗？你果真是为了迎娶名门闺秀才要抛弃我？”
埃克托抽出一只手，郑重其事地覆在自己的心口上。
“我向我的名誉发誓，”他信誓旦旦道。
“既然如此，那我便信你一次。”
珍妮款款折步走回了厅房正中央。她挺直腰板伫立在穿衣镜前，戴上了自己的小礼帽，从容不迫地整理着帽带，神情自若得仿佛什么变故都不曾发生过一样。收拾停当准备拔腿之际，她径直迈步走到特雷莫雷尔身前。“最后一次了，”她强压着嗓音力求坚决地说道，可那泛红含泪的泪眼却无情出卖了她内心的碎裂，“最后一次了，埃克托，咱们俩当真就此恩断义绝了吗？”
“非如此不可。”
珍妮下意识地做了一个手势，特雷莫雷尔并未能捕捉到；她整张面庞上掠过了一抹狠辣恶毒的神采；双唇微启似欲吐露某句尖酸刻薄的嘲讽；可她转瞬间便将情绪彻底平复了下去。
“我这就走，埃克托，”片刻沉吟之后她缓缓说道，“假使你当真是为了联姻娶妻才抛弃我，你此生再不会听到关于我的半点风声。”
“嗨，珍妮，我巴望着咱们往后依旧能做挚友。”
“哼，但倘若你是为了别的由头抛弃我，你给我牢牢记住我今天撂下的这句话：你必将横尸街头，而那个狐媚子，必将万劫不复！”
她猛地拉开房门；他试图上前握住她的柔荑，却被她一把粗暴地推开了。
“永别了！”
埃克托慌忙快步冲到窗前，目送她的离去以确保她当真彻底走远。只见她正快步穿过通往火车站的林荫大道。
“呼，总算是彻底了结了，”他如释重负地长舒了一口气，“平心而论，珍妮当真算是个好姑娘。”
