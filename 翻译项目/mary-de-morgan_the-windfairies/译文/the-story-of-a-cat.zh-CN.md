## The Story of a Cat / 一只猫的故事

===Original===
Once there lived an old gentleman who was a very rich old gentleman, and able to buy nearly everything he wanted. He had earned all his wealth for himself by trading in a big city, and now he had grown so fond of money that he loved it better than anything else in the world, and thought of nothing except how he could save it up and make more. But he never seemed to have time to enjoy himself with all that he had earned, and he was very angry if he was asked to give money to others. He lived in a handsome house all alone, and he had a very good cook who cooked him a sumptuous dinner every day, but he rarely asked anyone to share it with him, though he loved eating and drinking, and always had the best of wine and food. His cook and his other servants knew that he was greedy and hard, and cared for nobody, and though they served him well because he paid them, they none of them loved him.

===Chinese===
从前有一位老绅士（old gentleman），是个非常有钱的老绅士，差不多想要什么就能买什么。他的全部家财都是自己在大城市里做买卖挣来的，如今他爱钱爱得入了迷，爱它胜过世上的一切，满脑子只想着怎么把钱攒起来、再多赚一些。可是他挣下这许多钱财，却仿佛从没有工夫享用；若有人求他施舍钱财，他便要发好大的脾气。他独自住在一所漂亮的宅子里，雇了一位手艺极好的厨娘，天天给他做丰盛的晚餐，却难得请谁来同他共享，尽管他贪吃贪喝，桌上永远是最好的酒菜。厨娘和别的仆人都知道他既贪婪又刻薄，心里没有别人；他们看在工钱的份上把他伺候得妥妥帖帖，却没有一个爱戴他。

===Original===
It was one Christmas, and the snow lay thick upon the ground, and the wind howled so fiercely that the old gentleman was very glad he was not obliged to go out into the street, but could sit in his comfortable armchair by the fire and keep warm.

===Chinese===
有一年圣诞节，地上的积雪厚厚的，风声呼啸得那么凄厉，老绅士暗自庆幸自己不必上街，可以坐在炉火边舒舒服服的扶手椅里取暖。

===Original===
“It really is terrible weather,” he said to himself, “terrible weather;” and he went to the window and looked out into the street, where all the pavements were inches deep in snow. “I am very glad that I need not go out at all, but can sit here and keep warm for today, that is the great thing, and I shall have some ado to keep out the cold even here with the fire.”

===Chinese===
“这天气真是可怕，”他自言自语道，“真是可怕。”他走到窗前，朝街上望去，人行道上的积雪已有好几寸厚。“我真高兴今天压根儿不用出门，可以坐在这儿暖暖和和的，这才是顶要紧的。就算守着炉火，待在这屋里也还得费些功夫才挡得住寒气呢。”

===Original===
He was leaving the window, when there came up in the street outside an old man, whose clothes hung in rags about him, and who looked half frozen. He was about the same age as the old gentleman inside the window, and the same height, and had grey, curly hair, like his, and if they had been dressed alike anyone would have taken them for two brothers.

===Chinese===
他正要离开窗口，街上走来一个老头儿，衣衫褴褛地挂在身上，看上去都快冻僵了。他的年纪同窗内的老绅士相仿，个头一般高，也同他一样长着灰色的鬈发；两人若是穿一样的衣裳，谁都会把他们当成一对兄弟。

===Original===
“Oh, really,” said the old gentleman irritably, “this is most annoying. The parish ought to take up these sort of people, and prevent their wandering about the streets and molesting honest folk,” for the poor old man had taken off his hat, and began to beg.

===Chinese===
“哎呀，真是的，”老绅士恼火地说，“讨厌透了。教区就该把这类人收容起来，别让他们在街上晃荡，搅扰正经人。”原来那可怜的老头儿已经摘下帽子，开始乞讨了。

===Original===
“It is Christmas Day,” he said, and though he did not speak very loud, the old gentleman could hear every word he said quite plainly through the window. “It is Christmas Day, and you will have your dinner here in your warm room. Of your charity give me a silver shilling that I may go into an eating-shop, and have a dinner too.”

===Chinese===
“今天是圣诞节，”他说。他的声音并不很响，可老绅士隔着窗子，把他的话字字听得清清楚楚。“今天是圣诞节，您将在暖暖和和的屋里用晚餐。发发善心，赏我一枚银先令（shilling）吧，好让我进一家小饭铺，也吃上一顿晚餐。”

===Original===
“A silver shilling!” cried the old gentleman, “I never heard of such a thing! Monstrous! Go away, I never give to beggars, and you must have done something very wicked to become so poor.”

===Chinese===
“一枚银先令！”老绅士嚷道，“我从没听说过这种事！岂有此理！走开，我从不施舍乞丐，你落到这般穷酸地步，必定是造了什么大孽。”

===Original===
But still the old man stood there, though the snow was falling on his shoulders, and on his bare head. “Then give me a copper,” he said; “just one penny, that today I may not starve.”

===Chinese===
可那老头儿仍旧站在那里，任雪花落在他肩头，落在他光着的头顶。“那就给我一枚铜子儿吧，”他说，“只要一便士（penny），叫我今天不至于挨饿。”

===Original===
“Certainly not,” cried the old gentleman; “I tell you I never give to beggars at all.” But the old man did not move.

===Chinese===
“断然不行，”老绅士喊道，“我跟你说过了，我从来不施舍乞丐。”可老头儿动也不动。

===Original===
“Then,” he said, “give me some of the broken victuals from your table, that I may creep into a doorway and eat a Christmas dinner there.”

===Chinese===
“那么，”他说，“就给我一点您桌上的残羹剩饭吧，我好躲进一处门洞，在那里也算吃上一顿圣诞晚餐。”

===Original===
“I will give you nothing,” cried the old gentleman, stamping his foot. “Go away. Go away at once, or I shall send for the policeman to take you away.”

===Chinese===
“我什么也不会给你，”老绅士跺着脚喊道，“走开！马上走开！不然我叫警察来把你抓走。”

===Original===
The old beggar-man put on his hat and turned quietly away, but what the old gentleman thought was very odd was, that instead of seeming distressed he was laughing merrily, and then he looked back at the window, and called out some words, but they were in a foreign tongue, and the old gentleman could not understand them. So he returned to his comfortable armchair by the fire, still murmuring angrily that beggars ought not to be allowed to be in the streets.

===Chinese===
老乞丐（beggar-man）戴上帽子，静静地转身走了。可有一件事老绅士觉得好生古怪：这老头儿非但没有愁苦之色，反而畅快地笑着，随后还回头望着窗子，大声喊了几句话——那是一种外国话，老绅士听不明白。于是他回到炉边舒适的扶手椅里，嘴里还气鼓鼓地咕哝着，说街上实在不该放任这些乞丐出没。

===Original===
Next morning the snow fell more thickly than ever, and the streets were almost impassable, but it did not trouble the old gentleman, for he knew he need not go out and get wet or cold. But in the morning when he came down to breakfast, to his great surprise there was a cat on the hearthrug in front of the fire, looking into it, and blinking lazily. Now the old gentleman had never had any animal in his house before, and he at once went to it and said “Shoo-shoo!” and tried to turn it out. But the cat did not move, and when the old gentleman looked at it nearer, he could not help admiring it very much. It was a very large cat, grey and black, and had extremely long soft hair, and a thick soft ruff round its neck. Moreover, it looked very well fed and cared for, and as if it had always lived in comfortable places. Somehow it seemed to the old gentleman to suit the room and the rug and the fire, and to make the whole place look more prosperous and cosy even than it had done before.

===Chinese===
第二天早晨，雪下得比先前更紧，街上几乎没法通行，可这难不倒老绅士，因为他知道自己不必出门去挨冻受潮。可当他下楼来用早餐时，却大吃一惊：炉前的地毯上蹲着一只猫，正望着炉火，懒洋洋地眨着眼睛。老绅士家里从来养过任何牲畜，他立刻走过去，喝道：“嘘！嘘！”想把它轰出去。可那猫一动不动。老绅士凑近细看，又不由得在心里暗暗赞叹。那是只很大的猫，灰黑两色，毛又长又软，颈间围着一圈厚厚的软毛，如同领巾一般。而且它看上去被喂养照料得极好，仿佛一向住在安逸体面的地方。不知怎么，老绅士只觉得这猫同这屋子、这地毯、这炉火正相配衬，使整个宅子比先前更显得富足温馨。

===Original===
“A fine creature! a very handsome cat!” he said to himself; “I should really think that a reward would be offered for such an animal, as it has evidently been well looked after and fed, so it would be a pity to turn it away in a hurry.”

===Chinese===
“好家伙！真是只漂亮的猫！”他自言自语道，“这么一只分明被人精心喂养照料的猫，想来总会有人悬赏寻它吧，急匆匆把它赶走可就可惜了。”

===Original===
One thing struck him as very funny about the cat, and that was that though the ground was deep in snow and slush outside, the cat was quite dry, and its fur looked as if it had just been combed and brushed. The old gentleman called to his cook and asked if she knew how the cat had come in, but she declared she had not seen it before, and said she believed it must have come down the chimney as all the doors and windows had been shut and bolted. However, there it was, and when his own breakfast was finished the old gentleman gave it a large saucer of milk, which it lapped up not greedily or in a hurry, but as if it were quite used to good food and had had plenty of it always.

===Chinese===
有一件事他觉得好生奇怪：外面满地是深深的积雪和泥雪水，这猫却浑身干干的，那身毛就像刚刚梳刷过一样。老绅士唤来厨娘，问她可知道这猫是怎么进来的，厨娘一口咬定从前没见过它，还说门窗全都关得严严实实、上了闩，她琢磨这猫准是从烟囱里下来的。可不管怎么说，猫就在这儿。老绅士自己用过早餐，便给它一大碟牛奶。它舔得不慌不忙，也不贪馋，仿佛向来吃惯了好的，从来不曾短缺过。

===Original===
“It really is a very handsome animal, and most uncommon,” said the old gentleman, “I shall keep it awhile and look out for the reward;” but though he looked at all the notices in the street and in the newspapers, the old gentleman could see no notice about a reward being offered for a grey and black cat, so it stayed on with him from day to day.

===Chinese===
“这真是个漂亮家伙，极其少见，”老绅士说，“我且把它留下些时日，留意着悬赏的告示。”可他把街上的告示和报纸都看遍了，也没见哪处悬赏寻找一只灰黑色的猫，于是这猫便一天一天在他家住了下来。

===Original===
Every day the cat seemed to his master to grow handsomer and handsomer. The old gentleman never loved anything but himself, but he began to take a sort of interest in the strange cat, and to wonder what sort it was﻿—if it was a Persian or a Siamese, or some curious new sort of which he had never heard. He liked the sound of its lazy contented purring after its food, which seemed to speak of nothing but comfort and affluence. So the cat remained on till nearly a year had passed away.

===Chinese===
在主人眼里，这猫一天比一天俊俏。老绅士平生除了自己谁也不爱，如今却对这只怪猫生出几分兴趣，琢磨它究竟是哪个品种——是波斯猫（Persian），还是暹罗猫（Siamese），或是他闻所未闻的什么新奇品种。他爱听它饭后那懒洋洋、心满意足的呼噜声，那声音仿佛句句说的都是安逸与富足。就这样，这猫一直住了下去，不知不觉将近一年过去了。

===Original===
It was not very long before Christmas that an acquaintance of the old gentleman’s came to his rooms on business. He knew a great deal about all sorts of animals and loved them for their own sakes, but of course he had never talked to the old gentleman about them, because he knew he did not love anything. But when he saw the grey cat, he said at once﻿—

===Chinese===
圣诞节前不久，老绅士的一位熟人因事登门。此人对各种动物都极有见识，真心喜爱它们本身，不过他从不与老绅士谈论动物，因为他知道老绅士什么都不爱。可他一见这只灰猫，立刻便说——

===Original===
“Do you know that this is a very valuable creature, and I should think would be worth a great deal?”

===Chinese===
“您可知道这是只极名贵的家伙？依我看，它能值一大笔钱呢。”

===Original===
At these words the old gentleman’s heart beat high. Here, he thought, would be a piece of great luck if a stray cat could make him richer than he was before.

===Chinese===
这话听得老绅士心怦怦直跳。他想，一只野猫若能叫他比先前更有钱，那可真是天大的好运。

===Original===
“Why, who would want to buy it?” he said. “I don’t know anybody who would be so foolish as to give any money for a cat which is of no use in life except to catch mice, when you can so easily get one for nothing.”

===Chinese===
“嗬，谁肯买它？”他说，“猫这种东西，除了捉老鼠别无用处，白捡也容易得很，我可不知道谁会傻到肯为它掏钱。”

===Original===
“Ah, but many people are very fond of cats, and would give much for rare sorts like this. If you want to sell it, the right thing would be to send it to the Cat Show, and there you would most likely take a prize for it, and then someone would be sure to buy it, and, it may be, would give a great deal. I don’t know what kind it is, or where it comes from, for I have never seen one the least like it, but for that reason it is very sure to be valuable.”

===Chinese===
“啊，可爱猫的人多着呢，遇上这样的稀罕品种，肯出大价钱的尽有。您若想卖它，正路子就是送它去猫展（Cat Show），十有八九能得个奖，然后保管有人来买，说不定还肯出好大一笔钱。我不晓得它是什么品种，从哪里来，因为我从没见过有哪只猫跟它有一丝相像——可正因为这样，它必定非常值钱。”

===Original===
Upon this the old gentleman almost laughed with joy.

===Chinese===
老绅士听了，欢喜得几乎笑出声来。

===Original===
“Where is the Cat Show?” he asked; “and when is it to be held?”

===Chinese===
“猫展在哪儿？”他问，“什么时候举办？”

===Original===
“There will be a Cat Show in this city quite soon,” said his acquaintance; “and it will be a particularly good one, for the new Princess is quite crazy about cats, and she is coming to it, and it is said that she doesn’t mind what she gives for a cat if she sees one she likes.”

===Chinese===
“本城很快就要办一场猫展，”熟人说，“而且场面会特别好，因为新来的公主爱猫爱得入迷，她要亲自来看呢。据说只要让她瞧上一只中意的猫，出多大价钱她都不在乎。”

===Original===
So then he told the old gentleman how he should send his name and the cat’s name to the people who managed the show, and where it was to be held, and went away, leaving the old gentleman well pleased, but to himself he laughed and said, “I don’t think that old man thinks of anything on earth but making money. How pleased he was at the idea of selling that beautiful cat if he could get something for it!”

===Chinese===
接着他告诉老绅士，该怎样把他的姓名和猫的名字报给主办猫展的人，又告诉了他举办的地点，然后便告辞了，留下老绅士满心欢喜。那位熟人独自暗笑道：“我看这老头儿除了赚钱，世上什么都不想。一听说能把那只漂亮猫卖个好价钱，瞧把他乐的！”

===Original===
When he had gone, the grey puss came and rubbed itself about his master’s legs, and looked up in his face as though it had understood the conversation, and did not like the idea of being sent to the show. But the old gentleman was delighted, and sat by the fire and mused on what he was likely to get for the cat, and wondered if it would not take a prize.

===Chinese===
熟人走后，灰猫儿便走过来，在主人的腿边蹭来蹭去，仰起脸望着他，仿佛方才的话它全听懂了，很不乐意被送去参加猫展。可老绅士正得意呢，坐在炉边盘算着这猫能给自己换回多少钱，寻思它说不定还能得个奖。

===Original===
“I shall be sorry to have to send it away,” he said; “still, if I could get a good round sum of money it would be a real sin not to take it, so you will have to go, puss; and it really was extraordinary good luck for me that you ever came here.”

===Chinese===
“把你送走，我还会舍不得呢，”他说，“不过，要是真能换来满满一大笔钱，不拿才是真正的罪过。所以你只好去一趟了，猫儿。你能上我这儿来，实在是我的天大运气。”

===Original===
The days passed, and Christmas Day came, and again the snow fell, and the ground was white. The wind whistled and blew, and on Christmas morning the old gentleman stood and looked out of the window at the falling snow and rain, and the grey cat stood beside him, and rubbed itself against his hand. He rather liked stroking it, it was so soft and comfortable, and when he touched the long hair he always thought of how much money he should get for it.

===Chinese===
日子一天天过去，圣诞节到了，又下起雪来，大地一片白。风呼呼地刮着。圣诞日早晨，老绅士站在窗前，望着外面纷落的雨雪，灰猫站在他身旁，在他手上蹭着。他颇喜欢抚弄它，那毛那么柔软熨帖；可每回手碰到那身长毛，他想起的总是它能给自己换多少钱。

===Original===
This morning he saw no old beggar-man outside the window, and he said to himself: “I really think they manage better with the beggars than they used to, and are clearing them from the town.”

===Chinese===
这天早晨，他没在窗外看见老乞丐，便自言自语道：“我看如今对乞丐的管束倒是比往年像样了，正把他们从城里清出去呢。”

===Original===
But just as he was leaving the window he heard something scratching outside, and there crawled on to the windowsill another cat. It was a very different creature to the grey cat on the rug. It was a poor, thin, wretched-looking animal, with ribs sticking through its fur, and it mewed in the most pathetic manner, and beat itself against the pane. When it saw it, the handsome grey puss was very much excited, and ran to and fro, and purred loudly.

===Chinese===
可他正要离开窗口，忽听得外面有什么东西在抓挠，只见另一只猫爬上了窗台。它同地毯上的灰猫儿全然是两路货色：一个可怜巴巴、瘦骨嶙峋、一脸苦相，肋骨根根凸出毛外，叫声凄惨已极，一下一下往窗玻璃上扑。那俊俏的灰猫儿一见它，登时激动起来，跑来跑去，呼噜呼噜叫得响亮。

===Original===
“Oh you disgraceful-looking beast!” said the old gentleman angrily; “go away, this is not the place for an animal like you. There is nothing here for stray cats. And you look as if you had not eaten anything for months. How different to my puss here!” and he tapped against the window to drive it away. But still it would not go, and the old gentleman felt very indignant, for the sound of its mewing was terrible. So he opened the window, and though he did not like to touch the miserable animal, he took it up and hurled it away into the snow, and it trotted away, and in the deep snow he could not see the way it went.

===Chinese===
“哦，你这丢人现眼的东西！”老绅士怒道，“走开！这里可不是你这号畜生待的地方。这儿没有野猫的好处。瞧你这副样子，倒像几个月没吃过一口饭了，跟我这只猫儿差得多么远！”说着他敲敲窗子赶它走。可它偏不走，老绅士心头火起，那猫叫声实在凄厉难听。于是他打开窗子，虽说不愿碰那可怜的畜生，还是一把抓起它，狠狠掼进雪地里。它一路小跑着去了，雪太深，他望不见它跑去的路径。

===Original===
But that evening, after he had had his Christmas dinner, as he sat by the fire with the grey puss on the hearthrug beside him, he heard again the noise outside the window, and then he heard the stray cat crying and mewing to be let in, and again the grey and black cat became very much excited, and dashed about the room, and jumped at the window as if it wanted to open it.

===Chinese===
可那天晚上，他用过圣诞晚餐，坐在炉边，灰猫儿卧在身旁的炉前地毯上，忽又听得窗外响动，接着便听见那只野猫哭叫着、喵喵哀鸣着要进来。那灰黑色的猫儿又激动起来，满屋乱窜，朝窗户扑跳，仿佛想自己把窗子打开。

===Original===
“I shall really be quite glad when I have sold you at the Cat Show,” said the old gentleman, “if I am going to have all sorts of stray cats worrying here,” and for the second time he opened the window, and seized the trembling, half-starved creature, and this time he threw it with all his might as hard as he could throw. “And now there is an end of you, I hope,” he said as he heard it fall with a dull thud, and settled himself again in his armchair, and the grey puss returned to the hearthrug, but it did not purr or rub itself against its master.

===Chinese===
“早知道要有各色野猫上这儿来纠缠，等你一进猫展卖掉，我还真求之不得呢。”老绅士说着，第二次打开窗子，一把抓住那浑身发抖、饿得半死的畜生，这一回他用尽全身力气，掼得远远的。“这下你可完了，但愿如此。”他说。他听得那猫闷闷地一声跌落，便重新在扶手椅里坐定。灰猫儿回到炉前地毯上，却不呼噜了，也不再到主人身上来蹭。
