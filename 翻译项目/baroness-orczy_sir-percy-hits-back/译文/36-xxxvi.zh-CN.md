## XXXVI / 第三十六章

===Original===
One must of necessity turn once more to the *Moniteur* of the 22nd Messidor year II of the Republic One and Indivisible. There in the *Choix des Rapports* XXV 516﻿–﻿17, despite its sobriety of language and paucity of detail, there is ample proof that throughout the proceedings it was the action of one unknown that precipitated the final catastrophe. “*Un géant*,” we are told, “*fût le premier à lancer l’accusation fausse contre le Président du Tribunal, et un tumulte irrépressible s’ensuivit*.”

===Chinese===
我们势必还得再一次去翻统一不可分割的共和国二年穗月（Messidor）二十二日那期的《导报》（*Moniteur*）。在《报告选辑》（*Choix des Rapports*）XXV 卷 516–17 页上，尽管措辞节制、细节寥寥，却不乏充分的证据，证明整场审判的进程中，正是那一个无名之人的行动，促成了最后这场大祸。据载：「一个巨人第一个抛出对法庭庭长的虚假指控，随之而起的是一场无法遏止的骚乱」（*Un géant fût le premier à lancer l’accusation fausse contre le Président du Tribunal, et un tumulte irrépressible s’ensuivit*）。

===Original===
“False,” you observe. But on that 16th day of June 1794, Chauvelin of the National Convention, member of committees and confidant of Robespierre, did, we know, stand in danger of being dragged out into the open and hung on the nearest lamp post. The crowd was in no mood even to wait for the paraphernalia of the guillotine. They wanted to see the arch-traitor, the perjurer, who had sworn false oaths and lied in order to save himself and his brood, hang then and there. The giant spoken of in the *Choix des Rapports* had, it seems, hardly waited till the words were out of Fleurette’s mouth, before he pushed his way to the forefront of the crowd, with vigorous play of his powerful elbows. Down he was now, in the body of the court. In the struggle, his ragged shirt had been half torn off his shoulders, and his broad chest and sinewy arms could be seen, nude and immense, and coated with grime. Out of one of the pockets of his tattered breeches he had produced another uncooked carrot, and into this he bit lustily, then with a wide sweep of the arm he launched one by one against the President of the Tribunal the damning invectives which the *Moniteur* has characterized as false. “Traitor!” he cried. “Liar and perjurer! Citizens all, have you in all your lives ever witnessed such infamy?”

===Chinese===
「不实之词，」你会这样说。可是我们知道，在 1794 年 6 月 16 日那一天，国民公会的肖维兰——好几个委员会的委员、罗伯斯庇尔（Robespierre）的心腹——当真处在被拖到露天、吊上最近一根灯柱的危险之中。人群连等断头台那一套行头架起来的耐性都没有。他们要亲眼看着那个罪大恶极的叛徒、那个赌假咒发假誓的伪誓者——为了保全自己和他那一窝子，赌的是假咒，撒的是谎——当场吊死。《报告选辑》里说的那个大个子，似乎压根儿没等弗勒蕾特的话音落地，就抡开两条有力的胳膊肘，一路拱到了人群的最前排。此刻他已经下到了法庭场子的当中。一路挤撞里，他身上那件破衬衫已被扯掉半边，露出宽阔的胸膛和虬结的臂膀，赤裸裸、巍巍然，覆着一层油垢。他从破裤子的一只口袋里又掏出一根生胡萝卜，对着它狠狠咬上一大口，随后手臂大开大阖地一挥，一句接一句，朝法庭庭长掷去那足以定罪的痛骂——《导报》倒已把那些痛骂定性为不实之词。「叛徒！」他喊道。「骗子！发假誓的！公民们，大伙儿这辈子可曾见过这样的无耻勾当？」

===Original===
The *Choix des Rapports* describes the tumult as irrepressible. Indeed at that moment it would have been easier to dam a raging torrent with one pair of hands, than to suppress the riotous confusion that ensued. Fleurette of a truth stood there forgotten, so did Adèle and Godet. All eyes were fixed on the President, every menacing gesture tended in his direction, all the strident cries, the insults, the varied and foul epithets were hurled against him. There were but few sober tempers in that crowded room at the moment. A dozen perhaps: no more. Older men, one or two women who watched rather than yelled. And what they saw interested and puzzled them, so much that, when the time came, when everybody else was shouting themselves hoarse to the verge of mania, they still kept cool and silent.

===Chinese===
《报告选辑》把那场骚乱形容为无法遏止。的确，在那一刻，凭一双赤手去堵住一条咆哮的激流，也比压下随之而起的那阵乱局容易些。说实话，弗勒蕾特站在那儿，早被忘了个干净，阿黛尔（Adèle）和戈岱（Godet）也一样。所有的眼睛都钉在庭长身上，每一个威吓的手势都朝他比划，一切尖利的呼喊、侮辱、五花八门的污言秽语，全冲他掷去。那一刻，满堂拥挤的人里，头脑还清醒的没有几个。兴许十来个：绝不会再多。几个上了年纪的男人，一两个只顾观看、不肯叫喊的女人。而他们的所见所闻又使他们既纳闷又着迷，着迷到这般地步：到了后来，旁人个个喊哑了嗓子、喊到发狂的边缘，他们却还保持着冷静和沉默。

===Original===
Like everybody else these few were gazing on the President. They saw him standing there on the bench like a figure carved in stone, and, like a stone, his face was of a grey, ashen colour. His eyes looked dim and colourless as if a hand had drawn a film over them; his lips were parted, his nostrils distended. The breath seemed to come with difficulty out of his lungs. A figure, in truth of terror and despair. But calm and still. Motionless as a stone. The giant munching his carrot had waved his huge arms about and yelled himself hoarse until he had lashed all the spectators into a state of frenzy. Finally he strode across the room, and came to a halt close to the judges’ bench facing the President.

===Chinese===
同旁人一样，这几位也凝望着庭长。他们看见他站在法官席上，像一尊石雕的人像，而那张脸也同石头一般，是一种灰败的死灰之色。一双眼睛黯淡无光，仿佛有一只手在上面蒙了一层翳；嘴唇微张，鼻孔翕张。气息仿佛很艰难才从他肺里透出来。说实话，那是一副恐怖与绝望铸成的形象。却又平静，却安然。纹丝不动，宛如石头。那嚼着胡萝卜的大个子挥舞两条巨臂，直着嗓子喊到哑，终于把满场看客抽打得癫狂起来。最后他大步横穿大厅，在紧挨法官席、正对庭长的地方收住了脚。

===Original===
The three judges had been watching him all along: Pochart and Danou with undisguised glee, and President Chauvelin with that stony stare out of his colourless eyes. But even as the giant approached, Chauvelin, though apparently motionless, seemed inwardly to sink within himself, to crouch as a hunted beast in face of the menacing enemy. And suddenly like that of an automaton, up went his arm. With finger outstretched he pointed at the giant and one word escaped his trembling, rigid lips.

===Chinese===
三位法官自始至终都望着他：波夏尔（Pochart）和达努（Danou）毫不掩饰地幸灾乐祸，庭长肖维兰则用那双无色的眼睛，石雕一般死盯着。可就在大个子逼将过来时，肖维兰虽然外表纹丝不动，内里却仿佛整个人往下沉缩，像一头被追猎的野兽对着逼上来的仇敌那般蜷伏。突然间，仿佛自动木偶一般，他的手臂霍地抬起。手指笔直地指向那大个子，一个词从他颤抖而僵直的唇间逸了出来。

===Original===
“You!”

===Chinese===
「你！」

===Original===
Those who were watching him could not understand the word, for it was spoken in an alien tongue. Nor could they understand what happened afterwards. But what actually did happen was that the grimy giant threw back his head and gave a quaint and altogether pleasant laugh.

===Chinese===
望着他的人听不懂这个词，因为它说的是一种外国语。随后发生的事，他们同样听不懂。而实际发生的是：那满身油垢的大块头仰起头，发出一阵古怪而十足愉快的笑。

===Original===
“Why yes!” he said in the same alien tongue, which no one present understood. “At your service, my dear M. Chambertin.”

===Chinese===
「可不是嘛！」他用同一种外国语说道——在场没有一个人听得懂。「听候吩咐，我亲爱的尚贝尔坦先生（M. Chambertin）。」

===Original===
And Chauvelin murmured almost under his breath:

===Chinese===
肖维兰几乎压在嗓子底下，喃喃道：

===Original===
“You have your revenge at last, Sir Percy.”

===Chinese===
「你到底还是报了仇，珀西爵士。」

===Original===
“Hitting back as you see, my friend.”

===Chinese===
「你瞧，这不就叫反击嘛，我的朋友。」

===Original===
It all passed unperceived in the midst of the irrepressible tumult, save by those few who sober-tempered chose to watch rather than to yell. It is doubtful whether even Pochart and Danou, who sat close by, saw anything of this brief, this mysterious scene.

===Chinese===
这一切都在那场无法遏止的喧腾中央，悄没声儿地过去了，只有那几个头脑清醒、宁可观看而不肯叫喊的人瞧在了眼里。就连坐在近旁的波夏尔和达努，怕是也没有看见这转瞬即逝、这神秘的一幕。

===Original===
The very next moment the grimy giant, this time with a hoarse and not at all pleasant laugh, had hurled his half-munched carrot straight into the President’s face. Then facing the crowd once more he threw up his great arms high above his head.

===Chinese===
紧接着的下一刻，那满身油垢的大块头——这一回是一声沙哑而毫不愉快的笑——把他啃剩的半截胡萝卜，直直掷到了庭长脸上。随后他再次转向人群，把两条巨臂高高举过头顶。

===Original===
“Why should we wait, citizens,” he shouted louder than the rest of the yelling crowd. “*A la lanterne*, I say, the traitor and his brood. The guillotine is ready outside the Place. The executioner is to hand. Why wait?”

===Chinese===
「干吗还要等呢，公民们，」他喊道，嗓门压过周遭喊叫的人群。「我说，吊上路灯去（*A la lanterne*），吊死那叛徒和他那一窝子。断头台在广场外头现成架着。刽子手就在手边。还等什么？」

===Original===
Nothing could have pleased the crowd better. They were all like tigers scenting blood, demanding it, licking their jaws in anticipation.

===Chinese===
再没有比这更叫人群痛快的了。他们活像一群嗅着血腥的老虎，嚷着要血，馋涎欲滴地舔着牙床。

===Original===
“Who is for a front place for the spectacle?” a man shouted from the rear of the crowd.

===Chinese===
「这场好戏的前排位子，谁要？」人群后头一个男人喊道。

===Original===
“*A moi!* the front place,” a woman cried in response.

===Chinese===
「*A moi*（归我）！前排的位子，」一个女人应声喊道。

===Original===
“*A moi! A moi!*” came from every side.

===Chinese===
「*A moi! A moi!*（归我！归我！）」四下里齐声喊起。

===Original===
Then the general scramble began. A stampede down the gradients. The clatter of wooden sabots against the floor. The screams of women and children pushed and squeezed by the crowd. The grounding of arms, the click of bayonets, the words of command from the officer in charge of the guard, who were there to maintain order and who were quite powerless. They did of a truth try to stem the mob, to prevent the mad rush, the trampling, the stampede. But there were in reality too few of them for the task. All available fighting men being required for the army abroad, these were for the most part too inexperienced and too incompetent; raw recruits, half-trained for a wholly inadequate corps of gendarmerie. The officers did what they could, but the men themselves were soon caught in the vortex. Having no idea of discipline or duty, they soon became just a part of the mob, allowed themselves to be carried along by the crowd. They were just as excited, just as eager to see the President of a revolutionary tribunal sent summarily to the guillotine, as anyone else. Their lust for the spectacle was as keen as that of any ragamuffin in the place. They were but half-trained ragamuffins themselves, and as every man these days was at least as good as his officer and owed him neither obedience nor respect, it was small wonder that in emergencies like these, the soldiers got out of hand, whilst the officers, shrugging their shoulders, viewed the scene with indifference.

===Chinese===
于是，一场全场大争抢开始了。顺着坡道的一路狂奔。木鞋（sabots）磕在地板上的咔嗒乱响。被人群推搡挤压的女人孩子们的尖叫。枪托顿地，刺刀咔哒，带队的军官发出口令——这队警卫本是为维持秩序而来，此刻却全然无能为力。说实话，他们倒真想拦住暴民，拦住那疯狂的冲撞、踩踏和奔逃。可他们的人数实在少得办不成这桩差事。能打仗的壮丁全数征去补充对外作战的军队了，眼下这些人多半既无经验又不管用：净是些生手新兵，为一支全然不成气候的宪兵队伍受过半吊子的操练。军官们尽了力，可士兵们自己转眼便被卷进了漩涡。他们脑瓜里全没有纪律和职责的概念，很快便成了暴民的一分子，任凭人潮裹挟着走。要看一位革命法庭庭长被从速送上断头台，他们同旁人一样兴奋、一样眼热。对这场好戏的馋劲，他们不输场子里任何一个破衣烂衫的小混混。他们自己本就是一群半吊子操练出来的小混混；何况如今这年月，当小兵的至少同长官平起平坐，既不欠他服从、也不欠他敬意，所以碰上这样的乱子，士兵撒了手，军官耸耸肩膀冷眼旁观，也就不足为怪了。

===Original===
In the meanwhile the grimy giant had effectually fought his way along the floor of the house as far as the bar of the accused, where Fleurette, wide-eyed, deathly pale, half-crazy now with terror, had just fallen forward unconscious across the railing, drooping like a lily that is battered by the storm.

===Chinese===
与此同时，那满身油垢的大块头已经着着实实地杀开一条路，沿着大厅的地面一路挤到了被告栏跟前——弗勒蕾特正在那儿，瞪着眼，面如死灰，这会儿已吓得半疯，刚刚昏死过去，扑倒在栏杆上，像一株被暴雨摧折的百合，蔫蔫地垂着。

===Original===
“And *à moi* the traitors,” the giant shouted, and it was marvellous how his booming voice rang above the uproar and the confusion.

===Chinese===
「还有那俩叛徒，*à moi*（归我）啦，」大块头喊道；他那洪钟般的嗓音竟响亮地盖过满场的喧嚣纷乱，真是不可思议。

===Original===
He dragged Fleurette’s inanimate body from the bar and flung it over his shoulder, as if it were a bundle of goods. Then with two huge strides he was right in front of the judges’ bench, and there turned back to face the crowd again.

===Chinese===
他把弗勒蕾特失了知觉的身子从被告栏里拖出来，往肩上一甩，活像甩一捆货物。随后两大步，他已站到法官席正前方，在那儿又转过身来，面对人群。

===Original===
“Take your places for the spectacle, citizens,” he cried. “I’ll bring the actors along.”

===Chinese===
「各位占好看戏的位子吧，公民们，」他喊道。「戏子我来带。」

===Original===
He looked almost unreal as he stood there, dominating the crowd, grimy, unkempt, immense, with blackened face and huge bare chest, and the inanimate body of the girl across his massive shoulders. He seemed a being from another world, a Titan, a monster, a fiend-like fury, the embodiment of all the hates and the furies that animated the rest of the crowd. They glanced at him and trembled; some of them who had not wholly forgotten their age and innocence surreptitiously crossed themselves.

===Chinese===
他站在那儿，威临全场，几乎显得不像个真人：满身油垢，蓬头垢面，躯体巍然，脸膛熏黑，胸膛宽阔地袒露着，姑娘失了知觉的身子横扛在他厚实的双肩上。他仿佛是来自另一个世界的生灵，一位泰坦（Titan），一头怪物，一尊恶魔般的狂怒，是点燃全场其余众人的一切仇恨与暴怒的化身。众人瞥他一眼，直打哆嗦；有几个还不曾把旧日岁月和清白无邪忘个干净的人，偷偷画了个十字。

===Original===
“Take your places for the spectacle, citizens,” he went on lustily. “One actor I have ready for you. Who will bring the other?”

===Chinese===
「各位占好看戏的位子吧，公民们，」他兴致勃勃地继续喊。「一个戏子我给你们备现成了。另一个哪个去请？」

===Original===
Three men in the forefront of the crowd were at that moment standing quite close to the judges’ bench, where the President lay back in his chair, dead to everything about him, alive only through the intensity of his agony. In response to the Titan’s suggestion, which was greeted with loud applause by the crowd, the three men scrambled over the desk, seized the inert person of the President between them. One of them flung a sack over his head. Thus adorned they hoisted him upon their shoulders while the crowd stamped and shouted with glee.

===Chinese===
就在那一刻，人群前排有三个男人正站在离法官席很近的地方——庭长瘫在椅子里，对周遭一切浑然如死，只凭那阵剧痛的剧烈才透出活气。应着这泰坦的提议——它博得人群响亮的喝彩——三个汉子翻过案桌，把庭长那瘫软的身子七手八脚架住。一个往他头上罩了条麻袋。这般装扮停当，他们把他扛上肩头，人群欢喜得直跺脚、直呐喊。

===Original===
“*Un tumulte irrépressible s’ensuivit*,” says the *Choix des Rapports* in the *Moniteur* of the 22nd Messidor. Tumult is but a poor word to express the actions of that multitude. Men and women and children had become blind, insentient with lust, mad with hatred and excitement.

===Chinese===
《报告选辑》在穗月二十二日的《导报》上写道：「随之而起的是一场无法遏止的骚乱」（*Un tumulte irrépressible s’ensuivit*）。「骚乱」这个词，实在描不尽那群人的行状。男人、女人和孩子，全都瞎了眼，欲念烧得没了知觉，仇恨和亢奋逼得发了狂。

===Original===
“Take your places for the spectacle,” the Titan shouted, “and I’ll bring along the actors for you.”

===Chinese===
「各位占好看戏的位子吧，」泰坦吼道，「戏子我这就给你们带来。」

===Original===
And so they rushed out in a compact, struggling mass, hurrying, scurrying, fighting and pushing and struggling. Out in the open, in the Place de la République, into the sunshine and under the blue vault of heaven they rushed. The guillotine was set up there ready for its afternoon work, but, as the grimy giant had said, “Why wait?” Why indeed! No one was in a mood for waiting. The blackest traitor this town had ever seen had tried to save himself and his brood by slandering worthy citizens of the République. By the by, where were they? Adèle of unknown parentage and the swaggering Lieutenant Godet? Ah bah! they were forgotten. Lost in the crowd. Who cared? Time enough to cheer them when the traitors and slanderers were punished. Who cared indeed? For the moment the most important thing in the world was to secure a place of vantage for witnessing the wonderful spectacle. The President of a revolutionary tribunal, a representative of the people in the National Convention, was not often to be seen in Orange mounting the steps of the guillotine. That spectacle was reserved for the Parisians﻿—lucky people!﻿—who saw the heads of ci-devant kings and queens, of generals and dukes and duchesses and of countless other aristos roll into the basket. Therefore everyone scrambled for a good seat. The houses all round the Place were invaded by the mob; windows and balconies were soon filled with eager faces; boys and men swarmed on the roofs, clung to the rain-pipes, the gargoyles on the Hôtel de Ville, the lampposts and lamp-brackets. Many were injured in the struggle. But that made no matter so long as one got a good seat. Fortunately the weather was glorious. The sun shone gaily on this scene which suggested a coming pageant.

===Chinese===
