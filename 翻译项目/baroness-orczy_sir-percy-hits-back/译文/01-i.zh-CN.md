## I / 第一章

===Original===

On the spot where the Hôtel Moderne now rears its more ambitious head, there stood at that time a cottage with sloping red-tiled roof and whitewashed walls. It was owned by one Baptiste Portal, an old peasant of the Dauphiné, who dispensed refreshments to travellers and passersby, as his father and grandfather had done before him, in the shape of somewhat thin *vin du pays* and an occasional glass of eau-de-vie, while he spent his slack time chiefly in grumbling at the fact that the new posting-inn on the high road had taken all his trade away. He did not see the necessity of the posting-inn, did not old Baptiste, nor for a matter of that of the high road or the post-chaise. Before all these new notions had come into the heads of the government people up in Paris, travellers had been content to come squelching through the mud on the back of a good horse, or come ploughing through inches of dust in the old *coche*. So why not now? And was not the old wine of Les Amandiers as good and better than the vinegar dispensed at the more pretentious posting-inn? The place was called Les Amandiers because at the back of the house there were two anaemic almond-trees with gaunt, twisted arms which covered themselves in the spring with sickly blooms, and in the summer with dust. In front of the house, up against the whitewashed wall, there was a wooden bench on which Baptiste’s privileged customers were wont to sit on fine evenings, to drink their vin du pays and join the old man in his wholesale condemnation of the government “up in Paris” and its newfangled ways. From this vantage-point a glorious view was obtained over the valley of the Buëche, and beyond Laragne as far as the peaks of Pelvoux: whilst to the right towered in the distance the grand old citadel of Sisteron with its turrets and fortifications dating from the fourteenth century, and the stately church of Notre Dame. But views and winding rivers, snowy peaks and medieval fortresses did not interest Baptiste Portal’s customers nearly as much as the price of almonds or the alarming increase in the cost of living.

===Chinese===

如今，在摩登旅馆（Hôtel Moderne）昂起它更气派的楼头的地方，当年立着一座村舍，红瓦坡顶，墙皮刷得雪白。店主人是巴普蒂斯特·波塔尔（Baptiste Portal），多菲内（Dauphiné）的一个老农，做的是祖上传下的款待行旅的营生——他父亲、他祖父当年怎么做，他也怎么做——卖些略嫌寡淡的本地土酒（*vin du pays*），偶尔再来一杯烧酒（eau-de-vie）；至于闲工夫，他多半用来嘟囔抱怨：大路上新开的那家客栈，把他家的生意全抢光了。老巴普蒂斯特就是看不出那客栈有什么必要——何止客栈，连那条大路、那种驿车（post-chaise），他通通看不出有什么必要。在巴黎当官的生出这些新花样之前，旅客们向来心满意足：要么骑一匹好马，一路踏着泥浆吧唧吧唧地来；要么坐着老式的乡村公共马车（*coche*），在几寸厚的尘土里犁地一般拱过来。如今凭什么就不行了？再说，杏树园（Les Amandiers）的老酒，难道不比那家更神气的客栈里卖的酸醋一样地好、还好上十分吗？这地方所以叫作“杏树园”，是因为屋后长着两棵面黄肌瘦的杏树，枝干枯瘦虬曲，春天给自己披一身病恹恹的花，夏天蒙一层灰扑扑的土。屋前贴着白灰墙摆着一条木凳，天气晴好的傍晚，巴普蒂斯特那些有脸面的老主顾惯于坐在那里，喝着本地土酒，同老头儿一道，把“巴黎城里”的政府和它那些新派做派骂个体无完肤。从这个居高临下的好位置望出去，景致极佳：比埃什河（Buëche）河谷尽收眼底，越过拉拉涅（Laragne），一直望到佩尔武（Pelvoux）的群峰；右侧远处，锡斯特龙（Sisteron）那座苍老雄伟的大城堡巍然耸立，塔楼与壁垒还是十四世纪传下来的，旁边立着庄严的圣母教堂（Notre Dame）。只是，蜿蜒的河流也好，雪峰古堡也好，在巴普蒂斯特·波塔尔的主顾们看来，都远不及杏仁的行情、生活费的高涨来得要紧。

===Original===

Now on this particular afternoon in May the mistral was blowing mercilessly across the valley from over the snows of Pelvoux, and the cold and the dust had driven all the good Portal’s customers indoors. The low-raftered room, decorated with strings of onions which hung from the ceiling together with a bunch or two of garlic, of basil and other potherbs, and perfumed also with the aroma of the pot-au-feu simmering in the kitchen, had acquired just that right atmosphere, cosy, warm and odorous, beloved of every true man born in Dauphiné. It was a memorable afternoon, remembered long afterwards and retold by the gossips of Sisteron and Laragne in all its dramatic details. But at this hour, nothing more dramatic had occurred than the arrival of a detachment of soldiers, under the command of an under-officer, who had come up from Orange, so they said, in order to fetch away the young men who were wanted for the army. They had demanded supper and shelter for the night.

===Chinese===

且说五月里的这个下午，密史脱拉风（mistral）正越过佩尔武的积雪，无情地刮过河谷来；天又冷，尘又大，把好心的波塔尔的主顾们全都赶进了屋。这间屋梁低矮的屋子，天花板上挂着一串串洋葱，还悬着一两把大蒜、罗勒和别样香草；厨房里煨着家常炖菜（pot-au-feu），香气一阵一阵透出来——恰好酿成了那种恰到好处的气氛：暖和，安逸，气味厚实，是每一个多菲内土生土长的真正汉子都心爱的。这是一个值得纪念的下午；事过多年，锡斯特龙和拉拉涅的爱嚼舌的人们还会把它讲给人听，一五一十，连那些富有戏剧性的细节也不放过。可是在眼下这个时辰，还谈不上什么戏剧性：无非是一小队士兵开到，领队的是个下级军官——据说是从奥朗日（Orange）开上来的，要把那些该去当兵的年轻人带走。他们要了晚饭，还要过夜的地方。

===Original===

Of course soldiers, as soldiers, were very much disapproved of by those worthies of Sisteron who frequented Les Amandiers, more especially now when what they did was to fetch away the young men for cannon-fodder, to fight the English and prolong this awful war which caused food to be so dear and hands for harvesting so scarce. But on the other hand, soldiers as company were welcome. They brought news of the outside world, most of it bad, it is true﻿—nothing good did happen anywhere these days﻿—but news nevertheless. And though at the recital of what went on in Paris, in Lyons or even as near as Orange, the guillotine, the tumbrils, the wholesale slaughter of tyrants and aristos, one shuddered with horror and apprehension, there were always the lively tales of barrack-life to follow, the laughter, the ribald song, and something of life seemed to filtrate into this sleepy half-dead corner of old Dauphiné.

===Chinese===

当然，在常来杏树园的那些锡斯特龙体面人眼里，兵——单说“兵”这个身份——本来就十分要不得，如今更是如此：这伙兵办的差事，就是把年轻人抓去当炮灰，去打英国人，去把这场可恶的战争拖长——仗打得粮价这样贵，收庄稼的人手这样缺。可是话又说回来，兵当作伴儿，倒受欢迎。他们带来外面世界的消息，多半是坏消息，不假——如今这年头，哪儿也出不来好事——可终归是消息。虽说一听人讲起巴黎、里昂（Lyons）、乃至近在眼前的奥朗日的情形——断头台呀，死囚车呀，对暴君和贵族佬们的大开杀戒呀——人人都要吓得心里发毛、脊背发凉，可接下来总还有营房生活的新鲜故事，有哄笑，有荤曲儿；于是一星半点的生气，似乎就这样渗进了老多菲内这个昏昏欲睡、半死不活的角落。

===Original===

The soldiers﻿—there were a score of them﻿—occupied the best place in the room, as was only fitting; they sat squeezed tightly against one another like dried figs in a box, on the two benches on either side of the centre trestle table. Old Baptiste Portal sat with them, beside the officer. Some kind of lieutenant this man appeared to be, or other subaltern; but oh dear me! these days one could hardly tell an officer from the ragtag and bobtail of the army, save for the fact that he wore epaulettes. Now this man﻿—but there! what was the use of comparing these ruffians with the splendid officers of the King’s armies in the past?

===Chinese===

士兵们——总有二十来个——占了屋里最好的位子，这也合该如此；他们分坐在当中那张支架长桌两侧的两条板凳上，一个紧挤着一个，活像盒子里码紧的干无花果。老巴普蒂斯特·波塔尔也同他们坐在一起，挨着军官。这人看模样是个中尉，再不就是别的什么下级军官；可是天可怜见！如今这年月，若不是肩上多一副肩章，你简直分不出军官同队伍里的残兵游勇来。至于这个人嘛——唉，罢了！拿这路痞棍去比当年王家军队里那些器宇轩昂的军官，又有什么用呢？

===Original===

This one certainly was not proud. He sat with his men, joked, drank with them, and presently he convened friend Portal to a glass of wine: “*A la santé*,” he added, “*de la République*, and of Citizen Robespierre, the great and incorruptible master of France!”

===Chinese===

这一位倒当真不摆架子。他同手下的兵坐在一起，说说笑笑，跟他们一块儿喝酒，不多会儿便邀老朋友波塔尔也来上一杯：“祝健康（*A la santé*），”他又添上一句，“祝共和国（*de la République*），也祝罗伯斯庇尔公民——法兰西伟大而不可腐蚀的主宰！”

===Original===

Baptiste, wagging his old head, had not liked to refuse, because soldiers were soldiers and these had been at great pains to explain to him that the reason why the guillotine was kept so busy was because Frenchmen had not yet learned to be good Republicans.

“We’ve cut off the head of Louis Capet and of the widow Capet too,” the officer had added with grim significance, “but there are still Frenchmen who are bad patriots and hanker after the return of the tyrants.”

===Chinese===

巴普蒂斯特摇着他苍老的脑袋，却不敢推辞：兵到底是兵；何况这伙人已经煞费苦心向他讲明白了，断头台之所以这样忙个不停，就是因为法国人还没学会做规矩的共和党人。

“路易·卡佩的脑袋，咱们砍了；卡佩寡妇的脑袋，咱们也砍了，”军官接着说，话里带着冷森森的深意，“可还有那么些法国人是坏爱国者，还眼巴巴地盼着暴君们卷土重来呢。”

===Original===

Now Baptiste, like all his like in the Dauphiné, had learned in childhood to worship God and honour the King. The crime of regicide appeared to him unforgivable, like that mysterious sin against the Holy Ghost, which M. le Curé used vaguely to hint at, and which no one understood. In addition to that, Baptiste greatly resented His late Majesty King Louis XVI and his august Queen being irreverently referred to as Louis Capet and the widow Capet. But he kept his own counsel and silently drank his wine. What his thoughts were at the moment was nobody’s business.

===Chinese===

巴普蒂斯特呢，同多菲内所有同他一路的人一样，打小儿受的就是敬上帝、尊君王的教育。弑君这桩罪，在他看来无可饶恕，就像亵渎圣灵那桩玄妙的罪过——本堂神父（M. le Curé）从前影影绰绰地点过那么一两回，可谁也没听懂过。不但如此，先王路易十六陛下和他尊贵的王后，如今竟被人没上没下地叫作路易·卡佩、卡佩寡妇，这也叫巴普蒂斯特心里老大不痛快。但他把话揣在肚里，闷声喝干了自己的酒。他这会儿在想些什么，与旁人无干。

===Original===

After that, talk drifted to the neighbourhood: the aristos who still clung to the land which by right belonged to the people. Neither Baptiste nor his customers﻿—old peasants from the district﻿—were a match for the lieutenant and his corporals in such discussions. They did not dare argue, only shook their heads and sighed at the coarse jests which the soldiers uttered against people and families whom everyone in the Dauphiné knew and esteemed.

The Frontenacs for instance.

The talk and the jests had turned on the Frontenacs: people who had owned the land for as long as the oldest inhabitant could remember and God only knows how long before that. Well! it appeared that in the eyes of these soldiers of the Republic the Frontenacs were bad patriots, tyrants and traitors. Didn’t Citizen Portal know that?

===Chinese===

打那以后，话头就转到了本乡本土：谈那些贵族佬——至今还死攥着照理该归人民的土地。在这种题目上，巴普蒂斯特同他的主顾——本区的老农们——哪里是那位中尉和他那几个下士的对手。他们不敢争辩，只是摇头，叹气，听凭士兵们把粗野的笑话往那几户人家身上泼——那几户人家，多菲内里里外外谁不认得、谁不敬重。

比如弗朗特纳克（Frontenac）一家。

话头和笑谑果然都落到弗朗特纳克一家头上：这片土地归他们家，年头久到本区最老的寿星也只能记得“打记事起就是”，再往前，只有天晓得了。嘿！原来在这些共和国士兵眼里，弗朗特纳克一家是坏爱国者、是暴君、是叛徒。波塔尔公民难道不知道么？

===Original===

No! Portal did not﻿—he had never been called “citizen” before, and didn’t like it: he was just Baptiste to those who knew him, *quoi*?﻿—Nor would he admit that the Frontenacs were traitors. There was Monsieur, who knew more about cattle and almonds than any man for leagues around. How could he be a bad patriot? And Madame, who was very good and pious, and Mademoiselle who was so ill and delicate. But on this there followed an altercation﻿—stern rebuke of Baptiste from the officer for talking of “Monsieur,” of “Madame” and “Mademoiselle.” Bah! there were no aristos left these days. “Aren’t we all citizens of France?” the lieutenant concluded grandiloquently.

===Chinese===

不知道！波塔尔不知道——他活到这把年纪，头一回被人称作“公民”，还老大不爱听：在熟人嘴里，他就是巴普蒂斯特，可不是么？——他也不肯承认弗朗特纳克一家是叛徒。且不说那位先生（Monsieur），论牛马、论杏仁，方圆多少里没有比他更在行的，他怎么会是坏爱国者？还有那位夫人（Madame），心肠那样好，那样虔诚；还有那位小姐（Mademoiselle），身子又那样娇，那样弱。可这句话惹出了一场口角——军官疾言厉色地训斥巴普蒂斯特，不许他“先生”“夫人”“小姐”地乱叫。呸！如今这年头，哪还剩什么贵族佬。“咱们不全都是法兰西的公民么？”中尉拿腔拿调地下了断语。

===Original===

Silence and submission on the part of all the groundlings which followed on the lieutenant’s rebuke, somewhat mollified the latter’s aggressive patriotism. He condescended to relate how he had been deputed to make a perquisition in the house of the Frontenacs, and if anything was found the least compromising, then the devil help the whole brood: their lives would not be worth an hour’s purchase. In fact, in the lieutenant’s opinion﻿—and who better qualified to hold one?﻿—the Frontenacs were already judged, condemned, and as good as guillotined. He held with the “law of the suspect,” lately enacted by the National Assembly, did Lieutenant Godet.

===Chinese===

中尉一顿训斥之后，满屋的乡巴佬鸦雀无声、俯首帖耳，这倒把他那咄咄逼人的爱国气焰抚平了几分。他屈尊讲起自己的差事来：原来他奉了委派，要去弗朗特纳克家宅里搜查；但凡搜出一星半点连累人的东西，那就让魔鬼去保佑他们一窝子吧——他们的性命连一个钟头也换不来。说实在的，照中尉的高见——这高见，谁比他更有资格持有呢？——弗朗特纳克一家早就是审过了、判定了、同送上断头台只差一刀的了。嫌疑犯法令是国民议会（National Assembly）新近颁布的，戈岱中尉（Lieutenant Godet）举双手拥护。

===Original===

Again much wagging of heads! “The Committees in all Sections,” Godet now goes on airily, and proceeds to pick his teeth after that excellently stewed scrag-end of mutton, “the Committees in all Sections are ordered in future to arrest all persons who are suspect.”

No one knows what is a Committee, nor yet a Section: but they are evidently fearsome things. But no matter about them: the thing is who are the “suspect” who are thus arrestable?

===Chinese===

跟着又是一片摇头！“各区（Section）的委员会，”戈岱这会儿轻飘飘地接着讲，一面把那顿美味的炖羊颈肉吃罢，剔起牙来，“各区的委员会奉了命令，今后凡有形迹可疑的人，一概逮捕。”

谁也不知道委员会是个什么，区又是个什么：可它们分明是些叫人害怕的物事。不过这些且不去管它：要紧的是，那些说抓就能抓的“嫌疑犯”，究竟是些什么人？

===Original===

“The Frontenacs are suspect,” the lieutenant explains whilst sucking his toothpick, “and so are all persons who by their actions﻿—or﻿—their writings have become﻿—er﻿—suspect.”

===Chinese===

“弗朗特纳克一家就是嫌疑犯，”中尉一面嘬着牙签，一面讲解，“凡是凭行动——或者——凭文字，变成了——呃——嫌疑犯的人，也都是。”

===Original===

Not very illuminating perhaps, but distinctly productive of awe. The worthies of Sisteron, those who are privileged to sit close to the centre table and actually to put in a word with the soldiers, sip their wine in silence. Just below the tiny window at the end of the room two charcoal-burners, or woodcutters﻿—I know not what they are﻿—are lending an attentive ear. They dare not join in the conversation because they are comparative strangers, vagabonds really, come to pick up a few sous by doing menial work too lowering for a local peasant to do. One of them is small and slender, but looks vigorous; the other, much older, with stooping shoulders, and grey, lank hair that falls over a wrinkled forehead. He is harassed by a constant, tearing cough which he strives in vain to suppress out of respect for the company.

===Chinese===

这话讲得也许不甚明白，威势却是一等一的。锡斯特龙的那些体面人——有幸挨着中间长桌坐、真能同士兵们搭上一句话的——都一声不响地小口呷着酒。就在屋子尽头那扇小窗底下，坐着两个烧炭夫，要不就是砍柴的——我也道不明他们究竟是干什么的——正竖起耳朵听着。他们不敢搭腔，因为他们是十足的外乡人，说穿了就是流浪汉，专靠干些本地庄稼汉嫌掉价的贱活儿，挣几个苏（sou）。两人里一个身材瘦小，瞧着却精悍；另一个上了年纪，肩膀佝偻，灰白稀疏的头发耷拉在起皱的额前。一阵阵撕心的咳嗽折磨着他；出于对在场诸位的敬意，他拼命想忍住，却总是忍不住。

===Original===

“But,” the worthy Portal puts in tentatively, “how does one know Monsieur le﻿—I mean citizen officer, that a person is in verity suspect?”

===Chinese===

“不过，”可敬的波塔尔试探着插了一句嘴，“长官先生——呃，我是说公民长官——一个人到底凭什么知道，什么人当真是个嫌疑犯呢？”

===Original===

The lieutenant explains with a sweeping gesture of the toothpick: “If you are a good patriot, Citizen Portal, you are able to recognize a Suspect in the street, you can seize him by the collar then and there, and you may drag him off before the Committee, who will promptly clap him in prison. ===Chinese===

中尉把牙签大手一挥，讲解道：“你要是个好爱国者，波塔尔公民，你就能在街面上认出嫌疑犯来，当场揪住他的领子，把他拖到委员会去，委员会立刻把他下进牢里。还有，记住，”他意味深长地补上一句，“如今法兰西总共有四万四千个委员会。”

===Original===

“Forty-four thousand?” somebody exclaims.

“And twenty-three,” Godet replies, gloating over his knowledge of this trifling detail.

“Forty-four thousand and twenty-three,” he reiterates and claps the table with the palm of his hand.

===Chinese===

“四万四千？”有人失声叫起来。

“还带二十三个零头，”戈岱答道，为自家连这点零碎都一清二楚而扬扬得意。

“四万四千零二十三，”他又重申一遍，一巴掌拍在桌面上。

===Original===

===Original===

“ ’Tis little Fleurette who will weep her eyes out,” good old Baptiste said with a doleful shake of the head, “if anything happens to Mad﻿—to the citizens up at the château.”

“She is Armand’s daughter﻿—Citizen Armand you know﻿—why﻿—?”
