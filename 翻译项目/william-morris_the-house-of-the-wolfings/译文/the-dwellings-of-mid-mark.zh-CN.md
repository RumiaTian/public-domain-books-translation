## I The Dwellings of Mid-Mark / ## I 中部马克的居留地

===Original===
The tale tells that in times long past there was a dwelling of men beside a great wood. Before it lay a plain, not very great, but which was, as it were, an isle in the sea of woodland, since even when you stood on the flat ground, you could see trees everywhere in the offing, though as for hills, you could scarce say that there were any; only swellings-up of the earth here and there, like the upheavings of the water that one sees at whiles going on amidst the eddies of a swift but deep stream.
On either side, to right and left the tree-girdle reached out toward the blue distance, thick close and unsundered, save where it and the plain which it begirdled was cleft amidmost by a river about as wide as the Thames at Sheene when the flood-tide is at its highest, but so swift and full of eddies, that it gave token of mountains not so far distant, though they were hidden. On each side moreover of the stream of this river was a wide space of stones, great and little, and in most places above this stony waste were banks of a few feet high, showing where the yearly winter flood was most commonly stayed.
You must know that this great clearing in the woodland was not a matter of haphazard; though the river had driven a road whereby men might fare on each side of its hurrying stream. It was men who had made that Isle in the woodland.

===Chinese===
故事讲述，往古之时，曾有一群人在一片大森林之畔栖居。居所前面是一片平原，不算十分辽阔，却宛如林海中的一座岛屿：因为即使你站在平地上，远望四方天际，也无处不见树木；至于山丘，则几乎说不上有无，只见大地在这里那里微微隆起，仿佛人在湍急而深沉的河流中、于漩涡之间不时见到的那一阵阵涌起的水波。
左右两翼，树木的环带向蓝色的远方伸展出去，茂密繁凑，绵延不绝，唯有当中一线，被一条河流连同它所环抱的平原从中劈开——那河之宽，约当泰晤士河（Thames）在希恩（Sheene）处满潮至顶时的宽度，然而水流湍急，漩涡重重，暗示着山峦并非远在天边，只是隐而不可见。再者，这河的两岸各有一片宽阔的乱石之滩，大石小石杂陈其上；多数地方，石滩之上更有几尺高的岸堤，标示着每年冬汛通常止于何处。
你须知，林中这一大片空旷之地，绝非出于偶然；虽然河流冲出了一条水路，使人得以沿着它奔腾的水流两岸往来通行，然而在这林海之中造出这座岛屿的，终究是人。

===Original===
For many generations the folk that now dwelt there had learned the craft of iron-founding, so that they had no lack of wares of iron and steel, whether they were tools of handicraft or weapons for hunting and for war. It was the men of the Folk, who coming adown by the riverside had made that clearing. The tale tells not whence they came, but belike from the dales of the distant mountains, and from dales and mountains and plains further aloof and yet further.
Anyhow they came adown the river; on its waters on rafts, by its shores in wains or bestriding their horses or their kine, or afoot, till they had a mind to abide; and there as it fell they stayed their travel, and spread from each side of the river, and fought with the wood and its wild things, that they might make to themselves a dwelling-place on the face of the earth.
So they cut down the trees, and burned their stumps that the grass might grow sweet for their kine and sheep and horses; and they diked the river where need was all through the plain, and far up into the wildwood to bridle the winter floods: and they made them boats to ferry them over, and to float down stream and track upstream: they fished the river’s eddies also with net and with line; and drew drift from out of it of far-travelled wood and other matters; and the gravel of its shallows they washed for gold; and it became their friend, and they loved it, and gave it a name, and called it the Dusky, and the Glassy, and the Mirkwood-water; for the names of it changed with the generations of man.

===Chinese===
如今栖居彼处的人民，许多世代以来已习得冶铸铁器之艺，故而铁器钢器从不匮乏，无论是工匠的器具，还是狩猎与征战用的兵器。开辟出这片空地的，正是这族民的男儿，他们沿河而下至此。故事并未言明他们来自何处，但多半是来自远山的溪谷，来自更远而又更远的溪谷、山峦与平原。
总之，他们是沿河而下的：或乘木筏浮于水上，或驾大车（wains）行于岸畔，或跨着他们的马匹牛只，或徒步而行，直到心生驻留之念；于是在这机缘所落之处，他们止住了行程，自河的两岸散布开去，与森林及林中的野兽搏斗，为的是在大地表面为自己造出一处家园。
于是他们砍倒树木，焚烧树桩，好教青草长得甘美，以饲他们的牛、羊、骏马；他们在平原上各处必要之地筑堤约束河流，更远筑到荒林深处，以驾驭冬日的洪水；他们造起船只，用以摆渡过河，用以顺流漂下、溯流挽拽；他们又拿网拿线在河的漩涡里捕鱼；从河中捞起远途漂来的木材及其他物件；在河中浅滩淘洗砂砾以取黄金。这河于是成了他们的朋友，他们喜爱它，给它起了名字，唤它作幽暗河（the Dusky）、澄光河（the Glassy）、密尔克伍德水（the Mirkwood-water）；因为河的名字，也随人世的世代更易。

===Original===
There then in the clearing of the wood that for many years grew greater yearly they drave their beasts to pasture in the new-made meadows, where year by year the grass grew sweeter as the sun shone on it and the standing waters went from it; and now in the year whereof the tale telleth it was a fair and smiling plain, and no folk might have a better meadow.
But long before that had they learned the craft of tillage and taken heed to the acres and begun to grow wheat and rye thereon round about their roofs; the spade came into their hands, and they bethought them of the ploughshare, and the tillage spread and grew, and there was no lack of bread.
In such wise that Folk had made an island amidst of the Mirkwood, and established a home there, and upheld it with manifold toil too long to tell of. And from the beginning this clearing in the wood they called the Mid-mark: for you shall know that men might journey up and down the Mirkwood-water, and half a day’s ride up or down they would come on another clearing or island in the woods, and these were the Upper-mark and the Nether-mark: and all these three were inhabited by men of one folk and one kindred, which was called the Markmen, though of many branches was that stem of folk, who bore divers signs in battle and at the council whereby they might be known.

===Chinese===
于是，在那片许多年来年年增拓的林间空地上，他们驱赶牲畜到新辟的草场放牧；阳光照临，积水退去，那里的青草一年甜似一年；到了故事所讲述的年代，那里已是一片明媚可亲的平原，任是哪一族人民，也占不到比这更好的草场了。
但远在那之前，他们已习得耕稼之艺，着意于田亩，开始在自家屋顶四周种起小麦黑麦；锹锄到了他们手里，他们又想到犁铧，于是耕作蔓延滋长，面包从不短缺。
这族人便这样在密尔克伍德（Mirkwood）当中造出一座岛屿，在那里建立起家园，又以种种辛劳将它撑持维系——其中周折，一言难尽。而从最初起，他们便把这林间空地称作中部马克（the Mid-mark）：因为你当知道，人沿密尔克伍德水上溯下行，无论哪个方向骑行半日之程，便会遇见林中又一片空地、又一座岛屿，那便是上部马克（the Upper-mark）与下部马克（the Nether-mark）；这三处所居的，本是同族同血的人民，称作马克人（the Markmen），只是这一族的根干枝分繁多，在战阵与议事之时，各打着不同的徽号，借以互相辨识。

===Original===
Now in the Mid-mark itself were many Houses of men; for by that word had they called for generations those who dwelt together under one token of kinship. The river ran from South to North, and both on the East side and on the West were there Houses of the Folk, and their habitations were shouldered up nigh unto the wood, so that ever betwixt them and the river was there a space of tillage and pasture.
Tells the tale of one such House, whose habitations were on the west side of the water, on a gentle slope of land, so that no flood higher than common might reach them. It was straight down to the river mostly that the land fell off, and on its downward-reaching slopes was the tillage, “the Acres,” as the men of that time always called tilled land; and beyond that was the meadow going fair and smooth, though with here and there a rising in it, down to the lips of the stony waste of the winter river.
Now the name of this House was the Wolfings, and they bore a Wolf on their banners, and their warriors were marked on the breast with the image of the Wolf, that they might be known for what they were if they fell in battle, and were stripped.

===Chinese===
且说中部马克本境之内，有许多人的家族（Houses）；因为世世代代以来，他们素来用这个词，称呼那些在同一亲缘徽号之下同居共处之人。河流自南向北奔流，东岸西岸都住着这族民的各家族，他们的居所紧紧倚向森林，因而在居所与河流之间，永远隔着一片耕地与牧场。
故事要讲的，便是这样一个家族：其居所在水的西岸，坐落于一处平缓的坡地上，寻常的洪水纵然涨高，也漫不到那里。那片土地大抵径直向河边倾落，下延的坡上便是耕地，即“耕亩”（the Acres）——当时的人总用这词称呼耕作过的土地；耕亩之外便是草场，平整秀美，只是间或略有隆起，一直延展到冬河那片乱石滩的唇边。
这家族的名字，唤作沃尔夫族（the Wolfings）。他们的旗帜上绣着一头狼，他们的武士胸前也打着狼的图像，如此，纵使他们阵亡沙场、被人剥去衣甲，人们也能认出他们本是何等样人。

===Original===
The house, that is to say the Roof, of the Wolfings of the Mid-mark stood on the topmost of the slope aforesaid with its back to the wildwood and its face to the acres and the water. But you must know that in those days the men of one branch of kindred dwelt under one roof together, and had therein their place and dignity; nor were there many degrees amongst them as hath befallen afterwards, but all they of one blood were brethren and of equal dignity. Howbeit they had servants or thralls, men taken in battle, men of alien blood, though true it is that from time to time were some of such men taken into the House, and hailed as brethren of the blood.
Also (to make an end at once of these matters of kinship and affinity) the men of one House might not wed the women of their own House: to the Wolfing men all Wolfing women were as sisters: they must needs wed with the Hartings or the Elkings or the Bearings, or other such Houses of the Mark as were not so close akin to the blood of the Wolf; and this was a law that none dreamed of breaking. Thus then dwelt this Folk and such was their Custom.
As to the Roof of the Wolfings, it was a great hall and goodly, after the fashion of their folk and their day; not built of stone and lime, but framed of the goodliest trees of the wildwood squared with the adze, and betwixt the framing filled with clay wattled with reeds. Long was that house, and at one end anigh the gable was the Man’s-door, not so high that a man might stand on the threshold and his helm-crest clear the lintel; for such was the custom, that a tall man must bow himself as he came into the hall; which custom maybe was a memory of the days of onslaught when the foemen were mostly wont to beset the hall; whereas in the days whereof the tale tells they drew out into the fields and fought unfenced; unless at whiles when the odds were over great, and then they drew their wains about them and were fenced by the wain-burg. At least it was from no niggardry that the door was made thus low, as might be seen by the fair and manifold carving of knots and dragons that was wrought above the lintel of the door for some three foot’s space. But a like door was there anigh the other gable-end, whereby the women entered, and it was called the Woman’s-door.

===Chinese===
中部马克沃尔夫族的屋宇，亦即他们的“大屋顶”（the Roof），便坐落在前述坡地的最高处，背倚荒林，面朝耕亩与河水。但你须知，在那年月，同一血亲支系的男儿共居于一个屋顶之下，在其中各有位分与尊荣；他们之间也不像后世那样生出许多等级，凡同一血统的人皆为兄弟，尊荣同等。话虽如此，他们也有仆役，即奴隶（thralls），都是战阵上掳来、血统外邦的人；不过诚然，这类人中也时时有被接入家族、尊为血亲兄弟的。
再者（索性把这类亲缘婚姻之事一并说尽），同一家族的男子不得娶本家族的女子：在沃尔夫族男子眼中，凡沃尔夫族女子皆如姊妹；他们必须与哈特族（the Hartings）、埃尔克族（the Elkings）、贝林族（the Bearings），或马克中其他与狼血不那么亲近的各族通婚；这是一条谁也未曾梦想违犯的法律。这族人便这样居住着，这便是他们的习俗。
至于沃尔夫族的大屋顶，那是一座宏大壮美的殿堂，一如他们族人与时代的风尚；不以石头石灰筑成，而是取荒林中最魁伟的佳木，以锛斧削正削方，构作骨架，骨架之间填上掺和芦苇的黏土。那屋宇甚是狭长，一端近山墙处是“男门”（the Man’s-door），门不算高，人立在门槛上，盔顶的翎饰也越不过门楣；因为风俗如此：高大的汉子进堂必得俯首躬身。这风俗，或许是对强敌来犯年月的记念——那时节，敌人大半习惯于围困殿堂；而到故事讲述的年代，人们已出到田野之间，坦然无垒而战；只有偶尔众寡过于悬殊之时，他们才把大车围拢起来，倚车垒（wain-burg）自守。至少，门造得这般低，绝非出于吝啬，只消看门楣之上约三尺宽的那一片雕工便知——绳结与蛟龙之纹，繁美多姿。而在另一端山墙近旁，还有一扇相类的门，供妇人出入，唤作“女门”（the Woman’s-door）。

Near to the house on all sides except toward the wood were there many bowers and cots round about the penfolds and the byres: and these were booths for the stowage of wares, and for crafts and smithying that were unhandy to do in the house; and withal they were the dwelling-places of the thralls. And the lads and young men often abode there many days and were cherished there of the thralls that loved them, since at whiles they shunned the Great Roof that they might be the freer to come and go at their pleasure, and deal as they would. Thus was there a clustering on the slopes and bents betwixt the acres of the Wolfings and the wildwood wherein dwelt the wolves.
As to the house within, two rows of pillars went down it endlong, fashioned of the mightiest trees that might be found, and each one fairly wrought with base and chapiter, and wreaths and knots, and fighting men and dragons; so that it was like a church of later days that has a nave and aisles: windows there were above the aisles, and a passage underneath the said windows in their roofs. In the aisles were the sleeping-places of the Folk, and down the nave under the crown of the roof were three hearths for the fires, and above each hearth a luffer or smoke-bearer to draw the smoke up when the fires were lighted. Forsooth on a bright winter afternoon it was strange to see the three columns of smoke going wavering up to the dimness of the mighty roof, and one maybe smitten athwart by the sunbeams. As for the timber of the roof itself and its framing, so exceeding great and high it was, that the tale tells how that none might see the fashion of it from the hall-floor unless he were to raise aloft a blazing faggot on a long pole: since no lack of timber was there among the men of the Mark.
At the end of the hall anigh the Man’s-door was the dais, and a table thereon set thwartwise of the hall; and in front of the dais was the noblest and greatest of the hearths; (but of the others one was in the very midmost, and another in the Woman’s-Chamber) and round about the dais, along the gable-wall, and hung from pillar to pillar were woven cloths pictured with images of ancient tales and the deeds of the Wolfings, and the deeds of the Gods from whence they came. And this was the fairest place of all the house and the best-beloved of the Folk, and especially of the older and the mightier men: and there were tales told, and songs sung, especially if they were new: and thereto also were messengers brought if any tidings were abroad: there also would the elders talk together about matters concerning the House or the Mid-mark or the whole Folk of the Markmen.

至于房子的近旁，除了朝林的一面，四面在围栏与牛栏（byres）的环拱之间，散布着许多棚屋小舍：这些是堆存什物的棚房，也是作种种工艺与打铁的所在——那些活计在大堂里做来不便；同时它们也是奴隶们的居处。少年与青年男子往往一连多日住在那里，受爱他们的奴隶们温存照拂；因为他们有时要避着大屋顶（the Great Roof），图个来去随心、行事自如的自由。就这样，在沃尔夫族的耕亩与栖狼的荒林之间，坡坡坎坎之上，屋舍簇聚成群。
至于堂的内部，两行巨柱纵贯全堂，皆以所能寻得的最魁伟的树木制成，每一根都精心雕琢过，柱础柱头、花环绳结、搏斗的武士与蛟龙，无不具备；因此整座殿堂宛如后世有中殿与侧廊的教堂：侧廊之上有窗，窗下廊顶里面各有一条走道。族人的睡处便设在侧廊里；而中殿之上、屋脊穹顶之下，沿堂设有三处灶膛生火，每处灶膛之上悬着一具引烟罩（luffer，即承烟之器），火燃起来时便将烟抽引上去。的确，在晴朗的冬日午后，看三道烟柱摇摇曳曳升向巨顶的幽暗，其中一道或许还被日光照得斜截一段，那是很奇异的景象。至于屋顶本身的木料与构架，其高其大，逾越常度，故而故事讲说：若不把一束燃着的柴火缚上长竿高高擎起，谁也不能从堂中的地面上看清它的构造——因为在马克人这里，木材是从来不缺的。
大堂尽头靠近男门处是高台（dais），台上横设着一张长桌；高台前面，是全堂最尊贵宏大的那处灶膛（其余两处，一在堂的正中央，一在女室（the Woman’s-Chamber））；高台四周，沿山墙一带，从这柱到那柱，悬挂着织造的帷幔，上面绘着古老传说的图景、沃尔夫族的功业，以及他们所从出的诸神（the Gods）的事迹。这是全屋最美好之处，也是族人最钟爱之所，年长的人与雄杰的人尤其喜爱：在这里，有人讲故事，有人唱歌谣，故事歌谣若是新出则尤妙；若外间有什么风声消息，信使也被引到此处；长老们也在这里聚谈家族的事、中部马克的事，乃至全体马克人大族的事。

Yet you must not think that their solemn councils were held there, the folk-motes whereat it must be determined what to do and what to forbear doing; for according as such councils, (which they called Things) were of the House or of the Mid-mark or of the whole Folk, were they held each at the due Thing-steads in the Wood aloof from either acre or meadow, (as was the custom of our forefathers for long after) and at such Things would all the men of the House or the Mid-mark or the Folk be present man by man. And in each of these steads was there a Doom-ring wherein Doom was given by the neighbours chosen, (whom now we call the Jury) in matters between man and man; and no such doom of neighbours was given, and no such voice of the Folk proclaimed in any house or under any roof, nor even as aforesaid on the tilled acres or the depastured meadows. This was the custom of our forefathers, in memory, belike, of the days when as yet there was neither house nor tillage, nor flocks and herds, but the Earth’s face only and what freely grew thereon.
But over the dais there hung by chains and pulleys fastened to a tie-beam of the roof high aloft a wondrous lamp fashioned of glass; yet of no such glass as the folk made then and there, but of a fair and clear green like an emerald, and all done with figures and knots in gold, and strange beasts, and a warrior slaying a dragon, and the sun rising on the earth: nor did any tale tell whence this lamp came, but it was held as an ancient and holy thing by all the Markmen, and the kindred of the Wolf had it in charge to keep a light burning in it night and day forever; and they appointed a maiden of their own kindred to that office; which damsel must needs be unwedded, since no wedded woman dwelling under that roof could be a Wolfing woman, but would needs be of the houses wherein the Wolfings wedded.
This lamp which burned ever was called the Hall-Sun, and the woman who had charge of it, and who was the fairest that might be found was called after it the Hall-Sun also.

然而你不要以为，他们庄严的议事——那些决定何事当行、何事当止的族民大会（folk-motes）——是在那里举行的；因为这类议事会（他们称之为“廷”，Things）无论是家族的、中部马克的还是全族的，都各自在林中相应的廷场（Thing-steads）举行，远离开耕地与草场（我们的祖先此后长时期都沿用这风俗）；在这样的廷会上，家族、中部马克或全族的男子，无不一人一人亲身到场。这些场地，每一处都设有一个判决环（Doom-ring），人与人之间的纠纷，便由推选出来的邻人（即我们今日所称的陪审团）在环中断决；这样的邻人之判、这样的人民之声，从不在任何屋宇之内、任何屋顶之下宣示，甚至如前所述，也不在耕过的田亩或放牧的草场上宣示。这是我们祖先的风俗，多半是为了记念那尚无屋宇、尚无耕稼、尚无畜群，只有大地的表面与其上自由生长之物的年月。
然而，在高台上空，有链子与滑轮系在屋顶高处的一根系梁上，垂下悬着一盏奇妙的灯，以玻璃制成；却不是当时当地族人所制的玻璃，而是一种如祖母绿一般清朗明净的碧色，通体饰着金色的图形与花结、奇异的走兽、一位屠龙的武士，还有冉冉升起于大地之上的太阳：没有任何故事说得出这灯从何而来，但全体马克人都奉它为古老而神圣之物，狼族受命在其中日夜点着一盏灯，永世不熄；他们从本族中指派一位少女执掌此职；这少女必须未嫁，因为凡居于那屋顶之下的已嫁妇人，都不可能是沃尔夫族女子，而必定出自沃尔夫族通婚的那些家族。
这盏长明的灯，唤作“堂阳”（the Hall-Sun）；而司掌此灯的女子——须是能寻见的最美之人——也循灯之名，同样唤作“堂阳”。
