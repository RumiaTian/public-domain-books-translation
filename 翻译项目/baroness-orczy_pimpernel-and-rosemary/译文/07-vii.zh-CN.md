## VII / 七
===Original===

The next two or three weeks were like a dream for Rosemary Fowkes. She left herself no time to think. The future beckoned to her with enticing arms, holding prospects of activities, of work that would fill the mind to the exclusion of memory. That evening when she rose from her knees, she rose with a resolve, and never for one moment after that did she allow herself an instant of regret. She wrote a line to Jasper to tell him that she would do as he wished; she was prepared to marry him as soon as his own arrangements were completed.
She also wrote to General Naniescu, agreeing to his proposal. She reserved to herself complete freedom of action to send any articles or reports she chose to English or foreign Press; all that she desired from him was a confirmatory letter, promising that nothing she ever wrote would pass through the censor’s hands. This he at once sent her. Nothing could be more fair, more straightforward. Rosemary’s chivalrous mind responded wholeheartedly to Naniescu’s generosity, and the feeling that it would probably be in her power to do real good, not only to individuals but to peoples, acted as a soothing balm upon her bruised heart.
On the other hand, nothing could have exceeded Jasper’s kindness and consideration during the days immediately preceding her marriage. It almost seemed as if his super-sensitive soul had received a faint inkling of what was going on in Rosemary’s mind. Nothing appeared too onerous, no sacrifice too great where Rosemary’s comfort and desires were at stake, and at times﻿—such are the contradictions of a woman’s nature﻿—she felt almost impatient with him for his magnanimity, almost obsessed by the unselfishness of his love.
She only saw Peter Blakeney once before she and Jasper left for Budapest, and that was on the day of her wedding. By one of those involuntary blunders so peculiar to dim-sighted lovers, Jasper Tarkington had asked Peter to be his best man. What it was that had induced Peter to accept, Rosemary could not conjecture. His impulses had always been strange and unaccountable, and this one was more unaccountable than most. Perhaps he merely wished to pander to his own mad desire to see her once again, perhaps it was just a semi-barbaric instinct in him that pushed him to self-torture.

接下来的两三个星期，对罗斯玛丽·福克斯来说恍如一场梦。她不给自己留一点思考的工夫。未来正张开诱人的双臂向她招手，捧出一派事业的前景，一桩桩足以填满心头、把记忆挤出去的工作。那天晚上她从膝上站起来时，是带着决断站起来的；打那以后，她一刻也不容自己有半瞬的悔意。她给贾斯珀写了一封短信，告诉他她愿意照他的意思办；只等他自己的安排停当，她随时可以同他结婚。
她还写信给南尼斯库将军，接受了他的提议。她给自己保留了完全的行动自由：想给英国或外国报刊投什么文章、什么报道，全由她自己；她向他要求的只有一封确认函，保证她写的任何东西都不经检查官之手。这封信他立刻就寄来了。再没有比这更公道、更爽直的了。罗斯玛丽那侠义的心肠全心全意地响应了南尼斯库的慷慨，而那种也许真能为个人、甚至为民族做点实事的感觉，像一剂安抚的香膏，敷在她受了伤的心上。
另一方面，在她婚期前的那些日子里，贾斯珀的体贴和关怀也无可复加。他那过分敏感的灵魂，仿佛已隐隐约约摸到了罗斯玛丽心里正在进行的事。凡关系到罗斯玛丽的舒适和心愿，没有什么差事是太重的，没有一样牺牲是太大的；有时——女人的天性本就这般矛盾——她几乎因他的宽宏而不耐烦，几乎被他那份无私的爱缠得发慌。
她同贾斯珀动身去布达佩斯之前，只见过彼得·布莱克尼一面，那正是在她结婚的那天。贾斯珀·塔金顿犯了近视的情人特有的那种不由自主的糊涂，竟请彼得做他的伴郎。是什么促使彼得答应下来，罗斯玛丽猜不透。他的冲动向来古怪、说不清，这一次比哪一次都更说不清。也许他不过想满足那再见她一面的疯狂欲望，也许只是他身上那股半野蛮的本能，推着他去自我折磨。

Rosemary by now had sufficient hold over herself to meet him calmly; not one line in her beautiful face, not one look in her haunting eyes, betrayed what she felt, after the wedding ceremony, when she accepted Peter’s warmly expressed good wishes for her happiness. Even her sensitive ear could not detect the faintest note of irony or bitterness in his voice. After that he said a few words about the projected journey to Hungary, about which Jasper had spoken to him. She would be seeing his relatives there﻿—the Imreys, the Heves. Elza Imrey was his mother’s sister and such a dear, and Philip used to be a jolly boy; but Rosemary knew them all. She knew she would be made very welcome. Peter ended by speaking with great earnestness about his little cousin Anna Heves; her father, who had been Mrs. Blakeney’s only brother, was dead, and Peter had an idea that Anna was not altogether happy.
“She has left home for some reason I can’t quite fathom,” he said, “and lives now at Kolozsvár﻿—I mean Cluj. She writes to me sometimes, and when I know the exact day when you will be in Cluj, I will write and tell her to go and see you. I suppose you will put up at the Pannonia?”
Rosemary nodded and Peter went on talking about little Anna, as he called her. “I know you will be kind to her,” he said. “You remember her as a child, of course; in a way she is still a child, and so pretty and enthusiastic. Give her a kiss from me when you see her.”
Which Rosemary, of course, promised to do. Then she gave him her hand, without saying anything, for she could not trust herself to speak much, and he kissed it just above the wrist, but more like a knight doing homage to his lady than a lover who gazed, perhaps for the last time, on the woman he worshiped.
It was after the marriage ceremony that the dreamland in which Rosemary had moved these past days became more intangible, more of a spirit-world than before. The brief days in a dreary hotel at Folkestone would have been unendurable but for her state of mind, which almost amounted to semiconsciousness. Then came the weary journey to Budapest, the sleepless night in the train, the awful meals in the crowded, stuffy restaurant-car, the ceaseless *rub-a-dub-dub*, *rub-a-dub-dub* of the wheels that bore her away farther﻿—ever farther from that bygone world which had become the might-have-been.

到了这时，罗斯玛丽已能把自己约束得足以平静地见他；婚礼之后，她接受彼得那番热烈道出的幸福祝愿时，美丽的脸上一丝纹路、那双萦怀难忘的眼睛里一丝神色，都没有泄露她心中的感受。连她那双敏感的耳朵，也听不出他嗓音里有半点讥诮或苦涩的影子。随后他谈了几句匈牙利之行——贾斯珀同他说过这事。她会在那边见到他的亲戚——伊姆雷家、赫维斯家。埃尔莎·伊姆雷是他母亲的妹妹，是位可人；菲利普从前是个快活孩子；可这些罗斯玛丽都认得。她知道自己会受到殷勤的接待。末了，彼得极恳切地谈起了他的小表妹安娜·赫维斯；她的父亲——布莱克尼太太唯一的兄弟——已经去世，彼得总觉得安娜过得并不怎么快活。
“她因为某种我摸不透的原因离家了，”他说，“现在住在克洛兹瓦尔（Kolozsvár）——我是说克卢日（Cluj）。她有时给我写信。等你确定了到克卢日的日子，我就写信叫她去看你。你们会住潘农尼亚旅馆吧？”
罗斯玛丽点头，彼得又继续谈小安娜——他这样称呼她。“我知道你会待她好的，”他说，“你当然记得她小时候的样子；某种意义上说她现在还是个孩子，又漂亮又热心。见到她时替我吻她一下。”
这个罗斯玛丽自然答应了。然后她伸出手去，什么也没说，因为不敢多开口；他在她腕上轻轻吻了那只手，不像一个也许正最后一次凝望心上人的恋人，倒像一位向贵妇致敬的骑士。
婚礼之后，罗斯玛丽这几天游历其中的梦境，变得更加抓摸不透了，比先前更像一个精灵世界。在福克斯通一家乏味的旅馆里度过的短短几日，若不是她那近乎半昏迷的心境，本是捱不下来的。接着是去布达佩斯的困乏旅程：火车上不眠的一夜，拥挤闷热的餐车里难以下咽的饭食，车轮没完没了的*哐嗒哐嗒*、*哐嗒哐嗒*，载着她越来越远——离那个已成“本可如是”的旧世界越来越远。

And through it all, like a ray of light, so persistent that it ceased to impress, was Jasper’s constant, unwearying care of her. He never seemed too tired to minister to her wants, to arrange cushions for her, a footstool, to open or close the window, the thousand and one little attentions, in fact, which most travellers are too self-engrossed to render.
And as Rosemary sat in her corner seat during those two wearisome days gazing out of the window with eyes that failed to take in the beauties of successive landscapes, her mind gradually became at peace with her heart. Her youth, her buoyancy of spirits, reasserted themselves, made her envisage life in all its brightest aspects, as it presented itself before her with cornucopia filled to the brim with all that made it worth the living. Work and a noble mate! What more could heart of woman desire? And Rosemary closed her eyes, and in a quickly fleeting dream sighed for the one thing that would have made her life a paradise, and﻿—still dreaming﻿—she felt hot tears of regret trickle slowly down her cheeks.
She woke to feel Jasper’s arms around her and his lips kissing away her tears.

而在这一切之中，像一道光线一样贯穿始终、持久得反而不为人觉察的，是贾斯珀对她无微不至、不懈不怠的照料。他从不显得疲惫得顾不上伺候她的需要：替她垫靠垫、摆脚凳、开窗关窗，以及千百件大多数旅人因为只顾自己而懒得去做的小殷勤。
那劳顿的两天里，罗斯玛丽坐在角落的座位上望着窗外，一任眼前次第闪过的美景从眼中溜走；渐渐地，她的头脑同她的心达成了和解。她的青春、她轻快的天性重新抬头，教她从最明亮的一面看待人生——人生正捧着一只堆满值得活着的一切的丰饶角站在她面前。事业，和高贵的伴侣！女人的心还能奢求什么？于是罗斯玛丽合上眼，在一个倏忽即逝的梦里，为那唯一能把她的生命变成天堂的东西叹息了一声——仍旧在梦里——她感到悔恨的热泪顺着脸颊慢慢地淌。
她醒过来时，只觉贾斯珀的手臂搂着她，他的唇正把她的眼泪一一吻去。
