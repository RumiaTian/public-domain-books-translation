## V / 五
===Original===

Outside in the corridor Rosemary met Sir George Orange, who claimed her then and there and dragged her willy-nilly to his wife’s box. She never looked back once to see what Peter was doing. He had become merged in the crowd, and, anyway, this was the end.
She found herself presently being talked to, flattered, adulated by the distinguished Romanian who turned the full battery of his mellow eyes and his persuasive tongue upon her, bent on making a breach in the wall of her prejudices and her thinly veiled enmity.
She told no one, not even Jasper, the gist of her conversation with Naniescu. He had put a proposal before her﻿—a proposal which meant work for Rosemary Fowkes﻿—the Uno of the *International Review*. He had proposed that she should go to Transylvania, study for herself the conditions now prevailing in the territory occupied by Romania, and publish the result of her studies in the English and American Press. And this was just the sort of work that Rosemary longed for, now, more than at any other time of her life. Naniescu had played his cards well. He had known how to flatter, insidiously, delicately, this popular writer who had captured the public fancy, and whose influence with pen and personality was paramount with a vast section of review and newspaper readers in England. What he had proposed could in no way hurt the most delicate scruples of an oversensitive conscience, and the proposal came as a veritable Godsend to Rosemary at this moment when her whole soul was in a turmoil of remorse, longing, and rebellion. That her love for Peter Blakeney was not dead, she had known well enough all along, but she had little dreamed until this hour how completely it still possessed her, what power his glance, his touch, his nearness still had over her. She had thought of her love as a heap of smouldering ashes, and lo! it had proved itself to be a devastating fire that burned fiercely beneath.
And Peter?
Peter had set the future above the present; his pride above his love, and she, wounded to the quick, had allowed ambition and pride to throw her into Jasper Tarkington’s arms. It was all done now. Irrevocably done. But even at the moment when she most bitterly regretted the past, she was resolved to keep her word loyally to Jasper. Sitting beside him in the car that took her home from the Albert Hall ball, she allowed her hand to rest contentedly in his.

门外的走廊里，罗斯玛丽遇上了乔治·奥兰治爵士，他当场便把她认下，不由分说拉到妻子的包厢里去了。她一次也没有回头去看彼得在做什么。他已经没入人群里了；再说，这也就是终结了。
不一会儿，她就发觉自己正被那位尊贵的罗马尼亚人攀谈着、恭维着、奉承着；他把那双温柔的眼睛和那条娓娓动听的舌头全部火力都对准了她，一心要在她偏见和那层薄纱似的敌意的高墙上轰开一道缺口。
她同南尼斯库谈话的要旨，她谁也没有告诉，连贾斯珀也没告诉。他向她提出了一项提议——一项意味着给罗斯玛丽·福克斯、《国际评论》（International Review）的“乌诺”派下差事的提议。他提议她到特兰西瓦尼亚去，亲自考察罗马尼亚占领区目前的状况，把考察结果发表在英美报刊上。而这正是罗斯玛丽渴望的那一类工作，此刻比她一生任何时候都更渴望。南尼斯库这一手玩得漂亮。他知道怎样阴柔而巧妙地奉承这位风靡一时的作家——她赢得了公众的喜爱，她的笔和她的个人魔力，对英国评论和报纸读者中的一大群人有着至高无上的影响。他所提议的事，无论如何伤不着最敏感的良心、最纤细的顾忌；而对此刻的罗斯玛丽——她的整个灵魂正泡在悔恨、渴望与反抗的翻腾里——这项提议无异于天赐。她对彼得·布莱克尼的爱并没有死，这一点她心里一直清楚；可直到此刻她才约略梦到，这爱依然多么彻底地占有着她，他的目光、他的触碰、他的贴近，依然对她有多大的威力。她一直把自己的爱当作一堆焖着的死灰，可你瞧！它偏偏证实自己是一场烈火，在底下凶猛地烧。
那么彼得呢？
彼得把将来摆在了现前之上，把骄傲摆在了爱情之上；而她，伤到了心坎里，便任凭野心和骄傲把她抛进了贾斯珀·塔金顿的怀抱。如今一切都定了。无可挽回地定了。可即使在她最痛悔过去的此刻，她也决心忠诚地履行对贾斯珀的诺言。坐在送她从阿尔伯特音乐厅舞会回家的汽车里，她让自己的手安安稳稳地留在他的手里。

===Original===

His arm was round her, and her cheek rested against his shoulder. She did not speak, for she was very tired, but she listened, unshrinking, to the tender words which he whispered in her ear. Dear, kind Jasper! He had thoughts only for her. From the moment when she finally promised that she would be his wife, he had loaded her with delicate attentions and exquisite gifts. Every word he spoke was soothing and restful, so different to Peter’s tempestuous outbursts, his unrestrained, passionate eloquence that would leave her limp and bruised, unable to understand his next mood, his sudden indifference to everything save his own future pursuits.

他的手臂搭着她的肩，她的脸颊靠在他肩上。她不说话，因为她太累了；可他把那些温柔的话贴在她耳边低语时，她毫不畏缩地听着。亲爱又厚道的贾斯珀！他心里只有她。从她最终答应做他妻子的那一刻起，他就用体贴的关切和精美的礼物把她堆得满满的。他说的每一句话都抚慰、都安然，同彼得的暴风骤雨多么不同——那不由分说、火热燎人的雄辩，总把她弄得瘫软乌青，摸不透他下一刻的心思，摸不透他对眼前一切、除他自己前程之外的一切，怎么说冷就冷了。
