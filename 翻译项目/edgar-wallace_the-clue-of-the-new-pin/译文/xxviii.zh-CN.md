## XXVIII / 二十八

===Original===

There came to police headquarters a tall, ruddy-skinned man of middle age. He wore a suit which was evidently not made for him, and he seemed a little depressed by his surroundings.
“I have an appointment with Inspector Carver,” he said and passed a letter across the desk to the police clerk, who read it and nodded.
“Inspector Carver is expecting you,” he said and called a messenger.

===Chinese===

一个身材高大、面色红润的中年男子来到了警察总部。他身上那套西装分明不是照他的身量做的，周遭的环境似乎又让他有些局促。
“我同卡弗探长有约。”他说着，把一封信从柜面上递给警察局文书；文书读罢，点了点头。
“卡弗探长正候着您。”他说着，唤来一名传达。

===Original===

Carver looked round as the door opened and viewed his caller with a speculative eye. Then he jumped up.
“Of course!” he said. “Sit down please.”
“I hope﻿—” began the man, “there isn’t going to be any trouble.”
“Not for you,” said Carver, “but I rather fancy there is trouble coming for somebody.”
The messenger closed the door and left them together.

===Chinese===

房门开处，卡弗回过头来，用一副审度的目光打量来客。随即他一跃而起。
“哎呀，果然是您！”他说，“请坐。”
“我希望——”那人开口道，“但愿别出什么乱子才好。”
“对您不会，”卡弗说，“不过我倒是觉得，有人要大祸临头了。”
传达掩上房门，留下他们两人。

===Original===

Half an hour later Inspector Carver telephoned for the office stenographer, and when the harassed man with the fresh face and ill-fitting clothes left the police office after a three-hour examination, Inspector Carver had material for much cogitation.
Tab called in the ordinary way of duty and they discussed the latest tragedy, but never once did Inspector Carver make reference to his visitor of the morning. That was his secret and too precious a one, in the circumstances, to breathe to a soul.

===Chinese===

半个钟头后，卡弗探长打电话叫来局里的速记员。等这位被折腾得疲惫不堪、面色红润、衣衫不合身的男子熬过三个钟头的盘问、走出警察局时，卡弗探长手里已经攥下了够他反复琢磨的许多材料。
塔布照常因公来访，两人谈起最近那桩惨案，可卡弗探长对上午那位来客连提都没提一句。那是他的秘密，在眼下这种情势下太珍贵了，不能向任何人吐露半个字。

===Original===

He drove that afternoon to the detention prison where Walters was awaiting trial and had a long talk with him. Yeh Ling was in his parlour, halfway through his long weekly letter to his son, when Inspector Carver was announced. He put down his brush and gazed impassively upon the servant who had brought in the inspector’s card.
“Is this man alone?” he asked.
“Yes, Yeh Ling. There is no man with him.”
Yeh Ling tapped his white teeth with his well-manicured nails.
“Come,” he said laconically and there was something in Carver’s face which told Yeh Ling all that he wanted to know. But there was a fight to be made yet and he was not without hope that this matter of the Trasmere murder and the tragedy that had followed, would be settled in a manner more consonant with his keen sense of obligation.

===Chinese===

当天下午，他驱车前往还押监狱——沃尔特斯正在那里候审——同他长谈了一番。禀报卡弗探长到访时，叶凌正坐在自己的客厅里，给他儿子的每周一封的长信才写到一半。他放下毛笔，木然望着那个递进探长名片的仆人。
“这人是独自来的？”他问。
“是的，叶凌。他没有带别人来。”
叶凌用修剪得整整齐齐的指甲，轻轻叩着雪白的牙齿。
“带他进来。”他言简意赅地说。卡弗脸上的某种神色，已把叶凌想知道的一切都告诉了他。不过，还有一场硬仗要打；对于特拉斯米尔谋杀案连同随后那桩惨案，他并非没有指望——指望事情最终能以一种更合乎他那强烈义务感的方式了结。

===Original===

The inspector did not come to the point at once. He accepted a cigar that the Chinaman offered to him, spoke jocularly of Yeh Ling’s letter writing, asked a question or two about Ursula Ardfern, and at last hinted at the object of his visit.
“Yeh Ling,” he said, “I think the Trasmere case is coming to a solution.”
Yeh Ling’s eyelids did not so much as flicker.
“In fact,” said the inspector, carefully examining the ash of his cigar, “I have found the murderer.”
Yeh Ling said nothing.
“I need very little confirmatory evidence to put the man who killed Jesse Trasmere on the trap,” Carver went on.

===Chinese===

探长没有一上来就直奔正题。他接过这位中国人敬上的雪茄，就叶凌写信的事说笑了几句，问了一两个关于乌苏拉·阿德芬的问题，最后才隐隐点出来意。
“叶凌，”他说，“依我看，特拉斯米尔案快要水落石出了。”
叶凌的眼皮连一下都没有颤动。
“事实上，”探长说着，仔细端详着自己雪茄上的烟灰，“凶手我已经找到了。”
叶凌一言不发。
“只消再添上极少的一点补强证据，就能把杀害杰西·特拉斯米尔的人送上绞架了。”卡弗接着说。

===Original===

“And you have come to me to furnish that evidence,” said Yeh Ling with a touch of irony.
Carver shook his head and smiled.
“I don’t know﻿—I didn’t think you would,” he said, and then almost sharply: “Where are the documents you took from the Trasmere house the night you went there with Miss Ardfern?”
The Chinaman got up without hesitation, unlocked a small safe in the corner of the room and brought out a thick packet of papers.
“They are all there?” asked Carver, shooting a suspicious glance at the other.
“All except two,” was the cool reply, “one of which has reference to my interest in the Golden Roof and that is with my lawyer﻿—”

===Chinese===

“于是您就上我这儿来，要我供上这些证据。”叶凌的话里带一丝讥诮。
卡弗摇了摇头，莞尔一笑。
“这我说不准——我本来也没指望您肯。”他说，随即语气近乎凌厉，“那天夜里您同阿德芬小姐到特拉斯米尔宅子里取走的那些文件，如今都在哪里？”
这位中国人毫不迟疑地起身，打开房间角落里一只小保险柜，取出一厚沓文件。
“都在这里头了？”卡弗问，朝对方射去一道狐疑的目光。
“只少了两份，”回答得冷冷静静，“其中一份关乎我在金顶楼的权益，在我律师手里——”

===Original===

“And the other?” asked the detective.
“That deals with matters of a sacred nature,” said Yeh Ling in that precise English that sounded almost affected.
Carver bit his lip.
“You know that this is the document I particularly want?” he asked.
“I guessed that,” was the reply. “Nevertheless, Mr. Carver, I cannot give it to you, and if you know so much,” for a second a ghost of a smile lit his brown eyes, “then you will know why it is not forthcoming.”

===Chinese===

“另一份呢？”这位侦探问。
“那一份涉及一些神圣性质的事务。”叶凌用他那字斟句酌、几乎听来做作的英语说。
卡弗咬了咬嘴唇。
“您知道，我特别想要的就是这一份吧？”他问。
“这个我猜到了。”对方答道，“然而，卡弗先生，我不能把它交给您。您既已知道这么多——”刹那间，一抹笑影点亮了他褐色的眼睛，“——那您也该知道，它为什么拿不出来。”

===Original===

“Does Miss Ardfern know?”
Yeh Ling shook his head.
“She is the one person who must not know,” he said emphatically. “If it were not for her﻿—” he shrugged his shoulders, “you might see it.”
Carver knew that he was opposed by a will greater than his own and that neither threats nor promises would move this impassive man from the attitude he had taken up.

===Chinese===

“阿德芬小姐知道吗？”
叶凌摇了摇头。
“她正是唯一不可以知道的人。”他加重语气说，“若不是为了她——”他耸了耸肩，“您原本是可以看的。”
卡弗明白，自己面对的是一道比他更坚强的意志；无论威胁还是许诺，都不能叫这个不动声色的人把他抱定的态度挪动分毫。

===Original===

“What does it matter whether you see this paper or not?” asked Yeh Ling. “You say you know the murderer, that you have sufficient evidence to put him on the trap﻿—but have you?”
His look was a challenge.
“You cannot convict a man on supposition, Mr. Carver. You must prove beyond any doubt whatever that Jesse Trasmere was killed by somebody who had the means of getting in and out that locked vault, and leaving the key on the table. It is not enough to say: ‘I am certain that this prisoner killed his﻿—benefactor.’ It is not sufficient that you can show motives. You must produce the means! Until you can say the murderer obtained admission to the vault by this or that door, in this or that way, or that he employed these or those means to restore the key to the table from the outside of a locked door through which no key could pass, you cannot secure a conviction. That is the law. I studied law at Harvard, and I have the rules of evidence at my fingertips,” he smiled faintly. “You see, Mr. Carver, that the confirmatory evidence you require cannot possibly be supplied by me.”

===Chinese===

“这张纸您看得着看不着，又有什么要紧？”叶凌反问，“您说您知道凶手是谁，说您手里的证据已足以送他上绞架——果真如此吗？”
他的目光形同一纸挑战书。
“单凭臆测，您是定不了一个人的罪的，卡弗先生。您必须证明得毫无疑义：杰西·特拉斯米尔死于某人之手，而此人有办法进出那间锁死的保险库，并把钥匙留在桌上。光说一句‘我确信这名囚犯杀死了他的——恩主’，是不够的。您能摆出动机，也不够。您必须拿出作案的手段来！直到您能说清凶手是从这扇门还是那扇门、以这种途径还是那种途径进入的保险库，说清他用了这样或那样的什么手段，把钥匙从一扇上了锁、钥匙根本无法穿越的门外送回到了桌上，您才拿得下有罪判决。法律便是如此。我在哈佛学的是法律，证据规则我可烂熟于心。”他淡淡一笑。“您瞧，卡弗先生，您所需要的补强证据，无论如何不可能由我来供给。”

===Original===

Carver knew that he was speaking no more than the truth; that he was against a dead wall unless some human eye had witnessed the murder, and the method by which the murderer escaped.
The logic of the Chinaman’s criticism was irresistible, and Carver, who had seen success within reach, experienced a sense of failure at the very moment when he thought that all his efforts were coming to fruition.
“Then tell me this,” he said. “I understand that on several occasions you have been followed by this Man in Black. Have you any idea who he is?”
“Yes,” said the other without hesitation, “but what is the value of my ideas? I could not swear to any facts, and facts are the meat and drink of juries, Mr. Carver.”

===Chinese===

卡弗知道他句句是实情：除非有一双世人的眼睛亲眼目睹了凶案、目睹了凶手脱身的手法，否则自己便是撞在一堵死墙上。
这位中国人评论的逻辑无可辩驳。卡弗本眼看成功触手可及，却在自以为心血即将结出果实的那一刻，尝到了挫败的滋味。
“那么请您告诉我一件事，”他说，“据我所知，那个黑衣人已经尾随过您好几回。他是什么人，您心里可有数？”
“有数。”对方毫不迟疑地答道，“可我的想法又值什么？任何事实我都无从起誓，而事实，才是陪审团吃惯喝惯的饭食，卡弗先生。”

===Original===

Carver got up and sighed heavily, and hearing him Yeh Ling broke into a fit of silent laughter.
“I am sorry,” he apologised, “but I am thinking of my favorite poem,” his eyes twinkled. “You remember the other gentleman who ’rose with a sigh, and he said, ‘Can this be? We are ruined by Chinese cheap labour.﻿ ﻿…’ ”
“I shall certainly not ‘go the Heathen Chinee,’ ” said Carver good-humouredly, “not for the moment. What I like about you, Yeh Ling, is your refreshing sanity. I don’t know that I have ever dealt with a man﻿—shall I say fought with a man?﻿—who would have given me greater pleasure to fence.”
The Chinaman performed a deep kowtow and his mock humility amused Carver long after he had left the shadow of the Golden Roof.

===Chinese===

卡弗站起身，重重叹了口气。叶凌听见了，忽然发作起一阵无声的大笑。
“失礼了，”他赔罪道，“只是我想起了我心爱的那首诗。”他两眼发亮，“您总还记得诗里另外那位先生吧——他‘叹了口气站起身来，说道：“竟会这样？我们全叫中国廉价劳工给毁了……”’”
“我是断断不会‘败于异教中国佬’的，”卡弗和颜悦色地说，“至少眼下还不会。叶凌，我喜欢您的一点，就是您那份令人神清气爽的清明通达。我说不好，我平生打过交道的——不妨说交过手的——人里头，可曾有过哪一位，比您更让我觉得交锋是一件快事。”
这位中国人深深行了一个磕头礼；他那佯装的谦卑，让卡弗在走出金顶楼的楼影之后许久，心里还觉得好笑。

===Original===

Yeh Ling, who seldom made any personal effort for the comfort of his guests, paid particular attention to the preparations which were being made in No. 6 that night. The Italian waiters, to whom the proprietor was almost unknown, were both nervous and annoyed, for nothing seemed to please Yeh Ling. He had the flowers changed half-a-dozen times. He had new cloths brought and at the last minute insisted upon the table being laid all over again. He brought the rarest of glass to adorn the board, unearthed unsuspected treasures in chinaware, and substituted them for the crockery of the restaurant. This done, he summoned to his room the maître d’hôtel and the wine chef, and chose the dinner with the most exquisite care.

===Chinese===

叶凌向来很少为宾客的舒适亲自费神，那天晚上却对 6 号房的备办格外上心。那些几乎没见过老板本人的意大利侍者，一个个又紧张又气恼，因为似乎没有一样东西能合叶凌的意。插花他叫人换了六回；新桌布也叫人取来，到了最后一刻，他还坚持把整张餐桌重新摆放一遍。他又取出最珍罕的玻璃器皿来装点桌面，翻出瓷器中无人知晓的珍藏，把餐馆的日常陶器统统换了下来。这一切办妥，他才把侍者总管和司酒师傅召到房里，以最精细的心思点定了菜式。

===Original===

“Yeh Ling has really done himself proud,” said Tab admiring the table.
The girl nodded. She had hoped that Yeh Ling would have chosen another room, but she had no real feeling of repugnance and besides she had been here since Trasmere’s death.
“It is very thrilling to be dining alone with a young man,” she said, handing her wrap to the waiter, “and I can only hope the scandal of it doesn’t get into the newspapers!”
“Shall we see Yeh Ling?” asked Tab, halfway through the dinner.

===Chinese===

“叶凌这回可真给自己挣足了体面。”塔布打量着餐桌，赞叹道。
姑娘点了点头。她原本希望叶凌会另选一间房，可她心里并无真正的不快，况且，特拉斯米尔死后她一直在这间房里用餐。
“单独同一位年轻男子吃饭，真是够刺激的。”她把披肩递给侍者时说道，“我只盼这桩丑闻别登上报纸才好！”
“咱们能见着叶凌吗？”吃到一半，塔布问。

===Original===

She shook her head.
“He never appears. He has only been in this room twice to my recollection.”
“It is our first appearance together in public,” said Tab solemnly. “I can count on our boys, but if any of those *Herald* thugs hear and catch a glimpse of your expensive ring, there are going to be scare lines in that deplorable rag﻿—the ‘Herald’ has no reticence on decency.”
She laughed softly and looked at the “expensive ring” that glittered and sparkled in the light of the shaded lamp.
“I asked Carver if he would come along after dinner,” said Tab, “but he is busy. He sent the most flowery and poetical messages to you﻿—really Carver is a surprising person; there is a whole world of romance hidden behind that somewhat unpleasing exterior, if you will pardon the journalese.”

===Chinese===

她摇了摇头。
“他从不露面。就我所记得的，他统共只进过这间房两回。”
“这是咱们俩头一回一起公开露面。”塔布郑重其事地说，“自家报馆的弟兄我信得过；可要是叫《先驱报》那帮恶客听了风声，再瞥上一眼您那枚昂贵的戒指，那份糟糕透顶的破报上就要登出耸人听闻的大字标题来了——《先驱报》在体统上是毫无忌惮的。”
她轻轻笑了，低头看那枚在灯罩滤下的光里熠熠闪烁的“昂贵戒指”。
“我请卡弗饭后来一趟，”塔布说，“可他忙。他托我向您转致最花团锦簇、最富诗意的问候——卡弗这人真是出人意料；他那副不怎么讨人喜欢的外表底下，藏着一整个浪漫世界，恕我借用一句报馆行话。”

===Original===

But if Carver could not come, they had a visitor. There came a tap and the door opened slowly.
“Great Moses!” said Tab springing up. “How the dickens did you know that we were here, Rex?”
“I spotted you,” said Rex Lander reproachfully, “slinking in at the side door like two guilty souls! May I offer my congratulations, Miss Ardfern, and lay at your feet the fragments of a broken heart?”
She laughed nervously at his jest.
“No, I can’t stay,” said Rex, “I have a party and, moreover, I am entertaining a man with terrific ideas on architecture. Isn’t that queer! Now that I am no longer arcing, I have conceived a passion for that unhallowed profession! Even old Stott is becoming an admirable personage in my eyes. Have you forgiven me, Miss Ardfern?”

===Chinese===

卡弗虽然来不了，他们却等来了一位访客。门上响起轻轻的叩声，房门缓缓开了。
“我的老天爷！”塔布一跃而起，“你到底怎么知道我们在这儿的，雷克斯？”
“我可是亲眼瞧见的，”雷克斯·兰德话里带着嗔怪，“瞧你们俩跟两个亏了心的魂灵似的，从边门溜了进来！请容我道贺，阿德芬小姐，并将一颗碎心的残片敬奉于您的足下。”
对他这句玩笑，她笑得有些不安。
“不，我不能久留，”雷克斯说，“我还有个饭局，况且我正陪着一位对建筑学怀有惊人高见的先生。怪也不怪！如今我不再干建筑这一行了，反倒对那门亵渎神明的行当起了满腔热忱！连老斯托特在我眼里都渐渐成了可敬可佩的人物。您原谅我了吗，阿德芬小姐？”

===Original===

“Oh yes,” she said quietly, “I have forgiven you a very long time ago.”
Rex’s baby eyes were very kindly, his plump face was wrinkled in a smile of amiable reflection.
“ ‘When a young man’s fancy﻿—’ ” he began and caught a reflection in the mirror.
From where Tab and the girl sat they could see nothing. Rex saw reflected the half-open door and a figure that stood motionless outside. He spun round with an exclamation.

===Chinese===

“哦，原谅了。”她平静地说，“很久很久以前，我就原谅你了。”
雷克斯那双娃娃眼盛满和善，胖乎乎的脸皱起一团笑纹，透着可亲的沉思之色。
“‘当年轻人的情思——’”他刚起了个头，就瞥见了镜子里的映影。
从塔布和姑娘所坐的位置望去，什么也看不见。雷克斯却在镜中望见那扇半开的门，望见一个在门外一动不动伫立的人影。他惊呼一声，猛地转过身去。
