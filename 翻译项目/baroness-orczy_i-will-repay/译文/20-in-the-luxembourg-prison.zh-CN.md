## XVIII In the Luxembourg Prison / XVIII 卢森堡狱中

===Original===
Juliette was alone at last—that is to say, comparatively alone, for there were too many aristocrats, too many criminals and traitors, in the prisons of Paris now, to allow of any seclusion of those who were about to be tried, condemned, and guillotined.

The young girl had been marched through the crowded streets of Paris, followed by a jeering mob, who readily recognised in the gentle, high-bred girl the obvious prey, which the Committee of Public Safety was wont, from time to time to throw to the hungry hydra-headed dog of the Revolution.

Lately the squalid spectators of the noisome spectacle on the Place de la Guillotine had had few of these very welcome sights: an aristocrat—a real, elegant, refined woman, with white hands and proud, pale face—mounting the steps of the same scaffold on which perished the vilest criminals and most degraded brutes.
===Chinese===
朱丽叶特终于独自一人了——也就是说，只是相对而言的独自一人；因为如今巴黎各座监狱里的贵族、罪犯和叛徒实在太多，那些即将受审、定罪、送上断头台的人，根本谈不上有什么清静的独处。

这位年轻姑娘被押着穿过巴黎拥挤的街道，身后跟着一群嘲骂起哄的暴民；他们一眼就认出，这个温柔而教养高贵的姑娘，正是救国委员会惯于时不时抛出去的那种现成的猎物——抛给革命那头饥饿的九头怪犬。

近来，断头台广场（Place de la Guillotine）上那出龌龊刑戏的脏看客们，已经难得见到这类极合他们胃口的景象了：一位贵族——一位真正的、优雅而有教养的女人，一双手白皙，一张脸高傲而苍白——正登上那同一座刑台的台阶，最卑劣的罪犯、最下贱的畜生，从前也正是在那上面毙命的。

===Original===
Madame Guillotine was, above all, catholic in her tastes, her gaunt arms, painted blood red, were open alike to the murderer and the thief, the aristocrats of ancient lineage, and the proletariat from the gutter.

But lately the executions had been almost exclusively of a political character. The Girondins were fighting their last upon the bloody arena of the Revolution. One by one they fell still fighting, still preaching moderation, still foretelling disaster and appealing to that people, whom they had roused from one slavery, in order to throw it headlong under a tyrannical yoke more brutish, more absolute than before.

There were twelve prisons in Paris then, and forty thousand in France, and they were all full. An entire army went round the country recruiting prisoners. There was no room for separate cells, no room for privacy, no cause or desire for the most elementary sense of delicacy.
===Chinese===
断头台夫人（Madame Guillotine）的口味最是来者不拒：她那两条漆成血色的枯瘦臂膀，对凶手和小偷，对古老门第的贵族和对阴沟里爬出来的无产者，一律敞开着。

然而近来，处决几乎清一色地带上了政治色彩。吉伦特派正在革命那血腥的角斗场上作最后的苦战。他们一个接一个倒下去，倒下去时仍在抗争，仍在鼓吹温和，仍在预言大祸将临，仍向那班民众苦苦呼吁——而当初正是他们把那班民众从一种奴役中唤醒，为的却是把他们一头栽进一副比先前更野蛮、更专横的暴政枷锁之下。

那时巴黎有十二座监狱，全法国有四万座，全都塞得满满当当。整整一支军队在全国各地巡行，招募囚犯。没有地方安置单人囚室，没有地方容人保有私密，最起码的廉耻之心，既无用武之地，也无人在意。

===Original===
Women, men, children—all were herded together, for one day, perhaps two, and a night or so, and then death would obliterate the petty annoyances, the womanly blushes caused by this sordid propinquity.

Death levelled all, erased everything.

When Marie Antoinette mounted the guillotine she had forgotten that for six weeks she practically lived day and night in the immediate companionship of a set of degraded soldiery.
===Chinese===
女人、男人、孩子——全都驱作一堆关在一起，关上一天，或许两天，再搭上个把夜晚；随后死亡便会来勾销那些细碎的烦扰，勾销这腌臜的共居在女人脸上惹起的红晕。

死亡抹平一切，勾销一切。

玛丽·安托瓦内特登上断头台的时候，早已忘却：整整六个星期，她实际上日夜同一伙堕落下流的兵厮守在一处。

===Original===
Juliette, as she marched through the streets between two men of the National Guard, and followed by Merlin, was hooted and jeered at, insulted, pelted with mud. One woman tried to push past the soldiers, and to strike her in the face—a woman! not thirty!—and who was dragging a pale, squalid little boy by the hand.

“*Crache donc sur l’aristo, voyons!*” the woman said to this poor, miserable little scrap of humanity as the soldiers pushed her roughly aside. “Spit on the aristocrat!” And the child tortured its own small, parched mouth so that, in obedience to its mother, it might defile and bespatter a beautiful, innocent girl.

The soldiers laughed, and improved the occasion with another insulting jest. Even Merlin forgot his vexation, delighted at the incident.
===Chinese===
朱丽叶特由两名国民自卫军士兵一左一右押着走过街道，身后跟着梅尔兰；人们向她发出嘘声、恣意嘲骂，侮辱她，朝她掷烂泥。一个女人试图从士兵身边挤过去打她的脸——一个女人！还不到三十岁！——手里还拖着一个面色灰败、衣衫龌龊的小男孩。

「*Crache donc sur l’aristo, voyons!*（来，朝这贵族吐口唾沫呀！）」士兵们把她粗鲁地推到一旁时，这女人对自己那一小团可怜巴巴的血肉这样说道。于是那孩子便折磨起自己干渴的小嘴，好遵从母命，去玷污、去唾污一个美丽无辜的姑娘。

士兵们放声大笑，乘兴又编了一句侮辱的俏皮话。连梅尔兰也忘了满腹的懊恼，为这场热闹乐不可支。

===Original===
But Juliette had seen nothing of it all.

She was walking as in a dream. The mob did not exist for her; she heard neither insult nor vituperation. She did not see the evil, dirty faces pushed now and then quite close to her; she did not feel the rough hands of the soldiers jostling her through the crowd: she had gone back to her own world of romance, where she dwelt alone now with the man she loved. Instead of the squalid houses of Paris, with their eternal device of Fraternity and Equality, there were beautiful trees and shrubs of laurel and of roses around her, making the air fragrant with their soft, intoxicating perfumes; sweet voices from the land of dreams filled the atmosphere with their tender murmur, whilst overhead a cloudless sky illumined this earthly paradise.

She was happy—supremely, completely happy. She had saved him from the consequences of her own iniquitous crime, and she was about to give her life for him, so that his safety might be more completely assured.
===Chinese===
可这一切，朱丽叶特什么也没有看见。

她走着，恍如梦中。那群暴民于她并不存在；辱骂与诟詈她一概听不见。她看不见那些时不时几乎贴到她脸上的邪恶污秽的面孔，也感觉不到士兵们推搡着她挤过人群的粗手：她已经回到了自己那个浪漫故事的世界，如今在那世界里，只有她同她心爱的男人独处。眼前不再是巴黎那些龌龊的房屋，不再有屋墙上那永恒的「博爱」与「平等」的标语；她四周是美丽的树木，是月桂与蔷薇的丛丛灌木，柔和醉人的芳香熏得空气甜丝丝的；梦乡传来的甜美嗓音，以温柔的絮语充盈着四下的空气，而头顶上万里无云的晴空，正朗朗照着这人间的乐园。

她是幸福的——至高无上地、完完全全地幸福。她已经把他从她自己那桩罪行的后果里救了出来，而她即将为他献出生命，好叫他的安全得到更加周全的保障。

===Original===
Her love for him he would never know; now he knew only her crime, but presently, when she would be convicted and condemned, confronted with a few scraps of burned paper and a torn letter-case, then he would know that she had stood her trial, self-accused, and meant to die for him.

Therefore the past few moments were now wholly hers. She had the rights to dwell on those few happy seconds when she listened to the avowal of his love. It was ethereal, and perhaps not altogether human, but it was hers. She had been his divinity, his madonna; he had loved in her that, which was her truer, her better self.

What was base in her was not truly her. That awful oath, sworn so solemnly, had been her relentless tyrant; and her religion—a religion of superstition and of false ideals—had blinded her, and dragged her into crime.
===Chinese===
她对他的爱，他永远不会知道；如今他知道的，只是她的罪行。但不久之后，待她被定罪、被判死刑，同那几片烧焦的纸屑、那只撕破的信匣当面对质之时，他就会明白：她是自首自诉、挺身受审，决意替他去死的。

因此，刚过去的这段时光，如今整个儿属于她了。她有权细细回味那幸福得屈指可数的几秒钟——那时，她正聆听着他吐露爱慕。那爱慕缥缈如天际的气息，或许并不全然属于人间，可它是她的。她曾是他奉为神圣的偶像，是他的圣母；他在她身上所爱的，是她那个更真实、更美好的自我。

她身上卑污的那一部分，其实并非真正的她。那个宣誓得如此庄重森严的可怖誓言，才是驱策她的冷酷暴君；而她的信仰——一种由迷信与虚妄理想凑成的信仰——蒙住了她的眼睛，把她拖进了罪恶。

===Original===
She had arrogated to herself that which was God’s alone—“Vengeance!” which is not for man.

That through it all she should have known love, and learned its tender secrets, was more than she deserved. That she should have felt his burning kisses on her hand was heavenly compensation for all she would have to suffer.

And so she allowed them to drag her through the sansculotte mob of Paris, who would have torn her to pieces then and there, so as not to delay the pleasure of seeing her die.
===Chinese===
她把唯独属于上帝的东西僭据到了自己身上——「复仇！」——而那，原不是留给凡人的。

而在这一切的罪孽之中，她竟还能识得爱情，还能懂得它温柔的奥秘，这已远非她所应得。她竟还能感到他炽热的吻落在自己的手上——这便是上天的补偿，补偿她此后必得承受的一切苦难。

于是她听凭他们拖着自己，穿过巴黎的无套裤汉群；那群人恨不得当场把她撕成碎片，好不耽误亲眼看她死去的那份乐子。

===Original===
They took her to the Luxembourg, once the palace of the Medici, the home of proud “Monsieur” in the days of the Great Monarch, now a loathsome, overfilled prison.

It was then six o’clock in the afternoon, drawing towards the close of this memorable day. She was handed over to the governor of the prison, a short, thickset man in black trousers and black-shag woollen shirt, and wearing a dirty red cap, with tricolour rosette on the side of his unkempt head.

He eyed her up and down as she passed under the narrow doorway, then murmured one swift query to Merlin:
===Chinese===
他们把她押到了卢森堡监狱——那里曾是美第奇家族的宫殿，大君主时代是骄横的王弟「Monsieur」殿下的府邸，如今成了一座可憎的、人满为患的监狱。

其时正是下午六点钟，这个不寻常的日子已近尾声。她被移交给监狱的典狱长——一个又矮又粗壮的汉子，穿着黑裤子、黑色粗绒毛衬衫，头上歪戴一顶肮脏的红便帽，蓬乱的脑袋一侧别着一枚三色帽花。

她从那道狭窄的门洞底下走过时，他把她从头到脚打量了一遍，随即飞快地向梅尔兰低声问了一句：

===Original===
“Dangerous?”

“Yes,” replied Merlin laconically.

“You understand,” added the governor; “we are so crowded. We ought to know if individual attention is required.”

“Certainly,” said Merlin, “you will be personally responsible for this prisoner to the Committee of Public Safety.”

“Any visitors allowed?”

“Certainly not, without the special permission of the Public Prosecutor.”
===Chinese===
「危险么？」

「危险，」梅尔兰言简意赅地答道。

「您明白，」典狱长补充道，「我们这儿挤得要命。总得弄清楚，是不是需要对这个犯人单独照看。」

「那是自然，」梅尔兰说，「这个犯人，得由你本人向救国委员会负责。」

「准不准探监？」

「断乎不准——除非有公诉人的特别许可。」

===Original===
Juliette heard this brief exchange of words over her future fate.

No visitor would be allowed to see her. Well, perhaps that would be best. She would have been afraid to meet Déroulède again, afraid to read in his eyes that story of his dead love, which alone might have destroyed her present happiness.

And she wished to see no one. She had a memory to dwell on—a short, heavenly memory. It consisted of a few words, a kiss—the last one—on her hand, and that passionate murmur which had escaped from his lips when he knelt at her feet:

“Juliette!”
===Chinese===
朱丽叶特听着这几句短短的对答，字字都关乎她日后的命运。

不会有人获准来看她了。也罢，这样或许倒好。她本会害怕再见到德鲁莱德，害怕从他眼中读出他那段已然死去的爱情的故事——唯独那个故事，才可能毁掉她此刻的幸福。

而她也不愿见任何人。她有一段回忆可供盘桓——短短的、天堂一般的回忆。那回忆只有几句话，一个吻——落在她手上的最后一个吻——还有他跪在她脚边时唇间逸出的那声热切的低唤：

「朱丽叶特！」
