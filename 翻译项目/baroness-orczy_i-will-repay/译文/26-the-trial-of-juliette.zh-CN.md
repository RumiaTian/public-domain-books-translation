## XXIV The Trial of Juliette / XXIV 朱丽叶特受审

===Original===

It is all indelibly placed on record in the *Bulletin du Tribunal Révolutionnaire*, under date 25th Fructidor, year I of the Revolution.

Anyone who cares may read, for the Bulletin is in the Archives of the Bibliothèque Nationale of Paris.

One by one the accused had been brought forth, escorted by two men of the National Guard in ragged, stained uniforms of red, white, and blue; they were then conducted to the small raised platform in the centre of the hall, and made to listen to the charge brought against them by Citizen Foucquier-Tinville, the Public Presecutor.

===Chinese===

这一切都凿凿有据，永志于《革命法庭公报》（*Bulletin du Tribunal Révolutionnaire*）之中，日期为革命元年果月二十五日。

凡有兴致者皆可一读，因为《公报》就存放在巴黎的法国国家图书馆（Bibliothèque Nationale）的档案里。

被告们被一个个提上堂来，由两名国民自卫军士兵押解着，他们身上那红、白、蓝三色的军服破烂不堪、污渍斑斑；随后被告们被引到大厅中央那座小小的平台上，听候公诉人（Public Prosecutor）富基耶-坦维尔（Foucquier-Tinville）公民向他们宣读的指控。

===Original===

They were petty charges mostly: pilfering, fraud, theft, occasionally arson or manslaughter. One man, however, was arraigned for murder with highway robbery, and a woman for the most ignoble traffic, which evil feminine ingenuity could invent.

These two were condemned to the guillotine, the others sent to the galleys at Brest or Toulon﻿—the forger along with the petty thief, the housebreaker with the absconding clerk.

There was no room in the prison for ordinary offences against the criminal code; they were overfilled already with so-called traitors against the Republic.

Three women were sent to the penitentiary at the Salpêtriere, and were dragged out of the court shrilly protesting their innocence, and followed by obscene jeers from the spectators on the benches.

===Chinese===

大多是些小罪名：小偷小摸、欺诈、盗窃，偶尔也有纵火或过失杀人。然而有一个男人被控犯了杀人兼拦路抢劫之罪，还有一个女人被控操持邪恶的女性机巧所能想出的最卑贱的营生。

这两人被判上断头台，其余的发往布雷斯特或土伦去服划桨苦役——伪造犯与顺手牵羊的小偷同列，破门入户者与卷款潜逃的伙计同行。

监狱里已经腾不出地方来关押违反刑律的普通罪犯了；那里早已被所谓的背叛共和国的卖国贼塞得满满当当。

三名妇人被发往萨尔佩特里埃（Salpêtriere）监狱收押，被拖出法庭时还尖声申辩着自己的清白，旁听席上的观众则报以一片下流的哄笑。

===Original===

Then there was a momentary hush.

Juliette Marny had been brought in.

She was quite calm, and exquisitely beautiful, dressed in a plain grey bodice and kirtle, with a black band round her slim waist and a soft white kerchief folded across her bosom. Beneath the tiny, white cap her golden hair appeared in dainty, curly profusion; her childlike, oval face was very white, but otherwise quite serene.

She seemed absolutely unconscious of her surroundings, and walked with a firm step up to the platform, looking neither to the right nor to the left of her.

===Chinese===

随后是一阵短暂的屏息静默。

朱丽叶特·马尔尼（Juliette Marny）被带进来了。

她十分平静，美得清雅绝伦，穿一件素灰色的紧身衣和衬裙，纤细的腰间束着一道黑带，胸前叠着一方柔软的白色头巾。小小的白色便帽底下，露出一头蓬松卷曲、精巧动人的金发；她那孩子气的鹅蛋脸十分苍白，除此之外却是一片安详。

她似乎全然没有觉察到自己身处的环境，迈着坚定的步子走上平台，目光既不朝右也不朝左。

===Original===

Therefore she did not see Déroulède. A great, a wonderful radiance seemed to shine in her large eyes﻿—the radiance of self-sacrifice.

She was offering not only her life, but everything a woman of refinement holds most dear, for the safety of the man she loved.

A feeling that was almost physical pain, so intense was it, overcame Déroulède, when at last he heard her name loudly called by the Public Prosecutor.

All day he had waited for this awful moment, forgetting his own misery, his own agonised feeling of an irretrievable loss, in the horrible thought of what *she* would endure, what *she* would think, when first she realised the terrible indignity, which was to be put upon her.

===Chinese===

因此她没有看见德鲁莱德（Déroulède）。一双大眼睛里仿佛闪耀着一片宏大而奇妙的光辉——那是自我牺牲的光辉。

她所奉献的不仅是自己的生命，还有教养有素的女人视若至宝的一切，为的是她所爱的那个男人的安全。

当德鲁莱德终于听到公诉人高声唤出她的名字时，一种几乎等同于肉体疼痛的感觉把他淹没了——那感觉强烈至此。

他等这可怕的时刻已经等了一整天，在一念可怕的思量中忘却了自身的苦楚、自己那无可挽回的失落带来的锥心之痛——那思量便是：*她*将承受什么，*她*会怎么想，当*她*头一回意识到那即将加在她身上的可怖屈辱的时候。

===Original===

Yet for the sake of her, of her chances of safety and of ultimate freedom, it was undoubtedly best that it should be so.

Arraigned for conspiracy against the Republic, she was liable to secret trial, to be brought up, condemned, and executed before he could even hear of her whereabouts, before he could throw himself before her judges and take all guilt upon himself.

Those suspected of treason against the Republic forfeited, according to Merlin’s most iniquitous Law, their rights of citizenship, in publicity of trial and in defence.

It all might have been finished before Déroulède knew anything of it.

The other way was, of course, more terrible. Brought forth amongst the scum of criminal Paris, on a charge, the horror of which, he could but dimly hope that she was too innocent to fully understand, he dared not even think of what she would suffer.

===Chinese===

然而为了她，为了她保全性命、终获自由的机会，事情无疑最好还是如此。

若以阴谋反对共和国的罪名受审，她便可能被秘密审判——还不及他打听到她的下落，还不及他扑到她的法官面前把一切罪名揽到自己身上，她就会被提堂、定罪、处决。

依照梅尔兰（Merlin）那部最不公不义的法令，凡被怀疑背叛共和国者，即丧失其公民权利——既丧失公开受审之权，亦丧失自行辩护之权。

这一切，可能早在德鲁莱德略有所闻之前，便已完结了。

另一条路当然更为可怕。被拖到罪恶巴黎的人渣中间，站到一桩罪名之下——他只能隐隐指望，她那样天真无邪，未必全然懂得这罪名的可怖——至于她将承受什么，他连想都不敢想。

===Original===

But undoubtedly it was better so.

The mud thrown at her robes of purity could never cling to her, and at least her trial would be public; he would be there to take all infamy, all disgrace, all opprobrium on himself.

The strength of his appeal would turn her judges’ wrath from her to him; and after these few moments of misery, she would be free to leave Paris, France, to be happy, and to forget him and the memory of him.

An overwhelming, all-compelling love filled his entire soul for the beautiful girl, who had so wronged, yet so nobly tried to save him. A longing for her made his very sinews ache; she was no longer madonna, and her beauty thrilled him, with the passionate, almost sensuous desire to give his life for her.

The indictment against Juliette Marny has become history now.

===Chinese===

但无疑，这样要好些。

掷向她那纯洁衣袍的污泥，永远沾不上她分毫；而至少她的审判将是公开的：他会在场，把一切恶名、一切耻辱、一切唾骂都揽到自己身上。

他申诉的力量会把她的法官们的怒火从她引向他自己；熬过这几刻的苦难之后，她便可自由地离开巴黎、离开法兰西，去享受幸福，去忘掉他，忘掉与他有关的一切记忆。

一种无可抗拒、令人不能自己的爱，充满了他的整个灵魂，为了这个美丽的姑娘——她曾那样深地冤屈了他，却又那样高尚地舍身救他。对她的思慕令他筋骨作痛；她不再是圣母了，她的美令他心旌摇荡，激起一种炽热的、近乎感官的渴望，要为她献出自己的生命。

对朱丽叶特·马尔尼的起诉，如今已成历史。

===Original===

On that day, the 25th Fructidor, at seven o’clock in the evening, it was read out by the Public Prosecutor, and listened to by the accused﻿—so the Bulletin tells us﻿—with complete calm and apparent indifference. She stood up in that same pillory where once stood poor, guilty Charlotte Corday, where presently would stand proud, guiltless Marie Antoinette.

And Déroulède listened to the scurrilous document, with all the outward calm his strength of will could command. He would have liked to rise from his seat then and there, at once, and in mad, purely animal fury have, with a blow of his fist, quashed the words in Foucquier-Tinville’s lying throat.

But for her sake he was bound to listen, and, above all, to act quietly, deliberately, according to form and procedure, so as in no way to imperil her cause.

===Chinese===

那一天，果月二十五日，晚上七时，公诉人宣读了起诉书，而被告——据《公报》所载——以全然的平静与表面的漠然听受了它。她站上那同一座示众的耻辱台：可怜而有罪的夏洛特·科黛（Charlotte Corday）曾在那里站过；而不久之后，高傲而无罪的玛丽·安托瓦内特（Marie Antoinette）也将站上去。

德鲁莱德听人宣读这份恶毒下流的文书，竭尽其意志之力，维持着外表的平静。他真想当即、立刻从座位上站起身来，以疯狂而纯乎兽性的暴怒，一拳把那些谎话砸灭在富基耶-坦维尔那撒谎的喉咙里。

可是为了她，他必须听下去，而且尤其必须行事平静、从容，循规蹈矩、照章办理，好丝毫不危及她的案子。

===Original===

Therefore he listened whilst the Public Prosecutor spoke.

“Juliette Marny, you are hereby accused of having, by a false and malicious denunciation, slandered the person of a representative of the people; you caused the Revolutionary Tribunal, through this same mischievous act, to bring a charge against this representative of the people, to institute a domiciliary search in his house, and to waste valuable time, which otherwise belonged to the service of the Republic. And this you did, not from a misguided sense of duty towards your country, but in wanton and impure spirit, to be rid of the surveillance of one who had your welfare at heart, and who tried to prevent your leading the immoral life which had become a public scandal, and which has now brought you before this court of justice, to answer to a charge of wantonness, impurity, defamation of character, and corruption of public morals. In proof of which I now place before the court your own admission, that more than one citizen of the Republic has been led by you into immoral relationship with yourself; and further, your own admission, that your accusation against Citizen-Deputy Déroulède was false and mischievous; and further, and finally, your immoral and obscene correspondence with some persons unknown, which you vainly tried to destroy. In consideration of which, and in the name of the people of France, whose spokesman I am, I demand that you be taken hence from this Hall of Justice to the Place de la Révolution, in full view of the citizens of Paris and its environs, and clad in a soiled white garment, emblem of the smirch upon your soul, that there you be publicly whipped by the hands of Citizen Samson, the public executioner; after which, that you be taken to the prison of the Salpêtriere, there to be further detained at the discretion of the Committee of Public Safety. And now, Juliette Marny, you have heard the indictment preferred against you, have you anything to say, why the sentence which I have demanded shall not be passed upon you?”

===Chinese===

于是，公诉人说话时，他听着。

「朱丽叶特·马尔尼，你现被指控：以虚假而恶毒的告发，诽谤了一位人民代表的人格；且正是经由这同一恶毒行径，你致使革命法庭对这位人民代表提起控告，对其宅邸施行入户搜查，并虚掷了本应归于共和国公务的宝贵光阴。你行此之事，并非出于对祖国那种被引入歧途的责任感，而是出于淫佚不洁的心性，为的是摆脱一个把你的福祉挂在心上、竭力阻止你过那种已成公共丑闻的不道德生活之人的监视；正是那种生活，如今把你带到这座法庭之前，来听候淫佚、污秽、诽谤人格与败坏公共道德等项罪名的追究。为证明上述各节，我今将你的自供呈于法庭：其一，你自认曾诱使共和国一个以上的公民与你本人发生不道德的关系；其二，你自认对德鲁莱德公会议员所作的告发，纯属虚假、纯属恶意；其三，亦即最后，你与若干身份不明之人往来的不道德而淫秽的书信——你曾妄图将其销毁，而未得逞。有鉴于此，并以法兰西人民——我即其代言人——之名，我要求将你从此地、从这座司法大厅（Hall of Justice）解往革命广场，置于巴黎及其近郊公民们的众目睽睽之下，令你身穿一件污秽的白衣——那是你灵魂污点的表征——由公共刽子手桑松（Samson）公民之手，将你当众鞭笞；鞭笞之后，将你解往萨尔佩特里埃监狱，由救国委员会（Committee of Public Safety）酌情决定继续羁押。现在，朱丽叶特·马尔尼，你已听取了对你的起诉，你可有什么话要说，说明为何不应将我所要求的判决施加于你？」

===Original===

Jeers, shouts, laughter, and curses greeted this speech of the Public Prosecutor.

===Chinese===

公诉人这篇演说，迎来的是奚落、叫喊、哄笑与咒骂。

===Original===

All that was most vile and most bestial in this miserable, misguided people struggling for Utopia and Liberty, seemed to come to the surface, whilst listening to the reading of this most infamous document.

The delight of seeing this beautiful, ethereal woman, almost unearthly in her proud aloofness, smirched with the vilest mud to which the vituperation of man can contrive to sink, was a veritable treat to the degraded wretches.

The women yelled hoarse approval; the children, not understanding, laughed in mirthless glee; the men, with loud curses, showed their appreciation of Foucquier-Tinville’s speech.

As for Déroulède, the mental agony he endured surpassed any torture which the devils, they say, reserve for the damned. His sinews cracked in his frantic efforts to control himself; he dug his fingernails into his flesh, trying by physical pain to drown the sufferings of his mind.

He thought that his reason was tottering, that he would go mad if he heard another word of this infamy. The hooting and yelling of that filthy mob sounded like the cries of lost souls, shrieking from hell. All his pity for them was gone, his love for humanity, his devotion to the suffering poor.

===Chinese===

这个为乌托邦与自由而挣扎的可悲而误入歧途的民族，其中最卑污、最兽性的东西，仿佛都在倾听这份最声名狼藉的文书被宣读之际，一齐泛上了表面。

眼看这位美丽的、超凡脱俗的女人——她那高傲的孤迥几乎不似人间之物——被泼上人类辱骂之辞所能沉到的最污秽的污泥，这种快意，对那班堕落的可怜虫来说，是一场名副其实的盛馔。

女人们哑着嗓子喊好；孩子们懵然不解，却兀自笑得没有一点欢意；男人们大声咒骂着，表示他们对富基耶-坦维尔这篇演说的赏识。

至于德鲁莱德，他所承受的精神酷刑，超出了人们传说中留给被打入地狱者的任何苦刑。他在疯狂克己的努力中，筋腱格格作响；他把指甲掐进肉里，想以肉体的疼痛来淹没心灵的苦楚。

他只觉自己的神智摇摇欲坠，只觉再听一个字这种丑恶，自己就要发疯。那群污糟暴民起哄的嘘声与号叫，听上去就像迷途的亡魂自地狱深处发出的尖啸。他对他们的一切怜悯都消失了，他对人类之爱、他对受苦穷人的赤忱，俱已烟消。

===Original===

A great, an immense hatred for this ghastly Revolution and the people it professed to free filled his whole being, together with a mad, hideous desire to see them suffer, starve, die a miserable, loathsome death. The passion of hate, that now overwhelmed his soul, was at least as ugly as theirs. He was, for one brief moment, now at one with them in their inordinate lust for revenge.

Only Juliette throughout all this remained calm, silent, impassive.

She had heard the indictment, heard the loathsome sentence, for her white cheeks had gradually become ashy pale, but never for a moment did she depart from her attitude of proud aloofness.

She never once turned her head towards the mob who insulted her. She waited in complete passiveness until the yelling and shouting had subsided, motionless save for her fingertips, which beat an impatient tattoo upon the railing in front of her.

The Bulletin says that she took out her handkerchief and wiped her face with it. *Elle s’essuya le front qui fut perlé de sueur.* The heat had become oppressive.

===Chinese===

对这场阴森可怖的革命、对它口口声声要解放的人民，一种巨大的、无边的恨意充满了他的全身，还夹杂着一种疯狂的、狰狞的欲望，要眼看他们受苦、挨饿、死得凄惨而可憎。此刻席卷他灵魂的这股恨的激情，至少同他们的恨一样丑恶。有短短的一瞬，他同他们化为一体，同样怀着那无节制的复仇贪欲。

自始至终，唯独朱丽叶特平静、沉默、不动声色。

起诉她听了，那可憎的判决她也听了——她那白皙的面颊已渐渐灰白如烬——但她那高傲孤迥的姿态，一刻也不曾改易。

她从不曾朝侮辱她的暴民回过一次头。她全然漠然地等着，直到那嚎叫与喧嚷平息下去；她纹丝不动，唯有指尖例外——那些指尖正不耐烦地在她面前的栏杆上敲出细碎的鼓点。

《公报》记道，她掏出手帕，拭了拭脸。*Elle s’essuya le front qui fut perlé de sueur.*（她拭去了额上沁出的汗珠。）暑热已变得逼人。

===Original===

The atmosphere was overcharged with the dank, penetrating odour of steaming, dirty clothes. The room, though vast, was close and suffocating, the tallow candles flickering in the humid, hot air threw the faces of the President and clerks into bold relief, with curious caricature effects of light and shade.

The petrol lamp above the head of the accused had flared up, and begun to smoke, causing the chimney to crack with a sharp report. This diversion effected a momentary silence among the crowd, and the Public Prosecutor was able to repeat his query:

“Juliette Marny, have you anything to say in reply to the charge brought against you, and why the sentence which I have demanded should not be passed against you?”

The sooty smoke from the lamp came down in small, black, greasy particles; Juliette with her slender fingertips flicked one of these quietly off her sleeve, then she replied:

===Chinese===

空气里塞满了蒸腾的脏衣服发散出的那股潮湿而钻骨刺鼻的气味。这座大厅虽然宏敞，却窒闷得令人窒息；牛脂蜡烛在湿热的空气里闪烁摇曳，把庭长和书记员们的面孔衬得格外突出，明暗之间显出古怪的漫画式效果。

被告头顶上方的煤油灯轰然窜起火苗，继而冒起烟来，灯罩啪地一声脆响，裂了开来。这场小插曲令人群静了片刻，公诉人于是得以重申他的问话：

「朱丽叶特·马尔尼，对于所提的指控，你有什么可答辩的？为什么不应把我所要求的判决施加于你？」

灯里的油烟化作油腻腻的黑色微粒簌簌落下；朱丽叶特用纤细的指尖，静静地把一粒从袖口上弹去，然后答道：

===Original===

“No; I have nothing to say.”

“Have you instructed an advocate to defend you, according to your rights of citizenship, which the Law allows?” added the Public Prosecutor solemnly.

Juliette would have replied at once; her mouth had already framed the No with which she meant to answer.

===Chinese===

「不；我没有什么要说的。」

「依照法律所准许的你的公民权利，你可曾延请辩护人来为你辩护？」公诉人庄严地补问道。

朱丽叶特本要立刻作答；她口中已凝成那个她准备作答的「不」字。

===Original===

But now at last had come Déroulède’s hour. For this he had been silent, had suffered and had held his peace, whilst twice twenty-four hours had dragged their weary lengths along, since the arrest of the woman he loved.

In a moment he was on his feet before them all, accustomed to speak, to dominate, to command.

“Citizeness Juliette Marny has entrusted me with her defence,” he said, even before the No had escaped Juliette’s white lips, “and I am here to refute the charges brought against her, and to demand in the name of the people of France full acquittal and justice for her.”

===Chinese===

然而此刻，德鲁莱德的时辰终于到了。为了这一刻，自他所爱的女人被捕以来，在两个昼夜疲惫而漫长地拖行的时光里，他一直沉默着、忍受着、缄口不言。

转眼之间，他已站起身来，面对着满堂众人——那个素来惯于发言、惯于统御、惯于号令的人。

「朱丽叶特·马尔尼女公民已把她的辩护托付于我，」不等那个「不」字从朱丽叶特苍白的唇间逸出，他便说道，「我此刻在此，就是要驳斥强加于她的罪名，并以法兰西人民之名，为她要求完全开释与公正。」
