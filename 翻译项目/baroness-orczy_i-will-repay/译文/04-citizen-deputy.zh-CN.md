## II Citizen-Deputy / II 公会议员

===Original===
When, presently, the young girl awoke, with a delicious feeling of rest and well-being, she had plenty of leisure to think.
So, then, this was his house! She was actually a guest, a rescued protégé, beneath the roof of Citoyen Déroulède.
He had dragged her from the clutches of the howling mob which she had provoked; his mother had made her welcome, a sweet-faced, young girl scarce out of her teens, sad-eyed and slightly deformed, had waited upon her and made her happy and comfortable.
Juliette de Marny was in the house of the man, whom she had sworn before her God and before her father to pursue with hatred and revenge.

===Chinese===
不久之后，年轻姑娘醒了过来，带着一种休憩与康宁的甜美感觉，她有的是闲暇去思索。
原来，这就是他的家！她居然成了客人，一个受救助的被庇护者，就待在德鲁莱德公民（Citoyen Déroulède）的屋檐之下。
是他把她从那群被她招惹来的、狂吼乱叫的暴众的魔爪里拖了出来；他的母亲对她欢迎备至；一个容貌甜美的少女——刚满二十的光景，眼神忧郁，身形微有残疾——侍奉着她，让她欢喜，让她舒适。
朱丽叶特·马尔尼（Juliette de Marny）如今就在这个人的家里——她曾在上帝面前、在父亲面前立誓，要带着仇恨与复仇之心追索到底的那个人。

===Original===
Ten years had gone by since then.
Lying upon the sweet-scented bed which the hospitality of the Déroulèdes had provided for her, she seemed to see passing before her the spectres of these past ten years—the first four, after her brother's death, until the old Duc de Marny's body slowly followed his soul to its grave.
After that last glimmer of life beside the deathbed of his son, the old Duc had practically ceased to be. A mute, shrunken figure, he merely existed; his mind vanished, his memory gone, a wreck whom Nature fortunately remembered at last, and finally took away from the invalid chair which had been his world.
Then came those few years at the Convent of the Ursulines. Juliette had hoped that she had a vocation; her whole soul yearned for a secluded, a religious, life, for great barriers of solemn vows and days spent in prayer and contemplation, to interpose between herself and the memory of that awful night when, obedient to her father's will, she had made the solemn oath to avenge her brother's death.

===Chinese===
自那时起，十年过去了。
她躺在德鲁莱德家殷勤待客的、散发着香气的床上，仿佛看见过去十年的种种魅影从眼前走过——头四年，是在她哥哥死后，直到老马尔尼公爵（Duc de Marny）的躯体缓缓地追随他的灵魂进了坟墓。
在儿子的灵榻旁燃尽最后一点生命之光以后，老公爵实际上已不复为人。一个缄默的、萎缩的身影，只是苟延残喘；神志散了，记忆没了，一具残躯——所幸造化终于记起了他，最后把他从那把曾是他整个世界的病榻上带走了。
然后是乌尔苏拉会（Ursulines）修道院里的那几年。朱丽叶特曾指望自己有修道的圣召；她的整个灵魂渴望一种隐修的、虔敬的生活，渴望用庄严誓愿筑成的重重屏障、用祈祷与冥想的日日夜夜，横隔在她自己与那个可怕夜晚的记忆之间——那一夜，她遵从父命，立下了为兄长之死复仇的庄严誓言。

===Original===
She was only eighteen when she first entered the convent, directly after her father's death, when she felt very lonely—both morally and mentally lonely—and followed by the obsession of that oath.
She never spoke of it to anyone except to her confessor, and he, a simple-minded man of great learning and a total lack of knowledge of the world, was completely at a loss how to advise.
The Archbishop was consulted. He could grant a dispensation, and release her of that most solemn vow.
When first this idea was suggested to her, Juliette was exultant. Her entire nature, which in itself was wholesome, lighthearted, the very reverse of morbid, rebelled against this unnatural task placed upon her young shoulders. It was only religion—the strange, warped religion of that extraordinary age—which kept her to it, which forbade her breaking lightly that most unnatural oath.

===Chinese===
她初次进修道院时只有十八岁，就在父亲去世之后；那时她觉得自己非常孤单——道德上与精神上的双重孤单——而那个誓言的魔念又紧追着她不放。
这件事她从不对任何人提起，除了听她告解的神师；而那是一位学问渊博、却不谙世事的质朴之人，全然不知该如何劝解。
他们去请教了总主教。他可以颁一道豁免，解除她那最庄严的誓愿。
这个想法初提给她时，朱丽叶特欣喜若狂。她那天性本是健全的、轻快的，与病态恰恰相反，她整个心性都在反抗压在她年轻肩头的这份违背人性的差事。只有宗教——那个非凡年代里被扭曲的怪异宗教——把她拴在这上面，不许她轻易背弃那道最违背人性的誓言。

===Original===
The Archbishop was a man of many duties, many engagements. He agreed to give this strange "*cas de conscience*" his most earnest attention. He would make no promises. But Mademoiselle de Marny was rich: a munificent donation to the poor of Paris, or to some cause dear to the Holy Father himself, might perhaps be more acceptable to God than the fulfilment of a compulsory vow.
Juliette, within the convent walls, was waiting patiently for the Archbishop's decision at the very moment, when the greatest upheaval the world has ever known was beginning to shake the very foundations of France.
The Archbishop had other things now to think about than isolated cases of conscience. He forgot all about Juliette, probably. He was busy consoling a monarch for the loss of his throne, and preparing himself and his royal patron for the scaffold.
The Convent of the Ursulines was scattered during the Terror. Everyone remembers the Thermidor massacres, and the thirty-four nuns, all daughters of ancient families of France, who went so cheerfully to the scaffold.

===Chinese===
总主教是个身兼数职、应酬繁多的人。他答应以最郑重的态度来考虑这个奇特的「*cas de conscience*」（良知问题）。他不作任何许诺。不过马尔尼小姐（Mademoiselle de Marny）很富有：给巴黎的穷人、或给教皇本人（Holy Father）所关切的事业一笔慷慨的捐赠，也许比履行一桩被迫立下的誓言更能蒙上帝悦纳。
朱丽叶特在修道院的高墙之内，正耐心等候总主教的裁决；就在此时，人类历史上从未有过的最大剧变，已开始撼动法兰西的根基。
如今总主教有比孤例良知更要紧的事要操心了。他大概把朱丽叶特忘得一干二净。他忙着安慰一位失去王位的君王，忙着让自己和那位王家庇护人做好上断头台的准备。
恐怖统治（Reign of Terror）期间，乌尔苏拉会修道院被解散了。热月（Thermidor）屠杀，人人记忆犹新：那三十四位修女，全是法兰西古老世家的女儿，欢欢喜喜地走上了断头台。

===Original===
Juliette was one of those who escaped condemnation. How or why, she herself could not have told. She was very young, and still a postulant; she was allowed to live in retirement with Pétronelle, her old nurse, who had remained faithful through all these years.
Then the Archbishop was prosecuted and imprisoned. Juliette made frantic efforts to see him, but all in vain. When he died, she looked upon her spiritual guide's death as a direct warning from God, that nothing could relieve her of her oath.
She had watched the turmoils of the Revolution through the attic window of her tiny apartment in Paris. Waited upon by faithful Pétronelle, she had been forced to live on the savings of that worthy old soul, as all her property, all the Marny estates, the *dot* she took with her to the convent—everything, in fact—had been seized by the Revolutionary Government, self appointed to level fortunes, as well as individuals.
From that attic window she had seen beautiful Paris writhing under the pitiless lash of the demon of terror which it had provoked; she had heard the rumble of the tumbrils, dragging day after day their load of victims to the insatiable maker of this Revolution of Fraternity—the Guillotine.

===Chinese===
朱丽叶特是逃过判决的人之一。何以能逃、为何能逃，连她自己也说不清。她年纪很轻，还是个望会生；她被准许退居静处，与佩特罗内勒（Pétronelle）——她那位在这些年头里始终忠心不渝的老奶妈——同住。
后来总主教被起诉、下狱。朱丽叶特拼了命想见他一面，却终究没能如愿。他死时，她把这位神师的死看作上帝直接的警示：什么都解除不了她的誓言。
她在巴黎那间小小寓所的老虎窗前，冷眼看着大革命的滚滚风潮。由忠心的佩特罗内勒侍奉着，她只能靠那位可敬老人的一点积蓄过活，因为她的一切财产、马尔尼家的全部田产、她带进修道院的嫁妆（*dot*）——事实上是一切——都被革命政府没收了；那个自封的政府，要拉平财产，也要拉平人。
从那扇老虎窗里，她眼看着美丽的巴黎在它自己招来的恐怖恶魔的无情鞭笞下翻滚扭动；她听着死囚车（tumbril）的辚辚声，日复一日拖着满车的牺牲者，送往这场「博爱革命」那永不知足的缔造者——断头台。

===Original===
She had seen the gay, lighthearted people of this Star-City turned to howling beasts of prey, its women changed to sexless vultures, with murderous talons implanted in everything that is noble, high or beautiful.
She was not twenty when the feeble, vacillating monarch and his imperious consort were dragged back—a pair of humiliated prisoners—to the capital from which they had tried to flee.
Two years later, she had heard the cries of an entire people exulting over a regicide. Then the murder of Marat, by a young girl like herself, the pale-faced, large-eyed Charlotte, who had committed a crime for the sake of a conviction. "Greater than Brutus!" some had called her. Greater than Joan of Arc, for it was to a mission of evil and of sin that she was called from the depths of her Breton village, and not to one of glory and triumph.
"Greater than Brutus!"

===Chinese===
她眼看着这座星辰之城（Star-City）里快活而无忧的人们变成了嚎叫的猛兽，看着它的女人们变成了失去女性特征的兀鹰，用杀气腾腾的利爪，攫住一切高贵、崇高而美丽的东西。
她还不满二十岁，那位软弱而优柔的君王和他威严的王后，就被当作一对受尽屈辱的囚徒，押回了他们曾试图逃离的都城。
两年之后，她听见全体人民为弑君而欢呼的声音。接着是马拉（Marat）之死，杀死他的也是一个和她一样的姑娘——那位面色苍白、眼睛大大的夏洛特（Charlotte），她为了一个信念而犯下了罪行。有人称她「比布鲁图斯更伟大！」她比贞德更伟大，因为从她布列塔尼村庄的深处召她去担当的，是一个邪恶与罪恶的使命，而非光荣与凯旋的使命。
「比布鲁图斯更伟大！」

===Original===
Juliette followed the trial of Charlotte Corday with all the passionate ardour of her exalted temperament.
Just think what an effect it must have had upon the mind of this young girl, who for nine years—the best of her life—had also lived with the idea of a sublime mission pervading her very soul.
She watched Charlotte Corday at her trial. Conquering her natural repulsion for such scenes, and the crowds which usually watched them, she had forced her way into the foremost rank of the narrow gallery which overlooked the Hall of the Revolutionary Tribunal.
She heard the indictment, heard Tinville's speech and the calling of the witnesses.

===Chinese===
朱丽叶特以她那热烈天性所能有的全部炽情，紧盯着夏洛特·科黛（Charlotte Corday）受审的经过。
想一想吧，这对一个年轻姑娘的心灵该是怎样的影响：九年来——那正是她生命中最好的年华——她同样活在一个崇高使命的念头里，那念头浸透了她的整个灵魂。
她注视着受审中的夏洛特·科黛。她克制了自己对这类场面、以及惯常围观这类场面的人群的本能反感，硬挤进了俯临革命法庭（Tribunal Révolutionnaire）大厅的那道狭窄楼廊的最前一排。
她听了起诉书，听了坦维尔（Tinville）的公诉陈词和证人的传唤。

===Original===
"All this is unnecessary. I killed Marat!"
Juliette heard the fresh young voice ringing out clearly above the murmur of voices, the howls of execration; she saw the beautiful young face, clear, calm, impassive.
"I killed Marat!"
And there in the special space allotted to the Citizen-Deputies, sitting among those who represented the party of the Moderate Gironde, was Paul Déroulède, the man whom she had sworn to pursue with a vengeance as great, as complete, as that which guided Charlotte Corday's hand.

===Chinese===
「这一切都是多余的。马拉是我杀的！」
朱丽叶特听见那清脆年轻的声音，在人声的嗡嗡嘈杂和咒骂的吼叫之上清亮地响起；她看见那张美丽的年轻面孔，明净、沉静、不动声色。
「马拉是我杀的！」
而就在专留给公会议员（Citizen-Deputy）们的那个特别席区里，坐在代表温和的吉伦特派（Gironde）的人们中间的，正是保罗·德鲁莱德（Paul Déroulède）——她曾立誓要以同夏洛特·科黛之手所指引的那样宏大、那样彻底的复仇去追索的那个人。

===Original===
She watched him during the trial, and wondered if he had any presentiment of the hatred which dogged him, like unto the one which had dogged Marat.
He was very dark, almost swarthy, a son of the South, with brown hair, free from powder, thrown back and revealing the brow of a student rather than that of a legislator. He watched Charlotte Corday earnestly, and Juliette who watched him saw the look of measureless pity, which softened the otherwise hard look of his close-set eyes.
He made an impassioned speech for the defence: a speech which has become historic. It would have cost any other man his head.
Juliette marvelled at his courage; to defend Charlotte Corday was equivalent to acquiescing in the death of Marat: Marat, the friend of the people; Marat, whom his funeral orators had compared to the Great, the Sacred Leveller of Mankind!

===Chinese===
审判期间她一直注视着他，心里纳闷，他是否预感到那纠缠着他的仇恨——与那纠缠过马拉的仇恨如出一辙。
他肤色很深，近乎黝黑，是个南方人的儿子，棕色的头发不扑粉，向后梳着，露出一个更像学者而非立法者的额头。他专注地望着夏洛特·科黛；而注视着他的朱丽叶特，看见一种无边的怜悯软化了他那双靠得很近的眼睛里原本严厉的神情。
他为被告作了一篇慷慨激昂的辩护陈词：一篇已成历史的陈词。换了任何别人，这颗脑袋是要保不住的。
朱丽叶特惊叹他的胆量：为夏洛特·科黛辩护，就等于认可马拉之死；而马拉是「人民之友」，是悼词作者们拿来比作人类那位伟大而神圣的拉平者（the Great, the Sacred Leveller of Mankind）的马拉！

===Original===
But Déroulède's speech was not a defence, it was an appeal. The most eloquent man of that eloquent age, his words seemed to find that hidden bit of sentiment which still lurked in the hearts of these strange protagonists of Hate.
Everyone round Juliette listened as he spoke: "It is Citoyen Déroulède!" whispered the bloodthirsty Amazons, who sat knitting in the gallery.
But there was no further comment. A huge, magnificently-equipped hospital for sick children had been thrown open in Paris that very morning, a gift to the nation from Citoyen Déroulède. Surely he was privileged to talk a little, if it pleased him. His hospital would cover quite a good many defalcations.
Even the rabid Mountain, Danton, Merlin, Santerre, shrugged their shoulders. "It is Déroulède, let him talk an he list. Murdered Marat said of him that he was not dangerous."

===Chinese===
但德鲁莱德的陈词不是辩护，而是呼吁。作为那个雄辩时代最雄辩的人，他的言辞仿佛探进了这些以恨为主角的怪人心中那一线尚在潜伏的柔情。
朱丽叶特四周的人都在听他讲：「是德鲁莱德公民！」坐在楼廊里一边织着毛线的那些嗜血悍妇悄声说。

连狂热的山岳派（Mountain）——丹东（Danton）、梅尔兰（Merlin）、桑特尔（Santerre）——也只是耸耸肩：「是德鲁莱德嘛，他爱说就让他说去。被害的马拉说过，他并不危险。」

===Original===
Juliette heard it all. The knitters round her were talking loudly. Even Charlotte was almost forgotten whilst Déroulède talked. He had a fine voice, of strong calibre, which echoed powerfully through the hall.
He was rather short, but broad-shouldered and well knit, with an expressive hand, which looked slender and delicate below the fine lace ruffle.
Charlotte Corday was condemned. All Déroulède's eloquence could not save her.
Juliette left the court in a state of mad exultation. She was very young: the scenes she had witnessed in the past two years could not help but excite the imagination of a young girl, left entirely to her own intellectual and moral resources.

===Chinese===
这一切朱丽叶特都听见了。她四周织毛线的女人们在大声交谈。德鲁莱德讲话的时候，连夏洛特也几乎被忘了。他嗓音洪亮，音质浑厚，在大厅里回荡，极具气势。

What scenes! Great God!
And now to wait for an opportunity! Charlotte Corday, the half-educated little provincial should not put to shame Mademoiselle de Marny, the daughter of a hundred dukes, of those who had made France before she took to unmaking herself.
But she could not formulate any definite plans. Pétronelle, poor old soul, her only confidante, was not of the stuff that heroines are made of. Juliette felt impelled by duty, and duty at best is not so prompt a counsellor as love or hate.
Her adventure outside Déroulède's house had not been premeditated. Impulse and coincidence had worked their will with her.

那些场面！我的上帝！
现在只等时机了！夏洛特·科黛，那个受教育不全的小省城姑娘，不该让马尔尼小姐蒙羞——她是一百位公爵的后裔，属于那些缔造了法兰西的人，尽管后来法兰西亲手开始拆毁自己。

她在德鲁莱德家门外的那场历险，并非预谋。是冲动与巧合主宰了她。

She had been in the habit, daily, for the past month, of wandering down the Rue Ecole de Médecine, ostensibly to gaze at Marat's dwelling, as crowds of idlers were wont to do, but really in order to look at Déroulède's house. Once or twice she saw him coming or going from home. Once she caught sight of the inner hall, and of a young girl in a dark kirtle and snow-white kerchief bidding him goodbye at his door. Another time she caught sight of him at the corner of the street, helping that same young girl over the muddy pavement. He had just met her, and she was carrying a basket of provisions: he took it from her and carried it to the house.
Chivalrous—eh?—and innately so, evidently, for the girl was slightly deformed: hardly a hunchback, but weak and unattractive-looking, with melancholy eyes, and a pale, pinched face.

But she reckoned on that interference: the whole thing had occurred to her suddenly, and she had carried it through.
Had not her father said to her that when the time came, God would show her a means to the end?

Would God's finger point again, and show her what to do next, how best to accomplish what she had sworn to do?

===Chinese===
而如今，她已置身于那个人的宅邸之内——就是那个害死她哥哥、又害得她悲伤的父亲，一个可怜的、神志全无的疯子，颤颤巍巍走向坟墓的人。
上帝的手指会不会再一次指给她看，下一步该做什么，怎样才能最好地完成她立誓要做的事？
