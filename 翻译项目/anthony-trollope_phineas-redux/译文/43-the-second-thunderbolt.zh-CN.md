## XLIII The Second Thunderbolt / 四十三 第二道霹雳
===Original===

The quarrel between Phineas Finn and Mr. Bonteen had now become the talk of the town, and had taken many various phases. The political phase, though it was perhaps the best understood, was not the most engrossing. There was the personal phase﻿—which had reference to the direct altercation that had taken place between the two gentlemen, and to the correspondence between them which had followed, as to which phase it may be said that though there were many rumours abroad, very little was known. It was reported in some circles that the two aspirants for office had been within an ace of striking each other; in some, again, that a blow had passed﻿—and in others, further removed probably from the House of Commons and the Universe Club, that the Irishman had struck the Englishman, and that the Englishman had given the Irishman a thrashing. This was a phase that was very disagreeable to Phineas Finn. And there was a third﻿—which may perhaps be called the general social phase, and which unfortunately dealt with the name of Lady Laura Kennedy. They all, of course, worked into each other, and were enlivened and made interesting with the names of a great many big persons. Mr. Gresham, the Prime Minister, was supposed to be very much concerned in this matter. He, it was said, had found himself compelled to exclude Phineas Finn from the Government, because of the unfortunate alliance between him and the wife of one of his late colleagues, and had also thought it expedient to dismiss Mr. Bonteen from his Cabinet﻿—for it had amounted almost to dismissal﻿—because Mr. Bonteen had made indiscreet official allusion to that alliance. In consequence of this working in of the first and third phase, Mr. Gresham encountered hard usage from some friends and from many enemies. Then, of course, the scene at Macpherson’s Hotel was commented on very generally. An idea prevailed that Mr. Kennedy, driven to madness by his wife’s infidelity, which had become known to him through the quarrel between Phineas and Mr. Bonteen﻿—had endeavoured to murder his wife’s lover, who had with the utmost effrontery invaded the injured husband’s presence with a view of deterring him by threats from a publication of his wrongs.

菲尼亚斯·芬恩与邦廷先生的胎龉，如今已成了全城的谈资，而且生出许多副面孔。政治这一副，虽则或许最看得明白，却最不引人入胜。还有私人的一副——关乎两位先生之间的正面口角、以及随后的通信；这一副嘛，可以说流言虽多，实情知道得极少。有些圈子说，这两位舰舰官位的人险些动了拳脚；另一些圈子说，已经打了一拳；更有一些——多半离下议院（the House of Commons）和「宇宙」俱乐部（the Universe）远些的——说，是爱尔兰人打了英格兰人，英格兰人把爱尔兰人揍了一顿。这一副最叫菲尼亚斯·芬恩难堪。还有第三副——或者可称全般社交的一副——不幸牵涉到劳拉·肯尼迪夫人的名字。这几副自然彼此串通，又被许多大人物的名姓装点得津津有味。首相格雷沙姆先生，就被认为与此事干系极大。据说，他因菲尼亚斯与他一位前同僚之妻的不幸结盟，不得不将其摒于政府之外；又因邦廷先生在公务上不明智地暗指这桩结盟，而觉得把他排出内阁为宜——那几乎就等于黜退了。这第一副与第三副一经串通，格雷沙姆先生便从一些朋友、许多敌人那里讨来了苛待。于是，麦克弗森旅馆那一幕自然被众口评说。流行的说法是：肯尼迪先生因妻子不贞而致疯——这不贞，他是从菲尼亚斯与邦廷先生的争吵中得知的——便图谋杀害妻子的情夫；而那情夫竟恬不知耻，登门闯进受害丈夫的跟前，想用威胁阻止他把筫情公之于众。

This murder had been nearly accomplished in the centre of the metropolis﻿—by daylight, as if that made it worse﻿—on a Sunday, which added infinitely to the delightful horror of the catastrophe; and yet no public notice had been taken of it! The would-be murderer had been a Cabinet Minister, and the lover who was so nearly murdered had been an Undersecretary of State, and was even now a member of Parliament. And then it was positively known that the lady’s father, who had always been held in the highest respect as a nobleman, favoured his daughter’s lover, and not his daughter’s husband. All which things together filled the public with dismay, and caused a delightful excitement, giving quite a feature of its own to the season.
No doubt general opinion was adverse to poor Phineas Finn, but he was not without his party in the matter. To oblige a friend by inflicting an injury on his enemy is often more easy than to confer a benefit on the friend himself. We have already seen how the young Duchess failed in her attempt to obtain an appointment for Phineas, and also how she succeeded in destroying the high hopes of Mr. Bonteen. Having done so much, of course she clung heartily to the side which she had adopted;﻿—and, equally of course, Madame Goesler did the same. Between these two ladies there was a slight difference of opinion as to the nature of the alliance between Lady Laura and their hero. The Duchess was of opinion that young men are upon the whole averse to innocent alliances, and that, as Lady Laura and her husband certainly had long been separated, there was probably﻿—something in it. “Lord bless you, my dear,” the Duchess said, “they were known to be lovers when they were at Loughlinter together before she married Mr. Kennedy. It has been the most romantic affair! She made her father give him a seat for his borough.”
“He saved Mr. Kennedy’s life,” said Madame Goesler.
“That was one of the most singular things that ever happened. Laurence Fitzgibbon says that it was all planned﻿—that the garotters were hired, but unfortunately two policemen turned up at the moment, so the men were taken. I believe there is no doubt they were pardoned by Sir Henry Coldfoot, who was at the Home Office, and was Lord Brentford’s great friend. I don’t quite believe it all﻿—it would be too delicious; but a great many do.”

这桩谋杀几乎得手于首都的市中心——光天化日之下，仿佛这样更糟——又是在星期日，为这桩大祸妙趣横生的恐怖更添无限滋味；然而竟无人过问！那图谋行凶者做过内阁大臣；那险些被谋杀的情夫做过副国务大臣，如今还是个议员。而且人们言之凿凿：那位身为贵族、素来备受敬重的女方父亲，偏袒的竟是女儿的情人，不是女儿的女婿。凡此种种，合在一起，叫公众又惊又喜，为本季平添了独一份的特色。
无疑，舆论总的说来不利于可怜的菲尼亚斯·芬恩；可在这件事上，他也并非没有自己的一党。替朋友去伤朋友的仇人，往往比给朋友帮忙还容易。我们已经看到，那位年轻的公爵夫人替菲尼亚斯谋差事如何失败，也看到她如何成功地毁了邦廷先生的青云之望。既出了这么大力气，她自然一心贴在她选定的这一边；——同样自然，格斯勒夫人也随之站了过来。这两位夫人之间，对劳拉夫人与她们那位英雄之间结盟的性质，见解略有出入。公爵夫人认为，青年男子总的来说不喜欢清清白白的结盟；既然劳拉夫人同丈夫分居已久，那么这中间——多半有点什么。「上帝保佑你，亲爱的，」公爵夫人说，「她嫁给肯尼迪先生以前，他们同在洛夫林特的时候，就人尽皆知是一对情人。这是最罗曼蒂克的一段情！她逼着她父亲把自己选区的一个席位给了他。」
「他救过肯尼迪先生的命，」格斯勒夫人说。
「那是天底下最蹊跷的事之一。劳伦斯·菲茨吉本说，那全是安排好的——那些勒抢的匪徒是雇来的，可惜两个警察偏偏当场出现，人赃俱获。我相信，他们后来确实被亨利·科德富特爵士赦了——他当时在内政部，是布伦特福德伯爵的至交。我不全信——那可就太妙了；可很多人信。」

===Original===

Madame Goesler, however, was strong in her opinion that the report in reference to Lady Laura was scandalous. She did not believe a word of it, and was almost angry with the Duchess for her credulity.
It is probable that very many ladies shared the opinion of the Duchess; but not the less on that account did they take part with Phineas Finn. They could not understand why he should be shut out of office because a lady had been in love with him, and by no means seemed to approve the stern virtue of the Prime Minister. It was an interference with things which did not belong to him. And many asserted that Mr. Gresham was much given to such interference. Lady Cantrip, though her husband was Mr. Gresham’s most intimate friend, was altogether of this party, as was also the Duchess of St. Bungay, who understood nothing at all about it, but who had once fancied herself to be rudely treated by Mrs. Bonteen. The young Duchess was a woman very strong in getting up a party; and the old Duchess, with many other matrons of high rank, was made to believe that it was incumbent on her to be a Phineas Finnite. One result of this was, that though Phineas was excluded from the Liberal Government, all Liberal drawing-rooms were open to him, and that he was a lion.
Additional zest was given to all this by the very indiscreet conduct of Mr. Bonteen. He did accept the inferior office of President of the Board of Trade, an office inferior at least to that for which he had been designated, and agreed to fill it without a seat in the Cabinet. But having done so he could not bring himself to bear his disappointment quietly. He could not work and wait and make himself agreeable to those around him, holding his vexation within his own bosom. He was dark and sullen to his chief, and almost insolent to the Duke of Omnium. Our old friend Plantagenet Palliser was a man who hardly knew insolence when he met it. There was such an absence about him of all self-consciousness, he was so little given to think of his own personal demeanour and outward trappings﻿—that he never brought himself to question the manners of others to him. Contradiction he would take for simple argument. Strong difference of opinion even on the part of subordinates recommended itself to him. He could put up with apparent rudeness without seeing it, and always gave men credit for good intentions.

然而格斯勒夫人坚信，有关劳拉夫人的传闻是无耻谰言。她一个字也不信，几乎要怨公爵夫人轻信。
很可能，许多夫人都与公爵夫人同一见解；可这并不妨碍她们站在菲尼亚斯·芬恩一边。她们不明白，为什么一位小姐爱上了他，他就该被关在官门之外；首相这份铁面的德性，她们殊不以为然。这是把手伸进了不该管的事。许多人还断言，格雷沙姆先生就爱这样插手。坎特里普夫人——虽则她丈夫是格雷沙姆先生最亲密的朋友——全然站在这一党；圣邦吉老公爵夫人也是，尽管她对内情一无所知，只是当年自觉受过邦廷太太的无礼。年轻的公爵夫人拉帮结派的本事极大；于是老公爵夫人连同许多高门贵妇，都被说得笃信自己有义务做一名「菲尼亚斯派」。其结果之一是：菲尼亚斯虽被摒于自由党政府之外，一切自由党的客厅却都为他敞开，他成了一头红人。
邦廷先生极不明智的行止，又给这一切添了作料。他到底接受了贸易委员会主席这个次一等的位置——至少比原先内定的低了一等——而且答应不入内阁而就任。可既然如此，他却做不到把失望默默咽下。他不能一边埋头苦干、静待时机、周旋讨喜于左右，一边把怨气锁在自己胸中。他对首脑阴沉乖戾，对奥姆尼姆公爵几乎无礼。我们的老朋友金雀花·帕利瑟，是个几乎不识「无礼」为何物的人。他浑身没有半点自觉，从不留意自己的仪态排场——也就从不计较别人对他的态度。顶撞，他只当平常的争论；部下意见与他激烈相左，他反觉可取。表面的粗鲁他能视而不见地受下，而且总把人往好意上想。

And with it all he had an assurance in his own position﻿—a knowledge of the strength derived from his intellect, his industry, his rank, and his wealth﻿—which made him altogether fearless of others. When the little dog snarls, the big dog does not connect the snarl with himself, simply fancying that the little dog must be uncomfortable. Mr. Bonteen snarled a good deal, and the new Lord Privy Seal thought that the new President of the Board of Trade was not comfortable within himself. But at last the little dog took the big dog by the ear, and then the big dog put out his paw and knocked the little dog over. Mr. Bonteen was told that he had﻿—forgotten himself; and there arose new rumours. It was soon reported that the Lord Privy Seal had refused to work out decimal coinage under the management, in the House of Commons, of the President of the Board of Trade.
Mr. Bonteen, in his troubled spirit, certainly did misbehave himself. Among his closer friends he declared very loudly that he didn’t mean to stand it. He had not chosen to throw Mr. Gresham over at once, or to make difficulties at the moment;﻿—but he would not continue to hold his present position or to support the Government without a seat in the Cabinet. Palliser had become quite useless﻿—so Mr. Bonteen said﻿—since his accession to the dukedom, and was quite unfit to deal with decimal coinage. It was a burden to kill any man, and he was not going to kill himself﻿—at any rate without the reward for which he had been working all his life, and to which he was fully entitled, namely, a seat in the Cabinet. Now there were Bonteenites in those days as well as Phineas Finnites. The latter tribe was for the most part feminine; but the former consisted of some half-dozen members of Parliament, who thought they saw their way in encouraging the forlorn hope of the unhappy financier.
A leader of a party is nothing without an organ, and an organ came forward to support Mr. Bonteen﻿—not very creditable to him as a Liberal, being a Conservative organ﻿—but not the less gratifying to his spirit, inasmuch as the organ not only supported him, but exerted its very loudest pipes in abusing the man whom of all men he hated the most. The *People’s Banner* was the organ, and Mr. Quintus Slide was, of course, the organist.

而与此同时，他对自己的地位自有一份笃定——深知自己凭智力、勤勉、门第与财富得来的力量——使他全然无所畏惧。小狗匝牙，大狗并不把那牙同自己联系起来，只当小狗身上不自在。邦廷先生匝了好一阵牙，这位新任掌玺大臣便以为，新任贸易委员会主席是心里不自在。可到后来，小狗咬住了大狗的耳朵，大狗便伸出爪子，把小狗掠了个四脚朝天。有人告诉邦廷先生，他——忘乎所以了；新的流言随之四起。不久便有传言：掌玺大臣已拒绝在下议院受贸易委员会主席的调度、经办十进币制了。
邦廷先生心绪纷乱，行止确实有失检点。在亲近的朋友中间，他高声宣称自己不打算忍了。他没有立刻甩开格雷沙姆先生、没有当场作难；——可若无内阁席位，他不打算再居现职、再支持政府。帕利瑟自袭爵以来已全然不中用了——邦廷先生如是说——办十进币制根本不配。这副担子能压死任何人，他不打算压死自己——至少，不先领到那份他操劳一生、理应全数归他的酬劳，即一个内阁席位。如今世上有「邦廷派」，正如有「菲尼亚斯派」。后一派大半是女流；前一派则由六七位议员组成，他们自以为看得清路，乐于撺撙这位失意理财家的孤注一掷。
一党之首没有喉舌便一无所有；一张喉舌便应运而起支持邦廷先生——作为自由党人，这喉舌并不怎么体面，因为它是张保守党的报纸——可它照样熨帖他的心，因为这张喉舌不但撑他，还拿出它最响的管风琴音栓，痛骂他天下头一号痛恨的那个人。这张喉舌便是《人民旗帜报》，琴师自然非昆图斯·斯莱德先生莫属。

> Early in last March we found it to be our duty to bring under public notice the conduct of the member for Tankerville in reference to a transaction which took place at a small hotel in Judd Street, and as to which we then ventured to call for the interference of the police. An attempt to murder the member for Tankerville had been made by a gentleman once well known in the political world, who﻿—as it is supposed﻿—had been driven to madness by wrongs inflicted on him in his dearest and nearest family relations. That the unfortunate gentleman is now insane we believe we may state as a fact. It had become our special duty to refer to this most discreditable transaction, from the fact that a paper, still in our hands, had been confided to us for publication by the wretched husband before his senses had become impaired﻿—which, however, we were debarred from giving to the public by an injunction served upon us in sudden haste by the Vice-Chancellor. We are far from imputing evil motives, or even indiscretion, to that functionary; but we are of opinion that the moral feeling of the country would have been served by the publication, and we are sure that undue steps were taken by the member for Tankerville to procure that injunction.

> 那位靠妇人的势力谋得丰厚俸禄的漂亮爱尔兰议员，经我们这番话，想必不会再成为公帑的负担。但我们不能说，我们对此事已然满意，也不能说我们相信公众已经问到底了——凡关公众的事，公众有权问到底。我们至今无从得知：邦廷先生既已被内定为财政大臣——这项任命，已由其党魁在下议院亲口宣布——为何又被摒于内阁之外，改置一个因这一摒除而格外低眉的职位。没有人告诉我们这是为什么；——但我们相信，我们有根据说，这是坦克维尔议员施加影响运作的结果；而且我们深信，国家的公务由此蒙受了重大伤害。
> 格雷沙姆先生赶着那样一队歪歪扭扭的马，那份胆气若不闯出大祸，倒也可以折抵他的无能；要我们夸这队马里的哪一匹，本非我们的本分；但若说其中还有一匹，上坡的稳活我们信得过，那便是邦廷先生。格雷沙姆先生尚未把多比尼先生赶下台，就抢先几周宣布新任财政大臣的人选，这份轻率令我们骇然；——但看到国家财政将托付给格雷沙姆先生罗致来共事的诸君中最称职的一位，我们仍不能不喜。然而菲尼亚斯·芬恩先生带着他的娘子军再度插手，邦廷先生便被打发去了贸易委员会，内阁席次也无。倘若这番可耻的暗盘操作的结果，是本会期未满，邦廷先生已坐上自由党的头把交椅，我们一点也不惊讶。若果真如此，恶事倒结了善果。但无论怎样，我们不禁感到：这般结果，竟出自我们中间两三个决非上等人的私室丑闻，这是政府之耻，议会之耻，国家之耻。
