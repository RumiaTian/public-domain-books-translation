## IV / IV

On the Wharf of Enchantment / 在迷魅之埠

===Original===
Not in our makeup, to be sure—not in the pose which is preceded by the tantaras of a trumpet—do the essential traits in our character first reveal themselves. But truly in the little things the real self is exteriorised. Shakib observes closely the rapid changes in his co-adventurer’s humour, the shadowy traits which at that time he little understood. And now, by applying his palm to his front, he illumines those chambers of which he speaks, and also the niches therein. He helps us to understand the insignificant points which mark the rapid undercurrents of the seemingly sluggish soul of Khalid. Not in vain, therefore, does he crystallise for us that first tear he shed in the harbour of Manhattan. But his gush about the recondite beauty of this pearl of melancholy, shall not be intended upon the gustatory nerves of the Reader. This then we note—his description of New York harbour.

“And is this the gate of Paradise,” he asks, “or the port of some subterrestrial city guarded by the Jinn? What a marvel of enchantment is everything around us! What manifestations of industrial strength, what monstrosities of wealth and power, are here! These vessels proudly putting to sea; these tenders scurrying to meet the Atlantic greyhound which is majestically moving up the bay; these barges loading and unloading schooners from every strand, distant and near; these huge lighters carrying even railroads over the water; these fireboats scudding through the harbour shrilling their sirens; these careworn, grim, strenuous multitudes ferried across from one enchanted shore to another; these giant structures tickling heaven’s sides; these cable bridges, spanning rivers, uniting cities; and this superterrestrial goddess, torch in hand—wake up, Khalid, and behold these wonders. Salaam, this enchanted City! There is the Brooklyn Bridge, and here is the Statue of Liberty which people speak of, and which are as famous as the Cedars of Lebanon.”

But Khalid is as impassive as the bronze goddess herself. He leans over the rail, his hand supporting his cheek, and gazes into the ooze. The stolidity of his expression is appalling. With his mouth open as usual, his lips relaxed, his tongue sticking out through the set teeth—he looks as if his head were in a noose. But suddenly he braces up, runs down for his lute, and begins to serenade—Greater New York?

“On thee be Allah’s grace,
Who hath the well-loved face!”

===Chinese===
诚然，我们性情的那些本质特征，绝非在我们做作的姿态中——并非在伴随着号角嘟嘟声亮相的那种姿态中——最先显露出来。唯有在细微之处，真实的自我方才外现。沙基布细细观察着他这位同道中人情绪的瞬息变幻，观察着彼时他还茫然不解的那些隐约特质。如今他将手掌贴上额前，便照亮了他所说的那些殿堂，以及其中的壁龛。他助我们领会那些标记着哈立德那看似迟滞的灵魂中暗流迅变的细微之处。因此，他为我们结晶出哈立德在曼哈顿港湾所洒下的第一滴泪，并非徒然。但他对这颗忧郁之珠那隐奥之美的滔滔颂赞，我们断不敢拿来烦扰读者的味觉神经。于是我们记下这一点——他对纽约港的描述。

「这难道是乐园之门，」他问道，「还是精灵（Jinn）守护的某座地下之城的海港？我们周围的一切是何等迷人的奇景！这里又是何等工业伟力的彰显，何等财富与权势的奇观！这些船只傲然出海；这些小轮船争先恐后地迎向那艘正庄严驶入海湾的大西洋快邮船；这些驳船在来自远近各海岸的纵帆船旁装卸；这些巨大的平底船甚至把铁路都载过了水面；这些消防船鸣着汽笛飞速穿行于港中；这些忧心忡忡、神情冷峻、劳苦奔波的人群被渡船从一处迷魅之岸载到另一处；这些搔弄天庭两胁的巨型建筑；这些横跨江河、联结城市的悬索大桥；还有这位手持火炬的天上女神——醒来吧，哈立德，看看这些奇迹。祝平安，这座迷魅之城！那边是布鲁克林大桥（Brooklyn Bridge），这边就是人们所说起的自由女神像（Statue of Liberty），它们的名气一如黎巴嫩雪松（Cedars of Lebanon）。」

可哈立德却同那尊铜女神一般无动于衷。他倚在栏杆上，一只手撑着脸颊，凝视着脚下的淤泥。他神情的那种木然，令人不安。他像往常一样张着嘴，双唇松弛，舌尖从咬紧的牙缝里探出来——那模样活像脑袋已被套进了绞索。可忽然他振作起来，跑下舱去取来他的琵琶，开始对着大纽约（Greater New York）弹唱起来——

「愿真主的恩宠归于你，
你那备受钟爱的容颜！」

===Original===
No; not toward this City does his heart flap its wings of song. He is on another sea, in another harbour. Indeed, what are these wonders as compared with those of the City of Love? The Statue of Eros there is more imposing than the Statue of Liberty here. And the bridges are not of iron and concrete, but of rainbows and—moonshine! Indeed, both these lads are now on the wharf of enchantment; the one on the palpable, the sensuous, the other on the impalpable and unseen. But both, alas, are suddenly, but temporarily, disenchanted as they are jostled out of the steamer into the barge which brings them to the Jahannam of Ellis Island. Here, the unhappy children of the steerage are dumped into the Bureau of Emigration as—such stuff! For even in the land of equal rights and freedom, we have a right to expect from others the courtesy and decency which we ourselves do not have to show, or do not know.

===Chinese===
不；他的心并不朝这座城扑扇它歌唱的双翼。他身在另一片海，另一座港。诚然，这些奇观若与爱之城的奇观相比，又算得了什么？那里的厄洛斯神像，比这里的自由女神像还要宏伟。那里的桥不是铁与混凝土所造，而是由彩虹与——月光织成！诚然，这两个少年此刻都立在迷魅之埠上；一个立在有形可触的感官之埠，另一个立在无形不可见的迷魅之埠。可叹的是，两人都猝然间——虽则是暂时的——被祛了魅：他们被轮船上的人推搡着赶进一艘驳船，那驳船把他们载向了埃利斯岛（Ellis Island）这座火狱（Jahannam）。在这里，三等舱里那些不幸的孩子们被倒进移民局（Bureau of Emigration），被视作——这等货色！因为即便在这片平权与自由的国土上，我们也理当有权指望他人以礼相待、以正相待，哪怕我们自己无需如此示人，或不懂得如何示人。

===Original===
These are sturdy and adventurous foreigners whom the grumpy officers jostle and hustle about. For neither poverty, nor oppression, nor both together can drive a man out of his country, unless the soul within him awaken. Indeed, many a misventurous cowering peasant continues to live on bread and olives in his little village, chained in the fear of dying of hunger in a foreign land. Only the brave and daring spirits hearken to the voice of discontent within them. They give themselves up to the higher aspirations of the soul, no matter how limited such aspirations might be, regardless of the dangers and hardship of a long sea voyage, and the precariousness of their plans and hopes. There may be nothing noble in renouncing one’s country, in abandoning one’s home, in forsaking one’s people; but is there not something remarkable in this great move one makes? Whether for better or for worse, does not the emigrant place himself above his country, his people and his Government, when he turns away from them, when he goes forth propelled by that inner self which demands of him a new life?

And might it not be a better, a cleaner, a higher life? What say our Masters of the Island of Ellis? Are not these straggling, smelling, downcast emigrants almost as clean inwardly, and as pure, as the grumpy officers who harass and humiliate them? Is not that spirit of discontent which they cherish, and for which they carry the cross, so to speak, across the sea, deserving of a little consideration, a little civility, a little kindness?

===Chinese===
这些，便是那些被脾气暴躁的官员们推来搡去、呼来喝去的强健而富于冒险精神的外邦人。因为无论是贫困，还是压迫，抑或二者并至，都不能把一个人赶出自己的国土，除非他内在的灵魂已然觉醒。诚然，多少命运多舛、瑟缩怯懦的农人，依旧在他的小村庄里靠面饼和橄榄果腹，被那客死异乡、死于饥馁的恐惧死死锁住。唯有那些勇毅果敢的灵魂，才肯倾听心中那股不满之声。他们任凭自己投身于灵魂那更高的渴慕——无论那渴慕何等有限——不顾漫漫远航的危险与艰辛，不顾他们的谋划与希冀何等渺茫。抛弃自己的国土，背弃自己的家园，离弃自己的族人，这或许并无多少高贵可言；可是，一个人迈出的这一大步，难道没有什么不凡之处吗？无论为福为祸，当移民背过身去，当他被那索求新生的内在自我所推动而远行时，他岂不是已将自己凌驾于他的国土、他的族人、他的政府之上？

而这新生，难道不能是一种更好、更洁净、更高尚的生活吗？我们埃利斯岛上的诸位老爷怎么说？这些衣衫不整、浑身汗臭、神情颓丧的移民，他们内在的洁净与纯真，难道不几乎与那些凌辱欺侮他们的暴躁官员一样吗？他们所珍视的那股不满之心——可以说，他们正是背着这股不满的十字架漂洋过海的——难道不值得一点体恤、一点礼遇、一点善意吗？

===Original===
Even louder than this Shakib cries out, while Khalid open-mouthed sucks his tongue. Here at the last station, where the odours of disinfectants are worse than the stench of the steerage, they await behind the bars their turn; stived with Italian and Hungarian fellow sufferers, uttering such whimpers of expectancy, exchanging such gestures of hope. Soon they shall be brought forward to be examined by the doctor and the interpreting officer; the one shall pry their purses, the other their eyes. For in this United States of America we want clear-sighted citizens at least. And no cold-purses, if the matter can be helped. But neither the eyes, alas, nor the purses of our two emigrants are conformable to the Law; the former are filled with granulations of trachoma, the latter have been emptied by the sharpers of Marseilles. Which means that they shall be detained for the present; and if within a fortnight nothing turns up in their favour, they shall certainly be deported.

Trachoma! a little granulation on the inner surface of the eyelids, what additional misery does it bring upon the poor deported emigrant? We are asked to shed a tear for him, to weep with him over his blasted hopes, his strangled aspirations, his estate in the mother country sold or mortgaged—in either case lost—and his seed of a new life crushed in its cotyledon by the physician who might be shortsighted himself, or even blind. But the law must be enforced for the sake of the clear-sighted citizens of the Republic. We will have nothing to do with these poor blear-eyed foreigners.

===Chinese===
沙基布喊得比这还要响亮，而哈立德则张着嘴，咂着自己的舌头。在这最后一站，消毒剂的气味比三等舱的恶臭还要难闻，他们在栅栏后面等候轮到自己；与意大利和匈牙利的难友们挤作一团，那些人发出满怀期盼的呜咽之声，彼此交换着满怀希望的手势。不久他们便要被带到前面，接受医生和口译官的查验；一个要扒开他们的钱袋，另一个要扒开他们的眼睛。因为在这美利坚合众国，我们至少需要目光炯炯的公民。至于囊中羞涩者，若可避免，则一概不要。可叹的是，我们这两位移民的眼睛与钱袋，都不合于法律：前者布满了沙眼（trachoma）的颗粒，后者早在马赛便被那些骗子掏空了。这意味着，他们暂时要被拘留起来；倘若半月之内并无对他们有利的转机，他们必将被遣返。

沙眼！眼睑内面的一点颗粒，给那可怜的被遣返移民又添了多少苦楚？我们被请求为他洒下一滴泪，与他一同为他那被毁的希冀、被扼杀的抱负、在故国已被变卖或抵押的田产——无论哪种皆是血本无归——以及他那新生的种子尚在子叶之中萌芽便被一位也许自己也近视、甚至全盲的医生所碾碎，而痛哭一场。可为了共和国那些目光炯炯的公民，法律必须得到执行。我们不愿与这些眼目昏花的外邦穷鬼有任何瓜葛。

===Original===
And thus our grievous Scribe would continue, if we did not exercise the prerogative of our Editorial Divan. Rather let us pursue our narration. Khalid is now in the hospital, awaiting further development in his case. But in Shakib’s, whose eyes are far gone in trachoma, the decision of the Board of Emigration is final, irrevokable. And so, after being detained a week in the Emigration pen, the unfortunate Syrian must turn his face again toward the East. Not out into the City, but out upon the sea, he shall be turned adrift. The grumpy officer shall grumpishly enforce the decision of the Board by handing our Scribe to the Captain of the first steamer returning to Europe—if our Scribe can be found! For this flyaway son of a Phoenician did not seem to wait for the decision of the polyglot Judges of the Emigration Board.

And that he did escape, we are assured. For one morning he eludes the grumpy officer, and sidles out among his Italian neighbours who were permitted to land. See him genuflecting now, to kiss the curbstone and thank Allah that he is free. But before he can enjoy his freedom, before he can sit down and chuckle over the success of his escapade, he must bethink him of Khalid. He will not leave him to the mercy of the honourable Agents of the Law, if he can help it. Trachoma, he knows, is a hard case to cure. And in ten days, under the care of the doctors, it might become worse. Straightway, therefore, he puts himself to the dark task. A few visits to the Hospital where Khalid is detained—the patients in those days were not held at Ellis Island—and the intrigue is afoot. On the third or fourth visit, we can not make out which, a note in Arabic is slipt into Khalid’s pocket, and with a significant Arabic sign, Shakib takes himself off.

The evening of that very day, the trachoma-afflicted Syrian was absent from the ward. He was carried off by Iblis—the porter and a few Greenbacks assisting. Yes, even Shakib, who knew only a few English monosyllables, could here make himself understood. For money is one of the two universal languages of the world, the other being love. Indeed, money and love are as eloquent in Turkey and Dahomey as they are in Paris or New York.

===Chinese===
我们这位悲愤的录事本来还会这样滔滔不绝下去，若非我们行使了编者议事会的特权。还是让我们继续叙述吧。哈立德如今住进了医院，静候案情的进一步进展。可沙基布的眼睛沙眼已深，移民局（Board of Emigration）的裁决对他而言乃是终局、无可挽回。于是，在那座移民拘留圈里被关了一周之后，这位不幸的叙利亚人不得不再次将脸朝向东方。不是把他放进城里，而是把他丢到海上，他将被遣散。那位暴躁的官员将要粗暴地执行移民局的裁决，把我们这位录事交给第一艘返欧轮船的船长——倘若我们这位录事还能被找到的话！因为这个腓尼基人的轻浮之子，似乎并不肯坐等那班操着多种语言的移民局法官们的裁决。

至于他确已逃脱，这一点我们可以肯定。因为一天早晨，他甩脱了那位暴躁的官员，混在获准登岸的意大利邻人中间溜了出去。你看他此刻正屈膝跪下，亲吻路沿石，感谢真主让他重获自由。然而，在享用这自由之前，在坐下来为自己的脱身之计暗自得意之前，他得先念及哈立德。只要他力所能及，他就不肯把哈立德交由那些可敬的执法代理人去发落。他知道，沙眼是难治之症。而在医生的照料下过个十天，说不定还会恶化。于是他立刻着手那桩见不得光的勾当。他到拘留哈立德的医院去访了几次——彼时病人并不关在埃利斯岛上——阴谋便已暗中铺开。到了第三或第四次造访——我们分不清是哪一次——一张阿拉伯文的字条被塞进了哈立德的口袋，沙基布使了个意味深长的阿拉伯式眼色，便抽身离去。

就在当天傍晚，那位患了沙眼的叙利亚人便不在病房里了。他是被伊布力斯（Iblis）劫走的——门房和几张绿背钞（Greenbacks）从旁助了一臂之力。是啊，就连只懂几个英文单音节的沙基布，在此地也能叫人听懂他的意思。因为金钱是世上两种通用语言之一，另一种便是爱。诚然，金钱与爱，无论在土耳其还是在达荷美（Dahomey），都和它们在巴黎或纽约一样雄辩。

And here we reach one of those hedges in the *Histoire Intime* which we must go through in spite of the warning-signs. Between two paragraphs, to be plain, in the one of which we are told how the two Syrians established themselves as merchants in New York, in the other, how and wherefor they shouldered the peddling-box and took to the road, there is a crossed paragraph containing a most significant revelation. It seems that after giving the matter some serious thought, our Scribe came to the conclusion that it is not proper to incriminate his illustrious Master. But here is a confession which a hundred crosses can not efface. And if he did not want to bring the matter to our immediate cognisance, why, we ask, did he not rewrite the page? Why did he not cover well that said paragraph with crosses and arabesques? We do suspect him here of chicanery; for by this plausible recantation he would shift the responsibility to the shoulders of the Editor, if the secret is divulged. Be this as it may, no red crosses can conceal from us the astounding confession, which we now give out. For the two young Syrians, who were smuggled out of their country by the boatmen of Beirut, and who smuggled themselves into the city of New York (we beg the critic’s pardon; for, being foreigners ourselves, we ought to be permitted to stretch this term, smuggle, to cover an Arabic metaphor, or to smuggle into it a foreign meaning), these two Syrians, we say, became, in their capacity of merchants, smugglers of the most ingenious and most evasive type.

We now note the following, which pertains to their business. We learn that they settled in the Syrian Quarter directly after clearing their merchandise. And before they entered their cellar, we are assured, they washed their hands of all intrigues and were shrived of their sins by the Maronite priest of the Colony. For they were pious in those days, and right Catholics. ’Tis further set down in the *Histoire Intime*:

“We rented a cellar, as deep and dark and damp as could be found. And our landlord was a Teague, nay, a kindhearted old Irishman, who helped us put up the shelves, and never called for the rent in the dawn of the first day of the month. In the front part of this cellar we had our shop; in the rear, our home. On the floor we laid our mattresses, on the shelves, our goods. And never did we stop to think who in this case was better off. The safety of our merchandise before our own. But ten days after we had settled down, the water issued forth from the floor and inundated our shop and home. It rose so high that it destroyed half of our capital stock and almost all our furniture. And yet, we continued to live in the cellar, because, perhaps, every one of our compatriot-merchants did so. We were all alike subject to these inundations in the winter season. I remember when the water first rose in our store, Khalid was so hard set and in such a pucker that he ran out capless and in his shirt sleeves to discover in the next street the source of the flood. And one day, when we were pumping out the water he asked me if I thought this was easier than rolling our roofs in Baalbek. For truly, the paving-roller is child’s play to this pump. And a leaky roof is better than an inundated cellar.”

至此，我们来到了《私密史》（Histoire Intime）中一道篱障前——尽管竖着警示牌，我们仍须穿行而过。坦白说，在两段之间——一段讲这两个叙利亚人如何在纽约立身成为商人，另一段讲他们为何、又如何扛起货郎箱、上路去走街串巷——夹着一段被画了叉的文字，其中却藏着一项至关重要的揭发。看来，我们的录事对此事郑重思量一番之后，得出结论：把这位显赫大师牵连进去，实在不妥。可这里却有一番自白，一百个叉也涂不掉它。倘若他当真不愿让我们立刻知晓此事，那么我们要问，他为何不索性把这一页重写一遍？为何不把那段话用红叉和阿拉伯式花体涂得严严实实？我们在此确有理由怀疑他耍了花招；因为借着这一番貌似恳切的撤回之辞，一旦秘密败露，他便可把责任推到编者肩上。无论如何，再多的红叉也无法向我们遮掩那番惊人的自白，如今我们且把它公之于众。因为这两个年轻的叙利亚人——他们被贝鲁特（Beirut）的船夫偷渡出国，又把自己偷渡进了纽约城（恳请批评者见谅；既然我们自己也是外邦人，理应容许我们把「偷渡」一词拉长些，好罩住一个阿拉伯语的隐喻，或偷渡进它一层外来的含义）——这两个叙利亚人，我们要说，他们在身为商人的身份上，成了最精巧、也最滑头的走私客。

现在我们记下如下一段，关涉他们的营生。我们得知，他们在货物一通关之后便在叙利亚区（Syrian Quarter）落了脚。而在踏入他们的地下室之前，据我们所知，他们已洗净了手上的种种阴谋，并由殖民地的马龙派（Maronite）神父赦免了罪过。因为他们在那些日子里是虔诚的，是地地道道的天主教徒。《私密史》中尚有如下记载：

「我们租了一间地下室，要多深、多暗、多潮，便有多深、多暗、多潮。我们的房东是个蒂格（Teague）——不，是个心肠好的爱尔兰老翁，他帮我们搭起货架，从不在月初头一天的清晨来催租。这地下室的前半截是我们的店铺；后半截是我们的家。我们把褥垫铺在地上，把货品摆在架上。我们从未停下来想想，这种情形下究竟谁过得更好。我们把货品的安全置于自身的安全之前。可是在我们安顿下来十天之后，水便从地下涌出，淹没了我们的店铺和家。水涨得那样高，毁掉我们一半的本钱和几乎全部的家具。然而我们仍继续住在地下室里，因为——也许——我们每一位同胞商人都是如此。在冬季，我们都同样要遭受这样的水患。我记得水头一次在我们的店里涌上来时，哈立德窘迫焦急得不得了，连帽子也顾不上戴，只穿着衬衣袖子便跑出去，跑到隔壁那条街去查找水患的源头。又有一日，我们正往外泵水，他问我，我是不是觉得这活儿比在巴勒贝克（Baalbek）滚压自家屋顶还要轻松。因为说实话，那铺路的石碾子比起这台水泵来，简直就是小孩子的把戏。一间漏雨的屋顶，毕竟胜过一间被水淹没的地下室。」
