## VIII / VIII

With the Houris / 与呼丽们在一起

===Original===
From the house of law the dervish Khalid wends his way to that of science, and from the house of science he passes on to that of metaphysics. His staff in hand, his wallet hung on his shoulder, his silver cigarette case in his pocket, patient, confident, content, he makes his way from one place to another. Unlike his brother dervishes, he is clean and proud of it, too. He knocks at this or that door, makes his wish known to the servant or the mistress, takes the crumbs given him, and not infrequently gives his prod to the dogs. In the vestibule of one of the houses of spiritism, he tarries a spell and parleys with the servant. The Mistress, a fair-looking, fair-spoken dame of seven lustrums or more, issues suddenly from her studio, in a curiously designed black velvet dressing-gown; she is drawn to the door by the accent of the foreigner’s speech and the peculiar cadence of his voice. They meet: and magnetic currents from his dark eyes and her eyes of blue, flow and fuse. They speak: and the lady asks the stranger if he would not serve instead of begging. And he protests, “I am a Dervish at the door of Allah.” “And I am a Spirit in Allah’s house,” she rejoins. They enter: and the parley in the vestibule is followed by a tête-à-tête in the parlour and another in the dining-room. They agree: and the stranger is made a member of the Spiritual Household, which now consists of her and him, the Medium and the Dervish.

===Chinese===
德尔维希（dervish）哈立德，从律法之舍走向科学之舍，又从科学之舍转赴玄学之舍。手中拄着行杖，肩上挂着褡裢，口袋里揣着他的银烟盒，耐心、自信、知足，他从一处踅到另一处。与他的德尔维希弟兄们不同，他是洁净的，且以此为傲。他敲敲这家的门，又敲敲那家的门，向仆人或是主妇道明来意，接下施舍给他的那点残屑，并不时给那些恶狗一点颜色看看。在一处招魂之家的门廊里，他盘桓了片刻，与仆人攀谈起来。那位主妇——一位相貌端好、言语婉转、年约七个五年期有余的夫人——忽地从她的工作室里走了出来，身披一件式样奇异的黑丝绒晨衣；引她到门前来的，是那异乡人话语里的口音，与他嗓音中那种奇特的抑扬。两人相会：他那双黑眼睛与她那双蓝眼睛之间，磁性的电流奔涌交融。两人交谈：夫人问这位异乡人，可愿意去做工，而不去乞讨。他抗声道：「我是立于真主（Allah）门前的一名德尔维希。」「而我，」她应道，「是真主宅中的一灵。」两人入内：门廊里的那番攀谈之后，是客厅里的一席密语，又是饭厅里的一席。两人达成默契：这位异乡人便成了那「灵性之家」的一员——此家如今只由她与他构成，灵媒（the Medium）与德尔维希。

===Original===
Now, this fair-spoken dame, who dotes on the occult and exotic, delights in the aroma of Khalid’s cigarettes and Khalid’s fancy. And that he might feel at ease, she begins by assuring him that they have met and communed many times ere now, that they have been friends under a preceding and long vanished embodiment. Which vagary Khalid seems to countenance by referring to the infinite power of Allah, in the compass of which nothing is impossible. And with these mystical circumlocutions of ceremony, they plunge into an intimacy which is bordered by the metaphysical on one side, and the physical on the other. For though the Medium is at the threshold of her climacteric, Khalid afterwards tells Shakib that there be something in her eyes and limbs which always seem to be waxing young. And of a truth, the American woman, of all others, knows best how to preserve her beauty from the ravages of sorrow and the years. That is why, we presume, in calling him, “child,” she does not permit him to call her, “mother.” Indeed, the Medium and the Dervish often jest, and somewhiles mix the frivolous with the mysterious.

===Chinese===
这位言语婉转的夫人，素来钟情于玄秘与异域之物，哈立德那烟卷的香、哈立德那奇思的香，都教她心喜。为着让他自在，她一上来便向他保证：他们在此前早已相会、相谈过许多次，在前一番业已消逝的久远化身之中，他们曾为友。这番奇想，哈立德似乎也予以首肯——他把一切归于真主那无穷的大能，在大能所及的范围之内，没有什么是不可能的。就在这套玄秘的客套与迂回之间，他们陷入了一段亲密：其一侧以玄学为界，另一侧以肉身为界。因为灵媒虽已站在她那更年之候的门槛上，哈立德后来却告诉沙基布，她的眼与她的肢体之中，总有某种东西，仿佛永远在向年轻处滋长。说真的，美国女人，比之任何别的女人，最懂得如何护住自己的美，使之免受哀愁与岁月的摧残。正因如此，我们揣想，她唤他作「孩子」，却不许他唤她作「母亲」。确实，灵媒与德尔维希时常戏谑，有时也将轻佻与玄秘搅在一处。

===Original===
We would still follow our Scribe here, were it not that his pruriency often reaches the edge. He speaks of “the liaison” with all the rude simplicity and frankness of the *Arabian Nights*. And though, as the Mohammedans say, “To the pure everything is pure,” and again, “Who quotes a heresy is not guilty of it”; nevertheless, we do not feel warranted in rending the veil of the reader’s prudery, no matter how transparent it might be. We believe, however, that the pruriency of Orientals, like the prudery of Occidentals, is in fact only an appearance. On both sides there is a display of what might be called verbal virtue and verbal vice. And on both sides, the exaggerations are configured in a harmless pose. Be this as it may, we at least, shall withhold from Shakib’s lasciviousness the English dress it seeks at our hand.

===Chinese===
若不是我们这位抄写人的好色之辞每每逼近那条边线，我们原还要在此跟着他往下走的。他谈及「那番私情」，带着《一千零一夜》（Arabian Nights）那种粗犷的质朴与坦白。虽然如穆斯林所言，「于纯洁者，万物皆洁」，又如他们所言，「引用异端之人，并不为异端之罪」；然而我们仍觉得，无权去撕开读者那层端庄的帷幕——无论那帷幕多么透明。不过我们相信，东方人的好色，正似西方人的端庄，其实不过是一种表象。两边所展示的，都可称作「言辞上的德行」与「言辞上的罪过」。而两边的夸张，也都摆出一种于人无害的姿态。无论如何，至少我们，决不替沙基布那番淫辞，穿上它向我们讨要的那身英装。

===Original===
We note, however, that Khalid now visits him in the cellar only when he craves a dish of *mujaddara*; that he and the Medium are absorbed in the contemplation of the Unseen, though not, perhaps, of the Impalpable; that they gallivant in the Parks, attend Bohemian dinners, and frequent the Don’t Worry Circles of Metaphysical Societies; that they make long expeditions together to the Platonic North-pole and back to the torrid regions of Swinburne; and that together they perform their *zikr* and drink at the same fountain of ecstasy and devotion. Withal, the Dervish, who now wears his hair long and grows his finger nails like a Brahmin, is beginning to have some manners.

===Chinese===
然而我们留意到：哈立德如今只有在馋一碗穆贾达拉（mujaddara）时，才会下到地窖里去看他；他与灵媒潜心凝视着那「不可见」，虽然未必是那「不可触」；他们一道在公园里招摇，赴波希米亚（Bohemian）的宴席，常去出入那些玄学学会的「莫愁圈」；他们一同远征，远至柏拉图式的北极，又折回斯温伯恩（Swinburne）的炎热之境；他们又一同行他们的齐克尔（zikr），同饮那一脉狂喜与虔信之泉。此外，这位如今蓄起长发、留起指甲、状若婆罗门的德尔维希，也总算开始有了几分规矩。

===Original===
The Medium, nevertheless, withholds from him the secret of her art. If he desires, he can attend the séances like every other stranger. Once Khalid, who would not leave anything unprobed, insisted, importuned; he could not see any reason for her conduct. Why should they not work together in Tiptology, as in Physiology and Metaphysics? And one morning, dervish-like, he wraps himself in his aba, and, calling upon Allah to witness, takes a rose from the vase on the table, angrily plucks its petals, and strews them on the carpet. Which portentous sign the Medium understands and hastens to minister her palliatives.

===Chinese===
然而，灵媒却向他守着她那一门技艺的秘密。他若愿意，可以像任何一个陌生客那样出席降神会。哈立德一向凡事都要探个究竟，有一回便执意追问、苦苦纠缠；他实在看不出她那番举止有何道理。为何他们不能在招魂术（tiptology，即 spirit-table-tipping）上一同用功，一如他们在生理学与玄学上一同用功？于是一天早晨，他一副德尔维希做派，把自己裹进他的阿巴（aba，阿拉伯斗篷），唤真主作证，从桌上的花瓶里取出一朵玫瑰，愤愤地扯下花瓣，撒在地毯上。这一不祥之兆，灵媒一看便懂，赶忙施以她的缓兵之辞。

===Original===
“No, Child, you shall not go,” she begs and supplicates; “listen to me, are we not together all the time? Why not leave me alone then with the spirits? One day you shall know all, believe me. Come, sit here,” stroking her palm on her lap, “and listen. I shall give up this tiptology business very soon; you and I shall overturn the table. Yes, Child, I am on the point of succumbing under an awful something. So, don’t ask me about the spooks any more. Promise not to torment me thus any more. And one day we shall travel together in the Orient; we shall visit the ruins of vanished kingdoms and creeds. Ah, to be in Palmyra with you! Do you know, Child, I am destined to be a Bedouin queen. The throne of Zenobia is mine, and yours too, if you will be good. We shall resuscitate the glory of the kingdom of the desert.”

===Chinese===
「不，孩子，你不许走，」她又是求、又是告；「听我说，我们岂不是时时刻刻都在一起？那何不把我一个人留给那些精灵呢？终有一日你会尽知一切的，信我。来，坐这里，」她在自己膝上摩挲着手心，「听着。我很快便会丢开这套招魂术的营生；你和我，要一同把那张桌子掀翻。是的，孩子，我已濒于在某样可怕之物下屈服。所以，别再问我那些鬼魂的事了。答应我，别再这样折磨我。有朝一日，我们要一同去东方游历；我们要去看那些消逝了的王国与信仰的废墟。啊，能与你同在帕尔米拉（Palmyra）！你可知道，孩子，我命里注定要做一位贝都因（Bedouin）女王。芝诺比娅（Zenobia）的宝座是我的，也是你的——只要你乖。我们要复兴那沙漠王国的荣光。」

===Original===
To all of which Khalid acquiesces by referring as is his wont to the infinite wisdom of Allah, in whose all-seeing eye nothing is impossible.

===Chinese===
对这一切，哈立德概然默许——一如他的习惯，把话头归于真主那无穷的智慧，在真主那无所不见的眼中，没有什么是不可能的。

===Original===
And thus, apparently satisfied, he takes the cigarette which she had lighted for him, and lights for her another from his own. But the smoke of two cigarettes dispels not the threatening cloud; it only conceals it from view. For they dine together at a Bohemian Club that evening, where Khalid meets a woman of rare charms. And she invites him to her studio. The Medium, who is at first indifferent, finally warns her callow child. “That woman is a writer,” she explains, “and writers are always in search of what they call ‘copy.’ She in particular is a huntress of male curiosities, *originales*, whom she takes into her favour and ultimately surrenders them to the reading public. So be careful.” But Khalid hearkens not. For the writer, whom he afterwards calls a flighter, since she, too, “like the van of the brewer only skims the surface of things,” is, in fact, younger than the Medium. Ay, this woman is even beautiful﻿—to behold, at least. So the Dervish, a captive of her charms, knocks at the door of her studio one evening and enters. Ah, this then is a studio! “I am destined to know everything, and to see everything,” he says to himself, smiling in his heart.

===Chinese===
于是，他一副显然心足意满的样子，接过她替他点燃的烟卷，又从自己的烟卷上为她点燃另一支。然而两支烟卷的烟雾，并不能驱散那团逼人之云；它只是把它遮了起来，使之不入眼罢了。因为那天傍晚，他们一同在一家波希米亚俱乐部用饭，在那里，哈立德遇见了一位风韵罕有的女子。她邀他到她的工作室去。灵媒起先不以为意，末了才警告她这个不更事的稚子。「那女人是个作家，」她解释道，「而作家们，总是在搜寻他们所谓的『素材』。她尤其是一位猎取男性奇珍——那些『怪人』（originales）——的猎手，先把他们宠幸一番，最终再把他们献出去，交给读书的大众。所以你要当心。」可哈立德偏不听。因为那位作家——他后来称她作一个「浮掠者」，因为她「也像酿酒桶口那层浮沫，只掠事物的表皮」——事实上比灵媒年轻。是啊，这女子甚至可以说是美的——至少看起来是。于是这位做了她魅力之囚的德尔维希，某天傍晚敲响了她工作室的门，走了进去。啊，原来这便是一间工作室！「我命中注定要尽知一切、尽见一切，」他心里微笑着，对自己说。

===Original===
The charming hostess, in a Japanese kimono receives him somewhat orientally, offering him the divan, which he occupies alone for a spell. He is then laden with a huge scrapbook containing press notices and reviews of her many novels. These, he is asked to go through while she prepares the tea. Which is a mortal task for the Dervish in the presence of the Enchantress. Alas, the tea is long in the making, and when the scrapbook is laid aside, she reinforces him with a lot of magazines adorned with stories of the short and long and middling size, from her fertile pen. “These are beautiful,” says he, in glancing over a few pages, “but no matter how you try, you can not with your pen surpass your own beauty. The charm of your literary style can not hold a candle to the charm of your﻿—permit me to read your hand.” And laying down the magazine, he takes up her hand and presses it to his lips. In like manner, he tries to read somewhat in the face, but the Enchantress protests and smiles. In which case the smile renders the protest null and void.

===Chinese===
那位迷人的女主人，身着一袭日本和服，颇有几分东方风味地迎接了他，请他在长榻上坐，他独自在那榻上占了一阵。随后，一只硕大的剪贴簿被压到了他手里，里头尽是报界对她那许多部小说的评介与书评。她请他翻阅，自己则去沏茶。这对在女妖（the Enchantress）跟前的德尔维希而言，实在是一桩要命的苦差。哎，茶迟迟沓沓，那剪贴簿被搁到一边之后，她又拿一大摞杂志来增补他的负担——那上头尽出自她那支多产的笔，点缀着长长短短、不长不短的故事。「这些真美，」他随意翻了几页说道，「可无论你怎样努力，你那支笔总也胜不过你自己的美。你那文笔的风韵，给你那——请许我一读你的手——的风韵提鞋也不配。」说罢他把杂志放下，拉起她的手，按到自己唇上。他又照此办理，要在那张脸上读出一点什么，女妖却一边抗议、一边微笑。在这一情形下，那微笑便使抗议归于无效。

===Original===
Henceforth, the situation shall be trying even to the Dervish who can eat live coals. He oscillates for some while between the Medium and the Enchantress, but finds the effort rather straining. The first climax, however, is reached, and our Scribe thinks it too sad for words. He himself sheds a few rheums with the fair-looking, fair-spoken Dame, and dedicates to her a few rhymes. Her magnanimity, he tells us, is unexampled, and her fatalism pathetic. For when Khalid severs himself from the Spiritual Household, she kisses him thrice, saying, “Go, Child; Allah brought you to me, and Allah will bring you again.” Khalid refers, as usual, to the infinite wisdom of the Almighty, and, taking his handkerchief from his pocket, wipes the tears that fell﻿—from her eyes over his. He passes out of the vestibule, silent and sad, musing on the time he first stood there as a beggar.

===Chinese===
