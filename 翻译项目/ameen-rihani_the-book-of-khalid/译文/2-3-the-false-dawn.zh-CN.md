## III / III

The False Dawn / 虚假的黎明

===Original===
What the Arabs always said of Andalusia, Khalid and Shakib said once of America: a most beautiful country with one single vice—it makes foreigners forget their native land. But now they are both suffering from nostalgia, and America, therefore, is without a single vice. It is perfect, heavenly, ideal. In it one sees only the vices of other races, and the ugliness of other nations. America herself is as lovely as a dimpled babe, and as innocent. A dimpled babe she. But wait until she grows, and she will have more than one vice to demand forgetfulness.

Shakib, however, is not going to wait. He begins to hear the call of his own country, now that his bank account is big enough to procure for him the Pashalic of Syria. And Khalid, though his pushcart had developed to a stationary fruit stand—and perhaps for this very reason—is now desirous of leaving America anon. He is afraid of success overtaking him. Moreover, the Bronx Park has awakened in him his long dormant love of Nature. For while warming himself on the flames of knowledge in the cellar, or rioting with the Bassarides of Bohemia, or canvassing and speechifying for Tammany, he little thought of what he had deserted in his native country. The ancient historical rivers flowing through a land made sacred by the divine madness of the human spirit; the snow-capped mountains at the feet of which the lily and the oleander bloom; the pine forests diffusing their fragrance even among the downy clouds; the peaceful, sun-swept multicoloured meadows; the trellised vines, the fig groves, the quince orchards, the orangeries: the absence of these did not disturb his serenity in the cellar, his voluptuousness in Bohemia, his enthusiasm in Tammany Land.

===Chinese===
阿拉伯人向来如是说安达卢西亚（Andalusia），哈立德与沙基布某日也曾如是说美国：这是一片至美的国度，却独有一桩毛病——它使外来之客忘却故土。可如今二人都为乡愁所苦，于是这美国便再无一桩毛病可言了。它尽善尽美，恍如天国，理想无瑕。置身其中，人所见的只是异族的恶习、他国的丑陋。美国自身则如一个带着酒窝的婴孩，一样可爱，一样无邪。一个带着酒窝的婴孩，她便是。可且待她长大，她必将有不止一桩毛病，足以叫人非忘不可。

然而沙基布并不打算等。如今他在银行里的存款，已足够替他买下叙利亚的帕夏（pasha）之位，他遂开始听见自己祖国的召唤了。而哈立德呢，虽然他的货郎车已发展成一处固定的水果摊——或许正因这一缘故——如今也巴望早些离开美国。他怕成功追上他。再者，布朗克斯公园（the Bronx）已唤醒他久已蛰伏的对自然之爱。因为当他在地窖里向知识的火焰取暖之时，或在波希米亚（Bohemia）与酒神狂女（Bassarides）一同纵乐之时，或为塔马尼（Tammany）奔走演说之时，他几乎不曾想起自己在故国所抛下的一切：那些在一片因人类精神之神圣癫狂而成为圣地的国土上流淌的、有悠久历史可考的河流；那些山脚下百合与夹竹桃一齐盛开的雪冠山峦；那些连柔云之间也弥漫着清芬的松林；那些宁静的、阳光漫卷的、斑斓多彩的草甸；那一架架棚架上的葡萄藤，一片片无花果林，一处处榅桲果园，一座座橘园——这一切的缺席，都不曾扰乱他在地窖里的安宁、他在波希米亚的放浪、他在塔马尼之地的热忱。

===Original===
And we must not forget to mention that, besides the divine voice of Nature and native soil, he long since has heard and still hears the still sweet voice of one who might be dearer to him than all. For Khalid, after his return from Bohemia, continued to curse the houris in his dreams. And he little did taste of the blessings of “sore labour’s bath, balm of hurt minds.” Ay, when he was not racked and harrowed by nightmares, he was either disturbed by the angels of his visions or the succubi of his dreams. And so, he determines to go to Syria for a night’s sleep, at least, of the innocent and just. His cousin Najma is there, and that is enough. Once he sees her, the houris are no more.

Now Shakib, who is more faithful in his narration than we first thought—who speaks of Khalid as he is, extenuating nothing—gives us access to a letter which he received from the Bronx a month before their departure from New York. In these Letters of Khalid, which our Scribe happily preserved, we feel somewhat relieved of the dogmatism, fantastic, mystical, severe, which we often meet with in the K.L. MS. In his Letters, our Syrian peddler and seer is a plain blunt man unbosoming himself to his friend. Read this, for instance.

===Chinese===
且我们切莫忘了提起：除了自然与故土那神圣的声音之外，他早已听见、且仍在听见一个或许于他比一切都更亲切之人的、那静而甜美的声音。因为哈立德自波希米亚归来之后，在梦中仍不停地咒骂那些天园美女（houris）。而「辛劳之浴，伤神之膏」的福分，他几乎未曾尝过。是啊，当他不被噩梦拷掠翻犁之时，便要么为他幻象中的天使所惊扰，要么为他梦中的女妖（succubi）所纠缠。于是他决意前往叙利亚，至少去寻一夜——属于无辜者与义人的一夜——安眠。他的表妹娜吉玛（Najma）便在那里，这便够了。他一旦见着她，那些天园美女便不复存在了。

话说沙基布——他叙述之忠实远过我们最初的设想，他把哈立德如实道来，无所粉饰——让我们得以读到一封信，那是他们离开纽约之前一个月，他从布朗克斯收到的。在这些由我们的抄写人幸运保存下来的「哈立德书简」之中，我们总算稍稍摆脱了那种我们在赫迪夫图书馆手稿（K.L. MS.）里常常遇见的——教条、奇幻、神秘而严峻——的口气。在他的书信里，我们这位叙利亚的货郎兼先知，乃是一个质朴直率之人，向他的朋友倾吐衷肠。且读这一段，为例。

===Original===
“My loving Brother:

“It is raining so hard tonight that I must sleep, or in fact keep, within doors. Would you believe it, I am no more accustomed to the luxuries of a soft spring-bed, and I can not even sleep on the floor, where I have moved my mattress. I am sore, broken in mind and spirit. Even the hemlock grove and the melancholy stillness of the river, are beginning to annoy me. Oh, I am tired of everything here, tired even of the cocktails, tired of the pushcart, tired of earning as much as five dollars a day. Next Sunday is inauguration day for my stationary fruit stand; but I don’t think it’s going to stand there long enough to deserve to be baptized with champagne. If you come up, therefore, we’ll have a couple of steins at the Hermitage and call it square.—O, I would square myself with the doctors by thrusting a poker down my windpipe: I might be able to breathe better then. I pause to curse my fate.—Curse it, Jahannam-born, curse it!—

===Chinese===
「我亲爱的兄弟：

「今夜雨下得这样大，我只得在屋里睡觉——或者说，呆着。你信不信？我已不再习惯软弹簧床那种奢华，可我连地板上也睡不着——我已把褥子搬到了地板上。我浑身酸痛，身心俱碎。连那片铁杉林、连那条河的忧郁死寂，也开始惹我烦了。唉，这里的一切我都厌倦了，连鸡尾酒也厌倦了，连货郎车也厌倦了，连一天能挣五块钱也厌倦了。下个星期日，是我那固定水果摊的开张大吉之日；可我不觉得它能在那儿站得足够久，久到配得上一场香槟洗礼。所以你若上来，我们便到「隐士居」（the Hermitage）去喝上两大杯啤酒，就算扯平了。——啊，我恨不得把一根拨火棍捅进自己的气管，好与那些医生们算清这笔账：那样我也许倒能喘得畅快些。我且停笔，咒一咒我的命运。——咒它，这火狱（Jahannam）里生出来的东西，咒它！——

===Original===
“I can not sleep, nor on the spring-bed, nor on the floor. It is two hours past midnight now, and I shall try to while away the time by scrawling this to you. My brother, I can not long support this sort of life, being no more fit for rough, ignominious labor. ‘But why,’ you will ask, ‘did you undertake it?’ Yes, why? Strictly speaking, I made a mistake. But it’s a noble mistake, believe me—a mistake which everybody in my condition ought to make, if but once in their lifetime. Is it not something to be able to make an honest resolution and carry it out? I have heard strange voices in prison; I have hearkened to them; but I find that one must have sound lungs, at least, to be able to do the will of the immortal gods. And even if he had, I doubt if he could do much to suit them in America. O, my greatest enemy and benefactor in the whole world is this dumb-hearted mother, this America, in whose iron loins I have been spiritually conceived. Paradoxical, this? But is it not true? Was not the Khalid, now writing to you, born in the cellar? Down there, in the very loins of New York? But alas, our spiritual Mother devours, like a cat, her own children. How then can we live with her in the same house?

===Chinese===
「我睡不着，弹簧床上睡不着，地板上也睡不着。此刻已过了半夜两点，我便试着胡乱涂这几笔给你，好把这时间消磨过去。我的兄弟，我长久支撑不了这种生活了，我已不再适合做粗重而屈辱的营生。『可你当初为何，』你会问，『要去做它呢？』是，为何？严格说来，我犯了一个错。可这是一个高贵的错，信我——一个凡处我之境地的人，纵使一生只一次，也理当去犯的错。能够立下一个诚实的决心、并把它付诸实行，这岂不也算一桩事么？我在狱中曾听见奇异的声音；我曾侧耳倾听它们；可我发觉，一个人至少得有一副健全的肺，才能行那不朽诸神的旨意。便算他有，我也疑心他在美国做不出多少能令他们称心的事来。啊，我在这个世上最大的仇敌，也是最大的恩人，便是这位铁石心肠的母亲——这美国——我便是属灵地在她那铁一般的腰腹里受孕的。这听来矛盾？可难道不真么？现在写信给你的这个哈立德，岂不正是生在地窖里的么？在那底下，在纽约的腰腹之中？可是唉，我们这位属灵的母亲，竟像猫一样，吞吃她自己的孩子。那么，我们又怎能与她同住一屋之下呢？

===Original===
“I need not tell you now that the ignominious task I set my hands to, was never to my liking. But the ox under the yoke is not asked whether he likes it or not. I have been yoked to my pushcart by the immortal gods; and soon my turn and trial will end. It must end. For our country is just beginning to speak, and I am her chosen voice. I feel that if I do not respond, if I do not come to her, she will be dumb forever. No; I can not remain here any more. For I can not be strenuous enough to be miserably happy; nor stupid enough to be contentedly miserable. I confess I have been spoiled by those who call themselves spiritual sisters of mine. The houris be dam’d. And if I don’t leave this country soon, I’ll find myself sharing the damnation again—in Bohemia.—

===Chinese===
「如今我已不必再告诉你：我亲手做起来的这桩屈辱营生，从来就不合我的意。可是轭下的牛，并无人问它喜不喜欢。我乃是被不朽诸神套上了我的货郎车；而我的轮值与试炼，不久便将终了。它非终了不可。因为我们的祖国正开始说话，而我便是她所选中的声音。我觉得，我若不应答，我若不回到她那里，她将永远喑哑。不；我再不能在此地呆下去了。因为我还不够刻苦，够不上做一个痛苦而幸福的人；我也不够愚蠢，够不上做一个知足而痛苦的人。我承认，我已被那些自称是我属灵姊妹的人们宠坏了。让那些天园美女见鬼去罢。我若不早日离开这个国度，便会发觉自己又分得了那份诅咒——在波希米亚。——

===Original===
“The power of the soul is doubled by the object of its love, or by such labor of love as it undertakes. But, here I am, with no work and nobody I can love; nay, chained to a task which I now abominate. If a labor of love doubles the power of the soul, a labor of hate, to use an antonym term, warps it, poisons it, destroys it. Is it not a shame that in this great Country—this Circe with her golden horns of plenty—one can not as much as keep his blood in circulation without damning the currents of one’s soul? O America, equally hated and beloved of Khalid, O Mother of prosperity and spiritual misery, the time will come when you shall see that your gold is but pinchbeck, your gilt-edge bonds but death decrees, and your god of wealth a carcase enthroned upon a dunghill. But you can not see this now; for you are yet in the false dawn, floundering tumultuously, worshipping your mammoth carcase on a dunghill—and devouring your spiritual children. Yes, America is now in the false dawn, and as sure as America lives, the true dawn must follow.

“Pardon, Shakib. I did not mean to end my letter in a rhapsody. But I am so wrought, so broken in body, so inflamed in spirit. I hope to see you soon. No, I hope to see myself with you on board of a Transatlantic steamer.”

===Chinese===
「灵魂之力，因它所爱之对象、或因它所从事的那份爱之劳作，而得以倍增。可我在此，既无事可做，亦无人可爱；不，还被锁在一桩如今我所痛恨的差事上。爱之劳作既能倍增灵魂之力，恨之劳作——姑且用其反义词——则扭曲它、毒害它、毁灭它。在这片伟大的国度里——这位顶戴丰饶金角的喀耳刻（Circe）——一个人竟不能使自己的血液周流不息，而不咒诅自己灵魂的潜流，这岂不可耻？啊，美国，哈立德既恨且爱的美国，啊，这繁华之母，亦是属灵苦楚之母，终有一日你会看见：你的金子不过是铜黄赝品，你的金边债券不过是死亡判决书，你的财神不过是一具端坐在粪堆之上的尸骸。可你现在还看不见这些；因为你尚在虚假的黎明之中，喧嚣地挣扎着，膜拜你那粪堆之上的巨硕尸骸——吞食着你属灵的儿女。是的，美国如今正处在虚假的黎明里，而美国一日尚存，真正的黎明便必随之而来。

「原谅我，沙基布。我本不打算以一通狂想曲来收束这封信。可我已被激荡至此，身体如此破碎，精神如此炽烈。我盼望早日见着你。不，我盼望早日与你在同一艘横渡大西洋的轮船上。」

===Original===
And is not Khalid, like his spiritual Mother, floundering, too, in the false dawn of life? His love of Nature, which was spontaneous and free, is it not likely to become formal and scientific? His love of Country, which begins tremulously, fervently in the woods and streams, is it not likely to end in Nephelococcygia? His determination to work, which was rudely shaken at a pushcart, is it not become again a determination to loaf? And now, that he has a little money laid up, has he not the right to seek in this world the cheapest and most suitable place for loafing? And where, if not in the Lebanon hills, “in which it seemed always afternoon,” can he rejoin the Lotus-Eaters of the East? This man of visions, this fantastic, rhapsodical—but we must not be hard upon him. Remember, good Reader, the poker which he would thrust down his windpipe to broaden it a little. With asthmatic fits and tuberous infiltrations, one is permitted to commune with any of Allah’s ministers of grace or spirits of Jahannam. And that divine spark of primal, paradisical love, which is rapidly devouring all others—let us not forget that. Ay, we mean his cousin Najma. Of course, he speaks, too, of his nation, his people, awaking, lisping, beginning to speak, waiting for him, the chosen Voice! Which reminds us of how he was described to us by the hashish-smokers of Cairo.

In any event, the Reader will rejoice with us, we hope, that Khalid will not turn again toward Bohemia. He will agree with us that, whether on account of his health, or his love, or his mission, it is well, in his present fare of mind and body, that he is returning to the land “in which it seemed always afternoon.”

===Chinese===
而哈立德，岂不同他那属灵的母亲一样，也在人生的虚假黎明里挣扎着么？他对自然之爱，本是自发的、自由的，岂不会变得刻板而科学化么？他对祖国之爱，发端时是那样颤栗、那样炽烈地在林间与溪畔，岂不会终结于「云中鹃城」（Nephelococcygia）么？他做工的决心，在货郎车前已被狠狠动摇，如今岂不又变作游手好闲的决心了么？如今他既攒下了一点钱，岂不有权在这世上寻一个最便宜、最合宜的去处去消闲度日么？而除了在黎巴嫩群山之间——那片「仿佛永远是午后」之地——他又能在何处与东方的食莲人（Lotus-Eaters）重逢呢？这个幻象之人，这个异想天开、狂放不羁的——可我们不该对他太苛刻。好读者，请记起他那根想捅进自己气管、好把它撑宽些的拨火棍罢。一个患着气喘、肺里又有结核浸润的人，是有资格去与真主（Allah）的任何恩典之使、或火狱里任何精灵交通的。还有那一点原始的、乐园之爱的神圣火花，正在迅速吞灭其他一切——这一点我们也切莫忘了。是啊，我们指的就是他的表妹娜吉玛。当然，他也说到他的国家、他的人民——正在苏醒、正在牙牙学语、正开始说话、正等候着他这位选中的声音！这倒叫我们想起，开罗那些吸哈希什（hashish）的人曾怎样向我们描绘他。

无论如何，我们盼读者能与我们一起庆幸：哈立德不再掉头重返波希米亚了。他也会赞同我们：无论是为了他的健康，为了他的爱，还是为了他的使命，以他当下身心所处的这般境况，回到那片「仿佛永远是午后」的土地上去，原是再好不过的事。
