## IV / IV

The Last Star / 末星

===Original===
Is it not an ethnic phenomenon that a descendant of the ancient Phoenicians can not understand the meaning and purport of the Cash Register in America? Is it not strange that this son of Superstition and Trade can not find solace in the fact that in this Pyx of Business is the Host of the Demiurgic Dollar? Indeed, the omnipresence and omnipotence of it are not without divine significance. For can you not see that this Cash Register, this Pyx of Trade, is prominently set up on the altar of every institution, political, moral, social, and religious? Do you not meet with it everywhere, and foremost in the sanctuaries of the mind and the soul? In the Societies for the Diffusion of Knowledge; in the Social Reform Propagandas; in the Don’t Worry Circles of Metaphysical Gymnasiums; in Alliances, Philanthropic, Educational; in the Board of Foreign Missions; in the Sacrarium of Vaticinatress Eddy; in the Church of God itself;—is not the Cash Register a divine symbol of the credo, the faith, or the idea?

===Chinese===
一个古代腓尼基人（Phoenicians）的后裔，竟领会不了美国那台「收银机」（Cash Register）的意义与用意，这难道不是一种种族学上的奇观么？这一个迷信与贸易之子，竟不能在这桩买卖的「圣体盒」（Pyx）里盛着「造化美元」（Demiurgic Dollar）的圣体一事之中找到慰藉，这岂不古怪？诚然，它的无所不在与无所不能，并非没有神圣的意味。因为难道你看不见——这台收银机，这台贸易的圣体盒，赫然被供在每一类机构——政治的、道德的、社会的、宗教的——的祭坛之上么？难道你不是处处遇见它，且 foremost 在心智与灵魂的圣所之内最先遇见它么？在「知识传播会」里；在「社会改革宣传团」里；在「玄学体操馆」的「莫愁圈」里；在种种慈善的、教育的「联合会」里；在「海外布道部」里；在女预言家艾迪夫人（Vaticinatress Eddy）的圣龛里；甚至在真主自己的教会里——难道这台收银机不正是那信条、那信仰、或那理念的神圣象征么？

===Original===
“To trade, or not to trade,” Hamlet-Khalid exclaims, “that is the question: whether ’tis nobler in the mind to suffer, etc., or to take arms against the Cash Registers of America, and by opposing end—” What? Sacrilegious wretch, would you set your face against the divinity in the Holy Pyx of Trade? And what will you end, and how will You end by it? An eternal problem, this, of opposing and ending. But before you set your face in earnest, we would ask you to consider if the vacancy or chaos which is sure to follow, be not more pernicious than what you would end. If you are sure it is not, go ahead, and we give you Godspeed. If you have the least doubt about it—but Khalid is incapable now of doubting anything. And whether he opposes his theory of immanent morality to the Cash Register, or to Democracy, or to the ruling powers of Flunkeydom, we hope He will end well. Such is the penalty of revolt against the dominating spirit of one’s people and ancestors, that only once in a generation is it attempted, and scarcely with much success. In fact, the first who revolts must perish, the second, too, and the third, and the fourth, until, in the course of time and by dint of repetition and resistance, the new species of the race can overcome the forces of environment and the crushing influence of conformity. This, we know, is the biological law, and Khalid must suffer under it. For, as far as our knowledge extends, he is the first Syrian, the ancient Lebanon monks excepted, who revolted against the ruling spirit of his people and the dominant tendencies of the times, both in his native and his adopted Countries.

===Chinese===
「做买卖，还是不做买卖，」哈姆雷特式的哈立德（Hamlet-Khalid）高呼道，「这才是问题所在：究竟是于心忍受、等等较为高尚，还是奋起对抗美国的收银机，并借由反抗来终结——」终结什么？你这亵渎的恶徒，你竟要同这贸易之圣体盒中的神性作对么？你又要终结什么，你又怎样借它来终结你自己？这「反抗」与「终结」之难题，原是亘古如斯。但在你当真铁了心之前，我们倒要请你三思：随后必将来临的那片空缺或混乱，会不会比你所要终结的为害更甚？你若确信不然，便放手去做，我们祝你马到成功。你若对此有丝毫犹疑——可哈立德如今已无力再怀疑任何事了。无论他是把自己那一套「内在道德」论去对抗收银机，还是去对抗民主，还是去对抗那「奴才王国」（Flunkeydom）的当道权势，我们都愿他有个好收场。反叛自己民族与祖宗那主导精神所须付出的代价，便是：一代人之中仅有一人敢试，且鲜有成功的。事实上，头一个反叛的必死，第二个亦然，第三、第四个亦然，直到随着时间的推移、凭着反复与抗争，这一族类的新种方能克服环境的压力与从俗的碾压力量。我们知道，这便是生物学上的法则，而哈立德必得受它辖制。因为据我们所知，他是头一个叙利亚人——古代黎巴嫩（Lebanon）的修士除外——竟在自己的故国与归化之国两处，都起而反抗其民族的主宰精神与时代的主导风气。

===Original===
Yes, the ethos of the Syrians (for once we use Khalid’s philosophic term), like that of the Americans, is essentially money-seeking. And whether in Beirut or in New York, even the moralists and reformers, like the hammals and grocers, will ask themselves, before they undertake to do anything for you or for their country, “What will this profit us? How much will it bring us?” And that is what Khalid once thought to oppose and end. Alas, oppose he might—and End He Must. How can an individual, without the aid of Time and the Unseen Powers, hope to oppose and end, or even change, this monstrous mass of things? Yet we must not fail to observe that when we revolt against a tendency inimical to our law of being, it is for our own sake, and not the race’s, that we do so. And we are glad we are able to infer, if not from the K.L. MS., at least from his Letters, that Khalid is beginning to realise this truth. Let us not, therefore, expatiate further upon it.

===Chinese===
不错，叙利亚人（此处我们姑且一用哈立德的哲学术语）的气质，与美国人的一样，其根本乃是逐利的。无论在贝鲁特（Beirut）还是在纽约（New York），便是那些道德家与改革家，也如同搬运夫与杂货商一般，在着手为你、或为他们的国家做任何事之前，都要先自问一句：「这于我们有何益处？能给我们带来多少？」而这一桩，正是哈立德一度想要反抗、想要终结的。唉，反抗他或许能够——而「终结」他却必须。一个孤零零的个人，若无「时间」与那些「不可见之力」相助，又怎能指望去反抗、去终结、甚至只是改变这一团怪物般堆积的事物呢？然而我们不能不看到：当我们起来反抗一种与我们的「存在法则」相敌的倾向时，我们如此做，乃是为我们自己，并非为整个族类。而我们很高兴能够推断——倘若不是从赫迪夫图书馆手稿（K.L. MS.），至少也是从他的《书信集》里——哈立德已经开始领悟这个道理了。故而，我们在此不再赘述。

===Original===
If the reader will accompany us now to the cellar to bid our Syrian friends farewell, we promise a few things of interest. When we first came here some few years ago in Winter, or to another such underground dwelling, the water rose ankle-deep over the floor, and the mould and stench were enough to knock an ox dead. Now, a scent of attar of roses welcomes us at the door and leads us to a platform in the centre, furnished with a Turkish rug, which Shakib will present to the landlord as a farewell memento.

===Chinese===
读者若愿随我们下到地窖，去与我们的叙利亚朋友道别，我们担保有几桩趣事可看。几年前冬天我们头一回到这里时——或者说，到另一处这样的地下住所时——积水漫过地板有脚踝深，那霉味与恶臭，足以把一头牛当即熏翻。如今，一股玫瑰香精（attar of roses）的气味在门口迎我们，引我们走到屋中央一处铺着土耳其地毯的台子上——这张地毯，沙基布将作为临别的纪念赠与房东。

===Original===
And here are our three Syrians making ready for the voyage. Shakib is intoning some verses of his while packing; Im-Hanna is cooking the last dish of *mujaddara*; and Khalid, with some vague dream in his eyes, and a vaguer, far-looming hope in his heart, is sitting on his trunk wondering at the variety of things Shakib is cramming into his. For our Scribe, we must not fail to remind the Reader, is contemplating great things of State, is nourishing a great political ambition. He will, therefore, bethink him of those in power at home. Hence these costly presents. Ay, besides the plated jewellery—the rings, bracelets, brooches, necklaces, earrings, watches, and chains—of which he is bringing enough to supply the peasants of three villages, see that beautiful gold-knobbed ebony stick, which he will present to the *vali*, and this precious gold cross with a ruby at the heart for the Patriarch, and these gold fountain pens for his literary friends, and that fine Winchester rifle for the chief of the tribe Anezah. These he packs in the bottom of his trunk, and with them his precious dilapidated copy of al-Mutanabbi, and—what MS. be this? What, a Book of Verse spawned in the cellar? Indeed, the very embryo of that printed copy we read in Cairo, and which Shakib and his friends would have us translate for the benefit of the English reading public.

===Chinese===
我们的三位叙利亚人正在为这趟远行收拾行装。沙基布一边装箱一边吟诵自己几句诗；伊姆-汉娜（Im-Hanna）正在烧最后一锅穆贾达拉（*mujaddara*，扁豆饭）；而哈立德呢，眼里含着一场模糊的梦，心头揣着一团更模糊、远远地隐现的希望，坐在自己的箱子上，惊奇于沙基布往他那口箱子里塞进去的五花八门的东西。因为我们这位抄写人——不可不提醒读者一句——正盘算着一桩有关国政的大事业，正酝酿着一个宏大的政治抱负。所以他要顾念到故国当道的那一班人。于是便有了这些贵重的礼物。是啊，除了那批镀金首饰——戒指、镯子、胸针、项链、耳环、怀表与表链——他带的多到足够配给三个村子的农人之外，且看那根金头的黑檀木手杖，他是要献给瓦利（*vali*，省督）的；再看那枚镶着红宝石的赤金十字架，是要献给宗主教的；还有那几支金笔，是送给他的文人朋友的；以及那支精良的温彻斯特来福枪，是献给阿内扎部落（Anezah）酋长的。他把这些统统压在箱底，连同他那部珍贵的、破烂不堪的穆太奈比（al-Mutanabbi）诗集——咦，这是哪一部手稿？什么，一部在地窖里生出来的诗集？不错，正是我们在开罗（Cairo）所读到的那部印本最初的胚胎；沙基布和他的朋友们当年曾力劝我们把它迻译出来，以飨英语读者。

===Original===
For our Scribe is the choragus of the Modern School of Arabic poetry. And this particular Diwan of his is a sort of rhymed inventory of all the inventions and discoveries of modern Science and all the wonders of America. He has published other Diwans, in which French morbidity is crowned with laurels from the *Arabian Nights*. For this Modern School has two opposing wings, moved by two opposing forces, Science being the motive power of the one, and Byron and De Musset the inspiring geniuses of the other. We would not be faithful to our Editorial task and to our Friend, if we did not give here a few luminant examples of the Diwan in question. We are, indeed, very sorry, for the sake of our readers, that space will not allow us to give them a few whole *qasidas* from it. To those who are so fortunate as to be able to read and understand the Original, we point out the Ode to the Phonograph, beginning thus:

“O Phonograph, thou wonder of our time,
					
					
					Thy tongue of wax can sing like me in rhyme.”

And another to the Brooklyn Bridge, of which these are the opening lines:

“O Brooklyn Bridge, how oft upon thy back
					
					
					I tramped, and once I crossed thee in a hack.”

And finally, the great Poem entitled, On the Virtue and Benefit of Modern Science, of which we remember these couplets:

“Balloons and airships, falling from the skies,
					
					
					Will be as plenty yet as summer flies.
					
					
					⋮
					
					
					“Electricity and Steam and Compressed Air
					
					
					Will carry us to heaven yet, I swear.”

Here be rhymed truth, at least, which can boast of not being poetry. Ay, in this MS. which Shakib is packing along with al-Mutanabbi in the bottom of his trunk to evade the Basilisk touch of the Port officials of Beirut, is packed all the hopes of the Modern School. Pack on, Shakib; for whether at the Mena House, or in the hashish-dens of Cairo, the Future is drinking to thee, and dreaming of thee and thy School its opium dreams. And Khalid, the while, sits impassive on his trunk, and Im-Hanna is cooking the last dinner of *mujaddara*.

===Chinese===
因为我们这位抄写人，乃是阿拉伯诗歌「现代派」的合唱长（choragus）。而他这一部「迪万」（Diwan，诗集）可说是一份押韵的清单，罗列着现代科学的一切发明与发现，以及美洲的一切奇观。他还出版过别的几部迪万，在其中，法兰西的病态被戴上《一千零一夜》（*Arabian Nights*）的桂冠。因为这现代派本有两片对立的羽翼，为两股对立之力所推动：科学是其一片的原动力，拜伦（Byron）与德·缪塞（De Musset）则是其另一片的灵感天才。我们若不在此给出这部迪万的几个明亮的样品，便既对不起我们这位编者的职分，也对不起我们的朋友。事实上，我们也颇替读者惋惜——限于篇幅，不能从其中给他们抄出几首完整的卡西达（*qasidas*，阿拉伯颂诗）。对于有幸能读能懂原文的人，我们且指出那首《咏留声机》（Ode to the Phonograph），它是这样开篇的：

『哦，留声机啊，你这我们时代之奇观，

另有一首咏布鲁克林大桥（Brooklyn Bridge），其开篇几行如下：

『哦，布鲁克林大桥，多少回我在你背上

最后，是那首题作《论现代科学之德性与利益》的大诗，我们记得其中这几组对句：

『气球与飞艇，自天而降，
　　　　将如夏日苍蝇一般多见。

　　　　⋮

　　　　「电力、蒸汽，与压缩之空气，

这里头至少有押韵的真理可言，可以自夸之处唯在不算是诗。是啊，沙基布此刻正把这部手稿——连同穆太奈比一起——压进他箱底，为的是躲过贝鲁特港口关员那「蛇怪之触」（Basilisk touch）；而现代派的全部希望，也都打包在其中了。装罢，沙基布，只管装罢；因为无论是在米娜宫（Mena House），还是在开罗的哈希什（hashish）烟寮里，「未来」都在向你举杯，都在做着它的鸦片之梦，梦见你、梦见你和你的学派。而哈立德这厢只木然坐在自己的箱子上，伊姆-汉娜在烧最后一顿穆贾达拉。

Emigration has introduced into Syria somewhat of the three prominent features of Civilisation: namely, a little wealth, a few modern ideas, and many strange diseases. And of these three blessings our two Syrians together are plentifully endowed. For Shakib is a type of the emigrant, who returns home prosperous in every sense of the word. A Book of Verse to lure Fame, a Letter of Credit to bribe her if necessary, and a double chin to praise the gods. This is a complete set of the prosperity, which Khalid knows not. But he has in his lungs what Shakib the poet can not boast of; while in his trunk he carries but a little wearing apparel, his papers, and his blankets. And in his pocket, he has his ribbed silver cigarette case—the only object he can not part with—a heart-shaped locket with a little diamond star on its face—the only present he is bringing with him home—and a third-class passage across the Atlantic. For Khalid will not sleep in a bunk, even though it be furnished with eiderdown cushions and tiger skins.

Ay, quickly please, especially for our sake and the Reader’s. Now, the dinner is finished, the rug is folded and presented to our landlord with our salaams, the trunks are locked and roped, and our Arabs will silently steal away.
