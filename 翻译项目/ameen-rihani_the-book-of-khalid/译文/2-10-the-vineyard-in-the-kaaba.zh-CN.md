## X / X

The Vineyard in the Kaaba / 克尔白中的葡萄园

===Original===
This, then, is the cave of our troglodyte! Allah be praised, even the hermits of the Lebanon mountains, like the prophets of America and other electric-age species, are subject to the laws of evolution. A cottage and chapel set in a vineyard, the most beautiful we have yet seen, looms up in this rocky wilderness like an oasis in a desert. For many miles around, the vicinage presents a volcanic aspect, wild, barren, howlingly dreary. At the foot of Mt. Sanneen in the east, beyond many ravines, are villages and verdure; and from the last terrace in the vineyard one overlooks the deep chasm which can boast of a rivulet in winter. But in the summer its nakedness is appalling. The sun turns its pocket inside out, so to speak, exposing its boulders, its little windrows of sands, and its dry ditches full of dead fish spawn. And the cold, rocky horizon, rising so high and near, shuts out the sea and hides from the Hermit the glory of the sundown. But we can behold its effects on Mt. Sanneen, on the clouds above us, on the glass casements in the villages far away. The mountains in the east are mantled with etherial lilac alternating with mauve; the clouds are touched with purple and gold; the casements in the distance are scintillating with mystical carbuncles: the sun is setting in the Mediterranean﻿—he is waving his farewell to the hills.

===Chinese===

Sanneen）脚下，越过一道道深沟，方有村落与青翠；从葡萄园最末一层梯田望去，可俯瞰一道深谷，那深谷在冬天倒还勉强有一条细流可夸。可一到夏天，它的赤裸便令人骇然。太阳，可以说，把它整个口袋翻了过来，露出它那些巨石、那一缕缕被风吹成的细沙垄，还有它那些塞满死鱼卵的干沟。而那冷峻的、岩石嶙峋的地平线升得又高又近，挡住了大海，也把日落的荣光从这位隐修士眼前遮去。但我们可以看见那荣光落在桑宁山上、落在我们头顶的云上、落在远处村落里的玻璃窗扉上的效果。东面的群山披上了空灵的淡紫与锦葵色相间的氅衣；云朵被染上紫与金；远处的窗扉则闪烁着神秘的炭火宝石般的光：太阳正在地中海（Mediterranean）上落下——正向这些山峦挥手作别。

===Original===
We reach the first gate of the Hermitage; and the odour peculiar to monks and monkeries, a mixed smell of mould and incense and burning oil, greets us as we enter into a small open space in the centre of which is a Persian lilac tree. To the right is a barbed-wire fence shutting in the vineyard; directly opposite is the door of the chapel; and near it is a wicket before which stands a withered old woman. Against the wall is a stone bench where another woman is seated. As we enter, we hear her, standing at the wicket, talking to someone behind the scene. “Yes, that is the name of my husband,” says she. “Allah have mercy on his soul,” sighs an exiguous voice within; “pray for him, pray for him.” And the woman, taking to weeping, blubbers out, “Will thirty masses do, think your Reverence?” “Yes, that will cheer his soul,” replies the oracle.

===Chinese===
我们走到隐修院的头一道门前；一进门，修士与修道院所特有的那股气味——一股霉味、乳香与燃油混在一起的气味——便迎面扑来，我们走进一小片空地，空地正中长着一棵波斯丁香。右边是一道铁丝篱笆，围着那座葡萄园；正对面是小教堂的门；门旁有一道小窗，窗前站着一个形容枯槁的老妇。墙边有一条石凳，上头坐着另一个妇人。我们一进来，便听见那个站在小窗前的妇人，正在与窗后什么人说话。「是的，那便是我丈夫的名字，」她说。「愿真主怜悯他的灵魂，」里头一个细如游丝的声音叹道，「为他祈祷罢，为他祈祷罢。」那妇人于是哭了起来，抽抽搭搭地说：「做三十台弥撒，够了吗，神父您看？」「够了，那会使他的灵魂得安慰的，」那神谕般的回答说道。

But the money collected for masses, let it here be said, as well as the income of the vineyard, the Hermit touches not. The monks are the owners of the occult establishment, and they know better than he what to do with the revenue. But how far this ancient religious Medium can go in the spirit-world, and how honest he might be in his otherworldliness, let those say who have experience in spookery and table-rapping.

===Chinese===

===Original===
Now, the women having done and gone, the wicket is open, and the serving-monk ushers us through the dark and stivy corridor to the rear, where a few boxes marked “Made in America”﻿—petroleum boxes, these﻿—are offered us as seats. Before the door of the last cell are a few potsherds in which sweet basil plants are withering from thirst. Presently, the door squeaks, and one, not drooping like the plants, comes out to greet us. This is Father Abd’ul-Messiah (Servitor of the Christ), as the Hermit is called. Here, indeed, is an up-to-date hermit, not an antique troglodyte. Lean and lathy, he is, but not hungry-looking; quick of eye and gesture; quick of step, too. He seems always on the alert, as if surrounded continually with spirits. He is young, withal, or keeps so, at least, through the grace and ministration of Allah and the Virgin. His long unkempt hair and beard are innocent of a single white line. And his health? “Through my five and twenty years of seclusion,” said he, “I have not known any disease, except, now and then, in the spring season, when the sap begins to flow, I am visited by Allah with chills and fever.﻿—No; I eat but one meal a day.﻿—Yes; I am happy, Allah be praised, quite happy, very happy.”

===Chinese===

===Original===

这便是阿卜杜勒·麦西哈静室的清单。我们想，要紧的东西并未漏掉多少。是的；在我们未曾提到的第四个角落里，放着三四个煤油罐，里头装着干粮。他从一只罐里摸出一把无花果干，又从另一只里拈出一小撮乳香，送给我们，作为他爱与祝福的记号。在结束这篇记述之前，有一件事我们很想强调。这隐修营生里跟钱有关的部分，不必苛责；因为我们须得记住，这位诚实的基督之仆，在这件事上是坚毅到不肯由着自己做主的。也请记住，修道院的开销账目是很可观的。因此，倘若修士中有一位蒙天赋、擅长独居与清修，他的师兄弟们便可从中取利。说真的，我们被告知，这隐修院的进项——亦即阿卜杜勒·麦西哈在秘术与农事两方面努力所换来的那笔金子的总和——便足以支付这修院一年的开销。除此以外，我们对这桩事再无可说。但哈立德却有话要说。从他论《孤独之用》（The Uses of Solitude）那一篇冗长的冥想之中，我们摘出如下几段：

===Original===
“Everyone’s life at certain times,” writes he, “is either a Temple, a Hermitage, or a Vineyard: everyone, in order to flee the momentary afflictions of Destiny, takes refuge either in God, or in Solitude, or in Work. And of a truth, work is the balm of the sore mind of the world. God and Solitude are luxuries which only a few among us nowadays can afford. But he who lives in the three, though his life be that of a silk larva in its cocoon, is he not individually considered a good man? Is he not a mystic, though uncreative, centre of goodness? Surely, his influence, his Me alone considered, is living and benign, and though it is not life-giving. He is a flickering taper under a bushel; and this, *billah*, were better than the pissasphaltum-souls which bushels of quackery and pretence can not hide. But alas, that a good man by nature should be so weak as to surrender himself entirely to a lot of bad men. For the monks, my brother Hermit, being a silk worm in its cocoon, will asphyxiate the larva after its work is done, and utilise the silk. Ay, after the Larva dies, they pickle and preserve it in their chapel for the benefit of those who sought its oracles in life. Let the beef-packers of America take notice; the monks of my country are in the market with ‘canned hermits!’

===Chinese===
「每个人的生命在某几个时刻，」他写道，「要么是一座神殿，要么是一所隐修院，要么是一座葡萄园：每一个人，为了逃避命运一时的苦厄，所投奔的，不是真主，便是孤独，便是劳作。而诚然，劳作乃是这世界酸痛心智的镇痛之膏。真主与孤独是奢侈品，今日我们之中只有少数人消受得起。但那活在这三者之中的人——纵使他的生命不过是茧中的一条蚕——就其自身而论，岂不是一个好人么？岂不是一个虽无创造力、却是善的神秘中心么？诚然，他的影响——单就他的那个『我』而言——是活生生而良善的，哪怕它并不能赋予生命。他是斗底下一点摇曳的微烛；而这，*billah*（凭真主起誓），已胜过那些沥青灵魂（pissasphaltum-souls）——那斗，再多的庸医与虚伪也遮盖不住的。然而可叹啊，一个天性良善的人，竟软弱到把自己整个交付给一群恶人。因为那些修士——我的隐修士兄弟啊——既是一条茧中之蚕，待它做完它的工作，便会把那蚕儿闷死，而取用它的丝。是啊，那蚕儿一死，他们便把它腌制起来，保存在他们的小教堂里，好让那些生前曾求问它神谕的人得益。让美国的牛肉罐头商们注意了罢：我们这国的修士们，也把『隐修士罐头』拿到市上卖了！

===Original===
“And this Larva, be it remembered, is not subject to decay; a saint does not decompose in the flesh like mortal sinners. One of these, I have been told, dead fifty years ago and now canonised, can be seen yet in one of the monasteries of North Lebanon, keeping well his flesh and bones together﻿—divinely embalmed. It has been truly said that the work of a good man never dies; and these leathery hermits continue in death as in life to counsel and console the Faithful.

“In the past, these Larvae, not being cultivated for the market, continued their natural course of development and issued out of their silk prisons full fledged moths. But those who cultivate them today are in sore need. They have masses and indulgences to sell; they have big bills to pay. But whether left to grow their wings or not, their solitude is that of a cocoon larva, narrow, stale, unprofitable to the world. While that of a philosopher, a Thoreau, for instance, might be called Nature’s filter; and one, issuing therefrom benefited in every sense, morally, physically, spiritually, can be said to have been filtered through Solitude.”

===Chinese===
「而且请记着，这蚕儿是不会腐坏的；一位圣徒并不像凡世的罪人那样在肉身上朽烂。我听说，其中一位，五十年前去世、如今已然封圣，至今仍可在北黎巴嫩的一座修院里见到，筋骨皮肉一一完好——是属天地经过了防腐处理的。常言道：一个善人所做的工永不朽；而这些皮革般的隐修士，在死后一如生前，仍继续为信众们出谋、慰解。

「从前，这些蚕儿并不为着市上的买卖而饲养，便顺其自然地发育下去，从它们那丝织的牢狱里钻出来，成了羽翼丰满的蛾。可今日养蚕的人却窘迫得很。他们有弥撒与赦罪券要卖；他们有大笔账单要付。但无论是否让它们长出翅膀，它们的孤独总归是茧中蚕的孤独——狭隘、陈腐、于世无益。而一位哲人——比如梭罗（Thoreau）——的孤独，倒不妨称之为大自然的过滤器；一个人从其中出来，道德、身体、灵性各方面都得了益处，便可以说：他是经过了孤独这一道过滤的。」

===Original===
“The study of life at a distance is inutile; the study of it at close range is defective. The only method left, therefore, and perhaps the true one, is that of the artist at his canvas. He works at his picture an hour or two, and retires a little to study and criticise it from a distance. It is impossible to withdraw entirely from life and pretend to take an interest in it. Either like my brother Hermit in these parts, a spiritual larva in its cocoon, or like a Thoreau, who during his period of seclusion, peeped every fortnight into the village to keep up at least his practice of human speech. Else what is the use of solitude? A life of fantasy, I muse, is nearer to the heart of Nature and Truth than a life in sackcloth and ashes.﻿ ﻿…

===Chinese===
「远远地研究人生，是徒劳的；凑近了研究它，又是有缺陷的。因此剩下的唯一法子，或许也是真正的法子，便是画家在画布前的那种法子：他在画前工作一两个钟头，便退后几步，从远处端详它、批评它。人不可能整个从人生中抽身，却又假称对它怀有兴趣。要么像我这一带的隐修士兄弟——茧中的一条灵性的蚕——要么像梭罗那样：他在隐居期间，每两周便溜进村子里窥一眼，好歹把自己说人话的功夫维持下去。否则孤独又有何用？我默想：一种幻想的生活，比披麻蒙灰的生活，更近于自然与真理之心。……」

===Original===
“And yet, deeply considered, this eremitic business presents another aspect. For does not the eremite through his art of prayer and devotion, seek an ideal? Is he not a transcendentalist, at least in the German sense of the word? Is not his philosophy above all the senses, as the term implies, and common sense included? For through Mother Church, and with closed eyes, he will attain the ideal, of which my German philosopher, through the logic-mill, and with eyes open, hardly gets a glimpse.

“The devout and poetic souls, and though they walk among the crowd, live most of their lives in solitude. Through Mother Sorrow, or Mother Fancy, or Mother Church, they are ever seeking the ideal, which to them is otherwise unattainable. And whether a howler of Turabu or a member of the French Academy, man, in this penumbra of faith and doubt, of superstition and imagination, is much the same. ‘The higher powers in us,’ says Novalis, ‘which one day, as Genii, shall fulfil our will, are for the present, Muses, which refresh us on our toilsome course with sweet remembrances.’ And the jinn, the fairies, the angels, the muses, are as young and vivacious today as they were in the Arabian and Gaelic Ages of Romance.

===Chinese===
「然而深加思量，这隐修的营生还有另一面。因为那位隐修士，借着他那祈祷与虔诚的艺术，岂不也是在追求一个理想么？他岂不是一个超验论者——至少在德文那种意义上？他的哲学岂不是凌驾于一切感官之上（一如其名所示），连常识也包括在内？因为借着母教会，闭上双眼，他将达到那理想；而我那位德国哲学家，透过逻辑的磨盘、睁着双眼，却几乎连一瞥也难得。

「那些虔敬而富于诗意的灵魂，纵使他们行在人丛之中，他们一生大部分光阴仍是活在孤独里。他们借着忧愁之母、或幻想之母、或教会之母，永远在追寻那个理想——对他们而言，舍此别无他途可达。无论是一个为图拉布（Turabu）哀号的人，还是法兰西学院（French Academy）的一员，人处在这信仰与怀疑、迷信与想象的半明半暗之中，原是差不多的。『我们心中那些更高的力量，』诺瓦利斯（Novalis）说，『有朝一日将作为精灵（Genii）来成全我们的意志，而眼下它们还是缪斯（Muses），以甘美的回忆，在我们艰辛的途程上使我们苏醒振作。』而那些精灵（Jinn）、仙女、天使、缪斯，今日仍与当年阿拉伯与盖尔人的浪漫时代一样年轻、一样活泼。

===Original===
“But whether Mother Church or Poetry or Philosophy or Music be the magic-medium, the result is much the same if the motive be not religiously sincere, sincerely religious, piously pure, lofty, and humane. Ay, my Larva-Hermit, with all his bigotry and straitness of soul, stands higher than most of your artists and poets and musicians of the present day. For a life sincerely spent between the Temple and the Vineyard, between devotion and honest labour, producing to one man of all mankind some positive good, is not to be compared with the life which oscillates continuously between egoism and vanity, quackery and cowardice, selfishness and pretence, and which never rises, do what it may, above the larva state.﻿ ﻿…

===Chinese===
「然而无论那施法的媒介是教会之母、是诗、是哲学、还是音乐，结果都差不多——倘若那动机不是宗教上的诚恳、诚恳的宗教、虔敬的纯洁、高尚而仁慈的话。是啊，我的那位蚕儿隐修士，纵使他满心的偏执与心地狭隘，却比你们今日大多数的艺术家、诗人、音乐家站得更高。因为一种真诚地消磨在神殿与葡萄园之间、消磨在虔诚与诚实的劳作之间的生命——它能给全人类中的某一个人产出某种切实的善——是不能与另一种生命相提并论的：那另一种生命，永远在自我与虚荣、庸医与怯懦、自私与虚伪之间来回摆动，无论怎样挣扎，也超不过那条蚕的阶段。……」

===Original===
“Let everyone cultivate with pious sincerity some such vineyard as my Hermit’s and the world will not further need reform. For through all the vapour and mist of his ascetic theology, through the tortuous chasm of his eremitic logic, through the bigotry and crass superstition of his soul, I can always see the Vineyard on the one side of his cell, and the Church on the other, and say to myself: Here be a man who is never idle; here be one who loves the leisure praised by Socrates, and hates the sluggishness which Iblis decks and titivates. And if he crawls between his Church and his Vineyard, and burrows in both for a solution of life, nay, spins in both the cocoon of his ideal, he ought not to be judged from on high. Come thou near him; descend; descend a little and see: has he not a task, and though it be of the taper-under-the-bushel kind? Has he not a faith and a sincerity which in a Worm of the Earth ought to be reckoned sublime? ‘If there were sorrow in heaven,’ he once said to me, ‘how many there would continuously lament the time they wasted in this world?’

“O my Brothers, build your Temples and have your Vineyards, even though it be in the rocky wilderness.”

===Chinese===
「让每一个人都以虔敬的诚意，去耕种一座像我这隐修士那样的葡萄园罢，这世界便不必再求改革了。因为透过他那苦行神学的种种蒸气与迷雾，透过他那隐修逻辑的曲折深渊，透过他灵魂里的偏执与粗陋的迷信，我总能看见：他那静室的一边是葡萄园，另一边是教堂；我便对自己说：这里有一个从不闲散的人；这里有一个喜爱苏格拉底（Socrates）所称颂的那种闲暇、而憎恶那被伊布力斯粉饰装扮的怠惰的人。倘若他在自己的教堂与葡萄园之间匍匐，并在两者之中都挖掘人生的解答，不，在两者之中都纺出他那理想的茧，那他就不该被人从高处来论断。你且走近他些；下来；下来一点儿看看：他岂不是有一项任务么，纵然那是属于斗底微烛那一类的？他岂不是有一种信仰与诚实，在一条地里的虫子身上，也该算作崇高的么？『倘若天上有忧愁，』他有一次对我说，『那里该有多少人要不住地哀叹自己在这世上虚掷的光阴呢？』

「我的弟兄们啊，去建造你们的神殿，去拥有你们的葡萄园罢，哪怕那是在岩石嶙峋的荒野之中。」
