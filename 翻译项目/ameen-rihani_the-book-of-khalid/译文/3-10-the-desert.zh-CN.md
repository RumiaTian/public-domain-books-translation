## X / X

The Desert / 沙漠

===Original===
We remember seeing once a lithographic print representing a Christmas legend of the Middle Ages, in which a detachment of the Heavenly Host﻿—big, ugly, wild-looking angels﻿—are pursuing, with sword and pike, a group of terror-stricken little devils. The idea in the picture produced such an impression that one wished to see the helpless, pitiful imps in heaven and the armed winged furies, their pursuers, in the other place. Now, as we go through the many pages of Shakib’s, in which he dilates of the mischances, the persecutions, and the flights of Khalid, and of which we have given an abstract, very brief but comprehensive, in the preceding Chapters, we are struck with the similarity in one sense between his *Dustur*-legend, so to speak, and that of the Middle Ages to which we have alluded. The devils in both pictures are distressing, pitiful; while the winged persecutors are horribly muscular, and withal atrociously armed.

Indeed, this legend of the Turkish angels of Fraternity and Equality, pursuing the Turkish little devils of reaction, so called, is most killing. But we can not see how the descendants of Yakut and Seljuk Khan, whether pursuers or pursued, whether *Dustur* winged furies they be, or Hamidian devils, are going to hold their own in face of the fell Dragon which soon or late must overtake them. That heavy, slow-going, slow-thinking Monster﻿—and it makes little difference whether he comes from the North or from the West﻿—will wait until the contending parties exhaust their strength and then﻿—but this is not our subject. We would that this pursuing business cease on all sides, and that everybody of all parties concerned pursue rather, and destroy, the big strong devil within them. Thus sayeth the preacher. And thus, for once, we, too. For does not every one of these furious angels of Equality, whether in Constantinople, in Berlin, in Paris, in London, or in New York, sit on his wings and reveal his horns when he rises to power? We are tired of wings that are really nothing but horns, misshaped and misplaced.

Look at our French-swearing, whiskey-drinking Tataric angels of the *Dustur*! Indeed, we rejoice that our poor little Devil is now beyond the reach of their dripping steel and rickety secondhand gibbets. And yet, not very far; for if the British Government consent or blink, Khalid and many real reactionists whom Cairo harbours, would have to seek an asylum elsewhere. And the third flight might not be as successful as the others. But none such is necessary. On the sands of the Libyan desert, not far from Cairo and within wind of Helwan, they pitch their tents. And Mrs. Gotfry is staying at al-Hayat, which is a stone’s throw from their evening fire. She would have Khalid live there too, but he refuses. He will live with his cousin and Shakib for a while. He is captivated, we are told, by that little cherub of a babe. But this does not prevent him from visiting his friend the Bahaist Priestess every day and dining often with her at the Hotel.

诚然，这则所谓「博爱」与「平等」的土耳其天使、追逼着所谓「反动派」的土耳其小魔鬼的传说，最是令人发噱。然而我们看不出，雅库特（Yakut）与塞尔柱汗（Seljuk Khan）的子孙——无论其为追赶者抑或被赶者，无论其为杜斯图尔的生翼怒灵，抑或哈米德式的魔鬼——在那条迟早必将追上他们的凶龙面前，将如何自存。那头笨重、行动迟缓、心思迟钝的巨怪——而它从北方来、抑或从西方来，并无多大分别——会一直候着，等到相争的双方力竭之时，然后……但这并非我们的题目。我们但愿这追赶的营生能从四面八方一并止息，而所有党派的所有人，宁可掉头去追赶、去扑灭各自心中那头又大又强的魔鬼。传道者如是说。而这一回，我们亦如是说。因为那些「平等」的怒焰天使——无论在君士坦丁堡、在柏林、在巴黎、在伦敦、抑或在纽约——岂不一朝大权在握，便都坐到自己的双翼之上、露出本来的犄角么？我们早已厌倦了那些不过是犄角、却畸形而错位的翅膀了。

且看我们那些满口法文脏话、豪饮威士忌的杜斯图尔的鞑靼式天使罢！诚然，我们庆幸我们那可怜的小魔鬼如今已逃出他们那淋漓的刀锋与那些摇摇欲坠的二手绞架所能及之处。然而也并不很远；因为一旦英国政府首肯、抑或睁一只眼闭一只眼，哈立德以及开罗所庇护的许多真正的反动派，便不得不到别处去另觅避难之所。而这第三度的逃亡，未必能像前几回那般顺遂。但这样的逃亡已无必要。在利比亚沙漠的沙地上，离开罗不远、在赫尔万（Helwan）的风声可闻之处，他们扎下了营。而戈特弗里夫人（Mrs. Gotfry）正下榻于海亚特（al-Hayat）——那儿离他们夜炊之火不过一箭之遥。她本要哈立德也住到那里去，可他不肯。他要与他的表妹以及沙基布同住一阵子。据我们所知，他已被那个小天使般的婴孩迷住了心窍。但这并不妨碍他每日去探访他的朋友那位巴哈伊女祭司（Bahaist Priestess），并常常与她在旅邸共进晚餐。

She, too, not infrequently comes to the camp. Indeed, finding the solitude agreeable she has a tent pitched near theirs. And as a relief from the noise and bustle of tourists and the fatiguing formalities of Hotel life, she repairs thither for a few days every week.

Now, in this austere delicacy of the desert, where allwhere is the softness of pure sand, Khalid is perfectly happy. Never did he seem so careless, our Scribe asserts, and so jovial and childlike in his joys. Far from the noise and strife of politics, far from the bewildering tangle of thought, far from the vain hopes and dreams and ambitions of life, he lives each day as if it were the last of the world. Here are joys manifold for a weary and persecuted spirit: the joy of having your dearest friend and comrade with you; the joy of nursing and helping to restore to health and happiness the woman dearest to your heart; the joy of a Love budding in beauty and profusion; and﻿—this, the rarest and sublimest for Khalid﻿—the joy of worshipping at the cradle﻿—of fondling, caressing, and bringing up one of the brightest, sweetest, loveliest of babes.

Najib is his name﻿—it were cruel to neutralise such a prodigy﻿—and he is just learning to walk and lisp. Khalid teaches him the first step and the first monosyllable, receiving in return the first kiss which his infant lips could voice. With what joy Najib makes his first ten steps! With what zest would he practise on the soft sands, laughing as he falls, and rising to try again. And thus, does he quickly, wonderfully develop, unfolding in the little circle of his caressers﻿—in his mother’s lap, in Shakib’s arms, on Khalid’s back, on Mrs. Gotfry’s knee﻿—the irresistible charm of his precocious spirit.

她，也不时来到营中。诚然，她发觉这清寂之地颇惬心意，便在他们营帐近旁另支了一顶帐篷。而作为对游客的喧嚣扰攘、旅邸生活那令人疲惫的繁文缛节的一种调剂，她每周总要到那里去消磨几日。

如今，在这沙漠那素朴而精微的意境之中——四下里无处不是那纯净柔沙的绵软——哈立德可谓幸福之至。我们的抄写人断言，他从未见过哈立德如此无忧无虑，如此在其乐事之中那般欢快而天真。远离政治的喧嚣与纷争，远离思想的纷乱纠葛，远离人生那虚妄的希望、梦想与野心，他把每一个日子都过得如同这世界的末日一般。此处有一个疲倦而受逼迫的灵魂所能享的种种欢乐：有你最亲爱的朋友与同志相伴之乐；有看护、并助你心头最珍视的女子重获健康与幸福之乐；有一段在美与丰盛之中萌发的爱情之乐；以及——这对哈立德而言最稀有、最崇高的——在摇篮前膜拜之乐——即抚弄、怜惜、并抚养一个最明慧、最甜美、最可爱的小婴孩之乐。

纳吉布（Najib）是他的名字——把这样一个神童加以「中性化」未免太残忍——他刚刚在学步、学语。哈立德教他迈出第一步、吐出第一个单音字，而所得的回报，便是那双婴唇所能发出的第一个吻。纳吉布迈出他最初的十步时，是带着何等的欢欣！他在柔软的沙地上练习得何等起劲——摔倒了便笑，爬起来再试。就这样，他发育得又快又奇妙，在那小小一圈怜爱他的人中间——在母亲的膝上、在沙基布的怀里、在哈立德的背上、在戈特弗里夫人的膝头——渐渐展露出他那早慧之灵那不可抗拒的魅力。

In two months of desert life, Najib could run on the sands and sit down when tired to rest; in two months he could imitate in voice and gesture whatever he heard or saw: the donkey’s bray, and with a tilt of the head like him; the cry of the cock; the shrill whistle of the train; and the howling of donkey boys. His keen sense of discrimination in sounds is incredible. And one day, seeing a Mohammedan spreading his rug to pray, he begins to kneel and kiss the ground in imitation of him. He even went into the tent and brought Khalid’s jubbah to spread it on the sand likewise for that purpose. So sensitive to outside impressions is this child that he quickly responds to the least suggestion and with the least effort. Early in the morning, when the chill of night is still on the sands, he toddles into Khalid’s tent cooing and warbling his joy. A walking jasmine flower, a singing ray of sunshine, Khalid calls him. And the mother, on seeing her child thus develop, begins to recuperate. In this little garden of happiness, her hope begins to blossom.

But Khalid would like to know why Najib, on coming into his tent in the morning and seeing him naked, always pointed with his little finger and with questioning smile, to what protruded under the navel. The like questions Khalid puts with the ease and freedom of a child. And writes full pages about them, too, in which he only succeeds in bamboozling himself and us. For how can we account for everything a child does? Even the psychologist with his reflex-action theory does not solve the whole problem. But Khalid would like to know﻿—and perhaps not so innocently does he dwell upon this subject as upon others﻿—he would like to know the significance of Najib’s pointed finger and smile. It may be only an accident, Khalid. “But an accident,” says he, “occurring again and again in the same manner under stated conditions ceases to be such.” And might not the child, who is such an early and keen observer, have previously seen his mother in native buff, and was surprised to see that appendage in you, Khalid?

Even at al-Hayat Najib is become popular. Khalid often comes here carrying him on his back. And how ready is the child to salaam everybody, and with both hands, as he stands on the veranda steps. “Surely,” says Khalid, “there is a deeper understanding between man and child than between man and man. For who but a child dare act so freely among these polyglots of ceremony in this little world of frills and frocks and feathers? Who but a child dare approach without an introduction any one of these solemn-looking tourists? Here then is the divine source of the sweetest and purest joy. Here is that one touch of Nature which makes the whole world kin. For the child, and though he be of the lowest desert tribe, standing on the veranda of a fashionable Hotel, can warm and sweeten with the divine flame that is in him, the hearts of these sour-seeming, stiff-looking tourists who are from all corners of the earth. Is not this a miracle? My professor of psychology will say, ‘Nay.’ But what makes the heart leap in that grave and portly gentleman, who might be from Finland or Iceland, for all I know, when Najib’s hand is raised to him in salutation? What makes that stately and sombre-looking dame open her arms, when Najib plucks a flower and, after smelling it, presents it to her? What makes that reticent, meditative, hard-favoured ancient, who is I believe a psychologist, what makes him so interested in observing Najib when he stands near the piano pointing anxiously to the keyboard? For the child enjoys not every kind of music: play a march or a melody and he will keep time, listing joyously from side to side and waving his hand in an arch like a maestro; play something insipid or chaotic and he will stand there impassive as a statue.”

在沙漠生活了两个月，纳吉布便能在沙上奔跑，疲倦时还会自己坐下歇息；两个月里，他已能以声以态模仿他所闻所见的一切：驴子的嘶鸣——并且像驴那样把头一歪；雄鸡的啼叫；火车的尖厉汽笛声；以及赶驴孩子们的吆喝。他对声音的敏锐辨别力，简直令人难以置信。一日，他见一位穆斯林（Mohammedan）铺开跪毯要礼拜，便也学着他的样，跪下来亲吻土地。他甚至走进帐篷，把哈立德的那件阿拉伯长袍（jubbah）拖出来，也同样铺在沙地上，作此用途。这孩子对外来的印象如此敏感，以至于他只需极微的提示、极少的力气，便能迅速作出回应。清晨，当夜的寒意犹留在沙上之时，他便蹒跚地走进哈立德的帐篷，咕咕哝哝、嘤嘤呀呀地唱出他的欢喜。一朵会走的茉莉花，一缕会歌唱的阳光——哈立德这样唤他。而那位母亲，看着自己的孩子这般成长，也开始恢复了元气。在这一小片幸福的园地里，她的希望开始绽放。

但是哈立德却想知道，何以纳吉布清早走进他的帐篷、看见他赤身裸体之时，总要用那根小指头、带着疑问的微笑，指着肚脐底下那个突出的东西。这一类问题，哈立德总是带着孩童般的从容与无拘无束地提出来。他还就这些问题写下整整几页，结果不过是把他自己、也把我们绕得云里雾里。因为孩子的一举一动，我们又怎能事事都给出一个交代？便是那位心理学家，凭他那套「反射动作」之说，也不能把整个问题解答清楚。但哈立德偏要想个明白——而且他在这个题目上所盘桓的，也许并不像在别的题目上那样天真——他想知道纳吉布那一指、那一笑，究竟意味着什么。那也许不过是一次偶然罢，哈立德。「可是，」他说，「一桩偶然之事，倘若在既定条件下再三再四地以同样方式发生，它便不再是偶然了。」再者，这孩子既是个如此早慧而敏锐的观察者，难道他先前不曾见过他母亲的一丝不挂之态，因而才惊讶地发觉你哈立德身上竟长着那样一件附属物么？

便是在海亚特，纳吉布也成了众人所宠爱的人物。哈立德常常背着他来到这里。而这孩子站在游廊的台阶上，是何等热切地向每一个人行色兰（salaam）之礼——并且是双手齐上。「诚然，」哈立德说，「人与孩童之间的理解，比人与人之间的更深。因为在这满是褶边、裙裾与羽毛的小小世界里，在这群操着种种语言的礼数之客中间，除了一个孩童，谁还敢如此无拘无束地行动？除了一个孩童，谁还敢不经人引见，便径直走近这些神色肃穆的游客中的任何一位？故而此处便有着那最甜美、最纯净之乐的神圣源头。此处便有那「一点天性之触」，使得整个世界皆为一家。因为这孩子——哪怕他出身于沙漠中最低微的部族——站在一座时髦旅邸的游廊之上，也能以他心中那一点神圣的火焰，去温暖、去甘甜化这些看似冷峻、神色僵硬、来自天涯海角的游客们的心。这岂不是一个奇迹？我那位心理学教授定会说：『否。』然而，当纳吉布举手向他致意之时，是什么使那位——据我所知也许来自芬兰或冰岛——那位庄严而魁梧的先生心头一跃？是什么使那位雍容而面色沉郁的夫人，在纳吉布摘下一朵花、闻过之后再献给她之时，张开了双臂？又是什么使那位寡言、好思、面貌粗陋的老者——我相信他是一位心理学家——是什么使他如此兴致勃勃地观察着纳吉布，当那孩子站在钢琴边、焦急地指着琴键之时？因为这孩子并非什么音乐都喜欢：奏一支进行曲或一段旋律，他便合着拍子，欢快地左右摇摆，把手在空中划出一道拱形，活像一位指挥家（maestro）；奏一段乏味或混乱的东西，他便如一尊雕像那般木然站在那里。」

And “the reticent hard-favoured ancient,” who turns out to be an American professor of some ology, explains to Khalid why lively music moves children, while soft and subtle tones do not. But Khalid is not open to argument on the subject. He prefers to believe that children, especially when so keenly sensitive as his prodigy, understand as much, if not more, about music as the average operagoer of today. But that is not saying much. The professor furthermore, while admitting the extreme precocity of Najib’s mind, tries to simplify by scientific analysis what to Khalid and other laymen seemed wonderful, almost miraculous. Here, too, Khalid botches the arguments of the learned gentleman in his effort to give us a summary of them, and tells us in the end that never after, so long as that professor was there, did he ever visit al-Hayat.

He prefers to frolic and philosophise with his prodigy on the sands. He goes on all four around the tent, carrying Najib on his back; he digs a little ditch in the sand and teaches him how to lie therein. Following the precept of the Greek philosophers, he would show him even so early how to die. And Najib lies in the sand-grave, folds his hands on his breast and closes his eyes. Rising therefrom, Khalid would teach him how to dance like a dervish, and Najib whirls and whirls until he falls again in that grave.

When Mrs. Gotfry came that day, Khalid asked the child to show her how to dance and die, and Najib begins to whirl like a dervish until he falls in the grave; thereupon he folds his arms, closes his eyes, and smiles a pathetic smile. This by far is the masterpiece of all his feats. And one evening, when he was repeating this strange and weird antic, which in Khalid’s strange mind might be made to symbolise something stranger than both, he saw, as he lay in the grave, a star in the sky. It was the first time he saw a star; and he jumped out of his sand-grave exulting in the discovery he had made. He runs to his mother and points the star to her.﻿ ﻿…

And thus did Khalid spend his halcyon months in the desert. Here was an arcadia, perfect but brief. For his delight in infant worship, and in the new Love which was budding in beauty and profusion, and in tending his sick cousin who was recovering her health, and in the walks around the ruins in the desert with his dearest comrade and friend﻿—these, alas, were joys of too pure a nature to endure.

他宁可与他那位神童在沙地上嬉戏、玄想。他绕着帐篷四肢着地地爬，背上驮着纳吉布；他在沙里掘出一条小沟，教他如何躺在其中。循着希腊哲学家的遗训，他要趁这么早便教他如何去死。于是纳吉布便躺在那沙坟之中，双手交叠于胸前，闭上双眼。从坟中起来之后，哈立德又教他如何像一位德尔维希（dervish）那样起舞，纳吉布便一旋再旋，直旋到再度跌进那座坟里。

那日戈特弗里夫人到来之时，哈立德便请那孩子向她表演如何起舞、如何去死；纳吉布便如德尔维希一般旋转起来，直旋到跌进坟里；随即他交起双臂、阖上双眼，露出一个凄婉的微笑。这一手，远胜他一切绝技，可谓登峰造极。某晚，当他又在重复这古怪而诡异的把戏——这在哈立德那奇特的心思中，未尝不可象征比两者都更奇异的什么——他躺在坟里之时，望见了天上的一颗星。这是他头一回看见一颗星；他便从那沙坟里一跃而起，为自己这一发现而欢跃不已。他奔向母亲，把那颗星指给她看。……

哈立德便这样在沙漠里度过了他那宁馨的数月。此处曾是一片阿卡狄亚（Arcadia）——完美然而短暂。因为他在婴孩膜拜之中所得的欢欣，在那段于美与丰盛之中萌发的新爱情里所得的欢欣，在看护他那位正康复的病表妹时所得的欢欣，在与他最亲爱的同志兼友伴一同漫步于沙漠废墟之间时所得的欢欣——唉，这些都是太纯粹了的欢乐，难以长久。
