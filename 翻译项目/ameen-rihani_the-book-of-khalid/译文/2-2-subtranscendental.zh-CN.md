## II / II

Subtranscendental / 亚超验

===Original===
Deficiencies in individuals, as in States, have their value and import. Indeed, that sublime impulse of perfectibility, always vivacious, always working under various forms and with one underlying purpose, would be futile without them, and fatuous. And what were life without this incessant striving of the spirit? What were life without its angles of difficulty and defeat, and its apices of triumph and power? A banality this, you will say. But need we not be reminded of these wholesome truths, when the striving after originality nowadays is productive of so much quackery? The impulse of perfectibility, we repeat, whether at work in a Studio, or in a Factory, or in a Prison Cell, is the most noble of all human impulses, the most divine.

Of that Chapter, In Prison, we have given what might be called the exogenous bark of the Soul, or that which environment creates. And now we shall endeavour to show the reader somewhat of the ludigenous process, by which the Soul, thrumming its own strings or eating its own guts, develops and increases its numbers. For Khalid in these gaol-days is much like Hamlet’s player, or even like Hamlet himself—always soliloquising, tearing a passion to rags. And what mean these outbursts and objurgations of his, you will ask; these suggestions, fugitive, rhapsodical, mystical; this furibund allegro about Money, Mediums, and Bohemia; these sobs and tears and asseverations, in which our Lady of the Studio and Shakib are both expunged with great *billahs*;—the force and significance of these subliminal uprushes, dear Reader, we confess we are, like yourself, unable to understand, without the aid of our Interpreter. We shall, therefore, let him speak.

个人如国家，其缺陷亦自有其价值与意义。诚然，那种臻于完美的崇高冲动——它永远生气勃勃，永远以千般面目运作、而怀着一贯的宗旨——倘若没有这些缺陷，便会徒劳无功，甚至流于愚妄。而生命倘若没有这种精神上无休无止的奋争，又将若何？倘若没有它那些艰难与挫败的棱角，那些胜利与权势的尖峰，又将若何？你必定要说，这是老生常谈。然而当今之世，世人追逐独创性竟产出如许江湖骗术，我们难道不需要有人来提醒这些有益的真理么？我们再说一遍：那臻于完美的冲动，无论它运作于画室、工厂，还是囚室之中，都是人类一切冲动里最崇高、最神圣的一种。

关于《在狱中》那一章，我们所给的，不妨说是灵魂的外生（exogenous）之皮壳——亦即环境所造就的那一层。如今我们将勉力向读者略略展示那内衍（ludigenous）的历程：灵魂正是借此，拨弄自身的琴弦、抑或啃噬自身的脏腑，从而发育滋长、繁衍其数。因为哈立德在狱中的这些日子，颇像哈姆雷特（Hamlet）剧中的伶人，甚至像哈姆雷特本人——总在独白，总把一腔激情撕成碎片。你要问，他这些迸发与咒詈意味着什么？这些忽隐忽现、狂想式的、神秘的暗示？这些围绕着金钱、灵媒（Mediums）与波希米亚（Bohemia）的狂暴快板？这些抽泣、眼泪与激切之誓——其中我们画室的夫人与沙基布皆被几声重重的「凭真主起誓（billah）」一笔勾销？——至于这些潜意识涌升之力与义，亲爱的读者，我们承认，与你一样，若无我们的诠释者相助，我们也茫然不解。因此，我们且让他自己来说。

“When in prison,” writes Shakib, “Khalid was subject to spasms and strange hallucinations. One day, when I was sweating in the effort to get him out of gaol, he sends me word to come and see him. I go; and after waiting a while at the Iron gate, I behold Khalid rushing down the isle like an angry lion. ‘What do you want,’ he growled, ‘why are you here?’ And I, amazed, ‘Did you not send for me?’ And he snapped up, ‘I did; but you should not have come. You should withhold from me your favours.’ Life of Allah, I was stunned. I feared lest his mind, too, had gone in the direction of his health, which was already sorrily undermined. I looked at him with dim, tearful eyes, and assured him that soon he shall be free. ‘And what is the use of freedom,’ he exclaimed, ‘when it drags us to lower and darker depths? Don’t think I am miserable in prison. No; I am not—I am happy. I have had strange visions, marvellous. O my Brother, if you could behold the sloughs, deeper and darker than any prison-cell, into which *you* have thrown me. Yes, *you*—and another. O, I hate you both. I hate my best lovers. I hate You—no—no, no, no.’ And he falls on me, embraces me, and bathes my cheeks with his tears. After which he falters out beseechingly, ‘Promise, promise that you will not give me any more money, and though starving and in rags you find me crouching at your door, promise.’ And of a truth, I acquiesced in all he said, seeing how shaken in body and mind he was. But not until I had made a promise under oath would he be tranquillised. And so, after our farewell embrace, he asked me to come again the following day and bring him some books to read. This I did, fetching with me Rousseau’s *Emile* and Carlyle’s *Hero-Worship*, the only two books he had in the cellar. And when he saw them, he exclaimed with joy, ‘The very books I want! I read them twice already, and I shall read them again. O, let me kiss you for the thought.’ And in an ecstasy he overwhelms me again with suffusing sobs and embraces.

“What a difference, I thought, between Khalid of yesterday and Khalid of today. What a transformation! Even I who know the turn and temper of his nature had much this time to fear. Surely, an alienist would have made a case of him. But I began to get an inkling into his cue of passion, when he told me that he was going to start a little business again, if I lend him the necessary capital. But I reminded him that we shall soon be returning home. ‘No, not I,’ he swore; ‘not until I can pay my own passage, at least. I told you yesterday I’ll accept no more money from you, except, of course, the sum I need to start the little business I am contemplating.’ ‘And suppose you lose this money,’ I asked.—‘Why, then *you* lose *me*. But no, you shall not. For I know, I believe, I am sure, I swear that my scheme this time will not be a failure in any sense of the word. I have heavenly testimony on that.’—‘And what was the matter with you yesterday? Why were you so queer?’ ‘O, I had nightmares and visions the night before, and you came too early in the morning. See this.’ And he holds down his head to show me the back of his neck. ‘Is there no swelling here? I feel it. Oh, it pains me yet. But I shall tell you about it and about the vision when I am out.’—And at this, the gaoler comes to inform us that Khalid’s minutes are spent and he must return to his cell.”

「『哈立德在狱中时，』沙基布写道，『动辄发作痉挛，生种种奇异的幻觉。某日，我正为着把他弄出监狱而到处奔走、汗流浃背，他却捎话叫我去见他。我便去了；在铁门外候了一阵，便见哈立德如一头怒狮般沿着过道冲将下来。『你来做什么，』他咆哮，『你为何在此？』我大惊，答道：『不是你叫人传话叫我来么？』他厉声道：『是我叫的；可你本不该来。你本该把你的恩惠从我身上收回。』凭真主的生命起誓，我怔住了。我生怕他的心智也已朝着他身体的那个方向垮了下去——他的身体早已可悲地衰败。我以朦胧含泪的眼睛望着他，向他保证他很快便会重获自由。『自由又有何用，』他叫道，『倘若它把我们拖到更低、更暗的深渊？别以为我在狱中受苦。不；我并非如此——我是快乐的。我见过种种异象，奇妙无比。啊，我的兄弟，你若能瞧一瞧你把我投入的那些泥淖——它们比任何囚室都更深、更暗！是你——是的，是你——还有另一个人。啊，我恨你们两个。我恨最爱我的人。我恨你——不——不，不，不。』说着他扑到我身上，搂住我，用他的泪水沐浴我的双颊。随后他颤声恳求道：『答应我，答应我你不再给我钱；哪怕你见我饿到将死、衣衫褴褛地蜷在你门前，也要答应我。』说真的，见他身心震骇至此，他所说的一切我无不依从。可非等我发了誓、立了诺，他总不肯安宁。于是，作别拥抱之后，他请我次日再来，并带几本书给他解闷。我照办了，给他捎去卢梭（Rousseau）的《爱弥儿》（Emile）与卡莱尔（Carlyle）的《英雄崇拜》（Hero-Worship）——这两本也是他在地窖里仅有之书。他一见便欣喜叫道：『正合我心意！它们我已读过两遍，还要再读。啊，为了这份心思，让我亲你一下。』说罢一阵狂喜，又以盈眶的抽泣与拥抱将我淹没。』」

「『昨日之哈立德与今日之哈立德，差别何其之大，我心想。何等的一场蜕变！便是我这般深知他性情的转折与脾气的人，这一回也着实忧惧。诚然，换作精神病学家，必得拿他立一个病症。可当他告诉我，他打算再做一点小生意——只要我借他那必要的本钱——我便开始隐约窥见他那一腔激情的线索了。但我提醒他，我们不久便要返乡了。『不，我不返，』他起誓；『至少要等我自己付得起船资再说。我昨日告诉过你，你的钱我一分也不再收，当然，我做这桩小本生意所需的那笔款子除外。』『万一你蚀了本呢？』我问。——『哎呀，那你就失去我了。但不，你不会的。因为我知道，我相信，我确信，我起誓——我这一回的谋划，无论从何种意义上讲都不会失败。此事我有天上的见证。』——『那你昨日是怎么了？为何那般古怪？』『啊，前一夜我做了噩梦、见了异象，而你一大早就来了。你瞧这个。』说着他低下头，让我看他后颈。『这里没肿么？我摸得着。唉，至今还疼。但等我出去之后，再细细告诉你——连同那个异象。』——正说着，狱卒过来告诉我们：哈立德的时刻已尽，他必须回到自己的囚室了。』」

===Original===
All of which from our Interpreter is as clear as God Save the King. And from which we hope our Reader will infer that those outbursts and tears and rhapsodies of Khalid did mean somewhat. They did mean, even when we first approached his cell, that something was going on in him—a revolution, a coup d’état, so to speak, of the spirit. For a Prince in Rags, but not in Debts and Dishonour, will throttle the Harpy which has hitherto ruled and degraded his soul.

But the dwelling, too, of that soul is sorely undermined. And so, his leal and loving friend Shakib takes him later to the best physician in the City, who after the tapping and auscultation, shakes his head, writes his prescriptions, and advises Khalid to keep in the open air as much as possible, or better still, to return to his native country. The last portion of the advice, however, Khalid can not follow at present. For he will either return home on his own account or die in New York. “If I can not in time save enough money for the Steamship Company,” he said to Shakib, “I can at least leave enough to settle the undertaker’s bill. And in either case, I shall have paid my own passage out of this New World. And I shall stand before my Maker in a shroud, at least, which I can call my own.”

我们的诠释者所说这一切，清楚得如同《天佑国王》（God Save the King）。我们也希望读者由此推知，哈立德那些迸发、那些眼泪、那些狂想，确乎有所指。它们所指的——甚至当我们初近他的囚室之时——是他内心正有所进行：一场革命，一场精神上的政变（coup d'état），可以这样说。因为一位衣衫褴褛——却不负债、不失节——的王子，终将扼死那只至今统治并辱没他灵魂的鹰身女妖（Harpy）。

然而，那灵魂的居所也已可悲地颓败。于是，他那忠诚而多情的朋友沙基布后来便带他去见城中最好的医师。医师一番叩诊听诊之后，摇了摇头，开了方子，劝哈立德尽量待在户外，更好的则是回到他的祖国去。然而这后半段劝告，哈立德此时却无法听从。因为：要么他靠自己的盘缠返乡，要么便死在纽约。『万一我不能及时攒够给轮船公司的船资，』他对沙基布说，『至少我能留下足够的钱去结清殡葬人的账。无论哪一种情形，我都已自付船资，离开了这个新世界。而我立在我的造物主（Maker）面前时，至少能裹着一件我称得起我自己的寿衣。』

===Original===
To which Shakib replies by going to the druggist with the prescriptions. And when he returns to the cellar with a package of four or five medicine bottles for rubbing and smelling and drinking, he finds Khalid sitting near the stove—we are now in the last month of Winter—warming his hands on the flames of the two last books he read. *Emile* and *Hero-Worship* go the way of all the rest. And there he sits, meditating over Carlyle’s crepitating fire and Rousseau’s writhing, sibilating flame. And it may be he thought of neither. Perhaps he was brooding over the resolution he had made, and the ominous shaking of the doctor’s head. Ah, but his tutelar deities are better physicians, he thought. And having made his choice, he will pitch the medicine bottles into the street, and only follow the doctor’s advice by keeping in the open air.

Behold him, therefore, with a note in hand, applying to Shakib, in a formal and businesslike manner, for a loan; and see that noble benefactor and friend, after gladly giving the money, throw the note into the fire. And now, Khalid is neither dervish nor philosopher, but a man of business with a capital of twenty-five dollars in his pocket. And with one-fifth of this capital he buys a secondhand pushcart from his Greek neighbour, wends his way with it to the marketplace, makes a purchase there of a few boxes of oranges, sorts them in his cart into three classes—“there is no equality in nature,” he says, while doing this—sticks a price card at the head of each class, and starts, in the name of Allah, his business. That is how he will keep in the open air twelve hours a day.

对此，沙基布的回答是拿着方子去了药铺。待他带着一包四五瓶外擦、嗅吸、内服的药水回到地窖，却见哈立德坐在炉子旁——此时已是冬季最后一个月——正用他读过的最后两本书的火焰烘手。《爱弥儿》与《英雄崇拜》亦步了其余一切的后尘。他便坐在那里，对着卡莱尔那毕毕剥剥的火、卢梭那扭曲嘶啸的焰，冥然凝思。也许他两者都未想及。也许他正琢磨着自己所下的决心，与医师那不祥的摇头。啊，但他的守护神祇是更好的医者，他想。既已作出抉择，他便把那几只药瓶尽数掷到街上，只遵从医师一项劝告：待在户外。

那么且看他罢：手执一张借据，一本正经、公事公办地向沙基布求贷；再看那位高尚的恩主与朋友，慷慨解囊之后，把那张借据掷入炉火。至此，哈立德既非德尔维希（dervish），亦非哲士，而是一个口袋里揣着二十五块本钱的生意人。他以这本钱的五分之一，向他的希腊邻居买了一辆二手手推车，推着它走向集市，在那里购入几箱橘子，把它们在车里分成三等——『天地间原无平等，』他一边分一边说——在每一等前面插上标价的纸牌，便奉真主（Allah）之名，开了张。这便是他如何一天在户外待上十二个钟头的法子。

But in the district where he is known he does not long remain. The sympathy of his compatriots is to him worse than the doctor’s medicines, and those who had often heard him speechifying exchanged significant looks when he passed. Moreover, the police would not let him set up his stand anywhere. “There comes the pushcart orator,” they would say to each other; and before our poor Syrian stops to breathe, one of them grumpishly cries out, “Move on there! Move on!” Once Khalid ventures to ask, “But why are others allowed to set up their stands here?” And the “copper” (we beg the Critic’s pardon again) coming forward twirling his club, lays his hand on Khalid’s shoulder and calmly this: “Don’t you think I know you? Move on, I say.” O Khalid, have you forgotten that these “coppers” are the minions of Tammany? Why tarry, therefore, and ask questions? Yes, make a big move at once—out of the district entirely.

Now, to the East Side, into the Jewish Quarter, Khalid directs his cart. And there, he falls in with Jewish fellow pushcart peddlers and puts up with them in a cellar similar to his in the Syrian Quarter. But only for a month could he suffer what the Jew has suffered for centuries. Why? There is this difference between the cellar of the Semite Syrian and that of the Semite Jew: in the first we eat *mujaddara*, in the second, kosher but stinking flesh; in the first we read poetry and play the lute, in the second we fight about the rent and the division of the profits of the day; in the first we sleep in linen “as white as the wings of the dove,” in the second on pieces of smelly blankets; the first is redolent of attar of roses, Shakib’s favourite perfume, the second is especially made insufferable by that stench which is peculiar to every Hebrew hive. For these and other reasons, Khalid separates himself from his Semite fellow peddlers, and makes this time a bigger move than the first.

但在他为人所知的那个区里，他待不长。同胞们的同情，对他而言比医师的药还难熬；往日常听他演说的人们，在他经过时彼此交换着意味深长的眼色。况且，警察也不准他在任何地方摆摊。他们会彼此说道：『那个手推车演说家来了。』于是，不等我们可怜的叙利亚人停下喘口气，其中之一便恶声吆喝：『往前走！往前走！』哈立德有一回壮胆问：『可为何别人可以在这里摆摊？』那「铜徽」（copper，我们再次请批评家见谅）上前旋着警棍，一手按在哈立德肩上，从容说道：『你以为我不认得你？往前走，我说。』啊，哈立德，你莫非忘了这些「铜徽」是坦慕尼（Tammany）的爪牙？那你又何必逗留、又何必发问？是的，赶紧来一个大挪步——完全挪出这个区。

于是，哈立德把他的车推向东区，推入犹太区（Jewish Quarter）。在那里，他与犹太籍的手推车同行们搭上了伙，与他们一同挤在一间与他在叙利亚区（Syrian Quarter）的地窖相仿的地窖里。可犹太人受了几百年的苦，他连一个月也忍不下来。缘故何在？闪族叙利亚人的地窖与闪族犹太人的地窖，有此一别：在第一类地窖里我们吃穆贾达拉（mujaddara），在第二类里吃的是合于教规（kosher）却发臭的肉；在第一类里我们读诗、弹鲁特琴，在第二类里我们为房租、为当日盈利的分割而争吵；在第一类里我们睡在『白如鸽翼』的亚麻布上，在第二类里睡在一片片发臭的毯子碎块上；第一类地窖洋溢着沙基布心爱的玫瑰精油（attar of roses）之香，第二类则因那种每一个希伯来人聚居处所特有的恶臭，而尤其叫人难以忍受。因这些以及别样缘故，哈立德脱离了他的闪族同行，这一次挪了一个比头一回更大的步子。

Ay, even to the Bronx, where often in former days, shouldering the peddling-box, he tramped, will he now push his orange-cart and his hopes. There, between City and Country, nearer to Nature, and not far from the traffic of life, he fares better both in health and purse. It is much to his liking, this upper end of the City. Here the atmosphere is more peaceful and soothing, and the police are more agreeable. No, they do not nickname and bully him in the Bronx. And never was he ordered to move on, even though he set up his stand for months at the same corner. “Ah, how much kinder and more humane people become,” he says, “even when they are not altogether out of the City, but only on the outskirts of the country expanse.”

Khalid passes the Spring and Summer in the Bronx and keeps in the open air, not only in the day, but also in the night. How he does this, is told in a letter which he writes to Shakib. But does he sleep at all, you ask, and how, and where? Reader, we thank you for your anxiety about Khalid’s health. And we would fain show you the Magic Carpet which he carries in the lockbox of his pushcart. But see for yourself, here be neither Magic Carpet, nor Magic Ring. Only his papers, a few towels, a blanket, some underwear, and his coffee utensils, are here. For Khalid could forego his *mujaddara*, but never his coffee, the Arab that he is. But an Arab on the wayfare, if he finds himself at night far from the camp, will dig him a ditch in the sands and lie there to sleep under the living stars. Khalid could not do thus, neither in the City nor out of it. And yet, he did not lodge within doors. He hired a place only for his pushcart; and this, a small padlock-booth where he deposits his stock in trade. But how he lived in the Bronx is described in the following letter:

===Chinese===
是啊，哪怕到了布朗克斯（the Bronx）——往日他常扛着货郎箱在那儿长途跋涉——他如今也要推着他的橘子车、推着他的希望前去。在那里，在城市与乡野之间，更近自然，又离人间的往来不远，他的身体与钱袋都过得更好些。这座城市的这一上端，甚合他的意。这里的空气更为宁静、更为宜人，警察也较为和气。不，他们在布朗克斯并不给他起绰号、并不欺侮他。即便他在同一个街角一连摆了几个月的摊，他也从未被勒令『往前走』。『啊，人是变得多么更仁慈、更人道，』他说，『哪怕他们并非完全出了城，只不过是在辽阔乡野的边上。』

哈立德在布朗克斯度过了春夏两季，且始终待在户外——不但白日里，夜里也是。他怎样做到的，记在他写给沙基布的一封信里。可他到底睡不睡觉呢，你要问，又怎么睡，睡在哪里？读者啊，我们感谢你对哈立德健康的关切。我们也乐意把他在手推车锁箱里随身带着的那张魔毯（Magic Carpet）指给你看。可你自己瞧罢，这里既无魔毯，也无魔戒（Magic Ring）。只有他的文件、几条毛巾、一条毯子、一些内衣，以及他的煮咖啡器具。因为哈立德可以舍弃他的穆贾达拉，却绝不能舍弃他的咖啡——他到底是个阿拉伯人。可一个阿拉伯人行路之人，若夜里发觉自己远离营地，便会在沙里挖一道沟，躺下去在活生生的星辰之下安眠。哈立德却做不到这般——无论在城里还是城外。然而，他也并不下榻于户内。他只为他的手推车租了一个安身处：那是一间小小的挂锁棚屋，他在那里存放他那点买卖家什。至于他在布朗克斯是怎样过活的，详见下面这封信：

===Original===
“My loving Brother Shakib,

“I have been two months here, in a neighbourhood familiar to you. Not far from the place where I sleep is the sycamore tree under which I burned my peddling-box. And perhaps I shall yet burn there my pushcart too. But for the present, all’s well. My business is good and my health is improving. The money-order I am enclosing with this, will cancel the note, but not the many debts, I owe you. And I hope to be able to join you again soon, to make the voyage to our native land together. Meanwhile I am working, and laying up a little something. I make from two to three dollars a day, of which I never spend more than one. And this on one meal only; for my lodging and my lunch and breakfast cost next to nothing. Yes, I can be a pushcart peddler in the day; I can sleep out of doors at night; I can do with coffee and oranges for lunch and breakfast; but in the evening I will assert my dignity and do justice to my taste: I will dine at the Hermitage and permit you to call me a fool. And why not, since my purse, like my stomach, is now my own? Why not go to the Hermitage since my pushcart income permits of it? But the first night I went there my shabbiness attracted the discomforting attention of the fashionable diners, and made even the waiters offensive. Indeed, one of them came to ask if I were looking for somebody. ‘No,’ I replied with suppressed indignation; ‘I’m looking for a place where I can sit down and eat, without being eaten by the eyes of the vulgar curious.’ And I pass into an arbor, which from that night becomes virtually my own, followed by a waiter who from that night, too, became my friend. For every evening I go there, I find my table unoccupied and my waiter ready to receive and serve me. But don’t think he does this for the sake of my black eyes or my philosophy. That disdainful glance of his on the first evening I could never forget, *billah*. And I found that it could be baited and mellowed only by a liberal tip. And this I make in advance every week for both my comfort and his. Yes, I am a fool, I grant you, but I’m not out of my element there.

“After dinner I take a stroll in the Flower Gardens, and crossing the rickety wooden bridge over the river, I enter the hemlock grove. Here, in a sequestered spot near the river bank, I lay me on the grass and sleep for the night. I always bring my towels with me; for in the morning I take a dip, and at night I use them for a pillow. When the weather requires it, I bring my blankets too. And hanging one of them over me, tied to the trees by the cords sown to its corners, I wrap myself in the other, and praise Allah.

“These and the towels, after taking my bath, I leave at the Hermitage; my waiter minds them for me. And so, I suspect I am happy—if, curse it! I could but breathe better. O, come up to see me. I’ll give you a royal dinner at the Hermitage, and a royal bed in the hemlock grove on the riverbank. Do come up, the peace of Allah upon thee. Read my salaam to Im-Hanna.”

===Chinese===
「我亲爱的兄弟沙基布：

我来此已两月，所在之处是你熟悉的。离我睡觉之处不远，便是那棵梧桐树——当初我就在它下面烧了我的货郎箱。说不定我将来还要在那里烧掉我的手推车。但眼下，一切都好。我的生意不错，身体也在好转。随信附上的这张汇票，足以勾销我欠你的那张借据，却勾销不了我欠你的诸多恩情。我希望不久便能与你再度相聚，一同航向我们的故土。此间我一边做工，一边攒下一点什么。我每天挣两块到三块钱，从中花费的从不超过一块。而这一块只花在一顿饭上；因为我的住处、我的午饭与早饭几乎一文不花。是的，白天我可以做手推车货郎；夜里我可以睡在露天；午饭和早饭我可以只就着咖啡和橘子打发；可到了傍晚，我要维护我的尊严、款待我的口味：我要到『隐庐』（Hermitage）去用晚餐，并任你骂我傻瓜。为何不可呢？既然我的钱袋，一如我的胃，如今是我自己的？既然我手推车的进项足以承担，为何不能到『隐庐』去？可我第一夜去到那里，我的寒酸惹来那些时髦食客叫人不自在的注目，连侍者都对我带了几分敌意。果然，其中一个过来问我是否在找人。『不，』我按捺着愤懑答道，『我在找一个可以坐下吃饭、却不被俗气好奇的目光吞吃的地方。』说着我步入一座棚亭——从那一夜起，它便几乎成了我的专有之地——身后跟着一个侍者，他也从那一夜起成了我的朋友。因为每晚我去到那里，都见我的桌子空着、我的侍者已准备好迎候我、伺候我。但你别以为他是冲着我的黑眼睛或我的哲学才如此。他第一夜那一道轻蔑的眼风，我一辈子也忘不了，凭真主起誓（billah）。而我发觉，唯有慷慨的小费才能把它逗软、把它焐暖。为此我每周提前打赏——既为我舒服，也为他舒服。是的，我承认我是个傻瓜，但我在那里并不格格不入。

「晚饭后我到花圃（Flower Gardens）里去散步，过了河上那座摇摇晃晃的木桥，便进入铁杉林。在河岸旁一处幽僻之地，我躺在草地上过夜。我总是把毛巾带在身边；因为清晨我要下水浸一浸，夜里则拿它当枕头。天候需要时，我也把毯子带上。把其中一条——四角系上绳子、拴在树上——张成顶篷罩在我上方，我便裹进另一条里，赞颂真主。

「这些以及毛巾，我浴罢便留在『隐庐』；我的侍者替我看着。是以，我疑心自己是快乐的——若是，该死！——我能呼吸得更顺畅些就好了。啊，上来看我罢。我要在『隐庐』请你一顿王者的晚餐，在河岸上的铁杉林里给你一张王者的床。一定要来，愿真主的平安降在你身上。请代我向伊姆-汉娜（Im-Hanna）致色兰（salaam）。」

===Original===
And during his five months in the Bronx he did not sleep five nights within doors, we are told, nor did he once dine out of the Hermitage. Even his hair, a fantastic fatuity behind a pushcart, he did not take the trouble to cut or trim. It must have helped his business. But this constancy, never before sustained to such a degree, must soon cease, having laid up, thanks to his pushcart and the people of the Bronx, enough to carry him, not only to Baalbek, but to *Aymakanenkan*.

===Chinese===
而他在布朗克斯的那五个月里，据说他没有五个晚上睡在户内，也未曾有一次在『隐庐』以外的地方用晚餐。连他那头蓬发——推着手推车的一桩荒唐怪诞——他也懒得去剪、去修。想必它倒帮了他的生意。然而这份恒心，先前从未持续到这般地步，不久便要告终：多亏了他的手推车和布朗克斯的人们，他已攒下了足够的钱，足以送他到的地方，不只是巴勒贝克，还有阿伊玛卡能坎（Aymakanenkan）。
