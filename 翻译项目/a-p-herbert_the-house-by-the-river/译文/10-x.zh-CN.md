## X / 十

===Original===

So the weeks went by. And John and Stephen saw little of each other. Indeed, they saw little of anyone. Then, towards the end of June, Margery Byrne got up for the first time, and little Joan came home from her grandmother’s. In a week Margery was completely and delightedly “up,” full of plans and longing to take up life exactly where she had left it. Stephen found her curiously eager for company, and especially the company of old friends; it seemed to her so long since she had seen them. Very soon she asked why John Egerton was so neglecting them. “Get him to come round, Stephen,” she said. “Ring him up now.” Stephen had lately told her the story of the inquest, of the local feeling and faction; and Margery had at once determined that she would think nothing of it. She would do as the Whittakers did; not that she was prepared in any case to believe evil of John. Yet at the back of her mind there was just a hint of curiosity about it.

So Stephen reluctantly rang him up﻿—reluctantly because he had wanted to work that evening, and because he feared this meeting. But he did not dare to seem unwilling.

And John Egerton came. He had known for some days that he would soon have to do it, and he, too, had been afraid. But this evening he was almost glad of the invitation. The long weeks of semi-isolation had tried him very severely. The sense of being an outcast from his fellows, suspected, despised, had grown unreasonably and was a perpetual irritant to the nerves. He had an aching to go again into a friend’s house, to sit and talk again with other men. And even the house of the Byrnes and the company of the Byrnes might be a soothing relief from his present loneliness.

===Chinese===

就这样，几个星期过去了。约翰和斯蒂芬很少见面。说实在的，他们跟谁都很少见面。到了六月底，玛杰丽·伯恩头一回下了床，小琼也从外婆家回来了。不出一个星期，玛杰丽便欢天喜地地彻底「起身」了，满脑子计划，一心巴望着把生活从她撂下的地方分毫不差地接续下去。斯蒂芬发现她出奇地渴望有人来做伴，尤其渴望老朋友来做伴；在她自己看来，仿佛已经好久好久没见着他们了。没过多久她就问，约翰·埃格顿怎么这样冷落他们。「让他过来嘛，斯蒂芬，」她说。「这就给他打电话。」斯蒂芬新近已把验尸审讯的始末、地方上的风议和派别讲给她听了；玛杰丽当即拿定主意，对这事绝不放在心上。她要照惠特克夫妇那样行事；何况她本来就无论如何不肯相信约翰会有什么恶行。只是在她心底深处，对这事到底还存着一丝好奇。

于是斯蒂芬勉勉强强地给他挂了电话——之所以勉强，一则是那天晚上他本想工作，二则是他害怕这次见面。可他又不敢显出半点不情愿来。

约翰·埃格顿来了。几天来他早知道这事很快躲不掉了，他心里也是怕的。可今天晚上，对这份邀请他几乎是感到高兴的。半与世隔绝的漫长几星期，把他磨得很苦。遭同侪摒弃、受人猜疑、被人鄙夷的感觉，已经滋长得全无道理，成了时时刻刻硌着神经的一根刺。他心里渴望得发疼，只想再走进一座朋友的家宅，再同别的男人坐下来谈一谈。哪怕是伯恩家的宅子、伯恩家的陪伴，对他眼下的孤寂，或许也算得一帖抚慰、一点解脱。


===Original===

And now that Margery was up and well, the time was surely near when something would be done about this business. Unpleasant things had happened. The family of the Gaunts had been to see him. They had come again this evening﻿—in the middle of supper﻿—sly, grasping, malicious people, a decayed husband of about fifty with a drooping, ragged moustache, with watery eyes and the aspect of a wet rat, and an upright, aggressive, spiteful little wife, with an antique bonnet fixed very firmly on the extreme summit of her yellowish hair. She had thin lips, a harsh voice, and an unpleasant manner. There was also a meek son of about twenty, and Emily’s fiancé, who looked conscientiously sad and respectable and wore a bowler hat. But the woman did all the talking. The men only interposed when they felt that she was going too far to be effective.

They wanted money. The men might be half-ashamed of wanting it, but they wanted it, and they clearly expected to get it. They assumed as common ground that John had made away with Emily and had only been preserved from arrest by the strange eccentricities of the law. They did not want trouble made, but there it was: Emily had been a good daughter to them and had contributed money to the household; and it was only fair that something should be done to heal the injury to their affections and their accounts. If not, of course, there would *have* to be trouble.

===Chinese===

如今玛杰丽既已起身大好，把这桩事作个了结的日子，想必近了。不痛快的事已经出了几桩。冈特一家上门找过他。就在今天晚上——正吃晚饭的当口——他们又来了：一家人鬼鬼祟祟、贪得无厌、心怀歹毒。做丈夫的约莫五十岁，一副破落户相，一把蔫塌塌、乱蓬蓬的胡子，一双水汪汪的眼睛，一脸落汤老鼠的神气；做妻子的却身板笔挺、个头矮小，凶横刻毒，一顶老式女帽牢牢扣在发黄头发尖顶的正中央。她嘴唇薄薄的，嗓音刺耳，举止惹人厌。还有一个约莫二十岁、低眉顺眼的儿子，再有就是埃米莉的未婚夫——这人一副悲戚得恪尽本分、体体面面的模样，头上戴一顶圆顶硬礼帽。不过话全让那女人一个人说了。男人们只在觉得她闹得过火、快要坏事的时候，才插上一句。

他们要钱。男人们对要钱这件事也许有一半羞惭，可他们要，而且明摆着指望到手。他们把一桩事当作不待言的前提：约翰害死了埃米莉，只因法律种种古怪乖僻的门道，才没有落到被逮捕的地步。他们并不想把事情闹大，可是话又说回来：埃米莉生前是他们的好女儿，往家里贴过钱；如今给他们感情上和账面上的创伤作个补赔，天公地道。不然的话，那自然就*非*闹出事来不可了。


===Original===

John Egerton, disgusted and humiliated, had nobly kept his temper, but firmly refused to give them a penny. They had gone away, muttering threats. John had no idea what they would do, but they filled him with loathing and fear. He could not endure this much longer for any man’s sake. Stephen must release him.

But the evening at the Byrnes’ house did nothing to clear things up. Rather it aggravated the tangle. Mrs. Byrne was lying on the sofa, looking more fragile yet more delicious than he had ever seen her. She greeted him very kindly and they talked for a little, while Stephen sat rather glumly in the window-seat staring out at the river.

===Chinese===

约翰·埃格顿又恶心又屈辱，却豁达大度地按住了性子，只是分文不给，一口回绝。他们走了，临走时咕哝着些威胁。他们会干出什么来，约翰无从知道，可这一家人叫他满心憎恶和恐惧。这样的事，他无论为着哪个朋友，都再受不了多久了。斯蒂芬必须放他脱身。

可是在伯恩家度过的这个晚上，非但无助于把事情理清，反倒叫这团乱麻缠得更紧了。伯恩太太躺在沙发上，比他历来所见的更显得娇弱，也更显得甜美。她十分亲切地招呼他，两人闲谈了一会儿；斯蒂芬却闷闷地坐在窗座上，直勾勾地望着外头的河。


===Original===

She spoke happily of Stephen Michael Hilary Byrne, of his charm and his intelligence, and how already he really had something of Stephen about him; and as she said that she smiled at Stephen. And she leaned back with a little sigh of content and looked round at her drawing-room, rich with warm and comfortable colour, at the striped material of delicate purple, at the Japanese prints she had bought with Stephen at a sale, at the curious but excellent wallpaper of dappled grey, and the pleasant rows of books on the white shelves, at the flowers in the Chinese bowl which Stephen had bought for her in some old shop, and the mass of roses on the shiny Sheraton table; then she looked out through the window at the red light of a tug sliding mysteriously down through the steely dark and back again at Stephen. And John knew that she was counting up her happiness; and he thought with an intense pity and rage how precarious that happiness was. He realized then that he could not allow Stephen to “do the right thing”; he would not press for it. After all, it was a small thing for himself to suffer, this petty local suspicion, even the visitations of the Gaunts, compared with the suffering which this dear and delicate lady would have to bear if the truth were told. Surely it was an easy sacrifice for a man to make.

So John sat glowing with sentiment and resolution, and Margery pondered the happiness of life, and Stephen brooded darkly in the window, and they were all silent. Then Margery suggested that the two men should sing together as they used to do; and they sang. They sang odd things from an Old English song book, picked out at random as they turned over the leaves. And it seemed as if every song in that book must have for those two some hidden and sinister meaning. It was bad enough, in any case, to stand there together behind Margery at the piano, and try to sing as they had sung in the old days, when nothing had happened. But these songs had some terrible innuendoes: “Blow, blow, thou winter wind,” they sang first, and “Sigh no more, ladies.” And when they came to “a friend’s ingratitude” and “fellowship forgot” and “Men were deceivers ever,” the two men became foolishly self-conscious. They looked studiously in front of them, and each in his heart hoped that the other had not noticed, hoped that his own expression was perfectly normal and composed. It was exceedingly foolish. There were other songs like this, and after a few more Stephen said shortly that that was enough.

===Chinese===

她欢欢喜喜地说起斯蒂芬·迈克尔·希拉里·伯恩，说这孩子多么可人疼、多么聪明，说他身上当真已经有点儿斯蒂芬的影子了；说这话时，她朝斯蒂芬一笑。随后她往后一靠，轻轻叹出一口心满意足的气，环视自己的客厅——满室温暖安适的色彩：那淡紫条纹的布料，那几张她同斯蒂芬在一场拍卖会上买来的日本版画，那古怪却极雅致的灰色斑纹墙纸，白书架上一排排可人的书，中国瓷钵里插着的鲜花——那钵是斯蒂芬在一家旧铺子里给她买的——还有亮闪闪的谢拉顿（Sheraton）式桌上那一大捧玫瑰；然后她望向窗外，看一艘拖船的红灯在钢灰色的夜色里神秘地滑向下游，随即又回过头来看斯蒂芬。约翰明白，她这是在盘点自己的幸福；他带着一阵剧烈的怜悯和愤怒想道，这幸福何其悬危。他这时明白了：他不能让斯蒂芬「做该做的事」；他不会去逼他。毕竟，同这位亲爱的、娇弱的夫人一旦真相大白便须承受的痛苦相比，他自己受的这点罪——区区的地方猜疑，乃至冈特家的屡屡登门——实在是小事一桩。这样一场牺牲，一个男子汉想必是轻易担当得起的。

于是约翰坐着，胸中燃着感慨与决意；玛杰丽细细回味着人生的幸福；斯蒂芬在窗边阴郁地出神；三个人都不作声。随后玛杰丽提议，两位男士该像从前那样一块儿唱唱歌；他们就唱了。他们唱的是从一本英国古歌集里信手翻出来的古怪曲子，翻到哪页是哪页。那本歌集里的每一支歌，于这两个人似乎都必定藏着某种隐秘而不祥的意味。本来，并肩站在玛杰丽的钢琴后面，竭力像无事的时代那样唱歌，就已经够难堪的了。偏生这些歌里尽是可怕的指桑骂槐：头一支唱的是「吹吧，吹吧，你这冬天的风」，接着是「姑娘们，别再叹息」。待唱到「朋友的忘恩负义」「被忘却的情谊」和「男人从来是骗子」，两个男人都傻里傻气地局促起来。他们煞有介事地望着正前方，各自心里巴望对方没有察觉，巴望自己脸上的神色照旧端正镇定。真是愚不可及。这样的歌还有几支；又唱了几支之后，斯蒂芬简短地说了句，够了。


===Original===

Then they tried to talk again; but the men could think of no topic which did not somehow lead them near to Emily Gaunt and such dangerous ground. Even when Margery began to speak of the motorboat, the men seemed to be stricken silly and dumb. Margery wondered what ailed them, till she remembered about John’s “wood-collecting” evidence, and blushed suddenly at her folly.

Stephen went down with John to the front door feeling certain that he would there and then “have it out.” But John said nothing, only a quick “Good night.” He did not look at Stephen. They felt then like strangers to each other. And Stephen, marvelling at John’s silence and strangely moved by his coldness, became suddenly anxious to get at his thoughts.

===Chinese===

随后他们又想聊聊；可是两个男人想不出一个话题，不绕来绕去把他们引到埃米莉·冈特和那类险地跟前的。就连玛杰丽说起摩托艇的时候，两个男人也仿佛中了邪，又傻又哑。玛杰丽纳闷他们这是犯了什么病，直到想起约翰那「拾柴火」的证词，才猛地为自己出的傻招红了脸。

斯蒂芬陪约翰下楼送到前门，满以为就要当场「把话摊开来」了。可约翰什么也没说，只匆匆道了一声「晚安」。他看也不看斯蒂芬。那一刻，两人形同陌路。斯蒂芬惊讶于约翰的沉默，又被他的冷淡莫名地打动，忽然急切起来，非要探出他的心思不可。

Stephen went slowly upstairs, astonished and ashamed, with a confused sense of humiliation and relief. And while he felt penitent and mean in the face of this magnanimity of John’s, he could not avoid a certain conceited contentment with the wisdom and success of his planning.

Yes, it was very satisfactory. And now he could get on with the poem about “Chivalry.” He sat down at his table and pulled out the scribbled muddle of manuscript. But he wrote no word that night. He sat for a long time staring at the paper, thinking of the chivalry of John Egerton. And it brought no inspiration.

===Chinese===

斯蒂芬慢慢上楼去，又惊又愧，屈辱与宽慰在心头搅作一团。面对约翰这般的宽宏大量，他一面觉得负疚、觉得自己卑劣，一面却又禁不住一阵自鸣得意的快慰——为自己的深谋远虑，为自己的处处得手。

是的，一切都十分圆满。如今他可以接着写他的长诗《骑士精神》了。他在桌前坐下，抽出那摞涂得乱七八糟的手稿。可那天夜里他一个字也没有写出来。他久久坐着，瞪着纸页，想着约翰·埃格顿的骑士精神。而这，并没有带来什么灵感。
