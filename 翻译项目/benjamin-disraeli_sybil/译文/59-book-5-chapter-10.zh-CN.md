### X / 十
===Original===

Mowbray was in a state of great excitement. It was Saturday evening: the mills were closed; the news had arrived of the arrest of the Delegate.
“Here’s a go!” said Dandy Mick to Devilsdust. “What do you think of this?”
“It’s the beginning of the end,” said Devilsdust.
“The deuce!” said the Dandy, who did not clearly comprehend the bent of the observation of his much pondering and philosophic friend, but was touched by its oracular terseness.
“We must see Warner,” said Devilsdust, “and call a meeting of the people on the moor for tomorrow evening. I will draw up some resolutions. We must speak out; we must terrify the capitalists.”
“I am all for a strike,” said Mick.
“ ’Tisn’t ripe,” said Devilsdust.
“But that’s what you always say, Dusty,” said Mick.
“I watch events,” said Devilsdust. “If you want to be a leader of the people you must learn to watch events.”
“But what do you mean by watching events?”
“Do you see Mother Carey’s stall?” said Dusty, pointing in the direction of the counter of the good-natured widow.
“I should think I did; and what’s more, Julia owes her a tick for herrings.”
“Right,” said Devilsdust: “and nothing but herrings are to be seen on her board. Two years ago it was meat.”
“I twig,” said Mick.
“Wait till it’s wegetables; when the people can’t buy even fish. Then we will talk about strikes. That’s what I call watching events.”
Julia, Caroline, and Harriet came up to them.
“Mick,” said Julia, “we want to go to the Temple.”
“I wish you may get it,” said Mick shaking his head. “When you have learnt to watch events, Julia, you will understand that under present circumstances the Temple is no go.”
“And why so, Dandy?” said Julia.
“Do you see Mother Carey’s stall?” said Mick, pointing in that direction. “When there’s a tick at Madam Carey’s there is no tin for Chaffing Jack. That’s what I call watching events.”
“Oh! as for the tin,” said Caroline, “in these halftime days that’s quite out of fashion. But they do say it’s the last night at the Temple, for Chaffing Jack means to shut up, it does not pay any longer; and we want a lark. I’ll stand treat; I’ll put my earrings up the spout﻿—they must go at last, and I would sooner at any time go to my uncle’s for frolic than woe.”
“I am sure I should like very much to go to the Temple if anyone would pay for me,” said Harriet, “but I won’t pawn nothing.”

莫布雷全城陷入巨大的激愤之中。那是星期六傍晚：纱厂都停了工；代表被捕的消息传来了。
「这可热闹了！」丹迪·米克对魔鬼尘说。「你怎么看这事儿？」
「这是末日的开端。」魔鬼尘说。
「见鬼！」米克说。他并不怎么明白他这位好深思、爱讲哲理的朋友这话的用意，却被这话神谕般的简峻打动了。
「咱们得去找沃纳，」魔鬼尘说，「召集民众明晚在荒原上开大会。我来起草几份决议。咱们必须把话说明白；必须让资本家们吓一跳。」
「我举双手赞成罢工。」米克说。
「火候未到。」魔鬼尘说。
「可你老这么说，尘哥。」米克说。
「我观察时势。」魔鬼尘说。「你要想当人民的领袖，就得学会观察时势。」
「可你说的观察时势，到底什么意思？」
「你瞧见凯里寡妇的摊子没有？」尘哥说着，朝那位好脾气寡妇的柜台方向指了指。
「那还用说；不光瞧见，朱莉娅还赊着她一笔记账的鲱鱼钱呢。」
「着啊，」魔鬼尘说：「如今她案板上只剩鲱鱼了。两年前摆的还是肉。」
「我开窍了。」米克说。
「等案板上换成青菜、连鱼老百姓都买不起的时候，咱们再谈罢工。这才叫观察时势。」
朱莉娅、卡罗琳和哈丽雅特走到他们跟前。
「米克，」朱莉娅说，「我们想去圣殿馆。」
「想得倒美。」米克摇着头说。「等你学会了观察时势，朱莉娅，你就会明白，眼下这光景，圣殿馆去不成。」
「为什么去不成，丹迪？」朱莉娅说。
「你瞧见凯里寡妇的摊子没有？」米克朝那边一指。「在凯里大婶这儿还记着账，兜里就没响儿给妙语杰克。这就叫观察时势。」
「哦！要说钱，」卡罗琳说，「这做半工的日子，那玩意儿早不时兴了。不过人家都说，今晚是圣殿馆最后一夜，妙语杰克打算关门了，再开下去不划算；我们正想乐呵乐呵。我来请客；我把耳环当出去——反正早晚得当，我要进当铺，宁可为了玩乐，也强似为了愁苦。」
「要是有谁替我掏钱，我巴不得去圣殿馆呢，」哈丽雅特说，「可我什么都不当。」

“If we only pay and hear them sing,” said Julia in a coaxing tone.
“Very like,” said Mick; “there’s nothing that makes one so thirsty as listening to a song, particularly if it touches the feelings. Don’t you remember, Dusty, when we used to encore that German fellow in ‘Scots wha ha.’ We always had it five times. Hang me if I wasn’t blind drunk at the end of it.”
“I tell you what, young ladies,” said Devilsdust, looking very solemn, “you’re dancing on a volcano.”
“Oh! my,” said Caroline. “I am sure I wish we were; though what you mean exactly I don’t quite know.”
“I mean that we shall all soon be slaves,” said Devilsdust.
“Not if we get the Ten-Hour Bill,” said Harriet.
“And no cleaning of machinery in meal time,” said Julia; “that is a shame.”
“You don’t know what you are talking about,” said Devilsdust. “I tell you, if the capitalists put down Gerard we’re done for another ten years, and by that time we shall be all used up.”
“Lor! Dusty, you quite terrify one,” said Caroline.
“It’s a true bill though. Instead of going to the Temple we must meet on the moor, and in as great numbers as possible. Go you and get all your sweethearts. I must see your father, Harriet; he must preside. We will have the hymn of labour sung by a hundred thousand voices in chorus. It will strike terror into the hearts of the capitalists. This is what we must all be thinking of if we wish labour to have a chance, not of going to Chaffing Jack’s and listening to silly songs. D’ye understand?”
“Don’t we!” said Caroline; “and for my part for a summer eve I prefer Mowbray Moor to all the Temples in the world, particularly if it’s a sociable party and we have some good singing.”
This evening it was settled among the principal champions of the cause of labour, among whom Devilsdust was now included, that on the morrow there should be a monster meeting on the moor to take into consideration the arrest of the delegate of Mowbray. Such was the complete organisation of this district that by communicating with the various lodges of the Trades Unions fifty thousand persons, or even double that number, could within four-and-twenty hours on a great occasion and on a favourable day be brought into the field. The morrow being a day of rest was favourable, and the seizure of their cherished delegate was a stimulating cause. The excitement was great, the enthusiasm earnest and deep.

「我们不过是付钱听听唱歌罢了。」朱莉娅娇声娇气地说。
「说得轻巧，」米克说；「没什么比听歌更叫人口渴的了，尤其是那种动感情的歌。你记不记得，尘哥，咱们从前老给那个唱《苏格兰人》的德国佬喝彩要求再来一个。回回都叫他唱上五遍。不瞒你说，听完我准醉得两眼发花。」
「我跟你们说吧，各位小姐，」魔鬼尘说，神色十分郑重，「你们这是在火山口上跳舞。」
「哎呀，我的天！」卡罗琳说。「我倒真希望是在火山口上跳呢；尽管你到底是什么意思，我不大明白。」
「我的意思是，咱们大伙儿很快就要当奴隶了。」魔鬼尘说。
「只要十小时法案通过了，就当不成。」哈丽雅特说。
「还有，不许在吃饭的时候擦机器，」朱莉娅说；「那才丢人呢。」
「你们不知道自己在说什么，」魔鬼尘说。「我告诉你们，资本家要是把杰拉德压下去，咱们再完十年，到那时候咱们就都熬干了。」
「老天爷！尘哥，你可把人吓坏了。」卡罗琳说。
「可这是实打实的事。咱们不该去圣殿馆，得去荒原上集会，人数越多越好。你们去把相好的都叫上。我得去见你父亲，哈丽雅特；得由他来主持。咱们要让劳动赞歌由十万人齐声合唱。好叫恐惧扎进资本家们的心里。咱们要想让劳动有一条活路，就得人人想着这个，而不是去妙语杰克那儿听傻歌。懂不懂？」
「那还用说！」卡罗琳说；「要我说，夏天的傍晚，莫布雷荒原比天底下所有的圣殿馆都强，尤其要是大伙儿凑得热闹，又有好歌听。」
当天晚上，劳工事业的主要斗士们——如今魔鬼尘也名列其中——议定：明日将在荒原上举行大规模集会，商讨莫布雷代表被捕一事。这一带的组织如此完备：只要与各工会分会一打招呼，赶上重大时机和合适的日子，二十四小时之内就能纠集五万人，甚至十万人到场。次日恰是安息日，条件有利；他们爱戴的代表被抓，又是一剂兴奋剂。群情激奋，热忱真挚而深沉。

===Original===

There was enough distress to make people discontented without depressing them. And Devilsdust after attending a council of the Union, retired to rest and dreamed of strong speeches and spicy resolutions, bands and banners, the cheers of assembled thousands, and the eventual triumph of the sacred rights.
The post of the next morning brought great and stirring news to Mowbray. Gerard had undergone his examination at Bow Street. It was a long and laborious one; he was committed for trial for a seditious conspiracy, but he was held to bail. The bail demanded was heavy; but it was prepared and instantly proffered. His sureties were Morley and a Mr. Hatton. By this post Morley wrote to his friends, apprising them that both Gerard and himself intended to leave London instantly, and that they might be expected to arrive at Mowbray by the evening train.
The monster meeting of the moor it was instantly resolved should be converted into a triumphant procession, or rather be preceded by one. Messengers on horseback were sent to all the neighbouring towns to announce the great event. Every artisan felt as a Muslim summoned by the sacred standard. All went forth with their wives and their children to hail the return of the patriot and the martyr. The Trades of Mowbray mustered early in the morning, and in various processions took possession of all the churches. Their great pride was entirely to fill the church of Mr. St. Lys, who not daunted by their demonstration, and seizing the offered opportunity, suppressed the sermon with which he had supplied himself and preached to them an extemporary discourse on “Fear God and honour the King.” In the dissenting chapels thanksgivings were publicly offered that bail had been accepted for Walter Gerard. After the evening service, which the Unions again attended, they formed in the High Street and lined it with their ranks and banners. Every half hour a procession arrived from some neighbouring town with its music and streaming flags. Each was received by Warner or some other member of the managing committee, who assigned to them their appointed position, which they took up without confusion, nor was the general order for a moment disturbed.

苦难已足以使人不满，却还压不垮人心。魔鬼尘参加了工会的一次会议后，回屋歇下，梦里尽是雄辩的演说和火辣的决议、乐队与旗帜、千万人的欢呼，以及神圣权利的最终胜利。
第二天早晨的邮车给莫布雷带来了激动人心的大新闻。杰拉德已在弓街受审。审讯漫长而费力；他被以煽动性阴谋罪提交审判，但获准保释。保金要得很重；却早已备好，当即呈上。他的保人是莫利和一位哈顿先生。同一班邮车，莫利写信给朋友们，告知他们他和杰拉德都打算立即离开伦敦，可望乘晚班火车抵达莫布雷。
荒原上的大规模集会当即决定改为一场凯旋式游行，或者不如说，先举行游行。骑马的信使被派往所有邻镇，宣告这件大事。每个工匠都觉得自己像被圣旗召唤的穆斯林。人人携妻带子涌上街头，欢迎爱国者与殉道者归来。
莫布雷各行会一清早就集合起来，分头列队占满了所有教堂。他们最大的得意，是把圣利斯先生的教堂坐得满满当当；圣利斯先生没有被这阵势吓住，反而抓住送到眼前的机会，收起预备好的布道词，即兴向他们宣讲了一篇「敬畏上帝，尊敬君王」。在非国教礼拜堂里，人们公开为沃尔特·杰拉德获准保释献上感恩。晚间礼拜——工会会员再次出席——之后，他们在大街列队，用队列和旗帜排满长街。每隔半小时，便有一支队伍从邻镇开来，带着乐队和飘扬的旗帜。每支队伍都由沃纳或管理委员会的其他成员迎接，被引到指定的位置，各就各位，毫不紊乱；总体秩序一刻也未曾打乱。

Sometimes a large party arrived without music or banners, but singing psalms and headed by their minister; sometimes the children walked together, the women following, then the men each with a ribbon of the same colour in his hat: all hurried, yet spontaneous and certain, indications how mankind under the influence of high and earnest feelings recur instantly to ceremony and form; how when the imagination is excited it appeals to the imagination, and requires for its expression something beyond the routine of daily life.
It was arranged that the moment the train arrived and the presence of Gerard was ascertained, the trade in position nearest to the station should commence the hymn of labour, which was instantly to be taken up by its neighbour, and so on in succession, so that by an almost electrical agency the whole population should almost simultaneously be assured of his arrival.
At half past six o’clock the bell announced that the train was in sight; a few minutes afterwards Dandy Mick hurried up to the leader of the nearest Trade, spoke a few words, and instantly the signal was given and the hymn commenced. It was taken up as the steeples of a great city in the silence of the night take up the new hour that has just arrived; one by one the mighty voices rose till they all blended in one vast waving sea of sound. Warner and some others welcomed Gerard and Morley, and ushered them, totally unprepared for such a reception, to an open carriage drawn by four white horses that was awaiting them. Orders were given that there was to be no cheering or any irregular clamour. Alone was heard the hymn. As the carriage passed each trade, they followed and formed in procession behind it; thus all had the opportunity of beholding their chosen chief, and he the proud consolation of looking on the multitude who thus enthusiastically recognised the sovereignty of his services.
The interminable population, the mighty melody, the incredible order, the simple yet awful solemnity, this representation of the great cause to which she was devoted under an aspect that at once satisfied the reason, captivated the imagination, and elevated the heart﻿—her admiration of her father, thus ratified as it were by the sympathy of a nation﻿—added to all the recent passages of her life teeming with such strange and trying interest, overcame Sybil.

有时来的是一大群人，没有乐队也没有旗帜，却唱着圣诗，由他们的牧师领着；有时孩子们走在前头，妇女们跟在后面，再后面是男人，每人帽子上都插着同色的缎带：这一切虽然匆忙，却是自发的、确凿的迹象，表明人类在高尚而真挚的情感驱使下，如何霎时回归仪礼与形式；表明想象力一经点燃，它便要诉诸想象，要求一种超越日常惯例的东西来表达自己。
已经议定：火车一到，确证杰拉德在场，距车站最近的那个行会便领唱劳动赞歌，相邻的行会立刻接上，如此依次相传，仿佛电流一般，让全体民众几乎在同一时刻得知他的到来。
六点半钟，钟声报出火车已入望；几分钟后，丹迪·米克疾步跑到最近那个行会的领队跟前，说了几句，信号立刻发出，赞歌开唱了。歌声被接续起来，就像万籁俱寂的夜里，大都会的座座尖塔依次报出刚到的新钟点；雄浑的歌声一声声升起，终于汇成一片浩荡起伏的声的海洋。沃纳和另外几个人迎上杰拉德和莫利，把他们——两人全然没有料到这样的迎接——引到一辆由四匹白马拉着的敞篷马车上，那车正候着他们。已下达命令：不许欢呼，不许有任何杂乱的喧哗。唯独听得见赞歌。马车经过每个行会的队列，他们便跟上去，在车后结成游行队伍；这样人人都得以亲眼看见自己选中的领袖，而他也怀着自豪的慰藉，注视着这万千大众——他们如此热烈地承认他功业的至尊地位。
望不到头的人群，雄壮的旋律，难以置信的秩序，朴素而令人敬畏的庄严——她献身的伟大事业，以这样一种面貌呈现出来，既满足了理智，又摄住了想象，还提升了心灵——她对父亲的敬爱，此刻仿佛得到了举国同情的加冕——再加上她近来经历的种种奇诡而揪心的人生段落，西比尔终于支持不住了。
