### XIII / 十三
===Original===

It was the Spring of last year, and Lady Bardolf was making a morning visit to Lady St. Julians.
“I heard they were to be at Lady Palmerston’s last night,” said Lady St. Julians.
“No,” said Lady Bardolf shaking his head, “they make their first appearance at Deloraine House. We meet there on Thursday I know.”
“Well, I must say,” said Lady St. Julians, “that I am curious to see her.”
“Lord Valentine met them last year at Naples.”
“And what does he say of her.”
“Oh! he raves!”
“What a romantic history! And what a fortunate man is Lord Marney. If one could only have foreseen events!” exclaimed Lady St. Julians. “He was always a favourite of mine though. But still I thought his brother was the very last person who ever would die. He was so very hard!”
“I fear Lord Marney is entirely lost to us,” said Lady Bardolf looking very solemn.
“Ah! he always had a twist,” said Lady St. Julians, “and used to breakfast with that horrid Mr. Trenchard, and do those sort of things. But still with his immense fortune, I should think he would become rational.”
“You may well say immense,” said Lady Bardolf. “Mr. Ormsby, and there is no better judge of another man’s income, says there are not three peers in the kingdom who have so much a year clear.”
“They say the Mowbray estate is forty thousand a year,” said Lady St. Julians. “Poor Lady de Mowbray! I understand that Mr. Mountchesney has resolved not to appeal against the verdict.”
“You know he has not a shadow of a chance,” said Lady Bardolf. “Ah! what changes we have seen in that family! They say the writ of right killed poor Lord de Mowbray, but to my mind he never recovered the burning of the castle. We went over to them directly, and I never saw a man so cut up. We wanted them to come to us at Firebrace, but he said he should leave the county immediately. I remember Lord Bardolf mentioning to me, that he looked like a dying man.”
“Well I must say,” said Lady St. Julians, rallying as it were from a fit of abstraction, “that I am most curious to see Lady Marney.”
The reader will infer from this conversation that Dandy Mick, in spite of his stunning fall, and all dangers which awaited him on his recovery, had contrived in spite of fire and flame, sabre and carbine, trampling troopers and plundering mobs, to reach the Convent of Mowbray with the box of papers.

那是去年春天，巴多尔夫夫人正到圣朱利安夫人府上作晨间拜访。
「听说他们昨晚要去帕默斯顿夫人府上来着。」圣朱利安夫人说。
「不，」巴多尔夫夫人摇着头说，「他们的首次露面是在德洛雷因公馆。我知道，我们星期四将在那里相见。」
「唔，说实话，」圣朱利安夫人说，「我倒真想见见她。」
「瓦伦丁勋爵去年在那不勒斯遇见过他们。」
「他说她怎么样？」
「哦！他赞不绝口！」
「多么浪漫的一段往事！马尼勋爵又是多幸运的人啊。要是人能未卜先知就好了！」圣朱利安夫人喊道。「不过他一向是我中意的人。可我总以为他哥哥是天底下最死不了的人。他那么硬实！」
「我看马尼勋爵算是完全脱离我们这个圈子了，」巴多尔夫夫人说，神色十分郑重。
「唉！他总有点别扭劲儿，」圣朱利安夫人说，「从前老同那个讨厌的特伦查德先生一起吃早饭，净干这类事。可凭他那份了不得的家产，我总该想他会变得理性些。」
「你说『了不得』还真说得对，」巴多尔夫夫人说。「奥姆斯比先生说过——论搭量别人进项的眼力，没人比他更准——全王国一年有这么多净收入的贵族，数不出三位。」
「人家说莫布雷那宗产业一年四万镑呢。」圣朱利安夫人说。「可怜的德·莫布雷夫人！我听说芒特切斯尼先生已决意不对判决上诉了。」
「你也知道，他连一线胜诉的希望都没有，」巴多尔夫夫人说。「唉！那一家子出了多大的变迁啊！都说那道权利令状要了可怜的德·莫布雷勋爵的命；依我看，城堡那场大火之后，他就再没缓过来。我们立刻赶去看他们，我从没见过一个男人垮成那样。我们想请他们到我们菲尔布雷斯去住，可他说他马上就要离开本郡了。我记得巴多尔夫勋爵同我讲过，他那样子活像个将死的人。」
「唔，说真的，」圣朱利安夫人说，仿佛从一阵出神中回过味来，「我最想见见的，还是马尼夫人。」
读者从这番谈话里可以推想出：丹迪·米克虽然摔得晕死过去，醒来后又险象环生，却硬是闯过了烈火浓烟、马刀与卡宾枪、践踏的铁骑与劫掠的暴民，把那匣文书送到了莫布雷修道院。

There he enquired for Sybil, in whose hands, and whose hands alone he was enjoined to deposit them. She was still absent, but faithful to his instructions, Mick would deliver his charge to none other, and exhausted by the fatigues of the terrible day, he remained in the courtyard of the Convent, lying down with the box for his pillow until Sybil under the protection of Egremont herself returned. Then he fulfilled his mission. Sybil was too agitated at the moment to perceive all its import, but she delivered the box into the custody of Egremont, who desiring Mick to follow him to his hotel bade farewell to Sybil, who equally with himself, was then ignorant of the fatal encounter on Mowbray Moor.
We must drop a veil over the anguish which its inevitable and speedy revelation brought to the daughter of Gerard. Her love for her father was one of those profound emotions which seemed to form a constituent part of her existence. She remained for a long period in helpless woe, soothed only by the sacred cares of Ursula. There was another mourner in this season of sorrow who must not be forgotten; and that was Lady Marney. All that tenderness and the most considerate thought could devise to soften sorrow and reconcile her to a change of life which at the first has in it something depressing were extended by Egremont to Arabella. He supplied in an instant every arrangement which had been neglected by his brother, but which could secure her convenience and tend to her happiness. Between Marney Abbey where he insisted for the present that Arabella should reside and Mowbray, Egremont passed his life for many months, until by some management which we need not trace or analyse, Lady Marney came over one day to the Convent at Mowbray and carried back Sybil to Marney Abbey, never again to quit it until on her bridal day, when the Earl and Countess of Marney departed for Italy where they passed nearly a year, and from which they had just returned at the commencement of this chapter.
During the previous period however many important events had occurred. Lord Marney had placed himself in communication with Mr. Hatton, who had soon become acquainted with all that had occurred in the muniment room of Mowbray Castle. The result was not what he had once anticipated; but for him it was not without some compensatory circumstances.

他在那里求见西比尔——嘱托写得明白，那匣东西只许交到她一人手中。她还没回来；米克恪守嘱托，谁也不肯交付；可怕的一天已叫他筋疲力尽，他便留在修道院的院子里，拿匣子当枕头躺下，直等到西比尔在埃格雷蒙特本人的护送下回来。于是他交了差。西比尔当时心绪太乱，未能尽察此事的分量；她把匣子托付给埃格雷蒙特保管；埃格雷蒙特嘱米克随他回旅馆，向西比尔道了别——她同他一样，此刻还不知道莫布雷荒原上那场致命的遭遇。
那真相必然很快揭晓，它带给杰拉德的女儿的痛楚，我们只好掩上一幅帷幕。她对父亲的爱，属于那种深沉得仿佛构成她生命一部分的情感。她久久沉浸在无告的哀恸中，唯有厄休拉神圣的照拂能给她慰藉。这个悲哀的时节还有一位哀悼者，不可忘却；那就是马尼夫人。凡是柔情与最体贴的思虑所能想出的办法——好减轻悲恸，使她安于那起初难免令人沮丧的生活变故——埃格雷蒙特都施与了阿拉贝拉。他哥哥先前忽略的一切安排，凡能保障她的安逸、增进她的幸福的，他都顷刻间办妥。好几个月里，埃格雷蒙特的日子就在马尼修道院——他坚持阿拉贝拉目前住在那里——与莫布雷之间往返度过；直到有一天，靠着一番我们不必细究的运筹，马尼夫人亲自来到莫布雷修道院，把西比尔接回马尼修道院；此后西比尔再没有离开，直到出阁之日——那天，马尼伯爵和伯爵夫人动身去了意大利，在那里住了将近一年；本章开篇时，他们才刚刚归来。
不过在那之前的一段日子里，发生了许多重大事件。马尼勋爵同哈顿先生取得了联系；哈顿很快便尽知莫布雷城堡文书室里发生的一切。结果并不如他当初所料；但对他来说，也并非没有几分补偿。

True another, and an unexpected rival, had stepped on the stage with whom it was vain to cope, but the idea that he had deprived Sybil of her inheritance, had ever, since he had became acquainted with her, been the plague-spot of Hatton’s life, and there was nothing that he desired more ardently than to see her restored to her rights, and to be instrumental in that restoration. How successful he was in pursuing her claim, the reader has already learnt.
Dandy Mick was rewarded for all the dangers he had encountered in the service of Sybil, and what he conceived was the vindication of popular rights. Lord Marney established him in business, and Mick took Devilsdust for a partner. Devilsdust having thus obtained a position in society and become a capitalist, thought it but a due homage to the social decencies to assume a decorous appellation, and he called himself by the name of the town where he was born. The firm of Radley, Mowbray, and Co., is a rising one; and will probably furnish in time a crop of members of Parliament and Peers of the realm. Devilsdust married Caroline, and Mrs. Mowbray became a great favorite. She was always perhaps a little too fond of junketting but she had a sweet temper and a gay spirit, and sustained her husband in the agonies of a great speculation, or the despair of glutted markets. Julia became Mrs. Radley, and was much esteemed: no one could behave better. She was more orderly than Caroline, and exactly suited Mick, who wanted a person near him of decision and method. As for Harriet, she is not yet married. Though pretty and clever, she is selfish and a screw. She has saved a good deal and has a considerable sum in the Savings’ Bank, but like many heiresses she cannot bring her mind to share her money with another. The great measures of Sir Robert Peel, which produced three good harvests, have entirely revived trade at Mowbray. The Temple is again open, newly-painted, and re-burnished, and Chaffing Jack has of course “rallied” while good Mrs. Carey still gossips with her neighbours round her well-stored stall, and tells wonderful stories of the great stick-out and riots of ’42.
And thus I conclude the last page of a work, which though its form be light and unpretending, would yet aspire to suggest to its readers some considerations of a very opposite character. A year ago.

不错，另有一位出手意料的对手登了场，同他相争是无望的；可是，一想到是自己夺去了西比尔的继承权——自他认识她以来，这念头始终是哈顿心上的疫疤；他最热切盼望的，莫过于眼看她重归名分，并由自己出力成全这一归还。他在追讨她的权益上成效如何，读者已经知晓。
丹迪·米克为西比尔效力、也为他心目中民权的伸张而历尽险阻，如今得了报偿。马尼勋爵出资助他开业，米克又拉魔鬼尘入了伙。魔鬼尘既然由此取得了社会地位、当上了资本家，便觉得改个体面名字，也算对社交体统应尽的敬意，于是用了自己出生市镇的名字。拉德利—莫布雷商号如今蒸蒸日上；假以时日，多半还会产出一茬下院议员和王国的贵族来。魔鬼尘娶了卡罗琳；莫布雷太太成了人人喜爱的人物。她也许总有点太爱寻欢作乐，可她性儿甜、心气旺，丈夫在大投机里煎熬、在市场过剩的绝望里挣扎时，全靠她支撑。朱莉娅成了拉德利太太，颇受敬重：没人比她行事更得体。她比卡罗琳更有条理，正对米克的心思——他正需要身边有个果决而有章法的人。至于哈丽雅特，她还没嫁人。她人俊俏又机灵，只是自私，还是个抠门儿。她攒下了不少钱，储蓄银行里有一笔可观的款子；可同许多女继承人一样，她怎么也下不了决心同人分享自己的钱。罗伯特·皮尔爵士的那些大举措带来了三个好年成，莫布雷的市面完全复兴了。圣殿馆重新开张，漆了新漆、擦得锃亮；妙语杰克自然也「重振」了雄风；好心的凯里大婶仍旧守着她货物充盈的摊子同街坊闲磕牙，讲起四二年那场大罢工和大骚乱，那故事可神了。
就这样，我为这部书写完了最后一页。它的形式虽轻快，并不张扬，却仍想请读者思索一些性质截然相反的问题。一年前——

I presumed to offer to the public some volumes that aimed to call their attention to the state of our political parties; their origin, their history, their present position. In an age of political infidelity, of mean passions and petty thoughts, I would have impressed upon the rising race not to despair, but to seek in a right understanding of the history of their country and in the energies of heroic youth﻿—the elements of national welfare. The present work advances another step in the same enterprise. From the state of Parties it now would draw public thought to the state of the People whom those parties for two centuries have governed. The comprehension and the cure of this greater theme depend upon the same agencies as the first: it is the past alone that can explain the present, and it is youth that alone can mould the remedial future. The written history of our country for the last ten reigns has been a mere phantasma; giving to the origin and consequence of public transactions a character and colour in every respect dissimilar with their natural form and hue. In this mighty mystery all thoughts and things have assumed an aspect and title contrary to their real quality and style: Oligarchy has been called Liberty; an exclusive Priesthood has been christened a National Church; Sovereignty has been the title of something that has had no dominion, while absolute power has been wielded by those who profess themselves the servants of the People. In the selfish strife of factions two great existences have been blotted out of the history of England﻿—the Monarch and the Multitude; as the power of the Crown has diminished, the privileges of the People have disappeared; till at length the sceptre has become a pageant, and its subject has degenerated again into a serf.
It is nearly fourteen years ago, in the popular frenzy of a mean and selfish revolution which neither emancipated the Crown nor the People, that I first took the occasion to intimate and then to develop to the first assembly of my countrymen that I ever had the honour to address, these convictions. They have been misunderstood as is ever for a season the fate of truth, and they have obtained for their promulgator much misrepresentation as must ever be the lot of those who will not follow the beaten track of a fallacious custom.

我斗胆向公众献上过几卷书，意在唤起他们对我国政党状况——其渊源、其历史、其眼下处境——的注意。在一个政治上无所信守、情操鄙琐、胸襟狭小的时代，我想让正在成长的一代铭记：不要绝望，而要从正确理解本国历史之中、从英勇青年的活力之中，去寻求国运昌隆的要素。
眼下这部书，在这同一桩事业上又迈出了一步。它想把公众的思绪从政党的状况，引向两个世纪以来受这些政党治理的民众的境况。理解并疗救这个更重大的题目，所依赖的路径与前一个相同：唯有过去能解释现在，也唯有青年能塑造补救的未来。近十朝以来，我们国家写下来的历史不过是一幅幻影；它赋予公共事务的起源与后果的面目和色彩，同它们天然的形貌色调处处相左。在这重宏大的迷障之中，一切思想与事物都顶上了与其实质和品格相悖的名目：寡头统治被称作自由；一个排他的教士集团被奉为国民教会；「主权」成了一件并无统治权之物的尊号；而绝对的权力，却握在自称人民之仆的人们手里。在党派自私的倾轧中，两大存在被从英格兰的历史上抹去了——君主与民众；王权的权柄日益削减，人民的特权也随之消失；直到最后，权杖沦为一场排场，而权杖下的臣民，重新沦落为农奴。
约莫十四年前——正值一场既不曾解放王权、也不曾解放民众的卑琐自私的革命掀起全民狂热之际——我第一次得机缘，向我有幸致辞的第一届本国议会，先是暗示、继而阐发了这些信念。它们曾被误解——真理在一个时期里总难逃此命；也为它们的传布者招来诸多歪曲——不肯因循谬误惯例的陈轨的人，总注定要承受这些。

But time that brings all things has brought also to the mind of England some suspicion that the idols they have so long worshipped and the oracles that have so long deluded them are not the true ones. There is a whisper rising in this country that loyalty is not a phrase, faith not a delusion, and popular liberty something more diffusive and substantial than the profane exercise of the sacred rights of sovereignty by political classes.
That we may live to see England once more possess a free Monarchy and a privileged and prosperous People, is my prayer; that these great consequences can only be brought about by the energy and devotion of our youth is my persuasion. We live in an age when to be young and to be indifferent can be no longer synonymous. We must prepare for the coming hour. The claims of the future are represented by suffering millions; and the youth of a nation are the trustees of posterity.

但是，万象皆备的时间，也使英格兰的心智生出几分疑实：他们长久膜拜的偶像、长久蛊惑他们的神谕，恐怕并非真神。这个国家正升起一阵低语：忠诚不是一句空话，信仰不是一场幻梦；而民众的自由，也远比各政治阶级亵渎地行使主权神圣权利来得更广布、更实在。
愿天从人愿，让我们活着看到英格兰重新拥有一个自由的君主制和一个享有权利、繁荣昌盛的人民——这是我的祈愿；这样重大的后果，唯有靠我们青年的精力与献身方能达成——这是我的信念。我们生活在一个时代：年轻与冷漠，再不能划等号。我们必须为即将到来的时刻做准备。未来的诉求，由受苦的千百万人民代表；而一个民族的青年，是后世的受托人。
