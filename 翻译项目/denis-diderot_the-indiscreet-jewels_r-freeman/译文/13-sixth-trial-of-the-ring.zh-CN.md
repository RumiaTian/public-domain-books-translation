## XIII Of the Opera at Banza / 论班扎的歌剧
===Original===

Of all the public diversions of Banza, none supported itself but the opera. Utmiutsol and Utremifasolasiututut, two celebrated musicians, one of whom was growing old, and the other was but just new-fledged, alternately occupied the lyric scene. Each of these two original authors had his partisans. The ignorant and the grey-bearded dotards stood up for Utmiutsol; the smart young fellows and the Virtuosi were for Utremifasolasiututut: and the people of taste, as well young as old, held them both in high esteem. Utremifasolasiututut, said the latter, is excellent when he is good, but he sleeps at times; and, pray, to whom does not that happen? Utmiutsol holds up better, and is more uniform. He is full of beauties; yet he has not one, of which there are not examples to be found, and even more striking, in his rival; in whom there are strokes to be observed, which are entirely his own, and are not to be met with anywhere but in his works. Old Utmiutsol is simple, natural, smooth, sometimes too smooth, and that is his fault. Young Utremifasolasiututut is singular, brilliant, composed, learned, sometimes too learned: but perhaps that is his hearer’s fault. The one has but one opening, beautiful indeed, but repeated at the head of all his pieces. The other has made as many openings as pieces, and they all pass for masterpieces. Nature guided Utmiutsol in the ways of melody; study and experience discovered the sources of harmony to Utremifasolasiututut. Who ever knew how to declaim, and who will ever speak a part like the old man? Who will compose light catches, voluptuous airs, and symphonies in character like the young one? Utmiutsol is the only person who understood dialogue. Before Utremifasolasiututut nobody distinguished the delicate touches, which separate the tender from the voluptuous, the voluptuous from the passionate, the passionate from the lascivious. Nay some partisans of the latter pretend, that if Utmiutsol’s dialogue is superior to his, this is not so much owing to the inequality of their talents, as to the difference of the poets, whom they made use of. “Read, read,” cried they, “the scene of Dardanus, and you will be convinced, that if we give good words to Utremifasolasiututut, Utmiutsol’s charming scenes will be revived.”

===Chinese===

班扎的一切公众娱乐里，只有歌剧自立得住。乌特米乌特索尔同乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特（Utremifasolasiututut），两位大名鼎鼎的音乐家——一个渐渐老了，一个羽翼方新——轮流占着抒情舞台。这两位独创的作家各有拥趸。无知之辈和白胡子老糊涂捧乌特米乌特索尔；伶俐的小伙子和鉴赏行家拥乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特：而有品味的人，不论老少，对两位一样推重。后一派说：乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特好起来精彩绝伦，只是时有昏睡；可谁保得住不犯这个呢？乌特米乌特索尔更稳，更匀。他满纸妙处；然而没有一处，在他的对手那里找不到先例，甚至更惊人；而对手笔下有几笔，纯是他自家的，除他的作品外别处遇不着。老乌特米乌特索尔简洁、自然、流畅，有时太流畅了，这是他的毛病。年轻的乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特奇特、辉煌、沉稳、有学问，有时太有学问：不过那兴许是听客的毛病。一位只有一个序曲，固然美，却安在他每一部作品的头上；另一位写了多少部就有多少个序曲，个个被誉为杰作。自然领着乌特米乌特索尔走旋律的路；钻研与阅历为乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特揭示了和声的源头。谁曾经会朗诵、往后谁还能像老头子那样念台词？谁又能像年轻的这位，谱轻快的叠歌、艳丽的咏叹、合体的配乐？乌特米乌特索尔是唯一懂得对话的人。乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特之前，没有人分辨得出那些细腻的笔触：温柔与艳冶之间、艳冶与热烈之间、热烈与淫荡之间的分界。不但如此，后者的拥趸里还有人说：乌特米乌特索尔的对话若胜过他，未必因为两人才分悬殊，多半因为他们用的诗人不同。“读一读，读一读《达达努斯》那场戏，”他们喊，“你们就会信：只要给乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特好词儿，乌特米乌特索尔那些迷人的场面就能复活。”

===Original===

However that be, in my time, the whole town flock’d to the tragedies of the latter, and people stifled one another at the interludes of the former.
They were just then exhibiting in Banza an excellent piece of Utremifasolasiututut, which would never have been represented but in nightcaps, had not the favorite Sultana had the curiosity to see it. And besides, the periodical indisposition of Toys favored the jealousy of the fiddles, and made the principal actress flinch. She, who supplied her place, had not so good a voice, but as she made amends by her manner of acting, nothing hindered the Sultan and the favorite from honouring the piece with their presence.
Mirzoza was already come, Mangogul comes, the curtain is raised, they begin. Everything went on marvellously well: Miss Chevalier had effaced the memory of Miss le Maure, and they were at the fourth act, when the Sultan bethought himself, in the middle of a chorus, which he thought lasted too long, and had already cost the favorite two yawns, to point his ring on all the singers. Never was there seen on the stage so odd and comical a sight. Thirty women were struck dumb on a sudden. Their mouths were wide open, and they kept the same theatrical attitudes they held before. And at the same time their Toys made their throats sore with the violence of singing, this a “Pont-neuf,” that a “Vaudeville polisson,” another a very indecent parody, and all of them extravagances relative to their characters. On one hand was heard, *oh vraiment ma comere, oui*; on the other, *quoi douze fois?* Here, *qui me baise, est-ce Blaise?* There, *rien, Père Cyprien, ne vous retient*. *Infine*, they all wound themselves up into a strain, so high, so rude, and so mad, that they formed the most extravagant brawling ridiculous choir, that had been heard before and since that of —﻿—﻿no﻿—﻿—﻿d﻿—﻿—﻿on﻿—﻿—﻿

All this while the Orchestra went on, and the loud laughs of the pit and boxes joined to the sound of the instruments, and the singing of the Toys, completed the cacophony.
Some of the actresses, fearing lest their Toys, tired with quavering impertinences, might resolve to speak them out, ran behind the scenes: but they escaped with the fright only. Mangogul being persuaded that the public would learn nothing new, turn’d off his ring.

无论怎样，在我那个时候，全城人挤破头去看后者的悲剧，看前者的幕间歌舞时，人挤得喘不上气。

其时班扎正上演乌特雷米法索拉西乌图图特的一部杰作；若不是宠妃苏丹娜好奇要看，这部戏本会同睡帽一起埋没了。而且，多亏玩具们周期性的喑哑，也助长了提琴师们的醋意，叫女主角怯了场。顶替她的那位嗓子差些，好在拿做派补了；苏丹同宠妃赏光驾临，也就没有什么拦着的了。

米尔佐扎先到，曼戈古尔随后，幕启，开演。一切进行得妙极：谢瓦利耶小姐抹掉了勒莫尔小姐的记忆，戏已到第四幕，苏丹在一支合唱里忽然起了念头——那合唱他嫌太长，已经劳宠妃打了两个呵欠——把戒指对准了全体歌者。舞台上从未见过这样古怪滑稽的场面。三十个女人霎时齐哑。嘴大张着，先前的舞台姿势一一保持不动。与此同时，她们的玩具唱得嗓子都哑了：这只来一支《新桥小调》，那支来一支“浪荡沃德维尔”，另一支唱不堪入耳的仿作，唱的全是同各自角色相称的荒唐货。这边一句*“哦真的吗，我的老姐妹，是吗”*；那边一句*“什么，十二回？”*；这里*“亲我的是谁，可是布莱兹？”*；那里*“什么也拦不住你，西普里安神父”*。*末了*，个个拔到一个又高、又野、又疯的调门上，汇成了有史以来最荒唐、最聒耳、最好笑的合唱队——比——不——的——那支——

这半天，乐队照奏不误；正厅和包厢的哄笑，和着乐器的声响与玩具们的歌唱，凑足了这场不谐和的大杂烩。

有几个女演员生怕自己的玩具颤音唱累了无礼话，索性开口明说，逃到了后台：不过她们只受了场虚惊。曼戈古尔料定公众听不出什么新东西，把戒指转了过去。

===Original===

In an instant all the Toys were silent, the bursts of laughter ceased, the audience grew calm, the play was resumed and finished peaceably. The curtain drop’d, the Sultana and Sultan went off, and the Toys of our actresses went to their several appointments, where they were to have a different employment from singing.
This affair made a great noise. The men laugh’d, the women were alarmed, the Bonzas were scandalized, the academicians rack’d their brains about it. But what did Orcotomus say? Orcotomus triumph’d. He had hinted in one of his memoirs, that the Toys would infallibly sing: they had sung accordingly, and this phenomenon, which disconcerted his brethren, was to him a new ray of light, and completed the confirmation of his system.
