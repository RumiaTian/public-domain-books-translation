## XXVIII Thirteenth Trial of the Ring / 戒指的第十三次试验
===Original===

I am not a great portrait-maker. I have exempted the reader from that of the favorite Sultana; but I can never condescend to remit him that of the Sultan’s mare. She was of a middling size, and had a pretty good gait; the chief fault found with her in that regard was, that she did not bridle her head sufficiently. Her colour was white, with blue eyes, small hoofs, clean legs, firm hams, and light haunches. She had been taught to dance for a long time, and she made her bows like a master of the ceremonies. Upon the whole she was a pretty beast enough, and remarkably gentle: she was easily mounted, but one must be an excellent horseman to be able to keep the saddle. She had belong’d to the senator Aaron: but on a fine evening the skittish creature took fright, threw the judge, and ran full speed to the Sultan’s studs, carrying with her the saddle, bridle, furniture, housings and caparisons of value; which became her so well, that it was not thought proper to send them back.
Mangogul went into his stables, accompanied by his first secretary Ziguezague. “Listen attentively,” said he, “and write.”﻿—That very instant he turn’d his ring on the mare, which fell to leaping, prancing, kicking, bouncing, and neighing under the tail.﻿—“Where are your thoughts,” said the prince to his secretary, “write then.”
“Sultan,” replied Ziguezague, “I wait till your highness begins.”
“My mare,” says Mangogul, “will dictate to you this once, write.”
Ziguezague, whom this order reduced too low in his own opinion, assumed the liberty of representing to the Sultan, that he would always esteem it a high honour to be his secretary, but not that of his mare. “Write, I tell you,” said the Sultan again.
“Prince, I cannot,” replied Ziguezague: “I know not the orthography of this sort of words.”
“Write however,” said the Sultan once more.
“I am excessively mortified, to be obliged to disobey your highness,” added Ziguezague; “but﻿—”
“But you are a scoundrel,” interrupted Mangogul, incensed at a refusal so much out of place; “quit my palace, and never appear there more.”
Poor Ziguezague disappear’d, having learn’d by experience, that a man of spirit ought not to enter the palaces of most part of the great, without leaving his sentiments at the gate. His deputy was called. He was a Provençal, frank, honest, and thoroughly disinterested.

我不算高明的画像师。宠妃苏丹娜的像，我已免了读者；可苏丹那匹母马的像，我永远腾不出这个情面。她中等身材，步子相当好；这方面人们对她主要的挑剔，是头收得不够。她通体白色，蓝眼睛，蹄子小，腿干净，后腿结实，臀部轻捷。她学过多年的舞，行礼像位司仪大师。总的来说是头够标致的畜生，而且出奇温顺：上她容易，可想坐稳鞍子，得是顶好的骑手。她原属元老阿隆：可一个晴朗的傍晚，这淘气的畜生受了惊，把法官摔下去，撒腿直奔苏丹的御厩，随身带走了鞍子、笼头、马具、马衣和值钱的蔽身；那副行头同她这样相称，大家觉得不便送还了。

曼戈古尔进了御厩，由他的首席秘书齐格扎格（Ziguezague）陪着。“仔细听，”他说，“记。”——话音未落，他把戒指转向那母马；母马登时又蹿、又刨、又踢、又蹦，还朝着尾巴嘶鸣。——“你的魂儿上哪儿去了，”王子对秘书说，“记呀。”

“苏丹，”齐格扎格答道，“我等殿下开口。”

“我的母马，”曼戈古尔说，“这回由她口授，你记。”

齐格扎格觉得这道指令贬他贬得太狠，冒昧进言：能为殿下做秘书，永远是他莫大的荣莘；给母马做秘书，可不在其内。“记，我说，”苏丹又说。

“君主，我不能，”齐格扎格回道：“这类话的拼法我不懂。”

“尽管记。”苏丹再说一遍。

“违抗殿下，叫我难过极了，”齐格扎格又道；“可是——”

“可是你是个恶棍，”曼戈古尔被这不合时宜的顶撞激怒，打断他；“滚出我的宫，永远别再露面。”

可怜的齐格扎格退了下去，得了个教训：一个有心气的人，进大多数权贵的宫殿，总得把心气留在门口。他的副手被叫了来。那是个普罗旺斯人，爽直，诚实，全无算计。

He flew whither he thought his duty and fortune called him, made a low bow to the Sultan, a lower still to his mare, and wrote everything that the beast vouchsafed to dictate.
I must beg leave to refer those, who are curious to know her discourse, to the archives of Congo. This prince immediately ordered copies of it to be distributed among all his interpreters and professors of foreign languages, both ancient and modern. One said, that it was a scene of some old Greek tragedy, which to him appear’d very moving; another, by the strength of his genius discovered, that it was an important fragment of Egyptian theology: a third pretended, that it was the Exordium of Hannibal’s funeral oration in the Punic language; and a fourth asserted, that the piece was writ in Chinese, and that it was a very devout prayer to Confucius.
While the Literati were trying the Sultan’s patience with their learned conjectures, he recollected Gulliver’s travels, and made no doubt, but that a person, who had lived so long as this Englishman, in an island, where horses have a government, laws, kings, gods, priests, a religion, temples and altars, and who seemed so perfectly well instructed in their manners and customs, was a thorough master of their language. Accordingly Gulliver read and interpreted the mare’s discourse off hand, notwithstanding the orthographical errors, with which it abounded. Nay, it is the only good translation of it in all Congo. Mangogul learned for his own private satisfaction, and for the honour of his system, that it was an historical abridgment of the amours of an old Pacha of three tails with the little mare, which had been attack’d by an infinite number of jackasses before him: a singular anecdote, the truth of which however was not unknown, either to the Sultan, or to any other person at court, at Banza, and in the rest of the empire.

他飞奔到职责同前程召唤他的地方，向苏丹深深一躬，向母马躬得更深，把那畜生肯口授的一切，尽数记下。

凡好奇她这篇议论的，恕我请他去查刚果的档案。这位君主当即命人誊出多份，分发给全体译官同古今外语教授。一位说，这是某部希腊旧悲剧里的一场，看去极动人；另一位凭其天才之力，考出这是埃及神学的重要残篇；第三位断言，这是汉尼拔悼词的 Punic 语开篇；第四位咬定，原文是中文，是一篇对孔子的虔敬祷文。

学者们拿学问的猜测磨苏丹耐心的当口，他记起了《格列佛游记》，毫不怀疑：这位英国人在一座马有政府、法律、君王、神祇、祭司、宗教、庙宇同祭坛的岛上住了那么久，对那里的风俗制度又那样烂熟，必是马语的大师。于是格列佛当场把母马的议论读了出来、译了出来——尽管满纸拼法错误。说真的，那是全刚果唯一的好译本。曼戈古尔为私下的满足、也为他体系的荣营得知：那是一部老三尾帕夏同小母马情史的节略——母马先前曾被无数头公驴进攻过：一桩奇闻，其真实性，无论苏丹本人、还是宫廷、班扎同帝国其余地方的任何人，倒都心里有数。
