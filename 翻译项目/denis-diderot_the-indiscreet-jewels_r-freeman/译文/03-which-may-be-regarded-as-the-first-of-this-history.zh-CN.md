## III Which May Be Regarded as the First of This History / 本章或可视为本书之始
===Original===

Mirzoza had already fixed Mangogul for some years. These lovers had said, and a thousand times repeated, all that a violent passion suggests to persons who have the most wit. They were got as far as confidences, and they would impute it to themselves as a crime, to conceal the most minute circumstance of their lives from each other. These singular suppositions, “If heaven, which has placed me on the throne, had given me an obscure low birth, would you have deign’d to descend down to me, would Mirzoza have crown’d me?”
“Should Mirzoza happen to lose the few charms which she is thought to have, would Mangogul love her still?” These suppositions, I say, which exercise the fancy of ingenious lovers, which sometimes make tender lovers quarrel, and frequently oblige the most sincere lovers to tell untruths, were quite worn out between our pair.
The favorite, who possess’d in a supreme degree, the necessary and uncommon talent of making a good narrative, had drained the scandalous history of Banza. As she had not the best constitution, she was not always disposed to receive the Sultan’s caresses, nor he always in the humour of offering them. In short, there were some days, in which Mangogul and Mirzoza had little to say, hardly anything to do, and in which, without any diminution of love, they amused themselves but indifferently. Those days were rare indeed, but there were some; and this was one of them.
The Sultan was carelessly stretch’d on a sofa, opposite to the favorite, who was knotting in silence. The weather did not permit them to take a walk. Mangogul would not venture to propose a party of piquet; and this posture had lasted near a quarter of an hour, when the Sultan, yawning several times, said, “It must be allowed, that Geliotta sung like an angel.”
“And that your highness is tired to death,” answered the favorite.
“No, Madam,” replied Mangogul, endeavouring to smother a yawn, “the minute that one sees you, is not that of tiresomeness.”
“If that is not a polite compliment, ’tis nobody’s fault but your own,” rejoin’d Mirzoza: “but you ponder, you are absent, you yawn. Prince, what ails you?”
“I know not,” said the Sultan.
“But I guess,” continued the favorite. “I was eighteen, when I had the good fortune to please you. It is full four years since you began to love me. Eighteen and four make twenty-two. Therefore I am now very old.”

米尔佐扎拴住曼戈古尔已有些年头了。这对情侣把炽情所能进给最有机智的人的话，都说过了，又千遍万遍地重复过了。他们已进展到交心的地步，谁若向对方瞒下生平最细小的一桩事，都算犯一重罪。那些奇特的假设——“倘若把我捧上宝座的上天，给了我一个寒微低贱的出身，你还会俯就我么？米尔佐扎还会属意于我么？”“倘若米尔佐扎失掉了人们以为她仅有的那几点姿色，曼戈古尔还会爱她么？”——我说，这些磨砺聪慧情侣想象力的假设，有时叫深情的情侣怄气，又常常逼得最诚实的情侣撒谎，在我们这一对之间早已用得稀烂。

这位宠妃以登峰造极的程度，怀有那门难得而必需的本事——把故事讲得好。她已把班扎那部丑闻史挤干了。她体质不件，不是总乐于承苏丹的爱抚，苏丹也不是总有奉承的兴致。总之，有些日子，曼戈古尔同米尔佐扎没有什么可说，几乎没有什么可做，两人的爱情虽未减损，彼此却兴致寥寥。这样的日子实在少有，可到底有；这一天便是其一。

苏丹懒懒地横在一张沙发上，对面是默然打着结子的宠妃。天气不容他们出门散步。曼戈古尔不敢贸然提议打一场皮克牌。这姿势已持续了近一刻钟，苏丹连打几个呵欠，开口道：“总得承认，杰利奥塔唱得像个天使。”

“也总得承认，殿下您闷得要死。”宠妃回敬。

“不，夫人，”曼戈古尔回道，竭力感住一个呵欠，“见到您的那一分钟，绝不会是无聊的一分钟。”

“这若算不得一句漂亮的恭维，那可怨不得别人，怨您自己，”米尔佐扎顶回去：“您在出神，您心不在焉，您打呵欠。君主，您怎么了？”

“我不知道。”苏丹说。

“可我猜得着，”宠妃接着说。“我有幸博得您欢心时，是十八岁。您爱我至今，足足四年了。十八加四，是二十二。所以呀，我现在很老了。”

Mangogul smiled at this calculation. “But if I am no longer worth anything for pleasure,” added Mirzoza, “I will at least demonstrate that I am very good for advice. The variety of amusements which attend you, has not been able to secure you against disgust. You are disgusted. Prince, there is your disease.”
“I do not allow, that you have hit it off,” says Mangogul: “but supposing you have, do you know a remedy?” Mirzoza answered the Sultan, after a moment’s pause, that his highness seem’d to take so much pleasure at the narratives she made him of the gallantries of the town, that she was sorry she had no more to relate to him, or that she was not better informed of those of the court; that she would have tried that expedient, till she thought of somewhat better. “I think it a good one,” says Mangogul: “But who knows the stories of all those fools; and though they were known to any, who could relate them like you?”
“Let us learn them however,” replied Mirzoza. “Whosoever it be that tells them, I am certain that your highness will gain more by the matter, than you will lose by the form.”
“I shall join with you, if you please, in fancying the adventures of the court ladies very diverting,” says Mangogul: “but though they were to be a hundred times more so, what does that avail, if it be impossible to come at them?”
“There may be a difficulty in it,” answers Mirzoza, “but in my opinion, that is all. The Genius Cucufa, your relation and friend, has done greater things. Why do you not consult him?”
“Ah, joy of my heart!” cried the Sultan, “you are an admirable Creature. I make no doubt but the Genius will employ all his power in my favour. This moment I shut myself up in my closet, and invoke him.”
Accordingly Mangogul arose, kissed the favorite on the left eye, pursuant to the custom of Congo, and departed.

曼戈古尔朝这笔账笑了。“可我若是对于欢乐已一钱不值，”米尔佐扎接着说，“我至少要证明，我对于出主意还大有用处。伴着您的那些五花八门的消遣，竟没能保您不淢。您淢了。君主，您的病根就在这里。”

“我不承认你猜对了，”曼戈古尔说：“可就算猜对了，你可有药方？”米尔佐扎顿了片刻，回禀苏丹说：殿下听她讲城里那些风流的事，听的那样有兴味，只可惜她没有更多的可讲，或者对宫里的事不大知情；她想不出更好的法子之前，本要一直试这条权宜之计的。“我看这主意就好，”曼戈古尔说：“可是谁知道那群傻瓜的故事呢？就算有人知道，谁又能像你这样讲？”

“那也还是可以去打听，”米尔佐扎回道。“不管讲的人是谁，我肯定殿下在这事情上得的，要多过在形式上失的。”

“就算依你，就算宫里太太们的艳遇十分有趣，”曼戈古尔说：“可它们就是再有趣一百倍，若没法子探到手，又有什么用？”

“这里头许有难处，”米尔佐扎答道，“可依我看，难处仅此而已。精灵库库法（Cucufa），您的亲戚兼朋友，干过的事比这大得多。您何不去请教他？”

“啊，我心头的欢喜！”苏丹叫道，“你真是个妙人儿。我不怀疑精灵必定竭尽全力帮我。我这就关进小书房去召他。”

于是曼戈古尔起身，依着刚果的习俗，在宠妃的左眼上亲了一下，出去了。
