## XXII / 二十二

===Original===

Of Truisms: Treated Reasonably

He seemed, indeed, to find food for wonder everywhere. It was as if he had awakened from a dragging nightmare of life made up of unimportant tasks and tedious useless little habits, to see life as it really was, and to rejoice in its exquisite wonderfulness.

How poignantly strange it was that life could afford him nothing save consciousness of the moment immediately at hand! Memory and anticipation, whatever else they might do﻿—and they had important uses, of course, in rousing emotion﻿—yet did not deal directly with reality. What you regretted, or were proud of, having done yesterday was no more real now than the deeds of Caesar Borgia or St. Paul; and what you looked forward to within the half-hour was as nonexistent as the senility of your unborn great-grandchildren. Never was man brought into contact with reality save through the evanescent emotions and sensations of that single moment, that infinitesimal fraction of a second, which was passing now. This commonplace, so simple and so old, bewildered Kennaston when he came unreservedly to recognize its truth.﻿ ﻿…

===Chinese===

论自明之理：理性处之

他仿佛当真在在处处都能觅得惊奇之资。他又像是刚从一场拖沓的人生噩梦中醒来——那梦由种种无足轻重的差事与乏味无用的小习惯织成——一觉醒来，看清了人生的本来面目，并为它那精微入妙的奇妙而欢欣。

人生所能给他的，竟只有对眼面前这一刻的知觉，再无别物——这是何等锥心的奇事！记忆与期待，无论此外还能做些什么——它们自然有大用处，能唤起情感——却并不直接同真实打交道。你昨日做过而此刻引以为悔、或引以为傲的事，其不真实，恰如凯撒·博尔吉亚（Caesar Borgia）或圣保罗（St. Paul）的功业；你盼着半小时内到来的事，也同你那尚未出世的曾孙们的衰朽老境一般，子虚乌有。人从来不曾接触到真实，除非借着那独一无二的一刻、那一秒之中无限小的一份——亦即此刻正在逝去的这瞬息——的倏忽情绪与感觉。这条老生常谈如此简单、又如此古老，待肯纳斯顿毫无保留地认清它的真实时，却不由得叫他目眩神迷。……

===Original===

To live was to be through his senses conscious, one by one, of a restricted number of these fractions of a second. Success in life, then, had nothing to do with bank-accounts or public office, or any step toward increasing the length of one’s obituary notices, but meant to be engrossed utterly by as many as possible of these instants. And complete success required a finding, in these absorbing instants, of employment for every faculty he possessed. It was for this that Kennaston had always vaguely longed; and to this, if only in dreams, he now attained.

If only in dreams he debated: why, and was he not conscious, now, in his dreams, of every moment as it fled? And corporal life in banks and ballrooms and legislative halls and palaces, nowhere had anything more than that to offer mortal men.

It is not necessary to defend his course of reasoning; to the contrary, its fallacy is no less apparent than its conduciveness to unbusinesslike conclusions. But it is highly necessary to tell you that, according to Felix Kennaston’s account, now, turn by turn, he was in Horvendile’s person rapt by nearly every passion, every emotion, the human race has ever known. True, throughout these dramas into which chance plunged him, in that he knew always he was dreaming, he was at once performer and spectator; but he played with the born actor’s zest﻿—feeling his part, as people say﻿—and permitting the passion he portrayed to possess him almost completely.

===Chinese===

所谓活着，便是经由感官，逐一意识到这数目有限的一瞬瞬。如此说来，人生的成功，同银行存款、同官职、同任何足以把讣闻加长一步的举措，皆毫无干系；它意味着：被尽可能多的这样一些刹那全然迷醉。而圆满的成功，还要求在这些摄人的刹那里，为他所拥有的每一分才具都觅得用武之地。肯纳斯顿素来隐隐渴求的正是这个；而如今——哪怕只在梦里——他达到了。

若说只在梦里，他也这样辩难：何以见得？如今他在梦里，难道不也知觉着每一个正在飞逝的时刻么？而肉身的生活——在银行里、舞厅里、议事厅里、宫殿里的生活——无论何处，能拿给凡人的，也超不出这些去。

他这一套推理，无须谁来辩护；恰恰相反，其中的谬误，与其易于引出不合实务的结论，是同样明白的。但有一点极有必要奉告于你：据费利克斯·肯纳斯顿自述，他如今以霍文迪尔之身，挨次被人类有史以来几乎每一种激情、每一种情感摄去了心魂。诚然，在这些机缘把他卷入的戏剧里，正因为他始终知道自己是在做梦，他既是演员又是观众；可他演来带着天生演员的那股热兴——用人们的话说，入了戏——并且容许他所搬演的激情几乎完全占据自己。

===Original===

Almost completely, be it repeated; for there was invariably a sufficient sense of knowing he was only dreaming to prevent entire abandonment to the raw emotion. Kennaston preferred it thus. He preferred in this more comely way to play with human passions, rather than, as seemed the vulgar use, to consent to become their battered plaything.

It pleased him, too, to be able to have done with such sensations and emotions as did not interest him; for he had merely to touch Ettarre, and the dream ended. In this fashion he would very often terminate an existence which was becoming distasteful﻿—resorting debonairly to this sort of suicide, and thus dismissing an era’s social orderings and its great people as toys that, played with, had failed to amuse Felix Kennaston.

===Chinese===

是几乎完全——且容重说一遍；因为他心里始终存着一份足够的自觉，知道自己只是在做梦，足以拦住他整个人委身于那股生猛的情感。肯纳斯顿宁可如此。他宁愿以这种较为体面的方式，同人世的诸般激情嬉戏，而不肯像世人通常的遭际那样，甘心沦为被激情折磨得破烂的玩物。

还有一桩也教他称意：凡是他不感兴趣的感觉与情感，都能随手了结；因为他只消碰一碰埃塔尔，梦便收场了。他常常就这样终结一段渐觉可厌的存在——潇洒地诉诸这一种自杀，就这样把一个时代的社会规制连同它的伟人们当作玩具打发了：这些玩意儿玩是玩过了，却没能逗乐费利克斯·肯纳斯顿。
