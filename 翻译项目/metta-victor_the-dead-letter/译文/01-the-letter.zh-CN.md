## I The Letter / 一 信件
===Original===

I paused suddenly in my work. Over a year’s experience in the Dead Letter office had given a mechanical rapidity to my movements in opening, noting and classifying the contents of the bundles before me; and, so far from there being anything exciting to the curiosity, or interesting to the mind, in the employment, it was of the most monotonous character.
Young ladies whose love letters have gone astray, evil men whose plans have been confided in writing to their confederates, may feel but little apprehension of the prying eyes of the Department; nothing attracts it but objects of material value﻿—sentiment is below par; it gives attention only to such tangible interests as are represented by bank-bills, gold-pieces, checks, jewelry, miniatures, et cetera. Occasionally a grave clerk smiles sardonically at the ridiculous character of some of the articles which come to light; sometimes, perhaps, looks thoughtfully at a withered rosebud, or bunch of pressed violets, a homely little pincushion, or a bookmark, wishing it had reached its proper destination. I can not answer for other employees, who may not have even this amount of heart and imagination to invest in the dull business of a Government office; but when I was in the Department I was guilty, at intervals, of such folly﻿—yet I passed for the coldest, most cynical man of them all.
The letter which I held in my paralyzed fingers when they so abruptly ceased their dexterous movements, was contained in a closely-sealed envelope, yellowed by time, and directed in a peculiar hand to “John Owen, Peekskill, New York,” and the date on the stamp was “October 18th, 1857”﻿—making the letter two years old. I know not what magnetism passed from it, putting me, as the spiritualists say, en rapport with it; I had not yet cut the lappet; and the only thing I could fix upon as the cause of my attraction was, that at the date indicated on the envelope, I had been a resident of Blankville, twenty miles from Peekskill﻿—and something about that date!
Yet this was no excuse for my agitation; I was not of an inquisitive disposition; nor did “John Owen” belong to the circle of my acquaintance. I sat there with such a strange expression upon my face, that one of my fellows, remarking my mood, exclaimed jestingly:
“What is it, Redfield? A check for a hundred thousand?”

我猛地停下手中的活计。在死信处理处一年多的经历，已让我拆信、登记、分类面前一捆捆信件的动作带上了一种机械般的麻利；这差事非但没什么能勾起好奇、引人入胜之处，反倒枯燥乏味到了极点。
年轻小姐们石沉大海的情书，恶人们落在纸上、递与同伙的密谋，都大可不必担心本部门那双窥探的眼睛；除了具有实际价值之物，什么都引不起它的注意——情感在这里一文不值；它只关照那些看得见摸得着的利益，比如钞票、金币、支票、首饰、袖珍画像等等。偶尔，某位一本正经的文书会对冒出来的某件荒唐物件露出一丝冷笑；有时，或许还会若有所思地望着一朵枯萎的玫瑰花蕾、一小束压干的紫罗兰、一只家常的小针插或一枚书签，但愿它当初送到了该去的人手里。别的雇员如何，我不好代答——他们兴许连这一点点心思和想象，都不肯投进政府机关这桩乏味的营生里；可我在部里那阵子，隔三差五就要犯一回这样的傻气——饶是这样，我在众人眼里还是最冷淡、最刻薄的一个。
我的手指本来灵活麻利，却陡然僵住不动；攥在指间的这封信，装在一只封得严严实实的信封里，信封已被岁月熏黄，上面以一种特别的笔迹写着"纽约州皮克斯基尔，约翰·欧文收"，邮戳上的日期是"一八五七年十月十八日"——算来这封信已经搁了两年。我说不清有什么磁力从信里透出来，用唯灵论者的话说，把我与它"接上了灵"；我还没裁开封盖呢；我能认定的唯一缘由是：信封上标着的那个日子，我正住在布兰克维尔，离皮克斯基尔二十英里——还有那个日子本身的某种名堂！
然而这不足以解释我心头的激动；我本不是个好窥探的性子；"约翰·欧文"也与我的交际圈子无涉。我就那么坐着，脸上神情古怪，一位同僚瞧出我的模样，打趣道：
"怎么了，雷德菲尔德？一张十万元的支票？"

“I am sure I don’t know; I haven’t opened it,” I answered, at random; and with this I cut the wrapper, impelled by some strongly-defined, irresistible influence to read the time-stained sheet enclosed. It ran in this wise:

> “**Dear Sir**﻿—It’s too bad to disappoint you. Could not execute your order, as everybody concerned will discover. What a charming day!﻿—good for taking a picture. That old friend I introduced you to won’t tell tales, and you had not better bother yourself to visit him. The next time you find yourself in his arms, don’t feel in his left-hand pocket for the broken toothpick which I lent him. He is welcome to it. If you’re at the place of payment, I shan’t be there, not having fulfilled the order, and having given up my emigration project, much against my will; so, govern yourself accordingly. Sorry your prospects are so poor, and believe me, with the greatest possible esteem,
> “Your disappointed **Negotiator**.”

To explain why this brief epistle, neither lucid nor interesting in itself, should affect me as it did, I must go back to the time at which it was written.

"我实在不知道；还没拆呢。"我随口答道；说着便裁开封套，被一股轮廓分明、无可抗拒的力道驱使着，去读里头那张为时光染污的信纸。信上是这么写的：

> "**亲爱的先生**——叫您失望，实在抱歉。您的吩咐无法办到，相关人等自会明白。多么宜人的一天！——正宜拍照。我引荐给您的老朋友不会走漏风声，您也不必劳神再去拜访他。下回您落进他怀里，可别再往他左边口袋里摸那根断牙签，那是我借给他的。就送他好了。您若到了付款的地方，我可不在场，因为吩咐没办成，移民的计划也违背本意地作罢了；所以，您好自为之。遗憾您的前景如此黯淡，并请相信我怀着十二万分的敬意，
> 您失望的**经办人**"

这封短信本身既不明晰，也乏趣味，却叫我那样震动——要解释个中缘由，我得回到它落笔的那个年月。
