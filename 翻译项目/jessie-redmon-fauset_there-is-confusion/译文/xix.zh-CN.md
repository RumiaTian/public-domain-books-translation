## XIX / 十九

===Original===
One of the mysteries of the ages will be solved with the answer to the question: Why do men consider women incalculable? Peter had been hurt by Joanna’s indifference again and again, she had refused a dozen times to marry him, she had scolded him, teased him, slighted him. Yet she had always come back to his eager arms. In spite of this he had been unable to see in her attitude at Christmas and in the unkind letter which she had written the logical outcome of her earlier acts﻿—all of which by enduring he had tacitly endorsed.

He read the letter in a maze of anger and wounded pride. Before he knew it he had caught up his cap and started for Maggie’s house. By the time the long, yellow, crawling car had jolted him over the uneven reaches of Lombard Street and set him down at Fifteenth he was in a fever of bitterness, resentment and self-pity. Maggie hardly knew him when he entered her little sitting-room.

===Chinese===
亘古谜团之一，一旦回答了下面这个问题便会告破：男人为什么认为女人不可捉摸？彼得被乔安娜的冷淡伤了一回又一回，她十几次拒绝嫁给他，骂过他、逗弄过他、冷落过他。可她总会回到他热切张开的怀抱里。尽管如此，他却始终没能看出来，她在圣诞节时的态度、她写来的那封狠心的信，正是她先前种种作为的必然结果——那些作为，他都一一忍了下来，也就等于默默认可了。

他满腔怒火、自尊受创，昏头涨脑地把那封信读了一遍。不知不觉间，他已抓起帽子，动身往玛吉家去。等那辆长长的、黄澄澄、慢吞吞的电车载着他，在隆巴德街高低不平的一路上颠簸到十五街口把他放下，他心里已经烧起一团又苦、又怨、又自怜的火。走进她那间小客厅时，玛吉几乎认不出他来。

===Original===
“Oh, Peter,” she went up to him swiftly, “something awful has happened.”

He showed her the letter, striding up and down the room as she read it.

She lifted her head to say to him: “She doesn’t mean it; you know Joanna, always making a mountain out of a molehill.”

Instead she heard herself saying: “How could she possibly write such things to you﻿—you’ve always been so kind.”

她抬起头，本想对他说：「她不是当真的；你了解乔安娜的为人，她总是小题大做。」

出口的却是：「她怎么可能给你写这样的话——你一向待她那么好。」

===Original===
“Too kind,” he muttered. “I tell you what, Maggie, Joanna’s got no heart, she’s all head, all ideas and if you don’t see and act her way, she’s got no use for you.”

“I do think she thinks herself a lot better than anyone else,” Maggie said slowly, remembering Joanna’s letter to her about Philip.

“Well, she is, you know,” he put in unexpectedly. “Oh, Lord, what am I going to do without her!”

Genuinely touched, she sat down on the little box-couch beside him and slid her arm around his shoulder. “After all, you’ve still got me, Peter.”

===Chinese===
「就是太好了，」他咕哝道，「我跟你说吧，玛吉，乔安娜没有心，她全是脑子、全是主张，你要是不按她的眼光看事、照她的路子行事，她就用不着你了。」

「我倒真觉得，她自以为比谁都强得多。」玛吉慢吞吞地说，心里想起乔安娜写给她谈菲利普的那封信。

「这个嘛，她确实是比谁都强，你是知道的。」他出其不意地插了一句。「唉，天哪，没有她我可怎么好！」

她真心动了情，在他身旁那只小巧的箱柜式沙发上坐下，一条胳膊揽上他的肩膀。「说到底，你还有我呢，彼得。」

===Original===
He looked up at her, feeling the surge of a new idea in his heart. If he could only punish Joanna﻿—no not punish exactly, you couldn’t punish her, she was always too remote for that﻿—but shock her, let her see, as his boyhood’s phrase would have had it, that she was not the only pebble on the beach. Besides, what a revenge to cut loose altogether from the influence of her ideals and ally himself with one whom she would have characterized as having no ideals at all.

Before the thought was even shaped in his brain he was speaking:

“Of course I always have you, Maggie. How﻿—how would you like to spend your future with me?”

“What do you mean, Peter?”

===Chinese===
他抬眼望她，心头涌起一个新念头。要是能惩罚一下乔安娜就好了——不，说惩罚也不尽然，你没法惩罚她，她永远太超然，够不着——那就震她一震，让她瞧瞧，照他少年时代那句口头禅的说法，海滩上的石子可不止她一颗。再说，彻底斩断她那些理想对自己的影响，去同一个在她口中根本毫无理想可言的人结盟，这是何等痛快的一场报复。

这念头还没在脑子里成形，话已出了口：

「我当然一直都有你，玛吉。你——你可愿意同我共度往后的日子？」

「你这是什么意思，彼得？」

===Original===
“I mean, Joanna’s chucked me. You and I get along famously, you’ve got your divorce from Neal. Why not marry me?”

It was plain that though surprised she liked the idea. She saw herself suddenly transformed in this inhospitable snobbish city from Maggie Neal, alone and *déclassée*, into Mrs. Peter Bye, a model of respectability.

That he had no money, no accepted means of making a livelihood she understood would mean nothing. He was a Bye and she as his wife could go anywhere. She would show Alice Talbert! And afterwards when he got his degree!

But because she had once loved Philip she could judge what Peter might mean to Joanna. To her credit she hesitated.

===Chinese===
「我是说，乔安娜把我甩了。你我相处得这么融洽，你跟尼尔的婚也离了。干吗不嫁给我？」

明眼人一看便知，她虽觉意外，心里却喜欢这个主意。她仿佛看见自己在这座冷漠势利的城市里骤然变了身份：不再是孤零零、落魄失级（*déclassée*）的玛吉·尼尔，而成了彼得·拜太太——规矩体面的典范。

他没有钱，也没有什么拿得出手的正当营生，她明白这些都无关紧要。他是拜家的人，她做了他的妻子，什么样的场合都去得。她要让艾丽斯·塔尔伯特瞧瞧！还有，等他日后拿了学位！

但正因为她曾爱过菲利普，她掂量得出彼得对乔安娜意味着什么。难得的是，她还迟疑了一下。

===Original===
“Joanna probably doesn’t mean to let you go, Peter, she’s just angry and disappointed. She takes things harder than Sylvia or I. You know she really cares about you, and so do you about her.”

But he assured her that he did not. “She’s too exacting. Now there’s one thing about you, Maggie﻿—maybe it’s because you’ve already been married﻿—you know how to treat a man. Joanna makes you feel as though you were in a straitjacket all the time. I always feel ordinary when I’m with you.”

Neither of them noticed the doubtfulness of the compliment. In the end she accepted him. After all, she owed nothing to Joanna, who certainly had not considered her. How surprised she would be to think that Peter could so quickly find solace in her﻿—Maggie’s﻿—arms! And Joanna should learn, too, that he could become a success without everlastingly being pushed and prodded.

Hard on this thought came another. “Peter, you won’t have to work so hard now to get through school. I’ll help you. You know I’m doing very well with the hair-work.”

「乔安娜多半不是真要放你走，彼得，她只是一时生气、一时失望。她比西尔维娅和我都更容易把事情往心里去。你知道她真在乎你，你也真在乎她。」

可他一口咬定并非如此。「她太苛求了。你呢，玛吉，有一样好处——兴许是因为你结过一回婚——你知道怎么对待男人。乔安娜让你觉得仿佛时时刻刻穿着拘束衣。跟你在一起，我总觉得自己就是个平平常常的人。」

两人谁也没留意这句恭维话里的可疑之处。到头来她还是接受了他。说到底，她不欠乔安娜什么，乔安娜也压根没为她着想过。一想到彼得竟能这么快就在她——她玛吉的——怀抱里寻得慰藉，乔安娜该会多么吃惊！乔安娜也该领教领教：不必没完没了地又被推又被撵，他照样能出人头地。

这个念头刚落，紧跟着又冒出一个。「彼得，如今你把书念完，就不必再那么拼命苦干了。我来帮你。你知道的，我做头发这行营生做得挺好。」

===Original===
Afterwards, back in his room with the green iron bedstead and the Bye Bible, he felt a difference, a sense of let-downness. He threw himself across the bed and groaned, “Joanna, how could you?”

She could, that was evident. He was stupefied at the turn in his affairs. Five hours ago he had expected some day to be a physician and to marry Joanna Marshall. Now it seemed that he was going to be a musician and marry Maggie Neal.

“It isn’t true,” he told himself, fiercely. But it was true. There on the dresser were some cookies wrapped up in a red and white fringed napkin, Maggie’s gift when he left her.

===Chinese===
后来，回到自己那间摆着绿铁床架和拜家圣经的屋子，他觉出一种异样，一种泄了气似的失落。他一头横倒在床上，呻吟道：「乔安娜，你怎么能这样？」

她能，这是明摆着的。他让自己事情的这一转折惊得目瞪口呆。五个钟头以前，他还指望有朝一日当上医生、娶了乔安娜·马歇尔。如今看来，他是要当音乐家、娶玛吉·尼尔了。

「这不是真的，」他狠狠地对自己说。可这是真的。梳妆台上正摆着一包饼干，用一条红白相间的流苏餐巾裹着，是他离开她时玛吉送的礼物。

“I made them for you, hoping you would come in. Now you’ll be in often, often, won’t you? Oh, Peter, I’ll be good to you. I’ll be as unlike Joanna as possible.” He did not want her to be unlike Joanna. In fact, he did not want her at all.

He might as well take her, though, for Joanna did not want him. That was it, no matter how many women he unaccountably married, Joanna might be shocked but she would never really care. Or suppose she did care a little while, she would soon forget it with her singing and dancing. Still, he supposed he must tell her. He would write her a gay, mocking letter. “I hope you’ll be as happy with your art as I feel I shall be with Maggie. She suits me perfectly.”

After he had littered his desk and the floor beside it vainly with a veritable snowstorm of torn bits of paper, he let his head drop on his lean brown hands and went to sleep. Perhaps it would not be exact to say he cried himself to sleep, but there were certainly tears that burnt and scalded behind his eyelids.

His landlady complained of the torn paper the next morning. “ ’Tisn’t as though you didn’t have a nice wastepaper basket ready and waitin’, Mr. Bye.” As she finished speaking she handed him Joanna’s letter containing Goethe’s poem. The tenderness, the real love that blazed in the beautiful lines overwhelmed him. He could not tell her the truth after a letter like that. So he wrote her, postponing but hinting, he fondly believed, at the news which he must soon break to her. A month later, finding himself still unequal to the task, he wrote to Sylvia.

他把书桌和桌边的地板徒然撒满了碎纸片，简直下了一场纸屑的暴风雪；随后他把头垂在瘦削的棕色双手上，睡着了。若说他哭着哭着睡着了，也许并不确切，但眼皮底下确实有泪水在灼灼滚烫地烧着。

第二天早上，女房东抱怨那些碎纸。「又不是没给您备着一只好好的废纸篓，现现成成地等着您用呢，拜先生。」她话音刚落，便递给他乔安娜的来信，信里抄着歌德（Goethe）的那首诗。美丽的诗行里灼灼燃烧着的柔情，那份真爱，将他彻底压倒了。接到这样一封信之后，他没法把实情告诉她了。于是他给她回信，一面把这事往后拖，一面又——他一厢情愿地以为——暗示了他不久便不得不向她挑明的那个消息。一个月后，发觉自己仍旧开不了这个口，他便写信给西尔维娅。
