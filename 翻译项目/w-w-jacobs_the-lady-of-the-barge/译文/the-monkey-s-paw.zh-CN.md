## The Monkey’s Paw / 猴爪

### I / 一

===Original===
Without, the night was cold and wet, but in the small parlour of Laburnam Villa the blinds were drawn and the fire burned brightly. Father and son were at chess, the former, who possessed ideas about the game involving radical changes, putting his king into such sharp and unnecessary perils that it even provoked comment from the white-haired old lady knitting placidly by the fire.

“Hark at the wind,” said Mr. White, who, having seen a fatal mistake after it was too late, was amiably desirous of preventing his son from seeing it.

“I’m listening,” said the latter, grimly surveying the board as he stretched out his hand. “Check.”

“I should hardly think that he’d come tonight,” said his father, with his hand poised over the board.

“Mate,” replied the son.

“That’s the worst of living so far out,” bawled Mr. White, with sudden and unlooked-for violence; “of all the beastly, slushy, out-of-the-way places to live in, this is the worst. Pathway’s a bog, and the road’s a torrent. I don’t know what people are thinking about. I suppose because only two houses in the road are let, they think it doesn’t matter.”

===Chinese===
屋外，夜又冷又湿；拉伯纳姆别墅的小客厅里，百叶窗拉得严严实实，炉火烧得正旺。父子俩正下棋。当爹的对这棋自有一套大刀阔斧的革新主张，把自家老王一次又一次送进凶险而全无必要的险境，连坐在炉边安然织毛线的白发老太太，也忍不住评说了几句。

「听听这风。」怀特先生说。他的一步致命错着，看清时已经太迟，这会儿正一团和气地惦记着，别叫儿子也看出来。

「听着呢。」儿子说着，一边伸手探向棋盘，一边冷冷地打量局面。「将军。」

「我看他今晚多半不来了。」父亲说着，手悬在棋盘上方。

「将死。」儿子应道。

「住得这么偏远，坏处全在这儿了，」怀特先生突然发起火来，那股凶劲儿来得没头没脑，「天底下又阴又烂、前不着村后不着店的住处全数遍了，数这儿最糟。小路是烂泥塘，大路是激流。真不知道人们是怎么想的。我琢磨着，是因为这条路上一共才租出去两户人家，人家就觉得怎么着都无所谓了。」

===Original===
“Never mind, dear,” said his wife, soothingly; “perhaps you’ll win the next one.”

Mr. White looked up sharply, just in time to intercept a knowing glance between mother and son. The words died away on his lips, and he hid a guilty grin in his thin grey beard.

“There he is,” said Herbert White, as the gate banged to loudly and heavy footsteps came toward the door.

The old man rose with hospitable haste, and opening the door, was heard condoling with the new arrival. The new arrival also condoled with himself, so that Mrs. White said, “Tut, tut!” and coughed gently as her husband entered the room, followed by a tall, burly man, beady of eye and rubicund of visage.

“Sergeant-Major Morris,” he said, introducing him.

The sergeant-major shook hands, and taking the proffered seat by the fire, watched contentedly while his host got out whiskey and tumblers and stood a small copper kettle on the fire.

===Chinese===
「别往心里去，亲爱的，」他太太劝慰道，「下一盘说不定你就赢了。」

怀特先生猛地抬头，正好截住母子俩交换的一个心照不宣的眼色。话到嘴边又咽了回去，他把一点心虚的嬉笑藏进花白的稀胡子里。

「他来了。」赫伯特·怀特说。院门砰的一声大响，沉重的脚步声朝门口过来。

老人满怀待客的热忱，急忙起身开门，只听得他在门口跟新来的客人寒暄慰问；那客人也顾自诉起苦来，怀特太太不由「啧啧」两声，在丈夫进屋时轻轻咳了一下。丈夫身后跟着个高大魁伟的男人，一双圆溜溜的小眼睛，一张红彤彤的脸膛。

「莫里斯军士长。」他把客人向大家作了介绍。

军士长握过手，在炉边让出的座位上坐定，心满意足地瞧着主人取出威士忌和大玻璃杯，又把一只小铜壶坐上炉火。

===Original===
At the third glass his eyes got brighter, and he began to talk, the little family circle regarding with eager interest this visitor from distant parts, as he squared his broad shoulders in the chair and spoke of wild scenes and doughty deeds; of wars and plagues and strange peoples.

“Twenty-one years of it,” said Mr. White, nodding at his wife and son. “When he went away he was a slip of a youth in the warehouse. Now look at him.”

“He don’t look to have taken much harm,” said Mrs. White, politely.

“I’d like to go to India myself,” said the old man, “just to look round a bit, you know.”

“Better where you are,” said the sergeant-major, shaking his head. He put down the empty glass, and sighing softly, shook it again.

“I should like to see those old temples and fakirs and jugglers,” said the old man. “What was that you started telling me the other day about a monkey’s paw or something, Morris?”

===Chinese===
第三杯下肚，他眼里放起光来，话也开了头。他把宽阔的肩膀在椅子里端端正正一端，讲起蛮荒的景象和骁勇的作为；讲起战争、瘟疫和种种奇风异俗的民族。小小一家人围坐一旁，热切地打量这位远方来的客人。

「他这一去就是二十一年，」怀特先生朝太太和儿子点头示意，「走的时候，还是货栈里一个细条条的小伙子。你再瞧瞧他如今。」

「瞧着倒不像吃了多少苦头。」怀特太太客客气气地说。

「我倒想自己去一趟印度，」老人说，「就是四处随便开开眼，你明白的。」

「还是原地不动的好。」军士长说着摇了摇头。他放下空杯子，轻轻叹了口气，又摇了摇头。

「我倒想看看那些古庙、托钵僧和要把戏的，」老人说，「前几天你起了个头要讲给我听的那件事——猴爪还是什么的——到底是怎么回事，莫里斯？」

===Original===
“Nothing,” said the soldier, hastily. “Leastways nothing worth hearing.”

“Monkey’s paw?” said Mrs. White, curiously.

“Well, it’s just a bit of what you might call magic, perhaps,” said the sergeant-major, offhandedly.

His three listeners leaned forward eagerly. The visitor absentmindedly put his empty glass to his lips and then set it down again. His host filled it for him.

“To look at,” said the sergeant-major, fumbling in his pocket, “it’s just an ordinary little paw, dried to a mummy.”

===Chinese===
「没什么，」军人急忙说，「至少，没什么值得听的。」

「猴爪？」怀特太太好奇地问。

「唔，也就是一小点儿你们所谓的魔法罢，兴许。」军士长随口道。

三位听客一齐热切地探身向前。客人恍恍惚惚把空杯举到唇边，又放下了。主人替他斟满。

「论模样，」军士长一面在口袋里摸索一面说，「不过是只平平常常的小爪子，干得跟木乃伊似的。」

===Original===
He took something out of his pocket and proffered it. Mrs. White drew back with a grimace, but her son, taking it, examined it curiously.

“And what is there special about it?” inquired Mr. White as he took it from his son, and having examined it, placed it upon the table.

“It had a spell put on it by an old fakir,” said the sergeant-major, “a very holy man. He wanted to show that fate ruled people’s lives, and that those who interfered with it did so to their sorrow. He put a spell on it so that three separate men could each have three wishes from it.”

His manner was so impressive that his hearers were conscious that their light laughter jarred somewhat.

“Well, why don’t you have three, sir?” said Herbert White, cleverly.

===Chinese===
他从口袋里掏出一样东西递过来。怀特太太做了个怪相直往后缩，她儿子接过去，好奇地端详起来。

「它有什么特别的呢？」怀特先生一面从儿子手里接过来看，一面问；看过之后，把它搁在桌上。

「它叫一位老托钵僧施过咒，」军士长说，「是位非常神圣的人。他想叫人看明白：命运主宰着人的一生，凡是插手干预的，没有不吃苦头。他对它下了咒，好叫先后三个男人，每人从它那儿得到三个愿望。」

他的神气郑重得很，几个听客自己都觉得方才那阵轻飘飘的笑声有些刺耳。

「那么，您自己怎么不照样许三个，先生？」赫伯特·怀特乖觉地说。

===Original===
The soldier regarded him in the way that middle age is wont to regard presumptuous youth. “I have,” he said, quietly, and his blotchy face whitened.

“And did you really have the three wishes granted?” asked Mrs. White.

“I did,” said the sergeant-major, and his glass tapped against his strong teeth.

“And has anybody else wished?” persisted the old lady.

“The first man had his three wishes. Yes,” was the reply; “I don’t know what the first two were, but the third was for death. That’s how I got the paw.”

===Chinese===
军人拿中年人看不知天高地厚的后生惯有的那副眼光打量他。「我许过了。」他平静地说，那张斑斑点点的大脸泛上白色。

「那三个愿望当真都应验了？」怀特太太问。

「应验了。」军士长说；玻璃杯磕在他坚实的牙齿上。

「还有别人许过吗？」老太太追问。

「头一个人就许足了他的三个愿。是啊，」对方这样回答，「头两个愿是什么我不清楚，第三个愿求的是死。这只爪子就是这么落到我手里的。」

===Original===
His tones were so grave that a hush fell upon the group.

“If you’ve had your three wishes, it’s no good to you now, then, Morris,” said the old man at last. “What do you keep it for?”

The soldier shook his head. “Fancy, I suppose,” he said, slowly. “I did have some idea of selling it, but I don’t think I will. It has caused enough mischief already. Besides, people won’t buy. They think it’s a fairy tale; some of them, and those who do think anything of it want to try it first and pay me afterward.”

“If you could have another three wishes,” said the old man, eyeing him keenly, “would you have them?”

“I don’t know,” said the other. “I don’t know.”

He took the paw, and dangling it between his forefinger and thumb, suddenly threw it upon the fire. White, with a slight cry, stooped down and snatched it off.

===Chinese===
他的语调沉重得很，一屋子人都静了。

「既然你三个愿都许过了，这东西如今对你也没用了，莫里斯，」老人终于开口，「你还留着它做什么？」

军人摇了摇头。「兴许是图个新鲜劲儿吧，」他慢慢地说，「我原想过把它卖了，如今又不想卖了。它闯下的祸已经够多了。再说，也没人肯买。有人只当它是童话；就算有几个当真的，也都想先试过灵不灵，再付我钱。」

「要是还能再给你三个愿，」老人锐利地盯着他，「你还肯要吗？」

「不知道，」对方说，「不知道。」

他拿起那只爪子，用食指和拇指拈着晃了晃，冷不丁扔进了火里。怀特轻呼一声，弯下腰一把从火里抢了出来。

===Original===
“Better let it burn,” said the soldier, solemnly.

“If you don’t want it, Morris,” said the other, “give it to me.”

“I won’t,” said his friend, doggedly. “I threw it on the fire. If you keep it, don’t blame me for what happens. Pitch it on the fire again like a sensible man.”

The other shook his head and examined his new possession closely. “How do you do it?” he inquired.

“Hold it up in your right hand and wish aloud,” said the sergeant-major, “but I warn you of the consequences.”

“Sounds like the *Arabian Nights*,” said Mrs. White, as she rose and began to set the supper. “Don’t you think you might wish for four pairs of hands for me?”

===Chinese===
「还是让它烧了的好。」军人郑重地说。

「你不要它，莫里斯，」做主人的说，「就给我吧。」

「不给，」他的朋友执拗地说，「我把它扔进火了。你要留下它，往后出了什么事，可别怪我。做个明白人，把它扔回火里去。」

对方摇了摇头，把自己新到手的这件物件细细端详了一番。「怎么个许法？」他问。

「用右手举着它，把愿大声说出来，」军士长说，「不过后果如何，我可是警告过你了。」

「听着倒像《一千零一夜》，」怀特太太说着起身去摆晚饭，「你不觉得该许愿给我添四双手吗？」

===Original===
Her husband drew the talisman from pocket, and then all three burst into laughter as the sergeant-major, with a look of alarm on his face, caught him by the arm.

“If you must wish,” he said, gruffly, “wish for something sensible.”

Mr. White dropped it back in his pocket, and placing chairs, motioned his friend to the table. In the business of supper the talisman was partly forgotten, and afterward the three sat listening in an enthralled fashion to a second instalment of the soldier’s adventures in India.

“If the tale about the monkey’s paw is not more truthful than those he has been telling us,” said Herbert, as the door closed behind their guest, just in time for him to catch the last train, “we shan’t make much out of it.”

“Did you give him anything for it, father?” inquired Mrs. White, regarding her husband closely.

“A trifle,” said he, colouring slightly. “He didn’t want it, but I made him take it. And he pressed me again to throw it away.”

===Chinese===
她丈夫把那件护身符从口袋里掏了出来——军士长登时一脸惊惶，一把抓住他的胳膊——三个人不由得哄堂大笑。

「真要许愿，」他粗声粗气地说，「也许点正经的东西。」

怀特先生把它揣回口袋，摆好椅子，请朋友入席。张罗晚饭的当口，护身符被忘了个半截；饭后，三个人又听得出了神——军人讲起他在印度历险的续篇。

「要是猴爪的事不比他方才讲的这些更可靠，」客人前脚出门——正赶得上末班火车——赫伯特就说，「咱们也指望不上从它那儿得什么好处。」

「你给他钱了吗，孩子他爹？」怀特太太紧盯着丈夫问。

「一点小意思，」他说，脸微微一红，「他不肯要，我硬塞给了他。他又催了我一回，叫我把它扔了。」

===Original===
“Likely,” said Herbert, with pretended horror. “Why, we’re going to be rich, and famous and happy. Wish to be an emperor, father, to begin with; then you can’t be henpecked.”

He darted round the table, pursued by the maligned Mrs. White armed with an antimacassar.

Mr. White took the paw from his pocket and eyed it dubiously. “I don’t know what to wish for, and that’s a fact,” he said, slowly. “It seems to me I’ve got all I want.”

“If you only cleared the house, you’d be quite happy, wouldn’t you?” said Herbert, with his hand on his shoulder. “Well, wish for two hundred pounds, then; that’ll just do it.”

His father, smiling shamefacedly at his own credulity, held up the talisman, as his son, with a solemn face, somewhat marred by a wink at his mother, sat down at the piano and struck a few impressive chords.

“I wish for two hundred pounds,” said the old man distinctly.

===Chinese===
「那还用说。」赫伯特装出一脸惊恐，「嘿，咱们就要发财了，又要出名又要享福了。爹，头一个愿就许当皇帝吧；那样一来，你就再也受不着老婆的辖治了。」

他绕着桌子飞跑，身后是那位蒙了不白之冤的怀特太太，抡着一条椅背搭巾追个不停。

怀特先生从口袋里摸出猴爪，将信将疑地端详着。「许什么愿好，我还真说不上，这是实话，」他慢吞吞地说，「照我看，我要的东西都已齐了。」

「只要把这房子上的欠账还清，你就万事如意了，不是吗？」赫伯特一手搭着他肩膀说，「那就许两百镑吧；这个数，刚刚够。」

父亲暗笑自己的轻信，不好意思地举起护身符；儿子一脸庄严地在钢琴前坐下，敲出几个郑重的和弦——只是朝他母亲那边挤了挤眼，庄严里掺了点水分。

「我许愿要两百镑。」老人一字一句地说。

===Original===
A fine crash from the piano greeted the words, interrupted by a shuddering cry from the old man. His wife and son ran toward him.

“It moved,” he cried, with a glance of disgust at the object as it lay on the floor.

“Well, I don’t see the money,” said his son as he picked it up and placed it on the table, “and I bet I never shall.”

“It must have been your fancy, father,” said his wife, regarding him anxiously.

They sat down by the fire again while the two men finished their pipes. Outside, the wind was higher than ever, and the old man started nervously at the sound of a door banging upstairs. A silence unusual and depressing settled upon all three, which lasted until the old couple rose to retire for the night.

His hand grasped the monkey’s paw, and with a little shiver he wiped his hand on his coat and went up to bed.

### II / 二

===Original===
In the brightness of the wintry sun next morning as it streamed over the breakfast table he laughed at his fears. There was an air of prosaic wholesomeness about the room which it had lacked on the previous night, and the dirty, shrivelled little paw was pitched on the sideboard with a carelessness which betokened no great belief in its virtues.

“I suppose all old soldiers are the same,” said Mrs. White. “The idea of our listening to such nonsense! How could wishes be granted in these days? And if they could, how could two hundred pounds hurt you, father?”

“Might drop on his head from the sky,” said the frivolous Herbert.

“Morris said the things happened so naturally,” said his father, “that you might if you so wished attribute it to coincidence.”

“Well, don’t break into the money before I come back,” said Herbert as he rose from the table. “I’m afraid it’ll turn you into a mean, avaricious man, and we shall have to disown you.”

His mother laughed, and following him to the door, watched him down the road; and returning to the breakfast table, was very happy at the expense of her husband’s credulity. All of which did not prevent her from scurrying to the door at the postman’s knock, nor prevent her from referring somewhat shortly to retired sergeant-majors of bibulous habits when she found that the post brought a tailor’s bill.

===Chinese===
第二天早晨，冬日明亮的阳光洒满早餐桌，他想起夜里的害怕，不由得失笑。房间里透着一股乏味而爽利的家常气象，昨夜缺的就是这个；那只又脏又皱的小爪子被随手撂在餐具柜上，那份随意，足见没谁再拿它的灵验当回事。

「我看老当兵的都是一路货，」怀特太太说，「亏得咱们还听那种胡话！这年头，愿望还能应验？就算能，两百镑又怎么伤得了你，孩子他爹？」

「兴许从天上掉下来，正砸他脑袋上。」没正形的赫伯特说。

「莫里斯说，那些事来得自然得很，」他父亲说，「你只要愿意，尽可以说是碰巧。」

「得，我回来之前你可别动那笔钱，」赫伯特起身离桌时说，「我怕它把你变成个抠门儿的财迷，那我们只好不认你这个爹了。」

他母亲笑着送他到门口，望着他顺着大路走远；回到早餐桌上，还拿丈夫的轻信开心了好一阵。可这并不妨碍邮差一敲门她就一路小跑奔过去，也不妨碍她发现送来的是裁缝的账单时，把那些好杯中物的退休军士长没好气地数落了几句。
