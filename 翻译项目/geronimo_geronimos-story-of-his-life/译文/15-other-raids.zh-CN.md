## X Other Raids / 十 其他袭掠

===Original===
When reading the foregoing chapters of Apache raids one not acquainted with the lawlessness of the frontier might wonder how this tendency of the Apaches was developed to such a marked degree; but one acquainted with the real conditions﻿—the disregard for law by both Mexicans and white men along the border line of Old Mexico and Arizona in early days﻿—can readily understand where the Apache got his education in the art of conducting lawless raids. In order, therefore, that those who are unacquainted with the conditions as they were in southern Arizona during the eighties, may understand the environment of the Apaches, this chapter is given. The events herein narrated are taken by the author from many accounts given him by reliable men who lived in this section of country during the period mentioned.

===Chinese===
读罢前几章阿帕奇人的袭掠，不了解边疆不法之风的读者，也许会诧异阿帕奇人的这种习性何以发展到如此地步；但凡了解真实状况的人——早年老墨西哥与亚利桑那（Arizona）边界沿线，墨西哥人和白人同样视法律如无物——便不难明白，阿帕奇人这无法无天的袭掠之术是从哪里学来的。因此，为了让不了解 19 世纪 80 年代南亚利桑那实况的读者认识阿帕奇人所处的环境，笔者特编本章。本章所述事件，均由笔者取自当事时期居住在该地区的可靠人士提供的诸多记述。

#### Raid by White Men / 白人的袭掠

===Original===
In 1882 a company of six Mexican traders, who were known as “smugglers” because they evaded duties on goods which they brought into United States and sold in Arizona, were camped in Skeleton Canyon, ten miles north of the north line of Old Mexico. They were known to carry large sums of money, but as they were always armed and ready to defend their possessions they were not often molested. However, on this occasion, just as they were rising in the morning to prepare their breakfast, five white men opened fire on them from ambush and all save one of the Mexicans were killed. This one, though wounded, finally made his escape. A few days after the killing some cowboys on a roundup camped at this place and buried the remains (what the coyotes had left) of these five Mexicans. Two years later, at the same place, a cowboy found a leather bag containing seventy-two Mexican dollars, which small amount of money had been overlooked by the robbers.

===Chinese===
1882 年，六个墨西哥贩子结伙在骷髅峡谷（Skeleton Canyon）扎营，那地方在老墨西哥北界以北十英里。这些人因把货物运进美国、在亚利桑那贩卖而偷逃关税，人称「走私贩」。他们素来身怀巨款，但因其常年携械、随时准备护财，很少遭人打劫。然而这一次，正当他们清早起身预备早饭时，五个白人自埋伏中向他们开火，六个墨西哥人除一个外全部毙命。那一个虽负了伤，终究逃脱了。命案发生几天后，几个围拢畜群的牧牛人到此地扎营，掩埋了这五个墨西哥人的残骸（郊狼吃剩的部分）。两年后，一个牧牛人在同一地点拾到一只皮袋，内装七十二枚墨西哥银元——这笔小钱，是劫匪们漏下的。

===Original===
The men who did this killing lived in Arizona for many years afterwards, and although it was known that they had committed the depredation, no arrests followed, and no attempt was made by any of the Mexicans to recover the property of their fellow citizens.

===Chinese===
行凶杀人的那几个白人，此后在亚利桑那住了许多年；尽管人人都知道这桩劫案是他们干的，却无人被捕，墨西哥人方面也没有任何人试图为本国公民追回财物。

#### Mexican Raid / 墨西哥人的袭掠

===Original===
In 1884 a cattleman and four cowboys from his ranch started to drive some fat cattle to market at Tombstone, Arizona. The route they took led partly through Old Mexico and partly through Arizona. One night they camped in a canyon just south of the Mexican border. Next morning at daylight, the cowboy who had been on herd duty the last half of the night had just come in and aroused the camp when the Mexicans opened fire on them from ambush. The cattleman and one of the cowboys were severely wounded at the first volley and took shelter behind the camp wagon, from which position they fired as long as their ammunition lasted. The other three were only slightly wounded and reached cover, but only one escaped with his life. He remained in hiding for two days before his comrades found him. He saw the Mexicans rob the bodies of the dead and lead away their saddle horses, after having cooked breakfast for themselves in the deserted camp. He was severely wounded and all his ammunition was gone, hence he could only wait.

===Chinese===
1884 年，一个牧场主带着自家牧场的四名牧牛人，赶一批肥牛前往亚利桑那的墓碑镇（Tombstone）赶集。他们走的路线，一段在老墨西哥境内，一段在亚利桑那境内。一天夜里，他们在边界以南不远的一道峡谷里宿营。次日清晨天刚亮，值后半夜看畜的牧牛人刚回营叫醒众人，墨西哥人便从埋伏中开了火。牧场主和一名牧牛人头一排枪就身负重伤，躲到营车后面，在那里坚持射击，直到弹药打光。另外三人只受了轻伤，也都找到了掩蔽，但逃得性命的只有一个。他在藏身处躲了两天，才被同伴找到。他亲眼看见墨西哥人在空营里给自己做了早饭吃，然后劫掠死者的财物、牵走他们的坐骑。他伤势沉重，弹药尽失，只能等着。

===Original===
On the second day after this raid some of the cattle strayed back to the old ranch, thereby giving notice to the cowboys that there had been foul play. They found their wounded companions lying delirious near the decaying bodies of their comrades. No arrests were ever made in Mexico for these murders, and no attempt was made to recover damage or prosecute the robbers. The two instances above narrated will serve to show the reader what kind of an example was set for the Apaches by at least a portion of the inhabitants of the two Christian nations with whom they came in contact.

===Chinese===
劫案发生后的第二天，有几头牛自己跑回了老牧场，牧牛人们由此得知出了事。他们找到负伤的同伴时，他正神志昏乱地躺在同伴渐渐腐烂的尸体旁。墨西哥方面从未为这些凶案缉捕过任何人，也从未有人试图索赔或追究凶手。上面叙述的两桩事例足以向读者表明：至少同阿帕奇人打交道的这两个基督教国家的部分居民，为阿帕奇人树立了怎样的榜样。

#### Apache Raids / 阿帕奇人的袭掠

===Original===
It is thought well to give in this chapter some of the depredations of the Apaches, not told by Geronimo. They are given as told by our own citizens and from the white man’s point of view.

===Chinese===
本章酌情收录几件杰罗尼莫（Geronimo）未曾讲述的阿帕奇人劫掠事迹。这些事迹均出自我国公民之口，是以白人的视角记述的。

===Original===
In 1884 Judge McCormick and wife, accompanied by their young son, were driving from Silver City to Lordsburg, when they were ambushed by Apaches. The bodies of the adults were found soon afterward, but the child’s body was never recovered. Years afterwards, an Apache squaw told some of the settlers in Arizona that the little boy (about eight years old) cried so much and was so stubborn that they had to kill him, although their original intention was to spare his life.

===Chinese===
1884 年，麦考密克法官（Judge McCormick）偕妻子带着幼子驾车从银城（Silver City）前往洛兹堡（Lordsburg），途中遭阿帕奇人伏击。夫妇二人的尸体很快被人寻获，孩子的尸体却始终没有下落。多年以后，一个阿帕奇妇人告诉亚利桑那的几个移民：那个小男孩（约八岁）哭闹得太凶、性子太倔，他们只好杀了他——尽管起初是打算留他性命的。

===Original===
In 1882 a man named Hunt was wounded in a row in a saloon in Tombstone, Arizona. During this row two other men had been killed, and, to avoid arrest, Hunt and his brother went into the mountains and camped about ten miles north of Willow Springs to await the healing of his wounds. A few days after they came there, Apache Indians attacked them and killed the wounded brother, but the other, by hard riding, made good his escape.

===Chinese===
1882 年，一个名叫亨特（Hunt）的人在亚利桑那墓碑镇的一家酒馆斗殴中受了伤。那场斗殴还死了另外两个人；为躲避拘捕，亨特和他的兄弟躲进山里，在柳泉（Willow Springs）以北约十英里处扎营，等他养伤。他们到那里没几天，阿帕奇印第安人便来袭，杀死了负伤的那一个；另一个靠拼命运马，才算脱身。

===Original===
In 1883 two Eastern boys went into Arizona to prospect. Their real outing began at Willow Springs, where they had stayed two days with the cowboys. These cowboys had warned them against the Apaches, but the young men seemed entirely fearless, and pushed on into the mountains. On the second morning after they left the settlement, one of the boys was getting breakfast while the other went to bring in the pack horses that had been hobbled and turned loose the night before to graze. Just about the time he found his horses, two Apache warriors rode out from cover toward him and he made a hasty retreat to camp, jumping off of a bluff and in so doing breaking his leg.

===Chinese===
1883 年，两个东部来的小伙子到亚利桑那勘探矿藏。他们真正的旅程从柳泉开始——在那里他们同牧牛人们一起住了两天。牧牛人们告诫过他们提防阿帕奇人，但这两个年轻人似乎毫无畏惧，径直往山里去了。离开聚落后的第二个早晨，一个年轻人正做着早饭，另一个去牵回前夜绊上腿放开吃草的驮马。差不多就在他找到马的时候，两个阿帕奇武士从掩蔽处向他驰来，他急忙往营地奔逃，从一道断崖上跳下去，摔断了一条腿。

===Original===
A consultation was then held between the two Easterners and it was decided that perhaps all the stories they had been told of the Apache raids were true, and that it was advisable to surrender. Accordingly a white handkerchief was tied to the end of a pole and raised cautiously above the top of the bluff. In about ten minutes the two Indians﻿—one a very old warrior and the other a mere boy, evidently his son﻿—rode into camp and dismounted. The old warrior examined the broken limb, then without a word proceeded to take off the shirt of the uninjured youth, with strips of which he carefully bound up the broken leg. After this the two Indians ate the prepared breakfast and remounted their ponies. Then the old warrior, indicating the direction with his thumb, said “Doctor﻿—Lordsburg﻿—three days,” and silently rode away. The young men rode twenty-five miles to Sansimone, where the cowboys fitted them out with a wagon to continue their journey to Lordsburg, seventy-five miles further, where a physician’s services could be secured.

===Chinese===
两个东部人一合计，认定人们讲的关于阿帕奇袭掠的传闻恐怕全是真的，还是投降为妙。于是他们把一方白手帕缚在竿子头上，小心翼翼地从崖顶探了出去。约莫十分钟后，那两个印第安人——一个是十分年迈的武士，另一个不过是个孩子，显然是他儿子——骑马进营，下了马。老武士查看了那条断腿，然后一言不发，动手脱下那个未受伤的青年的衬衫，撕成布条，仔仔细细把断腿包扎好。此后两个印第安人吃掉了备好的早饭，重新上马。老武士用拇指指了指方向，说：「大夫——洛兹堡——三天。」便默默驰去了。两个青年骑马走了二十五英里到桑西蒙（Sansimone），牧牛人给他们套了一辆马车，让他们继续赶往七十五英里外的洛兹堡，在那里才请得到医生。

===Original===
In 1883 two prospectors, Alberts and Reese by name, were driving a team, consisting of a horse and a mule, through Turkey Creek bottoms, when they were shot by the Indians. The wagon and harness were left in the road, and the mule was found dead in the road two hundred yards from that place. Evidently the Indians had not much use for him. The guns of the prospectors were found later, but the horse they drove was not recovered.

===Chinese===
1883 年，两个名叫阿尔伯茨（Alberts）和里斯（Reese）的探矿人赶着一马一骡的大车，行经特基河（Turkey Creek）河滩，被印第安人开枪打死。大车和挽具弃在路中，那头骡子被人发现死在离出事地点二百码的路面上。显然印第安人用不上它。探矿人的枪后来找到了，他们驾车的马却再无下落。

===Original===
In none of the above-named instances were the bodies of the victims mutilated. However, there are many recorded instances in which the Apache Indians did mutilate the bodies of their victims, but it is claimed by Geronimo that these were outlawed Indians, as his regular warriors were instructed to scalp none except those killed in battle, and to torture none except to make them reveal desired information.

===Chinese===
上述各案中，遇害者的遗体均未遭残毁。不过，阿帕奇印第安人残毁遇害者遗体的记录也确有许多；但据杰罗尼莫分辩，那些都是亡命的不法印第安人所为，因为他麾下正规的武士曾受严令：只剥战死者的头皮，除非为逼取所需的口供，不得施刑。

===Original===
In 1884 two cowboys in the employment of the Sansimone Cattle Company were camped at Willow Springs, eighteen miles southwest of Skeleton Canyon, and not far from Old Mexico. Just at sundown their camp was surrounded by Apaches in war paint, who said that they had been at war with the Mexicans and wished to return to the United States. There were about seventy-five Indians in the whole tribe, the squaws and children coming up later. They had with them about one hundred and fifty Mexican horses. The Indians took possession of the camp and remained for about ten days, getting their supplies of meat by killing cattle of the company.

===Chinese===
1884 年，受雇于桑西蒙畜牧公司的两名牧牛人在柳泉扎营，那里在骷髅峡谷西南十八英里处，离老墨西哥不远。日落时分，一群涂着战彩的阿帕奇人包围了他们的营地，说自己刚同墨西哥人打过仗，想返回美国。全族约七十五个印第安人，妇人和孩子随后赶到。他们带着约一百五十匹墨西哥马。印第安人占据了营地，盘桓了约十天，宰杀公司的牛充作肉食。

===Original===
With this band of Indians was a white boy about fourteen years old, who had evidently been with them from infancy, for he could not speak a word of English, and did not understand much Spanish, but spoke the Apache language readily.

===Chinese===
这群印第安人中有个约十四岁的白人男孩，显然自幼便生活在他们中间，因为他连一句英语都不会讲，西班牙语也听不大懂，阿帕奇语却说得流利。

===Original===
They would allow but one of the cowboys to leave camp at a time, keeping the other under guard. They had sentinels with spyglasses on all the hills and peaks surrounding the camp.

===Chinese===
他们每次只准一个牧牛人离营，另一个留在看管之下。营地四周所有的山头和峰顶，都布了带望远镜的哨兵。

===Original===
One evening when one of the cowboys, William Berne, had been allowed to pass out of the camp, he noticed an Indian dismounted and, as he approached, discovered that the Indian had him under range of his rifle. He immediately dismounted, and standing on the opposite side from the redskin, threw his own Winchester across his horse’s neck, when the Indian sprang on his horse and galloped toward him at full speed, making signs to him not to shoot, and when he approached him, dismounted and pointing to the ground, showed Berne many fresh deer tracks. Then, as an understanding had been established, the cowboy remounted and went on his way, leaving the Apache to hunt the deer.

===Chinese===
一天傍晚，其中一个名叫威廉·伯恩（William Berne）的牧牛人获准出营，他望见一个印第安人下了马；待他走近，才发现自己已在对方步枪的准星之下。他立即下了马，站在那红种人的对面，把自己的温彻斯特步枪横搁在马颈上；那印第安人随即翻身上马，朝他疾驰而来，连连做手势叫他别开枪；到了近前，那印第安人下马指着地面，给伯恩看许多新鲜的鹿踪。误会既已消解，牧牛人重新上马自去赶路，把那阿帕奇人留给他的鹿。

===Original===
One day when this cowboy was about ten miles from camp, he found two splendid horses of the Indians. These horses had strayed from the herd. Thinking that they would in a way compensate for the cattle the Apaches were eating, he drove them on for about five miles into a canyon where there was plenty of grass and water and left them there, intending to come back after the departure of the Indians and take possession of them.

===Chinese===
有一天，这个牧牛人在离营地约十英里的地方，发现了印第安人的两匹好马。这两匹马是从马群里走散的。他心想，这多少可以抵偿阿帕奇人吃掉的牛，便把它们往前赶了约五英里，赶进一道水草丰足的峡谷留在那里，打算等印第安人走后再回来据为己有。

===Original===
On the tenth day after the arrival of this band of Indians, United States troops, accompanied by two Indians who had been sent to make the arrangements, arrived in camp, paid for the cattle the Apaches had eaten, took the Indians and their stock, and moved on toward Fort Bowie. The cowboys immediately started for the canyon where the two horses had been left, but had not gone far when they met two Indians driving these horses in front of them as they pushed on to overtake the tribe.

===Chinese===
这伙印第安人到来的第十天，美国军队在两名先行接洽的印第安人陪同下开进营地，付清了阿帕奇人吃掉的牛钱，带上这些印第安人和他们的牲口，往鲍伊堡（Fort Bowie）方向去了。牧牛人们立刻动身去那道藏马的峡谷，但走不多远，就迎面遇上两个印第安人，正赶着那两匹马走在前头，追赶他们的部落去了。

===Original===
Evidently the shrewdness of the paleface had not outwitted the red man that time.

===Chinese===
那一回，白种人的精明显然没能斗过红种人。

===Original===
Geronimo says he was in no wise connected with the events herein mentioned, but refuses to state whether he knows anything about them. He holds it unmanly to tell of any depredations of red men except those for which he was responsible.

===Chinese===
杰罗尼莫说，本章所述事件与他毫无干系，但被问及是否知情时，他不肯回答。他认为，除了自己担责的劫掠之外，去讲别的红种人的劫掠事迹，不是男子汉的作为。

===Original===
Such were the events transpiring in “Apache land” during the days when Geronimo was leading his warriors to avenge the “wrongs” of his people. This chapter will serve to show that the Apache had plenty of examples of lawlessness furnished him, and also that he was a very apt scholar in this school of savage lawlessness.

===Chinese===
在杰罗尼莫率领武士为族人的「冤仇」复仇的那些年月里，「阿帕奇人的土地」上发生的事情，大抵如此。本章足以表明：阿帕奇人从不缺少无法无天的样板可供效法，而在这所野蛮不法的学堂里，他又是个何等伶俐的学生。
