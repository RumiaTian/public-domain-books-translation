## II / II

### I First Hear of Mr. Andrew Lumley / 我初闻安德鲁·拉姆利先生

===Original===
A fortnight later—to be accurate, on the 21st of May—I did a thing I rarely do, and went down to South London on a County Court case. It was an ordinary taxicab accident, and, as the solicitors for the company were good clients of mine, and the regular county-court junior was ill in bed, I took the case to oblige them. There was the usual dull conflict of evidence. An empty taxicab, proceeding slowly on the right side of the road and hooting decorously at the corners, had been run into by a private motorcar, which had darted down a side street. The taxi had been swung round and its bonnet considerably damaged, while its driver had suffered a dislocated shoulder. The bad feature in the case was that the motorcar had not halted to investigate the damage, but had proceeded unconscientiously on its way, and the assistance of the London police had been called in to trace it. It turned out to be the property of a Mr. Julius Pavia, a retired East India merchant, who lived in a large villa in the neighbourhood of Blackheath, and at the time of the accident it had been occupied by his butler. The company brought an action for damages against its owner.

===Chinese===
两星期之后——确切地说是五月二十一日——我做了一件我极少做的事：南下伦敦南区，去办一桩郡法院（County Court）的案子。那是一起寻常的出租汽车事故。由于该公司的代理律师是我的老主顾，而那位固定的郡法院初级律师又卧病在床，我便接下这桩案子，算是帮他们一个忙。案情不出所料，是那种乏味的各执一词。一辆空载的出租汽车在马路右侧缓缓行驶，拐角处规规矩矩地按着喇叭，却被一辆从小巷里窜出来的私人汽车撞了个正着。出租车被撞得打了个旋儿，车头损毁得不轻，司机也肩关节脱了臼。案中最恶劣的一节是：那辆私人汽车肇事后竟没有停车查看损情，反倒不管不顾地扬长而去，于是只好请伦敦警方协助追缉。后来查明，车主是一位朱利厄斯·帕维亚（Julius Pavia）先生，退休的东印度商人，住在布莱克希思（Blackheath）附近的一幢大宅子里；出事时车是由他的管家驾驶的。出租汽车公司遂向车主提起了损害赔偿之诉。

===Original===
The butler, Tuke, by name, was the only witness for the defence. He was a tall man, with a very long, thin face, and a jaw the two parts of which seemed scarcely to fit. He was profuse in his apologies on behalf of his master, who was abroad. It seemed that on the morning in question—it was the 8th of May—he had received instructions from Mr. Pavia to convey a message to a passenger by the Continental express from Victoria, and had been hot on this errand when he met the taxi. He was not aware that there had been any damage, thought it only a slight grazing of the two cars, and on his master’s behalf consented to the judgment of the court.

===Chinese===
被告方唯一的证人，便是那位管家，名叫图克（Tuke）。他是个高个子，脸庞又长又瘦，下巴的两半仿佛不大合得拢。他替身在国外的主人连声致歉。据他说，出事那天早晨——也就是五月八日——他奉帕维亚先生之命，去给一位搭乘维多利亚车站（Victoria）欧洲大陆快车的旅客传句话，正火急火燎地赶着这趟差使，便撞上了那辆出租车。他压根儿不知道撞出了什么损伤，以为不过是两车轻微擦蹭，便代表主人接受法庭的判决。

===Original===
It was a commonplace business, but Tuke was by no means a commonplace witness. He was very unlike the conventional butler, much liker one of those successful financiers whose portraits you see in the picture papers. His little eyes were quick with intelligence, and there were lines of ruthlessness around his mouth, like those of a man often called to decisive action. His story was simplicity itself, and he answered my questions with an air of serious candour. The train he had to meet was the 11 a.m. from Victoria, the train by which Tommy had travelled. The passenger he had to see was an American gentleman, Mr. Wright Davies. His master, Mr. Pavia, was in Italy, but would shortly be home again.

The case was over in twenty minutes, but it was something unique in my professional experience. For I took a most intense and unreasoning dislike to that bland butler. I cross-examined with some rudeness, was answered with steady courtesy, and hopelessly snubbed. The upshot was that I lost my temper, to the surprise of the County Court judge. All the way back I was both angry and ashamed of myself. Halfway home I realised that the accident had happened on the very day that Tommy left London. The coincidence merely flickered across my mind, for there could be no earthly connection between the two events.

===Chinese===
这本是一桩司空见惯的案子，可图克其人却绝非一个司空见惯的证人。他与人们印象中那种千篇一律的管家全然不同，倒更像你在画报上见过的那种功成名就的金融家。他那双小眼睛机敏地滴溜溜转，嘴角四周刻着几道冷酷无情的纹路，像是那种常常需要当机立断的人物。他的说辞简单至极，回答我的盘问时也带着一种郑重其事的坦率。他要去接的车，是上午十一点从维多利亚车站开出的那一班——正是汤米搭乘的那一趟。他要去见的旅客，是一位美国先生，赖特·戴维斯（Wright Davies）。他的主人帕维亚先生此刻身在意大利，不过不久便会回国。

案子二十分钟便审结了，但在我执业的经历中，这却是独一无二的一遭。因为我竟对那位和颜悦色的管家生出了一种极其强烈、毫无道理的反感。我盘问他时多少带了几分粗暴，得到的却是始终如一的客气应答，到头来结结实实地碰了一鼻子灰。结果我发了脾气，连那位郡法院的法官都颇为吃惊。回去的一路上，我又是恼怒，又是替自己惭愧。归途过半，我才猛然想起：那桩事故发生的日子，恰是汤米离开伦敦的那一天。这巧合在我脑中不过一闪而过罢了，因为这两件事之间，断不可能有任何干系。

===Original===
That afternoon I wasted some time in looking up Pavia in the directory. He was there sure enough, as the occupier of a suburban mansion called the White Lodge. He had no city address, so it was clear that he was out of business. My irritation with the man had made me inquisitive about the master. It was a curious name he bore, possibly Italian, possibly Goanese. I wondered how he got on with his highly competent butler. If Tuke had been my servant I would have wrung his neck or bolted before a week was out.

===Chinese===
那天下午，我浪费了些工夫在行名簿上查帕维亚。他果然赫然在列，是城郊一幢名叫“白屋”（the White Lodge）的宅邸的住户。他没有城里的办公地址，可见早已不在商界做事了。我对那管家的一腔恼火，倒勾起了我对他主人的好奇。他这个名字颇为古怪，像是意大利人，又像是果阿人。我纳闷他跟那位精明强干的管家相处得如何。换了图克是我的仆人，不出一个礼拜，不是拧断他的脖子，就是我自己拔腿跑路。

===Original===
Have you ever noticed that, when you hear a name that strikes you, you seem to be constantly hearing it for a bit. Once I had a case in which one of the parties was called Jubber, a name I had never met before, but I ran across two other Jubbers before the case was over. Anyhow, the day after the Blackheath visit I was briefed in a big Stock Exchange case, which turned on the true ownership of certain bearer bonds. It was a complicated business which I need not trouble you with, and it involved a number of consultations with my lay clients, a famous firm of brokers. They produced their books and my chambers were filled with glossy gentlemen talking a strange jargon.

===Chinese===
你可曾留意过：一旦某个名字引起了你的注意，此后一阵子你便似乎到处都能听见它。从前我经手过一桩案子，其中一方姓贾伯（Jubber），这姓我此前从未见过，可案子还没了结，我又接连碰上了另外两个贾伯。话说回来，去布莱克希思的第二天，我接下了一桩证券交易所（the Stock Exchange）的大案，案情关乎若干不记名债券（bearer bonds）的真正归属。这事错综复杂，不必在此赘述烦扰诸位，只是其中牵涉与我的当事人——一家赫赫有名的经纪行——多次磋商。他们搬来了账册，我的事务所里一时间挤满了衣着光鲜的先生们，满口说着一种古怪的行话。

===Original===
I had to examine my clients closely on their practice in treating a certain class of bearer security, and they were very frank in expounding their business. I was not surprised to hear that Pitt-Heron was one of the most valued names on their lists. With his wealth he was bound to be a good deal in the city. Now I had no desire to pry into Pitt-Heron’s private affairs, especially his financial arrangements, but his name was in my thoughts at the time, and I could not help looking curiously at what was put before me. He seemed to have been buying these bonds on a big scale. I had the indiscretion to ask if Mr. Pitt-Heron had long followed this course, and was told that he had begun to purchase some six months before.

===Chinese===
我不得不就他们处理某类不记名证券的惯例，细细盘问我的当事人，而他们阐述起自家的生意来也十分坦率。听说皮特-赫伦是他们名单上最受看重的客户之一，我并不觉得意外。以他的财力，在城里大量出入本是理所当然。我本无意窥探皮特-赫伦的私事，尤其不想打听他的财务安排，无奈他的名字那时正萦绕在我心头，我不由得怀着好奇审视摆在我面前的那些材料。看样子他一直在大规模购入这类债券。我一时多嘴，冒昧问了句皮特-赫伦先生做这桩买卖有多久了，得到的回答是：大约半年前开始购入的。

===Original===
“Mr. Pitt-Heron,” volunteered the stockbroker, “is very closely connected in his financial operations with another esteemed client of ours, Mr. Julius Pavia. They are both attracted by this class of security.”

At the moment I scarcely noted the name, but after dinner that night I began to speculate about the connection. I had found out the name of one of Charles’s mysterious new friends.

===Chinese===
“皮特-赫伦先生，”那位股票经纪人主动说道，“在他的财务运作上，与我们的另一位贵客过从甚密——朱利厄斯·帕维亚先生。他们二位都对这类证券情有独钟。”

当时我几乎没有留意这个名字，可那天晚上吃过晚饭，我便开始琢磨起这层关系来。我总算又查出了查尔斯那帮神秘新朋友中一人的姓名。

===Original===
It was not a very promising discovery. A retired East India merchant did not suggest anything wildly speculative, but I began to wonder if Charles’s preoccupation, to which Tommy had been witness, might not be connected with financial worries. I could not believe that the huge Pitt-Heron fortune had been seriously affected, or that his flight was that of a defaulter, but he might have got entangled in some shady city business which preyed on his sensitive soul. Somehow or other I could not believe that Mr. Pavia was a wholly innocent old gentleman; his butler looked too formidable. It was possible that he was blackmailing Pitt-Heron, and that the latter had departed to get out of his clutches.

But on what ground? I had no notion as to the blackmailable thing that might lurk in Charles’s past, and the guesses which flitted through my brain were too fantastic to consider seriously. After all, I had only the flimsiest basis for conjecture. Pavia and Pitt-Heron were friends; Tommy had gone off in quest of Pitt-Heron; Pavia’s butler had broken the law of the land in order, for some reason or other, to see the departure of the train by which Tommy had travelled. I remember laughing at myself for my suspicions, and reflecting that, if Tommy could see into my head, he would turn a deaf ear in the future to my complaints of his lack of balance.

===Chinese===
这个发现算不上多有指望。一个退休的东印度商人，听不出什么穷凶极恶的投机味道；但我转念又想，汤米所目睹的查尔斯那种魂不守舍的心不在焉，莫非与财务上的烦恼有关？我无法相信皮特-赫伦那庞大的家产会元气大伤，也不信他的出走是一个亏空公款者的潜逃，但他说不定卷进了城里某桩见不得光的勾当，正啃噬着他那颗敏感的心。不知怎的，我总觉得帕维亚先生不会是个全然清白无辜的老绅士——他那管家看起来实在太厉害了。说不定他在敲诈皮特-赫伦，而后者出走便是为了挣脱他的魔掌。

可凭什么敲诈呢？查尔斯的过去可能藏着什么足以授人以柄的把柄，我毫无头绪，而那些掠过我脑海的猜测又太过荒诞，不值得认真对待。说到底，我据以推测的根据薄得可怜：帕维亚和皮特-赫伦是朋友；汤米为寻皮特-赫伦而出走；帕维亚的管家为了某种缘故，不惜触犯国法，也要去目送汤米所乘那趟列车的驶离。我记得我曾在心里嘲笑自己的疑神疑鬼，心想汤米若能看穿我这脑中的念头，日后我再抱怨他行事失衡，他大可以充耳不闻了。

===Original===
But the thing stuck in my mind, and I called again that week on Mrs. Pitt-Heron. She had had no word from her husband, and only a bare line from Tommy, giving his Moscow address. Poor child, it was a wretched business for her. She had to keep a smiling face to the world, invent credible tales to account for her husband’s absence, and all the while anxiety and dread were gnawing at her heart. I asked her if she had ever met a Mr. Pavia, but the name was unknown to her. She knew nothing of Charles’s business dealings, but at my request she interviewed his bankers, and I heard from her next day that his affairs were in perfect order. It was no financial crisis which had precipitated him abroad.

===Chinese===
可这桩心事怎么也挥之不去，那一周我便又去拜访了皮特-赫伦太太。她没有收到丈夫半个字的消息，汤米也只寄来寥寥一行，给了个莫斯科（Moscow）的地址。可怜的人儿，这于她实在是一桩折磨人的事。她得在外人面前维持一张笑脸，编出些说得过去的说辞来解释丈夫的缺席，而忧惧与惶恐无时无刻不在啃噬她的心。我问她可曾见过一位帕维亚先生，她对这个名字全然陌生。她对查尔斯的生意往来一无所知，但应我的请求，她去见了他的银行家。第二天她告诉我，他的账目井井有条。把他逼出国门的，绝非一场财务危机。

===Original===
A few days later I stumbled by the merest accident upon what sailors call a “cross-bearing.” At the time I used to “devil” a little for the Solicitor-General, and “note” cases sent to him from the different Government offices. It was thankless work, but it was supposed to be good for an ambitious lawyer. By this prosaic channel I received the first hint of another of Charles’s friends.

===Chinese===
几天之后，我纯粹出于偶然，撞上了水手们所说的“交叉方位”（cross-bearing）——两条方位线一交叉，位置便定了下来。那时我时常替副检察长（Solicitor-General）打打下手，替他“做摘记”，处理各政府部门转来的案子。这差事吃力不讨好，可据说于一个有野心的律师不无裨益。偏偏就是经由这条枯燥乏味的渠道，我第一次得到了关于查尔斯另一位朋友的线索。

===Original===
I had sent me one day the papers dealing with the arrest of a German spy at Plymouth, for at the time there was a sort of epidemic of roving Teutons who got themselves into compromising situations, and gravely troubled the souls of the Admiralty and the War-Office. This case was distinguished from the common ruck by the higher social standing of the accused. Generally the spy is a photographer or bagman who attempts to win the bibulous confidence of minor officials. But this specimen was no less than a professor of a famous German University, a man of excellent manners, wide culture, and attractive presence, who had dined with Port officers and danced with Admirals’ daughters.

===Chinese===
有一天，有人转来了一宗在普利茅斯（Plymouth）逮捕德国间谍的案卷，因为那段时日里，四下流窜的条顿人犹如瘟疫一般，屡屡使自己陷入形迹可疑的境地，令海军部（the Admiralty）和陆军部（the War Office）的大人们大为头疼。这一桩案子之所以从那一干庸常之辈中脱颖而出，全在于被告社会地位之高。寻常的间谍，不是摄影师便是旅行推销员，千方百计地去套那些小职员酒酣耳热时的口风。可这一位却是堂堂一所著名德国大学的教授，举止优雅、学识渊博、风度翩翩，与港务军官们同席进餐，与海军上将的千金们翩翩起舞。

===Original===
I have forgotten the evidence or what was the legal point submitted for the Law Officers’ opinion; in any case it matters little, for he was acquitted. What interested me at the time was the testimonials as to character which he carried with him. He had many letters of introduction. One was from Pitt-Heron to his wife’s sailor uncle; and when he was arrested one Englishman went so far as to wire that he took upon himself the whole costs of the defence. This gentleman was a Mr. Andrew Lumley, stated in the papers sent me to be a rich bachelor, a member of the Athenaeum and Carlton Clubs, and a dweller in the Albany.

===Chinese===
证据是什么，或者呈交法律长官们裁定的法律争点何在，我已经忘了；反正也无甚要紧，因为他最终被判无罪。当时引起我兴味的，是他随身携带的那些品行推荐信。他握有许多介绍信。其中一封出自皮特-赫伦之手，是写给皮特-赫伦太太那位当海军的叔父的；而他被捕之后，有一位英国人竟然拍来电报，声明愿独自承担全部辩护费用。这位先生便是安德鲁·拉姆利（Andrew Lumley）先生——卷宗里注明他是一位富有的单身汉，雅典娜神庙俱乐部（the Athenaeum）和卡尔顿俱乐部（Carlton Club）的会员，寓所设在奥尔巴尼（the Albany）。

===Original===
Remember, that till a few weeks before I had known nothing of Pitt-Heron’s circle, and here were three bits of information dropping in on me unsolicited, just when my interest had been awakened. I began to get really keen, for every man at the bottom of his heart believes that he is a born detective. I was on the lookout for Charles’s infrequent friends, and I argued that if he knew the spy and the spy knew Mr. Lumley, the odds were that Pitt-Heron and Lumley were acquaintances. I hunted up the latter in the Red Book. Sure enough, he lived in the Albany, belonged to half a dozen clubs, and had a country house in Hampshire.

===Chinese===
须知，直到几星期之前，我对皮特-赫伦的交际圈子还一无所知，而如今，恰逢我的兴味被勾起，竟有三条消息不请自来地落到我头上。我开始真正上了心——毕竟每个男人骨子里都暗自相信自己天生是个侦探。我正留心查访查尔斯那寥寥无几的朋友，于是推想：既然他认识那间谍，而那间谍又认识拉姆利先生，那么皮特-赫伦与拉姆利相识的可能性便极大。我在红皮书（the Red Book）里查了查后者的底细。果不其然，他住在奥尔巴尼，加入了半打俱乐部，在汉普郡（Hampshire）还有一所乡间宅邸。

===Original===
I tucked the name away in a pigeonhole of my memory, and for some days asked everyone I met if he knew the philanthropist of the Albany. I had no luck till the Saturday, when, lunching at the club, I ran against Jenkinson, the art critic.

===Chinese===
我把这个名字收进了记忆的某个分格中，此后好几天，逢人便问是否认识那位住在奥尔巴尼的“慈善家”。一直毫无所获，直到那个星期六，我在俱乐部吃午饭时撞见了艺术评论家詹金森（Jenkinson）。

===Original===
I forget if you know that I have always been a bit of a connoisseur in a mild way. I used to dabble in prints and miniatures, but at that time my interest lay chiefly in Old Wedgwood, of which I had collected some good pieces. Old Wedgwood is a thing which few people collect seriously, but the few who do are apt to be monomaniacs. Whenever a big collection comes into the market it fetches high prices, but it generally finds its way into not more than half a dozen hands. Wedgwoodites all know each other, and they are less cutthroat in their methods than most collectors. Of all I have ever met Jenkinson was the keenest, and he would discourse for hours on the “feel” of good jasper and the respective merits of blue and sage-green grounds.

===Chinese===
我忘了你是否知道，我一向算得上是个浅尝辄止的鉴赏家。从前我也曾在版画和袖珍画像上略事涉猎，可那阵子我的兴致主要落在老韦奇伍德瓷器（Old Wedgwood）上，其中也搜罗了几件精品。老韦奇伍德是样鲜有人认真收藏的东西，可一旦认真收藏起来，那少数几人往往入了魔一般。每逢一大宗藏品流入市场，总能卖出高价，但到头来一般也不过落入寥寥五六人之手。韦奇伍德藏家彼此都相识，搜罗手段也不像多数藏家那般你死我活。在我所见过的同好之中，詹金森是痴迷最深的一个，他能就上好碧玉瓷的“手感”、蓝色底子与鼠尾草绿底子孰优孰劣，滔滔不绝地议论上好几个钟头。

===Original===
That day he was full of excitement. He babbled through luncheon about the Wentworth sale, which he had attended the week before. There had been a pair of magnificent plaques, with a unique Flaxman design, which had roused his enthusiasm. Urns and medallions and whatnot had gone to this or that connoisseur, and Jenkinson could quote their prices, but the plaques dominated his fancy, and he was furious that the nation had not acquired them. It seemed that he had been to South Kensington and the British Museum and all sorts of dignitaries, and he thought he might yet persuade the authorities to offer for them if the purchaser would resell. ===Chinese===
