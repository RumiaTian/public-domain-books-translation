## V / V

### I Take a Partner / 我找了个搭档

===Original===
That meeting with Lumley scared me badly, but it also clinched my resolution. The most pacific fellow on earth can be gingered into pugnacity. I had now more than my friendship for Tommy and my sympathy with Pitt-Heron to urge me on. A man had tried to bully me, and that roused all the worst stubbornness of my soul. I was determined to see the game through at any cost.

But I must have an ally if my nerves were to hold out, and my mind turned at once to Tommy’s friend Chapman. I thought with comfort of the bluff independence of the Labour member. So that night at the House I hunted him out in the smoking-room.

===Chinese===
与拉姆利的那次会面把我吓得不轻，却也叫我下定了决心。世上最与世无争的人也能被激出斗性来。如今驱使我向前的，已不止是我对汤米的友情和对皮特-赫伦的同情。有人竟想威吓我，这一下把我骨子里最犟的那股拧劲儿全勾了出来。我横下心，无论如何也要把这盘棋看到底。

可要撑住神经，我非得有个帮手不可，我的念头当即转到了汤米的朋友查普曼身上。一想到那位工党议员那副直来直去、不倚仗人的做派，我心里便觉踏实。于是那天晚上在下议院，我到吸烟室里把他寻了出来。

===Original===
He had been having a row with the young bloods of my party that afternoon and received me ungraciously.

===Chinese===
那天下午他刚跟我党里那帮年轻公子哥儿闹了一场，见了我便没什么好脸色。

===Original===
“I’m about sick of you fellows,” he growled. (I shall not attempt to reproduce Chapman’s accent. He spoke rich Yorkshire with a touch of the drawl of the western dales.) “They went and spoiled the best speech, though I say it as shouldn’t, which this old place has heard for a twelvemonth. I’ve been workin’ for days at it in the Library. I was tellin’ them how much more bread cost under Protection, and the Jew Hilderstein started a laugh because I said kilometres for kilogrammes. It was just a slip o’ the tongue, for I had it right in my notes, and besides there furrin’ words don’t matter a curse. Then that young lord as sits for East Claygate gets up and goes out as I was gettin’ into my peroration, and he drops his topper and knocks off old Higgins’s spectacles, and all the idiots laughed. After that I gave it them hot and strong, and got called to order. And then Wattles, him as used to be as good a socialist as me, replied for the Government and his blamed Board and said that the Board thought this and the Board thought that, and was damned if the Board would stir its stumps. Well I mind the day when I was hanging on to the Board’s coattails in Hyde Park to keep it from talking treason.”

===Chinese===
“你们这帮家伙，我可真受够了，”他嚷嚷道。（查普曼的口音我就不费力去学了。他操一口浓重的约克郡话，还带着西部谷地那种拖长腔。）“他们偏偏搅黄了这破地方十二个月以来听过的最好的一篇演说——这话本不该我自己夸口。我在图书馆里对着这篇稿子磨了好几天工夫。我正给他们讲保护主义之下面包涨了多少钱，那个犹太人希尔德斯坦（Hilderstein）倒好，竟哄堂大笑起来，就因为我把‘公斤’说成了‘公里’。那不过是舌头打了个滑，我笔记里原本写得一点不错，再说那些外国字眼儿压根儿不值一提。紧接着，那个代表东克莱盖特（East Claygate）的年轻勋爵偏偏在我讲到结尾高潮时站起来往外走，他那顶大礼帽一掉，把老希金斯（Higgins）的眼镜也给碰飞了，一屋子蠢货全乐了。打那以后我可就火力全开，狠狠给了他们一顿，结果被勒令遵守秩序。接下来沃特尔斯（Wattles）——那个从前跟我一样是个呱呱叫的社会主义者的家伙——居然替政府、替他那个该死的委员会答话，说什么委员会这样想、委员会那样想，委员会死活不肯挪窝儿。我还清楚记得从前的日子，那时候我还在海德公园（Hyde Park）拽着那委员会的后衣摆，好不让它说出叛国的话来呢。”

===Original===
It took me a long time to get Chapman settled down and anchored to a drink.

“I want you,” I said, “to tell me about Routh﻿—you know the fellow I mean﻿—the ex-Union-Leader.”

===Chinese===
我费了好大工夫，才把查普曼安抚下来，让他坐定在一杯酒跟前。

“我想请你，”我说，“跟我说说劳思（Routh）的事——你知道我说的是谁——那个从前当过工会领袖的家伙。”

===Original===
At that he fairly blazed up.

“There you are, you Tories,” he shouted, causing a pale Liberal member on the next sofa to make a hurried exit. “You can’t fight fair. You hate the Unions, and you rake up any rotten old prejudice to discredit them. You can find out about Routh for yourself, for I’m damned if I help you.”

===Chinese===
一听这话，他顿时火冒三丈。

“又来了，你们这些托利党人，”他大喊大叫，吓得隔壁沙发上一个面皮白净的自由党议员匆匆逃了席。“你们从来不肯光明正大地较量。你们恨工会，就不择手段地翻出任何陈年烂芝麻的偏见来给它抹黑。要打听劳思，你们自个儿打听去，见鬼去吧，我才不帮你们。”

===Original===
I saw I could do nothing with Chapman unless I made a clean breast of it, so for the second time that day I told the whole story.

===Chinese===
我看出，若不向查普曼和盘托出，便拿他毫无办法，于是那天我第二回把事情的原委从头到尾讲了一遍。

===Original===
I couldn’t have wished for a better audience. He got wildly excited before I was half through with it. No doubt of the correctness of my evidence ever entered his head, for, like most of his party, he hated anarchism worse than capitalism, and the notion of a highly capitalised, highly scientific, highly undemocratic anarchism fairly revolted his soul. Besides, he adored Tommy Deloraine. Routh, he told me, had been a young engineer of a superior type, with a job in a big shop at Sheffield. He had professed advanced political views, and, although he had strictly no business to be there, had taken a large part in Trade Union work, and was treasurer of one big branch. Chapman had met him often at conferences and on platforms, and had been impressed by the fertility and ingenuity of his mind and the boldness of his purpose. He was the leader of the left wing of the movement, and had that gift of half-scientific, half-philosophic jargon which is dear at all times to the hearts of the half-baked. A seat in Parliament had been repeatedly offered him, but he had always declined; wisely, Chapman thought, for he judged him the type which is more effective behind the scenes.

===Chinese===
再没有比他更好的听众了。我才讲到一半，他便已激动得不行。对我所说之事是否确凿，他脑子里压根儿没生过一丝疑——因为同他党内大多数人一样，他恨无政府主义甚于恨资本主义，而一个资本高度集中、技术高度发达、又高度反民主的无政府主义，这念头本身就使他满心厌恶。何况，他对汤米·德洛兰推崇备至。他告诉我，劳思从前是个出类拔萃的年轻工程师，在谢菲尔德（Sheffield）一家大工厂里任职。他标榜激进的政治主张，尽管按规矩他压根儿没资格掺和，却在工会事务里插手颇深，还当上了一个大分会的财务主管。查普曼常在各种会议和讲台上遇见他，对他那心思之活、主意之多、图谋之大，印象极深。他是这场运动里左翼的领袖，天生有那种半科学、半哲学的切口本领——这等切口，半瓶醋的人们历来最是受用。议席曾屡次三番地奉送给他，他一概谢绝；查普曼认为谢绝得对，因为他断定劳思这种人，在幕后效力远比在台前更为得力。

===Original===
But with all his ability he had not been popular. “He was a cold-blooded, sneering devil,” as Chapman put it, “a sort of Parnell. He tyrannised over his followers, and he was the rudest brute I ever met.”

===Chinese===
可他空有一身本事，人缘却不大好。“他是个冷血、刻薄的魔鬼，”查普曼如是说，“有点儿帕内尔（Parnell）那路货色。他对追随者专制得很，而且是我这辈子见过的最粗鲁的畜生。”

===Original===
Then followed the catastrophe, in which it became apparent that he had speculated with the funds of his Union and had lost a large sum. Chapman, however, was suspicious of these losses, and was inclined to suspect that he had the money all the time in a safe place. A year or two earlier the Unions, greatly to the disgust of old-fashioned folk, had been given certain extralegal privileges, and this man Routh had been one of the chief advocates of the Unions’ claims. Now he had the cool effrontery to turn the tables on them and use those very privileges to justify his action and escape prosecution.

===Chinese===
接下来便是那场祸事——事情败露出来，他原来拿工会的公款去做投机，亏了一大笔。不过查普曼对这些亏损心存疑虑，倒倾向于怀疑那笔钱其实一直被他好好地藏在某个稳妥之处。一两年前，工会争得了若干法外特权——这事令老派人物深恶痛绝——而这个劳思，正是当年为工会主张摇旗呐喊最力者之一。如今他竟厚着脸皮倒打一耙，恰恰利用这些特权来为自己的行径辩护，逃脱了起诉。

===Original===
There was nothing to be done. Some of the fellows, said Chapman, swore to wring his neck, but he did not give them the chance. He had disappeared from England, and was generally believed to be living in some foreign capital.

===Chinese===
对此谁也无可奈何。查普曼说，有些同伙发誓要拧断他的脖子，可他没给他们这个机会。他从此在英国销声匿迹，一般都相信他隐居在某座外国都城里。

===Original===
“What I would give to be even with the swine!” cried my friend, clenching and unclenching his big fist. “But we’re up against no small thing in Josiah Routh. There isn’t a crime on earth he’d stick at, and he’s as clever as the old Devil, his master.”

===Chinese===
“我愿拿一切来换，好跟这头猪猡算清这笔账！”我的朋友大叫，攥紧又松开他那只好大的拳头。“可我们这次在约西亚·劳思（Josiah Routh）身上碰到的，绝非等闲之辈。天底下没有一桩罪是他干不出来的，而且他跟他那位主子老魔鬼一样奸滑。”

===Original===
“If that’s how you feel, I can trust you to back me up,” I said. “And the first thing I want you to do is to come and stay at my flat. God knows what may happen next, and two men are better than one. I tell you frankly, I’m nervous, and I would like to have you with me.”

Chapman had no objection. I accompanied him to his Bloomsbury lodgings, where he packed a bag, and we returned to the Down Street flat. The sight of his burly figure and sagacious face was a relief to me in the mysterious darkness where I now found myself walking.

===Chinese===
“你既这么想，我便信得过你会给我撑腰，”我说。“我要你头一件做的事，就是搬来我公寓住。天知道接下来还会出什么事，两个人总比一个人强。我跟你直说吧，我心里发虚，想有你在我身边。”

查普曼没有异议。我陪他到布卢姆斯伯里（Bloomsbury）的寓所，看着他收拾了一只提包，便一同回到唐街（Down Street）的公寓。他那一副魁梧的身板和那张精明的脸，在我如今深陷其中的那片诡秘黑暗里，着实叫我心安。

===Original===
Thus began my housekeeping with Chapman﻿—one of the queerest episodes in my life. He was the best fellow in the world, but I found that I had misjudged his character. To see him in the House, you would have thought him a piece of granite, with his Yorkshire bluntness and hard, downright, north-country sense. He had all that somewhere inside him, but he was also as romantic as a boy. The new situation delighted him. He was quite clear that it was another case of the strife between Capital and Labour﻿—Tommy and I standing for Labour, though he used to refer to Tommy in public as a “gilded popinjay,” and only a month before had described me in the House as a “viperous lackey of Capitalism.” It was the best kind of strife, in which you had not to meet your adversary with long-winded speeches but might any moment get a chance to pummel him with your fists.

===Chinese===
我与查普曼的同住生涯便由此开端——那是我平生最古怪的一段插曲。他是世上头等的好人，可我发觉自己先前看走了眼，错判了他的性子。在下议院里瞧见他，你会当他是一块花岗岩，带着约克郡人的生硬和北方人那种硬邦邦、直通通的见识。他骨子里确实有这些，可同时他又浪漫得像个孩子。这新局面叫他乐坏了。他满心认定这又是一场资本与劳工之间的较量——汤米和我这一边代表劳工，虽说他在人前惯常管汤米叫“镀金的花花公子”，而就在一个月前，他还在下议院里把我形容成“资本主义那条毒蛇般的走狗”呢。这种较量是最对他的胃口的那种：你不必拿长篇大论的演说去会你的对手，随时都有可能捞着机会用拳头狠狠揍他一顿。

===Original===
He made me ache with laughter. The spying business used to rouse him to fury. I don’t think he was tracked as I was, but he chose to fancy he was, and was guilty of assault and battery on one butcher’s boy, two cabbies, and a gentleman who turned out to be a bookmaker’s assistant. This side of him got to be an infernal nuisance, and I had many rows with him. Among other things, he chose to suspect my man Waters of treachery﻿—Waters, who was the son of a gardener at home, and hadn’t wits enough to put up an umbrella when it rained.

===Chinese===
他常逗得我笑岔了气。盯梢这种事最能把他激得火冒三丈。我倒不觉得有人像我那样盯他的梢，可他偏要自个儿认定有，结果犯了殴打罪——揍了一个屠户家的小伙计、两个马车夫，还有一位后来查明是赌档跑腿的先生。他这一面简直成了要命的麻烦，我俩为此吵过好几回。此外，他还偏偏疑心我那个听差沃特斯（Waters）叛变——沃特斯，老家花匠的儿子，下雨天都不见得有那份机灵劲儿去撑把伞的。

===Original===
“You’re not taking this business rightly,” he maintained one night. “What’s the good of waiting for these devils to down you? Let’s go out and down them.” And he announced his intention, from which no words of mine could dissuade him, of keeping watch on Mr. Andrew Lumley at the Albany.

His resolution led to a complete disregard of his Parliamentary duties. Deputations of constituents waited for him in vain. Of course he never got a sight of Lumley. All that happened was that he was very nearly given in charge more than once for molesting peaceable citizens in the neighbourhood of Piccadilly and Regent Street.

===Chinese===
“你这桩事办得不对路，”有一夜他坚持道。“干等着这些魔鬼来收拾你，有什么用？咱们该主动出去收拾他们。”他还宣布了一项决心——任我怎么劝也劝不住——要去奥尔巴尼（the Albany）盯安德鲁·拉姆利先生的梢。

这一决心使他全然抛开了议员的职责。选区来的代表团白白等他，等了个空。他当然连拉姆利的影儿也没见着。唯一的收获，是他不止一回差点被扭送警局——罪名是在皮卡迪利（Piccadilly）和摄政街（Regent Street）一带骚扰安分守己的良民。

===Original===
One night, on my way home from the Temple, I saw in the bills of the evening papers the announcement of the arrest of a Labour Member. It was Chapman, sure enough. At first, I feared that he had got himself into serious trouble, and was much relieved to find him in the flat in a state of blazing anger. It seemed that he had found somebody whom he thought was Lumley, for he only knew him from my descriptions. The man was in a shop in Jermyn Street, with a car waiting outside, and Chapman had﻿—politely, as he swore﻿—asked the chauffeur his master’s name. The chauffeur had replied abusively, upon which Chapman had hauled him from the driver’s seat and shaken him till his teeth rattled. The owner came out, and Chapman was arrested and taken off to the nearest police-court. He had been compelled to apologise and had been fined five pounds and costs.

===Chinese===
一天夜里，我从律师学院区（the Temple）回家的路上，看见晚报招贴上登着一条工党议员被捕的消息。果然是查普曼。起先我还怕他闯下了大祸，后来见他怒气冲冲地回了公寓，才大大松了口气。原来他认定自己找着了拉姆利——可他只凭我那几番描述认人。那人正在杰明街（Jermyn Street）的一家铺子里，门外停着一辆汽车，查普曼便——他赌咒说是客客气气地——向司机打听他主人的名姓。司机回了一嘴脏话，查普曼便把他从驾驶座上拽下来，直摇得他牙齿咯咯作响。那主人出来，查普曼便被拘捕，押往最近的治安法庭。他被迫赔了不是，还被罚了五英镑并讼费。

===Original===
By the mercy of Heaven, the chauffeur’s master was a moneylender of evil repute, so the affair did Chapman no harm. But I was forced to talk to him seriously. I knew it was no use explaining that for him to spy on the Powerhouse was like an elephant stalking a gazelle. The only way was to appeal to his incurable romanticism.

===Chinese===
亏得上天保佑，那司机的主人是个声名狼藉的放高利贷的，所以这事倒没损及查普曼半点名头。但我不得不郑重地跟他谈一谈。我深知，跟他解释他去盯动力室的梢就好比一头大象偷偷逼近一只羚羊，这话是白费唇舌。唯一的法子，只能去打动他那无可救药的浪漫心性。

===Original===
“Don’t you see,” I told him, “that you are playing Lumley’s game? He will trap you sooner or later into some escapade which will land you in jail, and where will I be then? That is what he and his friends are out for. We have got to meet cunning with cunning, and lie low till we get our chance.”

He allowed himself to be convinced, and handed over to me the pistol he had bought, which had been the terror of my life.

“All right,” he said, “I’ll keep quiet. But you promise to let me into the big scrap when it comes off.”

I promised. Chapman’s notion of the grand finale was a Homeric combat in which he would get his fill of fisticuffs.

===Chinese===
“你难道看不出，”我对他说，“你这样正中拉姆利的下怀？他迟早要诱你闹出点什么乱子，叫你蹲进大牢，到那时候我又怎么办？这正是他和他那帮人所图的。我们得以狡诈对付狡诈，按兵不动，等咱们的机会来。”

他到底被说通了，还把他买的那把手枪交了出来——那玩意儿可一直是我心头之患。

“行，”他说，“我消停着就是。可你得答应我，等那场大仗打起来的时候，让我也插一手。”

我答应了。在查普曼的心目中，那场压轴大戏便是一场荷马式（Homeric）的大搏斗，好让他痛痛快快地打个够。

===Original===
He was an anxiety, but all the same he was an enormous comfort. His imperturbable cheerfulness and his racy talk were the tonics I wanted. He had plenty of wisdom, too. My nerves were getting bad those days, and, whereas I had rarely touched the things before, I now found myself smoking cigarettes from morning till night. I am pretty abstemious, as you know, but I discovered, to my horror, that I was drinking far too many whiskeys-and-sodas. Chapman knocked me off all that and got me back to a pipe and a modest nightcap.

He did more, for he undertook to put me in training. His notion was that we should win in the end by superior muscles. He was a square, thickset fellow, who had been a good middleweight boxer. I could box a bit myself, but I improved mightily under his tuition. We got some gloves, and used to hammer each other for half an hour every morning. Then might have been seen the shameful spectacle of a rising barrister with a swollen lip and a black eye arguing in court, and proceeding of an evening to his country’s legislature, where he was confronted from the opposite benches by the sight of a Leader of the People in the same vulgar condition.

===Chinese===
他是个叫人操心的人物，可同时他又是莫大的安慰。他那不动声色的好兴致、那有滋有味的谈吐，正是我所需要的补剂。他也有的是见识。那段日子我的神经越来越糟，从前我难得沾这些玩意儿，如今却从早到晚一支接一支地抽着纸烟。诸位知道，我向来颇有节制，可我惊恐地发觉，自己掺了苏打水的威士忌喝得实在太多。查普曼把这一切都给我戒了，让我重新叼上烟斗，睡前至多浅酌一杯。

他做的还不止于此——他竟担当起为我操练体魄的差事来。他的主意是，我们最终要靠更胜一筹的筋骨取胜。他是个敦实粗壮的汉子，从前是个不错的中量级拳手。我自己也会那么两下子，可经他一调教，进步斐然。我们弄来几副拳套，每天早晨对揍上半个钟头。于是人们便有幸目睹这样一桩丢人的光景：一位前程似锦的律师，肿着嘴唇、乌青着眼在法庭上慷慨陈词，到了晚上又去出席国家的立法机构——而在对面的议席上，赫然坐着一位同样鼻青脸肿的人民领袖，与他遥遥相对。

===Original===
In those days I wanted all the relief I could get, for it was a beastly time. I knew I was in grave danger, so I made my will and went through the other doleful performances consequent on the expectation of a speedy decease. You see, I had nothing to grip on, no clear job to tackle, only to wait on the off-chance, with an atmosphere of suspicion thickening around me. The spying went on﻿—there was no mistake about that﻿—but I soon ceased to mind it, though I did my best to give my watchers little satisfaction. There was a hint of bullying about the spying.
