===Original===

From Stavanger I steered a straight course for the Humber.
I had no sooner left behind me the Norway coast than I began to meet the ships, the ships﻿—ship after ship; and by the time I entered the zone of the ordinary alternation of sunny day and sunless night, I was moving through the midst of an incredible number of craft, a mighty and widespread fleet.
Over all that great expanse of the North Sea, where, in its most populous days of trade, the sailor might perhaps sight a sail or two, I had now at every moment at least ten or twelve within scope of the glass, oftentimes as many as forty, forty-five.
And very still they lay on a still sea, itself a dead thing, livid as the lips of death; and there was an intensity in the calm that was appalling: for the ocean seemed weighted, and the air drugged.
Extremely slow was my advance, for at first I would not leave any ship, however remotely small, without approaching sufficiently to investigate her, at least with the spyglass: and a strange multitudinous mixture of species they were, trawlers in hosts, warships of every nation, used, it seemed, as passenger-boats, smacks, feluccas, liners, steam-barges, great four-masters with sails, Channel boats, luggers, a Venetian *burchiello*, colliers, yachts, *remorqueurs*, training ships, dredgers, two *dahabeeahs* with curving gaffs, Marseilles fishers, a Maltese *speronare*, American offshore sail, Mississippi steamboats, Sorrento lug-schooners, Rhine punts, yawls, old frigates and three-deckers, called to novel use, Stromboli caiques, Yarmouth tubs, xebecs, Rotterdam flat-bottoms, floats, mere gunwaled rafts﻿—anything from anywhere that could bear a human freight on water had come, and was here: and all, I knew, had been making westward, or northward, or both; and all, I knew, were crowded; and all were tombs, listlessly wandering, my God, on the wandering sea with their dead.
And so fair was the world about them, too: the brightest suavest autumn weather; all the still air aromatic with that vernal perfume of peach: yet not so utterly still, but if I passed close to the lee of any floating thing, the spicy stirrings of morning or evening wafted me faint puffs of the odour of mortality overripe for the grave.

从斯塔万格出发，我取直航向亨伯河口。
挪威海岸刚一抛在身后，我就开始遇见船，到处是船——一艘接着一艘；等我驶入白昼与黑夜正常交替的地带时，我正穿行在多得难以置信的船只中间，一支浩大而铺展的船队。
在那片辽阔的北海上——在它航运最盛的年代，水手或许也只能望见一两片帆——此刻我每一时刻，望远镜里至少有十艘十二艘，常有四十艘、四十五艘之多。
它们静静地躺在静止的海上，那海本身就是一件死物，青灰得如同死者的嘴唇；那平静里有种骇人的沉重：海洋仿佛坠了铅，空气仿佛下了药。
我的行进极其缓慢，因为起初任何一艘船，哪怕再远再小，我都要靠近到足以查探的程度，至少用望远镜看上一看：而它们是一支何等驳杂的船种大杂烩：成群的拖网渔船，各国的军舰——看样子被当客船用了，斯麦克渔船，费卢卡帆船，班轮，蒸汽驳船，张着帆的巨型四桅船，海峡渡轮，卢格帆船，一艘威尼斯*布尔基耶洛*游船，运煤船，游艇，法国拖轮，练习舰，挖泥船，两艘弯斜桁的达哈比耶船，马赛渔船，一艘马耳他*斯佩罗纳雷*，美国近海帆船，密西西比明轮船，索伦托斜桁纵帆船，莱茵河平底船，小帆艇，被改作新用的老式快速帆舰和三层甲板舰，斯特龙博利的卡伊克小帆船，雅茅斯老木桶船，谢贝克船，鹿特丹平底船，浮具，乃至仅仅装了舷缘的木筏——凡是世上任何地方能在水上载人的东西，全都来了，全都在这里：而我知道，它们全都在向西、向北、或既向西又向北赶；我知道，全都挤满了人；而全都是坟墓，载着它们的死者，在漂泊的海上恹恹漂泊，我的上帝。
它们周围的世界又是那样美好：最明媚和煦的秋日天气；静止的空气里全是那春日般的桃花香：可也不是全然不动：每当我从任何漂浮物的下风处擦过，清晨或傍晚带香料的微风，便给我送来一缕缕淡淡的、熟烂待葬的死亡气味。

So abominable and accursed did this become to me, such a plague and a hissing, vague as was the offence, that I began to shun rather than seek the ships, and also I now dropped my twelve, whom I had kept to be my companions all the way from the Far North, one by one, into the sea: for now I had definitely passed into a zone of settled warmth.
I was convinced, however, that the poison, whatever it might be, had some embalming, or antiseptic, effect upon the bodies: at Aadheim, Bergen and Stavanger, for instance, where the temperature permitted me to go without a jacket, only the merest hints and whiffs of the processes of dissolution had troubled me.

这事变得如此可憎可咒，如此瘟疫、如此笑柄——尽管那罪愆何在还说不清——以致我开始躲避船只，而不是寻访它们了；而且这时，我也把那十二个一路从极北带在身边作伴的，一个一个投入了海里：因为此刻我确凿已驶入暖意稳定的区域了。
不过我确信，那毒——管它是什么——对尸体有某种防腐、杀菌之效：比如在阿德海姆、卑尔根和斯塔万格，气温容许我单衣出行，腐解过程只以最轻微的暗示和气息打扰过我。
