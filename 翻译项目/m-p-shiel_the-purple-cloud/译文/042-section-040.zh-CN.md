I felt, I felt, that in this townlet, save the water-gnats of Norway, was no living thing; that the hum and the savour of Eternity filled, and wrapped, and embalmed it.
The houses are mostly of wood, some of them fairly large, with a porte-cochère leading into a semicircular yard, around which the building stands, very steep-roofed, and shingled, in view of the heavy snow-masses of winter. Glancing into one open casement near the ground, I saw an aged woman, stout and capped, lie on her face before a very large porcelain stove; but I paced on without stoppage, traversed several streets, and came out, as it became dark, upon a piece of grassland leading downward to a mountain-gorge. It was some distance along this gorge that I found myself sitting the next morning: and how, and in what trance, I passed that whole blank night is obliterated from my consciousness. When I looked about with the return of light I saw majestic fir-grown mountains on either hand, almost meeting overhead at some points, deeply shading the mossy gorge. I rose, and careless of direction, went still onward, and walked and walked for hours, unconscious of hunger; there was a profusion of wild mountain-strawberries, very tiny, which must grow almost into winter, a few of which I ate; there were blue gentianellas, and lilies-of-the-valley, and luxuriance of verdure, and a noise of waters. Occasionally, I saw little cataracts on high, fluttering like white wild rags, for they broke in the mid-fall, and were caught away, and scattered; patches also of reaped hay and barley, hung up, in a singular way, on stakes six feet high, I suppose to dry; there were perched huts, and a seemingly inaccessible small castle or burg, but none of these did I enter: and five bodies only I saw in the gorge, a woman with a babe, and a man with two small oxen.
About three in the afternoon I was startled to find myself there, and turned back. It was dark when I again passed through those gloomy streets of Aadheim, making for the quay, and now I felt both my hunger and a dropping weariness. I had no thought of entering any house, but as I passed by one open porte-cochère, something, I know not what, made me turn sharply in, for my mind had become as fluff on the winds, not working of its own action, but the sport of impulses that seemed external.

我感觉到了，我感觉到了：这座小城里，除了挪威的水蚋，再无活物；永恒之国的嗡响与气息，灌满了它，裹住了它，把它香殓了。
房屋大半是木头的，有些相当大，带一座车马门，通进一个半圆形的院子，房子绕院而立，屋顶极陡，盖着木瓦——那是防着冬天的大雪。我朝近地一间敞开的窗里瞥了一眼，看见一个上了年纪的妇人，胖胖的，戴着睡帽，伏脸躺在一座很大的瓷炉前；可我脚步不停，一路走过，穿过几条街，天黑下来时，走到一片通往山峡的草地上。第二天早晨我发觉自己坐着的，便是离山峡口有些距离的地方；那一整夜空白的夜，我是怎样、在何种恍惚里熬过的，意识里已全然抹去了。天光回转，我四下一望，只见两边都是长满冷杉的巍峨群山，有几处几乎在头顶相接，把生苔的峡谷罩得浓荫匝地。我起身，不辨方向，依旧前行，走了又走，几个钟头不知饥饿；沿途野山莓极多，极小，想必一直长到将近冬天，我吃了几颗；还有蓝色龙胆、铃兰，一片繁绿，水声不断。偶尔，我望见高处的小瀑布，像白色的破布条般飘动，因为它们在半空便跌碎了，被风掠去、吹散；还有一堆堆收割的干草和大麦，以一种奇特的方式，挂在六英尺高的木架上，我猜是为了晾干；有高踞的小屋，有一座似乎无路可攀的小小城堡（或者说堡寨），这些我都没有进：整个峡谷里，我只见到五具尸体：一个抱着婴儿的妇人，和一个牵两头小牛的汉子。
下午三点左右，我猛然惊觉自己竟在那里，便转身折回。再次穿过亚德海姆那些阴森的街道走向码头时，天已黑了，这时我才觉出饿来，还有一阵阵下坠般的疲惫。我本无意进任何人家，可路过一扇敞开的车马门时，不知什么东西使我猛地一转身走了进去——我的心智已成了风中的绒絮，不能自主地运转，只任一些仿佛外来的冲动摆布。

I went across the yard, and ascended a wooden spiral stair by a twilight which just enabled me to pick my way among five or six vague forms fallen there. In that confined place fantastic qualms beset me; I mounted to the first landing, and tried the door, but it was locked; I mounted to the second: the door was open, and with a chill reluctance I took a step inward where all was pitch darkness, the window-stores being drawn. I hesitated: it was very dark. I tried to utter that word of mine, but it came in a whisper inaudible to my ears: I tried again, and this time heard myself say: “*anyone?*” At the same time I had made another step forward, and trodden upon a soft abdomen; and at that contact terrors the most cold and ghastly thrilled me through and through, for it was as though I saw in that darkness the sudden eyeballs of Hell and frenzy glare upon me, and with a low gurgle of affright I was gone, helter-skelter down the stairs, treading upon flesh, across the yard, and down the street, with pelting feet, and open arms, and sobbing bosom, for I thought that all Aadheim was after me; nor was my horrid haste appeased till I was on board the *Boreal*, and moving down the fjord.
Out to sea, then, I went again; and within the next few days I visited Bergen, and put in at Stavanger. And I saw that Bergen and Stavanger were dead.
It was then, on the 19th August, that I turned my bow toward my native land.

我穿过院子，就着一片刚好容我择路的暮色，登上一道木旋梯，梯上倒着五六个模糊的人形。在那逼仄的地方，种种离奇的惶惑攝住了我；我上到第一处平台，推了推门，门锁着；我上到第二处：门开着，我带着一阵寒噤般的却步，向里跨了一步——里面一片漆黑，窗板全拉上了。我踌躇了：真黑啊。我想喊出我的那个词，可出口的只是一声自己耳朵也听不见的气音；我再试，这回听见自己说：「有人吗？」与此同时，我又向前迈了一步，一脚踩在一个柔软的肚腹上；那一触，最冰冷、最阴森的恐怖顿时把我彻头彻尾穿透，因为仿佛我在那黑暗里，看见地狱与癫狂的眼球骤然瞪定了我；我低低嗬了一声惊惶，人已飞奔下楼，一路踩着皮肉，穿过院子，奔下街道，双脚乱撞，双臂大张，胸口啜泣——我以为整个亚德海姆都在我身后追来；直到登上「北极号」，驶下峡湾，那可怕的狂奔才算罢休。
于是我又出海了；随后几天里，我到访了卑尔根，又进了斯塔万格。我看见卑尔根和斯塔万格都死了。
就在那时，八月十九日，我把船头转向了我的祖国。
