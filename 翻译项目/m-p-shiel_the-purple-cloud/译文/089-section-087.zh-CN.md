I sat a long time thinking such things by my bed that night, till finally I was disposed to sleep there. But I had no considerable number of candlesticks, nor was even sure of candles. I remembered, however, that Peter Peters, three doors away on the other side of the street, had had four handsome silver candelabra in his drawing-room, each containing six stems; and I said to myself: “I will search for candles in the kitchen, and if I find any, I will go and get Peter Peters’ candelabra, and sleep here.”
I took then the two lights which I had, my good God; went down to the passage; then down to the basement; and there had no difficulty in finding three packets of large candles, the fact being, I suppose, that the cessation of gaslighting had compelled everyone to provide themselves in this way, for there were a great many wherever I looked. With these I re-ascended, went into a little alcove on the second-floor where I had kept some drugs, got a bottle of carbolic oil, and for ten minutes went dashing all the corpses in the house. I then left the two lighted bits of candle on the waiting-room table, and, with the car-lamp, passed along the passage to the front-door, which was very violently banging. I stepped out to find that the storm had increased to a mighty turbulence (though it was dry), which at once caught my clothes, and whirled them into a flapping cloud about and above me; also, I had not crossed the street when my lamp was out. I persisted, however, half blinded, to Peters’ door. It was locked: but immediately near the pavement was a window, the lower sash up, into which, with little trouble, I lifted myself and passed. My foot, as I lowered it, stood on a body: and this made me angry and restless. I hissed a curse, and passed on, scraping the carpet with my soles, that I might hurt no one: for I did not wish to hurt anyone. Even in the almost darkness of the room I recognised Peters’ furniture, as I expected: for the house was his on a long lease, and I knew that his mother had had the intention to occupy it after his death. But as I passed into the passage, all was mere blank darkness, and I, depending upon the lamp, had left the matches in the other house.

那夜，我就这样在床边坐了许久，直坐到最后竟打算就在这里睡下。可我手头没有多少烛台，连蜡烛也没有把握。不过我想起街对面隔三个门的彼得·彼得斯，他家客厅里有四座漂亮的银烛台，每座插六支烛；我便对自己说：「我去厨房找蜡烛，若找得到，就去把彼得·彼得斯的烛台取来，就在这儿睡。」
于是我拿起仅有的两点光，我的老天；下到过道；再下到地下室；毫不费事就找到三大包粗蜡烛——我想是因为煤气灯一断，家家都不得不这样备货，我到哪儿都见着一大堆。我拿着它们重新上楼，进了二楼一间我一直存着药品的小壁龛间，取了一瓶石炭酸油，用十分钟把全屋的尸首都浇了一遍。然后我把两截点着的蜡烛留在候诊室的桌上，提着车灯，沿着过道走到前门——门正被风撞得砰砰乱响。我跨出门去，发现风暴已壮大成一场狂暴的骚动（虽然没下雨），它一把扯住我的衣裳，卷成一片扑打翻飞的云裹在我周身上方；还有，我还没过街，灯就灭了。可我执拗地、半盲地摸到彼得斯家门口。门锁着：但紧挨人行道有一扇窗，下扇提着，我没费什么事就攀了进去。脚往下一落，正踩在一具尸身上：这叫我火起，烦躁。我嘶声咒了一句，继续往里走，鞋底擦着地毯蹭，生怕碰伤了谁：我并不想碰伤任何人。哪怕屋里几乎全黑，我也认出了彼得斯的家具，同我料想的一样：因为这宅子是他长租的，而且我知道他母亲曾打算在他死后住进来。可我一拐进过道，四下便只是一片纯粹的黑暗，而我仗着有灯，火柴竟留在了对面那座宅子里。

I groped my way to the stairs, and had my foot on the first step, when I was stopped by a vicious shaking of the front-door, which someone seemed to be at with hustlings and the most urgent poundings: I stood with peering stern brows two or three minutes, for I knew that if I once yielded to the flinching at my heart, no mercy would be shown me in this house of tragedy, and thrilling shrieks would of themselves arise and ring through its haunted chambers. The rattling continued an inordinate time, and so instant and imperative, that it seemed as if it could not fail to force the door. But, though horrified, I whispered to my heart that it could only be the storm which was struggling at it like the grasp of a man, and after a time went on, feeling my way by the broad rail, in my brain somehow the thought of a dream which I had had in the *Boreal* of the woman Clodagh, how she let drop a fluid like pomegranate-seeds into water, and tendered it to Peter Peters: and it was a mortal purging draught; but I would not stop, but step by step went up, though I suffered very much, my brows peering at the utter darkness, and my heart shocked at its own rashness. I got to the first landing, and as I turned to ascend the second part of the stair, my left hand touched something icily cold: I made some quick instinctive movement of terror, and, doing so, my foot struck against something, and I stumbled, half falling over what seemed a small table there. Immediately a horrible row followed, for something fell to the ground: and at that instant, ah, I heard something﻿—a voice﻿—a human voice, which uttered words close to my ear﻿—the voice of Clodagh, for I knew it: yet not the voice of Clodagh in the flesh, but her voice clogged with clay and worms, and full of effort, and thick-tongued: and in that ghastly speech of the grave I distinctly heard the words:
Things being as they are in the matter of the death of Peter﻿ ﻿…
And there it stopped dead, leaving me so sick, my God, so sick, that I could hardly snatch my robes about me to fly, fly, fly, soft-footed, murmuring in pain, down the steps, down like a sneaking thief, but quick, snatching myself away, then wrestling with the cruel catch of the door which she would not let me open, feeling her all the time behind me, watching me.

我摸到楼梯口，一只脚才踏上第一级，便被前门一阵凶暴的摇晃止住了：似乎有什么人正在门外挤撞，擂得万分急迫。我蹙紧双眉站着，站了两三分钟：因为我知道，我一旦向心头的畏缩屈服，这座悲剧之宅是决计不会饶过我的，尖利的嘶叫会自己升起来，响彻它那些闹鬼的房间。那撼门声持续了长得离谱的时间，那样急切、那样专横，仿佛不可能不把门撞开。可我虽然毛骨悚然，仍对自己的心低语说，那只能是风暴，是它像一只人手在那门上挣扎；过了一阵，我继续往上走，扶着宽扶手探路，脑子里不知怎么浮起在*博雷尔*号上做过的那个梦：梦见那女人克洛达，她把一种石榴籽色的液体滴进水里，递给彼得·彼得斯：那是一剂致命的泻药；可我不肯停下，一级一级往上走，虽然苦不堪言，眉头紧蹙望着无边的黑暗，心惊于自己的莽撞。我到了第一段楼梯的平台，转身要上第二段时，左手触到一样冰凉刺骨的东西：我惊恐地一缩，缩时脚又绊在什么上，踉跄着半跌在一张像是小桌子的东西上。紧接着便响起一片可怕的喧闹，有件东西摔到了地上：而就在那一瞬，啊，我听见了什么——一个声音——一个人的声音，就贴着我耳边吐出话来——是克洛达的声音，我听得出：却又不是血肉的克洛达的声音，而是她那塞满黏土和蛆虫的声音，满含吃力，舌头发滞：在那坟墓的阴森言语里，我分明听见这句：
