===Original===

I could have made short work, and landed at Shetland, for I found myself as far westward as longitude 11° 23′ W.: but I would not: I was so afraid. The shrinking within me to face that vague suspicion which I had, turned me first to a foreign land.
I made for Norway, and on the first night of this definite intention, at about nine o’clock, the weather being gusty, the sky lowering, the air sombrous, and the sea hard-looking, dark, and ridged, I was steaming along at a good rate, holding the wheel, my poor port and starboard lights still burning there, when, without the least notice, I received the roughest physical shock of my life, being shot bodily right over the wheel, thence, as from a cannon, twenty feet to the cabin-door, through it head-foremost down the companionway, and still beyond some six yards along the passage. I had crashed into some dark and dead ship, probably of large size, though I never saw her, nor any sign of her; and all that night, and the next day till four in the afternoon, the *Boreal* went driving alone over the sea, whither she would: for I lay unconscious. When I woke, I found that I had received really very small injuries, considering: but I sat there on the floor a long time in a sulky, morose, disgusted, and bitter mood; and when I rose, pettishly stopped the ship’s engines, seeing my twelve dead all huddled and disfigured. Now I was afraid to steam by night, and even in the daytime I would not go on for three days: for I was childishly angry with I know not what, and inclined to quarrel with Those whom I could not see.
However, on the fourth day, a rough swell which knocked the ship about, and made me very uncomfortable, coaxed me into moving; and I did so with bows turned eastward and southward.
I sighted the Norway coast four days later, in latitude 63° 19′, at noon of the 11th August, and pricked off my course to follow it; but it was with a slow and dawdling reluctance that I went, at much less than half-speed. In some eight hours, as I knew from the chart, I ought to sight the lighthouse light on Smoelen Island; and when quiet night came, the black water being branded with trails of still moonlight, I passed quite close to it, between ten and twelve, almost under the shadow of the mighty hills: but, oh my God, no light was there.

===Chinese===

我本可以抄近路，在设得兰登岸的，因为我发现自己已西至西经 11 度 23 分：可我不肯：我怕。心里那点不敢面对的隐约的疑惧，使我先转向了一片外国的土地。
我驶向挪威。拿定这个主意的头一夜，约莫九点钟，天气阵阵起风，天色阴沉，空气晦暗，海面铁硬、乌黑、起脊，我正以不错的速度开着船，握着舵，我那可怜的左右舷灯还点在那里，忽然毫无预兆，我捱了此生最重的一记皮肉之震：整个人从舵轮上直抛过去，又像出膛的炮弹，二十英尺飞到舱门，头朝前破门摉下升降梯，还在过道里又滑出六码开外。我撞上了一条黑黑的死船，兴许还很大，虽然我从未看见她，也没看见她一点踪影；那一整夜，直到次日下午四点，「北极号」都独自在海上随意漂驶：因为我一直不省人事。醒来时，我发现自己伤得实在很轻，就势而论；可我在地板上坐了许久，一腔乖戾、阴郁、作呕、怨苦；起身时，我赌气停了船机，眼见我那十二个死人挤作一堆、七歪八扭。这下我夜里不敢开船了，连白天也三天不肯走：因为我孩子气地恼着一样我不知是什么的东西，一心想同那些我看不见的「谁」吵架。
不过第四天，一场把船颠得东倒西歪、叫我很不好受的大涌，哄得我动了身；我便掉头向东、向南驶去。
四天后，八月十一日正午，我在北纬 63 度 19 分望见了挪威海岸，标好航线沿岸而行；可我去得拖沓迟缓，老大不情愿，船速不到一半。照海图推算，八个钟头左右，我该望见斯莫伦岛上的灯塔；静夜来临时，黑色的海面烙着一道道凝然的月光，十点到十二点之间，我贴着它驶过，几乎在大山黑影的笼罩之下：可是，噢，我的上帝，那里没有灯。
