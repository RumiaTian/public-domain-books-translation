One most brilliant Autumn day I walked by the village market-cross at Barnard, come at last, but with a tenderness in my heart, and a reluctance, to where I was born; for I said I would go and see my sister Ada, and﻿—the other old one. I leaned and loitered a long time on the bridge, gazing up to the craggy height, which is heavy with waving wood, and crowned by the Castle-tower, the Tees sweeping round the mountain-base, smooth here and sunlit, but a mile down, where I wished to go, but would not, brawling bedraggled and lacerated, like a sweet strumpet, all shallow among rocks under reaches of shadow﻿—the shadow of Rokeby Woods. I climbed very leisurely up the hillside, having in my hand a bag with a meal, and up the stair in the wall to the top I went, where there is no parapet, but a massiveness of wall that precludes danger; and here in my miner’s attire I sat three hours, brooding sleepily upon the scene of lush umbrageous old wood that marks the long way the river takes, from Marwood Chase up above, and where the rapid Balder bickers in, down to bowery Rokeby, touched now with autumn; the thickness of trees lessening away toward the uplands, where there are far etherealized stretches of fields within hedgerows, and in the sunny mirage of the farthest azure remoteness hints of lonesome moorland. It was not till near three that I went down along the river, then, near Rokeby, traversing the old meadow, and ascending the old hill: and there, as of old, was the little black square with yellow letters on the gate-wall:

> Hunt Hill House.

一个无比晴朗的秋日，我走过巴纳德的村市十字架，终于回来了，心里却揣着温柔，揣着不忍，回到我出生的地方；因为我对自己说，我要去看看妹妹阿达，还有——另一位老人。我在桥上倚栏逗留了许久，仰望那嶙峋的高处：满山摇曳的林木沉甸甸，顶端耸着城堡的塔楼，蒂斯河绕着山脚一扫而过，到这里平顺如镜，映着日光；可往下一英里，正是我想去而不敢去的地方，它在岩石间咆哮着，蓬头垢面、遍体鳞伤，像个甜美的荡妇，在一段段阴影底下浅浅地流——那是罗克比树林的阴影。我非常悠闲地爬上山坡，手里提着一袋饭食，又顺着墙里的阶梯登上顶端：那里没有雉堞，只有厚厚的一堵墙，足以杜绝危险；我穿着一身矿工装束，在那里坐了三个钟头，睡意沉沉地俯瞰那片葱茏浓荫的老林景致，它标出这条河长长的路径：从上方的马伍德猎苑，湍急的鲍尔德河喧闹着汇入的地方，直到草木成拱的罗克比，如今都已染上秋色；树影渐稀，往高原方向淡去，那里有一方方树篱围拢的田野，远远地缥缈如烟，而在最远的天青色的蜃景里，隐约是寂寞的荒原。快到三点，我才沿河而下，在罗克比附近穿过老草场，登上老家的小山：那里，同旧时一样，门墙上一方小小的黑底黄字：

> 猎山庄。

No part, no house, I believe, of this countryside was empty of strange corpses: and they were in Hunt Hill, too. I saw three in the weedy plot to the right of the garden-path, where once the hawthorn and lilac tree had grown from well-rollered grass, and in the little bush-wilderness to the left, which was always a wilderness, one more: and in the breakfast-room, to the right of the hall, three; and in the new wooden clinker-built attachment opening upon the breakfast-room, two, half under the billiard-table; and in her room overlooking the porch on the first floor, the long thin form of my mother on her bed, with crushed-in left temple, and at the foot of the bed, face-downward on the floor, black-haired Ada in a nightdress.
Of all the men and women who died, they two alone had burying. For I digged a hole with the stable-spade under the front lilac; and I wound them in the sheets, foot and form and head; and, not without throes and qualms, I bore and buried them there.

我相信，这一带乡间没有任何角落、任何宅子空着没有外乡的尸首：猎山庄也有。我在花园小径右侧那块长满荒草的地上看见三具——那里从前是碾压平整的草坪，山楂树和丁香树就从那里长起；左边的小灌木荒园——它一向是荒的——里还有一具；门厅右侧的早餐室里，三具；早餐室旁边新接的木造轻艇式披屋里，两具，半个身子压在台球桌底下；而在二楼俯瞰门廊的她那间屋里，是我母亲瘦长的身形，躺在床上，左太阳穴塌陷；床脚的地板上，俯卧着穿睡衣的黑发阿达。
在所有死去的人里，唯有她们两个得到了埋葬。因为我用马厩的铁锹在前庭丁香树下挖了一个坑；我用床单把她们裹起来，脚、身体、头；然后，不是没有一阵阵的抽搐与反胃，我把她们抱起，埋在了那里。
