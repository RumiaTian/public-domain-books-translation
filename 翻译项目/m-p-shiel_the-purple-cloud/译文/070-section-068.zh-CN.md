I went onward by train-engine all along the coast to a region of iron-ore, alum, and jet-excavations round Whitby and Middlesborough. By byways near the small place of Goldsborough I got down to the shore at Kettleness, and reached the middle of a bay in which is a cave called the Hob-Hole, with excavations all around, none of great depth, made by jet-diggers and quarrymen. In the cave lay a small herd of cattle, though for what purpose put there I cannot guess; and in the jet-excavations I found nothing. A little further south is the chief alum-region, as at Sandsend, but as soon as I saw a works, and the great gap in the ground like a crater, where the lias is quarried, containing only heaps of alum-shale, brushwood-stacks, and piles of cement-nodules extracted from the lias, I concluded that here could have been found no hiding; nor did I purposely visit the others, though I saw two later. From round Whitby, and those rough moors, I went on to Darlington, not far now from my home: but I would not continue that way, and after two days’ indecisive lounging, started for Richmond and the lead mines about Arkengarth Dale, near Reeth. Here begins a region of mountain, various with glens, fells, screes, scars, swards, becks, passes, villages, river-heads, and dales. Some of the faces which I saw in it almost seemed to speak to me in a broad dialect which I knew. But they were not numerous in proportion: for all this countryside must have had its population multiplied by at least some hundreds; and the villages had rather the air of Danube, Levant, or Spanish villages. In one, named Marrick, I saw that the street had become the scene either of a great battle or a great massacre; and soon I was everywhere coming upon men and women, English and foreign, dead from violence: cracked heads, wounds, unhung jaws, broken limbs, and so on. Instead of going direct to the mines from Reeth, that waywardness which now rules my mind, as squalls an abandoned boat, took me somewhat further southwest to the village of Thwaite, which I actually could not enter, so occupied with dead was every spot on which the eye rested a hundred yards about it.

我乘着机车沿海岸一路前行，到了惠特比和米德尔斯伯勒周围那片铁矿、明矾与煤玉开采之地。在戈尔兹伯勒小村附近的小径上，我下到凯特尔内斯的海岸，走到一处海湾中部，湾里有个洞，叫霍布洞，四周全是采掘坑，都不深，是挖煤玉和采石的人开的。洞里卧着一小群牛，为何赶进去，我猜不出；煤玉坑里我一无所获。稍南便是主要的明矾产区，如桑森德，可我一望见那厂子和地上如火山口般的大缺口——那里采掘里阿斯统页岩，只剩一堆堆明矾页岩、柴禾垛和从页岩里拣出的一摞摞水泥结核——我便断定，这里藏不了人；其余的矿我也没特意去访，虽然后来望见过两处。从惠特比一带和那些荒凉的旷野出发，我到了达灵顿，离我家已不远了：可我不肯往那边去，摇摆不定地闲荡了两天之后，动身去里士满和里斯附近阿肯加斯谷一带的铅矿。从这里开始是一片山乡，沟谷、冈峦、碎石坡、峭壁、草甸、溪涧、隘口、村落、河源和山谷，变化不一。我在其中见到的有些面孔，几乎像是要用我所熟的一种宽腔宽调的土话同我说话。但按比例说他们并不多：因为这整片乡野的人口必是翻了至少几百倍；那些村子，倒更像多瑙河、黎凡特或西班牙的村子。在一个叫马里克的村子里，我看见那条街曾是一场大战或一场大屠杀的现场；很快我到处撞见的，都是死于暴力的男女，英国的、外国的：裂开的头、伤口、脱了臼的下颌、断掉的肢体，等等。从里斯没有直接去矿上，如今主宰我头脑的那股任性——像阵风摆弄一条弃船——把我带到西南稍远处的斯韦特村，那村我竟进不去：目光落在它周围一百码的每一处，处处都塞满了死人。

Not far from here I turned up, on foot now, a very steep, stony road to the right, which leads over the Buttertubs Pass into Wensleydale, the day being very warm and bright, with large clouds that looked like lakes of molten silver giving off grey fumes in their centre, casting moody shadows over the swardy dale, which below Thwaite expands, showing Muker two miles off, the largest village of Upper Swaledale. Soon, climbing, I could look down upon miles of Swaledale and the hills beyond, a rustic panorama of glens and grass, river and cloudshadow, and there was something of lightness in my step that fair day, for I had left all my maps and things, except one, at Reeth, to which I meant to return, and the earth, which is very good, was﻿—mine. The ascent was rough, and also long: but if I paused and looked behind﻿—I saw, I saw. Man’s notion of a Heaven, a Paradise, reserved for the spirits of the good, clearly arose from impressions which the earth made upon his mind: for no Paradise can be fairer than this; just as his notion of a Hell arose from the squalid mess into which his own foolish habits of thought and action turned this Paradise. At least, so it struck me then: and, thinking it, there was a hiss in my breath, as I went up into what more and more acquired the character of a mountain pass, with points of almost Alpine savagery: for after I had skirted the edge of a deep glen on the left, the slopes changed in character, heather was on the mountainsides, a fretting beck sent up its noise, then screes, and scars, and a considerable waterfall, and a landscape of crags; and lastly a broad and rather desolate summit, palpably nearer the clouds.

离这里不远，我这时已步行，向右拐上一条极陡的石头路，它越过巴特塔布斯山口通入温斯利谷；天气温暖晴朗，大片大片的云像熔银的湖泊，中心冒着灰烟，把忧郁的影子投在草色青青的谷上；谷地在斯韦特以下豁然敞开，望得见两英里外的马克，上斯韦尔谷最大的村子。攀登不久，我便能俯瞰绵延数英里的斯韦尔谷和谷外的群山，一幅沟谷与绿草、河流与云影的田园全景；那个晴日，我的脚步有几分轻快，因为我把地图等等一切都留在了里斯——只留了一张——本打算回去取；而这大地，这极好的大地——是我的。上山路既崎岖，又漫长：可我只要一停步回望——我便看见，便看见了。人心目中的天堂、乐园——预留给善人灵魂的所在——显然源于大地在他心头留下的印象：因为再没有乐园比这更美；正如他心目中的地狱，源于他自己的思想与行为的愚蠢习惯把这乐园精蹋成的那个污秽烂摊子。至少，我当时是这么想的；想着这个，我呼吸里带着咝咝声，走进了一段越来越具山口气象的路，有几处近乎阿尔卑斯的荒犷：因为沿左边一道深谷的边缘走过之后，山坡变了性子，山坡上尽是石楠，一条奔涌的溪涧哗哗作响，接着是碎石坡、峭壁，一道相当可观的瀑布，一片巉岩的景观；最后是一处宽旷而相当荒凉的高顶，分明地更挨着云了。
