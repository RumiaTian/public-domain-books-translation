===Original===

The dust, though very thin and flighty above-decks, lay thickly deposited below, and after having made a tour of investigation throughout the ship, the first thing which I did was to examine that﻿—though I had tasted nothing all day, and was exhausted to death. I found my own microscope where I had left it in the box in my berth to starboard, though I had to lift up Egan to get at it, and to step over Lamburn to enter the chart-room; but there, toward evening, I sat at the table and bent to see if I could make anything of the dust, while it seemed to me as if all the myriad spirits of men that have sojourned on the earth, and angel and devil, and all Time and all Eternity, hung silent round for my decision; and such an ague had me, that for a long time my wandering fingertips, all ataxic with agitation, eluded every delicate effort which I made, and I could nothing do. Of course, I know that an odour of peach-blossom in the air, resulting in death, could only be associated with some vaporous effluvium of cyanogen, or of hydrocyanic (“prussic”) acid, or of both; and when I at last managed to examine some of the dust under the microscope, I was not therefore surprised to find, among the general mass of purplish ash, a number of bright-yellow particles, which could only be minute crystals of potassic ferrocyanide. What potassic ferrocyanide was doing on board the *Boreal* I did not know, and I had neither the means, nor the force of mind, alas! to dive then further into the mystery; I understood only that by some extraordinary means the air of the region just south of the Polar environ had been impregnated with a vapour which was either cyanogen, or some product of cyanogen; also, that this deadly vapour, which is very soluble, had by now either been dissolved by the sea, or else dispersed into space (probably the latter), leaving only its faint after-perfume; and seeing this, I let my poor abandoned head drop again on the table, and long hours I sat there staring mad, for I had a suspicion, my God, and a fear, in my breast.

那灰尘在甲板上方虽又薄又轻，下层舱里却积得厚厚的。我把全船巡查一遍之后，头一件事就是来检验这灰——尽管我一整天粒米未进，已累得要死。我在右舷自己铺位的箱子里找到了我留下的显微镜，虽然得先把伊根抬起来才拿得到，又得跨过兰伯恩才进得了海图室；可就在那里，傍晚时分，我坐在桌前俯身去瞧，看能不能从这灰尘里瞧出些名堂；那一刻，我仿佛觉得，曾在世上寄居过的亿万人灵，天使与魔鬼，全部的时间与全部的永恒，都默默围在我四周等我的裁决；我寒战得那么厉害，以致好半天里，我游移的指尖激动得全不听使唤，每一次精细的摆弄都落了空，什么也做不成。当然，我明白，空气中桃花似的香气竟能致人死命，这只能同某种气态的氰气体，或者氢氰酸（即『普鲁士酸』）的气味，或两者的混合相关；待我终于设法把一些灰尘放到显微镜下检验时，我发现，在那一片发紫的灰烬总体中，杂着许多亮黄色的微粒，那只能是亚铁氰化钾的细小结晶——对此我并不惊讶。亚铁氰化钾到*博雷尔*号上来做什么，我不知道；而我当时既没有手段，唉，也没有心气，再往这谜底深处钻了；我只明白了两件事：一是某种异乎寻常的方式，使极区紧南面的空气饱含了一种蒸气，它或是氰，或是氰的某种产物；二是这致命的蒸气极易溶解，如今不是已被海水溶解，便是已消散于太空（多半是后者），只留下一缕淡淡的余香；看清这些，我让我那颗被弃的可怜脑袋又垂到桌上，久久地坐在那里，瞪着眼发怔，因为我心里有一样疑心，我的上帝，和一样恐惧。
