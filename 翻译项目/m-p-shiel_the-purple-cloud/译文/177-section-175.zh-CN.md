Well, from 11 a.m. till five in the afternoon, I thought it all out, lying in the damp flannels on my face on the sofa in the recess beside my bed, where it was quite dark behind the tattered piece of arras: and what things I suffered that day, and what deeps I sounded, and what prayers I prayed, God knows. What infinitely complicated the awful problem was this thought in my head: that to kill her would be far more merciful to her than to leave her alone, having killed myself: and, Heaven knows, it was for her alone that I thought, not at all caring for myself. To kill her was better: but to kill her with my own hands﻿—that was too hard to expect of a poor devil like me, a poor common son of Adam, after all, and never any sublime self-immolator, as two or three of them were. And hours I lay there with brows convulsed in an agony, groaning only those words: “To kill her! to kill her!” thinking sometimes that I should be merciful to myself too, and die, and let her live, and not care, since, after my death, I would not see her suffer, for the dead know not anything: and to expect me to kill her with my own hand was a little too much. Yet that one or other of us must die was perfectly certain, for I knew that I was just on the brink of failing in my oath, and matters here had reached an obvious crisis: unless we could make up our minds to part﻿ ﻿… ? putting the width of the earth between us? That conception occurred to me: and in the turmoil of my thoughts it seemed a possibility. Finally, about 5 p.m., I resolved upon something: and first I leapt up, went down and across the house into the arsenal, chose a small revolver, fitted it with cartridge, took it upstairs, lubricated it with lamp-oil, went down and out across the drawbridge, walked two miles beyond the village, shot the revolver at a tree, found its action accurate, and started back. When I came to the Castle, I walked along the island to the outer end, and looked up: there were her pretty cream Valenciennes, put up by herself, waving inward before the light lake-breeze at one open oriel; and I knew that she was in the Castle, for I felt it: and always, always, when she was within, I knew, for I felt her with me; and always when she was away, I knew, I felt, for the air had a dreadful drought, and a barrenness, in it.

就这样，从上午十一点到下午五点，我把一切想了个透：我趴在我床边凹室里的沙发上，穿着湿漉漉的法兰绒衣，凹室在那幅破旧的挂毯后面，相当暗：那一天我受了什么罪，探了多深的渊，做了怎样的祷告，只有上帝知道。
把这可怕的难题搅得无比复杂的，是我脑中的这个念头：杀了她，远比我自己先死、把她独自留下，对她仁慈得多：上天知道，我心里想的只有她，自己倒全不在乎。杀了她更好：可要我用这双手去杀——这对我这样一个可怜虫，终究只是亚当一个平平常常的子孙、从来不是什么崇高的自我牺牲者——未免指望太高了，他们里头倒有那么两三位是的。
一连几个钟头我躺在那儿，眉头拧成一团苦痛，嘴里只呻吟这几个字：“杀了她！杀了她！”有时又想，不如也饶了我自己，我去死，让她活，什么都不管——反正我死后也看不见她受苦，因为死人无所知：再说，指望我亲手杀她，实在有点过分。
然而我们两人必死一个，这一点确凿无疑：因为我知道我正站在背誓的崖边上，这里的事已到了明摆着的关口：除非……我们横下心分开……？让整个地球的宽度横在我们中间？这个念头冒出来过：在思绪的翻腾里，它似乎是一线可能。
最后，下午五点来钟，我拿定了主意：我先跳起来，下楼穿过宅子进军械库，挑了一支小号左轮，装上子弹，拿到楼上，用灯油润了滑，再下楼出吊桥，走到村子外面两英里，朝一棵树开了枪，见它机件精准，便动身回来。
到了城堡，我沿岛走到尽头，抬头仰望：她那幅漂亮的乳白瓦朗西纳花边窗帘，她自己挂上去的，正在一扇敞开的凸窗前，随着轻轻的湖风向里飘动；我知道她在城堡里，因为我感觉得到：每回、每回她在里面，我都知道，因为我感到她在我身边；而每回她不在，我也知道，也感觉得到，因为那时的空气里有一种可怕的干涸，一种荒芜。
