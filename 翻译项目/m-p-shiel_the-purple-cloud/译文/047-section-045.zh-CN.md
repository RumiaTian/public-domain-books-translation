I came in sight of the coast on the morning of the 26th August, somewhere about Hornsea, but did not see any town, for I put the helm to port, and went on further south, no longer bothering with the instruments, but coasting at haphazard, now in sight of land, and now in the centre of a circle of sea; not admitting to myself the motive of this loitering slowness, nor thinking at all, but ignoring the deep-buried fear of the tomorrow which I shirked, and instinctively hiding myself in today. I passed the Wash, I passed Yarmouth, Felixstowe. By now the things that floated motionless on the sea were beyond numbering, for I could hardly lower my eyes ten minutes and lift them, without seeing yet another there: so that soon after dusk I, too, had to lie still among them all, till morning: for they lay dark, and to move at any pace would have been to drown the already dead.
Well, I came to the Thames-mouth, and lay pretty well in among the Flats and Pan Sands towards eight one evening, not seven miles from Sheppey and the North Kent coast: and I did not see any Nore Light, nor Girdler Light: and all along the coast I had seen no light: but as to that I said not one word to myself, not admitting it, nor letting my heart know what my brain thought, nor my brain know what my heart surmised; but with a daft and mock-mistrustful under-look I would regard the darkling land, holding it a sentient thing that would be playing a prank upon a poor man like me.
And the next morning, when I moved again, my furtive eye-corners were very well aware of the Prince’s Channel lightship, and also the Tongue ship, for there they were: but I would not look at them at all, nor go near them: for I did not wish to have anything to do with whatever might have happened beyond my own ken, and it was better to look straight before, seeing nothing, and concerning one’s-self with one’s-self.
The next evening, after having gone out to sea again, I was in a little to the E. by S. of the North Foreland: and I saw no light there, nor any Sandhead light; but over the sea vast signs of wreckage, and the coasts were strewn with old wrecked fleets. I turned about S. E.

八月二十六日早晨，我在霍恩西一带望见了海岸，却没有看见什么城镇，因为我把舵转向左舷，继续向南，不再劳神摆弄仪器，只是信马由缰地沿岸而行，时而望得见陆地，时而被一圈海环在中央；不肯向自己承认这拖延迟缓的动机，也根本不去想，只把对明天那深埋的恐惧搁在一边，本能地把自己藏进今天里。我过了沃什湾，过了雅茅斯、费利克斯托。这时，一动不动漂在海上的东西已多得数不清，因为我几乎每低眼十分钟再抬起来，便会看见又一件：于是黄昏后不久，我也不得不躺在它们中间，一动不动，直到天明：因为它们黑压压躺着，一动就会撞上，任何速度的移动都等于把已死的再淹死一遍。
唉，我到了泰晤士河口，一天傍晚八点前后，正停在弗拉茨与潘桑兹中间，离谢佩岛和肯特北岸不到七英里：我没有望见诺尔灯船，也没有格德勒灯船：而且沿岸一路我没见过一盏灯：可这事我一个字也不对自己说，不承认，也不让我的心知道我的脑子在想什么，不让我的脑子知道我的心在猜什么；我只带着一种痴狂的、假意怀疑的眼波，去打量那昏暗的陆地，把它当作一个有知觉的东西，正在对一个像我这样的可怜人耍恶作剧。
第二天早晨我再动身时，我鬼祟的眼角早已清清楚楚望见了王子水道的灯船，还有塔恩灯船，因为它们就在那里：可我根本不看它们，也不靠近：因为凡是我自己见识之外可能发生的事，我一概不想沾惹；不如直望前方，什么也看不见，只管自己顾自己。
再下一天傍晚，重新出海之后，我到了北福兰角偏东偏南一点的地方：那里我没有望见灯，桑德海德也没有灯：只见海上大片大片失事的残迹，海岸上狼藉着陈年的失事船队。我转向东南。

, very slowly moving﻿—for anywhere hereabouts hundreds upon hundreds of craft lay dead within a ten-mile circle of sea﻿—and by two in the fore-day had wandered up well in sight of the French cliffs: for I had said: “I will go and see the light-beam of the great revolving-drum on Calais pier that nightly beams halfway oversea to England.” And the moon shone clear in the southern heaven that morning, like a great old dying queen whose Court swarms distantly from around her, diffident, pale, and tremulous, the paler the nearer; and I could see the mountain-shadows on her spotty full-face, and her misty aureole, and her lights on the sea, as it were kisses stolen in the kingdom of sleep; and all among the quiet ships mysterious white trails and powderings of light, like palace-corridors in some fairyland forlorn, full of breathless wan whispers, scandals, and runnings-to-and-fro, with leers, and agitated last embraces, and flight of the princess, and deathbed of the king; and on the N. E. horizon a bank of brown cloud that seemed to have no relation with the world; and yonder, not far, the white coast-cliffs, not so low as at Calais near, but arranged in masses separated by vales of sward, each with its wreck: but no light of any revolving-drum I saw.

，极慢地移动着——因为这带海上，方圆十英里之内，横七竖八躺着成百上千条死船——到天亮前两点，已游荡到望得见法国悬崖的地方：因为我曾对自己说：「我要去看看加来码头上那座大转鼓的灯，它夜夜把光柱投过半个海峡，照向英格兰。」那天早晨，月亮在南天清清地照着，像一位伟大而垂死的老王后，她的宫廷从她四周围远远散开，怯生生的、苍白而颤抖，越近的越苍白；我看得见她那带斑的圆脸上的山影，她雾蒙蒙的光晕，她落在海上的光，仿佛睡眠王国里偷来的吻；寂静的船群之间，到处是神秘的白色光痕和光屑，像某座荒凉仙乡里的宫中长廊，满是屏息的苍白低语、流言、奔走，斜眼瞟视，激动的最后拥抱，公主的出逃，国王的临终；东北天际上是一垛褐色的云堤，仿佛与世界全不相干；那边不远，是白色的海岸悬崖，不似近加来处那样低，而是成大片大片地排开，间隔着草皮的谷地，每一片下都有它的失事船：可我没有望见任何转鼓的灯。
