In the Severn Valley, somewhere in the plain between Gloucester and Cheltenham, in a rather lonely spot, I at that time travelling on a tricycle-motor, I spied a curious erection, and went to it. I found it of considerable size, perhaps fifty feet square, and thirty high, made of pressed bricks, the perfectly flat roof, too, of brick, and not one window, and only one door: this door, which I found open, was rimmed all round its slanting rims with india-rubber, and when closed must have been perfectly airtight. Just inside I came upon fifteen English people of the dressed class, except two, who were evidently bricklayers: six ladies, and nine men: and at the further end, two more, men, who had their throats cut; along one wall, from end to end were provisions; and I saw a chest full of mixed potassic chlorate and black oxide of manganese, with an apparatus for heating it, and producing oxygen﻿—a foolish thing, for additional oxygen could not alter the quantity of breathed carbonic anhydride, which is a direct narcotic poison. Whether the two with cut throats had sacrificed themselves for the others when breathing difficulties commenced, or been killed by the others, was not clear. When they could bear it no longer, they must have finally opened the door, hoping that by then, after the passage of many days perhaps, the outer air would be harmless, and so met their death. I believe that this erection must have been run up by their own hands under the direction of the two bricklayers, for they could not, I suppose, have got workmen, except on the condition of the workmen’s admission: on which condition they would naturally employ as few as possible.
In general, I remarked that the rich must have been more urgent and earnest in seeking escape than the others: for the poor realised only the near and visible, lived in today, and cherished the always-false notion that tomorrow would be just like today.

在塞文河谷，格洛斯特与切尔滕纳姆之间的平原上，一处相当偏僻的地方——当时我正骑一辆机动三轮车赶路——我望见一座古怪的构筑物，便向它驶去。我发现它相当大，约莫五十英尺见方、三十英尺高，用压制砖砌成，严整的平顶也是砖的，没有一扇窗，只有一扇门：这扇门我发现敞开着，倾斜的门框四周镶着橡胶，关起来想必密不透风。进门不远处，我撞见十五个英国人，皆衣饰体面，只有两个显见是砌砖匠：六位女眷，九个男人；而在最里头，还有两个男人，喉咙被割开了；沿一面墙从这头到那头堆满给养；我又看见一箱混合的氯酸钾和黑色氧化锰，配着加热产氧的装置——一件蠢事，因为补充氧气并不能改变呼出的碳酸气的量，而那是一种直接的麻醉性毒物。那两个割喉的，是呼吸困难开始时为其他人献祭了自己，还是被其他人所杀，不得而知。等到再也忍受不住，他们必定终于打开了门，指望那时——也许许多天已经过去——外面的空气已无害，于是迎头撞上了死亡。我相信这座构筑物是他们亲手在那两个砌砖匠指导下仓促砌起的，因为我想他们雇不到工人，除非以让工人也进来避难为条件：有这个条件，他们自然要用人越少越好。
大体上，我注意到富人求生的急切和认真必定远过他人：因为穷人只认得眼前和看得见的东西，活在今天，抱着那永远虚假的念头，以为明天会和今天一模一样。

In an outpatients’ waiting-room, for instance, in the Gloucester infirmary, I chanced to see an astonishing thing: five bodies of poor old women in shawls, come to have their ailments seen-to on the day of doom; and these, I concluded, had been unable to realise that anything would really happen to the daily old earth which they knew, and had walked with assurance on: for if everybody was to die, they must have thought, who would preach in the Cathedral on Sunday evenings?﻿—so they could not have believed. In an adjoining room sat an old doctor at a table, the stethoscope-tips still clinging in his ears: a woman with bared chest before him; and I thought to myself: “Well, this old man, too, died doing his work.﻿ ﻿…”
In this same infirmary there was one surgical ward﻿—for in a listless mood I went over it﻿—where the patients had died, not of the poison, nor of suffocation, but of hunger: for the doctors, or someone, had made the long room airtight, double-boarding the windows, felting the doors, and then locking them outside; they themselves may have perished before their precautions for the imprisoned patients were complete: for I found a heap of maimed shapes, mere skeletons, crowded round the door within. I knew very well that they had not died of the cloud-poison, for the pestilence of the ward was unmixed with that odour of peach which did not fail to have more or less embalming effects upon the bodies which it saturated. I rushed stifling from that place; and thinking it a pity, and a danger, that such a horror should be, I at once set to work to gather combustibles to burn the building to the ground.
It was while I sat in an armchair in the street the next afternoon, smoking, and watching the flames of this structure, that something was suddenly born in me, something from the lowest Hell: and I smiled a smile that never yet man smiled. And I said: “I will burn, I will burn: I will return to London.﻿ ﻿…”

比如，在格洛斯特贫民疗养院的一间门诊候诊室里，我偶然见到一桩骇人的事：五具披披肩的穷老妇的尸身，末日那天还来瞧她们的老毛病；我断定，她们至死没能明白，她们所熟识、每日安然行走的这颗老地球，当真会出什么事：因为，她们一定这样想，倘若人人都要死，星期天晚上还有谁在大教堂讲道呢？——所以她们不可能相信。隔壁房间里，一个老医生坐在桌旁，听诊器的听头还塞在耳中；他面前是一个袒开胸膛的女人；我心里想：「嗯，这老头，也是死在自己的岗位上。……」
还是在这所疗养院——因为我无精打采地把它巡查了一遍——有一间外科病房，病人们不是死于毒气，也不是死于窒息，而是死于饥饿：因为医生们，或者什么人，把那间长屋封得密不透风，窗子钉了双层板，门缝毡封，然后在外面落了锁；他们自己也许还没来得及完成对被困病人的这些防护，就先遭了难：因为我发现一堆残缺的形体，纯粹的骨架，拥挤在门内。我很清楚他们不是死于云雾之毒，因为病房里的疫气里丝毫不掺那种桃子香气——那香气浸透的尸体，多少总有防腐之效。我窒息般地夺路冲出那地方；一想这等恐怖存在既是可悯、又是祸患，我便立刻动手搜集引火之物，要把那座建筑烧成白地。
就在第二天下午，我坐在街上一张扶手椅里，抽着烟，望着那座建筑的大火时，有个东西在我体内骤然降生了，一个来自最底层地狱的东西：我露出了一个人类从未露出过的微笑。我说：「我要烧，我要烧：我要回伦敦去。……」
