===Original===

I went out from that building that morning feeling like a man bowed down with age, for the depths of unutterable horror into which I had had glimpses during that one night made me very feeble, and my steps tottered, and my brain reeled.
I got out into Farringdon Street, and at the near Circus, where four streets meet, had under my furthest range of vision nothing but four fields of bodies, bodies, clad in a rag-shop of every faded colour, or half-clad, or not clad at all, actually, in many cases, overlying one another, as I had seen at Reading, but here with a markedly more skeleton appearance: for I saw the swollen-looking shoulders, sharp hips, hollow abdomens, and stiff bony limbs of people dead from famine, the whole having the grotesque air of some macabre battlefield of fallen marionettes. Mixed with these was an extraordinary number of vehicles of all sorts, so that I saw that driving among them would be impracticable, whereas the street which I had taken during the night was fairly clear. I thought a minute what I should do: then went by a parallel back-street, and came out to a shop in the Strand, where I hoped to find all the information which I needed about the excavations of the country. The shutters were up, and I did not wish to make any noise among these people, though the morning was bright, it being about ten o’clock, and it was easy to effect entrance, for I saw a crowbar in a big covered furniture-van near. I, therefore, went northward, till I came to the British Museum, the cataloguing-system of which I knew well, and passed in. There was no one at the library-door to bid me stop, and in the great round reading-room not a soul, except one old man with a bag of goître hung at his neck, and spectacles, he lying up a book-ladder near the shelves, a “reader” to the last. I got to the printed catalogues, and for an hour was upstairs among the dim sacred galleries of this still place, and at the sight of certain Greek and Coptic papyri, charters, seals, had such a dream of this ancient earth, my good God, as even an angel’s pen could not half express on paper.

那天早晨，我从那座楼里走出来，感觉自己像个被年岁压弯的人：那一夜里我窥见了无可言说之恐怖的深渊，这使我衰弱极了，脚步踉跄，脑子发旋。
我出到法灵顿街，在近处的环形广场——四条街的交汇处——我最远的视野里，只有四片尸的旷野，尸体，尸体，裹着一家褪尽各色的旧衣铺，或半裸，或干脆一丝不挂，许多还层层叠压在一起，同我在雷丁见过的一样，只是这里的骸骨之相明显更甚：我看见饿死的人们那肿胀模样的肩、尖削的骻、凹陷的腹、僵硬的骨架四肢，整个场面透着一股滑稽之气，像一座傀儡兵横陈的阴森战场。其间还混杂着数量惊人的各式车辆，我一看便知从中行车断不可行，而夜里我走的那条街倒还相当空。我想了想该怎么办：便取道一条平行的后街，来到斯特兰德街的一家铺子门前，我希望在那里找到所需的关于全国各地矿坑的全部资料。铺子上着门板，我不愿在这些人中间弄出声响，虽然天色晴明，时约十点；进去倒容易，因为近旁一辆带篷的大家具货车里就有一根撬棍。于是我转向北行，一直走到大英博物馆，它的编目体系我很熟悉，便径直进去。图书馆门口无人喝止我，圆形大阅览室里也空无一人，只有一位颈上挂着甲状腺肿袋、戴着眼镜的老人，他伏在书架旁的书梯顶端，至死是个『读者』。我找到印刷目录，随后一个钟头都在楼上，置身于这静穆之地幽暗而神圣的陈列室之间；一见那些希腊文和科普特文的纸草、特许状和印玺，我便做起了一场关于这颗古老尘寰的大梦，我的上帝，连天使的笔也写不尽它的一半。

Afterwards, I went away loaded with a good hundredweight of Ordnance-maps, which I had stuffed into a bag found in the cloakroom, with three topographical books; I then, at an instrument-maker’s in Holborn, got a sextant and theodolite, and at a grocer’s near the river put into a sack-bag provisions to last me a week or two; at Blackfriars Bridge wharf-station I found a little sharp white steamer of a few tons, which happily was driven by liquid air, so that I had no troublesome fire to light: and by noon I was cutting my solitary way up the Thames, which flowed as before the ancient Britons were born, and saw it, and built mud-huts there amid the primaeval forest; and afterwards the Romans came, and saw it, and called it Tamesis, or Thamesis.

之后，我满载而去：一大包军械测量局的地图，足足一英担重，塞进一件从衣帽间找到的提包里，还带上三本地形学着作；随后我在霍尔本一家仪器行弄到六分仪和经纬仪，在河边一家杂货铺往一只帆布袋里装了一两周的口粮；在黑修士桥的码头车站，我发现一艘几吨位的小巧雪白汽艇，幸好它用液体空气驱动，我省了生火的麻烦：到中午，我已独自溯泰晤士河而上；这河自不列颠先民诞生之前便一直这样流，他们见了它，在原始森林中垒起泥舍；后来罗马人来了，见了它，称它塔梅西斯，或泰晤西斯。
