===Original===

That night, from eleven till the next day, there was a great storm over England: let me note it down. And ten days later, on the 17th of the month came another; and on the 23rd another; and I should be put to it to count the great number since. And they do not resemble English storms, but rather Arctic ones, in a certain very suggestive something of personalness, and a carousing malice, and a Tartarus gloom, which I cannot quite describe. That night at Guildford, after wandering about, and becoming very weary, I threw myself upon a cushioned pew in an old Norman church with two east apses, called St. Mary’s, using a Bible-cushion for pillow, and placing some distance away a little tin lamp turned low, whose ray served me for *veilleuse* through the night. Happily I had taken care to close up everything, or, I feel sure, the roof must have gone. Only one dead, an old lady in a chapel on the north side of the chancel, whom I rather mistrusted, was there with me: and there I lay listening: for, after all, I could not sleep a wink, while outside vogued the immense tempest. And I communed with myself, thinking: “I, poor man, lost in this conflux of infinitudes and vortex of the world, what can become of me, my God? For dark, ah dark, is the waste void into which from solid ground I am now plunged a million fathoms deep, the sport of all the whirlwinds: and it were better for me to have died with the dead, and never to have seen the wrath and turbulence of the Ineffable, nor to have heard the thrilling bleakness of the winds of Eternity, when they pine, and long, and whimper, and when they vociferate and blaspheme, and when they expostulate and intrigue and implore, and when they despair and die, which ear of man should never hear. For they mean to eat me up, I know, these Titanic darknesses: and soon like a whiff I shall pass away, and leave the world to them.” So till next morning I lay mumping, with shivers and cowerings: for the shocks of the storm pervaded the locked church to my very heart; and there were thunders that night, my God, like callings and laughs and banterings, exchanged between distant hilltops in Hell.

===Chinese===

那天夜里，从十一点起到第二天，一场大风暴横扫英格兰：让我把它记下来。十天后，本月17日，又来了一场；23日又一场；此后大大小小多得我简直数不过来。它们全不像英格兰的风暴，倒像北极的风暴，带着某种非常意味深长的私人怨气，带着狂欢般的恶意和塔耳塔洛斯式的幽冥，我说不太清楚。那夜在吉尔福德，我游荡了一番，困乏已极，便一头倒在圣玛丽教堂——一座带两座东端半圆堂的老诺曼教堂——一张带软垫的教堂长椅上，拿《圣经》的靠垫当枕头，又把一盏捻小了的锡灯放在稍远处，那一缕光便做了我通宵的*守夜灯*。幸而我事先留心关严了一切门窗，否则我敢肯定，屋顶非被掀走不可。偌大教堂里只有我一个死者作伴——北耳堂小礼拜堂里一位老太太，我对她总有点信不过——我就那样躺着听：因为任凭怎么着，我一觉也睡不着，外面肆虐着那场浩大的风暴。我自思自忖：「我这个可怜人，迷失在这万千无限的汇流和世界的旋涡里，我的上帝，我的下场会怎样呢？因为我现在从坚实的地面跌进的那片荒寂虚空，黑啊，黑得深不可测，我在里面沉了百万英寻，成了万千旋风的玩物：倒不如随死者一同死了干净，免得看见那不可言说者的震怒与狂乱，免得听见永恒之风的凛冽哀音——它们如何憔悴，如何渴念，如何呜咽；它们如何喧叫亵渎；它们如何抗争、密谋、恳求；它们又如何绝望、死去——这些，人耳本不该听见。因为我知道，这些提坦般的黑暗是要把我吃掉的：我很快就会像一缕轻烟消散，把世界留给它们。」就这样直到次日清晨，我一直蜷缩着打颤：因为风暴的震荡穿透紧闭的教堂，直透进我心里；那夜还有雷声，我的上帝，像地狱里相隔遥远的群山之巅，彼此应和的呼唤、大笑和调侃。
