===Original===

Three nights I slept in Stamboul itself at the palace of some sanjak-bey or emir, or rather dozed, with one slumbrous eye that would open to watch my visitors Sinbad, and Ali Baba, and old Haroun, to see how they slumbered and dozed: for it was in the small luxurious chamber where the bey received those speechless all-night visits of the Turks, long rosy hours of perfumed romance, and drunkenness of the fancy, and visionary languor, sinking toward morning into the yet deeper peace of dreamless sleep; and there, still, were the white *yataks* for the guests to sit cross-legged on for the waking dream, and to fall upon for the final swoon, and the copper brazier still scenting of essence-of-rose, and the cushions, rugs, hangings, the monsters on the wall, the haschish-chibouques, narghiles, hookahs, and drugged pale cigarettes, and a secret-looking lattice beyond the door, painted with trees and birds; and the air narcotic and grey with the pastilles which I had burned, and the scented smokes which I had smoked; and I all drugged and mumbling, my left eye suspicious of Ali there, and Sinbad, and old Haroun, who dozed. And when I had slept, and rose to wash in a room near the overhanging latticed balcony of the façade, before me to the north lay old Galata in sunshine, and that steep large street mounting to Pera, once full at every nightfall of divans on which grave dervishes smoked narghiles, and there was no space for passage, for all was divans, lounges, almond-trees, heaven-high hum, chibouques in forests, the dervish, and the innumerable porter, the horse-hirer with his horse from Tophana, and arsenal-men from Kassim, and traders from Galata, and artillery-workmen from Tophana; and on the other side of the house, the south end, a covered bridge led across a street, which consisted mostly of two immense blind walls, into a great tangled wilderness of flowers, which was the harem-garden, where I passed some hours; and here I might have remained many days, many weeks perhaps, but that, dozing one fore-day with those fancied others, it was as if there occurred a laugh somewhere, and a thing said: “But this city is not quite dead!” waking me from deeps of peace to startled wakefulness. And I thought to myself: “If it be not quite dead, it *will* be soon﻿—and with some suddenness!” And the next morning I was at the Arsenal.

===Chinese===

我在斯坦布尔城里睡了三夜，睡在某位桑贾克贝或埃米尔的宫里——或者说是打盹，一只惺忪的眼睛不时睁开，守着我的来访者辛巴达、阿里巴巴和老哈伦，看他们怎样瞌睡打盹：因为那正是贝伊接待土耳其人通宵无言来访的小小安乐寝殿——漫长的玫瑰色时辰，香的传奇，想象的沉醉，幻景的慵懒，向天明一路沉入无梦之眠更深的安宁；白色的坐褥（yataks）还在原处，供客人盘腿坐上去做那醒着的梦，再一头栽倒，完成最后的晕遁；铜火盆里还留着玫瑰精油的气味；还有坐垫、地毯、帷幔、墙上的怪兽图、大麻长烟斗、水烟筒、胡卡，和掺药的淡色香烟；门后还有那副神秘模样的花格窗，绘着树和鸟；空气里有我烧的香锭、我抽的香熏的烟，麻醉而灰蒙蒙的；我整个人被熏得迷迷糊糊，嘟嘟囔囔，左眼提防着那儿的阿里、辛巴达和老哈伦，而他们在打盹。睡够了，我起身到正面悬空花格阳台近旁的一间屋里洗漱，北面眼前是阳光下的老加拉塔，那条陡峭的大街爬上佩拉——从前每到黄昏，满街都是长榻，庄重的托钵僧们坐在上面抽水烟，没有插足的空隙，因为满街全是长榻、卧榻、杏树、响彻云天的嗡嗡声、林子似的长烟斗、托钵僧、数不清的挑夫、从托法纳牵马来的租马人、卡西姆来的兵工厂伙计、加拉塔来的买卖人、托法纳来的炮工；宅子另一头，南端，一道带顶的桥跨过一条街——那街多半由两堵巨大的盲墙构成——通进一大片花草的乱野，那是内院花园，我在里面消磨了几个钟头；我本可以在那儿待上许多天，也许许多星期，可是有天上午，我正同那些想象中的同伴打盹，仿佛什么地方响起一声笑，有个声音说：「可这座城还没有全死呢！」把我从安宁的深处惊醒，激灵一下睁开眼。我心中自语：「它要是还没全死，很快就会了——而且会死得相当突然！」第二天早晨我就到了兵工厂。
