## XXXII The Men of Shadowy Vale Come to the Spring Market at Burgstead / 三十二 幽谷之人来堡镇赶春市

===Original===
On the morrow betimes in the morning the Westland chapmen, who were now all come, went out from the House of the Face, where they were ever wont to be lodged, and set up their booths adown the street betwixt gate and bridge. Gay was the show; for the booths were tilted over with painted cloths, and the merchants themselves were clad in long gowns of fine cloth; scarlet, and blue, and white, and green, and black, with broidered welts of gold and silver; and their knaves were gaily attired in short coats of divers hues, with silver rings about their arms, and short swords girt to their sides. People began to gather about these chapmen at once when they fell to opening their bales and their packs, and unloading their wains. There had they iron, both in pigs and forged scrap and nails; steel they had, and silver, both in ingots and vessel; pearls from over sea; cinnabar and other colours for staining, such as were not in the mountains: madder from the marshes, and purple of the sea, and scarlet grain from the holm-oaks by its edge, and woad from the deep clayey fields of the plain; silken thread also from the outer ocean, and rare webs of silk, and jars of olive oil, and fine pottery, and scented woods, and sugar of the cane. But gold they had none with them, for that they took there; and for weapons, save a few silver-gilt toys, they had no market.

===Chinese===
翌日清晨，西土（the Westland）的客商们——此时已悉数到齐——自面神之宅（the House of the Face）而出，他们向来都在此处下榻；于是在城门与桥之间的街两旁支起摊棚。那场面甚是鲜丽：摊棚上张着彩绘布幔，商人们身着细布长袍，有猩红、蓝、白、绿、黑诸色，上以金银线绣边为饰；他们的仆役也打扮得光鲜，衣五色短衫，臂上戴银环，腰间悬短剑。客商们一开箱解囊、卸下车上的货物，众人便围拢过来。他们带来的货物中，有铁——既有铁锭，也有锻过的废铁与铁钉；有钢，有银——银锭与银器皆有；有海外来的珍珠；有朱砂及山中所无的各色染料：沼地所产的茜草、海中所制的紫、海滨橡树所产的猩红胭脂虫、平原深黏土田里所产的靛蓝；又有外洋来的丝线、罕见的丝绸织物、一坛坛橄榄油、精致的陶器、香料木、甘蔗所制的糖。唯独黄金他们未曾携来——因为黄金正是在此地收取的；至于兵器，除少量鎏金的玩物小件外，他们并不发卖。

===Original===
So presently they fell to chaffer; for the carles brought them little bags of the river-borne gold, so that the weights and scales were at work; others had with them scrolls and tallies to tell the number of the beasts which they had to sell, and the chapmen gave them wares therefor without beholding the beasts; for they wotted that the Dalesmen lied not in chaffer. While the day was yet young withal came the Dalesmen from the mid and nether Dale with their wares and set up their booths; and they had with them flasks and kegs of the wine which they had to sell; and bales of the good winter-woven cloth, some grey, some dyed, and pieces of fine linen; and blades of swords, and knives, and axes of such fashion as the Westland men used; and golden cups and chains, and fair rings set with mountain-blue stones, and copper bowls, and vessels gilt and parcel-gilt, and mountain-blue for staining. There were men of the Shepherds also with such fleeces as they could spare from the daily chaffer with the neighbours. And of the Woodlanders were four carles and a woman with peltries and dressed deer-skins, and a few pieces of well-carven woodwork for bedsteads and chairs and suchlike.

===Chinese===
于是众人不久便做起交易：汉子们拿来一小袋一小袋河中淘得的砂金，天平砝码随之忙碌起来；也有人带着卷册与符木（tallies），记下要出售的牲畜数目，客商们据此付给货物，并不去看牲口；因为他们知道谷民（the Dalesmen）在做买卖时从不说谎。日头尚早，谷中及下游的谷民也带着货物赶来，支起摊棚：他们带来了一瓶瓶、一桶桶要出售的酒；一捆捆冬日织成的好布，有素灰的，有染色的；还有上等的亚麻布匹；有剑刃、刀具、斧头，样式正如西土人所惯用；又有金杯金链，镶嵌山中蓝宝石的精美戒指，铜碗，以及鎏金或半鎏金的器皿，还有作染料用的山蓝。牧羊之民（the Shepherd-Folk）中也有人带来羊毛——那是从平日与邻人交易之余省下来的。林中汉子（the Woodland-Carles）也来了四个汉子、一个妇人，带着皮货、鞣制好的鹿皮，还有几件雕工精良的木器，是做床架、椅子之类的用物。

===Original===
Soon was the Burg thronged with folk in all its open places, and all were eager and merry, and it could not have been told from their demeanour and countenance that the shadow of a grievous trouble hung over them. True it was that every man of the Dale and the neighbours was girt with his sword, or bore spear or axe or other weapon in his hand, and that most had their bucklers at their backs and their helms on their heads; but this was ever their custom at all meetings of men, not because they dreaded war or were fain of strife, but in token that they were free men, from whom none should take the weapons without battle.

Such were the folk of the land: as for the chapmen, they were well-spoken and courteous, and blithe with the folk, as they well might be, for they had good pennyworths of them; yet they dealt with them without using measureless lying, as behoved folk dealing with simple and proud people; and many was the tale they told of the tidings of the Cities and the Plain.

There amongst the throng was the Bride in her maiden's attire, but girt with the sword, going from booth to booth with her guests of the Runaways, and doing those poor people what pleasure she might, and giving them gifts from the goods there, such as they set their hearts on. And the more part of the Runaways were about among the people of the Fair; but Dallach, being still weak, sat on a bench by the door of the House of the Face looking on well-pleased at all the stir of folk.

===Chinese===
不多时，堡镇（Burgstead）各处空地都挤满了人，个个兴高采烈、欢天喜地；单看他们的神色举止，决看不出有一重忧患的阴影笼罩在众人头上。诚然，谷中及四邻的男子个个腰悬刀剑，或手执长枪、斧头等兵器，大多数人背上还负着小圆盾，头上戴着盔；但这原是他们凡有人聚之处一向的规矩，并非因为畏惧战事、好兴干戈，而是以此为记：他们乃是自由之人，除非兵戎相见，谁也不能夺去他们的兵器。

当地之民便是如此；至于那些客商，谈吐文雅、举止有礼，与众人相处甚欢——这也难怪，他们从这些人身上做足了便宜买卖；然而他们与众人交易时，并不用漫天谎话，正如与淳朴而高傲之民打交道时所应当的那般；他们又讲了许多平原诸城（the Cities of the Plain）与平原（the Plain）上的见闻故事。

新娘（the Bride）也在人群之中，穿的是闺中少女的衣裳，腰间却佩着剑；她带着她那些逃奴（the Runaways）客人，从一个摊子走到另一个摊子，尽其所能讨这些可怜人的欢心，从摊上货物中挑出他们心仪之物赠给他们。那些逃奴大半都在集市的人群中走动；唯有达拉赫（Dallach）身子尚弱，坐在面神之宅门前一条长凳上，满心欢喜地看着人来人往的热闹。

===Original===
Hall-face was gone on the woodland ward; while Face-of-god went among the folk in his most glorious attire; but he soon betook him to the place of meeting without the Gate, where Stone-face and some of the elders were sitting along with the Alderman, beside whom sat the head man of the merchants, clad in a gown of fine scarlet embroidered with the best work of the Dale, with a golden chaplet on his head, and a good sword, golden-hilted, by his side, all which the Alderman had given to it him that morning. These chiefs were talking together concerning the tidings of the Plain, and many a tale the guest told to the Dalesmen, some true, some false. For there had been battles down there, and the fall of kings, and destruction of people, as oft befalleth in the guileful Cities. He told them also, in answer to their story of the Dusky Men, of how men even suchlike, but riding on horses, or drawn in wains, an host not to be numbered, had erewhile overthrown the hosts of the Cities of the Plain, and had wrought evils scarce to be told of; and how they had piled up the skulls of slaughtered folk into great hills beside the city-gates, so that the sun might no longer shine into the streets; and how because of the death and the rapine, grass had grown in the kings' chambers, and the wolves had chased deer in the Temples of the Gods.

===Chinese===
厅面（Hall-face）已去巡林之役了；面神则盛装走入人群之中；但他不久便转到城门外那处聚议之所去了，石面（Stone-face）与几位老人正与长老（the Alderman）同坐一处，长老身旁坐着众商之首，身着一件细绒猩红长袍，绣的是堡谷（Burgdale）最精的工，头戴金编花冠，腰悬一口鎏金剑柄的好剑——这一切都是长老那天早晨赠给他的。这几位首脑人物正谈论着平原上的消息，那客人向谷民讲了许多故事，有真有假。原来南方彼处曾有战役，有国王倾覆，有百姓遭殃——这在奸险的诸城之中乃是常事。他又应他们所讲的黝暗人（the Dusky Men）之事说道：有一族与此相类之人，骑马或乘辇车而来，其数不可胜计，不久之前倾覆了平原诸城的大军，所造之恶几乎难以尽述；他们把所杀之人的头颅堆在城门两旁，垒成一座座大丘，使日头再也照不进街巷之中；又因杀戮与劫掠，国王的宫室里长出了野草，神庙之中，狼群追逐着野鹿。

===Original===
"But," quoth he, "I know you, bold tillers of the soil, valiant scourers of the Wildwood, that the worst that can befall you will be to die under shield, and that ye shall suffer no torment of the thrall. May the undying Gods bless the threshold of this Gate, and oft may I come hither to taste of your kindness! May your race, the uncorrupt, increase and multiply, till your valiant men and clean maidens make the bitter sweet and purify the earth!"

===Chinese===
"然而，"他道，"我知道你们这些勇敢的耕夫、纵横野林（the Wildwood）的壮士，你们所能遭逢的最坏下场，也不过是执盾战死沙场，断不会受那为奴的苦楚。愿那不死的众神赐福这座城门的门槛！愿我常常能来此，领受你们的厚谊！愿你们这未曾腐化的种族繁衍兴旺，直到你们的壮士与贞洁的少女，将苦涩化为甘甜，涤净这大地！"

===Original===
He spake smooth-tongued and smiling, handling the while the folds of his fine scarlet gown, and belike he meant a full half of what he said; for he was a man very eloquent of speech, and had spoken with kings, uncowed and pleased with his speaking; and for that cause and his riches had he been made chief of the chapmen. As he spake the heart of Face-of-god swelled within him, and his cheek flushed; but Iron-face sat up straight and proud, and a light smile played about his face, as he said gravely:

"Friend of the Westland, I thank thee for the blessing and the kind word. Such as we are, we are; nor do I deem that the very Gods shall change us. And if they will be our friends, it is well; for we desire nought of them save their friendship; and if they will be our foes, that also shall we bear; nor will we curse them for doing that which their lives bid them to do. What sayest thou, Face-of-god, my son?"

"Yea, father," said Face-of-god, "I say that the very Gods, though they slay me, cannot unmake my life that has been. If they do deeds, yet shall we also do."

The Outlander smiled as they spake, and bowed his head to Iron-face and Face-of-god, and wondered at their pride of heart, marvelling what they would say to the great men of the Cities if they should meet them.

===Chinese===
他说得辞令圆融、面带微笑，一面抚弄着那件猩红细绒长袍的褶裥；他这番话，怕是有大半是真心。原是个极善言辞之人，曾与国王对语而不卑不亢、颇自得意；正因如此，加上他的财力，才被推为众客商之首。他说话之际，面神心潮激荡，双颊泛红；铁面（Iron-face）却挺身端坐，傲然不羁，嘴角浮起一丝淡笑，郑重说道：

"西土的朋友，多谢你的祝福与善言。我们是什么样的人，便是什么样的人；我并不以为众神便能改变我们。他们若肯与我们为友，那是好事；因为我们除他们的情谊外，别无所求；他们若要与我为敌，我们也承受得起；他们不过是各为其生而行事，我们决不因此而咒骂他们。面神，我的儿，你以为如何？"

面神道："父亲，我以为，便是众神亲自将我杀了，也不能抹除我已经走过的一生。他们行事，我们也行事。"

那外乡人（the Outlander）听他们说话，含笑点头，向铁面与面神拱手，心中暗暗惊异他们的心高气傲，不禁揣想：倘若他们遇见诸城中的大人物，又会说出怎样的话来。

===Original===
But as they sat a-talking, there came two men running to them from the Portway, their weapons all clattering upon them, and they heard withal the sound of a horn winded not far off very loud and clear; and the Chapman's cheek paled: for in sooth he doubted that war was at hand, after all he had heard of the Dalesmen's dealings with the Dusky Men. And all battle was loathsome to him, nor for all the gain of his chaffer had he come into the Dale, had he known that war was looked for.

But the chiefs of the Dalesmen stirred not, nor changed countenance; and some of the goodmen who were in the street nigh the Gate came forth to see what was toward; for they also had heard the voice of the horn.

Then one of those messengers came up breathless, and stood before the chiefs, and said:

"New tidings, Alderman; here be weaponed strangers come into the Dale."

===Chinese===
正谈间，自港路（the Portway）那头跑来两个汉子，身上兵器叮当作响，又听得近处号角之声嘹亮清越；那客商面色登时发白：他素来听闻谷民与黝暗人之间的事，此刻当真怕是战事临头了。他生平最厌恶兵戈，倘若早知道此地料将有战，莫说图这买卖上的赚头，他断不会踏入此谷。

然而谷民的首脑们纹丝不动，面不改色；街市近城门处有几个户主走出来看是怎么一回事，因为他们也听见了号角之声。

随即一个信使气喘吁吁奔近前来，立在那几位首脑面前，说道：

"长老，有新消息；有带兵器的陌生人入了山谷。"

===Original===
The Alderman smiled on him and said: "Yea, son, and are they a great host of men?"

"Nay," said the man, "not above a score as I deem, and there is a woman with them."

"Then shall we abide them here," said the Alderman, "and thou mightest have saved thy breath, and suffered them to bring tidings of themselves; since they may scarce bring us war. For no man desireth certain and present death; and that is all that such a band may win at our hands in battle today; and all who come in peace are welcome to us. What like are they to behold?"

Said the man: "They are tall men gloriously attired, so that they seem like kinsmen of the Gods; and they bear flowering boughs in their hands."

The Alderman laughed, and said: "If they be Gods they are welcome indeed; and they shall grow the wiser for their coming; for they shall learn how guest-fain the Burgdale men may be. But if, as I deem, they be like unto us, and but the children of the Gods, then are they as welcome, and it may be more so, and our greeting to them shall be as their greeting to us would be."

===Chinese===
长老对他笑了笑，说道："好啦，孩子，他们是一支大军么？"

那汉子道："不然，依我看不过二十来人，其中还有一个女子。"

长老道："那我们就在此迎候他们罢，你这口气原可省下，由他们自己来报信；这般人数，总不至于带来战祸。须知无人自寻必死之途——这样一支小队今日与我们在战场上相见，所能赢的，不过是一场眼前必死之战罢了；凡是怀善意而来的，我们都欢迎。他们相貌如何？"

那汉子道："都是些高大汉子，装束华美，看去宛如众神的亲族；他们手中都擎着花枝。"

长老大笑，说道："若是神祇，那真是再欢迎不过；他们此来定要长些见识，因为他们将见识到堡谷之民是如何好客。然而若如我所料，他们与我们相类，不过是众神的子孙，那也一样欢迎，说不定更加欢迎；我们待他们的礼数，当与他们待我们的一样。"

===Original===
Even as he spake the horn was winded nearer yet, and more loudly, and folk came pouring out of the Gate to learn the tidings. Presently the strangers came from off the Portway into the space before the Gate; and their leader was a tall and goodly man of some thirty winters, in glorious array, helm on head and sword by side, his surcoat green and flowery like the spring meads. In his right hand he held a branch of the blossomed blackthorn (for some was yet in blossom), and his left had hold of the hand of an exceeding fair woman who went beside him: behind him was a score of weaponed men in goodly attire, some bearing bows, some long spears, but each bearing a flowering bough in hand.

The tall man stopped in the midst of the space, and the Alderman and they with him stirred not; though, as for Face-of-god, it was to him as if summer had come suddenly into the midst of winter, and for the very sweetness of delight his face grew pale.

===Chinese===
他话音未落，号角之声又起，更近更响，众人纷纷自城门涌出探听消息。不多时，那些陌生人从港路转入了城门前的空地；为首一人是个三十来岁的魁梧好汉，装束华美，头戴盔，腰悬剑，罩袍碧绿，上绣繁花，宛如春日之草甸。他右手擎着一枝开花的黑刺李（其时尚有花未谢），左手携着一位极美貌的女子之手，那女子行在他身侧：他身后随着二十名带甲之士，装束鲜明，有的持弓，有的执长枪，但每个人手中都擎着一枝花枝。

那高大汉子在空地中央停下，长老及左右之人纹丝不动；至于面神，只觉如严冬之中忽然到了盛夏，那份欢喜之甘美，竟教他面色发白了。

===Original===
Then the newcomer drew nigh to the Alderman and said:

"Hail to the Gate and the men of the Gate! Hail to the kindred of the children of the Gods!"

But the Alderman stood up and spake: "And hail to thee, tall man! Fair greeting to thee and thy company! Wilt thou name thyself with thine own name, or shall I call thee nought save Guest? Welcome art thou, by whatsoever name thou wilt be called. Here may'st thou and thy folk abide as long as ye will."

===Chinese===
那新来之人走上前至长老跟前，说道：

"城门及守门之人万福！众神之子的亲族万福！"

长老起身答道："高大的壮士，你也万福！你与你的同伴们受我敬礼！你是要自报姓名呢，还是要我只称你为客？你愿人如何称呼你，都一律欢迎。你与你的人尽可在此久住，随你们心意。"

===Original===
Said the newcomer: "Thanks have thou for thy greeting and for thy bidding! And that bidding shall we take, whatsoever may come of it; for we are minded to abide with thee for a while. But know thou, O Alderman of the Dalesmen, that I am not sackless toward thee and thine. My name is Folk-might of the Children of the Wolf, and this woman is the Sun-beam, my sister, and these behind me are of my kindred, and are well beloved and trusty. We are no evil men or wrongdoers; yet have we been driven into sore straits, wherein men must needs at whiles do deeds that make their friends few and their foes many. So it may be that I am thy foeman. Yet, if thou doubtest of me that I shall be a baneful guest, thou shalt have our weapons of us, and then mayest thou do thy will upon us without dread; and here first of all is my sword!"

===Chinese===
那新来之人道："多谢你的礼遇与相邀！这相邀我们领下了，无论后果如何；因为我们正有意在你这里盘桓一时。然而，谷民的长老啊，你要知道，我对你与你族中人并非无罪在身。我的名字叫民强（Folk-might），出自狼之子民（the Children of the Wolf）；这女子是日光（the Sun-beam），我的妹妹；我身后这些人都是我的族人，都是我深爱而信赖之人。我们并非恶徒，亦非为非作歹之辈；然而我们被人逼入绝境，人在那种境地之中，有时不得不行些令朋友稀少、仇人增多的举动。所以我或许正是你的仇敌。然而，你若疑心我是个招祸之客，尽管取去我们的兵器，然后便可毫无顾忌地随意处置我们；这便是我的剑，先交与你！"

===Original===
Therewith he cast down the flowering branch he was bearing, and pulled his sword from out his sheath, and took it by the point, and held out the hilt to Iron-face.

But the Alderman smiled kindly on him and said:

"The blade is a good one, and I say it who know the craft of sword-forging; but I need it not, for thou seest I have a sword by my side. Keep your weapons, one and all; for ye have come amongst many and those no weaklings: and if so be that thy guilt against us is so great that we must needs fall on you, ye will need all your war-gear. But hereof is no need to speak till the time of the Folk-mote, which will be holden in three days' wearing; so let us forbear this matter till then; for I deem we shall have enough to say of other matters. Now, Folk-might, sit down beside me, and thou also, Sun-beam, fairest of women."

===Chinese===

说罢他又转身入内，进了厅堂，命人引新来之人到高台之上就座；送上最好的酒，及现成可得的肉食。他又吩咐众人尽力备下一席盛筵，以备傍晚之用；众人即刻依命去办。
