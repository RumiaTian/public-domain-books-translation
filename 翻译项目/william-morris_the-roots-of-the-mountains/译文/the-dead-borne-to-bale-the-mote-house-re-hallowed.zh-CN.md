## LI The Dead Borne to Bale: The Mote-House Re-Hallowed / 五十一 亡者舁至柴堆：议事厅重行圣化

===Original===
On the morrow they bore to bale their slain men, and there withal what was left of the bodies of the four chieftains of the Great Undoing. They brought them into a most fair meadow to the west of Silverstead, where they had piled up a very great bale for the burning. In that meadow was the Doom-ring and Thingstead of the Folk of the Wolf, and they had hallowed it when they had first conquered Silverdale, and it was deemed far holier than the Mote-house aforesaid, wherein the men of the kindred might hold no due court; but rather it was a Feast-hall, and a house where men had converse together, and wherein precious things and tokens of the Fathers were stored up.

===Chinese===
次日，他们将其阵亡之人舁至柴堆，并将大覆灭（the Great Undoing）中四位酋长遗体所余之残骸，一并付诸焚化。他们将遗体舁至银镇（Silverstead）以西一片极美之草甸，在那里早已垛起一座极大之柴堆，以供焚化。此草甸之中有狼族（the Folk of the Wolf）之判决环（the Doom-ring）与议事场（Thingstead），乃他们初征服银谷（Silverdale）之时即已祝圣之地；世人视此地为远较前述议事厅（the Mote-house）更为神圣——盖宗族之人于彼厅之中不得举行正式之裁断；彼处实乃一宴厅（Feast-hall），为众人相聚交谈之所，亦为珍藏贵重之物及列祖遗物之处。

===Original===
The Thingstead in the meadow was flowery and well-grassed, and a little stream winding about thereby nearly cast a ring around it; and beyond the stream was a full fair grove of oak-trees, very tall and ancient. There then they burned the dead of the Host, wrapped about in exceeding fair raiment. And when the ashes were gathered, the men of Burgdale and the Shepherds left those of their folk for the kindred to bury there in Silverdale; for they said that they had a right to claim such guesting for them that had helped to win back the Dale.

===Chinese===
草甸之中这一方议事场，繁花铺地、青草丰美，一道细溪蜿蜒其间，几乎绕成一环；溪流彼岸有一片极美之柞树林，极其高大古老。他们便在那里焚化大军之阵亡者，遗骸皆裹以极其华美之衣裳。待骨灰收聚既毕，堡谷（Burgdale）之人与牧羊之民（the Shepherds）将其本族阵亡者留下，托宗族在银谷就地安葬；因为他们说，那些助其夺回此谷之人，有权求得这般客葬之礼。

===Original===
But when the Burning was done and the bale quenched, and the ashes gathered and buried (and that was on the morrow), then men bore forth the Banners of the Jaws of the Wolf, and the Red Hand, and the Silver Arm, and the Golden Bushel, and the Ragged Sword, and the Wolf of the Woodland; and with great joy and triumph they brought them into the Mote-house and hung them up over the dais; and they kindled fire on the Holy Hearth by holding up a disk of bright glass to the sun; and then they sang before the banners. And this is somewhat of the song that they sang before them:

> Why are ye wending? O whence and whither?
 
 What shineth over the fallow swords?
 
 What is the joy that ye bear in hither?
 
 What is the tale of your blended words?
> No whither we wend, but here have we stayed us,
 
 Here by the ancient Holy Hearth;
 
 Long have the moons and the years delayed us,
 
 But here are we come from the heart of the dearth.
> We are the men of joy belated;
 
 We are the wanderers over the waste;
 
 We are but they that sat and waited,
 
 Watching the empty winds make haste.
> Long, long we sat and knew no others,
 
 Save alien folk and the foes of the road;
 
 Till late and at last we met our brothers,
 
 And needs must we to the old abode.
> For once on a day they prayed for guesting;
 
 And how were we then their bede to do?
 
 Wild was the waste for the people’s resting,
 
 And deep the wealth of the Dale we knew.
> Here were the boards that we must spread them
 
 Down in the fruitful Dale and dear;
 
 Here were the halls where we would bed them:
 
 And how should we tarry otherwhere?
> Over the waste we came together:
 
 There was the tangle athwart the way;
 
 There was the windstorm and the weather;
 
 The red rain darkened down the day.
> But that day of the days what grief should let us,
 
 When we saw through the clouds the dale-glad sun?
 
 We tore at the tangle that beset us,
 
 And stood at peace when the day was done.
> Hall of the Happy, take our greeting!
 
 Bid thou the Fathers come and see
 
 The Folk-signs on thy walls a-meeting,
 
 And deem today what men we be.
> Look on the Holy Hearth new-litten,
 
 How the sparks fly twinkling up aloof!
 
 How the wavering smoke by the sunlight smitten,
 
 Curls up around the beam-rich roof!
> For here once more is the Wolf abiding,
 
 Nor ever more from the Dale shall wend,
 
 And never again his head be hiding,
 
 Till all days be dark and the world have end.

===Chinese===
焚化既毕、柴堆熄灭，骨灰收聚安葬（此乃又次日之事），众人乃请出诸旗：狼之颚（the Jaws of the Wolf）、红手（the Red Hand）、银臂（the Silver Arm）、金斛（the Golden Bushel）、破锋剑（the Ragged Sword）、林中之狼（the Wolf of the Woodland）；他们满怀极大之喜悦与凯旋，将诸旗舁入议事厅，悬于高堂之上；又举一块明亮之玻璃圆盘对准太阳，于圣灶（the Holy Hearth）之上借日光以点燃火种；随后便在诸旗之前高歌。下面便是他们在诸旗之前所唱之歌的一部分：

> 尔等去向何方？自何处来，向何处去？
 
 闲置之剑上，有何光华闪烁？
 
 尔等怀中，携来何种欢欣？
 
 尔等交错之言，述何故事？
> 吾等不向他适，已驻足于此，
 
 此地，古老圣灶之旁；
 
 月月年年，久羁吾等之步履，
 
 然吾等今已自荒歉之渊而来归。
> 吾等乃迟来之喜乐之民；
 
 吾等乃荒原之上之漂泊者；
 
 吾等不过是枯坐等待之人，
 
 目送空寂之长风匆匆而过。
> 久矣、久矣，吾等枯坐，不识他人，
 
 唯见异族之民与道路之寇仇；
 
 直至迟迟、终见吾等之兄弟，
 
 吾等遂不得不归于旧日之居。
> 因一日，他们乞求留宿款待；
 
 吾等当时如何能不应其所请？
 
 荒原之于斯民歇息，实属过于荒凉，
 
 而此谷之富庶，吾等又深谙于心。
> 此处便是吾等须为他们铺设宴席之地，
 
 在下方丰饶可爱之谷中；
 
 此处便是吾等愿为他们安置卧处之厅：
 
 吾等又怎会在他处逗留？
> 吾等一同越过荒原而来：
 
 路上有荆棘丛莽横阻；
 
 又有狂风骤雨、风霜雪雹；
 
 血红的雨使白昼为之黯淡。
> 然在那诸日之一日，有何忧患能阻吾等，
 
 当吾等透过云隙，见到令此谷欢欣之日轮？
 
 吾等撕裂围困吾等之荆棘丛莽，
 
 至日暮之时，安然伫立。
> 幸福者之厅啊，请受吾等之礼敬！
 
 请你召列祖列宗前来观看
 
 厅墙之上氏族之徽会聚一堂，
 
 且看今日吾等为何等之人。
> 且看那新燃之圣灶，
 
 火星闪烁，飞升得何其高远！
 
 那摇曳之烟为日光所映照，
 
 袅袅盘绕于密梁之屋顶！
> 因狼族再度安居于此，
 
 永不再从此谷远去，
 
 再不至垂首藏头，
 
 直至诸日皆暗、天地终穷。
