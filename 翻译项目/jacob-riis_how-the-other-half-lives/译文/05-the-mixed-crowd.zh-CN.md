## III The Mixed Crowd / III 混杂的人群

===Original===
When once I asked the agent of a notorious Fourth Ward alley how many people might be living in it I was told: One hundred and forty families, one hundred Irish, thirty-eight Italian, and two that spoke the German tongue. Barring the agent herself, there was not a native-born individual in the court. The answer was characteristic of the cosmopolitan character of lower New York, very nearly so of the whole of it, wherever it runs to alleys and courts. One may find for the asking an Italian, a German, a French, African, Spanish, Bohemian, Russian, Scandinavian, Jewish, and Chinese colony. Even the Arab, who peddles “holy earth” from the Battery as a direct importation from Jerusalem, has his exclusive preserves at the lower end of Washington Street. The one thing you shall vainly ask for in the chief city of America is a distinctively American community. There is none; certainly not among the tenements. Where have they gone to, the old inhabitants? I put the question to one who might fairly be presumed to be of the number, since I had found him sighing for the “good old days” when the legend “no Irish need apply” was familiar in the advertising columns of the newspapers. He looked at me with a puzzled air. “I don’t know,” he said. “I wish I did. Some went to California in ’49, some to the war and never came back. The rest, I expect, have gone to heaven, or somewhere. I don’t see them ’round here.”

===Chinese===
有一次，我问第四选区（Fourth Ward）一条臭名昭著的小巷的经租人，这巷子里可能住着多少人。得到的回答是：一百四十户人家——一百户爱尔兰人，三十八户意大利人，还有两户说德语的。除那位女经租人本人不算，院里再无一个本土出生的人。这个回答，道出了纽约下城世界主义品格的典型一斑；推及全城，也几乎如此——凡是巷陌院落成片的地方。只要开口打听，就能找到意大利人、德国人、法国人、非洲人、西班牙人、波希米亚人（Bohemians，即捷克人）、俄国人、斯堪的纳维亚人、犹太人和华人的聚居地。连那个在炮台（the Battery）一带把「圣土」当作直接进口的货色、从耶路撒冷贩来叫卖的阿拉伯人，也在华盛顿街（Washington Street）南端占着一片独属的地盘。在美国这座头号大城市里，有一样东西你是问也白问的：一个道地美国人的社区。没有这样的东西；至少在廉租公寓（tenement）之中肯定没有。老居民们都到哪里去了？我把这个问题提给了一位大可以算作他们一伙的人，因为我先前听见他叹惋「过去的好日子」——那时「爱尔兰人不必应聘」的字样，还是报纸广告栏里的熟客。他一脸困惑地望着我。“不知道，”他说，“我要是知道就好了。有些人'49 年去了加利福尼亚，有些去打仗，再没有回来。其余的嘛，我琢磨着，不是上了天堂，就是去了别的什么地方。反正这一带我瞧不见他们。”

===Original===
Whatever the merit of the good man’s conjectures, his eyes did not deceive him. They are not here. In their place has come this queer conglomerate mass of heterogeneous elements, ever striving and working like whiskey and water in one glass, and with the like result: final union and a prevailing taint of whiskey. The once unwelcome Irishman has been followed in his turn by the Italian, the Russian Jew, and the Chinaman, and has himself taken a hand at opposition, quite as bitter and quite as ineffectual, against these later hordes. Wherever these have gone they have crowded him out, possessing the block, the street, the ward with their denser swarms. But the Irishman’s revenge is complete. Victorious in defeat over his recent as over his more ancient foe, the one who opposed his coming no less than the one who drove him out, he dictates to both their politics, and, secure in possession of the offices, returns the native his greeting with interest, while collecting the rents of the Italian whose house he has bought with the profits of his saloon. As a landlord he is picturesquely autocratic. An amusing instance of his methods came under my notice while writing these lines. An inspector of the Health Department found an Italian family paying a man with a Celtic name twenty-five dollars a month for three small rooms in a ramshackle rear tenement﻿—more than twice what they were worth﻿—and expressed his astonishment to the tenant, an ignorant Sicilian laborer. He replied that he had once asked the landlord to reduce the rent, but he would not do it.

===Chinese===
且不论这位好人的臆测有几分成色，他的眼睛没有骗他：他们是不在这里了。顶替他们而来的，是这一大团由异质成分拼成的古怪混合物，永远在翻腾较劲，恰似同杯之中的威士忌与水，结果也如出一辙：最终的溶合，外加一股弥漫不去的威士忌气味。当年一度不受欢迎的爱尔兰人，如今轮到他被意大利人、俄国犹太人和华人（Chinaman，当时用语）尾随进逼；他自己也亲手操办起排斥的营生，对付这些后来蜂拥而至的人群：刻毒不亚于当年，无效也不亚于当年。这些人涌到哪里，就把他从哪里挤走，以更稠密的一群群占据整片的街区、整条的街道、整个的选区。然而爱尔兰人的报复是彻底的。败中取胜——既胜过新近的对手，也胜过更古老的对手：一边是当初反对他来的人，一边是后来把他挤走的人；他向双方发号施令，替他们定下政治，又稳稳把持着官职，把本土人当年的问候连本带利地奉还，一面收着意大利人的房租——那幢房子，是他拿酒馆的利润买下的。当起房东来，他专断得有声有色。就在写下这几行文字的时候，一个逗人的实例恰好撞进我眼里，足见其手段：卫生局（Health Department）的一名稽查员发现一户意大利人家，每月付二十五美元给一个凯尔特姓氏的男人，租的是摇摇欲坠的后院廉租公寓（rear tenement）里的三间小屋——租金是其实值的两倍还多——稽查员便向房客表示诧异；那房客是个没见识的西西里劳工。他回答说，自己曾央告房东减些房租，可房东不肯。

===Original===
“Well! What did he say?” asked the inspector.

===Chinese===
“哦！他怎么说的？”稽查员问。

===Original===
“ ‘Damma, man!’ he said; ‘if you speaka thata way to me, I fira you and your things in the streeta.’ ” And the frightened Italian paid the rent.

===Chinese===
“‘该死的，伙计！’他说，‘你要是用这种口气跟我说话，我就把你连人带东西全撵到大街上去。’”吓坏了的意大利人照付了房租。

===Original===
In justice to the Irish landlord it must be said that like an apt pupil he was merely showing forth the result of the schooling he had received, reenacting, in his own way, the scheme of the tenements. It is only his frankness that shocks. The Irishman does not naturally take kindly to tenement life, though with characteristic versatility he adapts himself to its conditions at once. It does violence, nevertheless, to the best that is in him, and for that very reason of all who come within its sphere soonest corrupts him. The result is a sediment, the product of more than a generation in the city’s slums, that, as distinguished from the larger body of his class, justly ranks at the foot of tenement dwellers, the so-called “low Irish.”

===Chinese===
替这位爱尔兰房东说句公道话：他不过像个伶俐的学生，正在展布自己所受调教的成果，用自己的方式重演廉租公寓的那套章程罢了。叫人吃惊的，只是他的坦白。爱尔兰人天性并不亲近廉租公寓的生活，尽管凭着那份特有的随机应变，他立刻便能适应这里的种种境况。然而这种生活糟蹋的是他身上最好的东西；也正因如此，在一切落入它势力范围的人里头，最先被腐蚀的往往便是他。其结果，是一层沉淀物，城市贫民窟中一代有余的产物；有别于其族类的主体，这层人名列廉租公寓居民的最底端，倒也名实相副——所谓「下等爱尔兰人」（“low Irish”）是也。

===Original===
It is not to be assumed, of course, that the whole body of the population living in the tenements, of which New Yorkers are in the habit of speaking vaguely as “the poor,” or even the larger part of it, is to be classed as vicious or as poor in the sense of verging on beggary.

===Chinese===
当然，不能就此断定：栖身廉租公寓的全体居民——纽约人惯于含混地统称他们作「穷人」——乃至其中的大部分，都该归入恶棍之流，或穷到了濒于讨饭的地步。

===Original===
New York’s wage-earners have no other place to live, more is the pity. They are truly poor for having no better homes; waxing poorer in purse as the exorbitant rents to which they are tied, as ever was serf to soil, keep rising. The wonder is that they are not all corrupted, and speedily, by their surroundings. If, on the contrary, there be a steady working up, if not out of the slough, the fact is a powerful argument for the optimist’s belief that the world is, after all, growing better, not worse, and would go far toward disarming apprehension, were it not for the steadier growth of the sediment of the slums and its constant menace. Such an impulse toward better things there certainly is. The German ragpicker of thirty years ago, quite as low in the scale as his Italian successor, is the thrifty tradesman or prosperous farmer of today.6

===Chinese===
纽约的工薪劳动者别无可住之处，这更令人叹惋。他们因为得不到更好的家而实实在在受穷；又被捆死在漫天要价的房租上——恰似昔年农奴捆在土地上——眼看着房租节节攀升，囊中便越发空乏。奇怪的是，他们竟没有个个都被周遭环境迅速腐蚀。反过来说，倘若这里存在一股持续向上的劲头——纵然还谈不到爬出泥沼——那么这个事实便是乐观主义者一派的有力论据，证明这个世界到底是在变好，不是在变坏；若不是贫民窟的沉淀物增长得更稳固、其威胁更持久，这本足以教人大半卸下忧惧。朝更好处去的这样一股冲劲，确乎是有的。三十年前的德国拾荒者，当年在这一行里同他今天的意大利后继者一样垫底，如今已成了勤俭的店主或兴旺的农场主。6

===Original===
The Italian scavenger of our time is fast graduating into exclusive control of the corner fruit-stands, while his black-eyed boy monopolizes the boot-blacking industry in which a few years ago he was an intruder. The Irish hod-carrier in the second generation has become a bricklayer, if not the Alderman of his ward, while the Chinese coolie is in almost exclusive possession of the laundry business. The reason is obvious. The poorest immigrant comes here with the purpose and ambition to better himself and, given half a chance, might be reasonably expected to make the most of it. To the false plea that he prefers the squalid homes in which his kind are housed there could be no better answer. The truth is, his half chance has too long been wanting, and for the bad result he has been unjustly blamed.

===Chinese===
我们这个时代的意大利捡拾工，正迅速毕业，进阶到独占把持街角水果摊的地步；他的黑眼睛男孩则垄断了擦鞋的行当——就在几年前，他们闯进这一行时还只是外来的生客。到了第二代，爱尔兰的运灰小工已当上砌砖匠，弄不好还是本选区的市议员（Alderman）；而华人苦力（coolie）则几乎独占了洗衣业。道理是明摆着的。最穷的移民揣着改善境遇的目的和抱负来到这里；只要给他一半的机会，就有理由指望他把这机会用到十分。有人假称他偏爱其同类栖身的那种龌龊住所——对这套说辞，再没有比这更好的回答了。实情是，他那一半的机会迟迟无人给予；而由此生出的恶果，他却一直背着不公正的骂名。

===Original===
As emigration from east to west follows the latitude, so does the foreign influx in New York distribute itself along certain well-defined lines that waver and break only under the stronger pressure of a more gregarious race or the encroachments of inexorable business. A feeling of dependence upon mutual effort, natural to strangers in a strange land, unacquainted with its language and customs, sufficiently accounts for this.

===Chinese===
正如自东而西的移民循着纬线走，涌入纽约的外来人流也循着若干条界线分明的路线分布自己；这些路线，唯有在更爱群居的族类以更大的压力进逼、或铁面无私的商业节节侵占之时，才会动摇、断裂。对身在异邦、语言习俗一概生疏的客居者而言，倚仗彼此协力的心思本是天性；这一点，便足以解释上述现象。

===Original===
The Irishman is the true cosmopolitan immigrant. All-pervading, he shares his lodging with perfect impartiality with the Italian, the Greek, and the “Dutchman,” yielding only to sheer force of numbers, and objects equally to them all. A map of the city, colored to designate nationalities, would show more stripes than on the skin of a zebra, and more colors than any rainbow. The city on such a map would fall into two great halves, green for the Irish prevailing in the West Side tenement districts, and blue for the Germans on the East Side. But intermingled with these ground colors would be an odd variety of tints that would give the whole the appearance of an extraordinary crazy-quilt. From down in the Sixth Ward, upon the site of the old Collect Pond that in the days of the fathers drained the hills which are no more, the red of the Italian would be seen forcing its way northward along the line of Mulberry Street to the quarter of the French purple on Bleecker Street and South Fifth Avenue, to lose itself and reappear, after a lapse of miles, in the “Little Italy” of Harlem, east of Second Avenue. Dashes of red, sharply defined, would be seen strung through the Annexed District, northward to the city line. On the West Side the red would be seen overrunning the old Africa of Thompson Street, pushing the black of the negro rapidly uptown, against querulous but unavailing protests, occupying his home, his church, his trade and all, with merciless impartiality. There is a church in Mulberry Street that has stood for two generations as a sort of milestone of these migrations. Built originally for the worship of staid New Yorkers of the “old stock,” it was engulfed by the colored tide, when the draft-riots drove the negroes out of reach of Cherry Street and the Five Points. Within the past decade the advance wave of the Italian onset reached it, and today the arms of United Italy adorn its front. The negroes have made a stand at several points along Seventh and Eighth Avenues; but their main body, still pursued by the Italian foe, is on the march yet, and the black mark will be found overshadowing today many blocks on the East Side, with One Hundredth Street as the centre, where colonies of them have settled recently.

===Chinese===
爱尔兰人才是真正的世界主义移民。他无处不在，同意大利人、希腊人和「荷兰人」（“Dutchman”，当时纽约对德国移民的俗称）搭伙同住，不分彼此；唯有纯凭人多势众者能叫他退让，而对这几家他倒是一律看不顺眼。一张按国籍敷色的城市地图，上面的条纹会比斑马皮还多，颜色比任何一道彩虹都全。在这张地图上，全城将分为两大半：西区的廉租公寓地带，以爱尔兰人的绿色为主；东区，则以德国人的蓝色为主。但与这两种底色错杂相交的，还有种种古怪的色调，使全图看去俨然一床匪夷所思的百衲拼布被。从第六选区（Sixth Ward）——老收集池（Collect Pond）的旧址，在先辈们的年代，那池子承泄着几座如今早已不存的山丘的来水——意大利人的红色会沿着桑树街（Mulberry Street）一线奋力北进，直抵布利克街（Bleecker Street）与南第五大道上法国人紫色的地界，随即隐没，隔了数英里又重新冒头，现身于第二大道以东哈莱姆（Harlem）的「小意大利」（Little Italy）。一道道界线分明的红色短杠，会断断续续串过兼并区（the Annexed District），向北直排到市界。在西区，人们会看到红色漫过汤普森街（Thompson Street）的老「非洲」（Africa，当时对黑人聚居区的代称），挟着黑人（negro，当时书面用语）的黑色急速往城北推去——后者怨声连连，却徒劳无功——并以毫不留情的一视同仁，占下黑人的家宅、教堂、行当连同一切。桑树街上有一座教堂，两代人来一直立在那里，仿佛这些迁徙途中的一座里程碑。它起初是给「老世家」（“old stock”）中端方持重的纽约人作礼拜之所，后来被有色人种的潮水吞没——其时征兵暴乱把黑人赶出了樱桃街（Cherry Street）与五点区（Five Points）够得着的范围。近十年间，意大利人进击的先头浪头打到了这里；今天，统一意大利的纹章就装点在它的正面。黑人已沿着第七大道与第八大道的好几处节点扎住阵脚；但他们的大部队，在那意大利敌手的追赶之下，至今仍在迁徙途中。今天你会发现，黑色的印记正笼罩着东区的许多街区，以第一百街（One Hundredth Street）为中心——他们的一些群落新近就在那里落了脚。

===Original===
Hardly less aggressive than the Italian, the Russian and Polish Jew, having overrun the district between Rivington and Division Streets, east of the Bowery, to the point of suffocation, is filling the tenements of the old Seventh Ward to the river front, and disputing with the Italian every foot of available space in the back alleys of Mulberry Street. The two races, differing hopelessly in much, have this in common: they carry their slums with them wherever they go, if allowed to do it. Little Italy already rivals its parent, the “Bend,” in foulness. Other nationalities that begin at the bottom make a fresh start when crowded up the ladder. Happily both are manageable, the one by rabbinical, the other by the civil law. Between the dull gray of the Jew, his favorite color, and the Italian red, would be seen squeezed in on the map a sharp streak of yellow, marking the narrow boundaries of Chinatown. Dovetailed in with the German population, the poor but thrifty Bohemian might be picked out by the sombre hue of his life as of his philosophy, struggling against heavy odds in the big human beehives of the East Side. Colonies of his people extend northward, with long lapses of space, from below the Cooper Institute more than three miles. The Bohemian is the only foreigner with any considerable representation in the city who counts no wealthy man of his race, none who has not to work hard for a living, or has got beyond the reach of the tenement.

===Chinese===
俄国和波兰的犹太人，其进逼之劲不遑多让于意大利人：鲍厄里（the Bowery）以东、里文顿街（Rivington Street）与迪维森街（Division Street）之间的地段，他们已挤到了令人窒息的地步，此刻正灌满老第七选区直抵河滨的片片廉租公寓，并在桑树街的后巷里，同意大利人逐尺相争每一寸可立足之地。这两个族裔，在许多方面相异得无可救药，却有这一点相通：只要听任他们，他们走到哪里，就把贫民窟带到哪里。「小意大利」的污浊，已足以与其母体「弯道区」（the Bend）一较高下。别的从底层起步的民族，被沿着梯子往上挤时，尚能另起一段新程。所幸这两家都还管得住：一家可借拉比律法，一家可依世俗法律。在犹太人那种暗沉的灰色——他钟爱的颜色——同意大利人的红色之间，地图上会看见挤进一道窄而锐利的黄色，标出唐人街（Chinatown）局促的边界。与德意志人口犬牙交错的，是贫穷而勤俭的波希米亚人；凭他那生活乃至哲思一般阴郁的底色，便可把他认出来——他正在东区一座座巨大的人蜂巢里，以寡敌众地苦撑。他同胞的聚居地自库珀学院（Cooper Institute）以南一路向北，隔着长长的空档，绵延三英里有余。波希米亚人是城中唯一一个人数可观、却数不出一个本族富人的外来民族——没有谁不必为糊口而操劳，也没有谁能挣脱廉租公寓的掌握。

Down near the Battery the West Side emerald would be soiled by a dirty stain, spreading rapidly like a splash of ink on a sheet of blotting paper, headquarters of the Arab tribe, that in a single year has swelled from the original dozen to twelve hundred, intent, every mother’s son, on trade and barter. Dots and dashes of color here and there would show where the Finnish sailors worship their *djumala* (God), the Greek pedlars the ancient name of their race, and the Swiss the goddess of thrift. And so on to the end of the long register, all toiling together in the galling fetters of the tenement. Were the question raised who makes the most of life thus mortgaged, who resists most stubbornly its levelling tendency﻿—knows how to drag even the barracks upward a part of the way at least toward the ideal plane of the home﻿—the palm must be unhesitatingly awarded the Teuton. The Italian and the poor Jew rise only by compulsion. The Chinaman does not rise at all; here, as at home, he simply remains stationary. The Irishman’s genius runs to public affairs rather than domestic life; wherever he is mustered in force the saloon is the gorgeous centre of political activity. The German struggles vainly to learn his trick; his Teutonic wit is too heavy, and the political ladder he raises from his saloon usually too short or too clumsy to reach the desired goal. The best part of his life is lived at home, and he makes himself a home independent of the surroundings, giving the lie to the saying, unhappily become a maxim of social truth, that pauperism and drunkenness naturally grow in the tenements. 在靠近炮台的下城一带，西区的翡翠绿会被一块污渍弄脏，像吸墨纸上泼落的一滴墨，迅速洇开——那便是阿拉伯部落的总部；一年之间，它已从最初的十二人膨胀到一千二百人，个个男人都一门心思扑在做买卖、搞易货上。星星点点、长长短短的颜色，会在四处标出：芬兰水手在何处礼拜他们的 *djumala*（神），希腊货郎在何处供奉本族的古老名号，瑞士人又在何处敬奉节俭女神。如此一路数到这份长长名册的末尾——所有人都在廉租公寓这副磨人的镣铐里一同劳作。倘若有人要问：谁把这样典当出去的人生经营得最充分？谁最顽强地抵挡它拉平一切的倾向——懂得怎样把这营房至少朝家的理想平面往上拽上一程？那么这顶桂冠必须毫不犹豫地颁给条顿人（Teuton）。意大利人和穷苦的犹太人，非经外力逼迫，不肯往上升。华人则压根儿不上升；在这里同在国内一样，他只是原地不动。爱尔兰人的天分倾注于公共事务，而非家庭生活；凡他成群结队驻扎之处，酒馆便是政治活动那富丽堂皇的中心。德国人枉自想学他这一手；他那条顿式的机智太沉，从酒馆里搭起的政治梯子又通常不是太短便是太笨，够不到想望的目标。他一生中最美好的部分是在家里过掉的；他给自己营造起一个不受周遭摆布的家，由此戳穿了那句不幸已被奉为社会公理的俗话——说什么贫民化与酗酒，天然就长在廉租公寓里。他把自己的廉租公寓拾掇到极致；还应当添上一句：只要攒够了钱，他随时立刻搬出去，从此再不跨进廉租公寓的门槛一步。
