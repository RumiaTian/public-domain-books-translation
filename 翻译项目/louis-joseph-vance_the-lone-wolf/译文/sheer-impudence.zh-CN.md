## XV Sheer Impudence / 十五、胆大包天

===Original===
Though it had been nearly eight when they entered the restaurant, it was something after eleven before Lanyard called for his bill.
“We’ve plenty of time,” he had explained; “it’ll be midnight before we can move. The gentle art of housebreaking has its technique, you know, its professional ethics: we can’t well violate the privacy of Madame Omber’s strongbox before the caretakers on the premises are sound asleep. It isn’t *done*, you know, it isn’t class, to go burglarizing when decent, law-abiding folk are wide-awake.﻿ ﻿… Meantime we’re better off here than trapezing the streets.﻿ ﻿…”
It’s a silent web of side ways and a gloomy one by night that backs up north of Les Halles: old Paris, taciturn and sombre, steeped in its memories of grim romance. But for infrequent, flickering, corner lamps, the street that welcomed them from the doors of the warm and cosy restaurant was as dismal as an alley in some city of the dead. Its houses with their mansard roofs and boarded windows bent their heads together like mutes at a wake, black-cloaked and hooded; seldom one showed a light; never one betrayed by any sound the life that lurked behind its jealous blinds.

===Chinese===
他们进餐馆时将近八点，等兰亚德开口叫账单，已是十一点过响了。
「我们时间充裕得很，」他早先解释过；「非到午夜，咱们动不了手。入室行窃这门优雅艺术自有其技法，你也知道，自有其行内的道德：宅子里的看守人没睡熟之前，咱们不便去惊扰欧姆贝夫人保险箱的隐私。正经守法的好人都还大睁着眼的时候跑出去撬门，你知道，这种事是不兴（*done*）的，有失身份。……再说，与其在街面上荡来荡去，咱们还不如待在这儿。……」
中央市场（Les Halles）以北铺开一片纵横交错的小巷，入夜后更见阴沉：这是老巴黎，缄默而晦暗，浸透着阴郁传奇的记忆。若不是街角零零星星几盏摇曳的路灯，从温暖舒适的餐馆门口迎来他们的这条街，简直同死城里的小巷一般凄清。两旁复折式屋顶、窗板紧闭的房屋彼此俯首凑近，宛如守灵时一袭黑袍兜帽的哑仆；难得有一扇窗透出灯火；也从来没有一栋屋子发出半点声响，泄露严严实实的百叶窗后潜藏的人烟。

===Original===
Now again the rain had ceased and, though the sky remained overcast, the atmosphere was clear and brisk with a touch of frost, in grateful contrast to the dull and muggy airs that had obtained for the last twenty-four hours.
“We’ll walk,” Lanyard suggested﻿—“if you don’t mind﻿—part of the way at least; it’ll eat up time, and a bit of exercise will do us both good.”
The girl assented quietly.﻿ ﻿…
The drum of their heels on fast-drying sidewalks struck sharp echoes from the silence of that drowsy quarter, a lonely clamour that rendered it impossible to ignore their apparent solitude﻿—as impossible as it was for Lanyard to ignore the fact that they were followed.

===Chinese===
这时雨又停了，天空虽仍阴沉，空气却清冽爽利，带着一缕霜意，较之前二十四小时那沉闷湿黏的空气，令人为之一爽。
「咱们走路去吧，」兰亚德提议——「你要是不介意——至少走上一段；好消磨时光，活动活动筋骨，对咱们俩都有益处。」
姑娘静静地应允了。……
两人的鞋跟叩在渐干的人行道上，从这半睡街区的一片岑寂里敲出清脆的回声——这一孤零零的响动，叫人无法不理会他们眼下的形单影只——正如兰亚德无法不理会那个事实：他们被人跟上了。

===Original===
The shadow dogging them on the far side of the street, some fifty yards behind, was as noiseless as any cat; but for this circumstance﻿—had it moved boldly with unmuffled footsteps﻿—Lanyard would have been slow to believe it concerned with him, so confident had he felt, till that moment, of having given the Pack the slip.
And from this he diagnosed still another symptom of the Pack’s incurable stupidity!
Supremely on the alert, he had discovered the pursuit before they left the block of the restaurant. Dissembling, partly to avoid alarming the girl, partly to trick the spy, he turned this way and that round several corners, until quite convinced that the shadow was dedicated to himself exclusively, then promptly revised his first purpose and, instead of sticking to darker back ways, struck out directly for the broad, well-lighted and lively boulevard de Sébastopol.

===Chinese===
街对面那道人影缀着他们，落后五十来码，脚底下轻得像猫；若非这一层——倘或它大模大样、步声不掩地跟来——兰亚德还不会轻易相信这影子是冲他来的，因为直到那一刻，他一直自信早已把群狼甩脱了。
由此他又诊断出群狼那无可救药的愚蠢的又一症状！
他警觉到了极处，还没走出餐馆那一带就已发现了这趟跟踪。他不动声色——一半为免惊扰姑娘，一半为愚弄奸细——绕着好几个街角东转西拐，直到确信那道影子专门冲他一人而来；随即当机立断改了主意，不再拣昏暗的背街小巷走，径直奔向宽阔明亮、人烟稠密的塞瓦斯托波尔大道（boulevard de Sébastopol）。

===Original===

A hedge of forlorn fir-trees knee-deep in wooden tubs guarded its terrasse of round metal tables and spindle-shanked chairs; of which few were occupied. Inside, visible through the wide plate-glass windows, perhaps a dozen patrons sat round half as many tables﻿—no more﻿—idling over dominoes and gossip: steady-paced burghers with their wives, men in small ways of business of the neighbourhood.

“What is it?” the girl asked guardedly as the waiter scurried off to execute his commands. “You’ve not stopped in here for nothing!”
“True﻿—but lower, please!” he begged. “If we speak English loud enough to be heard it will attract attention.﻿ ﻿… The trouble is, we’re followed. But as yet our faithful shadow doesn’t know we know it﻿—unless he’s more intelligent than he seems. Consequently, if I don’t misjudge him, he’ll take a table outside, the better to keep an eye on us, as soon as he sees we’re apparently settled for some time. ===Original===
He interrupted himself while the waiter served them, then added sugar to his coffee, arranged the ink bottle and paper to his satisfaction, and bent over his pen.
“Come closer,” he requested﻿—“as if you were interested in what I’m writing﻿—and amused; if you can laugh a bit at nothing, so much the better. But keep a sharp eye on the windows. “The window to the right of the door we came in,” she said, smiling delightedly; “he’s standing behind the fir-trees, staring in.”

===Chinese===
