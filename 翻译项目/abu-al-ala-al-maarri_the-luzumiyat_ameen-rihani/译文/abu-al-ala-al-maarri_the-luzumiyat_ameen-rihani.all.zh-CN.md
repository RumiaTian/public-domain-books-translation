## To Abu al-ʻAlaʼ / 致阿布·阿拉

And in the cloister of thy pensive Fancy

## Preface / 序

===Original===
When Christendom was groping amid the superstitions of the Dark Ages, and the Norsemen were ravaging the western part of Europe, and the princes of Islam were cutting each other’s throats in the name of Allah and his Prophet, Abu al-ʻAlaʼ al-Maʻarri was waging his bloodless war against the follies and evils of his age. He attacked the superstitions and false traditions of law and religion, proclaiming the supremacy of the mind; he hurled his trenchant invectives at the tyranny, the bigotry, and the quackery of his times, asserting the supremacy of the soul; he held the standard of reason high above that of authority, fighting to the end the battle of the human intellect. An intransigent with the exquisite mind of a sage and scholar, his weapons were never idle. But he was, above all, a poet; for when he stood before the eternal mystery of Life and Death, he sheathed his sword and murmured a prayer.

===Chinese===
当基督教世界还在黑暗时代的种种迷信中摸索，诺斯人正在欧洲西部烧杀蹂躏，伊斯兰的诸王公正借着安拉及其先知之名互相割断彼此咽喉的时候，阿布·阿拉·麦阿里（Abu al-ʻAlaʼ al-Maʻarri）正对他那个时代的愚妄与罪恶，进行着一场不流血的战争。他抨击法律与宗教的迷信和虚假传统，宣告心智至高无上；他把犀利的抨击掷向那个时代的暴虐、偏执与江湖骗术，申言灵魂至高无上；他将理性的旌旗高举于权威的旗帜之上，把人类智力的战斗打到了终局。他是一位不妥协者，怀有圣贤与学者般的精妙心智，其武器从不曾闲置。但他首先是一位诗人；因为当他立于生与死的永恒奥秘面前时，他便收剑入鞘，低声祈祷。

===Original===
Abu al-ʻAlaʼ al-Maʻarri,[2] the Lucretius of Islam, the Voltaire of the East, was born in the spring of the year 973 AD, in the obscure village of Maʻarra,[3] which is about eighteen hours’ journey south of Halab (Aleppo). And instead of Ahmad ibn Abdallah ibn Sulaiman ut-Tanukhi (of the tribe of Tanukh), he was called Abu al-ʻAlaʼ (the Father of the Sublime), by which patronymic of distinction he is popularly known throughout the Arabic speaking world.

===Chinese===
阿布·阿拉·麦阿里[2]——伊斯兰的卢克莱修（Lucretius）、东方的伏尔泰（Voltaire）——公元 973 年春生于麦阿拉（Maʻarra）[3]，一座无名的村落，在哈莱卜（阿勒颇）以南约十八小时路程的地方。人们不叫他艾哈迈德·伊本·阿卜杜拉·伊本·苏莱曼·坦努希（Ahmad ibn Abdallah ibn Sulaiman ut-Tanukhi，属坦努赫部族），而叫他阿布·阿拉（Abu al-ʻAlaʼ，意即「崇高者之父」）；凭着这个显赫的父名，他在整个阿拉伯语世界广为人知。

===Original===
When a boy, Abu al-ʻAlaʼ was instructed by his father; and subsequently he was sent to Halab, where he pursued his studies under the tutelage of the grammarian Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn us-Sad. His literary proclivity was evinced in his boyhood, and he wrote verse, we are told, before he was ten. Of these juvenile pieces, however, nothing was preserved.

===Chinese===
阿布·阿拉幼年由父亲授学；随后他被送往哈莱卜，在语法学家穆罕默德·伊本·阿卜杜拉·伊本·萨德（Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn us-Sad）门下求学。他的文学禀赋在少年时代便已显露，据说他不到十岁就写起了诗。然而这些少年之作，却没有一篇留存下来。

===Original===
He was about five years old when he fell a victim to smallpox and almost lost his sight from it. But a weakness in his eyes continued to trouble him and he became, in middle age, I presume, totally blind.[4] Some of his biographers would have us believe he was born blind; others state that he completely lost his sight when he was attacked by the virulent disease; and a few intimate that he could see slightly at least with the right eye. As to whether or not he was blind when he was sent to Halab to pursue his studies, his biographers do not agree. My theory, based on the careful perusal of his poems and on a statement advanced by one of his biographers,[5] is that he lost his sight gradually, and total blindness must have come upon him either in his youth or his middle age.[6] Were we to believe that he was born blind or that he suffered the complete loss of his sight in his boyhood, we should be at a loss to know, not how he wrote his books, for that was done by dictation; not how he taught his pupils, for that was done by lectures; but how he himself was taught in the absence in those days of a regular system of instruction for the blind.

===Chinese===
他约莫五岁时染上天花，眼睛几乎因此毁掉。此后眼部的虚弱一直困扰着他，而我揣想，他在中年时便已全盲[4]。有些传记作者要我们相信他生来就盲；另一些则说，他是在染上那场恶疾时彻底丧失视力的；还有少数人暗示，他至少右眼尚能略见微光。至于他被送往哈莱卜求学时是否已经失明，传记作者们意见不一。我的推断——基于对他诗作的细读，以及他一位传记作者提出的说法[5]——是：他的视力是逐渐丧失的，全盲必然降临于他的青年或中年时期[6]。倘若我们相信他生而失明，或相信他在少年时便完全丧失了视力，我们便难以索解——不是他如何著书，因为那是靠口授完成的；也不是他如何授课，因为那是靠讲学完成的；而是他本人如何受教：须知在那个年代，还没有一套针对盲人的正规教育制度。

===Original===
In 1010 AD he visited Baghdad, the centre of learning and intelligence and the capital of the Abbasid caliphs, where he passed about two years and became acquainted with most of the literary men of the age.[7] He attended the lectures and the readings of the leading doctors and grammarians, meeting with a civil reception at the hand of most of them.

===Chinese===
公元 1010 年，他游历巴格达——那座学问与才智的中心、阿拔斯王朝哈里发们的都城。他在那里逗留了约莫两年，结识了当时文士中的多数[7]。他聆听领衔的博士与语法学家们的讲学与诵读，受到其中大多数人以礼相待。

===Original===
He also journeyed to Tripoli,[8] which boasted, in those days, of many public libraries; and, stopping at Latakia, he lodged in a monastery where he met and befriended a very learned monk. They discussed theology and metaphysics, digressing now and then into the profane. Indeed, the skepticism which permeates Abu al-ʻAla’s writings must have been nursed in that convent by both the monk and the poet.

===Chinese===
他还游历了的黎波里（Tripoli）[8]——那座城在当时以众多公共图书馆自豪；途中在拉塔基亚（Latakia）停留时，他寄宿在一座修道院里，在那里结识并结交了一位极有学问的修士。他们讨论神学与形而上学，不时离题，涉入世俗之谈。的确，弥漫于阿布·阿拉著作中的那种怀疑论，想必正是在那座修道院里，由修士与诗人共同哺育出来的。

===Original===
These are virtually the only data extant showing the various sources of Abu al-ʻAla’s learning; but to one endowed with a keen perception, a powerful intellect, a prodigious memory, together with strong innate literary predilections, they seem sufficient. He was especially noted for the extraordinary memory he possessed; and around this our Arab biographers and historians weave a thick net of anecdotes, or rather fables. I have no doubt that one with such a prodigious memory could retain in a few minutes what the average person could not; but when we are told that Abu al-ʻAlaʼ once heard one of his pupils speaking with a friend in a foreign tongue, and repeated there and then the long conversation, word for word, without having the slightest idea of its meaning, we are disposed to be skeptical. Many such anecdotes are recorded and quoted by his Arab biographers without as much as intimating a single doubt.[9] The fact that he was blind partly explains the abnormal development of his memory.

===Chinese===
这些几乎便是仅存的资料，足以显示阿布·阿拉学问的种种来源；但对于一个禀有敏锐感知、强大智力、惊人记忆，兼有与生俱来的强烈文学倾向的人，这些似乎已足够。他尤以非凡的记忆力著称；我们的阿拉伯传记作者与史家围绕这一点，编织了一张厚密的轶闻之网——毋宁说是寓言之网。我毫不怀疑，拥有如此惊人记忆力的人，能在几分钟里记住常人所不能记的东西；可是当我们被告知，阿布·阿拉有一次听见他的一个学生用某种外国语与友人交谈，竟能当场逐字复述那一整段长谈，而对其含义毫无所知，我们便不免要起疑了。他的阿拉伯传记作者们记录并征引了许多这样的轶闻，却连一丝怀疑也不曾暗示[9]。他是盲人这一事实，部分解释了他记忆力的异常发达。

===Original===
His career as poet and scholar dates from the time he returned from Baghdad. This, so far as is known, was the last journey he made; and his home became henceforth his earthly prison. He calls himself “A double-fettered Captive,”[10] his solitude being the one and his blindness the other. Like most of the scholars of his age, in the absence of regular educational institutions, with perhaps one or two exceptions, he had to devote a part of his time to the large number of pupils that flocked to Maʻarra from all parts of Asia Minor, Arabia, and India. Aside from this, he dictated to his numerous amanuenses on every possible and known subject. He is not only a poet of the first rank, but an essayist, a literary critic, and a mathematician as well. Everything he wrote was transcribed by many of his admirers, as was the fashion then, and thus circulated far and near. Nothing, however, was preserved but his *Diwans*, his *Letters* and the *Epistle of Forgiveness*,[11] of which I shall yet have occasion to speak.[12]

===Chinese===
他作为诗人与学者的生涯，始于自巴格达归来之时。据我们所知，这是他此生最后一次远行；从此，他的家便成了他尘世的牢狱。他自称「双重锁链下的囚徒」[10]——孤独为其一，失明为其二。与那个时代大多数学者一样——其时正规教育机构阙如，或许只有一两个例外——他不得不把一部分时间用来教授大批从小亚细亚、阿拉伯和印度各地涌到麦阿拉来的学生。除此以外，他还就一切可能的、已知的题目，向众多录事口授。他不仅是一流的诗人，还是散文家、文学批评家，也是数学家。他所写的一切，都照当时的风尚由众多仰慕者传抄，因而流传远近。然而留存下来的，只有他的《诗集》（*Diwans*）、《书信集》（*Letters*）和《宽恕书》（*Epistle of Forgiveness*）[11]——关于后者，我后面还有机会谈到[12]。

===Original===
His reputation as poet and scholar had now, after his return from Baghdad, overleaped the horizons, as one writer has it. Honors were conferred upon him successively by the rulers and the scholars of his age. His many noted admirers were in constant communication with him. He was now looked upon as “the master of the learned, the chief of the wise, and the sole monarch of the bards of his century.” Maʻarra[13] became the Mecca of every literary aspirant; ambitious young scholars came there for enlightenment and inspiration. And Abu al-ʻAlaʼ, although a pessimist, received them with his wonted kindness and courtesy. He imparted to them what he knew, and told them candidly what he would not teach, since, unlike other philosophers, he was not able to grasp the truth, nor compass the smallest of the mysteries of creation. In his latter days, youthful admirers sought his blessing, which he, as the childless father of all, graciously conferred, but with no self-assumed spiritual or temporal authority.

===Chinese===
正如一位作者所言，自巴格达归来之后，他作为诗人与学者的名声已跃出了地平线。那个时代的统治者与学者相继向他致以荣衔。他众多著名的仰慕者与他书信不绝。他此时被视为「学者之师、智者之长、他那世纪歌人中的独一君王」。麦阿拉[13]成了每一个文学进取者的麦加；雄心勃勃的年轻学子到那里去求启悟、求灵感。而阿布·阿拉虽是悲观主义者，却以他惯有的仁慈与谦和接待他们。他把所知道的倾囊相授，也坦白告诉他们自己不愿教什么，因为与其他哲学家不同，他既不能把握真理，也无法穷尽造化最微末的奥秘。晚年，年轻的仰慕者们来求他的祝福；他虽无子嗣，却如众人之父，欣然赐福，而不自负任何属灵或尘世的权威。

===Original===
For thirty years he remained a vegetarian, living the life of an ascetic.[14] This mode of living led his enemies to accuse him of renouncing Islam and embracing Brahminism, one of the tenets of which forbids the slaughter of animals. The accusation was rather sustained by the dispassionate attitude he held towards it, and, furthermore, by his vehement denunciation of the barbarous practice of killing animals for food or for sport.

===Chinese===
有三十年，他持素不茹荤，过着苦行者的生活[14]。这种生活方式使他的仇敌指控他背弃伊斯兰、皈依婆罗门教——婆罗门教的教义之一即禁止宰杀动物。而他面对这项指控所持的淡漠态度，加之他对为食或为戏而残杀动物的野蛮行径的激烈谴责，反倒使罪名愈坐愈实。

===Original===
Most of the censors of Abu al-ʻAlaʼ were either spurred to their task by bigotry or animated by jealousy and ignorance. They held him up to ridicule and opprobrium, and such epithets as heretic, atheist, renegade, etc., were freely applied. But he was supremely indifferent to them all,[15] and never would he cross swords with any particular individual; he attacked the false doctrines they were teaching, turning a deaf ear to the virulent vituperations they hurled upon him. I fail to find in the three volumes of his poems, even in the *Letters*, one acrimonious line savoring of personality.

===Chinese===
阿布·阿拉的大多数攻击者，或受偏执驱策，或为嫉妒与无知所鼓动。他们把他树为嘲弄与耻辱的靶子，异端、无神论者、叛教者之类的名号被随意加诸其身。但他对这一切漠然置之[15]，从不与任何具体的个人交锋；他攻击的是他们讲授的虚假教义，对他们掷来的恶毒詈骂则置若罔闻。在他的诗集三卷之中，甚至在《书信集》（*Letters*）里，我找不出一行带刺的、牵涉个人的刻毒文字。

===Original===
Ibn-Khillikan, the Plutarch of Arabia, who is cautious and guarded in his statements, speaking of Abu al-ʻAlaʼ, truly says:

“His asceticism, his deep sense of right and wrong, his powerful intellect, his prodigious memory, and his wide range of learning, are alike acknowledged by both friend and foe.”

===Chinese===
阿拉伯的普鲁塔克——伊本·赫利坎（Ibn-Khillikan）——下笔素来审慎而有分寸，谈到阿布·阿拉时，他说了一句实心话：

「他的苦行、他深厚的是非感、他强大的智力、他惊人的记忆力，以及他学问的广博，皆为友与敌所一致公认。」

===Original===
His pessimism was natural, in part hereditary. The man was nothing if not genuine and sincere. Ruthlessly he said what he thought and felt. He had no secrets to hide from the world, no thoughts which he dared not express. His soul was as open as Nature; his mind was the polished mirror of his age.[16] It may be that had he not been blind-stricken and had not smallpox disfigured his features, he might have found a palliative in human society. His pessimism might not have been cured, but it might have been rendered at least enticing. Good-fellowship might have robbed it of its sting. Nor is his strong aversion to marriage, in view of these facts, surprising.

===Chinese===
他的悲观主义是天然的，一部分出于遗传。此人若非本真而诚恳，便一无所是。他毫无留情地说出他所想、所感。他没有要向世人隐藏的秘密，没有不敢说出的话。他的灵魂像自然一样敞开；他的心智是他那个时代擦得光亮的镜子[16]。倘若他没有被失明击中，天花没有损毁他的容貌，他或许能在人群中寻得一份宽解。他的悲观主义未必能治愈，但至少可能变得动人一些；友朋之乐或许能拔去它的毒刺。考虑到这些，他对婚姻的强烈憎厌也就不足为怪了。

===Original===
He lived to know that “his fame spread from the sequestered village of Maʻarra to the utmost confines of the Arabic speaking world.” In the spring of 1055 AD he died, and was buried in a garden surrounding his home. Adh-Dhahabi states that there were present at his grave eighty poets, and that the Koran was read there two hundred times in a fortnight. Eighty poets in the small town of Maʻarra sounds incredible. But we must bear in mind that almost everyone who studies the Arabic grammar has also to study prosody and versification and thus become at least a rhymster. Even today, the death of a noted person among the Arabs, is always an occasion for the display of much eloquence and tears, both in prose and verse.

My desire is to confirm, not to weaken﻿—to expand, not contract﻿—the Oriental influence on the Occidental mind.

===Chinese===
收笔之前，我想提请人们注意一个问题。这个问题本身虽无关紧要，却值得一切阿拉伯与波斯文学爱好者的考量。我指的是欧玛尔·海亚姆（Omar Khayyam）与阿布·阿拉之间存在的思想上的相似。我有理由相信，前者是后者的摹仿者，或曰弟子。前一诗人的降生与后一诗人的去世相隔不远：两者都发生在十一世纪中叶前后。此间与海外的英语读书界对海亚姆早已成见在胸。因此，请不要以为我提出这一主张，是想动摇或削弱那份伟大的信心。我的愿望是确认而非削弱——是扩展而非收缩——东方对西方心智的影响。

===Original===
Whoever will take the trouble, however, to read Omar Khayyam in conjunction with what is here translated of Abu al-ʻAlaʼ, can not fail to see the striking similarity in thought and image of certain phases of the creed or the lack of creed of both poets.[24] To be sure, the skepticism and pessimism of Omar are to a great extent imported from Maʻarra. But the Arab philosopher in his religious opinions is far more outspoken than the Persian tentmaker. I do not say that Omar was a plagiarist; but I say this: just as Voltaire, for instance, acquired most of his liberal and skeptical views from Hobbes, Locke and Bayle, so did Omar acquire his from Abu al-ʻAlaʼ. In my notes to these quatrains I have quoted in comparison from both the Fitzgerald and the Herron-Allen versions of the Persian poet; and with so much or so little said, I leave the matter in the hands of the reader, who, upon a careful examination, will doubtless bear me out as to this point.

===Chinese===
然而，凡不惮其烦、肯把欧玛尔·海亚姆与此处所译的阿布·阿拉对读的人，都不能不见到两位诗人——在其信仰或曰无信仰的某些方面——思想与意象上惊人的相似[24]。诚然，欧玛尔的怀疑论与悲观主义，在很大程度上是从麦阿拉输入的。但这位阿拉伯哲学家在宗教见解上，远比那位波斯的帐篷匠直言无忌。我并不是说欧玛尔是剽窃者；但我要说：正如伏尔泰的自由与怀疑见解多半得自霍布斯、洛克与贝尔，欧玛尔的这些见解也得自阿布·阿拉。在这些四行诗的注释里，我征引了那位波斯诗人的菲茨杰拉德（Fitzgerald）与赫伦-艾伦（Herron-Allen）两种译本以资比较；说了这么多——或者说这么少——我便把这件事交到读者手中；读者细细考察之后，无疑会在这一点上为我说一句公道话。

## I[25] / 一[25]

===Original===
The sable wings of Night pursuing day
Across the opalescent hills, display
The wondrous star-gems which the fiery suns
Are scattering upon their fiery way.

===Chinese===
夜的乌黑翅膀追逐着白昼，
越过乳光斑斓的群山，展现出
奇妙的星辰宝石——那是炽烈的诸日
在它们炽烈的路途上撒下的。

## II / 二

===Original===
O my Companion, Night is passing fair,
Fairer than aught the dawn and sundown wear;
And fairer, too, than all the gilded days
Of blond Illusion and its golden snare.

===Chinese===
哦，我的同伴，夜美得非凡，
比黎明与黄昏披戴的一切更美；
也比金发的“幻妄”（Illusion）与其黄金罗网
所拥有的一切镀金时日更美。

## III[26] / 三[26]

===Original===
Hark, in the minarets muazzens call
The evening hour that in the interval
Of darkness Ahmad might remembered be—
Remembered of the Darkness be they all.

===Chinese===
听，宣礼员（muazzens）在宣礼塔上呼唤
黄昏的时辰——愿在黑暗的间隙里，
艾哈迈德（Ahmad）得蒙记念——
愿他们全都蒙那黑暗记念。

## IV[27] / 四[27]

===Original===
And hear the others who with cymbals try
To stay the feet of every passer-by:
The market-men along the darkling lane
Are crying up their wares.—Oh! let them cry.

===Chinese===
再听听那些敲着铜钹、
想留住每个过路人脚步的人们：
昏暗小巷里的市井商贩
正吆喝叫卖各自的货品。——哦！由他们吆喝吧。

## V / 五

===Original===
Mohammed or Messiah! Hear thou me,
The truth entire nor here nor there can be;
How should our God who made the sun and moon
Give all his light to One, I cannot see.

===Chinese===
穆罕默德还是弥赛亚！你听我说，
完整的真理既不在此，也不在彼；
创造了太阳与月亮的我们的神，
怎会把全部光明给与一人——我看不透。

## VI / 六

===Original===
Come, let us with the naked Night now rest
And read in Allah’s Book the sonnet best:
The Pleiads—ah, the Moon from them departs—
She draws her veil and hastens toward the west.

===Chinese===
来吧，让我们此刻与赤裸的夜同憩，
在安拉的书卷中读那最好的一首十四行：
昴星团（Pleiads）——啊，月亮正离她们远去——
她披上面纱，匆匆向西方赶路。

## VII[28] / 七[28]

===Original===
The Pleiads follow; and our Ethiop Queen,
Emerging from behind her starry screen,
Will steep her tresses in the saffron dye
Of dawn, and vanish in the morning sheen.

===Chinese===
昴星团尾随在后；而我们的埃塞俄比亚王后（Ethiop Queen）
正从她那星辰的屏风后面现身，
她将把长发浸入黎明的
藏红花染液，然后隐没于晨光。

## VIII / 八

===Original===
The secret of the day and night is in
The constellations, which forever spin
Around each other in the comet-dust;—
The comet-dust and humankind are kin.

===Chinese===
白昼与黑夜的秘密就在
诸星座之中，它们永远
在彗星之尘里彼此绕旋；——
彗星之尘与人类本是亲族。

## IX / 九

===Original===
But whether of dust or fire or foam, the glaive
Of Allah cleaves the planet and the wave
Of this mysterious Heaven-Sea of life,
And lo! we have the Cradle of the Grave.

===Chinese===
但无论是尘土、烈焰还是泡沫，安拉的
巨剑总劈开这神秘生命天海的
行星与波涛；看哪！
我们便有了那“坟墓的摇篮”。

## X / 十

===Original===
The Grave and Cradle, the untiring twain,
Who in the markets of this narrow lane
Bordered of darkness, ever give and take
In equal measure—what’s the loss or gain?

===Chinese===
坟墓与摇篮，这不知疲倦的一对，
在这条以黑暗为界的窄巷
市集上，永远等量地一予一取——
到底何所失，又何所得？

## XI / 十一

===Original===
Ay, like the circles which the sun doth spin
Of gossamer, we end as we begin;
Our feet are on the heads of those that pass,
But ever their Graves around our Cradles grin.

===Chinese===
是啊，正像太阳以游丝
织出的那些圆环，我们怎样开始便怎样结束；
我们的脚踩在逝者的头上，
而他们的坟墓总绕着我们的摇篮狞笑。

## XII / 十二

===Original===
And what avails it then that Man be born
To joy or sorrow?—why rejoice or mourn?
The doling doves are calling to the rose;
The dying rose is bleeding o’er the thorn.

===Chinese===
那么，人降生于世又有何益，
无论生于欢乐还是忧愁？——又何必欢喜或悲恸？
哀诉不休的鸽子正声声呼唤玫瑰；
垂死的玫瑰在荆棘上流着血。

## XIII[29] / 十三

===Original===
And he the Messenger, who takes away
The faded garments, purple, white, and gray
Of all our dreams unto the Dyer, will
Bring back new robes to-morrow﻿—so they say.

===Chinese===
而他，那信使，带走我们一切梦境
褪色的衣袍——紫的、白的、灰的——
送往染匠那里；到明天他
将带回崭新的袍子——人们如此说。

## XIV[30] / 十四

===Original===
But now the funeral is passing by,
And in its trail, beneath this moaning sky,
The *howdaj* comes﻿—both vanish into night;
To me are one, the sob, the joyous cry.

===Chinese===
但此刻送葬的行列正经过，
而在它后面，在这呜咽的天空下，
驮轿（howdaj）来了——两者都没入黑夜；
于我是一体的：那啜泣，那欢乐的呼喊。

## XV / 十五

===Original===
With tombs and ruined temples groans the land
In which our forbears in the drifting sand
Arise as dunes upon the track of Time
To mark the cycles of the moving hand

===Chinese===
大地伴着陵墓与倾颓的神庙呻吟，
我们的先祖就在那片土地的流沙里
隆起为沙丘，卧在时间的轨迹上，
标记着那移动之手的轮回

## XVI / 十六

===Original===
Of Fate. Alas! and we shall follow soon
Into the night eternal or the noon;
The wayward daughters of the spheres return
Unto the bosom of their sun or moon.

===Chinese===
命运之手。唉！我们不久也将随之而去，
进入那永恒的黑夜，或正午；
诸天的任性女儿们终要回归
到她们太阳或月亮的怀抱里。

## XVII[31] / 十七

===Original===
And from the last days of Thamud and ʻAd
Up to the first of Hashem’s fearless lad,
Who smashed the idols of his mighty tribe,
What idols and what heroes Death has had!

===Chinese===
而从赛莫德（Thamud）与阿德（ʻAd）的末日，
直到哈希姆（Hashem）家那无畏少年的开元——
他砸碎了自己强盛部族的偶像——
死亡曾拥有过何等的偶像与英雄啊！

## XVIII[32] / 十八

===Original===
Tread lightly, for the mighty that have been
Might now be breathing in the dust unseen;
Lightly, the violets beneath thy feet
Spring from the mole of some Arabian queen.

===Chinese===
轻些行走，因为那些逝去的伟人
也许此刻正隐在尘土中呼吸；
轻些，你脚下的紫罗兰，
正从某位阿拉伯王后的美痣上萌生。

## XIX / 十九

===Original===
Many a grave embraces friend and foe
Behind the curtain of this sorry show
Of love and hate inscrutable; alas!
The Fates will always reap the while they sow.

===Chinese===
多少坟墓拥抱着朋友，也拥抱着仇敌，
在这出可怜戏文的幕布后面，
那爱与恨深不可测；唉！
命运女神们总是边播种边收割。

## XX[33] / 二十

===Original===
The silken fibre of the fell *Zaqqum*,
As warp and woof, is woven on the loom
Of life into a tapestry of dreams
To decorate the chariot-seat of Doom.

===Chinese===
狰狞赞古姆（Zaqqum）的丝滑纤维，
作经线与纬线，在生命之织机上
织成一幅梦的挂毯，
去装点毁灭之神的战车座。

## XXI / 二十一

===Original===
And still we weave, and still we are content
In slaving for the sovereigns who have spent
The savings of the toiling of the mind
Upon the glory of Dismemberment.

===Chinese===
我们仍在编织，而且仍甘愿
为那些君王充当奴隶——他们早已把
心智辛劳攒下的积蓄
挥霍在「肢解」的荣光之上。

## XXII / 二十二

===Original===
Nor king nor slave the hungry Days will spare;
Between their fangèd Hours alike we fare:
Anon they bound upon us while we play
Unheeding at the threshold of their Lair.

===Chinese===
饥饿的日子既不放过君王，也不放过奴隶；
在它们獠牙的时辰之间，我们一律度日：
转眼它们便向我们扑来，而我们正嬉戏着，
不曾提防，就在它们兽穴的门槛旁。

## XXIII[34] / 二十三

===Original===
Then Jannat or Jahannam? From the height
Of reason I can see nor fire nor light
That feeds not on the darknesses; we pass
From world to world, like shadows through the night.

===Chinese===
那么，是天园还是火狱（Jahannam）？从理性
的高度，我看不见火也看不见光
不是以种种黑暗为食的；我们
从一界行向另一界，像影子穿过夜。

## XXIV / 二十四

===Original===
Or sleep﻿—and shall it be eternal sleep
Somewhither in the bosom of the deep
Infinities of cosmic dust, or here
Where gracile cypresses the vigil keep!

===Chinese===
或是睡——那会是永恒的长眠么，
在某处，在宇宙尘埃那深邃
无垠的怀抱里，还是就在这里，
在纤瘦柏树守着夜的地方！

## XXV / 二十五

===Original===
Upon the threshing-floor of life I burn
Beside the Winnower a word to learn;
And only this: Man's of the soil and sun,
And to the soil and sun he shall return.

===Chinese===
在生命的打谷场上，我燃烧着，
守在扬谷者身旁，想学得一句话；
而只有这一句：人出自泥土与太阳，
也将归于泥土与太阳。

## XXVI / 二十六

===Original===
And like a spider's house or sparrow's nest,
The Sultan's palace, though upon the crest
Of glory's mountain, soon or late must go:
Ay, all abodes to ruin are addrest.

===Chinese===
就像蜘蛛的屋或麻雀的巢，
苏丹的宫殿，纵然坐落
在荣誉之山的峰顶，迟早也必倾颓：
是啊，一切居所都注定走向毁亡。

## XXVII / 二十七

===Original===
So, too, the creeds of Man: the one prevails
Until the other comes; and this one fails
When that one triumphs; ay, the lonesome world
Will always want the latest fairy-tales.

===Chinese===
人类的种种信条也是如此：这一个得势，
直到那一个来临；而这一个衰落，
正当那一个得胜；是啊，这寂寞的世界
永远渴求着最新的童话。

## XXVIII / 二十八

===Original===
Seek not the Tavern of Belief, my friend,
Until the *Sakis* there their morals mend;
A lie imbibed a thousand lies will breed,
And thou'lt become a *Saki* in the end.

===Chinese===
我的朋友，别踏进信仰的酒馆，
直到那里的斟酒人（Saki）改好他们的德行；
饮下一句谎言，会滋生一千句谎言，
到头来你自己会变成一个斟酒人。

## XXIX[35] / 二十九[35]

===Original===
By fearing whom I trust I find my way
To truth; by trusting wholly I betray
The trust of wisdom; better far is doubt
Which brings the false into the light of day.

===Chinese===
凭着对我所信者心存疑惧，我寻得
通往真理的路；若全然轻信，我便辜负了
智慧的信托；怀疑要好得多，
它把虚假带到日光之下。

## XXX / 三十

===Original===
Or wilt thou commerce have with those who make
Rugs of the rainbow, rainbows of the snake,
Snakes of a staff, and other wondrous things?—
The burning thirst a mirage can not slake.

===Chinese===
难道你还要同那些匠人做买卖——
他们把彩虹织成毯子，把蛇幻作彩虹，
把手杖化为蛇，变出种种奇物？——
燃烧的焦渴，蜃景无法解消。

## XXXI[36] / 三十一[36]

===Original===
Religion is a maiden veiled in prayer,
Whose bridal gifts and dowry those who care
Can buy in *Mutakallim*'s shop of words
But I for such, a dirham can not spare.

===Chinese===
宗教是一位以祷告为面纱的少女，
她的聘礼与嫁妆，凡是有心的人
都能在穆塔卡利姆（Mutakallim）的辞藻铺子里买到，
但我，为此可一个迪拉姆（dirham）也掏不出。

## XXXII / 三十二

===Original===
Why linger here, why turn another page?
Oh! seal with doubt the whole book of the age;
Doubt every one, even him, the seeming slave
Of righteousness, and doubt the canting sage.

===Chinese===
为何在此流连，为何再翻一页？
哦！用怀疑封印整个时代之书；
怀疑每一个人，就连那貌似
正义之仆的人也怀疑，更要怀疑那伪善的圣哲。

## XXXIII[37] / 三十三[37]

===Original===
Some day the weeping daughters of Hadil
Will say unto the bulbuls: "Let's appeal
To Allah in behalf of Brother Man
Who's at the mercy now of Ababil."

===Chinese===
终有一日，哭泣的哈迪勒（Hadil）的女儿们
会对夜莺们说：“让我们
替人类兄弟向安拉申诉吧，
他如今正落在阿巴比尔（Ababil）的掌心里。”

## XXXIV / 三十四

===Original===
Of Ababil! I would the tale were true—
Would all the birds were such winged furies too;
The scourging and the purging were a boon
For me, O my dear Brothers, and for you.

===Chinese===
阿巴比尔啊！但愿那传闻是真——
但愿所有的鸟都是那样带翅的复仇者；
那鞭笞与涤罪将是恩赐，
于我，哦，我亲爱的兄弟们，也于你们。

## XXXV / 三十五

===Original===
Methinks Allah divides me to complete
His problem, which with Xs is replete;
For I am free and I am too in chains
Groping along the labyrinthine street.

===Chinese===
我想，安拉把我分解开来，为的是解开
他那道难题——里面布满了未知数X；
因为我自由，却又身戴锁链，
摸索着走过迷宫般的街巷。

## XXXVI / 三十六

===Original===
And round the Well how oft my Soul doth grope
Athirst; but lo! my Bucket hath no Rope:
I cry for water, and the deep, dark Well
Echoes my wailing cry, but not my hope.

===Chinese===
而围着那口井，我的灵魂何等频频
焦渴地摸索；但看哪！我的桶没有绳索：
我呼喊求水，那幽深的暗井
回响着我哀号的哭声，却不回应我的希望。

## XXXVII / 三十七

===Original===
Ah, many have I seen of those who fell
While drawing, with a swagger, from the Well;
They came with Rope and Bucket, and they went
Empty of hand another tale to tell.

===Chinese===
啊，我见过许多跌了跟头的人，
就在他们大摇大摆地从井中汲水之时；
他们带着绳子与水桶而来，走时
却两手空空，只落得另一段故事可讲。

## XXXVIII[38] / 三十八[38]

===Original===
The *I* in me standing upon the brink
Would leap into the Well to get a drink;
But how to rise once in the depth, I cry,
And cowardly behind my logic slink.

===Chinese===
我身内的那个“我”（I）站在井沿上，
一心想跳进井里喝上一口水；
可是一旦落入深处怎样再上来？我喊道，
便怯懦地缩到我的逻辑背后溜走。

## XXXIX / 三十九

===Original===
And she: “How long must I the burden bear?
How long this tattered garment must I wear?”
And I: “Why wear it? Leave it here, and go
Away without it﻿—little do I care.”

===Chinese===
她说：“这副重担我还得背多久？
这身褴褛我还得穿多久？”
我说：“何必穿它？把它留在这儿，
扔下它走吧——我才不在乎呢。”

## XL / 四十

===Original===
But once when we were quarreling, the door
Was opened by a Visitor who bore
Both Rope and Pail; he offered them and said:
“Drink, if you will, but once, and nevermore.”

===Chinese===
但有一回我们正吵着架，门
被一位来客推开，他手里
提着绳子和水桶；他把它们递上来说：
“喝吧，若你们愿意——只此一次，再无来日。”

## XLI / 四十一

===Original===
One draught, more bitter than the *Zaqqum* tree,
Brought us unto the land of mystery
Where rising Sand and Dust and Flame conceal
The door of every Caravanseri.

===Chinese===
那一饮，比赞古姆（Zaqqum）之树更苦，
把我们带进了神秘的国度，
那里腾起的沙、尘与烈焰遮蔽了
每一座商队客栈（Caravanseri）的大门。

## XLII / 四十二

===Original===
We reach a door and there the legend find.
“To all the Pilgrims of the Human Mind:
Knock and pass on!” We knock and knock and knock;
But no one answers save the moaning wind.

===Chinese===
我们走到一扇门前，在那里读到铭文：
“致人类心灵的一切朝圣者：
敲了门便继续前行！”我们敲了又敲，敲个不停；
却无人应门，唯有呜咽的风。

## XLIII / 四十三

===Original===
How like a door the knowledge we attain,
Which door is on the bourne of the Inane;
It opens and our nothingness is closed﻿—
It closes and in darkness we remain.

===Chinese===
我们求得的知识多么像一扇门，
这扇门立在虚无之境的边界上；
它打开，我们的虚无便被合上——
它合上，我们便留在黑暗之中。

## XLIV / 四十四

===Original===
Hither we come unknowing, hence we go;
Unknowing we are messaged to and fro;
And yet we think we know all things of earth
And sky﻿—the suns and stars we think we know.

===Chinese===
我们懵然无知地来到这里，又懵然无知地离去；
我们懵然无知地被差来遣去；
可我们却自以为认得大地上
和天空中的一切——自以为认得诸日与群星。

## XLV / 四十五

===Original===
Apply thy wit, O Brother, here and there
Upon this and upon that; but beware
Lest in the end﻿—ah, better at the start
Go to the Tinker for a slight repair.

===Chinese===
施展你的才智吧，兄弟啊，在这里
在那里，对这个对那个推敲一番；但小心，
免得到头来——啊，不如从一开始
就去找补锅匠做个小小的修补。

## XLVI / 四十六

===Original===
And why so much ado, and wherefore lay
The burden of the years upon the day
Of thy vain dreams? Who polishes his sword
Morning and eve will polish it away.

===Chinese===
何必这样大惊小怪，又为何
把经年累月的重担压在你
虚妄之梦的白昼上？谁若清晨
与黄昏都磨他的剑，终会把剑磨光。

## XLVII / 四十七

===Original===
I heard it whispered in the cryptic streets
Where every sage the same dumb shadow meets:
“We are but words fallen from the lips of Time
Which God, that we might understand, repeats.”

===Chinese===
我在隐秘的街巷里听见有人低语——
在那里，每个智者都遇见同一个哑影：
“我们不过是时间唇边落下的字词，
神为了让我们听懂，又将它们复述。”

## XLVIII[39] / 四十八[39]

===Original===
Another said: “The creeping worm hath shown,
In her discourse on human flesh and bone,
That Man was once the bed on which she slept﻿—
The walking dust was once a thing of stone.”

===Chinese===
另一个人说：“那蠕行的蛆虫
在她论人类血肉与骨骸的讲说里已表明：
人曾是她睡过的床——
这行走的尘土曾是石头做的东西。”

## XLIX[40] / 四十九[40]

===Original===
And still another: “We are coins which fade
 In circulation, coins which Allah made
 To cheat Iblis: the good and bad alike
 Are spent by Fate upon a passing shade.”
===Chinese===
还有一个人说：“我们是渐渐磨损的钱币，

在流通中磨损——是安拉铸下的钱币，

为的是瞒骗伊布利斯（Iblis）：善与恶一样，

都被命运花在一个匆匆而过的影子上。”

## L[41] / 五十[41]

===Original===
And in the pottery the potter cried,
 As on his work shone all the master’s pride—
 “How is it, *Rabbi*, I, thy slave, can make
 Such vessels as nobody dare deride?”
===Chinese===
在陶坊里，那陶工高声呼喊，

这时主宰的全副骄傲正照在他的作品上——

“这是怎么回事，拉比（Rabbi）？我，你的奴隶，竟能造出

无人敢嘲弄的器皿？”

## LI / 五十一

===Original===
The Earth then spake: “My children silent be;
 Same are to God the camel and the flea:
 He makes a mess of me to nourish you,
 Then makes a mess of you to nourish me.”
===Chinese===
于是大地开口说：“我的孩子们，安静些；

骆驼和跳蚤在神眼里是一回事：

祂把我糟践成秽土，好滋养你们，

再把你们糟践成秽土，好滋养我。”

## LII / 五十二

===Original===
Now, I believe the Potter will essay
 Once more the Wheel, and from a better clay
 Will make a better Vessel, and perchance
 A masterpiece which will endure for aye.
===Chinese===
如今我相信，那位陶匠还会再试

一次陶轮，并用更好的泥土

造出更好的器皿，说不定

还能造出一件永世长存的杰作。

## LIII / 五十三

===Original===
With better skill he even will remould
 The scattered potsherds of the New and Old;
 Then you and I will not disdain to buy,
 Though in the mart of Iblis they be sold.
===Chinese===
凭着更精的手艺，他甚至会把

新约与旧约的散碎陶片重新塑合；

那时你我便不至于不屑去买，

哪怕它们是在伊布利斯的市集上售卖的。

## LIV / 五十四

===Original===
Sooth I have told the masters of the mart
 Of rusty creeds and Babylonian art
 Of magic. Now the truth about myself—
 Here is the secret of my wincing heart.
===Chinese===
说实话，我已向那市集的主人们

诉说过——那锈蚀信条与巴比伦

魔法之术的市集。如今说说我自己的真相——

这便是我这颗畏缩之心的秘密。

## LV / 五十五

===Original===
I muse, but in my musings I recall
 The days of my iniquity; we’re all—
 An arrow shot across the wilderness,
 Somewhither, in the wilderness must fall.
===Chinese===
我沉思，但在沉思中我记起了

自己作孽的日子；我们都是——

一支射越旷野的箭，

无论射向何处，终要坠落在旷野里。

## LVI[42] / 五十六[42]

===Original===
I laugh, but in my laughter-cup I pour
 The tears of scorn and melancholy sore;
 I who am shattered by the hand of Doubt,
 Like glass to be remoulded nevermore.
===Chinese===
我笑，但在我这笑声之杯里，我斟入

讥诮的眼泪和深重的忧郁；

我这被“怀疑”（Doubt）之手击碎的人，

已如玻璃，再无重铸之日。

## LVII[43] / 五十七[43]

===Original===
I wheedle, too, even like my slave Zeidun,
 Who robs at dawn his brother, and at noon
 Prostrates himself in prayer—ah, let us pray
 That Night might blot us and our sins, and soon.
===Chinese===
我也会连哄带骗，正像我的奴隶宰敦（Zeidun），

他黎明时劫掠自己的兄弟，到了正午

却俯伏在地祈祷——啊，让我们祈祷

夜快快把我们连同我们的罪一并抹去。

## LVIII[44] / 五十八[44]

===Original===
But in the fatal coils, without intent,
 We sin; wherefore a future punishment?
 They say the metal dead a deadly steel
 Becomes with Allah’s knowledge and consent.
===Chinese===
但在那命定的罗网里，我们并非出于本心

便犯了罪；那又何须来世的惩罚？

人们说，死寂的金属必得凭安拉的

知晓与首肯，才化作夺命的钢铁。

## LIX[45] / 五十九[45]

===Original===
And even the repentant sinner’s tear
 Falling into Jahannam’s very ear,
 Goes to its heart, extinguishes its fire
 For ever and forever—so I hear.
===Chinese===
就连悔罪者的一滴泪，

落进火狱的耳中，

也会渗到它的心里，把它的火焰

永永远远地浇熄——我是这么听说的。

## LX / 六十

===Original===
Between the white and purple Words of Time
 In motley garb with Destiny I rhyme:
 The colored glasses to the water give
 The colors of a symbolry sublime.
===Chinese===
在时间那白色与紫色的言辞之间，

我身披彩衣，与命运押韵：

有色的玻璃赋予流水

那崇高象征的色彩。

## LXI / 六十一

===Original===
How oft, when young, my brothers I would shun
If their religious feelings were not spun
Of my own cobweb, which I find was but
A spider’s revelation of the sun.

===Chinese===
年少时，我常常躲开我的兄弟们，
倘若他们的宗教情感不是由
我自己的蛛网织成——如今我发觉，那不过是
一只蜘蛛对太阳的启示。

## LXII[46] / 六十二[46]

===Original===
Now, mosques and churches﻿—even a Kaaba Stone,
Korans and Bibles﻿—even a martyr’s bone﻿—
All these and more my heart can tolerate,
For my religion ’s love, and love alone.

===Chinese===
如今，清真寺与教堂——甚至一块天房黑石（Kaaba Stone），
《古兰经》与《圣经》——甚至殉道者的一段遗骨——
这一切乃至更多，我的心都能容忍，
因为我的宗教是爱，且唯有爱。

## LXIII / 六十三

===Original===
To humankind, O Brother, consecrate
Thy heart, and shun the hundred Sects that prate
About the things they little know about﻿—
Let all receive thy pity, none thy hate.

===Chinese===
兄弟啊，把你的心奉献给
全人类，避开那上百个夸夸其谈的宗派——
它们谈论的却是自己几乎一无所知的事物——
愿人人都得到你的怜悯，无人得到你的憎恨。

## LXIV / 六十四

===Original===
The tavern and the temple also shun,
For sheikh and libertine in sooth are one;
And when the pious knave begins to pule,
The knave in purple breaks his vow anon.

===Chinese===
酒馆与神庙也要避开，
因为谢赫（sheikh）与浪荡子实为一路；
当那虔诚的无赖开始呜呜咽咽地祈祷，
那穿紫袍的无赖随即也把誓愿抛却。

## LXV / 六十五

===Original===
“The wine’s forbidden,” say these honest folk,
But for themselves the law they will revoke;
The snivelling sheikh says he’s without a garb,
When in the tap-house he had pawned his cloak,

===Chinese===
“酒是被禁止的，”这些老实人说，
可轮到他们自己，便把教法废去；
那抽着鼻涕的谢赫说自己没了衣袍——
其实他在小酒铺里已把斗篷典当，

## LXVI / 六十六

===Original===
Or in the house of lust. The priestly name
And priestly turban once were those of Shame﻿—
And Shame is preaching in the pulpit now﻿—
If pulpits tumble down, I’m not to blame.

===Chinese===
又或是在淫欲之屋里。教士的名号
与教士的头巾，从前本属“羞耻”所有——
而“羞耻”如今正在讲坛上布道——
倘若讲坛坍塌下来，罪不在我。

## LXVII / 六十七

===Original===
For after she declaims upon the vows
Of Faith, she pusillanimously bows
Before the Sultan’s wine-empurpled throne,
While he and all his courtesans carouse.

===Chinese===
因为在她高声宣讲完
信仰的誓愿之后，她又怯懦地
躬身拜倒在苏丹那酒染紫红的御座前，
而他正同满座的宠姬纵酒狂欢。

## LXVIII / 六十八

===Original===
Carouse, ye sovereign lords! The wheel will roll
Forever to confound and to console:
Who sips today the golden cup will drink
Mayhap to-morrow in a wooden bowl﻿—

===Chinese===
痛饮吧，你们这些君王们！车轮将
永远滚转，搅乱人也抚慰人：
今日啜饮金杯的人，
明日或许要捧着木碗而饮——

## LXIX / 六十九

===Original===
And silent drink. The tumult of our mirth
Is worse than our mad welcoming of birth:﻿—
The thunder hath a grandeur, but the rains,
Without the thunder, quench the thirst of Earth.

===Chinese===
并且要默默地饮。我们欢愉的喧腾
比我们疯狂迎庆降生还要糟：——
雷霆自有其壮伟，而雨水，
无需雷霆，便能解大地的干渴。

## LXX / 七十

===Original===
The Prophets, too, among us come to teach,
Are one with those who from the pulpit preach;
They pray, and slay, and pass away, and yet
Our ills are as the pebbles on the beach.

===Chinese===
那些来到我们中间施教的先知们，
与讲坛上布道的人原是一体；
他们祈祷，杀戮，然后逝去，然而
我们的疾苦依旧像海滩上的卵石那样多。

## LXXI / 七十一

===Original===
And though around the temple they should run
For seventy times and seven, and in the sun
Of mad devotion drool, their prayers are still
Like their desires of feasting-fancies spun.

===Chinese===
纵使他们绕着神庙奔跑，
跑上七十个七次，在疯狂虔敬的
烈日下淌着涎水，他们的祈祷依旧
同他们宴饮空想纺成的欲念一般。

## LXXII / 七十二

===Original===
Oh! let them in the marshes grope, or ride
Their jaded Myths along the mountain-side;
Come up with me, O Brother, to the heights
Where Reason is the prophet and the guide.

===Chinese===
啊！由他们在沼泽里摸索，或者骑着
他们那疲乏的“神话”沿山麓而行；
兄弟啊，随我一起登上高处吧，
在那里，理性就是先知，就是引路人。

## LXXIII / 七十三

===Original===
“What is thy faith and creed,” they ask of me,
“And who art thou? Unseal thy pedigree.”—
I am the child of Time, my tribe, mankind,
And now this world’s my caravanseri.

===Chinese===
“你的信仰与信条是什么？”他们这样问我，
“你是何人？亮出你的世系来。”——
我是时间之子，我的部族，是全人类，
而今这整个世界，就是我的商队客栈。

## LXXIV / 七十四

===Original===
Swathe thee in wool, my Sufi friend, and go
Thy way; in cotton I the wiser grow;
But we ourselves are shreds of earth, and soon
The Tailor of the Universe will sew.

===Chinese===
把你自己裹进羊毛里去吧，我的苏菲朋友，自去
走你的路；我裹着棉衣，反倒越长越聪明；
但我们自己本是尘土的碎布，而不久
那“宇宙的裁缝”就要动针缝缀。

## LXXV / 七十五

===Original===
Ay! suddenly the mystic Hand will seal
The saint’s devotion and the sinner’s weal;
They worship Saturn, but I worship One
Before whom Saturn and the Heavens kneel.

===Chinese===
是啊！那只神秘之手会突然封结
圣徒的虔敬与罪人的福乐；
他们崇拜土星（Saturn），而我崇拜“唯一者”，
在祂面前，土星与诸天齐齐跪拜。

## LXXVI / 七十六

===Original===
Among the crumbling ruins of the creeds
The Scout upon his camel played his reeds
And called out to his people—“Let us hence!
The pasture here is full of noxious weeds.”

===Chinese===
在纷纷崩塌的信条废墟之间，
探路人骑在驼背上吹响芦笛，
向着他的族人高呼——“我们离开这儿吧！
这片牧场上长满了毒草。”

## LXXVII[47] / 七十七[47]

===Original===
Among us falsehood is proclaimed aloud,
But truth is whispered to the phantom bowed
Of conscience; ay! and Wrong is ever crowned,
While Right and Reason are denied a shroud.

===Chinese===
在我们中间，虚妄总被高声宣告，
真理却只低声说给良心那躬身
俯首的幽灵；是啊！“错误”永远戴着王冠，
“公义”与“理性”却连一袭裹尸布也得不到。

## LXXVIII / 七十八

===Original===
And why in this dark Kingdom tribute pay?
With clamant multitudes why stop to pray?
Oh! hear the inner Voice:—“If thou’lt be right,
Do what they deem is wrong, and go thy way.”

===Chinese===
为什么还要在这黑暗的国度里缴纳贡赋？
为什么还要随着喧嚷的人群停下祈祷？
哦！听那内在的声音：——“你若要行得正，
就去做他们认为错的事，然后走你的路。”

## LXXIX / 七十九

===Original===
Thy way unto the Sun the spaces through
Where king Orion’s black-eyed houris slew
The Mother of Night to guide the Wings that bear
The flame divine hid in a drop of dew.

===Chinese===
你的路通往太阳，要穿过那浩渺太空——
猎户座之王（Orion）的黑眼天女（houris）曾在那里
杀死夜之母，好为那对翅膀引路——它们驮着
藏在一滴露水之中的神圣火焰。

## LXXX / 八十

===Original===
Hear ye who in the dust of ages creep,
And in the halls of wicked masters sleep:—
Arise! and out of this wan weariness
Where Allah’s laughter makes the Devil weep.

===Chinese===
听着，你们这些在岁月尘土中爬行、
在恶主殿堂里酣睡的人：——
起来！从这苍白的倦怠中脱身吧——
在这里，安拉的大笑正令魔鬼哭泣。

## LXXXI / 八十一

===Original===
Arise! for lo! the Laughter and the Weeping
Reveal the Weapon which the Master’s keeping
Above your heads; Oh! take it up and strike!
The lion of tyranny is only sleeping.

===Chinese===
起来！看哪！那“大笑”与“恸哭”
正揭示主人悬守在你们
头顶上的那件武器；哦！拾起它，出击吧！
暴虐的雄狮此刻不过是在酣睡。

## LXXXII / 八十二

===Original===
Evil and Virtue? Shadows on the street
Of Fate and Vanity—but shadows meet
When in the gloaming they are hast’ning forth
To drink with Night annihilation sweet.

===Chinese===
邪恶与美德？不过是命运与浮华
街头上的影子——但影子们相遇，
是在暮色四合时，它们行色匆匆，
赶去与黑夜共饮那甜美的寂灭。

## LXXXIII / 八十三

===Original===
And thus the Sun will write and will efface
The mystic symbols which the sages trace
In vain, for all the worlds of God are stored
In his enduring vessels Time and Space.

===Chinese===
太阳便这样写了又涂去，
那些圣哲徒然追摹的神秘符号——
因为神的万千世界，全都收存在
他那恒久的容器“时间”与“空间”里。

## LXXXIV / 八十四

===Original===
For all my learning ’s but a veil, I guess,
Veiling the phantom of my nothingness;
Howbeit, there are those who think me wise,
And those who think me—even these I bless.

===Chinese===
我全部的学识，我想，不过是一层面纱，
遮掩着我那“虚无”的幽灵；
话虽如此，总有人把我看作智者，
也有人把我看作——这样的人，我也祝福。

## LXXXV / 八十五

===Original===
And all my years, as vapid as my lay,
Are bitter morsels of a mystic day—
The day of Fate, who carries in his lap
December snows and snow-white flowers of May.

===Chinese===
而我所有的岁月，像我的歌一样寡淡，
是那神秘之日的一口口苦涩碎食——
那是命运之日：他膝上怀抱着
十二月的积雪与五月雪白的花。

## LXXXVI / 八十六

===Original===
Allah, my sleep is woven through, it seems,
With burning threads of night and golden beams;
But when my dreams are evil they come true;
When they are not, they are, alas! but dreams.

===Chinese===
安拉啊，我的睡眠仿佛被织透了：
以夜的灼热丝缕、以金色的光束织成；
但我的梦若是恶梦，便会成真；
若不是，唉！便终究只是梦。

## LXXXVII / 八十七

===Original===
The subtle ways of Destiny I know;
In me she plays her game of “Give and Go.”
Misfortune I receive in cash, but joy,
In drafts on Heaven or on the winds that blow.

===Chinese===
命运那微妙的路数，我了然于心；
她在我身上玩她那“予了便走”的游戏。
灾祸我一律现款收讫，而欢乐呢，
只凭向上天或长风开出的期票。

## LXXXVIII / 八十八

===Original===
I give and go, grim Destiny—I play
Upon this checker-board of Night and Day
The dark game with thee, but the day will come
When one will turn the Board the other way.

===Chinese===
我也予了便走，冷酷的命运——我在这
昼夜的棋盘上同你对弈幽暗的一局，
但那一天终会到来，
有人会把棋盘翻转向另一面。

## LXXXIX / 八十九

===Original===
If my house-swallow, laboring with zest,
Felt like myself the burden of unrest,
Unlightened by inscrutable designs,
She would not build her young that cozy nest.

===Chinese===
倘若我檐下的家燕，满怀热忱地操劳，
也像我一样感到不安的重负——
那从不因莫测天意而稍减的重负——
她就不会为幼雏筑起那安乐的巢了。

## XC[48] / 九十[48]

===Original===
Thy life with guiltless life-blood do not stain—
Hunt not the children of the woods; in vain
Thou’lt try one day to wash thy bloody hand:
Nor hunter here nor hunted long remain.

===Chinese===
别让无辜生灵的血玷污你的生命——
不要猎杀林木间的儿女；终有一天，
你想洗净这沾血的手，只是徒劳：
猎者与被猎者，在此都留不长久。

## XCI / 九十一

===Original===
Oh! cast my dust away from thee, and doff
Thy cloak of sycophancy and like stuff:
I’m but a shadow on the sandy waste—
Enough of thy duplicity, enough!

===Chinese===
哦！把我这尘土从你身上抖落吧，再脱去
你那谄谀的斗篷以及诸如此类的什物：
我不过是沙荒之上的一道影子——
你的口是心非，够了，够了！

## XCII / 九十二

===Original===
Behold! the Veil that hid thy soul is torn
And all thy secrets on the winds are borne:
The hand of Sin has written on thy face
“Awake, for these untimely furrows warn!”

===Chinese===
看哪！遮蔽你灵魂的那道面纱已被撕裂，
你全部的秘密都随风飘散：
罪恶之手已在你脸上写下
“醒来吧，这些不合时宜的皱纹便是警告！”

## XCIII[49][50] / 九十三[49][50]

===Original===
A prince of souls, ’tis sung in ancient lay,
One morning sought a vesture of the clay;
He came into the Pottery, the fool—
The lucky fool was warned to stay away.

===Chinese===
据古歌所唱，有一位灵魂的君王，
某个清晨寻求一袭黏土的衣裳；
他走进了那陶坊，这愚人——
这走运的愚人，原曾受告诫莫要近前。

## XCIV[51][52] / 九十四[51][52]

===Original===
But I was not. Oh! that the Fates decree
That I now cast aside this clay of me;
My soul and body wedded for a while
Are sick and would that separation be.

===Chinese===
而我却未曾受此告诫。哦！但愿命运诸神
降旨，让我此刻便抛下这自我的黏土；
我的灵魂与肉体短暂结为一体，
如今已然病惫，只愿就此分离。

## XCV[53] / 九十五[53]

===Original===
“Thou shalt not kill!”—Thy words, O God, we heed,
Though thy two Soul-devouring Angels feed
Thy Promise of another life on this—
To have spared us both, it were a boon indeed.

===Chinese===
“不可杀人！”——神啊，你的话我们谨守，
尽管你那两位噬人灵魂的天使，
正以今生喂养你“另有一生”的应许——
若肯把我们两个一并宽免，那确是恩典。

## XCVI[54] / 九十六[54]

===Original===
Oh! that some one would but return to tell
If old Nubakht is burning now in hell,
Or if the workers for the Prophet’s prize
Are laughing at his Paradisal sell.

===Chinese===
哦！但愿有人能回来告诉我们一声，
老努巴赫特（Nubakht）如今是否正在火狱里燃烧；
又或者，那些为先知的赏赐效劳的人们，
是否正笑话他这桩天园的骗局。

## XCVII / 九十七

===Original===
Once I have tried to string a few Pearl-seeds
Upon my Rosary of wooden beads;
But I have searched, and I have searched in vain
For pearls in all the caverns of the creeds

===Chinese===
我曾试着把几粒珍珠之籽
串上我这木珠的念珠串；
但我寻了又寻，终是徒然——
在诸般信条的座座洞窟里觅不到珍珠

## XCVIII / 九十八

===Original===
And in the palaces of wealth I found
Some beads of wisdom scattered on the ground,
Around the throne of Power, beneath the feet
Of fair-faced slaves with flowers of folly crowned.

===Chinese===
而在财富的宫殿里我发现
几颗智慧的念珠散落在地上，
散在权力宝座的四周，落在
那头戴愚妄之花的姣面奴隶足下。

## XCIX / 九十九

===Original===
Thy wealth can shed no tears around thy bier,
Nor can it wash thy hands of shame and fear;
Ere thou departest with it freely part—
Let others plead for thee and God will hear.

===Chinese===
你的财富不会围着你的灵柩落泪，
也洗不净你双手沾着的羞耻与恐惧；
趁你启程之前，先与它慷慨分手——
让旁人替你恳求吧，神自会垂听。

## C / 一百

===Original===
For me thy silks and feathers have no charm
The pillow I like best is my right arm;
The comforts of this passing show I spurn,
For Poverty can do the soul no harm.

===Chinese===
于我，你的绸缎与羽绒毫无魅力，
我最中意的枕头是我自己的右臂；
这转瞬即逝的浮世之戏的安逸我尽皆鄙弃，
因为贫穷伤害不了灵魂。

## CI / 一百零一

===Original===
The guiding hand of Allah I can see
Upon my staff: of what use then is he
Who’d be the blind man’s guide? Thou silent oak,
No son of Eve shall walk with me and thee.

===Chinese===
安拉引路的手，我看得见，
它就在我的手杖上：那么，那自愿
做盲人向导的人又有何用？沉默的橡树啊，
没有一个夏娃（Eve）之子肯与你我同行。

## CII / 一百零二

===Original===
My life’s the road on which I blindly speed:
My goal’s the grave on which I plant a reed
To shape my Hope, but soon the Hand unseen
Will strike, and lo! I’m but a sapless weed.

===Chinese===
我的生命是我盲目疾驰其上的路：
我的终点是坟，坟上我栽一株芦苇，
借它塑出我的希望；但不一会儿，那看不见的手
便挥来一击——看哪！我不过是一株枯瘪的野草。

## CIII[55] / 一百零三[55]

===Original===
O *Rabbi*, curse us not if we have been
Nursed in the shadow of the Gate of Sin
Built by thy hand—yea, ev’n thine angels blink
When we are coming out and going in.

===Chinese===
哦，拉比，别诅咒我们，倘若我们
一直受哺于罪孽之门的暗影，
那门是你亲手所造——是啊，连你的众天使也要眨眼，
当我们在其间进进出出的时候。

## CIV[56] / 一百零四[56]

===Original===
And like the dead of Ind I do not fear
To go to thee in flames; the most austere
Angel of fire a softer tooth and tongue
Hath he than dreadful Munkar and Nakir.

===Chinese===
而像印度（Ind）的死者那样，我不惧怕
在烈焰中归向你；即便火中最森严的
天使，也长着更柔软的齿与舌，
比起那可怕的孟卡尔与纳基尔（Munkar and Nakir）。

## CV[57] / 一百零五[57]

===Original===
Now, at this end of Adam’s line I stand
Holding my father’s life-curse in my hand,
Doing no one the wrong that he did me:—
Ah, would that he were barren as the sand!

===Chinese===
此刻，我站在亚当（Adam）一脉的这一末端，
手里握着我父亲那生命的咒诅，
却不曾把他施于我的不义施于任何人：——
啊，但愿他像沙地一样荒瘠不育！

## CVI[58] / 一百零六[58]

===Original===
Ay, thus thy children, though they sovereigns be,
When truth upon them dawns, will turn on thee,
Who cast them into life’s dark labyrinth
Where even old Azrael can not see.

===Chinese===
是啊，你的儿女纵然贵为君王，
当真理在他们身上破晓，也会掉头攻击你，
是你把他们抛进了生命的黑暗迷宫，
在那里面，连年迈的亚兹拉尔（Azrael）也看不见。

## CVII / 一百零七

===Original===
And in the labyrinth both son and sire
Awhile will fan and fuel hatred’s fire;
Sparks of the log of evil are all men
Allwhere—extinguished be the race entire!

===Chinese===
而在这迷宫里，儿子与父亲
还会一时扇旺仇恨之火、为它添薪；
众人全是那根罪恶之木迸出的火星，
遍布各处——愿整个族类就此熄灭！

## CVIII / 一百零八

===Original===
If miracles were wrought in ancient years,
Why not today, O Heaven-cradled seers?
The highway’s strewn with dead, the lepers weep,
If ye but knew—if ye but saw their tears!

===Chinese===
倘若奇迹在远古岁月里曾显现，
为何今日不能，哦天上摇篮里的先知们？
大路上尸骸狼藉，麻风病人哭泣，
只要你们知道——只要你们看见他们的眼泪！

## CIX / 一百零九

===Original===
Fan thou a lisping fire and it will leap
In flames, but dost thou fan an ashy heap?
They would respond, indeed, whom thou dost call,
Were they not dead, alas! or dead asleep.

===Chinese===
你给一簇呢喃低语的火苗扇风，它便会
跃起成烈焰；可你去扇一堆灰烬又如何？
你呼唤的那些人，他们原会应答的，
若非已经死了，唉！或睡得死一般沉。

## CX / 一百一十

===Original===
The way of vice is open as the sky,
The way of virtue’s like the needle’s eye;
But whether here or there, the eager Soul
Has only two Companions—Whence and Why.

===Chinese===
罪恶之路敞阔如天空，
德行之路却窄似针眼；
而不论走此路还是彼路，热切的灵魂
只有两个同伴——“从何”（Whence）与“为何”（Why）。

## CXI / 一百一十一

===Original===
Whence come, O firmament, thy myriad lights?
Whence comes thy sap, O vineyard of the heights?
Whence comes the perfume of the rose, and whence
The spirit-larva which the body blights?

===Chinese===
苍穹啊，你的万千光焰从何而来？
高处的葡萄园啊，你的汁液从何而来？
玫瑰的芬芳从何而来，而那
被肉身摧残的灵魂之蛹又从何而来？

## CXII / 一百一十二

===Original===
Whence does the nettle get its bitter sting?
Whence do the honey bees their honey bring?
Whence our Companions, too—our Whence and Why?
O Soul, I do not know a single thing!

===Chinese===
荨麻从哪里得来它苦毒的螫刺？
蜜蜂从哪里采来它们的蜂蜜？
我们的同伴——“从何”与“为何”——又从何而来？
灵魂啊，我连一件事也不知道！

## CXIII / 一百一十三

===Original===
How many like us in the ages past
Have blindly soared, though like a pebble cast,
Seeking the veil of mystery to tear,
But fell accurst beneath the burning blast?

===Chinese===
往昔世代里，有多少像我们一样的人
曾盲目高飞，虽如抛掷出的石子，
想撕开那神秘的帷幕，
却在灼烈的狂风之下受咒坠落？

## CXIV / 一百一十四

===Original===
Why try to con the book of earth and sky,
Why seek the truth which neither you nor I
Can grasp? But Death methinks the secret keeps,
And will impart it to us by and by.

===Chinese===
为何要苦读大地与天空之书？
为何寻求那你我谁也
无法把握的真理？但死亡，我想，正保守着那秘密，
并将在日后向我们传授。

## CXV[59] / 一百一十五[59]

===Original===
The Sultan, too, relinquishing his throne
Must wayfare through the darkening dust alone
Where neither crown nor kingdom be, and he,
Part of the Secret, here and there is blown.

===Chinese===
苏丹（Sultan）也一样，一旦舍弃了王座，
就必须独自跋涉过渐暗的尘土，
那里既无王冠也无王国；而他，
那秘密的一部分，被吹得四处飘零。

## CXVI[60] / 一百一十六[60]

===Original===
To clay the mighty Sultan must return
And, chancing, help a praying slave to burn
His midnight oil before the face of Him,
Who of the Sultan makes an incense urn.

===Chinese===
强权的苏丹也必须归回泥土，
碰巧还能帮一个祈祷的奴隶
在祂面前燃点午夜的灯油——
祂把苏丹做成了一只香炉。

## CXVII[61] / 一百一十七[61]

===Original===
Turned to a cup, who once the sword of state
Held o’er the head of slave and potentate,
Is now held in the tippler’s trembling hand,
Or smashed upon the tavern-floor of Fate.

===Chinese===
化作了一只杯子的，是那曾经
把权剑擎在奴隶与君王头顶的人；
如今他被握在酒客颤抖的手里，
或被砸碎在命运的酒馆地板上。

## CXVIII[62] / 一百一十八[62]

===Original===
For this I say, Be watchful of the Cage
Of chance; it opes alike to fool and sage;
Spy on the moment, for to-morrow’ll be,
Like yesterday, an obliterated page.

===Chinese===
为此我说：当心那机缘的牢笼；
它向愚人与智者同样敞开；
窥伺此刻吧，因为明天将会
像昨天一样，是一页字迹涂销的白纸。

## CXIX / 一百一十九

===Original===
Yea, kiss the rosy cheeks of new-born Day,
And hail eternity in every ray
Forming a halo round its infant head,
Illumining thy labyrinthine way.

===Chinese===
是啊，去亲吻新生白昼的玫瑰色面颊，
在每一道光线里向永恒致敬——
那光环环绕着它婴孩的头颅，
照亮你迷宫一般的路途。

## CXX / 一百二十

===Original===
But I, the thrice-imprisoned, try to troll
Strains of the song of night, which fill with dole
My blindness, my confinement, and my flesh—
The sordid habitation of my soul.

===Chinese===
但我，这三重囚禁的，试着高声唱出
夜之歌的曲调——它们使我的失明、
我的幽禁、我的肉体盈满哀伤——
那是我灵魂卑污的居所。

## CXXI / 一百二十一

===Original===
Howbeit, my inner vision heir shall be
To the increasing flames of mystery
Which may illumine yet my prisons all,
And crown the ever living hope of me.

===Chinese===
然而，我的内在视界终将承继
那愈燃愈旺的神秘火焰，
它们或许仍会照亮我全部的牢狱，
并为我永生不灭的希望加冕。
