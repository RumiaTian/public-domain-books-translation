### I / I

===Original===
All the most important things in his life, St. Peter sometimes reflected, had been determined by chance. His education in France had been an accident. His married life had been happy largely through a circumstance with which neither he nor his wife had anything to do. They had been young people with good qualities, and very much in love, but they could not have been happy if Lillian had not inherited a small income from her father—only about sixteen hundred a year, but it had made all the difference in the world. A few memorable interregnums between servants had let him know that Lillian couldn’t pinch and be shabby and do housework, as the wives of some of his colleagues did. Under such conditions she became another person, and a bitter one.

===Chinese===
圣彼得有时想，他一生中所有最要紧的事，其实都是偶然决定的。他在法国所受的教育，纯属一桩意外。他的婚姻生活之所以幸福，在很大程度上也要归功于一件与他夫妻二人全不相干的情由。他们当年都是品行很好的年轻人，又深深地相爱，可要是莉莲没有从父亲那儿继承下一笔小小的进项——一年不过一千六百来块，那情形就会截然不同。他经历过几段两任佣人之间令人难忘的空档，由此深知莉莲做不到像他某些同事的太太那样精打细算、寒酸度日、亲手操持家务。处在那样的境地里，她就会变成另一个人，一个尖酸刻薄的人。

===Original===
Tom Outland had been a stroke of chance he couldn’t possibly have imagined; his strange coming, his strange story, his devotion, his early death and posthumous fame—it was all fantastic. Fantastic, too, that this tramp boy should amass a fortune for someone whose name he had never heard, for “an extravagant and wheeling stranger.” The Professor often thought of that curiously bitter burst from the baritone in Brahms’ *Requiem*, attending the words, “*He heapeth up riches and cannot tell who shall scatter them!*” The vehemence of this passage had seemed to him uncalled for until he read it by the light of the history of his own family.

===Chinese===
汤姆·奥特兰的出现，是一道他做梦也想不到的机缘；他奇特的到来，他奇特的故事，他的赤诚，他的英年早逝和身后的声名——这一切都离奇得很。同样离奇的还有：这个流浪少年竟会为一个他从未听过的姓氏、为「一个奢靡而油滑的陌生人」积下一笔家财。教授常常想起勃拉姆斯《德意志安魂曲》里男中音那段出奇愤激的乐句，唱的正是那些字眼：「他堆积财富，却不知将由谁来驱散！」从前他总觉得这一段的凌厉来得没来由，直到他借着自己一家人的遭际去读它，才明白过来。

===Original===
St. Peter thought he had fared well with fate. He wouldn’t choose to live his life over—he might not have such good luck again. He had had two romances: one of the heart, which had filled his life for many years, and a second of the mind—of the imagination. Just when the morning brightness of the world was wearing off for him, along came Outland and brought him a kind of second youth.

===Chinese===
圣彼得觉得自己在命运手里算是得了便宜。他不愿把这一生再重过一遍——未必还能再交这样好的运。他有过两段罗曼史：一段属于心，许多年间充盈了他的整个生活；第二段属于心智——属于想象。正当这世间的晨光朝晖在他身上渐渐褪色之际，奥特兰来了，给他带来了某种意义上的第二次青春。

===Original===
Through Outland’s studies, long after they had ceased to be pupil and master, he had been able to experience afresh things that had grown dull with use. The boy’s mind had the superabundance of heat which is always present where there is rich germination. To share his thoughts was to see old perspectives transformed by new effects of light.

===Chinese===
在奥特兰做学问的过程中，早在他俩已不再是师生之后许久，他仍能借以重新体味那些因日久用熟而变得寡淡的事物。这少年的心智里有一种丰沛过盛的热力，凡是生机丰茂之地，总有这种热力氤氲其间。与他共享所思所想，便如同看见旧的景致被新的光效所点化。

===Original===
If the last four volumes of *The Spanish Adventurers* were more simple and inevitable than those that went before, it was largely because of Outland. When St. Peter first began his work, he realized that his great drawback was the lack of early association, the fact that he had not spent his youth in the great dazzling Southwest country which was the scene of his explorers’ adventures. By the time he had got as far as the third volume, into his house walked a boy who had grown up there, a boy with imagination, with the training and insight resulting from a very curious experience; who had in his pocket the secrets which old trails and stones and watercourses tell only to adolescence.

===Chinese===
《北美的西班牙冒险家》最后四卷之所以比前头几卷更为单纯、更带一种水到渠成的味道，在很大程度上要归功于奥特兰。圣彼得刚开始动笔这部书时，就明白自己最大的短板是缺少早年的耳濡目染——他没能把青春岁月消磨在那片叫人眼花缭乱的西南部土地上，而那里正是他所写的那些探险家历险的舞台。等他写到第三卷，一个少年走进了他的家门，一个在那片土地上长大的少年，一个富有想象力、凭着一段极不寻常的经历磨砺出训练与眼力的少年；他衣袋里揣着的，是那些古道、石头与水道只肯对青春期吐露的秘密。

===Original===
Two years after Tom’s graduation they took the copy of Fray Garces’ manuscript that the Professor had made from the original in Spain, and went down into the Southwest together. By autumn they had been over every mile of his trail on horseback. Tom could take a sentence from Garces’ diary and find the exact spot at which the missionary crossed the Rio Colorado on a certain Sunday in 1775. Given one pueblo, he could always find the route by which the priest had reached the next.

===Chinese===
汤姆毕业两年之后，他们带着教授从西班牙的原稿抄录回来的加尔塞斯神父那份手稿的副本，一同南下深入西南部。到了秋天，他们已经骑着马把那位传教士走过的每一英里路都踏了一遍。汤姆能从加尔塞斯的日记里拈出一句话，就找到一七七五年某个礼拜天那位传教士渡过科罗拉多河的确切地点。只要给他一个普韦布洛村落，他总能找出那位神父是从哪条路走到下一个村落的。

===Original===
It was on that trip that they went to Tom’s Blue Mesa, climbed the ladder of spliced pine-trees to the Cliff City, and up to the Eagle’s Nest. There they took Tom’s diary from the stone cupboard where he had sealed it up years ago, before he set out for Washington on his fruitless errand.

===Chinese===
正是在那次旅行中，他们去了汤姆的蓝色台地，爬上那架用松树拼接成的梯子登上崖城，又登上鹰巢。在那儿，他们从汤姆多年前动身去华盛顿办那趟徒劳的差事之前封存起来的石橱里，取出了他的日记。

===Original===
The next summer Tom went with the Professor to Old Mexico. They had planned a third summer together, in Paris, but it never came off. Outland was delayed by the formalities of securing his patent, and then came August, 1914. Father Duchene, the missionary priest who had been Tom’s teacher, stopped in Hamilton on his way back to Belgium, hurrying home to serve in any capacity he might. The rugged old man stayed in Hamilton only four days, but in that time Outland made up his mind, had a will drawn, packed, and said goodbye. He sailed with Father Duchene on the *Rochambeau*.

===Chinese===
第二年夏天，汤姆随教授去了墨西哥。他们原计划第三个夏天一起去巴黎，可这事终未实现。奥特兰为办妥专利的种种手续耽搁下来，接着便到了一九一四年八月。曾经做过汤姆老师的传教士迪谢纳神父，在赶回比利时的途中于汉密尔顿停留，他急着回国去尽自己所能效力。这位硬朗的老人在汉密尔顿只待了四天，可就在这几天里，奥特兰拿定了主意，立好遗嘱，收拾行装，一一告别。他随迪谢纳神父一道登上了「罗尚博号」。

===Original===
To this day St. Peter regretted that he had never got that vacation in Paris with Tom Outland. He had wanted to revisit certain spots with him: to go with him some autumn morning to the Luxembourg Gardens, when the yellow horse-chestnuts were bright and bitter after rain; to stand with him before the monument to Delacroix and watch the sun gleam on the bronze figures—Time, bearing away the youth who was struggling to snatch his palm—or was it to lay a palm? Not that it mattered. It might have mattered to Tom, had not chance, in one great catastrophe, swept away all youth and all palms, and almost Time itself.

===Chinese===
直到今天，圣彼得仍在为没能和汤姆·奥特兰同去巴黎度那次假而惋惜。他曾想和他重访几处旧地：想和他一道，在某个秋日的早晨去卢森堡公园，那时雨后的黄色七叶树明艳而苦涩；想和他一同站在德拉克洛瓦的纪念碑前，看阳光在那组铜像上闪耀——时光之神，正把那个挣扎着想攫取棕榈枝的少年挟走——又或者，他原是要献上一枝棕榈？这也无妨。可对于汤姆，这就未必无妨了——只因一场浩劫般的偶然，把所有的青春、所有的棕榈枝，几乎连时光本身，都一并席卷而去了。

===Original===
And suppose Tom had been more prudent, and had not gone away with his old teacher? St. Peter sometimes wondered what would have happened to him, once the trap of worldly success had been sprung on him. He couldn’t see Tom building “Outland,” or becoming a public-spirited citizen of Hamilton. What change would have come in his blue eye, in his fine long hand with the back-springing thumb, which had never handled things that were not the symbols of ideas? A hand like that, had he lived, must have been put to other uses. His fellow scientists, his wife, the town and State, would have required many duties of it. It would have had to write thousands of useless letters, frame thousands of false excuses. It would have had to “manage” a great deal of money, to be the instrument of a woman who would grow always more exacting. He had escaped all that. He had made something new in the world—and the rewards, the meaningless conventional gestures, he had left to others.

===Chinese===
再假如汤姆当年更审慎些，没有随他那位老教师远走呢？圣彼得有时也会想，一旦世俗功业那张罗网朝他当头罩下，他会变成什么样子。他想象不出汤姆会去营建「奥特兰」那座宅子，或是成为汉密尔顿城里一位热心公益的市民。他那只蓝眼睛，那只拇指往后翻翘的修长好看的手——那只手从未碰过任何不属于理念象征的物件——又会起怎样的变化？那样的一只手，要是他活着，势必得派作别的用场。他的科学界同人、他的妻子、这座城市和这个州，都会对它提出许许多多的差事。它将不得不写下成千上万封无用的信件，编造成千上万个虚伪的托词。它将不得不「打理」一大笔钱财，沦为一个欲求日甚的女子的工具。这一切他都逃脱了。他在这个世界上造出了全新的东西——至于那些酬报，那些毫无意义的俗套虚文，他都留给了别人。
