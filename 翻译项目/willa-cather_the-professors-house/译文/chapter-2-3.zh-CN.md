### III / III

===Original===
We brought the bunch of cattle down to the winter range in the latter part of November. Early in December the foreman came along with generous provisions for Christmas. This time he brought with him a supercargo, a pitiful wreck of an old man he had picked up at Tarpin, the railroad town thirty miles northeast of us, where the Sitwells bought their supplies. This old man was a castaway Englishman, Henry Atkins by name. He had been a valet, and a hospital orderly, and a cook, and for many years was a table steward on the Anchor Line. Lately he had been cooking for a sheep outfit that were grazing in the cattle country, where they weren’t wanted. They had done something shady and had to get out in a hurry. They dropped old Henry at Tarpin, where he soon drank up all his wages. When Rapp picked him up there, he was living on handouts.
“I’ve told him we can’t pay him anything,” Rapp explained. “But if he wants to stay here and cook for you boys till I make my next trip, he’ll have plenty to eat and a roof over him. He was sleeping in the livery stable in Tarpin. He says he’s a good cook, and I thought he might liven things up for you at Christmas time. He won’t bother you, he’s not got any of the mean ways of a bum﻿—I know a bum when I see one. Next time I come down I’ll bring him some old clothes from the ranch, and you can hire him if you want to. All his baggage is that newspaper bundle, and there’s nothing in it but shoes﻿—a pair of patent leathers and a pair of sneakers. The important thing is, never, on any account, go off skylarking, you two, and leave him with the cattle. Not for an hour, mind you. He ain’t strong enough, and he’s got no head.”

===Chinese===
十一月下旬，我们把那一群牛赶到了冬季牧场。十二月初，工头来了，带着丰盛的圣诞用品。这一回他带来了一个搭脚的——一个叫人可怜的老废物，是他在塔平（Tarpin）捡到的。塔平是我们东北方三十英里开外的铁路小镇，西特韦尔家（Sitwells）就在那儿办货。这老头是个流落异乡的英国人，名叫亨利·阿特金斯（Henry Atkins）。他当过贴身男仆，当过医院的护理员，当过厨子，好多年都在锚行（Anchor Line）的轮船上做餐厅侍者。近来他在一帮放羊的人那儿做饭——那帮人在养牛的地界放羊，本就不受人待见，又干了点什么见不得人的勾当，匆匆忙忙只好滚蛋。他们把老亨利扔在了塔平，没过多久他就把工钱全喝光了。拉普（Rapp）在那儿把他领走的时候，他正靠人家的施舍过活。
「我跟他讲明了，我们一个子儿也付不起，」拉普解释说，「可要是他愿意留在这儿替你们俩做饭，等我下一趟来，那他吃的管够，还有个遮风挡雨的屋顶。先前他在塔平的马厩里过夜。他说自己是个好厨子，我想他兴许能让你们圣诞节热闹点儿。他不会给你们添乱的，他身上一点也没有那种二流子的坏毛病——二流子我一眼就认得出。下回我下来，从牧场给他带几件旧衣裳来，你们要愿意就雇下他。他全部的家当就是那个报纸打的包袱，里头除了鞋什么也没有——一双漆皮鞋，一双胶底帆布鞋。最要紧的一桩：你们俩无论如何不许撇下牛群出去撒野。一刻钟也不行，记着。他身子骨不顶事，又没个主见。」

===Original===
Life was a holiday for Blake and me after we got old Henry. He was a wonderful cook and a good housekeeper. He kept that cabin shining like a playhouse; used to dress it all out with piñon boughs, and trimmed the kitchen shelves with newspapers cut in fancy patterns. He had learned to make up cots when he was a hospital orderly, and he made our bunks feel like a Harvey House bed. To this day that’s the best I can say for any bed. And he was such a polite, mannerly old boy; simple and kind as a child. I used to wonder how anybody so innocent and defenceless had managed to get along at all, to keep alive for nearly seventy years in as hard a world as this. Anybody could take advantage of him. He held no grudge against any of the people who had misused him. He loved to tell about the celebrated people he’d been steward to, and the liberal tips they had given him. There with us, where he couldn’t get at whisky, he was a model of good behaviour. “Drink is me weakness, you might say,” he occasionally remarked apologetically. He shaved every morning and was as clean as a pin. We got to be downright fond of him, and the three of us made a happy family.

===Chinese===
得了老亨利之后，布雷克和我的日子简直像过节。他是个顶呱呱的厨子，又是个把家好手。他把那间小木屋拾掇得亮堂堂的，像座玩具屋似的；还常用矮松（piñon）的枝桠给它装点一番，又拿剪出花样的旧报纸给厨房的搁板镶边。他当医院护理员那会儿学过铺床，经他一拾掇，我们的铺位躺上去竟像哈维旅馆（Harvey House）的床那样舒服。时至今日，我要夸一张床，至多也就夸到这个地步。而且他是那么一个客气、有礼数的老伙计，心思单纯，心肠又软，像个孩子。我常纳闷，这么一个毫无心机、全无防备的人，究竟是怎么在这般狠硬的世道里挨过了将近七十个年头、活了下来。谁都能欺负他。可那些亏待过他的人，他一个也不记仇。他顶爱讲他从前侍候过的那些名流，讲他们赏的小费多么大方。跟我们在一块儿，够不着威士忌，他便是个规规矩矩的模范。「酒嘛，可以说是我的一个弱点，」他不时这样抱歉似的说一句。他每天早晨刮脸，浑身上下干净得挑不出一点碴。我们渐渐地真把他当自家人疼起来，我们仨成了一户快快活活的人家。

===Original===
Ever since we’d brought our herd down to the winter camp, the wild cattle on the mesa were more in evidence. They came down to the river to drink oftener, and loitered about, grazing in that low canyon so much that we began to call it Cow Canyon. They were fine-looking beasts, too. One could see they had good pasture up there. Henry had a theory that we ought to be able to entice them over to our side with salt. He wanted to kill one for beefsteaks. Soon after he joined us we lost two cows. Without warning they bolted into the mesa, as the foreman had said. After that we watched the herd closer; but a few days before Christmas, when Blake was off hunting and I was on duty, four fine young steers sneaked down to the water’s edge through the brush, and before I knew it they were swimming the river﻿—seemed to do it with no trouble at all. They frisked out on the other side, ambled up the canyon, and disappeared. I was furious to have them steal a march on me, and I swore to myself I’d follow them over and drive them back.

The next morning we took the herd a few miles east, to keep them out of mischief. I made some excuse to Blake, cut back to the cabin, and asked Henry to put me up a lunch. I told him my plan, but warned him not to bear tales. If I wasn’t home when Blake came in at night, then he could tell him where I’d gone.

===Chinese===
自从我们把牛群赶到冬营，台地（mesa）上的那群野牛便越发显眼了。它们更勤地下到河边来饮水，还在那条浅峡谷里四处溜达吃草，赖在那儿不肯走，久而久之我们便管那地方叫牛峡（Cow Canyon）。那一群也都是些体面的好牲口。一看便知，那上头有的是好草。亨利有一套说法：我们该能用盐把它们引到我们这边来。他想杀一头，吃牛排。他来了没多久，我们就丢了两头母牛。它们连个招呼也不打，一头扎进了台地——正像工头说的那样。打那以后我们把牛群盯得更紧了；可圣诞节前几天，布雷克出去打猎，正轮着我当班，四头上好的小公牛顺着灌木丛偷偷摸摸地蹭到水边，我还没回过神来，它们已经游过了河——简直毫不费力。它们在对岸蹦跶着上了岸，碎步小跑着进了峡谷，一晃眼不见了。叫它们这么抢了先，我气得够呛，暗暗发誓非要跟过去、把它们赶回来不可。

第二天早上，我们把牛群往东赶了几英里，免得它们再惹祸。我编了个由头支开布雷克，折回木屋，请亨利给我包一顿午饭。我把我的打算告诉了他，可叮嘱他别多嘴。要是我到夜里布雷克回来时还没到家，那时他再把我的去处告诉他。

===Original===
Henry went down to the river with me to watch me across. It had grown colder since morning, and looked like snow. The old man was afraid of a storm; said I might get snowed in. But I’d got my nerve up, and I didn’t want to put off making a try at it. I strapped my blanket and my lunch on my shoulders, hung my boots around my neck to keep them dry, stuffed my socks inside my hat, and we waded in. My horse took the water without any fuss, though he shivered a good deal. He stepped out very carefully, and when it got too deep for him, he swam without panic. We were carried downstream a little by the current, but I didn’t have to slide off his back. He found bottom after a while, and we easily made a landing. I waved goodbye to Henry on the other side and started up the canyon, running beside my horse to get warm.

===Chinese===
亨利陪我下到河边，看着我过河。自早上起天又冷了些，像是要下雪的模样。老头怕碰上风暴，说我别给雪困在了里头。可我那股子劲已经上来了，不想再拖。我把毯子和午饭绑在肩上，把靴子挂在脖子上好不让它沾水，袜子塞进帽子里，便下了水。我的马下水时一点不带含糊，虽说它哆嗦得厉害。它一脚一脚踩得很仔细，等水没过了它，它便不慌不忙地游了起来。我们被水流往下游冲了一截，可我并不用从它背上滑下去。过了一阵它踩到了底，我们顺顺当当地上了岸。我在对岸朝亨利挥了挥手道别，便顺着峡谷往上跑起来，在马旁边一路小跑着好暖暖身子。

===Original===
The canyon was wide at the water’s edge, and though it corkscrewed back into the mesa by abrupt turns, it preserved this open, roomy character. It was, indeed, a very deep valley with gently sloping sides, rugged and rocky, but well grassed. There was a clear trail. Horses have no sense about making a trail, but you can trust cattle to find the easiest possible path and to take the lowest grades. The bluish rock and the suntanned grass, under the unusual purple-grey of the sky, gave the whole valley a very soft colour, lavender and pale gold, so that the occasional cedars growing beside the boulders looked black that morning. It may have been the hint of snow in the air, but it seemed to me that I had never breathed in anything that tasted so pure as the air in that valley. It made my mouth and nostrils smart like charged water, seemed to go to my head a little and produce a kind of exaltation. I kept telling myself that it was very different from the air on the other side of the river, though that was pure and uncontaminated enough.

===Chinese===
峡谷在水边一带很宽阔，虽说它拐着急弯、拧着麻花似的往台地深处钻，却始终是这般开阔宽敞的模样。其实它是一条很深的山谷，两侧山坡缓缓地斜上去，嶙峋多石，却长满了草。有一条清楚的小径。马是不懂得挑路的，可牛你尽管信得过——它们总能找着最省脚力的道，专走坡度最缓的地方。那蓝灰色的岩石和被日头晒透的枯草，映在那一天少见的紫灰色天幕底下，使整条山谷染上了一种极柔和的颜色，淡紫，夹着浅金，于是那天早上偶尔长在巨石旁边的几株雪松，看上去便黑沉沉的。也许是因为空气里隐隐有要下雪的意思，我只觉得，我从前呼吸过的任何东西，都没有这条山谷里的空气这般纯净。它呛得我口鼻发辣，像苏打水冒泡似的，又似乎微微冲上了我的脑门，叫我生出一种昂扬之感。我一路走一路对自己说，这和河对岸的空气是两回事——虽说那边也够干净、够没受玷污的了。

===Original===
When I had gone up this canyon for a mile or so, I came upon another, opening out to the north﻿—a box canyon, very different in character. No gentle slope there. The walls were perpendicular, where they weren’t actually overhanging, and they were anywhere from eight hundred to a thousand feet high, as we afterward found by measurement. The floor of it was a mass of huge boulders, great pieces of rock that had fallen from above ages back, and had been worn round and smooth as pebbles by the long action of water. Many of them were as big as haystacks, yet they lay piled on one another like a load of gravel. There was no footing for my horse among those smooth stones, so I hobbled him and went on alone a little way, just to see what it was like. My eyes were steadily on the ground﻿—a slip of the foot there might cripple one.

===Chinese===
我顺着这条峡谷走了大约一英里光景，便撞见了另一条峡谷，朝着北面敞开——那是一条匣子峡谷（box canyon），性情与前一条全然不同。这里再没有什么缓坡。崖壁都是直上直下的，有的地方甚至向外倒悬，据我们日后测量，高度从八百到一千英尺不等。谷底是一片巨石的堆垒，都是不知多少年代以前从上方崩落的岩块，被流水长年累月地磨得像卵石一样圆滑。其中许多大得像一座座草垛，却互相叠压着，仿佛一堆碎石。这些光溜溜的石头上我的马没法落脚，我便把它拴住，独自再往前走了一小段，只想看看前头是什么光景。我的两眼紧紧盯着脚底下——在那地方脚底一滑，兴许就能叫人残废。

===Original===
It was such rough scrambling that I was soon in a warm sweat under my damp clothes. In stopping to take breath, I happened to glance up at the canyon wall. I wish I could tell you what I saw there, just as I saw it, on that first morning, through a veil of lightly falling snow. Far up above me, a thousand feet or so, set in a great cavern in the face of the cliff, I saw a little city of stone, asleep. It was as still as sculpture﻿—and something like that. It all hung together, seemed to have a kind of composition: pale little houses of stone nestling close to one another, perched on top of each other, with flat roofs, narrow windows, straight walls, and in the middle of the group, a round tower.

===Chinese===
这一路攀爬乱石，不一会儿我贴身的湿衣裳底下就腾起了一身热汗。我停下来喘口气，偶然一抬头，朝崖壁望去。但愿我能把那一刻我在那上头看见的，原原本本说给你听——就在那个头一个早晨，透过一层纷纷扬扬、轻轻落下的雪帘。在我头顶很高很高的地方，约莫一千英尺的光景，嵌在崖壁一张巨大的石洞里，我看见了一座小小的石头城，睡在那里。它静得像一尊雕塑——而且也确有几分像雕塑。它浑然一体，仿佛自有它的章法：一座座灰白的小石屋彼此紧紧依偎，层层叠叠地摞在一起，平的屋顶，窄窄的窗子，笔直的墙，而在这群屋子的正中，是一座圆塔。

===Original===
It was beautifully proportioned, that tower, swelling out to a larger girth a little above the base, then growing slender again. There was something symmetrical and powerful about the swell of the masonry. The tower was the fine thing that held all the jumble of houses together and made them mean something. It was red in colour, even on that grey day. In sunlight it was the colour of winter oak-leaves. A fringe of cedars grew along the edge of the cavern, like a garden. They were the only living things. Such silence and stillness and repose﻿—immortal repose. That village sat looking down into the canyon with the calmness of eternity. The falling snowflakes, sprinkling the piñons, gave it a special kind of solemnity. I can’t describe it. It was more like sculpture than anything else. I knew at once that I had come upon the city of some extinct civilization, hidden away in this inaccessible mesa for centuries, preserved in the dry air and almost perpetual sunlight like a fly in amber, guarded by the cliffs and the river and the desert.

===Chinese===
那座塔的比例美极了，在离基座不远的地方微微鼓出一圈更粗的腰身，再往上又重新收细。那砌石的弧线里有一种匀称而有力的东西。正是这座塔，是那根定海的神针，把那一堆杂乱的屋宇拢在一起，让它们有了意义。它是红的，哪怕在那样一个灰蒙蒙的日子也是红的。在阳光底下，它是冬日橡树叶的颜色。一排雪松沿着石洞的边缘长着，像一座花园。它们是那里头唯一活着的生灵。那样的静、那样的寂、那样的安息——一种不朽的安息。那座村落就这么俯瞰着峡谷，带着一种永恒的从容。纷纷落下的雪花，洒在矮松上，给它平添了一种特别的庄重。我说不出来。它比什么都更像一尊雕塑。我当下就明白，我撞见的是某一个已然绝灭的文明所留下的城，在这座人迹罕至的台地里藏了不知多少个世纪，在干燥的空气和几乎终年不歇的日照里保存下来，像琥珀里的一只蝇，由崖壁、河流和沙漠一同守着。

===Original===
As I stood looking up at it, I wondered whether I ought to tell even Blake about it; whether I ought not to go back across the river and keep that secret as the mesa had kept it. When I at last turned away, I saw still another canyon branching out of this one, and in its wall still another arch, with another group of buildings. The notion struck me like a rifle ball that this mesa had once been like a beehive; it was full of little cliff-hung villages, it had been the home of a powerful tribe, a particular civilization.

===Chinese===
我站在那里仰望它，心里却踌躇起来：这事我该不该就连布雷克也告诉他呢？我该不该回到河对岸去，把这个秘密守住，就像台地一直守着它那样？等我终于转身要走的时候，我又看见从这条峡谷里还分出另一条峡谷，而在它崖壁上，又是一个石洞，又有一群落落在一起的屋宇。一个念头像步枪子弹一样击中了我：这座台地从前一定像一座蜂房；它里头密密麻麻全是这种悬在崖壁上的小村落，它曾是一个强悍的部族、一个独特的文明的家园。

===Original===
That night when I got home Blake was on the riverbank waiting for me. I told him I’d rather not talk about my trip until after supper﻿—that I was beat out. I think he’d meant to upbraid me for sneaking off, but he didn’t. He seemed to realize from the first that this was a serious matter to me, and he accepted it in that way.

After supper, when we had lit our pipes, I told Blake and Henry as clearly as I could what it was like over there, and we talked it over. The town in the cliffs explained the irrigation ditches. Like all pueblo Indians, these people had had their farms away from their dwellings. For a stronghold they needed rock, and for farming, soft earth and a water main.

“And this proves,” said Roddy, “that there must have been a trail into the mesa at the north end, and that they carried their harvest over by the ford. If this Cow Canyon was the only entrance, they could never have farmed down here.” We agreed that he should go over on the first warm day, and try to find a trail up to the Cliff City, as we already called it.

那天夜里我到家时，布雷克正在河岸上等我。我跟他说，我这会儿宁愿先不谈这一趟——我累瘫了。我看他是本想责备我偷偷溜出去的，可他没有。他似乎一开始就明白，这事对我是顶要紧的，也就以那种态度认下了它。

吃过晚饭，我们点上了烟斗，我便尽力把那边是什么光景，原原本本讲给布雷克和亨利听，三个人一起商量起来。崖壁上那座城，正好替那些灌溉渠作了注解。和所有的普韦布洛印第安人（pueblo Indians）一样，这些人当年也是把田地开在离住处远远的地方。要筑一座堡垒，他们需要岩石；要种庄稼，则需要松软的泥土和一条水源。

===Original===
We talked and speculated until after midnight. It was Christmas eve, and Henry said it was but right we should do something out of the ordinary. But after we went to bed, tired as I was, I was unable to sleep. I got up and dressed and put on my overcoat and slipped outside to get sight of the mesa. The wind had come up and was blowing the squall clouds across the sky. The moon was almost full, hanging directly over the mesa, which had never looked so solemn and silent to me before. I wondered how many Christmases had come and gone since that round tower was built. I had been to Acoma and the Hopi villages, but I’d never seen a tower like that one. It seemed to me to mark a difference. I felt that only a strong and aspiring people would have built it, and a people with a feeling for design. That cluster of buildings, in its arch, with the dizzy drop into empty air from its doorways and the wall of cliff above, was as clear in my mind as a picture.
