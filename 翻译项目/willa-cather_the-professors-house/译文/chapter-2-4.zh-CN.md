### IV / IV

===Original===
Blake and I got over to the mesa together for the first time early in May. We carried with us all the food we could, and an ax and spade. It took us several days to find a trail leading from the bottom of the box canyon up to the Cliff City. There were gaps in it; it was broken by ledges too steep for a man to climb. Lying beside one of these, we found an old dried cedar trunk, with toe-notches cut in it. That was a plain suggestion. We felled some trees and threw them up over the gaps in the path. Toward the end of the week, when our provisions were getting low, we made the last lap in our climb, and stepped upon the ledge that was the floor of the Cliff City.
In front of the cluster of buildings, there was an open space, like a courtyard. Along the outer edge of this yard ran a low stone wall. In some places the wall had fallen away from the weather, but the buildings themselves sat so far back under the rim rock that the rain had never beat on them. In thunderstorms I’ve seen the water come down in sheets over the face of that cavern without a drop touching the village.

===Chinese===
五月初，布雷克和我头一回一起渡河上了台地。我们把能带的食物都带上了，还有一把斧头、一把铲子。我们花了好几天才找到一条从匣子峡谷（box canyon）谷底通往崖城（the Cliff City）的小路。那路断断续续，中间被几处太陡、人爬不上去的石台截断。在其中一道石台旁边，我们躺着一根早已干透的老雪松树干，上面凿着一级级踏脚的缺口。这是个明摆着的提示。我们砍倒了几棵树，把它们搭上路中那些缺口。快到那个礼拜的末尾、口粮将尽时，我们爬完了最后一段，踏上了那块石台——那就是崖城的地板。
在那片建筑群前面，有一块空地，像一个院落。院子外围沿边有一道低矮的石墙。有几处墙被风雨剥蚀坍了，可那些房子本身深深缩在檐岩（rim rock）底下，雨水从来打不到它们身上。打雷下雨的时候，我亲眼见过水像帘子一样顺着那洞窟的正面整面整面地往下泻，却没有一滴落进村子里。

===Original===
The courtyard was not choked by vegetation, for there was no soil. It was bare rock, with a few old, flat-topped cedars growing out of the cracks, and a little pale grass. But everything seemed open and clean, and the stones, I remember, were warm to the touch, smooth and pleasant to feel.

===Chinese===
院子里并没被草木塞满，因为没有泥土。那都是裸露的岩石，只有几棵老老的、平顶的雪松从裂缝里长出来，还有一点浅色的草。可一切都显得敞亮、洁净，那些石头我还记得，摸上去暖烘烘的，又光滑又好手感。

===Original===
The outer walls of the houses were intact, except where sometimes an outjutting corner had crumbled. They were made of dressed stones, plastered inside and out with ’dobe, and were tinted in light colours, pink and pale yellow and tan. Here and there a cedar log in the ceiling had given way and let the second-story chamber down into the first; except for that, there was little rubbish or disorder. As Blake remarked, wind and sun are good housekeepers.
This village had never been sacked by an enemy, certainly. Inside the little rooms water jars and bowls stood about unbroken, and yucca-fibre mats were on the floors.

===Chinese===
房屋的外墙大体完好，只是偶有一处凸出的墙角碎落了。墙是用琢好的石块砌成的，里外都抹上泥灰，还着了浅浅的颜色：粉红、淡黄、棕褐。有些地方天花板的雪松梁已经朽断，把二层的房间落进了下层；除此以外，几乎没有什么碎砖瓦砾、凌乱不堪。正如布雷克说的，风和太阳是好管家。
这座村庄显然从未被敌人劫掠过。一间间小屋里，水罐和钵碗还完好无损地摆着，地上铺着丝兰纤维编的垫子。

===Original===
We could give only a hurried look over the place, as our food was exhausted, and we had to get back over the river before dark. We went about softly, tried not to disturb anything﻿—even the silence. Besides the tower, there seemed to be about thirty little separate dwellings. Behind the cluster of houses was a kind of back courtyard, running from end to end of the cavern; a long, low, twilit space that got gradually lower toward the back until the rim rock met the floor of the cavern, exactly like the sloping roof of an attic. There was perpetual twilight back there, cool, shadowy, very grateful after the blazing sun in the front courtyard. When we entered it we heard a soft trickling sound, and we came upon a spring that welled out of the rock into a stone basin and then ran off through a cobble-lined gutter and dripped down the cliffs. I’ve never anywhere tasted water like it; as cold as ice, and so pure. Long afterward Father Duchene came out to spend a week with us on the mesa; he always carried a small drinking-glass with him, and he used to fill it at the spring and take it out into the sunlight. The water looked like liquid crystal, absolutely colourless, without the slight brownish or greenish tint that water nearly always has. It threw off the sunlight like a diamond.

===Chinese===
我们只能匆匆浏览一遍这个地方，因为食物已经吃光，天黑前还得赶过河回去。我们轻手轻脚地走动，尽量不去惊动任何东西——连那片寂静也不去打扰。除了那座塔楼，似乎大约还有三十来座各自独立的小屋。房屋群的后头，是一道后院，从洞窟这一头通到那一头；一个长长的、低矮的、暮色般的空间，越往后越低，直到檐岩与洞窟的地面相接，活像阁楼那倾斜的屋顶。那里是永恒的黄昏，凉爽，幽暗，在前院毒太阳下晒过之后，格外让人觉得舒服。我们走进去时，听见一阵轻柔的潺潺声，接着便发现一眼泉水，从岩石里涌出来，注进一个石盆，再沿着一条铺了鹅卵石的水沟流走，从崖壁上滴落下去。我后来在哪里也再没喝过那样的水；冷得像冰，又那样纯净。很久以后，迪谢纳神父（Father Duchene）出来在台地上和我们住了一个星期；他随身总带着一只小小的玻璃酒杯，常在那泉水边把杯子斟满，再端到阳光底下看。那水像流动的水晶，完全无色，不带水几乎总会有的那种微微的褐色或绿色。它折射阳光，像钻石一样。

===Original===
Beside this spring stood some of the most beautifully shaped water jars we ever found﻿—I gave Mrs. St. Peter one of them﻿—standing there just as if they’d been left yesterday. In the back court we found a great many things besides jars and bowls: a row of grinding-stones, and several clay ovens, very much like those the Mexicans use today. There were charred bones and charcoal, and the roof was thick with soot all the way along. It was evidently a kind of common kitchen, where they roasted and baked and probably gossiped. There were corncobs everywhere, and ears of corn with the kernels still on them﻿—little, like popcorn. We found dried beans, too, and strings of pumpkin seeds, and plum seeds, and a cupboard full of little implements made of turkey bones.

===Chinese===
这眼泉水旁边，立着我们所见过的几只形状最美的水罐——我后来把其中一只送给了圣彼得太太（Mrs. St. Peter）——它们就那样立在那儿，好像昨天才被人搁下似的。在后院里，除了罐和碗，我们还找出许许多多别的东西：一排磨石，还有几座泥灶，跟今天墨西哥人用的那种非常相像。有烧焦的骨头和木炭，屋顶沿着一路都结着厚厚的烟灰。这里显然是一种公共厨房，他们在那儿烤、在那儿烘，多半也在那儿说东道西。到处是玉米芯，还有带着粒儿的玉米穗——小小的，像爆米花。我们还找到了干豆子，一串串的南瓜籽、李子核，还有一个碗柜，满满的全是用火鸡骨头做成的小器具。

===Original===
Late that afternoon Roddy and I crossed the river and got back to our cabin to rest for a few days.

===Chinese===
那天傍晚，罗迪和我渡过河，回到我们的小屋，歇息了几天。

===Original===
The second time we went over, we found a long winding trail leading from the Cliff City up to the top of the mesa﻿—a narrow path worn deep into the stone ledges that overhung the village, then running back into the wood of stunted piñons on the summit. Following this to the north end of the mesa, we found what was left of an old road down to the plain. But making this road passable was a matter of weeks, and we had to get workmen and tools from Tarpin. It was a narrow footpath, barely wide enough for a surefooted mule, and it wound down through Black Canyon, dropping in loops along the face of terrifying cliffs. About a hundred feet above the river, it ended﻿—broke right off into the air. A wall of rock had fallen away there, probably from a landslide. That last piece of road cost us three weeks’ hard work, and most of our winter’s wages. We kept the workmen on long enough to build us a tight log cabin on the mesa top, a little way back from the ledge that hung over the Cliff City.

===Chinese===
我们第二次过去时，发现一条长长的、蜿蜒的小路，从崖城通上台地的顶端——一条窄窄的、深深磨进悬在村庄上方那些石台里的小径，然后拐进山顶那片矮矮的矮松（piñon）林里。沿这条路走到台地北端，我们找到了一段通往平原的旧路的残迹。但要把这段路修到能通行，得花上几个礼拜的工夫，我们只好从塔平（Tarpin）请来工人、借来工具。那是一条窄窄的步道，勉强只够一头走得稳当的骡子过去，它蜿蜒穿过黑峡谷（Black Canyon），沿着骇人的峭壁一圈一圈地盘旋而下。在离河面大约一百英尺的上空，它到了头——一下子断在了半空里。那里有一堵岩壁垮了下去，多半是哪次山崩塌的。这最后一截断路花了我们三个礼拜的苦工，还搭上了我们大半个冬天的工钱。我们把工人留着，让他们在台地顶上、悬着崖城的那道石台往后一点的地方，给我们造了一间严严实实的圆木小屋。

===Original===
While we were engaged in road-building, we made a shortcut from our cabin down to the Cliff City and Cow Canyon. Just over the Cliff City, there was a crack in the ledge, a sort of manhole, and in this we hung a ladder of pine-trunks spliced together with light chains, leaving the branch forks for footholds. By climbing down this ladder we saved about two miles of winding trail, and dropped almost directly into Cow Canyon, where we meant always to leave one of the horses grazing. Taking this route, we could at any time make a quick exit from the mesa﻿—we were used to swimming the river now, and in summer our wet clothes dried very quickly.

===Chinese===
修路那阵子，我们从屋里抄了一条近路，直通崖城和牛峡（Cow Canyon）。就在崖城正上方，石台上有一道裂缝，像一口竖井，我们在里头挂了一架用松树干以细铁链拼成的梯子，留下树杈的桠权当踏脚。从这架梯子爬下去，省下大约两英里弯弯绕绕的山路，几乎笔直地落进牛峡，我们打算总在那里留一匹马吃草。走这条路，我们随时都能从台地上迅速撤离——如今过河我们早已游惯了，夏天里湿衣服干得也快。

===Original===
Bill Hook, the liveryman at Tarpin, who’d sheltered old Henry when he was down and out, proved a good friend to us. He got our workmen back and forth for us, brought our supplies up on to the mesa on his pack-mules, and when one of us had to stay in town overnight he let us sleep in his hay barn to save a hotel bill. He knew our expenses were heavy, and did everything for us at a bottom price.

===Chinese===
塔平那家车马行的老板比尔·胡克（Bill Hook）——老亨利穷途落魄的时候，他曾收留过他——对我们是个够义气的朋友。他帮我们来回接送工人，用自己的驮骡把我们的给养送上台地，当我们哪一个得在镇上过夜时，他就让我们睡在他那干草棚里，省下一笔旅馆钱。他知道我们开销大，什么事都按最低的价给我们办。

===Original===
By the first of July our money was nearly gone, but we had our road made, and our cabin built on top of the mesa. We brought old Henry up by the new horse-trail and began housekeeping. We were now ready for what we called excavating. We built wide shelves all around our sleeping-room, and there we put the smaller articles we found in the Cliff City. We numbered each specimen, and in my daybook I wrote down just where and in what condition we had found it, and what we thought it had been used for. I’d got a merchant’s ledger in Tarpin, and every night after supper, while Roddy read the newspapers, I sat down at the kitchen table and wrote up an account of the day’s work.

===Chinese===
到七月初，我们的钱差不多花光了，可路修通了，台地顶上的小屋也盖好了。我们牵着老亨利从新开的马道上了山，开始起伙过日子。这下我们可以干我们所谓的「发掘」了。我们在卧房四面搭起宽宽的搁板，把从崖城里找到的小件物品都摆在上面。我们给每一件标本编了号，在我的日记本里记下它确切是在哪儿发现的、当时什么样子、我们觉得它是派什么用处的。我在塔平弄到了一本商人用的那种分类账，每天吃过晚饭，趁罗迪读报纸，我就坐到厨房桌前，把一天的工作写下账来。

===Original===
Henry, besides doing the housekeeping, was very eager to help us in the “rew-ins,” as he called them. He was more patient than we, and would dig with his fingers half a day to get a pot out of a rubbish pile without breaking it. After all, the old man had a wider knowledge of the world than either of us, and it often came in handy. When we were working in a pale pink house, with two stories, and a sort of balcony before the upper windows, we came on a closet in the wall of the upstairs room; in this were a number of curious things, among them a deerskin bag full of little tools. Henry said at once they were surgical instruments; a stone lancet, a bunch of fine bone needles, wooden forceps, and a catheter.

===Chinese===
亨利除了操持家务，还很热心地要帮我们干他所谓的「撸因斯」——他把 ruins（废墟）说成那样。他比我们更有耐性，为了从一堆垃圾里挖出一只罐子而不把它弄破，他会用手指头挖上半天。毕竟这老头比我们俩谁见过的世面都多，这一点常常很派得上用场。我们在一座浅粉色的房子里干活，那房子有两层，上层窗子前面有一种小阳台；我们在楼上房间的墙壁里发现一个壁橱，里面有一些稀奇古怪的东西，其中有一只装满小工具的鹿皮袋。亨利一口咬定那是外科器具：一把石制的柳叶刀、一束细骨针、木制的镊子，还有一根导液管。

===Original===
One thing we knew about these people; they hadn’t built their town in a hurry. Everything proved their patience and deliberation. The cedar joists had been felled with stone axes and rubbed smooth with sand. The little poles that lay across them and held up the clay floor of the chamber above, were smoothly polished. The door lintels were carefully fitted (the doors were stone slabs held in place by wooden bars fitted into hasps). The clay dressing that covered the stone walls was tinted, and some of the chambers were frescoed in geometrical patterns, one colour laid on another. In one room was a painted border, little tents, like Indian teepees, in brilliant red.

===Chinese===
关于这些人，有一点我们是清楚的：他们这座城不是草草盖起来的。一切都证明了他们的耐心和从容。那些雪松托梁是用石斧砍倒、再用沙子磨光的。横搭在梁上、撑起上层房间泥地的小木杆，都打磨得光光滑滑。门楣装配得十分仔细（门是石板，用木栓插进搭扣里固定住）。覆盖石墙的泥灰层着了色，有些房间里还画着几何图案的湿壁画，一种颜色叠在另一种颜色上。有一间房里有一道绘出的边饰，一顶顶小帐篷，像印第安人的圆锥帐篷，鲜红鲜红的。

===Original===
But the really splendid thing about our city, the thing that made it delightful to work there, and must have made it delightful to live there, was the setting. The town hung like a bird’s nest in the cliff, looking off into the box canyon below, and beyond into the wide valley we called Cow Canyon, facing an ocean of clear air. A people who had the hardihood to build there, and who lived day after day looking down upon such grandeur, who came and went by those hazardous trails, must have been, as we often told each other, a fine people. But what had become of them? What catastrophe had overwhelmed them?

===Chinese===
可我们这座城真正了不起的、叫人在这里干活觉得快活、当年住在这里想必也觉得快活的，是它的位置。这座城像一只鸟窝似的悬在崖壁上，俯瞰着下面的匣子峡谷，再往外是我们称作牛峡的开阔山谷，面对着一片清澈空气的海洋。一个有胆量在这儿建城的民族，日复一日地俯瞰着这样的壮阔景象、在那些险峻的山路上来来去去的民族——我们常常彼此说——一定是个了不起的民族。可他们去了哪儿呢？是什么灾祸把他们吞没了？

===Original===
They hadn’t moved away, for they had taken none of their belongings, not even their clothes. Oh, yes, we found clothes; yucca moccasins, and what seemed like cotton cloth, woven in black and white. Never any wool, but sheepskins tanned with the fleece on them. They may have been mountain sheep; the mesa was full of them. We talked of shooting one for meat, but we never did. When a mountain sheep comes out on a ledge hundreds of feet above you, with his trumpet horns, there’s something noble about him﻿—he looks like a priest. We didn’t want to shoot at them and make them shy. We liked to see them. We shot a wild cow when we wanted fresh meat.

===Chinese===
他们并不是搬走了，因为他们什么家当都没带走，连衣服都没带走。哦，是的，我们发现了衣服；丝兰纤维编的莫卡辛鞋，还有像是棉布的织物，织成黑白的花纹。从来没有羊毛，只有带着羊毛一起鞣制过的羊皮。那可能是岩羊；台地上到处都是。我们说过要打一只来吃肉，可始终没下手。当一只岩羊出现在你上方几百英尺的石台上，顶着他那喇叭似的角，那神情里有种高贵的气派——他看着像个祭司。我们不想朝他们开枪，把他们惊跑。我们乐意看着他们。要新鲜肉吃的时候，我们就打一头野牛。

===Original===
At last we came upon one of the original inhabitants﻿—not a skeleton, but a dried human body, a woman. She was not in the Cliff City; we found her in a little group of houses stuck up in a high arch we called the Eagle’s Nest. She was lying on a yucca mat, partly covered with rags, and she had dried into a mummy in that water-drinking air. We thought she had been murdered; there was a great wound in her side, the ribs stuck out through the dried flesh. Her mouth was open as if she were screaming, and her face, through all those years, had kept a look of terrible agony. Part of the nose was gone, but she had plenty of teeth, not one missing, and a great deal of coarse black hair. Her teeth were even and white, and so little worn that we thought she must have been a young woman. Henry named her Mother Eve, and we called her that. We put her in a blanket and let her down with great care, and kept her in a chamber in the Cliff City.

===Chinese===
我们终于碰上了原先的居民中的一个——不是一副骸骨，而是一具干透的人体，一个女人。她不在崖城里；我们在一处高高拱起、我们称作鹰巢的地方，一小群房屋里找到了她。她躺在一张丝兰纤维的垫子上，半盖着些破布，在那饮水的干燥空气里变成了一具木乃伊。我们觉得她是被人谋杀的；她腰间有一处大伤口，肋骨从干枯的皮肉里戳了出来。她嘴张着，像是在尖叫，那张脸经过这么多年，仍保留着一种极其痛苦的神情。鼻子缺了一部分，但她牙齿齐全，一颗不少，还有一头浓密的粗黑头发。她的牙齿齐整、洁白，磨损得那样少，我们断定她生前一定是个年轻女人。亨利给她取名「夏娃妈妈」，我们也就这么叫她。我们用毯子裹住她，小心翼翼地把她放下去，存放在崖城的一个房间里。

===Original===
Yes, we found three other bodies, but afterward. One day, working in the Cliff City, we came upon a stone slab at one end of the cavern, that seemed to lead straight into the rock. It was set in cement, and when we loosened it we found it opened into a small, dark chamber. In this there had been a platform, of fine cedar poles laid side by side, but it had crumbled. In the wreckage were three bodies, one man and two women, wrapped in yucca-fibre, all in the same posture and apparently prepared for burial. They were the bodies of old people. We believed they were among the aged who were left behind when the tribe went down to live on their farms in the summer season; that they had died in the absence of the villagers, and were put into this mortuary chamber to await the return of the tribe, when they would have their funeral rites. Probably these people burned their dead. Of course an archaeologist could have told a great deal about that civilization from those bodies. But they never got to an archaeologist﻿—at least, not on this side of the world.

===Chinese===
是的，后来我们又找到了另外三具遗体。有一天，我们在崖城里干活，在洞窟的一头碰到一块石板，它似乎一直通进岩石里去。那石板用泥灰粘着，我们把它撬松，发现它后面通向一个又小又黑的房间。里面原有一座用上好雪松杆一根根并排搭成的台子，但已经塌了。在塌落的残迹里有三具遗体，一男两女，都裹着丝兰纤维，姿势一模一样，显然是经过处理后待葬的。都是老人的遗体。我们猜想他们是部落夏季下山到农场居住时被留下的老人中的几个；他们在村民不在时死去，便被放进这间停灵的屋子，等候部落归来时再为他们举行葬礼。这些人大概是火葬死者的。当然，一个考古学家从这几具遗体上能看出许许多多关于那个文明的事。可它们始终没到一个考古学家手里——至少，在世界这一边没有。
