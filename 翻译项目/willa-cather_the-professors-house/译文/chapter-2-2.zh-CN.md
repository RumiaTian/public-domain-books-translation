### II / II

===Original===
The cabin stood in a little grove of piñons, about thirty yards back from the Cruzados river, facing south and sheltered on the north by a low hill. The grama grass grew right up to the doorstep, and the rabbits were running about and the grasshoppers hitting the door when we pulled up and looked at the place. There was no litter around, it was as clean as a prairie-dog’s house. No outbuildings, except a shed for our horses. The hillside behind was sandy and covered with tall clumps of deer-horn cactus, but there was nothing but grass to the south, with streaks of bright yellow rabbit-brush. Along the river the cottonwoods and quaking asps had already turned gold. Just across from us, overhanging us, indeed, stood the mesa, a pile of purple rock, all broken out with red sumach and yellow aspens up in the high crevices of the cliffs. From the cabin, night and day, you could hear the river, where it made a bend round the foot of the mesa and churned over the rocks. It was the sort of place a man would like to stay in forever.

===Chinese===
小木屋坐落在一小片矮松（piñon）林里，离克鲁萨多斯河（Cruzados river）约莫三十码远，朝南，北面有一道低矮的山岗替它挡着风。格兰马草（grama grass）一直长到门槛跟前，我们赶车到了、打量这地方的时候，野兔正四处乱跑，蚱蜢蹦到了门上。四下没有一点杂物，干净得像草原犬鼠的窝。除了一个拴马的棚子，再没别的附属屋舍。屋后的山坡是沙土地，一丛一丛高高的鹿角仙人掌（deer-horn cactus）盖满了；可南边除了草什么也没有，只一道道鲜黄的兔刷草（rabbit-brush）。河两岸的三角叶杨（cottonwoods）和白杨（quaking asps）已经转成了金黄。就在我们对岸——简直可以说是悬在我们头顶上——矗立着那座台地（mesa），一整堆紫色的岩石，崖壁高处的裂缝里尽是红的漆树（sumach）和黄的山杨。从屋里，日日夜夜，你都听得见河水——它在台地脚下拐了个弯，在石头上翻滚咆哮。这种地方，人巴不得一辈子待下去。

===Original===
I helped Rapp open the wooden shutters and sweep out the cabin. We put clean blankets on the bunks, and stowed away bacon and coffee and canned stuff on the shelves behind the cookstove. I confess I looked forward to cooking on an iron stove with four holes. Rapp explained to me that Blake and I wouldn’t be able to enjoy all this luxury together for a time. He wanted the herd kept some distance to the north as long as the grass held out up there, and Roddy and I could take turn about, one camping near the cattle and one sleeping in a bed.

“There’s not pasture enough down here to take them through a long winter,” he said, “and it’s safest to keep them grazing up north while you can. Besides, if you bring them down here while the weather’s so warm, they get skittish, and that mesa over there makes trouble. They swim the river and bolt into the mesa, and that’s the last you ever see of them. We’ve lost a lot of critters that way. The mesa has been populated by runaways from our herd, till now there’s a fine bunch of wild cattle up there. When the wind’s right, our cows over here get the scent of them and make a break for the river. You’ll have to watch ’em close when you bring ’em down.”

===Chinese===
我帮拉普（Rapp）推开木百叶窗，把屋里扫干净。我们在铺位上铺好干净的毯子，把咸肉、咖啡和罐头食品一一收进炉子后面的架子上。我承认，能在一口四眼铁炉子上做饭，我心里是盼着的。拉普跟我交代，布雷克和我一时半会儿还不能一块儿享受这番舒服。他想让牛群（herd）先待在北边一段距离的地方，只要那边的草还吃得住；我和罗迪（Roddy）轮班，一个在牛群旁边扎营，一个睡床铺。

「这儿下面的草场不够，养不过一整个长冬，」他说，「趁还能放，把它们留在北边吃草最稳妥。再说，天这么热的时候把它们赶下来，它们容易受惊（skittish），那边那座台地就惹麻烦。它们游过河，一头钻进台地去，那就再也见不着了。我们就这么丢了不少牲口（critters）。台地上住的全是从我们牛群里跑走的，到现在上头已经有挺不错的一大群野牛了。风向对的时候，我们这边的母牛闻得见它们的味儿，就撒开蹄子往河那边冲。等你们把它们赶下来，可得盯紧了。」

===Original===
I asked him whether nobody had ever gone over to get the lost cattle out.

Rapp glared at me. “Out of that mesa? Nobody has ever got into it yet. The cliffs are like the base of a monument, all the way round. The only way into it is through that deep canyon that opens on the water level, just where the river makes the bend. You can’t get in by that, because the river’s too deep to ford and too swift to swim. Oh, I suppose a horse could swim it, if cattle can, but I don’t want to be the man to try.”

I remarked that I had had my eye on the mesa all summer and meant to climb it.

“Not while you’re working for the Sitwell Company, you don’t! If you boys try any nonsense of that sort, I’ll fire you quick. You’d break your bones and lose the herd for us. You have to watch them close to keep them from going over, I tell you. If it wasn’t for that mesa, this would be the best winter range in all New Mexico.”

===Chinese===
我问他，难道就没人过去把走失的牛弄出来过。

拉普瞪着我。「从那座台地里弄出来？至今还没人能进去过呢。四周的崖壁就像一座纪念碑的基座，一圈全是那样。唯一的入口是那条直通到水面的深峡谷，就在河流拐弯的地方。可你也进不去，因为河水太深，没法蹚，又太急，没法游。哦，我想，要是牛能游过去，马大概也游得过去，可我不想当那个去试的人。」

我说，我整个夏天都盯着那座台地看，一心想爬上去。

「你给西特韦尔公司（Sitwell Company）干活的时候，想也别想！你们小子要是敢动这种糊涂念头，我立马把你们开了。你们会摔断骨头，还把牛群给我们丢了。我告诉你们，一定得盯紧了，别让它们跑过去。要不是那座台地，这儿就是全新墨西哥（New Mexico）顶好的冬季牧场了。」

===Original===
After the foreman left us, we settled down to easy living and fine weather; blue and gold days, and clear, frosty nights. We kept the cattle off to the north and east and alternated in taking charge of them. One man was with the herd while the other got his sleep and did the cooking at the cabin. The mesa was our only neighbour, and the closer we got to it, the more tantalizing it was. It was no longer a blue, featureless lump, as it had been from a distance. Its skyline was like the profile of a big beast lying down; the head to the north, higher than the flanks around which the river curved. The north end we could easily believe impassable﻿—sheer cliffs that fell from the summit to the plain, more than a thousand feet. But the south flank, just across the river from us, looked accessible by way of the deep canyon that split the bulk in two, from the top rim to the river, then wound back into the solid cube so that it was invisible at a distance, like a mouse track winding into a big cheese. This canyon didn’t break the solid outline of the mesa, and you had to be close to see that it was there at all. We faced the mesa on its shortest side; it was only about three miles long from north to south, but east and west it measured nearly twice that distance. Whether the top was wooded we couldn’t see﻿—it was too high above us; but the cliffs and canyon on the river side were fringed with beautiful growth, groves of quaking asps and piñons and a few dark cedars, perched up in the air like the hanging gardens of Babylon. At certain hours of the day, those cedars, growing so far up on the rocks, took on the bluish tint of the cliffs themselves.

===Chinese===
工头走了以后，我们便安顿下来，过起舒坦的日子，赶上好天气；白天蓝澄澄、金灿灿，夜里清清朗朗，降着白霜。我们把牛群放在北边和东边，轮流照看。一个人跟着牛群，另一个就在屋里睡觉、做饭。那座台地是我们唯一的邻居，我们越靠近它，它就越撩拨人心。它再也不是远处望去时那蓝蓝的、光秃秃的一团了。它的天际线像一头卧着的大兽的侧影：兽头朝北，比那两条被河水环绕的腰腹还要高。北端我们很容易相信是无法攀越的——陡直的崖壁从山顶一直落到平原，足有一千多英尺。可南面的腰腹，就在河对岸，看起来倒似乎上得去：一条深峡谷把整座山体劈成两半，从顶上的边缘一直裂到河边，再往这块实心的方块里蜿蜒回去，远远望去根本看不见，像一条老鼠道钻进了一块大奶酪。这条峡谷并没有打破台地那浑然一体的轮廓，你非得走到近处，才看得出它在那儿。我们正对着台地最短的一边；它南北不过三英里来长，东西却有将近两倍那么宽。顶上长没长树，我们看不见——它在我们头顶太高了；可河这边的崖壁和峡谷边上，却缀着一簇簇好看的草木：一片片的白杨和矮松，还有几棵黑黝黾的雪松，高高地悬在半空，活像巴比伦的空中花园。一天里有些时辰，那些长在如此高处的岩石上的雪松，会染上崖壁本身那种蓝幽幽的颜色。

===Original===
It was light up there long before it was with us. When I got up at daybreak and went down to the river to get water, our camp would be cold and grey, but the mesa top would be red with sunrise, and all the slim cedars along the rocks would be gold﻿—metallic, like tarnished gold-foil. Some mornings it would loom up above the dark river like a blazing volcanic mountain. It shortened our days, too, considerably. The sun got behind it early in the afternoon, and then our camp would lie in its shadow. After a while the sunset colour would begin to stream up from behind it. Then the mesa was like one great ink-black rock against a sky on fire.

===Chinese===
那上面天亮得比我们这儿早得多。天蒙蒙亮我起身，下到河边去打水，我们这营地还又冷又灰，可台地的顶上已经被日出照得通红，岩石边那些细细瘦瘦的雪松全成了金色——是金属那样的金，像失去光泽的金箔。有几个早晨，它在黑沉沉的河水上方浮现出来，像一座熊熊燃烧的火山。它也把我们的白天缩短了不少。午后刚过不久，太阳就躲到了它背后，于是我们的营地便躺在它的影子里。过一会儿，落日的颜色开始从它背后往上涌。那时台地就像一大块墨黑的岩石，衬着一片燃烧的天空。

===Original===
No wonder the thing bothered us and tempted us; it was always before us, and was always changing. Black thunderstorms used to roll up from behind it and pounce on us like a panther without warning. The lightning would play round it and jab into it so that we were always expecting it would fire the brush. I’ve never heard thunder so loud as it was there. The cliffs threw it back at us, and we thought the mesa itself, though it seemed so solid, must be full of deep canyons and caverns, to account for the prolonged growl and rumble that followed every crash of thunder. After the burst in the sky was over, the mesa went on sounding like a drum, and seemed itself to be muttering and making noises.

===Chinese===
怪不得这东西老搅扰我们、勾引我们；它老在我们眼前，老在变样。黑色的雷雨常常从它背后翻滚过来，像一头黑豹毫无预兆地朝我们扑下。闪电在它四周游走，又刺进它身体里去，我们总担心它会把那片灌木点着。我从没听见过哪儿打雷打得那样响。崖壁把雷声朝我们反弹回来，我们便想，这座台地虽说看上去是实心的，里头一定满是深深的峡谷和洞穴——不然每一次雷声炸过之后，那绵延不绝的低吼和轰隆就没法解释了。天上那一炸过去之后，台地还像鼓一样咚咚作响，仿佛它自己在嘟嘟囔囔、弄出声响。

===Original===
One afternoon I was out hunting turkeys. Just as the sun was getting low, I came through a sea of rabbit-brush, still yellow, and the horizontal rays of light, playing into it, brought out the contour of the ground with great distinctness. I noticed a number of straight mounds, like plough furrows, running from the river inland. It was too late to examine them. I cut a scrub willow and stuck a stake into one of the ridges, to mark it. The next day I took a spade down to the plantation of rabbit-brush and dug around in the sandy soil. I came upon an old irrigation main, unmistakable, lined with hard smooth cobbles and ’dobe cement, with sluices where the water had been let out into trenches. Along these ditches I turned up some pieces of pottery, all of it broken, and arrowheads, and a very neat, well-finished stone pickax.

That night I didn’t go back to the cabin, but took my specimens out to Blake, who was still north with the cattle. Of course, we both knew there had been Indians all over this country, but we felt sure that Indians hadn’t used stone tools for a long while back. There must have been a colony of pueblo Indians here in ancient times: fixed residents, like the Taos Indians and the Hopis, not wanderers like the Navajos.

===Chinese===
有天下午我出去打火鸡。太阳快要落山的时候，我穿过一大片兔刷草，还是黄澄澄的，水平的阳光斜斜地照进去，把地面的起伏照得分外清晰。我注意到有一些笔直的土垄，像犁沟一样，从河边一直往内陆延伸。天太晚了，来不及细看。我折了一根矮柳枝，插在其中一道土脊上做个记号。第二天，我带了把铲子下到那片兔刷草丛里，在沙土里挖了起来。我挖到了一条古老的灌溉主渠，错不了——渠底铺着又硬又光的鹅卵石和土坯（’dobe）水泥，还有放水入沟的闸口。沿着这些沟渠，我翻出了一些陶器的碎片——全碎了——还有箭头，和一把十分精巧、做得相当讲究的石鹤嘴锄。

那天夜里我没有回木屋，而是把发现的几样东西拿出去给布雷克看——他还留在北边守着牛群。我们俩当然都知道，这一带从前到处都是印第安人，可我们心里有数，印第安人早就有很久不用石器了。古时候这里一定有过一支普韦布洛印第安人（pueblo Indians）的聚落：是定居下来的居民，像陶斯印第安人（Taos Indians）和霍皮人（Hopi）那样，而不是像纳瓦霍人（Navajo）那样到处游荡。

===Original===
To people off alone, as we were, there is something stirring about finding evidences of human labour and care in the soil of an empty country. It comes to you as a sort of message, makes you feel differently about the ground you walk over every day. I liked the winter range better than any place I’d ever been in. I never came out of the cabin door in the morning to go after water that I didn’t feel fresh delight in our snug quarters and the river and the old mesa up there, with its top burning like a bonfire. I wanted to see what it was like on the other side, and very soon I took a day off and forded the river where it was wide and shallow, north of our camp. I rode clear around the mesa, until I met the river again where it flowed under the south flank.

On that ride I got a better idea of its actual structure. All the way round were the same precipitous cliffs of hard blue rock, but in places it was mixed with a much softer stone. In these soft streaks there were deep dry watercourses which could certainly be climbed as far as they went, but nowhere did they reach to the top of the mesa. The top seemed to be one great slab of very hard rock, lying on the mixed mass of the base like the top of an old-fashioned marble table. The channels worn out by water ran for hundreds of feet up the cliffs, but always stopped under this great rimrock, which projected out over the erosions like a granite shelf. Evidently, it was because of this unbroken top layer that the butte was inaccessible. I rode back to camp that night, convinced that if we ever climbed it, we must take the route the cattle took, through the river and up the one canyon that broke down to water-level.

===Chinese===
对于像我们这样离群索居的人来说，在一片荒野的土地里发现人类劳作和经营过的痕迹，是件叫人心头一动的事。它像一道讯息传到你这里，让你对你每天踩着的这片土地生出不同的感觉。这片冬季牧场比我到过的任何地方都更中我的意。每天早晨我从木屋门里出来去打水，总要为我们这暖和齐整的小窝、为这条河、为上头那座古老的台地——它的顶上烧得像一堆篝火——生出崭新的欢喜。我想看看它另一边是什么样子，没过多久便腾出一天，在我们营地北边那段河面又宽又浅的地方蹚水过了河。我骑马绕着台地整整走了一圈，一直走到河水从它南麓下面流出来的地方，又和河相遇。

那一趟跑下来，我对它真实的结构有了更清楚的认识。四面一圈都是同样陡峭的、坚硬的蓝色岩石崖壁，不过有些地方夹杂着一种软得多的石头。这些软的石层里，有些又深又干的水沟，顺着它们确实能爬上一段，可哪一条也通不到台地的顶上。顶上看上去像一大块极硬的整石板，盖在底下那驳杂的基座之上，活像一张老式大理石桌子的桌面。被水冲刷出来的沟壑顺着崖壁往上有好几百英尺，可总是在这道巨大的檐岩底下断了——那檐岩像一道花岗岩的架子，突出来盖在那些被侵蚀的沟壑之上。显然，正是因为这一层完整无缺的盖顶，这座方山才上不去。那天夜里我骑马回营地，心里认定：我们哪天真要爬上去，非得走牛走的那条路不可——从河里穿过去，再顺着那条一直裂到水面的峡谷上去。
