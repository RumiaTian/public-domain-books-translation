## I / 一

===Original===
You are, I suppose, by now in Rome. It is very curious how present to me are both Rome and yourself. There is a certain hill—you, and that is the curious part of it, will never go there—yet, yesterday, late in the evening, I stood upon its summit and you came walking from a place below. It is always midday there: the seven pillars of the Forum stand on high, their capitals linked together, and form one angle of a square. At their bases there lie some detritus, a broken marble lion, and I think but I am not certain, the bronze she-wolf suckling the two bronze children. Your dress brushed the herbs: it was grey and tenuous: I suppose you do not know how you look when you are unconscious of being looked at? But I looked at you for a long time—at my You.

===Chinese===
我想，你此刻该已到了罗马。说来奇怪，罗马和你本人竟都如此清晰地呈现在我眼前。有那么一座小山——而你，这正是奇怪之处，永远不会去到那里——然而昨天，夜深时分，我站在它的山顶，你从下面某个地方走了上来。那里永远是正午：广场的七根石柱高高矗立，柱头彼此相连，构成一个方形的一角。柱基四周散落着一些碎石、一只残破的大理石狮子，我想——但我不敢确定——还有那尊青铜母狼，正在哺育两个青铜孩童。你的衣裾拂过那些花草：是灰色的、轻盈的；我想你并不知道自己不经意间被人注视时是什么模样吧？可我注视了你很久——注视着我的那个「你」。

===Original===
I saw your husband yesterday at the club and he said that you would not be returning till the end of April. When I got back to my chambers I found a certain letter. I will tell you about it afterwards—but I forbid you to look at the end of what I am writing now. There is a piece of news coming: I would break it to you if I could—but there is no way of breaking the utterly unexpected. Only, if you read this through you will gather from the tenor, from the tone of my thoughts, a little inkling, a small preparation for my disclosure. Yes: it is a "disclosure."

===Chinese===
昨天我在俱乐部见到你丈夫，他说你到四月底才回来。我回到寓所后发现了一封信。我待会儿再跟你说它——但我禁止你现在就去翻看我正在写的这些东西的结尾。有一个消息将要到来：若能做到，我真想缓缓透露给你——可是对于全然出乎意料的事，根本没有缓缓透露的法子。不过，只要你把这封信读完，你将从字里行间、从我思绪的基调中，得到一点暗示，一点对我即将披露之事的小小心理准备。是的：这是一次「披露」。

===Original===
… Briefly, then, it was this letter—a business letter—that set me thinking: that made that hill rise before me. Yes, I stood upon it and there before me lay Rome—beneath a haze, in the immense sea of plains. I have often thought of going to Rome—of going with you, in a leisurely autumn of your life and mine. Now—since I have received that letter—I know that I shall never see any other Rome than that from an imagined hilltop. And when, in the wonderful light and shadelessness of that noon, last evening, you came from a grove of silver poplars, I looked at you—*my* you—for a very long while. You had, I think, a parasol behind your head, you moved slowly, you looked up at the capitals of those seven pillars.… And I thought that I should never—since you will not return before the end of April—never see you again. I shall never see again the you that every other man sees.…

===Chinese===
……那么，简而言之，正是这封信——一封公事信——引我陷入沉思：使那座小山在我眼前浮现。是的，我站在山顶，罗马便在我面前铺展开来——在一层薄雾之下，躺在广袤如海的平原之中。我常想去罗马——想和你一起去，在你我生命中某个从容的秋天。可如今——自从收到那封信——我知道我永远也见不到别的罗马了，除了那座想象中山顶上所见的。昨天傍晚，当你在那奇异的无影正午之光中，从一片银白杨树林里走出来时，我注视了你——*我的*你——注视了很久。我想，你身后撑着一把阳伞，你缓缓走动，抬头望着那七根石柱的柱头。……而我想，我将永远——既然你四月底之前不会回来——永远也再见不到你了。我将再也见不到那个别的男人所见的你了……

===Original===
You understand everything so well that already you must understand the nature of my disclosure. It is, of course, no disclosure to tell you that I love you. A very great reverence is due to youth—and a very great latitude is due to the dead. For I am dead: I have only lived through you for how many years now! And I shall never speak with you again. Some sort of burial will have been given to me before the end of April. I am a spirit. I have ended my relations with the world. I have balanced all my books, my will is made. Only I have nothing to leave—save to you, to whom I leave all that is now mine in the world—my memory.

===Chinese===
你事事都领悟得那样透彻，此刻想必已明白我这次披露的性质。当然，告诉你我爱你，算不得什么披露。青春理应得到极大的敬意——死者亦应得到极大的宽容。因为我已经死了：这些年来，我只是借你而活！而我将永远不再与你说话。四月底之前，我总该已得到某种安葬了。我是一个幽灵。我已断绝了与这个世界的一切关系。我结清了所有的账目，遗嘱也已立好。只是我没有什么可留下的——除了留给你，留给你我现在在这世上仅有的一切——我的回忆。

===Original===
It is very curious—the world now. I walked slowly down here from Gordon Square. I walked slowly—for all my work is done. On the way I met Graydon Bankes, the K.C. It would have astonished him if he could have known how unreal he looked to me. He is six feet high, and upon his left cheek there is a brown mole. I found it difficult to imagine why he existed. And all sorts of mists hurried past him. It was just outside the Natural History Museum. He said that his Seaford Railway Bill would come before Committee in June. And I wondered: what is June?… I laughed and thought: why June will never come!

June will never come. Imagine that for a moment. We have discussed the ethics of suicide. You see why June will never come!

===Chinese===
如今这世界——真奇怪。我从戈登广场一路慢慢走回这里。我走得很慢——因为我一切的事都已做完。路上我遇见了格雷登·班克斯御用大律师（K.C.）。他若知道当时他在我眼中是多么不真实，定会大吃一惊。他身高六英尺，左颊上有一颗褐色的痣。我很难想象他为何存在。各种各样的薄雾匆匆掠过他身旁。那地方就在自然历史博物馆外面。他说他的锡福德铁路法案将于六月提交委员会审议。而我纳闷：六月是什么？……我笑了，心想：六月永远不会来了！

六月永远不会来了。想想这意味着什么。我们讨论过自杀的伦理。你明白了为什么六月永远不会来了吧！

===Original===
You remember that ring I always wear? The one with a bulging, greenish stone. Once or twice you have asked me what stone it was. You thought, I know, that it was in bad taste and I told you I wore it for the sake of associations. I know you thought—but no: there has never been any woman but you.

===Chinese===
你记得我总戴的那枚戒指吗？镶着一颗鼓鼓的、发绿的石头的那枚。有一两次你问过我那是什么石头。我知道，你觉得它俗气，而我告诉你我戴它是为了某种纪念。我知道你以为——但不：除了你，从未有过任何别的女人。

===Original===
You must have felt a long time ago that there was not, that there could not have been another woman. The associations of the ring are not with the past of a finished affection, or hate, or passion, with all these forms of unrest that have a term in life: they looked forward to where there is no end—whether there is rest in it God alone knows. If it were not bad taste to use big words in extremities I would say there was Eternity in the ring—Eternity which is the negation of all that life may contain of losses and disappointments. Perhaps you have noticed that there was one note in our confidence that never responded to your touch. It was that note of universal negation contained within the glass film of the ring. It is not you who brought the ring into my life: I had it made years ago. It was in my nature always to anticipate a touch on my shoulder, to which the only answer could be an act of defiance. And the ring is my weapon. I shall raise it to my teeth, bite through the glass: inside there is poison.

===Chinese===
你一定早就感觉到，不曾、也不可能有过别的女人。那枚戒指所系的，并非某段已了结的恋情或恨意或激情的过往——并非种种在生命中有终局的不安；它们朝向的是一个没有尽头之处——其中是否有安宁，唯有上帝知道。若不是在绝境中使用大词太不得体，我会说那枚戒指里盛着永恒——永恒，即是对生命中一切损失与失意的否定。也许你早已注意到，在我们的推心置腹中，有一个音符从未响应过你的触碰。那便是封在戒指那层玻璃薄壳之中的、那普遍否定的音符。并非你把戒指带进了我的生命：多年前我便已请人做好了它。我的天性便是始终预感着有一只手会拍上我的肩头，而对此唯一的回答只能是一次决绝之举。这枚戒指便是我的武器。我将把它举到唇边，用牙齿咬破那层玻璃：里面有毒。

===Original===
I haven't concealed anything from you. Have I? And, with the great wisdom for which I love you, you have tolerated these other things. You would have tolerated this too, you who have met so many sinners and have never sinned.…

Ah, my dear one—that is why I have so loved you. From our two poles we have met upon one common ground of scepticism—so that I am not certain whether it was you or I who first said: "Believe nothing: be harsh to no one." But at least we have suffered. One does not drag around with one such a cannonball as I have done all these years without thinking some wise thoughts. And well I know that in your dreary and terrible life you have gained your great wisdom. You have been envied; you too have thought: Is any prospect fair to those among its trees? And I have been envied for my gifts, for my talents, for my wealth, for my official position, for the letters after my name, for my great and empty house, for my taste in pictures—for my… for my opportunities.

===Chinese===
我向你隐瞒过什么吗？没有吧？而你，以我爱你那份伟大的智慧，容忍了这些别的事情。你本来也会容忍这一件的——你，这个见过那么多罪人、自己却从未犯罪的人……

啊，我亲爱的人——这便是我如此爱你的缘由。从我们各自的极端，我们在一片共同的怀疑主义之地相遇了——以至于我不确定当初是你还是我先说出：「什么也不要信：对谁都不要苛刻。」但至少我们都受过苦。一个人拖着我这些年来拖着的这颗炮弹到处走，不可能不生出些睿智的念头。而我也深知，在你那阴郁可怕的一生中，你赢得了你那伟大的智慧。你被人嫉妒过；你也曾想过：置身于某片林木之中的人，还能看清那片风景的美吗？而我，则因我的天赋、才华、财富、官职、姓名后头的那些字母、我那又大又空的宅邸、我的绘画品味——因我的……因我的种种机缘，而被人嫉妒。

===Original===
Great criminals and the very patient learn one common lesson: Believe in nothing, be harsh to no one!

===Chinese===
大罪犯和极能忍耐的人，都学到了同一个道理：什么也不要信，对谁都不要苛刻！

===Original===
But you cannot understand how immensely leisurely I feel. It is one o'clock at night. I cannot possibly be arrested before eleven tomorrow morning. I have ten hours in which, without the shadow of a doubt, I can write to you: I can put down my thoughts desultorily and lazily. I have half a score of hours in which to speak to you.

===Chinese===
但你无法想象我现在感到何等悠闲。此刻是凌晨一点。明天上午十一点之前我绝无可能被捕。我有整整十个小时，可以毫无疑问地给你写信：我可以散漫慵懒地写下我的思绪。我有十个钟头来跟你说话。

===Original===
The stress of every secret emotion makes for sincerity in the end. Silence is like a dam. When the flood is at its highest the dam gives way. I am not conceited enough to think that I can sweep you along, terrified, in the rush of my confidences. I have not the elemental force. Perhaps it is just that form of "greatness" that I have lacked all my life—that profound quality which the Italians call *terribilita*. There is nothing overpowering or terrible in the confession of a love too great to be kept within the bounds of the banality which is the safeguard of our daily life. Men have been nerved to crime for the sake of a love that was theirs. The call of every great passion is to unlawfulness. But your love was not mine, and my love for you was vitiated by that conventional reverence which, as to nine parts in ten, is genuine, but as to the last tenth a solemn sham behind which hide all the timidities of a humanity no longer in its youth. I have been of my time—altogether of my time—lacking courage for a swoop, as a bird respects a ragged and nerveless scarecrow. Altogether a man of my time. Observe, I do not say "our time." You are of all time—you are the loved Woman of the first cry that broke the silence and of the last song that shall mark the end of this ingenious world to which love and suffering have been given, but which has in the course of ages invented for itself all the virtues and all the crimes. And being of this world and of my time I have set myself to deal ingeniously with my suffering and my love.

===Chinese===

===Original===
Now everything is over—even regrets. Nothing remains of finite things but a few days of life and my confession to make to you—to you alone of all the world.

===Chinese===
如今一切都结束了——连遗憾也结束了。有限之物中什么都不剩了，除了几天的生命，以及我要向你——向全世上独一无二的你——作的这番告白。

===Original===

This is the great secret—the heart of my confession. The distance did not count. No walls could make me safe. No solitude could defend me; and having no faith in the consolations of eternity I suffered too cruelly from your absence.

===Chinese===

这便是最大的秘密——我这次告白的核心。距离不起作用。任何墙壁都无法令我安全。任何独处都无法保护我；而又因我不信永恒的慰藉，你的不在令我受苦太深。

And now it is too late. Moreover, I am a man of my time, the time is not of great deeds but of colossal speculations. The moments when I was not with you had to be got through somehow. ## Preface II / 序二

===Original===

## V / 五

===Original===

这正是爱德华·伯登对小艾弗里丝小姐所做的。我不是说他不把她理想化——但他所看到的那个被圣化的她，是他那种特制茶叶的斟奉者，是他那种模样的孩子的母亲。而在我看来，这恰恰是一个十分正当的理由，说明女子若是聪明，便应当冷静地、出于理智地把命运托付给如今既诱惑她们、又被她们逃离的那一类危险男子。

===Original===
They flee from them, I am convinced, because they fear for their worldly material fortunes. They fear, that is to say, that the poet is not a stable man of business: they recognise that he is a gambler—and it seems to them that it is folly to trust to a gambler for lifelong protection. In that they are perhaps right. But I think that no woman doubts her power to retain a man's affection—so that it is not to the reputation for matrimonial instability that the poet owes his disfavour. A woman lives, in short, to play with this particular fire, since to herself she says: "Here is a man who has broken the hearts of many women. I will essay the adventure of taming him." And, if she considers the adventure a dangerous one, that renders the contest only the more alluring, since at heart every woman, like every poet, is a gambler. In that perhaps she is right.

===Chinese===
我深信，她们逃离他们，是因为担心自己在世俗物质上的命运。也就是说，她们担心诗人不是个可靠的生意人：她们承认他是个赌徒——在她们看来，把终身的庇护托付给一个赌徒，便是愚蠢。在这一点上她们也许没错。但我想，没有哪个女子会怀疑自己留住一个男人爱意的能力——所以诗人之所以不受青睐，并非因为他在婚姻上不稳定的名声。简而言之，一个女子活着，就是要玩这一种火，因为她对自己说："这个男人伤过许多女人的心。我要尝试驯服他这一场冒险。"而倘若她觉得这场冒险危险，那只会使这场较量更加诱人，因为每一个女子，正如每一个诗人，骨子里都是赌徒。在这点上她也许是对的。

===Original===
But it seems to me that women make a great mistake in the value of the stakes they are ready to pay in order to enter this game. They will stake, that is to say, their relatively great coin—their sentimental lives; but they hoard with closed fingers the threepenny bit which is merely the material future.

They prefer, that is to say, to be rendered the mere presiding geniuses of well-loaded boards. It is better to be a lady—which you will remember philologically means a "loaf-cutter"—than to be an Ideal.

===Chinese===
但在我看来，女子们为了加入这场赌局而愿意付出的赌注，其价值上有一个大错。也就是说，她们愿意押上她们相对大的硬币——她们的情感生活；却攥紧手指，吝惜那只不过关系到物质未来的三便士硬币。

也就是说，她们宁可只做一张摆满佳肴的餐桌的主持天才。做一位"夫人"——你当记得，从词源学上讲，这个字意为"切面包的人"——总好过做一个理想。

===Original===
And in this they are obviously wrong. If a woman can achieve the obvious miracle of making a dangerous man stable in his affections she may well be confident that she can persuade him to turn his serious attention to the task of keeping a roof over her head. Certainly, I know, if I were a woman which of the two types of men I would choose. Upon the lowest basis it is better for all purposes of human contracts to be married to a good liar than to a bad one. For a lie is a figurative truth—and it is the poet who is the master of these illusions. Even in the matter of marital relations it is probable that the poet is as faithful as the Edward Burdens of this world—only the Edward Burdens are more skilful at concealing from the rest of the world their pleasant vices. I doubt whether they are as skilful at concealing them from the woman concerned—from the woman, with her intuition, her power to seize fine shades of coolness and her awakened self-interest. Imagine the wife of Edward Burden saying to him, "You have deceived me!" Imagine then the excellent youth, crimsoning, stuttering. He has been taught all his life that truth must prevail though the skies fall—and he stammers: "Yes: I have betrayed you." And that is tragedy, though in the psychological sense—and that is the important one—Edward Burden may have been as faithful as the ravens, who live for fifteen decades with the same mate. He will, in short, blunder into a tragic, false position. And he will make the tragedy only the more tragic in that all the intellectual powers he may possess will be in the direction of perpetuating the dismal position. He will not be able to argue that he has not been unfaithful—but he will be able to find a hundred arguments for the miserable woman prolonging her life with him. Position, money, the interests of the children, the feelings of her family and of his—all these considerations will make him eloquent to urge her to prolong her misery. And probably she will prolong it.

===Chinese===
而在这点上她们显然错了。倘若一个女子能实现那一显然的奇迹——使一个危险男子在感情上稳定下来——她便大可自信，能说服他把严肃的注意力转向"为她撑起一片屋顶"这一任务。我当然知道，假如我是女子，我会在两类男子中选哪一类。即便从最低的立足点说，就人类一切契约的目的而言，嫁给一个善于说谎的人也胜过嫁给一个不善说谎的人。因为谎言是一种比喻性的真实——而诗人正是这些幻象的大师。即便在婚姻关系上，诗人很可能与这世上的爱德华·伯登们一样忠贞——只不过爱德华·伯登们更善于向世人掩饰他们那些令人愉快的恶习。我却怀疑，他们是否同样善于向那个有关的女子掩饰——向那个凭着直觉、凭着捕捉细微冷淡之色和已被唤醒的自利之心的女子。想象爱德华·伯登的妻子对他说："你欺骗了我！"再想象那个优秀青年涨红了脸、结结巴巴的样子。他从小受的教育便是：天塌下来真理也要伸张——于是他嗫嚅道："是的：我辜负了你。"这便是悲剧了，尽管从心理学的意义上——而这才是重要的意义——爱德华·伯登也许像渡鸦一样忠贞，那种鸟与同一伴侣相守一百五十年。简而言之，他将跌跌撞撞地陷进一个悲剧性的、虚假的处境。而他只会使这悲剧更加悲惨，因为他可能拥有的一切心智力量，都将用于延续这个阴郁的处境。他将无法辩称自己未曾不忠——但他将能找到上百条理由，劝说那个可怜的女子继续与他共度。地位、金钱、子女的利益、她家人和他家人的感受——所有这些考量都将使他能言善辩地劝她延续她的痛苦。而她多半会延续下去。

===Original===
This, of course, is due to the excellent Edward's lack of an instinctive sympathy. The poet, with a truer vision, will in the same case, be able to face his Miss Averies' saying: "You have deceived me!" with a different assurance. Supposing the deflection to have been of the momentary kind he will be able to deny with a good conscience since he will be aware of himself and his feelings. He will at least be able to put the case in its just light. Or, if the deflection be really temperamental, really permanent, he will be unable—it being his business to look at the deeper verities—to lie himself out of the matter. He will break, strictly and sharply. Or, if he do not, it may be taken as a sign that his Miss Averies is still of value to him—that she, in fact, is still the woman that it is his desire to have for his companion. This is true, of course, only in the large sense, since obviously there are poets whose reverence for position, the interest of children or the feelings of their friends and relatives, may outweigh their hatred of a false position. These, however, are poets in the sense that they write verse: I am speaking of those who live the poet's life; to such, a false position is too intolerable to be long maintained.

===Chinese===
这当然要归咎于那位优秀的爱德华缺乏一种出于本能的同情。而诗人，凭着更真切的洞察，在同样的情形下，将能以另一种笃定，面对他的艾弗里丝小姐那句"你欺骗了我！"假如那次偏离只是一时的，他便能问心无愧地否认，因为他了解自己和他的情感。他至少能将事情放在它恰当的光照下来陈述。又或者，假如那偏离确属性情上的、确属持久的，他便无法——因为正视更深层的真实乃他的本分——用谎言把自己摘出去。他会干脆利落地决裂。又或者，假如他不决裂，那便可视为一个信号：他的艾弗里丝小姐对他仍有价值——事实上，她仍是那个他渴望与之相伴的女子。当然，这只是在宽泛意义上成立，因为显然有些诗人，对地位、子女的利益或亲友感受的顾忌，可能压过了他们对虚伪处境的憎恶。然而那些只是在写诗这个意义上才算诗人的诗人；我说的是那些过着诗人生活的人；对他们而言，虚伪的处境太过难以忍受，无法长久维持。

===Original===
But this again is only one of innumerable side-issues: let me return to my main contention that a dinner of herbs with a dangerous man is better than having to consume the flesh of stalled oxen with Edward Burden. Perhaps that is only a way of saying that you would have done better to entrust yourself to me than to—(But no, your husband is a better man than Edward Burden. He has at least had the courage to revert to his passion. I went this afternoon to your chemists and formally notified them that if they supplied him with more than the exactly prescribed quantity of that stuff, I, as holding your power of attorney, should do all that the law allows me to do against them.)

===Chinese===
但这又不过是无数枝节问题之一：让我回到我的主要论点吧——与一个危险男子同食蔬菜之宴，也胜过与爱德华·伯登共啖圈栏肥牛之肉。也许这不过是另一种说法，说你当初若是把自己托付给我、而不是托付给——（但不，你丈夫是个比爱德华·伯登更好的人。他至少有勇气回到他的激情。今天下午我去了一趟你常去的药店，正式通知他们：假如他们再供应给他超出处方精确规定剂量的那种东西，我作为持有你委托书的人，将对他们采取一切法律所允许的行动。）

===Original===
Even to the dullest of men, marrying is for the most part an imaginative act. I mean marrying as a step in life sanctioned by law, custom and that general consent of mankind which is the hallmark of every irrational institution. By irrational I do not mean wrong or stupid. Marriage is august by the magnitude of the issues it involves, balancing peace and strife on the fine point of a natural impulse refined by the need of a tangible ideal. I am not speaking here of mere domestic peace or strife which for most people that count are a question of manners and a mode of life. And I am thinking of the peace mostly—the peace of the soul which yearns for some sort of certitude in this earth, the peace of the heart which yearns for conquest, the peace of the senses that dreads deception, the peace of the imaginative faculty which in its restless quest of a high place of rest is spurred on by these great desires and that great fear.

===Chinese===
即便对最迟钝的男子而言，结婚在很大程度上也是一种富于想象力的行为。我说的是作为人生中一步、受法律习俗认可、受人类普遍同意（那是每一种非理性制度的标志）所认可的结婚。所谓非理性，我并非指错误或愚蠢。婚姻因其所涉问题之重大而庄严——它在一个被某种切实理想所升华的自然冲动之尖端，权衡着和平与纷争。我在这里说的不是单纯的家常和气或龃龉，对大多数算得上数的人而言，那不过是举止与生活方式的问题。我主要想到的是那种和平——灵魂渴望在这世上某种确定的和平，心灵渴望征服的和平，感官畏惧受骗的和平，那不安地寻觅一处高远安息之所的想象力，被这些巨大渴望和那种巨大恐惧所鞭策的和平。

===Original===
And even Edward Burden's imagination is moved by these very desires and that very fear—or else he would not have dreamt of marrying. I repeat, marriage is an imaginative institution. It's true that his imagination is a poor thing but it is genuine nevertheless. The faculty of which I speak is of one kind in all of us. Not to everyone is given that depth of feeling, that faculty of absolute trust which *will not* be deceived, and the exulting masterfulness of the senses which are the mark of a fearless lover. Fearless lovers are rare, if obstinate, and sensual fools are countless as grains of sand by the seashore. I can imagine that correct young man perfectly capable of setting himself deliberately to worry a distracted girl into surrender.

===Chinese===
而即便是爱德华·伯登的想象力，也被这些渴望和那种恐惧所打动——否则他不会梦想结婚。我再说一遍，婚姻是一种想象力的制度。不错，他的想象力是贫乏的，但终究是真的。我所说的那种能力，在所有人身上都是同一种。并非人人都能拥有那种感情深度、那种*绝不肯*受骗的绝对信赖之力、那种欢腾主宰的感官之力——那是一个无所畏惧的恋人的标记。无所畏惧的恋人即便执拗也十分罕见，而感官的蠢人却如海边沙粒般数不胜数。我可以想象，那个四平八稳的年轻人，完全有能耐处心积虑地把一个六神无主的姑娘烦到投降。

## VI / 六

===Original===
I don't know why, tonight in particular, the fact that I am a dead man occurs to me very insistently. I had forgotten this for two whole days. If anyone very dear to you has ever been *in extremis* at a distance and you have journeyed to be at the last bedside, you will know how possible this is—how for hours at a time the mind will go wandering away from the main fact that is drawing you onwards, till suddenly it comes back: someone is dying at a distance. And I suppose one's I is the nearest friend that one has—and my I is dying at a distance. At the end of a certain number of days is the deathbed towards which I am hurrying—it is a fact which I cannot grasp. But one aspect grows more clear to me every time I return to this subject.

===Chinese===
我不知道为什么，偏偏今夜，"我已是一个死人"这一事实如此执拗地浮现于我心头。整整两天我都把它忘了。假如曾有一个你至亲的人在远方*危在旦夕*（*in extremis*），而你跋涉前去赴那最后的病榻之侧，你就会知道这是何等可能的事——心智如何会一连好几个钟头从那牵引你前行的主事实上游离开去，直到它忽然又回来：有人在远方正在死去。而我想，一个人的"我"便是他最近的挚友——而我的"我"正在远方死去。在某一数目的日子尽头，便是那张我正赶赴的病榻——这是一个我无法把握的事实。但每一次回到这个话题，都有一个方面在我眼中愈加清晰。

===Original===
You remember that, when we have discussed suicide, we have agreed that to the man of action death is a solution: to the man of thoughts it is none. For the man of action expresses himself in action, and death is the negation of action: the man of thought sees the world only in thoughts, and over thought death exercises no solution of continuity. If one dies one's actions cease, one's problem continues. For that reason it is only in so far as I am a man of action that I shall be dying. You understand what I mean—for I do not mean that it is my actions that have killed me. It is simply because I have taken refuge from my thoughts in action, and because after April 5 that refuge will be closed to me, that I take refuge in a final action which, properly speaking, is neither action nor refuge.

===Chinese===
你记得，我们讨论自杀时曾达成共识：对行动之人而言，死亡是一种解决；对思想之人而言则不然。因为行动之人以行动表达自己，而死亡是对行动的否定：思想之人仅在思想中看见世界，而死亡对思想并不能造成连续性的中断。人一死，行动便止息，而问题仍在继续。正因如此，仅就我是行动之人这一面而言，我才是将死之人。你明白我的意思——因为我并不是说杀死我的是我的行动。只不过是因为我曾在行动中逃避我的思想，而四月五日之后这一避难所将对我关闭，我才在一场最后的行动中寻求庇护——而这场行动严格说来既非行动，也非庇护。

===Original===
And perhaps I am no man of action at all, since the action in which I have taken refuge is properly speaking no action at all, but merely the expression of a frame of mind. I have gambled, that is to say I have not speculated. For the speculator acts for gain: the gambler in order to interest himself. I have gambled—to escape from you: I have tried to escape from my thoughts of you into divining the undivinable future. For that is what gambling is. You try for a rise: you try for a fall—and the rise or the fall may depend on the momentary madness of a dozen men who declare a war, or upon the rain from heaven which causes so many more stalks of wheat to arise upon so many million square inches of earth. The point is that you make yourself dependent upon caprice—upon the caprice of the weather or upon the movement in the minds of men more insane than yourself.

===Chinese===
而也许我根本不是什么行动之人，因为我所投身的那个行动，严格说来根本算不上行动，不过是某种心境的表达而已。我赌，也就是说，我并非投机。因为投机者为求利而行动：赌徒则为使自己获得兴致。我赌——是为了逃离你：我曾试图从对你的思念中逃脱，遁入对那不可测之未来的占卜。因为赌博正是如此。你押涨：你押跌——而那涨或跌，也许取决于十来个人一时的疯狂而宣战，也许取决于天降之雨使千百万平方英寸的土地上多长出那么多株麦穗。关键在于，你使自己依赖于反复无常——依赖于天气的反复无常，或依赖于比你更疯狂的那些人心中念头的变化。

===Original===
Today I have entered upon what is the biggest gamble of my whole life. Certain men who believe in me—they are not Edward Burdens, nevertheless they believe in me—have proposed to me to form a corner in a certain article which is indispensable to the daily life of the City. I do not tell you what it is because you will assuredly witness the effects of this inspiration.

===Chinese===
今天我已投身于我这一生中最大的一场豪赌。有几个相信我的人——他们不是爱德华·伯登，但他们相信我——向我提议，对某一种伦敦城（the City）日常运作所不可或缺的货物做一次囤积居奇。我不告诉你是哪一种，因为你必将亲眼目睹这一灵感的后果。

===Original===
You will say that, when this is accomplished, it will be utterly uninteresting. And that is literally true: when it is done it will be uninteresting. But in the multiplicity of things that will have to be done before the whole thing is done—in the waiting for things to take effect, in the failures perhaps more than in the successes, since the failures will imply new devising—in all the meticulous thought-readings that will be necessary, the interest will lie, and in the men with whom one is brought into contact, the men with whom one struggles, the men whom one must bribe or trick.

===Chinese===
你会说，这事一旦做成，便索然无味了。这话字面上不假：做成之后它便无趣了。但在整件事完成之前所必须做的那千头万绪之中——在等待事情见效的过程里，在失败之中也许更甚于在成功之中，因为失败意味着要重新筹谋——在所有那些必要的、细致入微的揣度人心之中，趣味便在于此，趣味也在于那些你不得不与之打交道的人，那些你与之角力的人，那些你必须收买或欺骗的人。

===Original===
And you will say: How can I who am to die in fourteen days embark upon an enterprise that will last many months or many years? That, I think, is very simple.

It is my protest against being called a man of action, the misconception that I have had to resent all my life. And this is a thought: not an action: a thought made up of an almost infinite number of erring calculations. You have probably forgotten that I have founded two towns, upon the south coast: originated four railways in tropical climates and one in the west of England: and opened up heaven knows how many mines of one kind or another—and upon my soul I had forgotten these things too until I began to cast about in my mind. And now I go to my death unmindful of these glories in so far as they are concrete. In that sense my death is utter: it is a solution. But, in so far as they are my refuges from you they remain problems to which, if my ghost is to escape you, I must return again and again.

===Chinese===
你会问：我一个十四天后就要死的人，怎能着手一项将历时数月乃至数年的事业？我想，这很简单。

这是我对被称作行动之人的抗议——那是我一生都不得不愤懑的一种误解。而这是一桩思想：不是一桩行动：一桩由近乎无穷数目的、出错的计算所构成的思想。你大概已经忘了，我曾在南海岸建立过两座城镇：在热带气候中发起过四条铁路、在英格兰西部发起过一条：还开辟过天知道多少这样那样的矿井——而老实说，在我开始搜肠刮肚之前，我自己也把这些事忘了。如今我去就死，对这些功业之具体的方面漠不关心。在这一意义上，我的死是彻底的：它是一种解决。但就它们是我逃避你的避难所这一面而言，它们仍是问题——假如我的幽灵要逃脱你，我便必须对这些问题一次又一次地回返。

===Original===
In dying I surrender to you and thus, for the inner self of myself, death is no ending but the commencement of who knows what tortures. It is only in the latent hope that death is the negation of consciousness that I shall take my life. For death, though it can very certainly end no problem, may at least make us unconscious of how, eventually, the problem solves itself. That, you see, is really the crux of the whole thing—that is why the man of action will take refuge in death: the man of thought, never. But I, I am the man of neither the one nor the other: I am the man of love, which partakes of action and of thought, but which is neither.

===Chinese===
在死中我向你投降，于是，对我那个内在的自我而言，死并非终结，而是天知道何种折磨的开端。我之所以将寻短见，仅是出于一种潜在的希望：死亡是对意识的否定。因为死亡，虽然它显然无法终结任何问题，却至少能使我们对那问题最终如何自行了结毫无知觉。你看，这才是整件事真正的要害所在——这便是为什么行动之人会遁入死亡：思想之人则永远不会。而我，我既非前者也非后者：我是爱之人，爱兼具行动与思想，却又既非此亦非彼。

===Original===
The lover is, perhaps, the eternal doubter—simply because there is no certain panacea for love. 亲爱的，我已是一个疲倦至极的人。你若知道这些年来我渴望你，正如我渴望你那样，究竟是怎样一回事，你一定会纳闷：我坐在那把椅子上，为何不不顾伯登那封信、把戒指举到牙齿之间、了结一切。我有一种不可遏制的对安息的渴望——或者那不过是渴求你的支撑。一想到我必须永远面对——或者说，在那还能延续的时间里一直面对——这般躲避对你的思念所带来的麻烦的躁动，那几乎叫人无法忍受。我之所以按捺住了，是因为我已经给你写下了这些信。我爱你，而我也知道你爱我——可是，倘若没有这些信，我便会以我的死在你身上留下一道伤口。既已写下它们，我便再无法对你下那样的狠手。我的意思是，假如我死时不让你知道缘由，那对你而言不过是一场伤心的死亡——毕竟，承受并忘却死亡，是世人的本分，也是你作为世人之一所应尽的本分。可如今你既已非知道不可，我便无法面对那种残忍——让你心中塞满你不该承受的悔恨之痛。因为我知道你原本是会悔恨的，而那悔恨并不该你有，因为我从没给过你任何机会、任何时间，让你能向我伸出手来。如今我把它给了你，专候你的裁决。

因为，摆在面前的确凿的两条路便是这样：我愿在这一年之内把伯登的产业清理得干干净净，并为我已写信告诉你的那套新的、相对稳妥的计划付诸实施，以赚得安稳可靠的钱：这一切我都会做，只要你肯在这一个限度上做我的人——与我们彼此分享念想这一个限度。又或者，你不肯，那我便要拿伯登的钱，比从前任何时候都更疯狂地赌下去。而那最终的结局便是死亡，便是从你身边得一处避难之所。

那么，我便这样站着，已获缓刑——而最终的裁决，握在你手里。
