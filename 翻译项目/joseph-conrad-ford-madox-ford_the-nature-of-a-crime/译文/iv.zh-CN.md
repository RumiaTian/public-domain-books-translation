## IV / 四

===Original===
I have just come in from *Tristan and Isolde*.

I had to hurry and be there for the first notes because you—my you—would, I felt, be sitting beside me as you have so often. That, of course, is passion—the passion that makes us unaccountable in our actions.

===Chinese===
我刚从《特里斯坦与伊索尔德》回来。

我不得不赶时间，好赶上开头那几个音符，因为我觉得你——我的那个你——会像往常那样坐在我身旁。这当然是激情——那种使我们的行为不可理喻的激情。

===Original===
I found you naturally: but I found, too, something else. It has always a little puzzled me why we return to Tristan. There are passages in that thing as intolerable as anything in any of the Germanic master's scores. But we are held—simply by the idea of the love-philtre: it's that alone that interests us. We do not care about the initial amenities of Tristan and the prima donna: we do not believe in Mark's psychologising: but, from the moment when those two dismal marionettes have drained unconsideringly the impossible cup, they become suddenly alive, and we see two human beings under the grip of a passion—acting as irrationally as I did when I promised my cabman five shillings to get me to the theatre in time for the opening bars.

===Chinese===
我当然找到了你：但我也找到了别的什么。为什么我们总是回到《特里斯坦》里来，这一直令我有点困惑。那部作品里有些段落，与那位日耳曼大师任何一部总谱中的任何东西一样令人难以忍受。但我们被牢牢攫住——仅仅是因为那春药（love-philtre）的念头：唯有它吸引着我们。我们并不在乎特里斯坦与那位女一号开场时的那些虚礼：我们也不相信马克的那些心理分析：但从那两个沉闷的木偶不假思索地饮下那杯不可思议之酒的那一刻起，他们骤然活了过来，我们看见两个被一种激情攫住的人——行事之非理性，正如我许诺给车夫五先令、好让我赶上开场前几个小节时一样。

===Original===
It is, you see, the love-philtre that performs this miracle. It interests—it is real to us—because every human being knows what it is to act, irrationally, under the stress of some passion or other. We are drawn along irresistibly: we commit the predestined follies or the predestined heroisms: the other side of our being acts in contravention of all our rules of conduct or of intellect. Here, in Tristan, we see such madness justified with a concrete substance, a herb, a root. We see a vision of a state of mind in which morality no longer exists: we are given a respite, a rest: an interval in which no standard of conduct oppresses us. It is an idea of an appeal more universal than any other in which the tired imagination of humanity takes refuge.

===Chinese===
你看，正是春药成就了这一奇迹。它引人入胜——对我们而言它是真实的——因为每一个人都知道，在某种激情的重压之下，非理性地行事是怎样的滋味。我们被不可抗拒地牵引着：我们犯下注定的蠢行或注定的壮举：我们本性的另一面行事起来，违背了我们全部的行为准则或理智准则。在这里，在《特里斯坦》中，我们看见这种癫狂被一种具体之物——一株草、一条根——证明了其正当。我们看见一种道德不复存在的心境的幻象：我们获得了一段喘息、一场歇息：一个不再有任何行为标准压迫我们的间歇。这是一种比任何别的诉求都更普遍的念头，人类疲惫的想象力便在其中栖身。

===Original===
The thought that somewhere in the world there should be something that I could give to you, or you to me, that would leave us free to do what we wish without the drag of the thought of what we owe, to each other, to the world! And after all, what greater gift could one give to another? It would be the essential freedom. For assuredly, the philtre could do no more than put it in a man's power to do what he would do if he were let loose. He would not bring out more than he had in him: but he would fully and finally express himself.

===Chinese===
一想到这世上某处该有一样东西，我可以给你、或你可以给我，使我们得以自由地做我们想做的事，而不被"我们对彼此、对这世界亏欠了什么"的念头所拖累！终究，一个人能给予另一个人的，还有什么比这更大的礼物呢？那将是本质的自由。因为可以肯定，春药所能做的，无非是使一个人得以做他一旦被解放便会去做的事。他不会焕发出超过他本己所含的东西：但他会完完全全地、一劳永逸地表达自己。

===Original===
Something unexpected has changed the current of my thoughts. Nothing can change their complexion, which is governed not by what others do but by the action which I must face presently. And I don't know why I should use the word unexpected, unless because at the moment I was very far from expecting that sort of perplexity. The correct thing to say would be that something natural has happened.

===Chinese===
一件出乎意料的事改变了我思绪的流向。什么也改变不了它们的色调——那色调不由旁人做什么来决定，而是由我即将面对的那一行动来决定。我也不知道为何要用"出乎意料"这个词，除非是因为当时我万没想到会遇上这一类困惑。准确的说法应当是：发生了一件再自然不过的事。

===Original===
Perfectly natural. Asceticism is the last thing that one could expect from the Burdens. Alexander Burden, the father, was an exuberant millionaire, in no vulgar way, of course; he was exuberant with restraint, not for show, with a magnificence which was for private satisfaction mainly. I am talking here of the ascetic temperament which is based on renunciation, not of mere simplicity of tastes, which is simply scorn for certain orders of sensations. There have been millionaires who have lived simply. There have been millionaires who have lived sordidly—but miserliness is one of the supreme forms of sensualism.

===Chinese===
再自然不过。禁欲主义，是人们最不可能从伯登一家身上期望的东西。父亲亚历山大·伯登是个意气风发的百万富翁，当然并不庸俗；他的意气风发带着克制，不为炫示，其豪奢主要供自己私下满足。我这里说的是那种以弃绝为基础的禁欲气质，而非仅仅是趣味上的简朴——后者不过是对某些种类感觉的轻蔑。有过活得简朴的百万富翁。也有过活得寒酸的百万富翁——但吝啬乃是感官主义最高形式之一。

===Original===
Poor Burden had a magnificent physique. The reserved abilities of generations of impoverished Burdens, starved for want of opportunities, matured in his immense success—and all their starved appetites too. But all the reserve quality of obscure Burdens has been exhausted in him. There was nothing to come to his son—who at most could have been a great match and is today looked upon in that light, I suppose, by the relations of his future wife. I don't know in what light that young man looks upon himself. His time of trial is coming.

===Chinese===
可怜的伯登有一副堂堂的体魄。一代代穷困潦倒的伯登们被匮乏的机会所饿杀的潜能，在他巨大的成功中成熟了——连同他们所有被饿杀的欲望。但那些默默无闻的伯登们积攒下来的底子，已在他身上消耗殆尽。没有什么可以传到他的儿子——这儿子至多算得上一桩好姻缘，今日他未来妻子的亲属们，我想，也正是这般看他。我不知道这年轻人如何看待自己。他受试炼的时刻就要来了。

===Original===
Yesterday at eight in the evening he came to see me. I thought at first he wanted some money urgently. But very soon I reflected that he need not have looked so embarrassed in that case. And presently I discovered that it was not money that he was in need of. He looked as though he had come, with that characteristic gravity of his—so unlike his father—to seek absolution at my hands. But that intention he judged more decorous, I suppose, to present to me as a case of conscience.

===Chinese===
昨天晚上八点他来看我。起初我以为他急需用钱。但很快我便想到，若是那样，他不必显得那样局促。不久我便发现，他所需要的并非钱。他那副神气，仿佛是带着他特有的那种庄重——与他父亲截然不同——来我这里寻求赦免。但那个意图，我想，他认为以"良心问题"的名义呈到我面前更为得体。

===Original===
Of course it was the case of a girl—not his fiancée. At first I thought he was in an ugly scrape. Nothing of the kind. The excellent creature who had accepted his protection for some two years past—how dull they must have seemed to her—was perhaps for that reason perfectly resigned to forego that advantage. At the same time, she was not too proud to accept a certain provision, compensation—whatever you like to call it. I had never heard of anything so proper in my life. He need not have explained the matter to me at all. But evidently he had made up his mind to indulge in the luxury of a conscience.

===Chinese===
当然，那是一个姑娘的事——不是他的未婚妻。起初我以为他陷入了一桩难堪的困境。完全不是。那个过去大约两年间接受他庇护的出色尤物——他对她必定乏味得很——也许正因如此，才完全甘心放弃这一好处。与此同时，她也不至于清高到不肯接受一笔安置费、补偿金——你爱怎么叫都行。我这辈子从未听过比这更得体的事。他本完全不必向我解释。但他显然已下定决心，要享受一番良心的奢侈。

===Original===
To indulge that sort of conscience leads one almost as far as indulged passion, only, I cannot help thinking, on a more sordid road. A luxury snatched from the fire is in a way purified, but to find this one he had gone apparently to the bottom of his heart. I don't charge him with a particularly odious degree of corruption, but I perceived clearly that what he wanted really was to project the sinful effect of that irregular connection—let us call it—into his regulated, reformed, I may say lawfully blessed state—for the sake of retrospective enjoyment, I suppose. This rather subtle, if unholy, appetite, he was pleased to call the voice of his conscience. I listened to his dialectic exercises till the great word that was sure to come out sooner or later was pronounced.

"It seems," he said, with every appearance of distress, "that from a strictly moral point of view I ought to make a clean breast of it to Annie."

===Chinese===
纵容那种良心，几乎和纵容的激情一样把人引向远处，只不过——我忍不住想——走的是一条更为卑污的路。从火中抢出的奢侈，在某种意义上是被净化过的，但为了找到这一份奢侈，他显然已深挖到了自己的心底。我并不指控他腐化到了何等可憎的程度，但我清楚地察觉到，他真正想要的，是把那段不正经的关系——姑且这么称呼——的罪恶性，投射到他规规矩矩、改过自新、可说是合法受祝福的状态中去——我想，是为了事后的回味。这一颇为精微、虽说并不神圣的嗜好，他乐于称之为他良心的声音。我听着他的那些辩证练习，直到那个迟早要冒出来的大词终于被说了出来。

"看来，"他一脸痛苦地说，"从严格的道德立场看，我应当向安妮全盘坦白。"

===Original===
I listened to him—and, by Heaven, listening to him I *do* feel like the Godhead of whom I have already written to you. You know, positively he said that at the very moment of his "fall" he had thought of what *I* should think of him. And I said:

"My good Edward, you are the most debauched person I have ever met."

===Chinese===
我听他说着——而天哪，听他说着的时候，我*确实*觉得自己像我此前写信跟你提到的那位神明。你知道，他竟然说，在他"堕落"的那一刻，他想到了*我*会怎么看他。于是我说：

"我亲爱的爱德华，你是我所遇见过的最为放荡的人。"

===Original===
His face fell, his soft lips dropped right down into a horseshoe. He had come to me as one of those bland optimists *would* go to his deity. He expected to be able to say: "I have sinned," and to be able to hear the Deity say: "That's all right, your very frank confession does you infinite credit." His deity was, in fact, to find him some way out of his moral hole. I was to find him some genial excuse; to make him feel good in his excellent digestion once more. That was, absolutely, his point of view, for at my brutal pronouncement he stuttered:

"But—but surely… the faults of youth… and surely there are plenty of others?…"

===Chinese===
他的脸沉了下来，那两片柔软的嘴唇垂成了一个马蹄形。他来见我，正如那些温吞的乐天派*会*去见他的神明那样。他指望能够说一声："我犯了罪"，然后听见神明说："不要紧，你这番坦率的自白令你光彩无限。"他的神明，其实是要替他找个法子从他的道德困境里脱身。我要替他寻一个亲切的借口；让他那副消化良好的肠胃再度舒服起来。这完完全全就是他的想法，因为在我那句粗暴的判词面前，他结巴了：

"可——可 surely……年轻的过失嘛……再说这种人 surely 多的是吧？……"

===Original===
I shook my head at him and panic was dropping out of his eyes: "Can't I marry Annie honourably?" he quavered. I took a sinister delight in turning the knife inside him. I was going to let him go anyhow: the sort of cat that I am always lets its mice go. (That mouse, by the by, has never again put in an appearance.)

"My dear fellow," I said, "does not your delicacy let you see the hole you put me into? It's to my interest that you should not marry Miss Averies and you ask me to advise you on the point."

===Chinese===
我冲他摇了摇头，惊慌正从他眼中淌出："我就不能光明正大地娶安妮吗？"他颤声说。我怀着一种阴险的快意，在他体内转动那把刀。我横竖是要放过他的：我这种人，向来放了爪下的老鼠。（顺便说一句，那只老鼠此后再未现身。）

"我亲爱的朋友，"我说，"你的体贴心难道没让你看出你把我置于何地为？你不娶艾弗里丝小姐才符合我的利益，你却来请我就此给我出主意。"

===Original===
His mouth dropped open: positively he had never considered that when he married I lost the confounded three hundred a year for administering the Burden Trust. I sat and smiled at him to give him plenty of time to let his mind agonize over his position.

"Oh, hang it," he said.… And his silly eyes rolled round my room looking for that Providence that he felt ought to intervene in his behalf. When they rested on me again I said:

"There, go away. Of course it's a fault of your youth. Of course every man that's fit to call himself a man has seduced a clergyman's daughter."

===Chinese===
他嘴巴张得大大的：他显然从未想过，他一结婚，我便失去打理伯登信托那每年可恶的三百镑。我坐着朝他微笑，好让他有充裕的时间，让他的心智为其处境而辗转煎熬。

"哦，真见鬼。"他说。……他那双愚蠢的眼睛在我房间里转来转去，寻找他觉得理应为他出头的那位天意。当那双眼睛重新落到我身上时，我说：

"好了，走吧。那当然是年轻的过失。当然啦，凡是配称男子汉的人，都勾引过一位牧师的女儿。"

===Original===
He said:

"Oh, but there was not anything common about it."

"No," I answered, "you had an uncommonly good time of it with your moral scruples. I envy you the capacity. You'll have a duller one with Miss Averies, you know."

That was too much for him to take in, so he smoothed his hat.

===Chinese===
他说：

"哦，可那其中没有任何庸俗的成分。"

"是啊，"我答道，"你带着你的道德顾忌，过得好不快活。我真羡慕你这本事。跟艾弗里丝小姐在一起，日子可就要乏味多了，你知道的。"

这话他一下子消化不了，于是他抚了抚帽子。

===Original===
"When you said I was… debauched… you were only laughing at me. That was hardly fair. I'm tremendously in earnest."

"You're only playacting compared with me," I answered. He had the air of buttoning his coat after putting a cheque into his breast pocket. He had got, you see, the cheque he expected: my applause of his successful seduction, my envy of his good fortune. That was what he had come for—and he got it. He went away with it pretty barefacedly, but he stopped at the threshold to let drop:

"Of course if I had known you would be offended by my having recourse to Annie's solicitors for the settlement.…":

I told him I was laughing at him about that too.

"It was the correct thing to do, you know," were the words he shut the door upon. The ass.…

===Chinese===
"你说我……放荡……你只是在取笑我。这不太公平。我是极其认真的。"

"跟我相比，你不过是在演戏。"我答道。他那副神情，像极了把一张支票放进胸前内袋后扣上外套的人。你看，他得到了他期待的那张支票：我对他那次成功勾引的赞许，我对他好运的羡慕。那才是他来此的目的——而他得到了。他相当厚颜地带着它走了，但他在门槛处停下，抛下一句：

"当然啦，我要是早知道你对我求助于安妮的律师来办财产协议会不高兴……"

我告诉他，那件事我也在取笑他。

"那可是合乎规矩的做法，你知道的。"——他甩下这句话，带上了门。这蠢驴……

===Original===
The phrase of his—that he had thought of me at the moment of his fall—gives you at once the measure of his respect for me. But it gave me much more. It gave me my cue: it put it into my head to say he was debauched. And, indeed, that is debauchery. For it is the introduction of one's morals into the management of one's appetites that makes an indulgence of them debauchery. Had my friend Edward regarded his seduction as the thing he so much desired me to tell him it was; a thing of youth, high spirits—a thing we all do—had he so regarded it I could not really have called it debauchery. But—and this is the profound truth—the measure of debauchery is the amount of joy we get from the indulgence of our appetites. And the measure of joy we get is the amount of excitement: if it brings into play not only all our physical but all our moral nature then we have the crucial point beyond which no man can go. It isn't, in fact, the professional seducer, the artist in seduction that gets pleasure from the pursuit of his avocation, any more than it is the professional musician who gets thrills from the performance of music. You cannot figure to yourself the violinist, as he fiddles the most complicated passage of a concerto, when he really surmounts the difficulty by dint of using all his knowledge and all his skill—you cannot imagine him thinking of his adviser, his mother, his God and all the other things that my young friend says he thought about. And it is the same with the professional seducer. He may do all that he knows to bring his object about—but that is not debauchery. It is, by comparison, a joyless occupation: it is drinking when you are thirsty. Putting it in terms of the most threadbare allegory—you cannot imagine that Adam got out of the fall the pleasure that Edward Burden got out of his bite of the apple.

===Chinese===
他那句话——他在堕落的那一刻想到了我——一下子便向你道出了他对我的敬意之深浅。但它给我的远不止于此。它给了我的话头：使我想出说他"放荡"这个主意。而事实上，那确实是放荡。因为正是把一个人的道德引入对其欲望的管理之中，才使对这些欲望的纵容变成了放荡。倘若我的朋友爱德华把他的勾引看作他极力想让我承认的那样——一件属于年轻气盛的事、一件人人都会做的事——倘若他这样看待它，我便不能真称之为放荡。但是——而这一点才是深刻的真相——放荡的程度，取决于我们从纵容欲望中所获得的快乐的多少。而我们所获快乐的多少，便是兴奋的程度：如果它不仅调动了我们全部的身体本性，也调动了我们全部的道德本性，那么我们便抵达了那任何人都无法超越的临界点。事实上，从其营生中获得快感的，并非职业的勾引者、勾引的艺术家，正如从演奏音乐中获得颤栗的，并非职业的音乐家。你无法想象那位小提琴家，在他拉奏一首协奏曲中最繁复的段落、凭借全部学识与技艺真正克服了难关之际——你无法想象他此时还在想他的顾问、他的母亲、他的上帝，以及我那位年轻朋友自称当时想到的一切别的东西。职业的勾引者也是如此。他可以施展浑身解数来达成目的——但那并非放荡。相较之下，那是一项无趣的营生：是口渴时的饮水。用最陈腐的寓言来说——你无法想象亚当从那次堕落中所获得的快感，能比得上爱德华·伯登从他那一口苹果中所获得的。

===Original===
But Edward Burden, whilst he shilly-shallied with "Shall I?" and "Shan't I?" could deliciously introduce into the matter *all* his human relationships. He could think of me, of his mother, of the fact that potentially he was casting to the winds the very cause for his existence. For assuredly, if Edward Burden have a cause for existence it is that he should not, morally or physically, do anything that would unfit him to make a good marriage. So he had, along with whatever physical pleasure there might be, the immense excitement of staking his all along with the tremendous elation of the debate within himself that went before. For he was actually staking his all upon the chance that he could both take what he desired and afterwards reconcile it with his conscience to make a good match. Well, he has staked and won. That is the true debauchery. That, in a sense, is the compensating joy that Puritanism gets.

===Chinese===
但爱德华·伯登，在他"要不要呢？""不要吧？"地犹豫不决之际，可以津津有味地把*全部*人际关系引入这件事中来。他可以想到我、想到他的母亲、想到他实际上正把自己存在的理由抛到九霄云外。因为可以肯定，假如爱德华·伯登有一个存在的理由，那就是他无论在道德上还是身体上，都不应做任何有损他结一门好姻缘的事。于是，在可能有的任何肉体快感之外，他还获得了一种巨大的兴奋——孤注一掷的兴奋，连同此前内心那场辩论所带来的巨大亢奋。因为他实际上是在拿自己的一切去赌：赌他既能得到他所渴望的，事后又能与自己的良心和解、结下一门好亲。好吧，他赌了，也赢了。那才是真正的放荡。在某种意义上，那正是清教主义所获得的补偿性快感。
