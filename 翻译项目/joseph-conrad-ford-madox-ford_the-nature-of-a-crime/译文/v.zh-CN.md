## V / 五

===Original===
I have just come in. Again you will not guess from where. From choosing a motorcar with Burden and his fiancée. It seems incredible that I should be called upon to preside at these preparations for my own execution. I looked at hundreds of these shiny engines, with the monstrously inflated white wheels, and gave a half-amused—but I can assure you a half-interested—attention to my own case. For one of these will one day—and soon now—be arrested in a long rush, by my extinction. In it there will be seated the two young people who went with me through the garages. They will sit in some sort of cushioned ease—the cushions will be green, or red, or blue in shiny leather. I think, however, that they will not be green—because Miss Averies let slip to me, in a little flutter of shy confidence, the words: "Oh, don't let's have green, because it's an unlucky colour." Edward Burden, of course, suppressed her with a hurried whisper as if, in thus giving herself away to me, she must be committing a sin against the house of Burden.

===Chinese===
我刚回来。这一次你也猜不出从哪里。从陪伯登和他未婚妻挑汽车回来。我竟被召来主持自己行刑前的种种准备，这实在令人难以置信。我看着上百辆亮闪闪的引擎、配着那大得畸形、鼓鼓的白轮子，半是好笑地——但我向你保证，也是半是饶有兴致地——关注着我自己的案子。因为其中某一辆，有朝一日——而且如今已不远了——将在一阵长长的疾驰中，因我的消亡而骤停。车里将坐着与我一同走过那些车行的两个年轻人。他们将以某种软垫的安逸坐定——那些软垫将是绿的、或红的、或蓝的亮面皮革。不过我想，它们不会是绿的——因为艾弗里丝小姐在一阵羞怯信赖的小小悸动中，对我漏出了一句："哦，咱们别挑绿的吧，因为那是不吉利的颜色。"爱德华·伯登当然急忙压低声音制止了她，仿佛她这样向我交了底，便是对伯登家族犯了罪。

===Original===
That, naturally, is the Burden tradition: a Burden's wife must possess frailties: but she must feign perfection even to a trusted adviser of the family. She must not confess to superstitions. It was amusing, the small incident, because it was the very first attempt that little Miss Averies has ever made to get near me. God knows what Edward may have made me appear to her: but I fancy that, whatever Edward may have said, she had pierced through that particular veil: she realizes, with her intuition, that I am dangerous. She is alarmed and possibly fascinated because she feels that I am not "straight"—that I might, in fact, be a woman or a poet. Burden, of course, has never got beyond seeing that I dress better than he does and choose a dinner better than his uncle Darlington.

===Chinese===
那自然便是伯登家的传统：伯登家的妻子可以有种种弱点：但她必须对家族最信赖的顾问也装出完美无缺的样子。她不可承认自己有迷信。这一桩小事颇有趣味，因为那是小小的艾弗里丝小姐为接近我所做的头一次尝试。天知道爱德华在她面前把我描绘成了什么样子：但我想，无论爱德华说过什么，她都已看穿了那一层帷幕：她凭直觉意识到，我是危险的。她惊惶，也许还着了迷，因为她感觉到我并不"直"——事实上，我也许是个女人，或是个诗人。伯登当然从未超出这一点：他只看得到我比他穿得好、比他叔叔达灵顿更会点菜。

===Original===
I came, of course, out of the motorcar ordeal with flying colours—on these lines. I lived, in fact, up to my character for being orthodox in the matter of comfort. I even suggested two little mirrors, like those which were so comforting to us all when we sat in hansom cabs. That struck Burden as being the height of ingenuity—and I know it proved to Miss Averies, most finally, that I am dangerous, since no woman ever looks in those little mirrors without some small motive of coquetry. It was just after that that she said to me:

"Don't you think that the little measures on the tops of the new canisters are extravagant for China tea?"

===Chinese===
当然，我从这场汽车的考验中凯旋而归——在这些方面。事实上，我不负我在舒适方面向来正统的名声。我甚至提议装两面小镜子，就像我们坐双轮马车时那般令人心安的小镜。伯登觉得这是巧思的巅峰——而我知道，这对艾弗里丝小姐最彻底地证明了我是个危险人物，因为没有一个女子照那种小镜子时不带一丝小小的卖弄之心的。正是在那之后，她对我说：

"您不觉得新茶罐顶上那些小量器，用来量中国茶，太奢侈了吗？"

===Original===
That, of course, admitted me to the peculiar intimacy that women allow to other women, or to poets, or to dangerous men. Edward, I know, dislikes the drinking of China tea because it is against the principle of supporting the British flag. But Miss Averies in her unequal battle with this youth of the classical features slightly vulgarized, called me in to show a sign of sympathy—to give at least the flicker of the other side—of the woman, the poet, or the pessimist among men. She asked me, in fact, not to take up the cudgels to the extent of saying that China tea is the thing to drink—that would have been treason to Edward—but she desired that her instinct should be acknowledged to the extent of saying that the measures of canisters should be contrived to suit the one kind of tea as well as the other. In his blind sort of way Edward caught the challenge in the remark and his straight brows lowered a very little.

"If you don't have more than three pounds of China tea in the house in a year it won't matter about the measures," he said. "We never use more at Shackleton."

"But it makes the tea too strong, Edward."

"Then you need not fill the measure," he answered.

"Oh, I wish," she said to me, "that you'd tell Edward not to make me make tea at all. I dread it. The servants do it so much better."

===Chinese===
那当然把我纳入了一种女子对别的女子、或对诗人、或对危险男子才会给予的特殊亲昵。我知道，爱德华不喜欢喝中国茶，因为那违背了拥护英国旗帜的原则。但艾弗里丝小姐在与这个五官端正却略带俗气的年轻人的这场力量悬殊的较量中，把我拉进来以示一点同情——至少闪现一下另一面的光——女人的一面、诗人的一面、或男人中悲观主义者的一面。她其实并非请我拔刀相助到宣称中国茶才该喝的地步——那对爱德华便是叛国——她只是希望她的直觉获得承认，承认茶罐的量器本应设计得同样适合这一种茶。爱德华以他那种浑然的方式察觉到了这话里的挑战，他那两道直眉微微压低了些。

"你一年里在家里用不了三磅以上的中国茶，量器的事就无所谓。"他说。"我们在沙克尔顿从没用超过这个数。"

"可那样泡出来的茶太浓了，爱德华。"

"那你就别把量器装满。"他答道。

"哦，我真希望，"她对着我，"您能劝劝爱德华，干脆别让我泡茶了。我怕得很。仆人们泡得好多了。"

===Original===
"So," I asked, "Edward has arranged everything down to the last detail?"

Edward looked to me for approval and applause.

"You see, Annie has had so little experience, and I've had to look after my mother's house for years." His air said: "Yes! You'll see our establishment will be run on the very best lines! Don't you admire the way I'm taming her already?"

===Chinese===
"那么，"我问，"爱德华已经把一切安排到最细的末节了？"

爱德华望着我，等着我的赞许和喝彩。

"你看，安妮没什么经验，而我打理我母亲的家已有好些年了。"他那副神气在说："没错吧！你将看到我们那个家会照着最上乘的路子来办！你难道不佩服我这么快就驯服了她吗？"

===Original===
I gave him, of course, a significant glance. Heaven knows why: for it is absolutely true that I am tired of appearing reliable—to Edward Burden or anyone else in the world. What I want to do is simply to say to Edward Burden: "No, I don't at all admire your dragging down a little bundle of ideals and sentiments to your own fatted calf's level."

===Chinese===
我当然回了他一个意味深长的眼色。天知道为什么：因为千真万确的是，我已厌倦了在任何人——爱德华·伯登或这世上任何旁人——面前显得可靠。我所想做的，不过是对爱德华·伯登说："不，我丝毫不欣赏你把一小束理想与情愫拖到你那头圈肥的牛犊的水平。"

===Original===
I suppose I have in me something of the poet. I can imagine that if I had to love or to marry this little Averies girl I should try to find out what was her tiny vanity and I should minister to it. In some way I should discover from her that she considered herself charming, or discreet, or tasteful, or frivolous, beyond all her fellows. And, having discovered it, I should bend all my energies to giving her opportunities for displaying her charm, her discreetness or her coquetry. With a woman of larger and finer mould—with you!—I should no doubt bring into play my own idealism. I should invest her with the attributes that I consider the most desirable in the world. But in either case I cannot figure myself dragging her down to my own social or material necessities.

===Chinese===
我想我身上有一点诗人的气质。我可以想象，假若我不得不爱、或娶这个小艾弗里丝姑娘，我会试着找出她那一点微小的虚荣，并加以侍奉。我总会有办法从她那里发现，她自认比她所有同伴都更迷人、或更审慎、或更有品味、或更轻灵。而一旦发现了它，我便会倾尽全力为她创造机会，去施展她的魅力、她的审慎或她的娇嗔。换了一个格局更大、更精微的女子——比如你！——我无疑会调动起我自己的理想主义。我会赋予她我所认为这世上最可向往的那些品质。但无论哪种情形，我都无法想象自己把她拖到我的社交或物质需求的水准上来。

===Original===
That is what Edward Burden is doing for little Miss Averies. I don't mean to say that he does not idealize her—but he sees her transfigured as the dispenser of his special brand of tea or the mother of the sort of child that he was. And that seems to me a very valid reason why women, if they were wise, should trust their fortunes cold-bloodedly and of set reason to the class of dangerous men that now allure them and that they flee from.

===Chinese===
这正是爱德华·伯登对小艾弗里丝小姐所做的。我不是说他不把她理想化——但他所看到的那个被圣化的她，是他那种特制茶叶的斟奉者，是他那种模样的孩子的母亲。而在我看来，这恰恰是一个十分正当的理由，说明女子若是聪明，便应当冷静地、出于理智地把命运托付给如今既诱惑她们、又被她们逃离的那一类危险男子。

===Original===
They flee from them, I am convinced, because they fear for their worldly material fortunes. They fear, that is to say, that the poet is not a stable man of business: they recognise that he is a gambler—and it seems to them that it is folly to trust to a gambler for lifelong protection. In that they are perhaps right. But I think that no woman doubts her power to retain a man's affection—so that it is not to the reputation for matrimonial instability that the poet owes his disfavour. A woman lives, in short, to play with this particular fire, since to herself she says: "Here is a man who has broken the hearts of many women. I will essay the adventure of taming him." And, if she considers the adventure a dangerous one, that renders the contest only the more alluring, since at heart every woman, like every poet, is a gambler. In that perhaps she is right.

===Chinese===
我深信，她们逃离他们，是因为担心自己在世俗物质上的命运。也就是说，她们担心诗人不是个可靠的生意人：她们承认他是个赌徒——在她们看来，把终身的庇护托付给一个赌徒，便是愚蠢。在这一点上她们也许没错。但我想，没有哪个女子会怀疑自己留住一个男人爱意的能力——所以诗人之所以不受青睐，并非因为他在婚姻上不稳定的名声。简而言之，一个女子活着，就是要玩这一种火，因为她对自己说："这个男人伤过许多女人的心。我要尝试驯服他这一场冒险。"而倘若她觉得这场冒险危险，那只会使这场较量更加诱人，因为每一个女子，正如每一个诗人，骨子里都是赌徒。在这点上她也许是对的。

===Original===
But it seems to me that women make a great mistake in the value of the stakes they are ready to pay in order to enter this game. They will stake, that is to say, their relatively great coin—their sentimental lives; but they hoard with closed fingers the threepenny bit which is merely the material future.

They prefer, that is to say, to be rendered the mere presiding geniuses of well-loaded boards. It is better to be a lady—which you will remember philologically means a "loaf-cutter"—than to be an Ideal.

===Chinese===
但在我看来，女子们为了加入这场赌局而愿意付出的赌注，其价值上有一个大错。也就是说，她们愿意押上她们相对大的硬币——她们的情感生活；却攥紧手指，吝惜那只不过关系到物质未来的三便士硬币。

也就是说，她们宁可只做一张摆满佳肴的餐桌的主持天才。做一位"夫人"——你当记得，从词源学上讲，这个字意为"切面包的人"——总好过做一个理想。

===Original===
And in this they are obviously wrong. If a woman can achieve the obvious miracle of making a dangerous man stable in his affections she may well be confident that she can persuade him to turn his serious attention to the task of keeping a roof over her head. Certainly, I know, if I were a woman which of the two types of men I would choose. Upon the lowest basis it is better for all purposes of human contracts to be married to a good liar than to a bad one. For a lie is a figurative truth—and it is the poet who is the master of these illusions. Even in the matter of marital relations it is probable that the poet is as faithful as the Edward Burdens of this world—only the Edward Burdens are more skilful at concealing from the rest of the world their pleasant vices. I doubt whether they are as skilful at concealing them from the woman concerned—from the woman, with her intuition, her power to seize fine shades of coolness and her awakened self-interest. Imagine the wife of Edward Burden saying to him, "You have deceived me!" Imagine then the excellent youth, crimsoning, stuttering. He has been taught all his life that truth must prevail though the skies fall—and he stammers: "Yes: I have betrayed you." And that is tragedy, though in the psychological sense—and that is the important one—Edward Burden may have been as faithful as the ravens, who live for fifteen decades with the same mate. He will, in short, blunder into a tragic, false position. And he will make the tragedy only the more tragic in that all the intellectual powers he may possess will be in the direction of perpetuating the dismal position. He will not be able to argue that he has not been unfaithful—but he will be able to find a hundred arguments for the miserable woman prolonging her life with him. Position, money, the interests of the children, the feelings of her family and of his—all these considerations will make him eloquent to urge her to prolong her misery. And probably she will prolong it.

===Chinese===
而在这点上她们显然错了。倘若一个女子能实现那一显然的奇迹——使一个危险男子在感情上稳定下来——她便大可自信，能说服他把严肃的注意力转向"为她撑起一片屋顶"这一任务。我当然知道，假如我是女子，我会在两类男子中选哪一类。即便从最低的立足点说，就人类一切契约的目的而言，嫁给一个善于说谎的人也胜过嫁给一个不善说谎的人。因为谎言是一种比喻性的真实——而诗人正是这些幻象的大师。即便在婚姻关系上，诗人很可能与这世上的爱德华·伯登们一样忠贞——只不过爱德华·伯登们更善于向世人掩饰他们那些令人愉快的恶习。我却怀疑，他们是否同样善于向那个有关的女子掩饰——向那个凭着直觉、凭着捕捉细微冷淡之色和已被唤醒的自利之心的女子。想象爱德华·伯登的妻子对他说："你欺骗了我！"再想象那个优秀青年涨红了脸、结结巴巴的样子。他从小受的教育便是：天塌下来真理也要伸张——于是他嗫嚅道："是的：我辜负了你。"这便是悲剧了，尽管从心理学的意义上——而这才是重要的意义——爱德华·伯登也许像渡鸦一样忠贞，那种鸟与同一伴侣相守一百五十年。简而言之，他将跌跌撞撞地陷进一个悲剧性的、虚假的处境。而他只会使这悲剧更加悲惨，因为他可能拥有的一切心智力量，都将用于延续这个阴郁的处境。他将无法辩称自己未曾不忠——但他将能找到上百条理由，劝说那个可怜的女子继续与他共度。地位、金钱、子女的利益、她家人和他家人的感受——所有这些考量都将使他能言善辩地劝她延续她的痛苦。而她多半会延续下去。

===Original===
This, of course, is due to the excellent Edward's lack of an instinctive sympathy. The poet, with a truer vision, will in the same case, be able to face his Miss Averies' saying: "You have deceived me!" with a different assurance. Supposing the deflection to have been of the momentary kind he will be able to deny with a good conscience since he will be aware of himself and his feelings. He will at least be able to put the case in its just light. Or, if the deflection be really temperamental, really permanent, he will be unable—it being his business to look at the deeper verities—to lie himself out of the matter. He will break, strictly and sharply. Or, if he do not, it may be taken as a sign that his Miss Averies is still of value to him—that she, in fact, is still the woman that it is his desire to have for his companion. This is true, of course, only in the large sense, since obviously there are poets whose reverence for position, the interest of children or the feelings of their friends and relatives, may outweigh their hatred of a false position. These, however, are poets in the sense that they write verse: I am speaking of those who live the poet's life; to such, a false position is too intolerable to be long maintained.

===Chinese===
这当然要归咎于那位优秀的爱德华缺乏一种出于本能的同情。而诗人，凭着更真切的洞察，在同样的情形下，将能以另一种笃定，面对他的艾弗里丝小姐那句"你欺骗了我！"假如那次偏离只是一时的，他便能问心无愧地否认，因为他了解自己和他的情感。他至少能将事情放在它恰当的光照下来陈述。又或者，假如那偏离确属性情上的、确属持久的，他便无法——因为正视更深层的真实乃他的本分——用谎言把自己摘出去。他会干脆利落地决裂。又或者，假如他不决裂，那便可视为一个信号：他的艾弗里丝小姐对他仍有价值——事实上，她仍是那个他渴望与之相伴的女子。当然，这只是在宽泛意义上成立，因为显然有些诗人，对地位、子女的利益或亲友感受的顾忌，可能压过了他们对虚伪处境的憎恶。然而那些只是在写诗这个意义上才算诗人的诗人；我说的是那些过着诗人生活的人；对他们而言，虚伪的处境太过难以忍受，无法长久维持。

===Original===
But this again is only one of innumerable side-issues: let me return to my main contention that a dinner of herbs with a dangerous man is better than having to consume the flesh of stalled oxen with Edward Burden. Perhaps that is only a way of saying that you would have done better to entrust yourself to me than to—(But no, your husband is a better man than Edward Burden. He has at least had the courage to revert to his passion. I went this afternoon to your chemists and formally notified them that if they supplied him with more than the exactly prescribed quantity of that stuff, I, as holding your power of attorney, should do all that the law allows me to do against them.)

===Chinese===
但这又不过是无数枝节问题之一：让我回到我的主要论点吧——与一个危险男子同食蔬菜之宴，也胜过与爱德华·伯登共啖圈栏肥牛之肉。也许这不过是另一种说法，说你当初若是把自己托付给我、而不是托付给——（但不，你丈夫是个比爱德华·伯登更好的人。他至少有勇气回到他的激情。今天下午我去了一趟你常去的药店，正式通知他们：假如他们再供应给他超出处方精确规定剂量的那种东西，我作为持有你委托书的人，将对他们采取一切法律所允许的行动。）

===Original===
Even to the dullest of men, marrying is for the most part an imaginative act. I mean marrying as a step in life sanctioned by law, custom and that general consent of mankind which is the hallmark of every irrational institution. By irrational I do not mean wrong or stupid. Marriage is august by the magnitude of the issues it involves, balancing peace and strife on the fine point of a natural impulse refined by the need of a tangible ideal. I am not speaking here of mere domestic peace or strife which for most people that count are a question of manners and a mode of life. And I am thinking of the peace mostly—the peace of the soul which yearns for some sort of certitude in this earth, the peace of the heart which yearns for conquest, the peace of the senses that dreads deception, the peace of the imaginative faculty which in its restless quest of a high place of rest is spurred on by these great desires and that great fear.

===Chinese===
即便对最迟钝的男子而言，结婚在很大程度上也是一种富于想象力的行为。我说的是作为人生中一步、受法律习俗认可、受人类普遍同意（那是每一种非理性制度的标志）所认可的结婚。所谓非理性，我并非指错误或愚蠢。婚姻因其所涉问题之重大而庄严——它在一个被某种切实理想所升华的自然冲动之尖端，权衡着和平与纷争。我在这里说的不是单纯的家常和气或龃龉，对大多数算得上数的人而言，那不过是举止与生活方式的问题。我主要想到的是那种和平——灵魂渴望在这世上某种确定的和平，心灵渴望征服的和平，感官畏惧受骗的和平，那不安地寻觅一处高远安息之所的想象力，被这些巨大渴望和那种巨大恐惧所鞭策的和平。

===Original===
And even Edward Burden's imagination is moved by these very desires and that very fear—or else he would not have dreamt of marrying. I repeat, marriage is an imaginative institution. It's true that his imagination is a poor thing but it is genuine nevertheless. The faculty of which I speak is of one kind in all of us. Not to everyone is given that depth of feeling, that faculty of absolute trust which *will not* be deceived, and the exulting masterfulness of the senses which are the mark of a fearless lover. Fearless lovers are rare, if obstinate, and sensual fools are countless as grains of sand by the seashore.
