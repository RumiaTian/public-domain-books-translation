## VI Atra Tells of How They Three Came Unto the Isle of Increase Unsought / VI 阿特拉讲述她们三人如何来到不期而获之岛
===Original===

We were born and bred in the land that lies southwest along this Great Water, and we waxed happily, and became fellows when we were yet but children, and thus grew up dear friends into maidenhood and womanhood. We were wooed by many men, but our hearts turned to none of them save unto three, who were goodly, kind, and valiant; and thou mayst call them the Golden Knight, who is Aurea’s man; the Green Knight, who is man of Viridis; and my man, the Black Squire. But in this was unhap, that because of certain feuds which had endured from old time, this love was perilous unto them and us; so that we lived in doubt and unrest.
Came a day, now three years ago, when the king of the whole land brought his folk into our lakeside country, and there held a court and a mote in a fair great meadow anigh to the water. But even as the mote was hallowed, and the Peace of God proclaimed at the blast of the war-horn, came we three woeful ladies clad in black and knelt before the lord king, and prayed him hearken us. And he deemed that we were fair, so he had compassion on us, and raised us up, and bade us speak.
So we told our tale, how that strife and wounds and death stood betwixt us and love; and we wept, and bewailed it, that our love must be slain because men were wroth with each other and not with us.
The king looked on us kindly, and said: Who be the swains for whom these lovely damsels make such a piece of work? So we named them, and said that they were there in the mote; and the king knew them for valiant men who had done him good service; and he cried out their names, and bade them stand forth out of the throng. So forth they stood, the Golden Knight, the Green Knight, and the Black Squire (and he also was now a knight); but now were they all three clad in black, and they were unarmed, save for their swords girt to their sides, without which no man amongst us may come to the mote, be he baron or earl or duke, or the very lord king himself.
So the king looked upon us and them, and laughed and said: Fair ladies, ye have got me by the nose, so needs must my body follow. Do ye three knights, whom I know for valiant men and true, take each his love by the hand, and let the weddings be tomorrow. Who then were joyful but us?

===Chinese===

我们生在、长在这大水西南岸边的土地上，我们快乐地成长，还是孩童时便结为伙伴，就这样相亲相爱，从少女长成了妇人。向咱们求婚的男人很多，可我们的心只向着三个人，他们都俊美、善良而英勇；你可以称他们为金甲骑士，他是奥蕾亚的人；绿骑士，是维里迪斯的人；还有我的人，黑衣侍从。但不幸在于：因着几桩自古延续下来的仇怨，这份爱情对他们和我们都是危险的；于是我们生活在疑惧与不安之中。
三年前的一天，全境之王率领他的部众来到我们湖畔的乡野，在临水的一片美丽大草地上开设法庭、举行民众大会。正当大会宣告神圣、随着战号的吹响宣布上帝的和平之时，我们三个哀伤的女子穿着黑衣前来，跪在王上面前，恳求他垂听我们。他觉得我们貌美，便动了怜悯之心，把我们扶起来，命我们陈说。
于是我们讲述了我们的遭遇：争斗、创伤与死亡如何横亘在我们与爱情之间；我们哭泣着，悲叹我们的爱情竟必须被扼杀，只因男人们彼此结怨，而不是与我们为仇。
王慈和地望着我们，说道：这几位可爱的姑娘为之这般兴师动众的情郎是些什么人？我们便说出他们的名字，说他们就在会场上；王知道他们都是为他立过功勋的勇士，便高声唱名，命他们从人群中站出来。于是金甲骑士、绿骑士和黑衣侍从（他如今也已是骑士了）站了出来；三人都穿着黑衣，未带甲胄，只有佩在腰间的剑——在我们那里，无论男爵、伯爵还是公爵，乃至王上本人，不佩剑是不得进入会场的。
王看看我们，又看看他们，笑道：美丽的女士们，你们牵住了我的鼻子，我的身子也只好跟着走了。你们三位骑士——我知道你们是忠勇之士——各自牵起你们爱人的手吧，婚礼就定在明天。除了我们，还有谁比我们更欢喜呢？

But even at the word the king spake arose great turmoil in the mote, for they smote the feud and contention awake, and men thronged forward against each other, and swords were drawn and brandished. But the king arose in his place and spake long and deftly, and waxed exceeding wroth, while none heeded him nor hearkened. And there stood our three men, who laid no hand to hilt, but abode heart-whole by seeming amid the tumult. And lovely they were to look on. At last the wise men and old barons went between, and by fair words appeased the trouble, and the mote grew hushed. Then spake the king: What is this, my thanes? I had deemed that my foemen were far away, and that ye that here are were all friends unto me and unto each other. But now must we try another rede. Therewith he turned unto our men and said: Ye champions, are ye so much in love with Love that ye will fight for him? They all yeasaid that, and then the king said: Then do I declare that these three will hold the field against all comers from matins till high noon, and that he who vanquisheth any one of them shall have his lady and wed her if he will, and, if he will, shall ransom her. And this field shall be foughten after two months’ frist in these fair meadows, when I return from the outermost marches of the south, whereto I am now wending. But when the battle is done, then let all men bow to the judgment of God, whether he be well content or not, and this on peril of life and limb. And now let there be deep peace between all men meanwhile; and if any break the peace, be he high or low, rich or unrich, churl or earl, I swear it by the souls of my fathers that he shall lose nought save his life therefor.
At these words was there a rumour of yeasay, and all men were content, save we three poor maidens, into whose hearts had now entered fear of loss and death.

===Chinese===

可就在王说话的当口，会场中掀起大乱，宿仇与争端被敲醒了，人们相互汹涌推挤，长剑纷纷出鞘挥舞。王从座位上起身，长篇大论、言辞恳切，怒火中烧，却无人理会、无人听从。我们的三位男子站在那里，手不碰剑柄，在骚乱中看似心境泰然。他们看起来真是英姿动人。终于，智者与老男爵们出面调停，用好言平息了风波，会场安静下来。于是王说道：这是怎么回事，我的臣属们？我本以为我的仇敌远在他方，以为在场的诸位都是我和彼此的朋友。如今只得另谋一策。说着他转向我们的男子：你们这几位勇士，难道如此热恋着爱情，肯为它而战么？三人都称是，王便说：那么我宣告：这三人将从晨祷时分起至正午，在此场上迎战一切来者；凡击败他们中任何一人者，即得他的爱人，愿意娶便娶她，不愿娶也可索取赎金。这场比武定于两个月后、我自南方极边返回之时，在此草地上进行。比武既毕，无论满不满意，所有人皆须服从上帝的裁决，违者性命肢体不保。从今直至那时，众人之间须保持深度和平；若有破坏和平者，无论贵贱贫富、平民或伯爵，我以我列祖列宗的灵魂起誓：他为此失去的，无非性命而已。
听了这话，会场响起一片赞同之声，人人都满意了，只有我们三个可怜的少女例外——丧失与死亡的恐惧已经钻进了我们心里。

But our kindreds on both sides were glad and proud, and they were not so bitter against us as they had been; they put hand to pouch, and let rear for us a fair pavilion of painted timber, all hung with silk and pictured cloths and Saracen tapestry, by the very lakeside; and gay boats gaily bedight lay off the said pavilion for our pleasure; and when all was done, it yet lacked a half month of the day of battle, and thither were we brought in triumph by the kindreds on a fair day of May, and there was not a sword or a spear amongst the whole company, and peaceful and merry was all by seeming. But we were not suffered to meet our lovers all this while, from the time when the mote was.
Now on a day came a messenger on the spur, and did us to wit that the king would be with us on the morrow, and that the day after, the fateful field should be foughten. Then, though the coming of this day had been so longed for by us, yet now it was at hand it cast us into all unrest and trouble, so that we scarce knew whether to go, or stand, or sit, or what to do with our bodies. Our folk, and all other men withal, were so busy making ready for the morrow of tomorrow, that they left us alone to wear through the day as we might.
Now it was afternoon, and the day hot and hazy, and we stood on the very lip of the land wearied with hope and fear, and striving to keep good countenance to each other; and there came a boat unto the shore gaily painted and gilded, and bedight with silken cloths and cushions; and the steerer thereof was a woman, not young, by seeming of fifty winters; red-haired she was, thin-lipped and narrow-eyed, flat-breasted and strait-hipped; an ungoodly woman, though her skin was white and smooth as for her age. Hast thou ever seen such an one, guest? Said Birdalone, smiling: Forsooth that have I; for such an one is my mistress to behold.
Well, said Atra, this dame stretched out her hands to us, and said: Will not the pretty ladies, the dear ladies, who have nought on hand this afternoon, come into my boat and look on the face of the water, so calm and fair as it is, and let their lovely hands go over the gunwale and play with the ripple, and so beguile this heavy time for a two hours; and then give a little gift of a piece or two of silver to a poor carline, who loveth all fair ladies and bright warriors, and who needeth a little livelihood?

===Chinese===

但我们两家的亲族又欢喜又骄傲，他们不像从前那样刻薄地对我们了；他们纷纷解囊，在紧临湖边之处为我们搭起一座彩木凉阁，四壁悬挂丝绸、画布与撒拉森织锦，一艘艘装点华美的游船泊在凉阁外供我们取乐；一切齐备时，离比武之日还有半个月。五月里一个晴朗的日子，亲族们风风光光地把我们送到那里，全队人马中没有一刀一矛，表面上处处平和欢快。可是自大会之日起，这段时间里一直不许我们与爱人相见。
一日，一名信使快马而来，告知我们王将于明日到达，后天便进行那场决定命运的比武。这一天的到来虽是我们久盼的，可它近在眼前时，我们却陷入极大的不安与慌乱，几乎不知该走、该站还是该坐，不知这身子如何安放。我们的人和所有旁人都在忙着为后日做准备，便任由我们自顾自捱过这一天。
那是午后，天气又热又雾蒙蒙的，我们站在岸边，被希望与恐惧折磨得疲惫不堪，还强撑着在彼此面前装出好脸色；这时一艘船靠了岸，船身彩绘描金，缀着绸布和坐垫；掌舵的是个女人，不年轻了，看上去约莫五十岁；红头发，薄嘴唇，眯缝眼，胸脯扁平，臀部干瘠；虽说皮肤以她的年纪还算白净光洁，总归是个相貌不讨喜的女人。客人，你可曾见过这样的人？鸟独微笑道：确实（Forsooth）见过；我的女主人看上去正是这样一位。

Now the woman seemed nought lovely unto us, and to me forsooth she seemed hateful; but we looked on each other, and we found that we were utterly weary of going up and down on the meadow, and lying about in the pavilion, and it seemed as if this would give us a little rest; withal we saw not that the woman could do us any hurt, whereas we were three, and strong enough as women go; nor were we mariners so evil but that we might sail or steer a boat at a pinch. So we stepped into the boat straightway, and the woman sat aft and paddled deftly with the steering oar, and we glided away from the land.
Soon we were come so far that we could but just see our pavilion through the haze, which had somewhat thickened, and we said to the woman that she should go about and make for the shore, and that then we would go to and fro a while along by our stead. She nodded yeasay, and began by seeming to dight the craft for return. But therewith the haze was grown suddenly into a low cloud, which came down upon us from the southwest in the arms of a cold breeze, that grew stronger every minute, so no wonder it was though the steerer might not keep head to wind; and then who was afraid and ashamed save ourselves?
But the woman said, and there seemed to be a mock in her voice: Ill luck, pretty ladies! Now is there nought for it but to drive, if we would not drown. But belike this duskiness will clear presently, and then at least we shall know whither we be going; and we may either turn back, or seek some other shelter, for I know the lake well; I know, I know.
We were too terror-stricken to speak, for we felt that still the wind grew stronger, and the lake began to rise into waves, and the craft to wallow; but well-nigh therewith was the dusk and the mist gone; the sky was bright blue overhead, and the westering sun shone cloudless; but on no land it shone, or on aught save the blue waters and the white wave-crests.
Then wept Aurea, and this Viridis here, but as to me, I grew wroth and cried out to the steerer: Accursed carline! thou hast betrayed us; never now may we get back to our pavilion till the fight is foughten, and our lovers will deem that we have forsaken them, and we are shamed forever. Well, well, said the carline, what remedy save patience for the winds and waves? And she laughed mockingly.

===Chinese===

这女人在我们看来毫无可爱之处，在我眼里简直是可憎的；但我们彼此对望，发觉我们实在厌倦了在草地上走来走去、在凉阁里东躺西卧，这趟船似乎能让我们稍稍歇息；再者我们也看不出这女人能把我们怎样，何况我们有三个人，就女子而言也算身强力壮；我们的驾船本领也不算太糟，急切间操帆掌舵总还应付得来。于是我们径直上了船，那女人坐在船尾，灵巧地划着舵桨，我们便离岸滑行了。
不久我们便行到那么远，透过渐浓的雾霭，凉阁已只剩隐约一点；我们对那女人说，该掉转船头驶回岸去了，回去后我们还要在住处附近再来回走走。她点头应允，看样子开始张罗着调转船身。可就在这时，雾霭骤然凝成一片低云，裹着一股冷风从西南方压到我们头上，风势一分钟比一分钟强劲，难怪掌舵人再也无法让船头顶风；这时候，又惊又愧的除了我们自己还有谁呢？
那女人开口了，话音里似乎带着讥诮：真倒霉，美丽的女士们！如今别无他法，只能随风漂了，不然就得淹死。不过这阴翳说不定马上就会散去，到那时我们至少知道自己身在何方；我们可以掉头回去，或者另寻一处避风的地方，这湖我很熟；我很熟，很熟。
我们惊吓得说不出话，因为只觉风势愈发强劲，湖面开始掀起波浪，船身颠簸摇摆；但几乎就在同时，昏暗与迷雾消散了；头顶天空湛蓝，西斜的太阳朗照无云；但它照不到任何陆地，照到的唯有蓝水与白浪。
奥蕾亚哭了，还有这位维里迪斯；至于我，我动了怒，朝掌舵的大声喊：可诅咒的老婆子！你出卖了我们；比武之前我们再也回不去凉阁了，我们的爱人会以为我们抛弃了他们，我们永远蒙羞了。好啦，好啦，老婆子说，对付风浪，除了忍耐还有什么法子？她讥嘲地笑了。

Quoth I: There is this remedy, that we three arise and lay hands on thee, and cast thee outboard, save thou straightway turn the boat’s head and back to the main. Forsooth I doubt not but that as thou hast raised this foul wind against us, thou canst raise a fair wind for us.
Hearken to the lovely lady! quoth the carline, how she deemeth me to be none other than the great God himself, to hold the winds in the hollow of my hand, and still the waves with a word! What! am I wrought somewhat after his image, kind ladies? And she grinned horribly therewith. Then she said again: As to thy remedy, sweetling, meseemeth it nought. For how shall ye sail this stormy water when your captain is gone, and ye but holiday sailors belike?
As she spake, a great wave came up from the windward, and brake over us, and half filled the craft, and lifted her bows up towering, and then down we went into the trough; and I sat cowed and quaking, and spake never another word.
Now began the sun to sink, and the wind abated, and the sea went down, but the boat sped on as swift as ever over the landless waters.
Now the sun was down, and dusk was at hand, and the carline spake, and drew a bright-gleaming sax from under her raiment: Damsels, I warn you that now it were best that ye obey me in all things; for though ye be three and I one, yet whereas I have here an edge-friend, I may take the life of any one of you, or of all three, as simply as I could cut a lamb’s throat. Moreover it will serve you better in the house whereto ye are wending, that I make a good tale of you rather than a bad. For the mistress of that house is of all might; and I must say it of her, though she is my very sister, yet she is not so sweet-tempered and kind of heart as I am, but somewhat rough and unyielding of mood, so that it is best to please her. Wherefore, maidens, I rede you be sage.
Our unhappy hearts were now so sunken in wanhope, that we had no word wherewith to answer her, and she spake: Now obey ye my bidding and eat and drink, that ye may come hale and sound to your journey’s end, for I would not give starvelings to my dear sister.

我说：法子是有的——我们三个一起动手把你抓住，扔到船外去，除非你立刻调转船头回大陆去。确实，我毫不怀疑：你既能招来这阵妖风害我们，也能给我们招来一阵顺风。
听听这位美丽的女士说的话！老婆子道，她竟把我当作伟大的上帝本尊了，能把风握在掌心，一句话便平息波浪！怎么！好心的女士们，难道我是照着他的模样造出来的么？说着她狞笑起来。随后她又说：至于你那个法子，小甜心，我看一钱不值。船夫没了，你们这些大概是逢年过节才出海的水手，怎么驾着船闯过这风浪滔天的湖面？
她说话间，一个大浪从上风头打来，在我们头顶炸开，灌满了半个船身，船头高高昂起，随即我们又坠入浪谷；我吓得缩成一团，浑身发抖，再没敢说一句话。
太阳开始西沉，风势渐弱，湖面平缓下来，但船依然疾驰如故，掠过不见陆地的水面。
太阳落山了，暮色将至，老婆子开口了，从衣下抽出一柄寒光闪闪的短剑：姑娘们，我警告你们，眼下最好事事听我的；你们虽有三人，我只有一个，可我手里有这位刃上之交，要取你们中任何一人、乃至三人共同的性命，就像割断羊羔的喉咙一样容易。再者（Moreover），在你们要去的那座宅子里，我把你们说得好些总比说得坏些对你们更有利。那宅子的女主人法力无边；虽说她是我亲姐妹，我也得讲——她可不如我这般好脾气、好心肠，性情相当粗暴执拗，所以最好顺着她。因此，姑娘们，我劝你们放聪明些。
我们不幸的心已沉入绝望，连一句话也答不上来。她便说：现在听从我的吩咐，吃点喝点吧，好让你们完完整整地到达旅程终点，我可不愿把饿得半死的人交给我亲爱的妹妹。

Therewith she brought forth victual for us, and that nought evil, of flesh and bread, and cheese and cakes, and good wine withal; and we were hunger-weary as well as sorrow-weary; and hunger did at that moment overcome sorrow, so we ate and drank, and, would we, would we not, something of heart came back to us thereby. Then again spake the carline: Now my will is that ye sleep; and ye have cushions and cloths enough to dight you a fair bed; and this bidding is easy for you to obey. Forsooth, so weary were we with sorrow, and our hunger was now quenched, that we laid us down and slept at once, and forgat our troubles.
When we awoke it was after the first dawn, and we were come aland even where thou didst this morning, guest. And thou mayst deem it wondrous, but so it was, that close to where our boat took land lay the ferry which brought thee hither.
Now the carline bade us get ashore, and we did so, and found the land wondrous fair, little as that solaced us then. But she said unto us: Hearken! now are ye come home; and long shall ye dwell here, for never shall ye depart hence save by the will of my sister and me, wherefore, once more, I rede you be good, for it will be better for you. Go forth now unto yonder house, and on the way ye shall meet the Queen of this land, and ye have nought to do but to say to her that ye are the Gift; and then shall she see to your matter.
Therewith she gat into her own craft, the Sending Boat, and therein did the deed and spake the words ye wot of, and was gone north-away; and when we turned to seek for our boat wherein we had come hither, it was gone.
We stood miserably for a while on the lip of the land, and then I said that we might as well go meet our fate as die there of grief and hunger. So we went, and came into those fair gardens, and as we went slowly up toward the house came on us a woman clad in red scarlet and grandly dight. A big woman she was, and like to her that beguiled us, but far younger and fairer of favour, foolish and proud of visage. She stared on us, and seemed half afeard of us at first, but asked us what we were, and I answered that we were the Gift. The Gift? said she, what meaneth that? Will ye obey me in all things? If ye gainsay it, ye will perish, unless ye can eat grass; for on this isle everything cometh from my hand.
What might we do? We all knelt down before her, and swore to do her will.

说着她给我们拿出吃食，倒也不坏：肉、面包、奶酪、糕饼，还有好酒；我们又累又饿，又悲又乏；那一刻饥饿压倒了悲伤，我们便吃了喝了，不管愿不愿意，身上总算恢复了几分气力。老婆子又开口道：现在我要你们睡觉；垫子和布料尽够你们铺一张好床；这道吩咐你们不难照办。确实，我们被悲伤折磨得疲惫不堪，饥饿也缓解了，便躺下立刻睡着了，忘掉了烦恼。
我们醒来时天刚破晓，船已靠岸，客人，正是你今早登岸的那个地方。你也许觉得离奇，但事实就是：我们船靠岸的地方近旁，正泊着后来把你渡到这儿来的那条渡船。
