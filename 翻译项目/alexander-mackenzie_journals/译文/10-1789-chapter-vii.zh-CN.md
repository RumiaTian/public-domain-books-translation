### VII / 七
===Original===

*Saturday, August 1.*﻿—We embarked at three this morning, the weather being clear and cold, with the wind at southeast. At three in the afternoon we traversed and landed to take the canoe in tow: here was an encampment of the natives, which we had reason to suppose they had quitted the preceding day. At five we perceived a family, consisting of a man, two women, and as many children, stationed by the side of the water, whom we had not seen before. They informed us, that they had but few fish, and that none of their friends were in the neighbourhood, except the inhabitants of one lodge on the other side of the river, and a man who belonged to them, and who was now occupied in hunting. I now found my interpreter very unwilling to ask such questions as were dictated to him, from the apprehension, as I imagined, that I might obtain such intelligence as would prevent him from seeing Athabasca this season. We left him with the Indian, and pitched our tents at the same place where we had passed the night on the fifth of last month. The English Chief came along with the Indian to our fire; and the latter informed us that the native who went down part of the river with us had passed there, and that we should meet with three lodges of his tribe above the river of the Bear Lake. Of the river to the westward he knew nothing, but from the relation of others. This was the first night since our departure from Athabasca, when it was sufficiently dark to render the stars visible.
*Sunday, August 2.*﻿—We set off at three this morning with the towing-line. I walked with my Indians, as they went faster than the canoe, and particularly as I suspected that they wanted to arrive at the huts of the natives before me. In our way, I observed several small springs of mineral water running from the foot of the mountain, and along the beach I saw several lumps of iron ore. When we came to the river of the Bear Lake, I ordered one of the young Indians to wait for my canoe, and I took my place in their small canoe. This river is about two hundred and fifty yards broad at this place, the water clear and of a greenish colour. When I landed on the opposite shore, I discovered that the natives had been there very lately from the print of their feet in the sand. We continued walking till five in the afternoon, when we saw several smokes along the shore.

*八月一日，星期六。*——今晨三点上船，天气晴朗寒冷，风在东南。下午三点我们横渡靠岸，把独木舟改作拖行：这里有一处土人营地，我们有理由断定他们前一天刚走。五点，我们望见水边驻着一户人家——一个男人、两个女人和同样数目的孩子——都是没见过的。他们告诉我们，鱼没几条，这一带也没有他们的朋友，只有河对岸一座棚屋的住户，还有一个属于他们的人，眼下正打猎去了。这时我发现通译很不情愿照我口授的话去问；我猜想，他是怕问出什么情报来，害他这一季见不着阿萨巴斯卡。我们把他留给那印第安人（Indians），在上个月五号过夜的老地方支起帐篷。英国酋长（The English Chief）随那印第安人来到我们的火堆边；后者告诉我们，先前陪我们顺流而下一段的那个土人打这里过去了，熊湖河口上游我们会遇上他部族的三座棚屋。至于西边那条河，他除了听人讲过，自己一无所知。这是离开阿萨巴斯卡以来头一个夜足够黑、看得见星星的夜晚。
*八月二日，星期日。*——今晨三点拖着缆上路。我同我的印第安人一道步行，他们走得比船快，尤其我疑心他们想赶在我前头到土人的棚屋去。一路上，我看见山脚下淌出几股小矿泉，滩边还有好几块铁矿石。走到熊湖河口，我吩咐一个年轻印第安人等我的独木舟，自己坐进他们的小舟。这条河在这里宽约二百五十码，水色清亮，带点绿。在对岸登岸时，我从沙上的脚印看出，土人新近来过。我们一直走到下午五点，望见沿岸有好几处炊烟。

===Original===

As we naturally concluded, that these were certain indications where we should meet the natives who were the objects of our search we quickened our pace; but, in our progress, experienced a very sulphurous smell, and at length discovered that the whole bank was on fire for a very considerable distance. It proved to be a coal mine, to which the fire had communicated from an old Indian encampment. The beach was covered with coals, and the English Chief gathered some of the softest he could find, as a black dye; it being the mineral, as he informed me, with which the natives render their quills black.
Here we waited for the large canoe, which arrived an hour after us. At half past ten we saw several Indian marks, which consisted of pieces of bark fixed on poles, and pointing to the woods, opposite to which is an old beaten road, that bore the marks of being lately frequented; the beach also was covered with tracks. At a small distance were the poles of five lodges standing; where we landed and unloaded our canoe. I then despatched one of my men and two young Indians to see if they could find any natives within a day’s march of us. I wanted the English Chief to go, but he pleaded fatigue, and that it would be of no use. This was the first time he had refused to comply with my desire, and jealousy, I believe, was the cause of it in the present instance; though I had taken every precaution that he should not have cause to be jealous of the Canadians. There was not, at this time, the least appearance of snow on the opposite mountains, though they were almost covered with it, when we passed before. Set two nets, and at eleven o’clock at night the men and Indians returned. They had been to their first encampment, where there were four fires, and which had been quitted a short time before; so that they were obliged to make the circuit of several small lakes, which the natives cross with their canoes. This encampment was on the borders of a lake which was too large for them to venture round it, so that they did not proceed any further. They saw several beavers and beaver lodges in those small lakes. They killed one of these animals whose fur began to get long, a sure indication that the fall of the year approaches. They also saw many old tracks of the moose and reindeer.

我们自然断定，这些正是我们要寻找的土人所在的确实迹象，便加快了脚步；可走着走着，闻到一股很重的硫磺味，最后发现整道河岸烧着火，绵延很远。原来是一座煤矿，火种是从一处旧印第安营地引过去的。滩上铺满了煤，英国酋长捡了几块他能找到的最软的，当黑色染料用；他告诉我，土人就是把豪猪刺染黑用的这种矿物。
我们在这里等大独木舟，它比我们晚到一个钟头。十点半，我们看见几处印第安记号，是把树皮绑在杆上、指向树林；对面有一条踩出来的旧路，看得出新近还常有人走；滩上也布满足迹。不远处立着五座棚屋的骨架；我们在那里登岸卸船。我随即派了一个人和两个年轻印第安人，看能不能在一天脚程内找到土人。我想让英国酋长去，他推说累了，去了也没用。这是他头一回拒绝我的差遣；我相信，这一回还是醋意在作怪，尽管我处处留心，不让他有理由猜忌加拿大人（Canadians）。这时对面的山上已见不到一点雪，虽然我们先前来时山上几乎全盖着雪。下了两张网；夜里十一点，人和印第安人回来了。他们到过那些人的第一处营地，有四堆火，刚撤不久；害得他们只好绕过好几个小湖——土人都是驾独木舟横渡的。那营地在一座湖边，湖太大，他们不敢绕，便没有再往前。他们在那些小湖里看见几只河狸和河狸巢。他们猎得一只河狸，毛已开始变长——秋天将至的确凿征兆。他们还看见许多驼鹿和驯鹿的旧蹄印。

===Original===

This is the time when the reindeer leave the plains to come to the woods, as the mosquitoes begin to disappear; I, therefore, apprehended that we should not find a single Indian on the river side, as they would be in or about the mountains setting snares to take them.
*Monday, August 3.*﻿—We proceeded with a strong westerly wind, at four this morning, the weather being cloudy and cold. At twelve it cleared up and became fine; the current also increased. The water had fallen so much since our passage down the river, that here, as in other places, we discovered many shoals which were not then visible. We killed several geese of a larger size than those which we had generally seen. Several Indian encampments were seen along the river, and we landed at eight for the night.
*Tuesday, August 4.*﻿—At four in the morning we renewed our course, when it was fine and calm. The night had been cold and a very heavy dew had fallen. At nine we were obliged to land in order to gum the canoe, when the weather became extremely warm. Numerous tracks of reindeer appeared on the side of the river. At half past five we took our station for the night, and set the nets. The current was very strong all day, and we found it very difficult to walk along the beach, from the large stones which were scattered over it.
*Wednesday, August 5.*﻿—We raised our nets, but had not the good fortune to take a single fish. The water was now become so low that the eddy currents would not admit of setting them. The current had not relaxed its strength; and the difficulty of walking along the beach was continued. The air was now become so cold; that our exercise, violent as it was, scarce kept us warm. We passed several points which we should not have accomplished, if the canoe had been loaded. We were very much fatigued, and at six were glad to conclude our toilsome march. The Indians killed two geese. The women, who did not quit the canoe, were continually employed in making shoes of moose-skin, for the men, as a pair did not last more than a day.
*Thursday, August 6.*﻿—The rain prevented us from proceeding till half past six, when we had a strong aft wind, which, aided by the paddles, drove us on at a great rate. We encamped at six to wait for our Indians, whom we had not seen since the morning; and at half past seven they arrived very much dissatisfied with their day’s journey.

===Chinese===

这正是驯鹿离开平原进林的时节，蚊子开始消散；因此我担心，河边一个印第安人也找不着了，他们必定进了山或在山边下套子捕鹿去了。
*八月三日，星期一。*——今晨四点，乘着强劲的西风前进，天气多云寒冷。十二点放晴转好；水势也涨了。自我们顺流而下，水位落了许多，这里同别处一样，露出好些当时看不见的浅滩。我们猎得几只鹅，比平常所见的大些。沿河见到几处印第安营地，八点上岸过夜。
*八月四日，星期二。*——早晨四点继续赶路，天气晴朗无风。夜里很冷，露水极重。九点我们不得不靠岸给独木舟涂胶，这时天气变得极热。河一侧出现驯鹿的无数蹄印。五点半我们扎营过夜，下了网。一整天水流都很急；滩上乱石遍地，沿岸步行非常吃力。
*八月五日，星期三。*——起了网，却连一条鱼也没捞着的运气。水位已落得这样低，洄流处没法下网了。水流丝毫未缓；沿滩步行的艰难依旧。天气冷到这般地步，我们那样剧烈的运动也几乎暖不过身子。我们绕过了好几处岬角——独木舟若载着货，是过不去的。我们疲惫不堪，六点钟总算结束了这段苦行，正求之不得。印第安人猎得两只鹅。妇人们不出独木舟，手上不停地给男人做驼鹿皮鞋——一双鞋撑不过一天。
*八月六日，星期四。*——雨把我们耽搁到六点半才动身，随后来了强劲的顺风，加上划桨，把我们推得飞快。六点我们扎营等印第安人，从早晨起就没见着他们；七点半他们到了，对这一天的行程满肚子不高兴。

===Original===

Two days had now elapsed since we had seen the least appearance of Indian habitations.
*Friday, August 7.*﻿—We embarked at half past three, and soon after perceived two reindeer on the beach before us. We accordingly checked our course; but our Indians, in contending who should be the first to get near these animals, alarmed and lost them. We, however, killed a female reindeer, and from the wounds in her hind legs, it was supposed that she had been pursued by wolves, who had devoured her young one: her udder was full of milk, and one of the young Indians poured it among some boiled corn, which he ate with great delight, esteeming it a very delicious food. At five in the afternoon we saw an animal running along the beach, but could not determine whether it was a grey fox or a dog. In a short time, we went ashore for the night, at the entrance of a small river, as I thought there might be some natives in the vicinity of the place. I ordered my hunters to put their fusees in order, and gave them ammunition to proceed on a hunting party the next day; they were also instructed to discover if there were any natives in the neighbouring mountains. I found a small canoe at the edge of the woods, which contained a paddle and a bow: it had been repaired this spring, and the workmanship of the bark excelled any that I had yet seen. We saw several encampments in the course of the day. The current of the river was very strong, and along the points equal to rapids.
*Saturday, August 8.*﻿—The rain was very violent throughout the night, and continued till the afternoon of this day, when the weather began to clear, with a strong, cold, and westerly wind. At three the Indians proceeded on the hunting expedition, and at eight they returned without having met with the least success; though they saw numerous tracks of the reindeer. They came to an old beaten road, which one of them followed for some time; but it did not appear to have been lately frequented. The rain now returned, and continued till the morning.
*Sunday, August 9.*﻿—We renewed our voyage at half past three, the weather being cold and cloudy; but at ten it became clear and moderate. We saw another canoe at the outside of the wood, and one of the Indians killed a dog, which was in a meagre, emaciated condition.

===Chinese===

到这时，已经两天没见到丝毫印第安人住所的痕迹了。
*八月七日，星期五。*——三点半上船，不多时便看见前方滩上有两头驯鹿。我们随即放缓了船；可我们的印第安人争先恐后要比谁先挨近那两只鹿，反而惊跑了它们。不过我们还是猎得一头母驯鹿；从后腿的伤看，它先前被狼追过，幼崽也被狼吃了：乳房里满是奶，一个年轻印第安人把奶倒进煮玉米里，吃得津津有味，当作极美的食物。下午五点，我们看见一只动物沿滩奔跑，却断不定是灰狐还是狗。不多时，我们在一条小河的入口上岸过夜，我想那地方附近或许有些土人。我吩咐猎手们把火枪拾掇好，发下弹药，明天出去打猎；还要他们探一探附近山里有没有土人。我在林缘发现一艘小独木舟，里面有一支桨和一张弓：今春才修过，蒙皮的做工胜过我迄今所见的一切。这一天我们见到几处营地。河水很急，沿岸岬角处的水势不亚于急流。
*八月八日，星期六。*——夜里的雨下得很凶，一直下到今天下午，天才开始放晴，刮起强劲寒冷的西风。三点，印第安人出发打猎，八点回来，一无所获，尽管见了驯鹿的无数蹄印。他们遇到一条踩出来的旧路，其中一个跟了一程；但看样子新近并没人走。这时雨又回来了，一直下到早晨。
*八月九日，星期日。*——三点半继续航程，天气阴冷；十点转晴转和。我们在林子外又见到一艘独木舟；一个印第安人打死一条狗，那狗瘦得皮包骨。

===Original===

We perceived various places where the natives had made their fires; for these people reside but a short time near the river, and remove from one bank to the other, as it suits their purposes. We saw a path which was connected with another on the opposite side of the river. The water had risen considerably since last night, and there had been a strong current throughout the day. At seven we made to the shore and encamped.
*Monday, August 10.*﻿—At three this morning we returned to our canoe; the weather fine and clear, with a light wind from the southeast. The Indians were before us in pursuit of game. At ten we landed opposite to the mountains which we had passed on the second of the last month, in order to ascertain the variation of the compass at this place: but this was accomplished in a very imperfect manner, as I could not depend on my watch. One of the hunters joined us here, fatigued and unsuccessful. As these mountains are the last of any considerable magnitude on the southwest side of the river, I ordered my men to cross to that side of it, that I might ascend one of them. It was near four in the afternoon when I landed, and I lost no time in proceeding to the attainment of my object. I was accompanied only by a young Indian, as the curiosity of my people was subdued by the fatigue they had undergone; and we soon had reason to believe that we should pay dearly for the indulgence of our own. The wood, which was chiefly of spruce firs, was so thick that it was with great difficulty we made our way through it. When we had walked upwards of an hour, the underwood decreased, while the white birch and poplar were the largest and tallest of their kind that I had ever seen. The ground now began to rise, and was covered with small pines, and at length we got the first view of the mountains since we had left the canoe; as they appeared to be no nearer to us, though we had been walking for three hours, than when we had seen them from the river, my companion expressed a very great anxiety to return; his shoes and leggins were torn to pieces, and he was alarmed at the idea of passing through such bad roads during the night. I persisted, however, in proceeding, with a determination to pass the night on the mountains and return on the morrow.

我们看出多处土人生火的痕迹；这些人河边住不长，随着需要从一岸挪到另一岸。我们见到一条小路，与对岸的另一条相接。自昨夜起水位涨了不少，一整天水流都很急。七点我们靠岸扎营。
*八月十日，星期一。*——今晨三点回到独木舟上；天气晴朗，微风从东南来。印第安人已先我们去追猎物了。十点，我们在上个月二日经过的那些山的对岸登岸，为的是测一测此地的罗盘偏角：可我信不过我的表，这事办得马马虎虎。一名猎手在这里同我们会合，疲惫不堪，一无所获。这些山是河西南一侧最后一列像样的高山，我便吩咐众人渡到那侧，好让我登一登。将近下午四点我登了岸，立刻着手办我的事。只有一个年轻印第安人作伴——我的人早被劳累磨掉了好奇心；而不久我们便有理由相信，我们这份好奇的代价不会小。林子主要是云杉，密得我们好不容易才挤过去。走了一个多钟头，灌木渐稀，白桦和杨树是我平生所见同类中最高大的。地面开始抬升，覆着小松树；终于，离开独木舟以来我们头一回望见了那些山——可徒步三个钟头之后，它们看上去同在河里望时一样远；同伴急得直想回头；他的鞋和绑腿都破成了碎片，一想到夜里还要走这样烂的路就心里发慌。我却执意前行，打定主意在山上过夜，明天再回。

As we approached them, the ground was quite marshy, and we waded in water and grass up to the knees, till we came within a mile of them, when I suddenly sunk up to my armpits, and it was with some difficulty that I extricated myself from this disagreeable situation. I now found it impossible to proceed; to cross this marshy ground in a straight line was impracticable, and it extended so far to the right and left, that I could not attempt to make the circuit; I therefore determined to return to the canoe, and arrived there about midnight, very much fatigued with this fruitless journey.
*Tuesday, August 11.*﻿—We observed several tracks along the beach, and an encampment at the edge of the woods, which appeared to be five or six days old. We should have continued our route along this side of the river, but we had not seen our hunters since yesterday morning. We accordingly embarked before three, and at five traversed the river, when we saw two of them coming down in search of us. They had killed no other animals than one beaver, and a few hares. According to their account, the woods were so thick that it was impossible to follow the game through them. They had seen several of the natives’ encampments, at no great distance from the river and it was their opinion that they had discovered us in our passage down it, and had taken care to avoid us; which accounted for the small number we had seen on our return.
I requested the English Chief to return with me to the other side of the river, in order that he might proceed to discover the natives, whose tracks and habitations we had seen there; but he was backward in complying with my desire, and proposed to send the young men; but I could not trust to them, and at the same time was become rather doubtful of him. They were still afraid lest I should obtain such accounts of the other river as would induce me to travel overland to it, and that they should be called upon to accompany me. I was, indeed, informed by one of my own people, that the English Chief, his wives and companions, had determined to leave me on this side of the Slave Lake, in order to go to the country of the Beaver Indians, and that about the middle of the winter he would return to that lake, where he had appointed to meet some of his relations, who, during the last spring, had been engaged in war.

*八月十一日，星期二。*——我们看见滩上好几串足迹，林边有一处营地，约有五六天的样子。我们本该沿河这一侧继续赶路，可从昨天早晨起就没见着猎手们。于是不到三点我们便上了船，五点横渡到对岸，正撞见他们两个下来找我们。他们猎获的只有一只河狸和几只野兔。据他们说，林子太密，猎物根本追不着。他们见过土人的几处营地，离河不远；照他们看，我们顺流而下时土人已发现了我们，特意躲开了；这也就解释了我们回程所见为何寥寥。
我请英国酋长随我回河对岸，好让他去寻那些土人——他们的足迹和住处我们在那边都见过；他却吞吞吐吐不肯应承，提议派年轻人去；我又信不过年轻人，同时对他本人也起了几分疑心。他们依旧提防着，生怕我打听到那条河的消息，动念由陆路过去，还要他们随行。说实话，我自己人里有一个告诉我：英国酋长已同他的妻子和随从们合计定当，要在大奴湖这一侧把我撇下，前往海狸印第安人（Beaver Indians）的乡野；隆冬时节再回那湖上去，他约了几位亲人在那里碰头——那些人今年春天还在打仗。

We now traversed the river, and continued to track the Indians till past twelve, when we lost all traces of them; in consequence, as we imagined, of their having crossed to the eastern side. We saw several dogs on both shores; and one of the young Indians killed a wolf, which the men ate with great satisfaction: we shot, also, fifteen young geese that were now beginning to fly. *Saturday, September 12.*﻿—The weather was cloudy, and also very cold. At eight, we embarked with a northeast wind, and entered the Lake of the Hills. About ten, the wind veered to the westward, and was as strong as we could bear it with the high sail, so that we arrived at Chipewyan fort by three o’clock in the afternoon, where we found Mr. Macleod, with five men busily employed in building a new house. Here, then, we concluded this voyage, which had occupied the considerable space of one hundred and two days.

===Chinese===

早晨四点半上船，一直行到傍晚六点，在六月三日我们扎过营的地方上岸过夜。
*九月十二日，星期六。*——天气多云，也很冷。八点，我们乘东北风上船，进入了丘之湖（Lake of the Hills）。十点左右，风向转西，强得正好够我们张高帆承受；下午三点，我们抵达奇佩维安堡，见麦克劳德先生正带着五个人忙着盖一栋新房子。于是，这趟航行到此结束——前后整整占去了一百零二天的光阴。
