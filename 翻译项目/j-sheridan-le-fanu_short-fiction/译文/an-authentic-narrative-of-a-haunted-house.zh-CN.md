## An Authentic Narrative of a Haunted House / 一幢凶宅的真实记述

===Original===
[The Editor of the *University Magazine* submits the following very remarkable statement, with every detail of which he has been for some years acquainted, upon the ground that it affords the most authentic and ample relation of a series of marvellous phenomena, in nowise connected with what is technically termed "spiritualism," which he has anywhere met with. All the persons—and there are many of them living—upon whose separate evidence some parts, and upon whose united testimony others, of this most singular recital depend, are, in their several walks of life, respectable, and such as would in any matter of judicial investigation be deemed wholly unexceptionable witnesses. There is not an incident here recorded which would not have been distinctly deposed to on oath had any necessity existed, by the persons who severally, and some of them in great fear, related their own distinct experiences. The Editor begs most pointedly to meet *in limine* the suspicion, that he is elaborating a trick, or vouching for another ghost of Mrs. Veal. As a mere story the narrative is valueless: its sole claim to attention is its absolute truth. For the good faith of its relator he pledges his own and the character of this Magazine. With the Editor's concurrence, the name of the watering place, and some special circumstances in no essential way bearing upon the peculiar character of the story, but which might have indicated the locality, and possibly annoyed persons interested in house property there, have been suppressed by the narrator. Not the slightest liberty has been taken with the narrative, which is presented precisely in the terms in which the writer of it, who employs throughout the first person, would, if need were, fix it in the form of an affidavit.]

Within the last eight years—the precise date I purposely omit—I I was ordered by my physician, my health being in an unsatisfactory state, to change my residence to one upon the seacoast; and accordingly, I took a house for a year in a fashionable watering-place, at a moderate distance from the city in which I had previously resided, and connected with it by a railway.

Winter was setting in when my removal thither was decided upon; but there was nothing whatever dismal or depressing in the change. The house I had taken was to all appearance, and in point of convenience, too, quite a modern one. It formed one in a cheerful row, with small gardens in front, facing the sea, and commanding sea air and sea views in perfection. In the rear it had coach-house and stable, and between them and the house a considerable grass-plot, with some flowerbeds, interposed.

Our family consisted of my wife and myself, with three children, the eldest about nine years old, she and the next in age being girls; and the youngest, between six and seven, a boy. To these were added six servants, whom, although for certain reasons I decline giving their real names, I shall indicate, for the sake of clearness, by arbitrary ones. There was a nurse, Mrs. Southerland; a nursery-maid, Ellen Page; the cook, Mrs. Greenwood; and the housemaid, Ellen Faith; a butler, whom I shall call Smith, and his son, James, about two-and-twenty.

We came out to take possession at about seven o'clock in the evening; everything was comfortable and cheery; good fires lighted, the rooms neat and airy, and a general air of preparation and comfort, highly conducive to good spirits and pleasant anticipations.

一日，孩子们正在后花园玩耍，我要他们指给我看，那偶尔如前文所述现身的女人，惯常在马厩墙根何处被人瞧见。这一确切地点，孩子们毫无异议，指得极其分明笃定。我提议：说不定地底下埋着什么东西；便劝他们拿小铲子在那里挖个坑，试试看。于是他们动起手来，等我傍晚归家时，他们已刨出了一块颚骨，上头还连着几颗牙齿。骨头已朽得厉害，行将碎裂，牙齿却完好无损。我辨不出这些是不是人的臼齿；但我把这块"化石"拿给前文提到的那位医生看——他次日傍晚前来——他断定是人的牙齿。一两日之后，另一位医生也得出了同样的结论。今日回顾全案，有一桩几乎不可思议之处：既有这样的证据摆在眼前，我竟没有雇个劳力，把那处地面彻底掘开搜查一番。我只能如实相告：事情就是这样。我对所闻、并在此一丝不苟记录下来的每一句话，其所述皆为真实的诚信，毫不怀疑。可我当时却生出一种冷漠懈怠之心，其缘由我彼时不懂，事后也始终不甚了了。我有一种模糊却不可动摇的印象：整件事总可归之于自然的缘由。直到我们离开此宅之后一段时间——顺带一提，我们后来才得知，此宅在我们入住之前便已有闹鬼之名——再行回想，我才发觉：欲以寻常道理圆满地解释所发生的一切，实有重重困难。其中大半，我们尽可武断地推给"想象"二字。可即便如此，篇首便有一桩奇处——莫如说是一桩不可能之事：如此众多的人，各不相同，在我们居住此宅的那短短一段时日里，竟几乎同时为种种不同的鬼影及其他幻象所扰；而他们此前从未、据我们所知此后也从未受过任何同类恐惧或妄想的纠缠。此外还有些事，也不能如此搪塞。屋顶之中那古怪的敲击声，我便曾亲耳屡屡听见。

There were also, which I before forgot to mention, in the daytime, rappings at the doors of the sitting-rooms, which constantly deceived us; and it was not till our "come in" was unanswered, and the hall or passage outside the door was discovered to be empty, that we learned that whatever else caused them, human hands did not. All the persons who reported having seen the different persons or appearances here described by me, were just as confident of having literally and distinctly seen them, as I was of having seen the hard-featured woman with the blind eye, so remarkably corresponding with Smith's description.

About a week after the discovery of the teeth, which were found, I think, about two feet under the ground, a friend, much advanced in years, and who remembered the town in which we had now taken up our abode, for a very long time, happened to pay us a visit. He good-humouredly pooh-poohed the whole thing; but at the same time was evidently curious about it. "We might construct a sort of story," said I (I am giving, of course, the substance and purport, not the exact words, of our dialogue), "and assign to each of the three figures who appeared their respective parts in some dreadful tragedy enacted in this house. The male figure represents the murderer; the ill-looking, one-eyed woman his accomplice, who, we will suppose, buried the body where she is now so often seen grubbing in the earth, and where the human teeth and jawbone have so lately been disinterred; and the young woman with dishevelled tresses, and black cloak, and the bloody scar across her throat, their victim. A difficulty, however, which I cannot get over, exists in the cheerfulness, the great publicity, and the evident very recent date of the house." "Why, as to that," said he, "the house is *not* modern; it and those beside it formed an old government store, altered and fitted up recently as you see. I remember it well in my young days, fifty years ago, before the town had grown out in this direction, and a more entirely lonely spot, or one more fitted for the commission of a secret crime, could not have been imagined."

此外还有一事，我先前忘了提及：白天，起居室的门上常常响起叩门声，屡屡将我们骗了；直到我们应了一声"进来"，却无人答话，开门一看，门外的厅堂或过道空无一人，我们这才明白：其起因不论为何，断非人手所为。所有禀报曾看见我所记述的各色人物或形影的人，都和我一样笃定：他们确确实实、清清楚楚地看见了，正如我看见那个面目刻薄、瞎了一只眼、与史密斯所述惊人吻合的妇人一样笃定。

发现牙齿之后约一周——那些牙齿，我记得是在地下约两英尺处挖出的——一位年事已高的老友前来探访，他记得我们如今安身的这座城镇，记得的时日可谓久远。他好脾气地把这事一股脑儿嗤之以鼻；但同时分明又满怀好奇。"我们大可编出一则故事来，"我说（此处所引，自是我们对话的大意与要旨，而非一字不差的原话），"给现身的这三个身影，各自分派在这宅子里上演的某出骇人悲剧中所应承担的角色。那个男影，是凶手；那个面目可憎的独眼妇人是他的同谋，我们不妨假定，是她把尸首埋在了她如今屡屡被人瞧见刨土翻寻、并新近掘出人牙与颚骨之处；而那个披发、身着黑色斗篷、喉间横着一道血痕的年轻女子，则是他俩的受害人。不过，有一层难处我无法逾越：这宅子如此明快、如此地处显要、又显然如此之新。" "嘿，说到这个，"他答道，"这宅子可并不新；它和它两旁那些房子，本是一座旧时的官家仓库，近来才如你所见这般改建装修的。我少时——五十年前——对它记忆犹新；那时这座城还未向这个方向扩展，再没有比这更偏僻冷清的地方了，再没有比这更宜于暗行凶险之事的所在了。"

I have nothing to add, for very soon after this my physician pronounced a longer stay unnecessary for my health, and we took our departure for another place of abode. I may add, that although I have resided for considerable periods in many other houses, I never experienced any annoyances of a similar kind elsewhere; neither have I made (stupid dog! you will say), any inquiries respecting either the antecedents or subsequent history of the house in which we made so disturbed a sojourn.
