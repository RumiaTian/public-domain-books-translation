### I / 第一章

## In Which Things Happen Near Barking Creek / 巴金溪畔诸事纪

===Original===
The wind howled dismally round a house standing by itself almost on the shores of Barking Creek. It was the grey dusk of an early autumn day, and the occasional harsh cry of a seagull rising discordantly above the wind alone broke the silence of the flat, desolate waste.

The house seemed deserted. Every window was shuttered; the garden was uncared for and a mass of weeds; the gate leading on to the road, apparently feeling the need of a deficient top hinge, propped itself drunkenly on what had once been a flowerbed. A few gloomy trees swaying dismally in the wind surrounded the house and completed the picture﻿—one that would have caused even the least imaginative of men to draw his coat a little tighter round him, and feel thankful that it was not his fate to live in such a place.

But then few people ever came near enough to the house to realize its sinister appearance. The road﻿—it was little better than a cart track﻿—which passed the gate, was out of the beaten way; only an occasional fisherman or farm labourer ever used it, and that generally by day when things assumed their proper proportion, and it was merely an empty house gradually falling to pieces through lack of attention. At night they avoided it if possible; folks did say that twelve years ago some prying explorer had found the bones of a skeleton lying on the floor in one of the upstairs rooms with a mildewed rope fixed to one of the beams in the ceiling. And then it had been empty for twenty years.

Even now when the wind lay in the east or northeast and the tide was setting in, there were those who said that you could see a light shining through the cracks in the shutters in that room upstairs, and that, should a man climb up and look in, he’d see no skeleton, but a body with purple face and staring eyes swinging gently to and fro, and tied by the neck to a beam with a rope which showed no trace of mildew. Ridiculous, of course; but then so many of these local superstitions are. Useful, too, in some cases; they afford a privacy from the prying attention of local gossips far more cheaply and effectively than high walls and bolts and bars.

即便到了如今，只要风从东面或东北面吹来、潮水上涨的时候，仍有人说，你能看见一道光从楼上那个房间护窗板的缝隙里透出来；还说，倘若有人爬上去往里张望，他看到的不会是骷髅，而是一具面孔发紫、双目圆睁的尸体，正被一根毫无霉迹的绳子吊住脖子，在横梁下轻轻地来回晃荡。这当然是荒唐话；可这一类的本地迷信大多如此。在某些情况下还颇有用处；它们比高墙、门栓和铁条都更廉价、更有效地，把爱饶舌的好事之客挡在了门外。

Three of them were nondescript specimens of humanity of the type that may be seen by the thousand hurrying into the City by the early business trains. 「好，」司机答道，「回头见。」

第二辆车向右一拐，朝北驶去，首领站在原地目送那盏尾灯消失。然后他回到自己的车上，没多久，伦敦外围最初的几处房舍便出现在眼前。直到他们抵达白教堂区（Whitechapel），首领才又开了口，嗓音里带着一丝压抑的兴奋。

「我们把它们搅得不安生了；搅得它们够呛。要不然，他们绝不会派扎博列夫来。考虑到警察这一层，他是个太大的人物，本不该冒这个险。」

「我正在考虑的，正是警察这一层，」他的同伴说。

大汉笑了起来。

「这事儿交给我，老伙计；这一层完全交给我好了。」
