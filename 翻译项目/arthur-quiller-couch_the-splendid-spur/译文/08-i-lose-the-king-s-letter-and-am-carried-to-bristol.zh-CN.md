## VIII I Lose the King's Letter; and Am Carried to Bristol / 我遗失了国王的密信；并被押往布里斯托尔

===Original===
Almost before daylight we were afoot, and the first ray of cold sunshine found us stepping from the woods into the plain, where now the snow was vanished and a glistening coat of rime spread over all things. Down here the pines gave way to bare elms and poplars, thickly dotted, and among them the twisting smoke of farmstead and cottage, here and there, and the morning stir of kitchen and stable very musical in the crisp air.
Delia stepped along beside me, humming an air or breaking off to chatter. Meeting us, you would have said we had never a care. The road went stretching away to the northwest and the hills against the sky there; whither beyond, we neither knew nor (being both young, and one, by this time, pretty deep in love) did greatly care. Yet meeting with a wagoner and his team, we drew up to enquire.

===Chinese===
天还没亮我们就上路了，第一缕冷冽的阳光照到我们身上时，我们正从树林里走出来，踏上平原。这时雪已经化尽，一层亮晶晶的白霜铺满了万物。松树到了这里便让位给光秃秃的榆树和白杨，稀疏地散布着，其间升起了农庄和小屋的袅袅炊烟，零零落落。厨房和马厩清晨的声响在冷冽的空气里格外悦耳。
德利娅走在我身旁，哼着一支小曲，不时停下来喋喋不休。若是有人遇见我们，准会说我们无忧无虑。大路向西北延伸，那边的山丘衬着天空；再往何处去，我们既不知道，也不大在意——毕竟我们两个都年轻，其中一个此刻已深深坠入了爱河。然而遇上一个赶车的和他的一队牲口，我们还是停下来打听。

===Original===
The wagoner had a shock of whitish hair and a face purple-red above, by reason of the cold, and purple-black below, for lack of a barber. He purs'd up his mouth and look'd us slowly up and down.
"Come," said I, "you are not deaf, I hope, nor dumb."
"Send I may niver!" the fellow ejaculated, slowly and with contemplation: "'tis an unseemly sight, yet tickling to the mirthfully minded. Haw—haw!" He check'd his laughter suddenly and stood like a stone image beside his horses.

===Chinese===
那车夫长着一头灰白的乱发，脸的上半截因为受冻而发紫发红，下半截因为久未剃须而紫黑。他噘起嘴巴，慢悠悠地上下打量我们。
"好了，"我说，"但愿你不是聋子，也不是哑巴。"
"老天保佑别叫我遇上！"那家伙拖长了声音，慢条斯理地说，"这可真是一副不太好看的样子，不过倒逗得人想笑。哈——哈！"他突然刹住笑声，像个石头雕像似的立在马旁边。

===Original===
"Good sir," said Delia, laying a hand on my arm (for I was growing nettled), "your mirth is a riddle: but tell us our way and you are free to laugh."
"Oh, Scarlet—Scarlet!" answer'd he: "and to me, that am a man o' blushes from my cradle!"
Convinced by this that the fellow must be an idiot, I told him so, and left him staring after us; nor heard the sound of his horses moving on again for many minutes.

===Chinese===
"好心的先生，"德利娅把手搭在我胳膊上说道（因为我正要恼火起来），"您的笑真是个谜——不过只要告诉我们路怎么走，您尽可以笑个够。"
"噢，红的——红的！"他回答，"对我这个打从娘胎出来就脸红的汉子来说！"
我断定这人准是个傻子，便直言相告，然后撇下他瞪着我们的背影；好半天才听到他的马车又动起来的声音。

===Original===
After this we met about a dozen on the road, and all paus'd to stare. But from one—an old woman—we learn'd we were walking toward Marlboro', and about noon were over the hills and looking into the valley beyond.
'Twas very like the other vale; only a pleasant stream wound along the bottom, by the banks of which the road took us. Here, by a bridge, we came to an inn bearing the sign of the Broad Face, and entered: for Captain Settle's stock of victuals was now done. A sour-fac'd woman met us at the door.
"Do you stay here," Delia advis'd me, "and drink a mug of beer while I bargain with the hostess for fresh food." She follow'd the sour-fac'd woman into the house.
But out she comes presently with her cheeks flaming and a pair of bright eyes. "Come!" she commanded, "come at once!" Setting down my half emptied mug, I went after her across the bridge and up the road, wondering. In this way we must have walk'd for a mile or more before she turn'd and stamp'd her little foot—
"Horrible!" she cried. "Horrible—wicked—shameful! Ugh!" There were tears in her eyes.

===Chinese===
之后我们在路上遇到了大约一打人，个个都停下来看我们。但有一位——一个老妇人——告诉我们，我们正朝马尔伯勒（Marlboro'）走去。将近中午时，我们翻过山丘，望见了那边的山谷。
那山谷跟先前经过的很像，只有一条宜人的溪流蜿蜒在谷底，大路就沿着河岸走。在一座桥边，我们看见一家挂着"宽脸"招牌的旅店，便走了进去——因为塞特尔上尉（Captain Settle）的食物已经吃完了。一个板着脸的女人在门口迎住了我们。
"你在这儿等着，"德利娅建议道，"喝杯麦酒，我跟女东家讨点新鲜食物去。"她跟着那板脸女人进了屋。
可是她很快就出来了，双颊绯红，一双眼睛亮闪闪的。"来！"她命令道，"快来！"我放下喝了半杯的麦酒，满腹疑惑地跟着她穿过桥走上大路。这样走了大约一英里多路，她才转过身来，跺着小脚——
"太可怕了！"她叫道，"太可怕了——邪恶——无耻！呸！"她眼里含着泪水。

===Original===
"What is shameful?"
She made no reply, but walk'd on again quickly.
"I am getting hungry, for my part," sigh'd I, after a little.
"Then you must starve!"
"Oh!"
She wheel'd round again.
"Jack, this will never do. If you are to have a comrade, let it be a boy."
"Now, I am very passably content as things are."
"Nonsense: at Marlboro', I mean, you must buy me a suit of boy's clothes. What are you hearkening to?"
"I thought I heard the noise of guns—or is it thunder?"
"Dear Jack, don't say 'tis thunder! I do mortally fear thunder—and mice."
"'Twouldn't be thunder at this time of year. No, 'tis guns firing."
"Where?—not that I mind guns."
"Ahead of us."

===Chinese===
"什么东西无耻？"
她没有回答，只是快步往前走。
"我倒是越来越饿了，"过了一会儿我叹道。
"那你只好挨饿！"
"噢！"
她又转过身来。
"杰克，这可不行。你若要找个同伴，不如找个男孩。"
"依我说，眼下这样我已经相当满意了。"
"胡说：我是说，到了马尔伯勒，你得给我买一套男孩子的衣服。你在听什么？"
"我好像听到了枪声——还是打雷？"
"亲爱的杰克，别说是打雷！我顶害怕打雷了——还有老鼠。"
"这个季节不会打雷的。不，那是枪声。"
"在哪儿？——不过枪声我不怕。"
"就在我们前面。"

===Original===
On the far side of the valley we enter'd a wood, thinking by this to shorten our way: for the road here took a long bend to eastward. Now, at first this wood seem'd of no considerable size, but thicken'd and spread as we advanced. 'Twas only, however, after passing the ridge, and when daylight began to fail us, that I became alarm'd. For the wood grew denser, with a tangle of paths crisscrossing amid the undergrowth. And just then came the low mutter of cannon again, shaking the earth. We began to run forward, tripping in the gloom over brambles, and stumbling into holes.
For a mile or so this lasted: and then, without warning, I heard a sound behind me, and look'd back, to find Delia sunk upon the ground.
"Jack, here's a to-do!"
"What's amiss?"
"Why, I am going to swoon!"

===Chinese===
在山谷的另一头，我们走进一片树林，想抄近路——因为这条路在这里向东绕了很大一个弯。起初这林子似乎不大，但越往里走越密越深。不过，翻过山脊、天色开始暗下来之后，我才着了慌。林子越来越密，灌木丛中纵横交错着各种小径。就在这时，低沉的炮声又响起来，震得地面发颤。我们开始往前跑，在暮色中被荆棘绊倒，跌进坑里。
这样跑了大约一英里路：突然间，我听到身后有声响，回头一看，发现德利娅瘫倒在地上。
"杰克，出事了！"
"怎么了？"
"唉，我要晕过去了！"

===Original===
The words were scarce out, when there sounded a crackling and snapping of twigs ahead, and two figures came rushing toward us—a man and a woman. The man carried an infant in his arms: and though I call'd on them to stop, the pair ran by us with no more notice than if we had been stones. Only the woman cried, "Dear Lord, save us!" and wrung her hands as she pass'd out of sight.
"This is strange conduct," thought I: but peering down, saw that Delia's face was white and motionless. She had swoon'd, indeed, from weariness and hunger. So I took her in my arms and stumbled forward, hoping to find the end of the wood soon. For now the rattle of artillery came louder and incessant through the trees, and mingling with it, a multitude of dull shouts and outcries. At first I was minded to run after the man and woman, but on second thought, resolv'd to see the danger before hiding from it.
The trees, in a short while, grew sparser, and between the stems I mark'd a ruddy light glowing. And then I came out on an open space upon the hillside, with a dip of earth in front; and beyond, a long ridge of pines standing up black, because of a red glare behind them; and saw that this came not from any setting sun, but was the light of a conflagration.

===Chinese===
话音未落，前方就传来了树枝噼啪折断的声响，两个人影朝我们冲来——一男一女。那男人怀里抱着一个婴儿：我虽然喊他们停下，两人却像我们是石头似的径直跑过去了。只有那女人叫了一声："天主啊，救救我们！"绞着双手消失在视野之外。
"这可真怪，"我想：但低头一看，发现德利娅脸色苍白，一动不动。她确实是又累又饿晕过去了。我于是把她抱起来，蹒跚着往前走，希望快些走出这片林子。因为此时炮火的隆隆声穿过树林传来，越来越响，连绵不断，夹杂着一片沉闷的呐喊和叫骂。起初我想去追那对男女，但转念一想，决定先看清危险再躲避。
没过多久，树木稀疏了，在树干之间我瞥见了一片红光。接着我来到山腰上的一片空地，面前是个凹地；再远处是一长排黑黝黝的松树，因为后面映着一片红光；我看出那光并非落日，而是一场大火的光焰。

===Original===
The glare danced and quiver'd in the sky, as I cross'd the hollow. It made even Delia's white cheek seem rosy. Up amid the pines I clamor'd, and along the ridge to where it broke off in a steep declivity. And lo! in a minute I look'd down as 'twere into the infernal pit.
There was a whole town burning below. And in the streets men were fighting, as could be told by their shouts and the rattle and blaze of musketry. For a garment of smoke lay over all and hid them: only the turmoil beat up as from a furnace, and the flames of burning thatches, and quick jets of firearms like lightning in a thundercloud. Great sparks floated past us, and over the trees at our back. A hot blast breath'd on our cheeks. Now and then you might hear a human shriek distinct amid the din, and this spoke terribly to the heart.
Now the town was Marlboro', and the attacking force a body of royal troops sent from Oxford to oust the garrison of the Parliament, which they did this same night, with great slaughter, driving the rebels out of the place, and back on the road to Bristol. Had we guess'd this, much ill luck had been spared us; but we knew nought of it, nor whether friends or foes were getting the better. So (Delia being by this time recover'd a little) we determined to pass the night in the woods, and on the morrow to give the place a wide berth.

===Chinese===
那红光在我穿过凹地时在天空中跳跃、摇曳。它甚至把德利娅苍白的脸颊映得泛起了红晕。我沿着松林往上爬，顺着山脊走到一处陡峭的断崖边。看哪！不一会儿我就往下望去，仿佛看到了地狱的深渊。
底下有一整座镇子在燃烧。街上人们在厮杀，可以从他们的呐喊和火枪的噼啪射击声中听出来。因为一层烟幕笼罩了一切，把他们遮住了：只有骚乱像从熔炉里升腾而起，茅草屋顶燃烧的火焰，以及枪口喷出的阵发火光，恰似雷雨云中的闪电。大团的火星从我们身边飘过，飘到身后的树顶上。一股热风吹到我们脸上。不时能听到一声尖厉的惨叫从嘈杂中传来，令人心惊胆战。
那镇子正是马尔伯勒，进攻的部队是王军从牛津派来驱逐议会军驻军的。就在这个晚上，他们以极大的伤亡夺回了此地，把叛军赶出去，往布里斯托尔方向溃退。我们若猜到此节，就能少受许多罪了；但我们对此一无所知，也不知道哪一方占了上风。因此（德利娅此时已经恢复了一些）我们决定在树林里过一夜，第二天一早远远绕开这地方。

===Original===
Retreating, then, to the hollow (that lay on the lee side of the ridge, away from the north wind), I gather'd a pile of great stones, and spread my cloak thereover for Delia. To sleep was impossible, even with the will for it. For the tumult and fighting went on, and only died out about an hour before dawn: and once or twice we were troubled to hear the sound of people running on the ridge above. So we sat and talked in low voices till dawn; and grew more desperately hunger'd than ever.
With the chill of daybreak we started, meaning to get quit of the neighborhood before any espied us; and fetch'd a compass to the south without another look at Marlboro'. At the end of two hours, turning northwest again, we came to some water meadows beside a tiny river (the Kennet, as I think), and saw, some way beyond, a high road that cross'd to our side (only the bridge was now broken down), and further yet, a thick smoke curling up; but whence this came I could not see. Now we had been avoiding all roads this morning, and hiding at every sound of footsteps. But hunger was making us bold. I bade Delia crouch down by the stream's bank, where many alders grew, and set off toward this column of smoke.

===Chinese===
于是我们退到那个凹地（在山脊的背风面，远离北风），我搬了一堆大石头，把斗篷铺在上面给德利娅。想睡觉也是不可能的，即便有这个意愿。骚乱和战斗还在继续，直到黎明前一小时左右才渐渐平息：我们还一两次被山脊上有人奔跑的声响惊扰。我们就那样坐着低声说话，一直说到天亮；肚子比先前更饿得厉害了。
清晨的寒意中我们动身了，打算在被人发现之前离开这一带。我们折向南方，再没有回头去看马尔伯勒。走了大约两个小时后，又转向西北，来到一条小河（我想是肯尼特河 Kennet）边的水草牧场，看见远处有一条大路横穿过来（不过桥已经断了），更远处有浓烟升起；但看不出烟从哪里来。这一早上我们一直在回避所有大路，一有脚步声就躲藏。但饥饿让我们壮了胆。我让德利娅蹲在长满赤杨的河岸上，自己朝那柱浓烟走去。

By the spot where the road cross'd I noted that many men and horses had lately pass'd hereby to westward, and, by their footmarks, at a great speed. A little further, and I came on a broken musket flung against the hedge, with a nauseous mess of blood and sandy hairs about the stock of it; and just beyond was a dead horse, his legs sticking up like bent poles across the road. 'Twas here that my blood went cold on a sudden, to hear a dismal groaning not far ahead. I stood still, holding my breath, and then ran forward again.
The road took a twist that led me face to face with a small whitewashed cottage, smear'd with black stains of burning. For seemingly it had been fir'd in one or two places, only the flames had died out: and from the back, where some outbuilding yet smoulder'd, rose the smoke that I spied. His face lay among the burn'd ruins of the door, that had woefully singed his beard and hair. A stain of blood ran across the door stone and into the road.

The upper room was but a loft. In it were two beds, whereof one was empty. On the edge of the other sat up a boy of sixteen or thereabouts, stark naked and moaning miserably. With one hand he seem'd trying to cover a big wound that gaped in his chest: the other, as my head rose over the ladder, he stretch'd out with all the fingers spread. And this was his last effort.
