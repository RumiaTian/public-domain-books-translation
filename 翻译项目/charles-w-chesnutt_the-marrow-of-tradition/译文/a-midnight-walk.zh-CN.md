## XIX A Midnight Walk / XIX. A Midnight Walk
===Original===

Ellis left the office of the Morning Chronicle about eleven o’clock the same evening and set out to walk home. His boardinghouse was only a short distance beyond old Mr. Delamere’s residence, and while he might have saved time and labor by a slightly shorter route, he generally selected this one because it led also by Major Carteret’s house. Sometimes there would be a ray of light from Clara’s room, which was on one of the front corners; and at any rate he would have the pleasure of gazing at the outside of the casket that enshrined the jewel of his heart. It was true that this purely sentimental pleasure was sometimes dashed with bitterness at the thought of his rival; but one in love must take the bitter with the sweet, and who would say that a spice of jealousy does not add a certain zest to love? On this particular evening, however, he was in a hopeful mood. At the Clarendon Club, where he had gone, a couple of hours before, to verify a certain news item for the morning paper, he had heard a story about Tom Delamere which, he imagined, would spike that gentleman’s guns for all time, so far as Miss Pemberton was concerned. So grave an affair as cheating at cards could never be kept secret﻿—it was certain to reach her ears; and Ellis was morally certain that Clara would never marry a man who had been proved dishonorable. In all probability there would be no great sensation about the matter. Delamere was too well connected; too many prominent people would be involved﻿—even Clara, and the editor himself, of whom Delamere was a distant cousin. The reputation of the club was also to be considered. Ellis was not the man to feel a malicious delight in the misfortunes of another, nor was he a pessimist who welcomed scandal and disgrace with open arms, as confirming a gloomy theory of human life. But, with the best intentions in the world, it was no more than human nature that he should feel a certain elation in the thought that his rival had been practically disposed of, and the field left clear; especially since this good situation had been brought about merely by the unmasking of a hypocrite, who had held him at an unfair disadvantage in the race for Clara’s favor.
The night was quiet, except for the faint sound of distant music now and then, or the mellow laughter of some group of revelers. Ellis met but few pedestrians, but as he neared old Mr. Delamere’s, he saw two men walking in the same direction as his own, on the opposite side of the street. He had observed that they kept at about an equal distance apart, and that the second, from the stealthy manner in which he was making his way, was anxious to keep the first in sight, without disclosing his own presence. This aroused Ellis’s curiosity, which was satisfied in some degree when the man in advance stopped beneath a lamppost and stood for a moment looking across the street, with his face plainly visible in the yellow circle of light. It was a dark face, and Ellis recognized it instantly as that of old Mr. Delamere’s body servant, whose personal appearance had been very vividly impressed upon Ellis at the christening dinner at Major Carteret’s. He had seen Sandy once since, too, at the hotel cakewalk. The negro had a small bundle in his hand, the nature of which Ellis could not make out.
When Sandy had stopped beneath the lamppost, the man who was following him had dodged behind a tree-trunk. When Sandy moved on, Ellis, who had stopped in turn, saw the man in hiding come out and follow Sandy. When this second man came in range of the light, Ellis wondered that there should be two men so much alike. The first of the two had undoubtedly been Sandy. Ellis had recognized the peculiar, old-fashioned coat that Sandy had worn upon the two occasions when he had noticed him. Barring this difference, and the somewhat unsteady gait of the second man, the two were as much alike as twin brothers.
When they had entered Mr. Delamere’s house, one after the other﻿—in the stillness of the night Ellis could perceive that each of them tried to make as little noise as possible﻿—Ellis supposed that they were probably relatives, both employed as servants, or that some younger negro, taking Sandy for a model, was trying to pattern himself after his superior. Why all this mystery, of course he could not imagine, unless the younger man had been out without permission and was trying to avoid the accusing eye of Sandy. Ellis was vaguely conscious that he had seen the other negro somewhere, but he could not for the moment place him﻿—there were so many negroes, nearly three negroes to one white man in the city of Wellington!
The subject, however, while curious, was not important as compared with the thoughts of his sweetheart which drove it from his mind. Clara had been kind to him the night before﻿—whatever her motive, she had been kind, and could not consistently return to her attitude of coldness. With Delamere hopelessly discredited, Ellis hoped to have at least fair play﻿—with fair play, he would take his chances of the outcome.

===Chinese===

埃利斯当天晚上十一点来钟离开《晨报》办公室，步行回家。他的公寓在老德拉米尔（Delamere）先生的住宅过去没多远的地方，走一条稍短的路本可省时省力，但他通常选这一条，因为它还经过卡特里特少校（Major Carteret）的家。有时候，克拉拉（Clara）房里会透出一缕灯光，她的房间在屋子前部的一角；无论如何，他总能享受凝望那座珍藏着他心头至宝的匣子外壁的快乐。不错，一想到情敌，这份纯然多情的快乐有时会掺进苦味；但恋爱中人苦乐都得咽下，谁又能说一丁点醋意不会给爱情添上几分滋味呢？不过这个特别的晚上，他心里满怀希望。两个钟头前他到克拉伦登俱乐部去为晨报核实一条新闻，在那里听到一桩关于汤姆·德拉米尔（Tom）的事，他想，就彭伯顿小姐而言，这件事会让那位先生永远翻不了身。牌局作弊这样严重的事不可能瞒得住——必定会传到她耳朵里；而埃利斯深信，克拉拉绝不会嫁给一个被证明品行不端的人。这件事多半不会闹出多大风波。德拉米尔家世太好、牵连太广，太多有头有脸的人物会被卷进去——连克拉拉本人，还有主编本人——德拉米尔正是主编的远房表亲。俱乐部的名誉也得顾及。埃利斯不是那种幸灾乐祸的人，也不是那种张开双臂欢迎丑闻与耻辱、仿佛它们印证了对人生的悲观论调的厌世者。可是，哪怕怀着世上最善良的意图，一想到情敌实际上已被除掉、场地已经扫清，他心里泛起一阵得意，这也不过是人之常情；何况这一大好局面，不过是因为揭穿了一个伪君子——此人曾让他在角逐克拉拉芳心的赛场上吃了不公平的亏。
夜很静，只是偶尔传来远处隐约的音乐声，或是哪一伙寻欢作乐的人圆润的笑声。埃利斯路遇的行人不多，走近老德拉米尔先生的住宅时，他看见街对面有两个男人，与他朝着同一个方向走去。他注意到两人保持着大致相等的间距，而后面那个，从其鬼鬼祟祟的赶路姿态判断，急于让前面那个保持在视野之内，又不肯暴露自己。这引起了埃利斯的好奇；当前面那人在一盏路灯下停住、站了片刻朝街对面张望、脸在黄色光圈里看得一清二楚时，他的好奇心算是得到了几分满足。那是一张黑面孔，埃利斯一眼认出，那是老德拉米尔先生的贴身仆人——在卡特里特少校家的婴儿洗礼宴上，此人的模样已深深印进了他的脑海。后来他又见过桑迪（Sandy）一次，是在旅馆的步态舞会上。那黑人手里提着一个小包，里面是什么，埃利斯看不出来。
桑迪在路灯下停住时，跟踪他的人闪到了一根树干后面。桑迪继续前行，同样停下脚步的埃利斯看见那藏身的人走出来，跟上了桑迪。等这第二个人走进光圈，埃利斯暗暗称奇：世上竟有如此相像的两个人。两人中前面的那个无疑正是桑迪。埃利斯认出了桑迪那件特别的旧式外衣——他两次留意此人时，他穿的都是这件。撇开这一点差别，再撇开第二个人略有些踉跄的步态，两人简直像孪生兄弟一样相像。
两人一前一后进了德拉米尔先生的宅子——夜那样静，埃利斯觉察得出，他们俩都极力想弄出尽可能小的声响——他猜想，他们多半是亲戚，都受雇为仆；要么就是哪个年轻些的黑人把桑迪当作样板，努力处处仿效自己的前辈。至于为什么要这样神神秘秘，他当然无从想象，除非是那年轻人未经许可外出，正想躲开桑迪那双能定罪的眼睛。埃利斯恍惚觉得在哪儿见过另外那个黑人，可一时又想不起——黑人实在太多了，在惠灵顿城里，黑人差不多三个对一个白人！
不过，这件事虽然蹊跷，比起把他从心头赶走的心上人来，就不算什么了。克拉拉前一晚对他很亲切——不管她动机如何，她确实亲切过，总不好再摆回那副冷淡面孔。德拉米尔既已无可挽回地声名扫地，埃利斯指望至少能落到一个公平竞争的境地——只要有公平的机会，输赢他甘愿碰碰运气。
