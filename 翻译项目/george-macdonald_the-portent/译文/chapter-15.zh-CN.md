## XV. The Chamber of Ghosts / XV. 幽灵之室

===Original===
But now she returned once more into the usual routine of the family. I fear I was unable to repress all signs of agitation when, next day, she entered the dining-room, after we were seated, and took her customary place at the table. Her behaviour was much the same as before; but her face was very different. There was light in it now, and signs of mental movement. The smooth forehead would be occasionally wrinkled, and she would fall into moods which were evidently not of inanity, but of abstracted thought. She took especial care that our eyes should not meet. If by chance they did, instead of sinking hers, she kept them steady, and opened them wider, as if she was fixing them on nothing at all, or she raised them still higher, as if she was looking at something above me, before she allowed them to fall. But the change in her altogether was such that it must have attracted the notice and roused the speculation of Lady Hilton at least. For me, so well did she act her part, that I was thrown into perplexity by it. And when day after day passed, and the longing to speak to her grew, and remained unsatisfied, new doubts arose. Perhaps she was tired of me. Perhaps her new studies filled her mind with the clear, gladsome morning light of the pure intellect, which always throws doubt and distrust and a kind of negation upon the moonlight of passion, mysterious, and mingled ever with faint shadows of pain. I walked as in an unresting sleep. Utterly as I loved her, I was yet alarmed and distressed to find how entirely my being had grown dependent upon her love; how little of individual, self-existing, self-upholding life, I seemed to have left; how little I cared for anything, save as I could associate it with her.

===Chinese===
然而此时，她又再一次回到了家中惯常的生活轨迹里。第二天，待我们都落座之后，她走进餐厅，在桌旁她寻常的位置坐下——恐怕我未能将心头那一阵激荡的痕迹尽数掩住。她的举止与从前大抵无异，面容却已截然不同。此刻那脸上有了光，有了心智活动的迹象。那光洁的前额偶尔会蹙起细纹，她会陷入种种心境——那分明不是空无一物的惘然，而是出神般的沉思。她格外留意，不让我们的目光相撞。倘若偶尔不慎撞上了，她也不再垂下眼帘，而是定定地望着，把眼睛睁得更大，仿佛正凝视着一片虚无；又或者把目光抬得更高，仿佛在看高悬于我头顶之上的什么东西，然后才任它落下来。但她身上这番整体的变化如此之大，至少必定已引人注目，勾起希尔顿夫人的留意与揣测。至于我，她这出戏演得如此周全，倒叫我陷入了一片困惑。日子一天天过去，想和她说话的渴望愈长愈盛，却总不得满足，于是新的疑虑纷至沓来。也许她已对我生厌。也许她那些新近的功课，正以纯净理智那澄明、欣然的晨光充盈着她的心智——而那晨光，总会把疑虑、不信，以及一种近乎否定的意味，投射到激情的月光之上；那月光本就幽秘，且永远掺着一缕淡淡的苦痛之影。我行走其间，如同行在一场不得安歇的睡梦里。我固然爱她爱到了极处，可一旦发觉自己的整个生命竟已如此彻底地仰赖着她的爱，我不禁既惊且痛：我似乎所剩的、那份独立自存、自我撑持的生命，已是何等之少；除却能与她相连的事物而外，我对任何事都不再在乎。

===Original===
I was sitting late one night in my room. I had all but given up hope of her coming. I had, perhaps, deprived her of the somnambulic power. I was brooding over this possibility, when all at once I felt as if I were looking into the haunted room. It seemed to be lighted by the moon, shining through the stained windows. The feeling came and went suddenly, as such visions of places generally do; but this had an indescribable something about it more clear and real than such resurrections of the past, whether willed or unwilled, commonly possess; and a great longing seized me to look into the room once more. I rose with a sense of yielding to the irresistible, left the room, groped my way through the hall and up the oak staircase—I had never thought of taking a light with me—and entered the corridor. No sooner had I entered it, than the thought sprang up in my mind—"What if she should be there!" My heart stood still for a moment, like a wounded deer, and then bounded on, with a pang in every bound. The corridor was night itself, with a dim, bluish-grey light from the windows, sufficing to mark their own spaces. I stole through it, and, without erring once, went straight to the haunted chamber. The door stood half open. I entered, and was bewildered by the dim, mysterious, dreamy loveliness upon which I gazed. The moon shone full upon the windows, and a thousand coloured lights and shadows crossed and intertwined upon the walls and floor, all so soft, and mingling, and undefined, that the brain was filled as with a flickering dance of ghostly rainbows. But I had little time to think of these; for out of the only dark corner in the room came a white figure, flitting across the chaos of lights, bedewed, besprinkled, bespattered, as she passed, with their multitudinous colours. I was speechless, motionless, with something far beyond joy. With a low moan of delight, Lady Alice sank into my arms. Then, looking up, with a light laugh—"The scales are turned, dear," she said. "You are in my power now; I brought you here. I thought I could, and I tried, for I wanted so much to see you—and you are come." She led me across the room to the place where she had been seated, and we sat side by side.

===Chinese===
一天深夜，我独坐房中。我几乎已放弃了她会前来的指望。也许，是我夺去了她梦游的能力。我正思忖着这个可能，忽然间，只觉自己仿佛正望进那间幽灵之室。它似乎被月亮照亮，月光透过彩色玻璃窗倾泻而入。这感觉来去骤然，正如对某处所在的一闪念通常那般；然而这一回，它带着一种难以言喻的东西，比那类对往昔的复现——无论出于有心还是无意——所惯有的更为清晰、更为真切；一股强烈的渴望攫住了我，想要再往那间屋里看上一眼。我怀着一种向不可抗拒之力俯首的心情起身，出了房间，一路摸索穿过门厅，登上橡木楼梯——我压根没想到该带一盏灯——走进了廊道。刚一踏入廊道，一个念头便在心头猛地跳出——“万一她在那里呢！”我的心停了一拍，像一头受了伤的鹿，随即又狂跳起来，每一下都带着一阵刺痛。廊道本身就是黑夜本身，只有窗外透进的一线幽暗的蓝灰微光，堪堪勾勒出窗子所在的位置。我悄然穿行其中，一步不差，径直走向那间幽灵之室。门半掩着。我走进去，为眼前那朦胧、幽秘、如梦似幻的美丽弄得一阵迷惘。月光正满满地照在窗上，千万道彩色的光与影在墙壁和地板上交错、缠绕，一切都那样柔和，那样交融，那样不可分辨，直把人的脑子填得满满当当，仿佛一场幽魅的彩虹在明灭舞动。但我几乎没有余暇去细想这些；因为从屋里唯一那处幽暗的角落里，飘出一个白色的身影，掠过那片光影的混沌，所过之处，被万千色彩沾润、洒落、溅满。我无言无语，一动不动，心头涌动着一种远胜于欣喜的东西。爱丽丝小姐发出一声低低的、欢愉的呢喃，倒入我的怀中。随即她抬起头来，轻轻一笑——“如今攻守易势了，亲爱的，”她说，“此刻你落在我的手心里了；是我把你引到这儿来的。我原想我能做到，便试了一试，因为我是这样渴望见你——而你，果然来了。”她领着我穿过房间，走到她方才坐着的地方，我们并肩坐下。

===Original===
"I thought you had forgotten me," I said, "or had grown tired of me."

"Did you? That was unkind. You have made my heart so still, that, body and soul, I sleep at night."

"Then shall I never see you more?"

"We can meet here. This is the best place. No one dares come near the haunted room at night. We might even venture in the evening. Look, now, from where we are sitting, across the air, between the windows and the shadows on the floor. Do you see nothing moving?"

===Chinese===
“我以为你已经把我忘了，”我说，“或是对我生了厌倦。”

“你是这样想的吗？那可真狠心。是你把我的心放得这样平静，平静到身与魂一体，夜里都能安然入睡。”

“那我岂不是再也见不到你了？”

“我们可以在这里相见。这里是最好的去处。夜里没人敢走近这间幽灵之室。我们甚至可以壮着胆子，在傍晚时分来。瞧，此刻，就从我们坐着的地方，望过这片虚空，望过窗子与地上阴影之间的那片空间。你当真什么也看不见在动吗？”

===Original===
I looked, but could see nothing. She resumed:—

"I almost fancy, sometimes, that what old stories say about this room may be true. I could fancy now that I see dim transparent forms in ancient armour, and in strange antique dresses, men and women, moving about, meeting, speaking, embracing, parting, coming and going. But I was never afraid of such beings. I am sure these would not, could not hurt us."

If the room was not really what it was well fitted to be—a rendezvous for the ghosts of the past—then either my imagination, becoming more active as she spoke, began to operate upon my brain, or her fancies were mysteriously communicated to me; for I was persuaded that I saw such dim undefined forms as she described, of a substance only denser than the moonlight, flitting, and floating about, between the windows and the illuminated floor. Could they have been coloured shadows thrown from the stained glass upon the fine dust with which the slightest motion in such an old and neglected room must fill its atmosphere? I did not think of that then, however.

===Chinese===
我望过去，却什么也看不见。她接着说：——

“有时候我几乎会以为，关于这间屋子流传的那些老话，或许是真的。此刻我就恍惚看见，有些朦胧透明的人影，穿着古旧的铠甲，穿着奇异的古式衣裳，有男有女，在四处走动、相会、交谈、相拥、分离、来来去去。可我从不害怕这样的存在。我确信他们不会、也不能伤害我们。”

倘若这间屋子并非真的如同它那般天然相宜、恰可作为的那样——一处供往昔亡魂聚首的所在——那么，要么是我那越听越活跃的想象，开始在我的脑中作祟，要么是她的种种遐想，被神秘地传递给了我；因为我竟信以为真，觉得自己看见了如她所描述的那般朦胧难辨的身影，其质料只比月光稍稍浓稠些许，在窗子与被照亮的地板之间翩跹、飘荡。莫非那是彩色玻璃投射在纤尘之上的彩色阴影？——在这样一间古老而久无人问的屋子里，最细微的一丝动静，都必然会让尘埃弥漫于空气之中。然而那时，我并未想到这一层。

===Original===
"I could persuade myself that I, too, see them," I replied. "I cannot say that I am afraid of such beings any more than you—if only they will not speak."

"Ah!" she replied, with a lengthened, meaning utterance, expressing sympathy with what I said; "I know what you mean. I, too, am afraid of hearing things. And that reminds me, I have never yet asked you about the galloping horse. I too hear sometimes the sound of a loose horseshoe. It always betokens some evil to me; but I do not know what it means. Do you?"

===Chinese===
“我也能说服自己，说我同样看见了他们，”我答道，“对于这样的存在，我也同你一样，说不上害怕——只要他们不开口说话。”

“啊！”她应道，那一声拖得长长的，意味深长，透出与我那番话的共鸣；“我懂你的意思。我也害怕听见一些声响。这倒提醒了我——那匹疾驰的马，我还从未问过你呢。我也时或听见松脱马蹄铁的声响。那于我总预示着某种凶险；可我不知道它究竟意味着什么。你知道吗？”

===Original===
"Do you know," I rejoined, "that there is a connection between your family and mine, somewhere far back in their histories?"

"No! Is there? How glad I am! Then perhaps you and I are related, and that is how we are so much alike, and have power over each other, and hear the same things."

"Yes. I suppose that is how."

"But can you account for that sound which we both hear?"

===Chinese===
“你可知道，”我接道，“在你我两家之间，在各自家世极遥远的某处，是有一段渊源的？”

“当真！竟有这事？我多高兴啊！那么，也许你和我本就是亲戚，正因如此，我们才这般相像，彼此间才有这般牵引之力，才会听见同样的声响。”

“是的。我想正是如此。”

“可是，我们俩都听见的那声响，你能解释得通吗？”

===Original===
"I will tell you what my old foster-mother told me," I replied. And I began by narrating when and where I had first heard the sound; and then gave her, as nearly as I could, the legend which nurse had recounted to me. I did not tell her its association with the events of my birth, for I feared exciting her imagination too much. She listened to it very quietly, however, and when I came to a close, only said:

"Of course, we cannot tell how much of it is true, but there may be something in it. I have never heard anything of the sort, and I, too, have an old nurse. She is with me still. You shall see her some day."

And we parted at the door of the haunted chamber. I watched the flickering with which her whiteness just set the darkness in motion, and nothing more, seeming to see it long after I knew she must have turned aside and descended the steps leading towards her own room. 于是我们在幽灵之室的门口作别。我望着她的白衣，那一点白仅只在黑暗中搅起一阵微微的摇曳，此外再无别的动静；即便在我明知她必然已经拐过弯、走下通往她自己房间的那段台阶之后许久，我仿佛仍看得见那点微光在晃动。随后我转过身，摸索着回到自己的房间。

===Original===
We often met after this in the haunted room. Indeed my spirit haunted it all day and all night long. And when we met amid the shadows, we were wrapped in the mantle of love, and from its folds looked out fearless on the ghostly world about us. Ghosts or none, they never annoyed us. Our love was a talisman, yea, an elixir of life, which made us equal to the twice-born—the disembodied dead. And they were as a wall of fear about us, to keep far off the unfriendly foot and the prying eye.

===Chinese===
此后我们便时常在这间幽灵之室中相会。事实上，我的魂灵日日夜夜都盘桓于此，不肯离去。当我们在阴影中相逢，便裹进了爱的氅衣，从它的褶皱间无畏地望着我们四周那鬼影憧憧的世界。无论那里究竟有没有鬼，他们从未惊扰过我们。我们的爱是一枚护符，甚而是一剂生命的灵药，使我们足以与那再生者——那脱离了肉身的亡魂——比肩而立。而他们则如同一道畏惧之墙环绕着我们，将不怀好意的脚步与窥探的目光远远隔开。

===Original===
In the griefs that followed, I often thought with myself that I would gladly die for a thousand years, might I then awake for one night in the haunted chamber, a ghost, among the ghosts who crowded its stained moonbeams, and see my dead Alice smiling across the glimmering rays, and beckoning me to the old nook, she, too, having come awake out of the sleep of death, in the dream of the haunted chamber. "Might we but sit there," I said, "through the night, as of old, and love and comfort each other, till the moon go down, and the pale dawn, which is the night of the ghosts, begin to arise, then gladly would I go to sleep for another thousand years, in the hope that when I next became conscious of life, it might be in another such ghostly night, in the chamber of the ghosts."

在后来接踵而至的那些悲痛里，我常常独自思忖：只要能在这间幽灵之室中再醒来一夜——化作一个鬼魂，跻身于那些簇拥在它彩色月光里的群鬼之间，看见我那已死的爱丽丝隔着明灭的光线含笑望我，向我招手，唤我回到那个旧日的角落，而她自己，也是在幽灵之室的这场大梦里，从死亡的沉睡中一同醒来的——那我便甘心死去一千年。“但愿我们能像从前那样，在那里坐上一整夜，”我说，“彼此相爱，彼此宽慰，直坐到月亮西沉，直坐到那惨淡的黎明——那黎明于幽魂而言便是黑夜——开始升起；那我便欣然再度沉睡上一个千年，只盼下一次再有了生命的知觉时，仍是在这样一个鬼魅的夜里，在这群鬼之室之中。”
