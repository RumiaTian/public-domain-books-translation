## V. Lady Alice / V. 爱丽丝小姐

===Original===
When the bell rang for dinner, I managed to find my way to the drawing-room, where were assembled Lady Hilton, her only daughter, a girl of about thirteen, and the two boys, my pupils. Lady Hilton would have been pleasant, could she have been as natural as she wished to appear. She received me with some degree of kindness; but the half-cordiality of her manner towards me was evidently founded on the impassableness of the gulf between us. I knew at once that we should never be friends; that she would never come down from the lofty tableland upon which she walked; and that if, after being years in the house, I should happen to be dying, she would send the housekeeper to me. All right, no doubt; I only say that it was so. She introduced to me my pupils; fine, open-eyed, manly English boys, with something a little overbearing in their manner, which speedily disappeared in relation to me. Lord Hilton was not at home. Lady Hilton led the way to the dining-room; the elder boy gave his arm to his sister, and I was about to follow with the younger, when from one of the deep bay windows glided out, still in white, the same figure which had passed me upon the lawn. I started, and drew back. With a slight bow, she preceded me, and followed the others down the great staircase. Seated at table, I had leisure to make my observations upon them all; but most of my glances found their way to the lady who, twice that day, had affected me like an apparition. What is time, but the airy ocean in which ghosts come and go!

===Chinese===
晚饭铃响时，我好不容易摸到了客厅，那里已经聚着希尔顿夫人（Lady Hilton）、她唯一的女儿——一个约莫十三岁的姑娘——还有那两个男孩，也就是我的学生。希尔顿夫人本可以是个令人愉快的人，只要她能像她所希望展现的那般自然。她带着几分和气接待了我；但她对我那种半热不冷的态度，分明是建立在我们之间那道不可逾越的鸿沟之上。我当即就明白：我们永远成不了朋友；她绝不会从她所行走的那片高高在上的台地上走下来；即便我在这宅子里住了许多年，有一日濒临死亡，她也只会把女管家打发到我床前。这自然无可厚非；我只不过如实说罢了。她把我的学生引见给我：那是两个清秀、眼神明亮、颇有男子气的英格兰男孩，举止间带着些许盛气凌人，不过在我面前这股气焰很快便消退了。希尔顿勋爵（Lord Hilton）不在家。希尔顿夫人领头往餐厅走去；大一点的男孩把胳膊伸给他的妹妹，我正要带着小的那个跟上去，这时，从一扇深深的凸窗里飘出一个身影——仍是一身白衣——正是白天在草坪上从我身旁经过的那个人。我一惊，不由得退后一步。她微微颔首，走在我前面，跟着其余的人下了那道大楼梯。入席之后，我有闲暇细细打量他们每一个人；但我的目光，大半都不由自主地落到了那位小姐身上——那一天里，她已两度像幽灵一般撼动了我。时间究竟是什么，不正是鬼魅们来来去去的那片空灵的海洋吗！

She was about twenty years of age; rather above the middle height, and rather slight in form; her complexion white rather than pale, her face being only less white than the deep marbly whiteness of her arms. Her eyes were large, and full of liquid night﻿—a night throbbing with the light of invisible stars. Her hair seemed raven-black, and in quantity profuse. The expression of her face, however, generally partook more of vagueness than any other characteristic. Lady Hilton called her Lady Alice; and she never addressed Lady Hilton but in the same ceremonious style.

I afterwards learned from the old housekeeper, that Lady Alice’s position in the family was a very peculiar one. Distantly connected with Lord Hilton’s family on the mother’s side, she was the daughter of the late Lord Glendarroch, and stepdaughter to Lady Hilton, who had become Lady Hilton within a year after Lord Glendarroch’s death. Lady Alice, then quite a child, had accompanied her stepmother, to whom she was moderately attached, and who had been allowed to retain undisputed possession of her. She had no near relatives, else the fortune I afterwards found to be at her disposal would have aroused contending claims to the right of guardianship.

===Chinese===
她约莫二十岁的年纪；身量略高于中等，体态颇为纤细；她的肤色与其说是苍白，不如说是白皙，而她面庞的白，只比她双臂那种深沉如大理石般的白略逊一筹。她的眼睛很大，盛满了流动的夜——那是一个搏动着无数看不见的星光之夜。她的头发看去乌黑如鸦，发量极丰。然而她脸上的神情，通常更多地带着一种恍惚迷离，胜过任何别的特质。希尔顿夫人唤她爱丽丝小姐（Lady Alice）；而她称呼希尔顿夫人，也总是用同一种客套郑重的口气。

后来我从那位老女管家口中得知，爱丽丝小姐在这个家里的地位十分特殊。她母系一支与希尔顿勋爵家有些远亲关系，是已故格兰达洛赫勋爵（Lord Glendarroch）的女儿，也是希尔顿夫人的继女——希尔顿夫人在格兰达洛赫勋爵去世后不到一年，便成了希尔顿夫人。爱丽丝小姐当时还是个小小的孩童，随她的继母一同过来；她对继母算是有几分依恋，而继母也被获准无可争议地保有对她的抚养。她再没有更近的亲属，否则，我后来发现归她支配的那笔财产，必会引出争夺监护权的种种诉求。

===Original===
Although she was in many respects kindly treated by her stepmother, certain peculiarities tended to her isolation from the family pursuits and pleasures. Lady Alice had no accomplishments. She could neither spell her own language, nor even read it aloud. Yet she delighted in reading to herself, though, for the most part, books which Mrs. Wilson characterised as very odd. Her voice, when she spoke, had a quite indescribable music in it; yet she neither sang nor played. Her habitual motion was more like a rhythmical gliding than an ordinary walk, yet she could not dance. Mrs. Wilson hinted at other and more serious peculiarities, which she either could not, or would not describe; always shaking her head gravely and sadly, and becoming quite silent, when I pressed for further explanation; so that, at last, I gave up all attempts to arrive at an understanding of the mystery by her means. Not the less, however, I speculated on the subject.

One thing soon became evident to me: that she was considered not merely deficient as to the power of intellectual acquirement, but in a quite abnormal intellectual condition. Of this, however, I could myself see no sign. The peculiarity, almost oddity, of some of her remarks, was evidently not only misunderstood, but, with relation to her mental state, misinterpreted. Such remarks Lady Hilton generally answered only by an elongation of the lips intended to represent a smile. To me, they appeared to indicate a nature closely allied to genius, if not identical with it﻿—a power of regarding things from an original point of view, which perhaps was the more unfettered in its operation from the fact that she was incapable of looking at them in the ordinary commonplace way. It seemed to me, sometimes, as if her point of observation was outside of the sphere within which the thing observed took place; and as if what she said, had a relation, occasionally, to things and thoughts and mental conditions familiar to her, but at which not even a definite guess could be made by me. I am compelled to acknowledge, however, that with such utterances as these mingled now and then others, silly enough for any drawing-room young lady; which seemed again to be accepted by the family as proofs that she was not *altogether* out of her right mind. She was gentle and kind to the children, as they were still called; and they seemed reasonably fond of her.

===Chinese===
尽管她继母在许多方面待她不薄，但若干特殊的禀性，却使她与一家人日常的消遣和乐事隔绝开来。爱丽丝小姐没有任何才艺。她既不能正确拼写自己的母语，甚至也不能把它大声朗读出来。然而她却酷爱默读，尽管读的多半是些被威尔逊太太（Mrs. Wilson）称作“古怪得很”的书。她开口说话时，声音里有一种难以形容的乐感；可她既不唱歌，也不弹奏。她日常的步态，与其说是寻常的行走，不如说更像一种合乎节奏的飘移，可她却不会跳舞。威尔逊太太还暗示了另一些更为要紧的怪异之处，却要么不能、要么不肯明说；每当我追问得更紧，她总是神情凝重而哀伤地摇着头，继而缄口不言；于是我最终放弃了借她之口探明这桩谜团的全部努力。然而，我对这件事的揣测，却并未因此稍减。

很快有一件事在我看来便了然了：在众人眼中，她不仅是心智学习能力有所欠缺，而且处于一种颇不正常的心智状态。然而这一点，我自己却看不出任何迹象。她有些话语所带的那种特殊、近乎古怪的意味，显然不仅被人误解，而且就她的精神状态而言，被作了错误的解读。这类话希尔顿夫人通常只用一个拉长嘴唇、意在表示微笑的动作来作答。在我看来，它们所指涉的，是一种与天才若合符节、乃至本质同一的天性——一种从独创的视角去观照事物的能力，而这能力之所以运作得如此无拘无束，或许正因为她是无法以寻常平庸的方式去看待事物的。有时我觉得，仿佛她的观察之点，正落在那被观察事物发生所在的领域之外；又仿佛她所说的，偶尔与她所熟稔的某些事物、念头和精神状态相关涉，而对这些，我连一个确切的猜测也难以做出。然而我不能不承认，在这样的言谈之间，时而也夹杂着另一些话，其浅薄足以媲美任何客厅里的娇小姐；而这些话，似乎又被这家人当作她神志并非*完全*失常的证据。她对那些孩子们——人们仍旧这样称呼他们——温婉而和善；而他们也似乎颇有几分喜欢她。

There was something to me exceedingly touching in the solitariness of this girl; for no one spoke to her as if she were like other people, or as if any heartiness were possible between them. Perhaps no one could have felt quite at home with her but a mother, whose heart had been one with hers from a season long anterior to the development of any repulsive oddity. But her position was one of peculiar isolation, for no one really approached her individual being; and that she should be unaware of this loneliness, seemed to me saddest of all. I soon found, however, that the most distant attempt on my part to show her attention, was either received with absolute indifference, or coldly repelled without the slightest acknowledgment.

But I return to the first night of my sojourn at Hilton Hall.

===Chinese===
这个姑娘身上那种孤零零的意味，于我而言有某种格外令人动容之处；因为没有人跟她说话时，是把她当作与常人一般无二的，也没有人仿佛觉得彼此之间能有半分真心。除了一个母亲，也许再没有人能在她身边感到完全自在——而那位母亲的心，早在她身上生出任何拒人千里的怪癖之前许久，便已与她同心相连。可她的处境却是一种格外彻底的孤绝，因为没有人真正触碰到她那个独立的自我；而她竟似浑然不觉这份孤独，这在我看来，是最令人心酸之处。然而我很快便发觉，但凡我稍作姿态，想要向她略献殷勤，所得到的回应要么是彻头彻尾的漠然，要么便是冷淡的拒斥，连一丝领情的表示也没有。

且让我回到我在希尔顿庄园（Hilton Hall）度过的第一个夜晚。
