## III. My Old Nurse's Story / III. 我老保姆的故事

===Original===
I was now almost nineteen. I had completed the usual curriculum of study at one of the Scotch universities; and, possessed of a fair knowledge of mathematics and physics, and what I considered rather more than a good foundation for classical and metaphysical acquirement, I resolved to apply for the first suitable situation that offered. But I was spared the trouble. A certain Lord Hilton, an English nobleman, residing in one of the midland counties, having heard that one of my father’s sons was desirous of such a situation, wrote to him, offering me the post of tutor to his two boys, of the ages of ten and twelve. He had been partly educated at a Scotch university; and this, it may be, had prejudiced him in favour of a Scotch tutor; while an ancient alliance of the families by marriage was supposed by my nurse to be the reason of his offering me the situation. Of this connection, however, my father said nothing to me, and it went for nothing in my anticipations. I was to receive a hundred pounds a year, and to hold in the family the position of a gentleman, which might mean anything or nothing, according to the disposition of the heads of the family. Preparations for my departure were immediately commenced. I set out one evening for the cottage of my old nurse, to bid her goodbye for many months, probably years. I was to leave the next day for Edinburgh, on my way to London, whence I had to repair by coach to my new abode—almost to me like the land beyond the grave, so little did I know about it, and so wide was the separation between it and my home. The evening was sultry when I began my walk, and before I arrived at its end, the clouds rising from all quarters of the horizon, and especially gathering around the peaks of the mountain, betokened the near approach of a thunderstorm. This was a great delight to me. Gladly would I take leave of my home with the memory of a last night of tumultuous magnificence; followed, probably, by a day of weeping rain, well suited to the mood of my own heart in bidding farewell to the best of parents and the dearest of homes. Besides, in common with most Scotchmen who are young and hardy enough to be unable to realise the existence of coughs and rheumatic fevers, it was a positive pleasure to me to be out in rain, hail, or snow.

===Chinese===
此时我年将十九。我已在苏格兰一所大学修毕了寻常的课程；凭着尚算扎实的数学与物理学识，又自忖于古典之学与思辨之学，已打下胜过"良好基础"的根基，便决意去谋头一个合宜的差事。然而这番周折，竟有人替我省了。有一位希尔顿勋爵（Lord Hilton），英格兰人，贵族，居于中部诸郡（the midland counties），听闻我父亲有个儿子正想寻这样一份差事，便写信给他，许我去做他家两个男孩——一个十岁、一个十二岁——的家庭教师。他自己在苏格兰的大学里读过一阵书，说不定正因如此，才偏向于要一位苏格兰籍的先生；而我的老保姆则以为，他之所以把这份差事给我，乃因两家之间有一桩由婚姻结成的远年旧谊。关于这层瓜葛，父亲对我只字未提，我在憧憬前程时也全不曾把它放在心上。我每年将得一百镑薪俸，并在那人家中居于"绅士"之位——这话或意味着什么都有，或意味着什么都没有，全看这家主事人的脾性。动身的诸般准备即刻着手做起。一日傍晚，我动身往老保姆的小屋去，为的是与她作别——这一别恐要历经数月，甚或数年。次日我便要启程赴爱丁堡（Edinburgh），取道伦敦（London），再从那里乘驿车赶往新的居所——那地方在我心中，几乎如同坟茔彼岸之地，我于它几乎一无所知，它与我的家之间又隔着迢迢天涯。我起程时傍晚闷热，未至终点，四面八方已升起云来，在那座山的群峰之上聚得尤紧，分明预示着雷暴将至。这于我却是一桩大大的快事。我乐于带着这样一个夜晚的记忆告别故园——一夜狂乱壮丽的喧嚣——其后多半还会继之以一个雨泪滂沱的白昼，正合我这颗心向最好的双亲、最可爱的家园道别时的心境。况且，凡年轻壮健、根本体认不到咳嗽与风湿热为何物的苏格兰人，多以为冒雨、冲雹、踏雪而行乃是确确实实的乐事，我亦与众人无异。

“Yes, indeed you must,” I replied.

"是呀，你确实得讲。"我答道。

===Original===
“How old the story is, I do not know. It has come down through many generations. My grandmother told it to me as I tell it to you; and her mother and my mother sat beside, never interrupting, but nodding their heads at every turn. Almost it ought to begin like the fairy tales, ‘Once upon a time,’—it took place so long ago; but it is too dreadful and too true to tell like a fairy tale.—There were two brothers, sons of the chief of our clan, but as different in appearance and disposition as two men could be. The elder was fair-haired and strong, much given to hunting and fishing; fighting too, upon occasion, I dare say, when they made a foray upon the Saxon, to get back a mouthful of their own. But he was gentleness itself to everyone about him, and the very soul of honour in all his doings. The younger was very dark in complexion, and tall and slender compared to his brother. He was very fond of book-learning, which, they say, was an uncommon taste in those times. He did not care for any sports or bodily exercises but one; and that, too, was unusual in these parts. It was horsemanship. He was a fierce rider, and as much at home in the saddle as in his study-chair. You may think that, so long ago, there was not much fit room for riding hereabouts; but, fit or not fit, he rode. From his reading and riding, the neighbours looked doubtfully upon him, and whispered about the black art. He usually bestrode a great powerful black horse, without a white hair on him; and people said it was either the devil himself, or a demon-horse from the devil’s own stud. What favoured this notion was, that, in or out of the stable, the brute would let no other than his master go near him. Indeed, no one would venture, after he had killed two men, and grievously maimed a third, tearing him with his teeth and hoofs like a wild beast. But to his master he was obedient as a hound, and would even tremble in his presence sometimes.

===Chinese===
"这故事有多古老，我也不晓得。它历经许多代人流传下来。我祖母怎样讲给我听，我便怎样讲给你听；她的母亲和我的母亲坐在一旁，从不插嘴，只在每一个转折处连连点头。它几乎该像童话那样起头——'从前'——因为那是太久太久以前的事了；可它又太可怖、太真实，不能当童话来讲。——从前有兄弟二人，是我们氏族首领的儿子，可二人的相貌与性情，竟有如天壤之别。兄长金发，体魄雄健，酷嗜狩猎与垂钓；遇着出征，我想也必不甘后人——那时候他们常去劫掠撒克逊人（the Saxon），好夺回一口本该属于自己的东西。然而他对身边众人，却又温存体贴至极，所行所为，无不以荣誉为立身之本。弟弟肤色黧黑，较之乃兄，身量高而单薄。他极好读书，据说在那个年代，这是少见的癖好。种种游猎武戏他一概不喜，唯独钟情一样；而那一样，在这些地方也是稀罕的——便是马术。他是个暴烈的骑手，在马鞍之上，犹如在书斋的座椅上一般安适。你或以为，在那么久远的年月，这附近并无多少宜于驰骋的去处；可是宜与不宜，他只管骑。因了他的好读书与好驰马，邻里看他都满腹狐疑，私底下嘀咕着什么邪术黑巫。他胯下常是一匹高大神骏的黑马，通体不见一根杂毛；人们便说，那要么就是魔鬼本尊，要么就是魔鬼自家马厩里牵出来的一匹妖马。助长这种说法的是：无论是在厩中还是在厩外，那畜生除了主人，谁也不肯挨近。说实话，在它踢死两人、又将第三人撕得血肉模糊——用牙用蹄如野兽一般——之后，再没人敢上前了。可它在主人面前，却驯顺得像条猎犬，有时甚至在他跟前瑟瑟发抖。

===Original===
“The youth’s temper corresponded to his habits. He was both gloomy and passionate. Prone to anger, he had never been known to forgive. Debarred from anything on which he had set his heart, he would have gone mad with longing if he had not gone mad with rage. His soul was like the night around us now, dark, and sultry, and silent, but lighted up by the red levin of wrath and torn by the bellowings of thunder-passion. He must have his will: hell might have his soul. Imagine, then, the rage and malice in his heart, when he suddenly became aware that an orphan girl, distantly related to them, who had lived with them for nearly two years, and whom he had loved for almost all that period, was loved by his elder brother, and loved him in return. He flung his right hand above his head, swore a terrible oath that if he might not, his brother should not, rushed out of the house, and galloped off among the hills.

===Chinese===
"这少年的性情，正与他的习性相副。他阴郁而又暴戾。动辄发怒，从无宽恕之名。凡是他心头所系之物，若被横加阻断，他若不因渴慕而狂，便要因暴怒而疯。他的灵魂就像此刻围裹着我们的夜——幽暗、闷热、死寂——却被怒火的红电照得通明，被雷鸣般的激情撕扯得支离破碎。他非达目的不可：哪怕将灵魂交予地狱。那么，你且想象他心头是何等的狂怒与怨毒——当他骤然发觉，一个与他们沾着一点远亲的孤女，在他们家中住了将近两年，而他也爱了她几乎整整两年，竟被他的兄长所爱，并且也爱他的兄长。他把右手往头顶一挥，发下一个可怖的毒誓：既然他得不到，他的兄弟也休想得到；随即冲出屋外，纵马驰入了群山之间。

===Original===
“The orphan was a beautiful girl, tall, pale, and slender, with plentiful dark hair, which, when released from the snood, rippled down below her knees. Her appearance formed a strong contrast with that of her favoured lover, while there was some resemblance between her and the younger brother. This fact seemed, to his fierce selfishness, ground for a prior claim.

===Chinese===
"那孤女生得极美，身量修长，肤色苍白，体态轻盈，一头浓密的黑发，一旦从发带中解放出来，便如波涛般垂至膝下。她的容止与她那位蒙她青睐的情人恰成强烈对照，而与那弟弟之间，倒有几分相似。这一层，在他那暴烈的私心看来，便是他先着人、先有主张的凭据。

===Original===
“It may appear strange that a man like him should not have had instant recourse to his superior and hidden knowledge, by means of which he might have got rid of his rival with far more of certainty and less of risk; but I presume that, for the moment, his passion overwhelmed his consciousness of skill. Yet I do not suppose that he foresaw the mode in which his hatred was about to operate. At the moment when he learned their mutual attachment, probably through a domestic, the lady was on her way to meet her lover as he returned from the day’s sport. The appointed place was on the edge of a deep, rocky ravine, down in whose dark bosom brawled and foamed a little mountain torrent. You know the place, Duncan, my dear, I dare say.”

(Here she gave me a minute description of the spot, with directions how to find it.)

===Chinese===
"像他这等人，竟没有立刻诉诸自己所通晓的那隐秘高超之学——借那手段，他本可更有把握、更少凶险地除去情敌——这听来或许奇怪；但我料想，那一刻他的激情盖过了他对自家伎俩的自觉。可我也并不以为，他当时便已预见到他的仇恨将以何种方式发作。在他得知二人两情相悦的那一刻——多半是从一个仆人口中得知的——那女子正动身去迎她打了一日野味归来的情人。二人相约之地，在一道幽深多石的岩壑边上，壑底幽暗的怀抱中，一条山涧奔腾着、翻起白沫。那地方，邓肯，我的心肝，我想你是晓得的。"

（此处她把这地点细细向我描绘了一番，又教我如何才能寻到它。）

===Original===
“Whether anyone saw what I am about to relate, or whether it was put together afterwards, I cannot tell. The story is like an old tree—so old that it has lost the marks of its growth. But this is how my grandmother told it to me.—An evil chance led him in the right direction. The lovers, startled by the sound of the approaching horse, parted in opposite directions along a narrow mountain-path on the edge of the ravine. Into this path he struck at a point near where the lovers had met, but to opposite sides of which they had now receded; so that he was between them on the path. Turning his horse up the course of the stream, he soon came in sight of his brother on the ledge before him. With a suppressed scream of rage, he rode headlong at him, and ere he had time to make the least defence, hurled him over the precipice. The helplessness of the strong man was uttered in one single despairing cry as he shot into the abyss. Then all was still. The sound of his fall could not reach the edge of the gulf. Divining in a moment that the lady, whose name was Elsie, must have fled in the opposite direction, he reined his steed on his haunches. He could touch the precipice with his bridle-hand half outstretched; his sword-hand half outstretched would have dropped a stone to the bottom of the ravine. There was no room to wheel. One desperate practicability alone remained. Turning his horse’s head towards the edge, he compelled him, by means of the powerful bit, to rear till he stood almost erect; and so, his body swaying over the gulf, with quivering and straining muscles, to turn on his hind-legs. Having completed the half-circle, he let him drop, and urged him furiously in the opposite direction. It must have been by the devil’s own care that he was able to continue his gallop along that ledge of rock.

===Chinese===
"我说下来这些，究竟有没有人亲眼瞧见，还是事后才被人拼凑出来的，我无从晓得。这故事就像一棵老树——老得连生长的痕迹都已磨灭。可我祖母便是这样讲给我听的。——一股邪缘引他走了正路。那对情人被渐近的马蹄声惊动，便沿着崖边一条狭窄的山径，各向相反的方向避去。他恰在二人相会之处附近扑上这条小径，而此时他们已退向径的两端；于是他便居于二人之间。他策马溯流而上，不多时便望见兄长在前方的岩架上。他压低一声狂怒的尖叫，纵马直冲过去，趁兄长来不及作丝毫抵挡，便把他掀下了悬崖。那壮士的无力回天，尽凝作一声绝望的呼号，随他一同坠入深渊。而后万籁俱寂。他坠落的声响，连崖边都到不了。他霎时便料定，那名叫埃尔西（Elsie）的女子，必已朝相反方向逃去，便猛地勒马，叫它坐回后蹄上。他勒缰的那只手微微伸出，几乎可触到悬崖；持剑的那只手若也微微一伸，便能将一块石头直落到壑底。根本没有回旋的余地。唯有一桩拼死可行之计尚存。他把马头朝向崖边，借着那副厉害的马衔铁逼它人立而起，几乎直立；而后，马身悬在深渊之上，筋肉颤栗绷紧，就凭两条后腿转了半个圈。半圈既成，他放它落下，发疯般地朝相反方向催它疾驰。他能沿着那道岩架继续飞奔，必是得了魔鬼亲自照看。

“He soon caught sight of the maiden. She was leaning, half fainting, against the precipice. She had heard her lover’s last cry, and although it had conveyed no suggestion of his voice to her ear, she trembled from head to foot, and her limbs would bear her no farther. He checked his speed, rode gently up to her, lifted her unresisting, laid her across the shoulders of his horse, and, riding carefully till he reached a more open path, dashed again wildly along the mountainside. The lady’s long hair was shaken loose, and dropped trailing on the ground. The horse trampled upon it, and stumbled, half dragging her from the saddlebow. He caught her, lifted her up, and looked at her face. She was dead. I suppose he went mad. He laid her again across the saddle before him, and rode on, reckless whither. Horse, and man, and maiden were found the next day, lying at the foot of a cliff, dashed to pieces. It was observed that a hind-shoe of the horse was loose and broken. Whether this had been the cause of his fall, could not be told; but ever when he races, as race he will, till the day of doom, along that mountainside, his gallop is mingled with the clank of the loose and broken shoe. For, like the sin, the punishment is awful: he shall carry about for ages the phantom-body of the girl, knowing that her soul is away, sitting with the soul of his brother, down in the deep ravine, or scaling with him the topmost crags of the towering mountain-peaks. There are some who, from time to time, see the doomed man careering along the face of the mountain, with the lady hanging across the steed; and they say it always betokens a storm, such as this which is now raving around us.”
