## XVIII. Old Friends / XVIII. 故人

===Original===
I made the journey by easy stages, chiefly on the back of a favourite black horse, which had carried me well in several fights, and had come out of them scarred, like his master, but sound in wind and limb. It was night when I reached the village lying nearest to my birth place.

When I woke in the morning, I found the whole region filled with a white mist, hiding the mountains around. Now and then a peak looked through, and again retired into the cloudy folds. In the wide, straggling street, below the window at which I had made them place my breakfast-table, a periodic fair was being held; and I sat looking down on the gathering crowd, trying to discover some face known to my childhood, and still to be recognized through the veil which years must have woven across the features. When I had finished my breakfast, I went down and wandered about among the people. Groups of elderly men were talking earnestly; and young men and maidens who had come to be fee’d, were joking and laughing. They stared at the Sassenach gentleman, and, little thinking that he understood every word they uttered, made their remarks upon him in no very subdued tones. I approached a stall where a brown old woman was selling gingerbread and apples. She was talking to a man with long, white locks. Near them was a group of young people. One of them must have said something about me; for the old woman, who had been taking stolen glances at me, turned rather sharply towards them, and rebuked them for rudeness.

===Chinese===
我一路分程而行，从容不迫，多半骑着一匹心爱的黑马。这匹马几番随我冲锋陷阵，从战火里出来时也和它的主人一样带着几处伤痕，可心肺完健、四肢无恙。待我抵达那座离我出生之地最近的村庄时，已是夜色深沉。

次日清晨醒来，只见四野都被一片白雾笼罩，把周遭的群山尽数遮没。偶尔有一座峰峦从雾中探出头来，转瞬又退回那云雾的褶皱之中。在我让人把早餐桌摆好的那扇窗下，一条宽阔而散乱的街道上，正开着定期的集市；我便坐着俯瞰那越聚越多的人群，想从中认出某张童年时相识、纵使岁月必已在面容上织就一层帷幕、却仍能辨识的脸来。吃过早饭，我下楼走到人群里四处闲逛。一群群上了年纪的男人正谈得入神；那些赶来受雇的年轻男女，则在一旁打趣说笑。他们盯着这位撒克逊人（Sassenach）模样的绅士瞧，全然想不到他竟听得懂他们嘴里吐出的每一个字，便用并不怎么压低的嗓门对他评头论足。我走到一个摊子跟前，一位皮肤黧黑的老妇人正在那里卖姜饼和苹果。她正与一个披着一头长白发的男人说话。他们近旁围着一群年轻人。其中一个想必是说了句什么关于我的话；因为那老妇人——她方才一直在偷眼打量我——颇为突兀地转过身朝着他们，责备他们无礼。

===Original===
“The gentleman is no Sassenach,” she said. “He understands everything you are saying.”

This was spoken in Gaelic, of course. I turned and looked at her with more observance. She made me a courtesy, and said, in the same language:

“Your honour will be a Campbell, I’m thinking.”

“I am a Campbell,” I answered, and waited.

“Your honour’s Christian name wouldn’t be Duncan, sir?”

“It is Duncan,” I answered; “but there are many Duncan Campbells.”

“Only one to me, your honour; and that’s yourself. But you will not remember me?”

===Chinese===
「这位绅士可不是撒克逊人，」她说，「你们说的每一个字他都听得懂。」

这番话自然是用盖尔语（Gaelic）说的。我转过身，更加留心地打量起她来。她朝我行了一个屈膝礼，用同一种语言说道：

「老爷您，我这么琢磨着，该是坎贝尔家的人吧。」

「我正是坎贝尔家的人，」我答道，便等着她说下去。

「老爷您的教名，该不会是邓肯吧，先生？」

「正是邓肯，」我答道，「不过世上叫邓肯·坎贝尔的多得很。」

「于我却只有一位，老爷；那便是您本人。可您怕是不记得我了吧？」

===Original===
I did not remember her. Before long, however, urged by her anxiety to associate her Present with my Past, she enabled me to recall in her timeworn features those of a servant in my father’s house when I was a child.

“But how could you recollect me?” I said.

“I have often seen you since I left your father’s, sir. But it was really, I believe, that I hear more about you than anything else, every day of my life.”

“I do not understand you.”

“From old Margaret, I mean.”

“Dear old Margaret! Is she alive?”

===Chinese===
我的确不记得她了。然而不多时，在她那急于将自己的如今与我的往昔勾连起来的热切催促之下，我终于从她那被岁月磨蚀的面容里，唤回了一个我幼年时父亲家中女仆的影子。

「可你怎么会认得我呢？」我说。

「自打离开您父亲家以后，我也常能见着您哪，先生。可说实话，只怕是这一辈子里，我每天听的、比什么都多的，就是关于您的事儿。」

「我不大懂你的意思。」

「我是说，从老玛格丽特那里听来的。」

「亲爱的老玛格丽特！她还健在？」

===Original===
“Alive and hearty, though quite bedridden. Why, sir, she must be within near sight of a hundred.”

“Where does she live?”

“In the old cottage, sir. Nothing will make her leave it. The new laird wanted to turn her out; but Margaret muttered something at which he grew as white as his shirt, and he has never ventured across her threshold again.”

“How do you see so much of her, though?”

“I never leave her, sir. She can’t wait on herself, poor old lady. And she’s like a mother to me. Bless her! But your honour will come and see her?”

“Of course I will. Tell her so when you go home.”

===Chinese===
「健在着哪，精神也足，只是已全然卧床不起了。嗳呀，先生，她离一百岁怕是近在眼前了。」

「她住在哪儿？」

「还住在那座老石屋里哪，先生。怎么劝都不肯挪窝。新来的领主（laird）要把她赶出去；可玛格丽特嘟囔了几句什么，竟把他吓得脸白得跟他的衬衫一个样，打那以后，他就再没敢跨过她那道门槛了。」

「可你又是怎么能常常见到她的呢？」

「我就没离开过她身边，先生。她自己照料不了自己啦，可怜的老太太。她待我就跟亲娘一样。愿上帝保佑她！可老爷您会来看看她吧？」

「我自然要去。你回家时就这么对她说。」

===Original===
“Will you honour me by sleeping at my house, sir?” said the old man to whom she had been talking. “My farm is just over the brow of the hill, you know.”

I had by this time recognised him, and I accepted his offer at once.

“When might we look for you, sir?” he asked.

“When shall you be home?” I rejoined.

“This afternoon, sir. I have done my business already.”

“Then I shall be with you in the evening, for I have nothing to keep me here.”

===Chinese===
「先生肯赏光到舍下歇宿一夜么？」方才与她说话的那个老人开口道，「我的农庄就在这山脊那一边，您是晓得的。」

这时我也已认出他来，便当即应下了他的邀请。

「先生，我们什么时候可以恭候您光临呢？」他问。

「你什么时候到家？」我反问道。

「今天下午，先生。我的事已经办完了。」

「那我傍晚便到府上叨扰，因为我没有什么事要在此处逗留。」

===Original===
“Will you take a seat in my gig?”

“No, thank you. I have my own horse with me. You can take him in too, I dare say?”

“With pleasure, sir.”

We parted for the meantime. I rambled about the neighbourhood till it was time for an early dinner.

===Chinese===
「先生坐我的轻便马车去可好？」

「不了，多谢。我自己骑了马来。我想，你那儿也能一并容得下它吧？」

「乐意之至，先生。」

我们就此暂且作别。我在邻近一带四处闲逛，直逛到该用一顿略早的正餐的时辰。
