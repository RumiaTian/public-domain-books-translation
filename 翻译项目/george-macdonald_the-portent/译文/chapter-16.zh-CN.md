## XVI. The Clanking Shoe / XVI. 叮当作响的马蹄铁

===Original===
Time passed. We began to feel very secure in that room, watched as it was by the sleepless sentry, Fear. One night I ventured to take a light with me.

“How nice to have a candle!” she said as I entered. “I hope they are all in bed, though. It will drive some of them into fits if they see the light.”

“I wanted to show you something I found in the library today.”

“What is it?”

I opened a book, and showed her a paper inside it, with some verses written on it.

===Chinese===
时光流逝。我们开始觉得在那间房里十分安稳，那房间既由恐惧这位不眠的哨兵守卫着。一天夜里，我大着胆子带了一支灯火前去。

“能有支蜡烛真好！”我一进门，她便说，“不过，我盼他们都已经上床睡了。要是被谁瞧见这光，会把他们中几个吓得抽风过去的。”

“我想给你看一样今天我在藏书室里找到的东西。”

“什么东西？”

我打开一本书，给她看夹在里面的那张纸，上头写着几行诗句。

===Original===
“Whose writing is that?” I asked.

“Yours, of course. As if I did not know your writing!”

“Will you look at the date?”

“*Seventeen hundred and ninety-three!* You are making game of me, Duncan. But the paper does look yellow and old.”

===Chinese===
“这是谁的字迹？”我问。

“当然是你的。好像我不认得你的字似的！”

“你看看那个日期？”

“*一七九三年*！你在拿我寻开心吧，邓肯。不过这纸瞧着倒真是又黄又旧。”

===Original===
“I found it as you see it, in that book. It belonged to Lord Hilton’s brother. The verses are a translation of part of the poem beside which they lie—one by Von Salis, who died shortly before that date at the bottom. I will read them to you, and then show you something else that is strange about them. The poem is called ‘Psyche’s Sorrow.’ Psyche means the soul, Alice.”

===Chinese===
“我找到它时，它就是你眼下看到的这副模样，夹在那本书里。那本书原是希尔顿勋爵的兄弟的。这几行诗，是翻译了与它并排的那首诗的一部分——那首诗出自冯·萨利斯（Von Salis）的手笔，他就在底下那个日期之前不久辞世。我读给你听，然后再让你瞧瞧它还有一处古怪的地方。那首诗题为《心灵之恸》（‘Psyche’s Sorrow’）。‘Psyche’（普赛克）就是灵魂的意思，爱丽丝。”

===Original===
“Psyche’s sighing all her prison darkens;
She is moaning for the far-off stars;
Fearing, hoping, every sound she hearkens—
Fate may now be breaking at her bars.

Bound, fast bound, are Psyche’s airy pinions:
High her heart, her mourning soft and low—
Knowing that in sultry pain’s dominions
Grow the palms that crown the victor’s brow;

That the empty hand the wreath encloses;
Earth’s cold winds but make the spirit brave;
Knowing that the briars bear the roses,
Golden flowers the waste deserted grave.

In the cypress-shade her myrtle groweth;
Much she loves, because she much hath borne;
Love-led, through the darksome way she goeth—
On to meet him in the breaking morn.

She can bear—”

“普赛克的叹息将她整个牢笼染暗；
她为那遥不可及的星辰哀泣；
怀着恐惧，怀着希望，她谛听每一丝声响——
命运此刻也许正撞断她的铁栏。

普赛克那轻盈的双翼被紧紧缚住：
她的心高扬，她的哀歌轻柔低回——
她深知，在酷热痛苦的疆域里，
正生长着加冕胜者额头的棕榈；

深知空空的手掌也能拢住花环；
尘世的寒风只会叫灵魂越发勇敢；
深知荆棘之上会开出玫瑰，
荒寂的弃冢也会绽放金色的花朵。

在柏树的浓荫下，她的桃金娘生长；
她爱得深，因为她承受得多；
在爱的引领下，她穿过幽暗的路途——
前去，在破晓的晨光里与他相会。

她能承受——”

===Original===
“Here the translation ceases, you see; and then follows the date, with the words in German underneath it—‘How weary I am!’ Now what is strange, Alice, is, that this date is the very month and year in which I was born.”

She did not reply to this with anything beyond a mere assent. Her mind was fixed on the poem itself. She began to talk about it, and I was surprised to find how thoroughly she entered into it and understood it. She seemed to have crowded the growth of a lifetime into the last few months. At length I told her how unhappy I had felt for some time, at remaining in Lord Hilton’s house, as matters now were.

===Chinese===
“你看，译诗到这里就断了；接下来便是那个日期，底下还附了一行德文——‘我好疲倦啊！’爱丽丝，离奇的是，这个日期，正正是我出生的那个年月。”

她对这番话的回应，不过是淡淡地应了一声。她的心神全落在那首诗本身上。她开始谈论起它来，而令我惊讶的是，她领会得何等透彻，懂得何等深切。她仿佛把一生的成长，都压缩进了过去的这几个月里。终于，我告诉她，时局既已如此，我还留在希尔顿勋爵家中，这一阵子心里有多不安。

===Original===
“Then you must go,” she said, quite quietly.

This troubled me.

“You do not mind it?”

“No. I shall be very glad.”

“Will you go with me?” I asked, perplexed.

“Of course I will.”

===Chinese===
“那你就得走了。”她说，语气十分平静。

这话叫我心中一沉。

“你不介意我走？”

“不。我会很高兴的。”

“你愿意跟我一道走吗？”我困惑地问。

“当然愿意。”

===Original===
I did not know what to say to this, for I had no money, and of course I should have none of my salary. She divined at once the cause of my hesitation.

“I have a diamond bracelet in my room,” she said, with a smile, “and a few guineas besides.”

“How shall we get away?”

“Nothing is easier. My old nurse, whom I mentioned to you before, lives at the lodge gate.”

“Oh! I know her very well,” I interrupted. “But she’s not Scotch?”

“Indeed she is. But she has been with our family almost all her life. I often go to see her, and sometimes stay all night with her. You can get a carriage ready in the village, and neither of us will be missed before morning.”

===Chinese===
我不知该对此说什么才好，因为我身无分文，而那份薪俸自然也是半个便士都拿不到的。她立刻便猜透了我犹豫的缘由。

“我房里有一只钻石手镯，”她带着笑说，“此外还有几个几尼。”

“我们怎么走得了呢？”

“再容易不过了。我从前跟你提过的那位老保姆，就住在庄园大门的门房里。”

“哦！她我熟得很，”我插嘴道，“可她不是苏格兰人吧？”

“她确实是苏格兰人。不过她几乎一辈子都跟在我们家里。我常常去看她，有时还在她那儿过夜。你可以在村子里把马车备好，明天早上之前，谁也不会发觉你我都不在了。”

===Original===
I looked at her in renewed surprise at the decision of her invention. She covered her face, as she seldom did now, but went on:

“We can go to London, where you will easily find something to do. Men always can there. And when I come of age—”

“Alice, how old are you?” I interrupted.

===Chinese===
我又惊又讶地望着她，为她那番筹谋的果决。她用手遮住了脸——这是她如今很少有的举动——却接着说下去：

“我们可以去伦敦，在那儿你很容易找到事做。男人在那里总找得到营生。等我到了年纪——”

“爱丽丝，你今年几岁了？”我插嘴问。

===Original===
“Nineteen,” she answered. “By the way,” she resumed, “when I think of it—how odd!—that”—pointing to the date on the paper—“is the very month in which I too was born.”

I was too much surprised to interrupt her, and she continued:

“I never think of my age without recalling one thing about my birth, which nurse often refers to. She was going up the stair to my mother’s room, when she happened to notice a bright star, not far from the new moon. As she crossed the room with me in her arms, just after I was born, she saw the same star almost on the tip of the opposite horn. My mother died a week after. Who knows how different I might have been if she had lived!”

===Chinese===
“十九。”她答道。“对了，”她接着说，“我想起来了——好生奇怪！——那个”——她指着纸上那个日期——“也正正是我出生的那个月份。”

我惊讶得无从插嘴，她便继续说下去：

“每逢想起自己的年纪，我总会记起一桩关于我降生的事，那是保姆常常提起的。那会儿她正上楼去我母亲的房间，偶然留意到一颗明亮的星，离那一弯新月不远。等我出生后不久，她抱着我穿过房间时，又看见了同一颗星，几乎贴在新月另一端的尖角上。我母亲过了一周便去世了。谁知道，倘若她活着，我又会是多么不同的一副模样！”

===Original===
It was long before I spoke. The awful and mysterious thoughts roused in my mind by the revelations of the day held me silent. At length I said, half thinking aloud:

“Then you and I, Alice, were born the same hour, and our mothers died together.”

===Chinese===
良久，我才开口。这一日所揭示的种种真相，在我心中唤起那些可畏而神秘的念头，叫我默然无语。终于，我半像自言自语地说：

“那么，爱丽丝，你和我，是同一个时辰生的，而我们的母亲，又是一同死去的。”

===Original===
Receiving no answer, I looked at her. She was fast asleep, and breathing gentle, full breaths. She had been sitting for some time with her head lying on my shoulder and my arm around her. I could not bear to wake her.

We had been in this position perhaps for half an hour, when suddenly a cold shiver ran through me, and all at once I became aware of the far-off gallop of a horse. It drew nearer. On and on it came—nearer and nearer. Then came the clank of the broken shoe!

At the same moment, Alice started from her sleep and, springing to her feet, stood an instant listening. Then crying out, in an agonised whisper—“The horse with the clanking shoe!” she flung her arms around me. Her face was white as the spectral moon which, the moment I put the candle out, looked in through a clear pane beside us; and she gazed fearfully, yet wildly-defiant, towards the door. We clung to each other. We heard the sound come nearer and nearer, till it thundered right up to the very door of the room, terribly loud. It ceased. But the door was flung open, and Lord Hilton entered, followed by servants with lights.

===Chinese===
不见她回答，我便看她。她已经沉沉睡去，呼吸轻柔而绵长。方才好一阵子，她一直把头靠在我肩上，我臂膀环着她。我不忍心叫醒她。

我们就这样保持着这个姿势，约莫过了半个钟头，忽然一阵寒颤传遍我全身，我猛然察觉到远处有一匹马在疾驰。它越来越近。一路奔来——越来越近。紧接着，传来了那只破裂马蹄铁叮当作响的声响！

就在同一瞬间，爱丽丝从睡中惊起，一跃站定，凝神听了片刻。随即她用痛苦万状的气声呼喊出声——“那匹挂着叮当作响马蹄铁的马！”她一把搂住了我。她的脸白得犹如那鬼魅般的月亮——我刚把蜡烛吹熄，月亮便从我们身旁一扇明净的窗格里探望进来；她满眼恐惧，却又带着一种狂乱的桀骜，朝门口望去。我们紧紧相拥。我们听见那声响越来越近，直到它轰然直冲到这间房的门前，响得骇人。声音停了。然而房门被猛地推开，希尔顿勋爵闯了进来，身后跟着举着灯火的仆人们。

===Original===
I have but a very confused remembrance of what followed. I heard a vile word from the lips of Lord Hilton; I felt my fingers on his throat; I received a blow on the head; and I seem to remember a cry of agony from Alice as I fell. What happened next I do not know.

When I came to myself, I was lying on a wide moor, with the night wind blowing about me. I presume that I had wandered thither in a state of unconsciousness, after being turned out of the Hall, and that I had at last fainted from loss of blood. I was unable to move for a long time. At length the morning broke, and I found myself not far from the Hall. I crept back, a mile or two, to the gates, and having succeeded in rousing Alice’s old nurse, was taken in with many lamentations, and put to bed in the lodge. I had a violent fever; and it was all the poor woman could do to keep my presence a secret from the family at the Hall.

===Chinese===
接下来发生的事，我只记得一片混乱。我听见希尔顿勋爵嘴里吐出一个下流的字眼；我感到自己的手指掐上了他的喉咙；我头上挨了一记重击；而在我倒下时，我似乎还记得爱丽丝发出一声痛苦的尖叫。再后来发生了什么，我便无从知晓了。

===Original===
When I began to mend, my first question was about Alice. I learned, though with some difficulty—for my kind attendant was evidently unwilling to tell me all the truth—that Alice, too, had been very ill; and that, a week before, they had removed her. But she either would not or could not tell me where they had taken her. I believe she could not. Nor do I know for certain to this day.

Mrs. Blakesley offered me the loan of some of her savings to get me to London. I received it with gratitude, and as soon as I was fit to travel, made my way thither. Through the help of one already mentioned in my story, I soon obtained a commission. From the field of Waterloo, I rode into Brussels with a broken arm and a sabre-cut in the head.

我坐着，在往事中冥想，直到在那群鬼攒动的夜里沉沉睡去。整整一夜，他们都在我四周——那些久远岁月里的男男女女。而我自己，也是他们中的一个。我在梦里游荡遍了整座宅子，身上穿着一件旧式的长袍，寻访一个既是爱丽丝、又像是另一个什么人的身影。我从这个房间游荡到那个房间，直到筋疲力尽，却始终寻她不着。终于，我放弃了搜寻，退回藏书室，把自己关在里面。在那儿，我从架上取下那本冯·萨利斯的诗集，竭力想从那位学生当年停笔之处，接着把《心灵之恸》的译文续完，并且自始至终，都把它当作我自己未竟的功课。

及至清晨我醒来，我入睡时那间鬼影攒动的屋子，已然消失了。阳光透过藏书室的窗户照了进来；在那落满灰尘的桌上，摊着冯·萨利斯那本诗集，正翻在《心灵之恸》（“Psyche’s Trauer”）那一页。而那张写着译文的纸，却不在那里了。我赶忙离开宅子，在仆人们起身之前，便已安然脱身。

===Original===
Sometimes I condensed my whole being into a single intensity of will—that she should come to me; and sustained it, until I fainted with the effort. She did not come. I desisted altogether at last, for I bethought me that, whether dead or alive, it must cause her torture not to be able to obey it.

Sometimes I questioned my own sanity. But the thought of the loss of my reason did not in itself trouble me much. What tortured me almost to the madness it supposed was the possible fact, which a return to my right mind might reveal—that there never had been a Lady Alice. What if I died, and awoke from my madness, and found a clear blue air of life, a joyous world of sunshine, a divine wealth of delight around and in me—but no Lady Alice—she having vanished with all the other phantoms of a sick brain! “Rather let me be mad still,” I said, “if mad I am; and so dream on that I have been blessed. Were I to wake to such a heaven, I would pray God to let me go and live the life I had but dreamed, with all its sorrows, and all its despair, and all its madness, that when I died again, I might know that such things had been, and could never be awaked from, and left behind with the dream.” But I was not mad, any more than Hamlet; though, like him, despair sometimes led me far along the way at the end of which madness lies.

有时，我把整个生命凝聚成一念专挚的心志——愿她来到我身边；并且苦苦撑持，直到我为此昏厥过去。她没有来。我终于彻底放弃了，因为我想到，无论她是死是活，不能顺从这一念召唤，于她必是一种折磨。

有时，我也会怀疑自己是否神志健全。然而，丧失理智这一念头本身，并不怎样令我忧心。真正把我折磨到几近癫狂的——正是这一念头所预设的那种癫狂——是一个可能的事实：一旦我恢复了清醒的心智，这事实也许便会显露——那就是，世上从来不曾有过一位爱丽丝小姐。倘若我死了，从癫狂中醒来，发觉眼前是一片澄澈蔚蓝、充盈生气的天空，一个阳光明媚的欢乐世界，一种神圣而丰盈的喜悦，将我环绕、将我充盈——却唯独没有爱丽丝小姐——她已随着这一颗病脑所生的其余种种幻影，一并消散了，那又当如何！“那就让我索性疯下去吧，”我说，“若我果然是疯的；好教我继续做着那曾经蒙福的梦。倘若我醒来，面对的竟是那样一个天堂，我定要祈求上帝，让我回去过那我以为只是梦见过的一生——连同它全部的悲伤、全部的绝望、全部的癫狂——好教我再次死去之时，能确知这一切都曾真实发生过，且永不能再被醒转、被当作一场梦抛在身后。”然而我并未发疯，正如哈姆雷特（Hamlet）未曾发疯一样；只不过，和他一样，绝望有时会把我引上一条长长的路，而那条路的尽头，便是癫狂。
