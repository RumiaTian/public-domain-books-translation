## XIII / 第十三章

===Original===
I had a sense of walking very fast﻿—almost of taking flight﻿—down a long dim corridor, and of a door that opened into an immense room. All that I remember of it, as I saw it then, was a number of pastel portraits of weak, vacuous individuals, in dulled, gilt, oval frames. The heads stood out from the panelling and stared at me from between ringlets, from under powdered hair, simpering, or contemptuous with the expression that must have prevailed in the monde of the time before the Revolution. At a great distance, bent over account-books and pink cheques on the flap of an escritoire, sat my aunt, very small, very grey, very intent on her work.

===Chinese===
我有一种感觉，觉得自己正沿着一条幽暗的长廊走得飞快——几乎是在飞逃——随后是一扇门，通向一间巨大的房间。当时眼中所见、如今还记得的，只有若干色粉肖像，画的是些孱弱、空虚的人物，框在发暗的镀金椭圆框里。那些头脸从护壁镶板上凸出来，瞪视着我——从长卷发的缝隙间，从扑粉的头发底下——或傻笑着，或不屑地摆出一副大革命前时代上流社会（monde）里必定盛行的神情。极远处，老姑母伏在一张斜面书桌翻下的活板上，对着账簿和粉红色支票，极小，极灰暗，极专注于她的活计。

===Original===
The people who built these rooms must have had some property of the presence to make them bulk large﻿—if they ever really did so﻿—in the eyes of dependents, of lackeys. Perhaps it was their sense of ownership that gave them the necessary prestige. My aunt, who was only a temporary occupant, certainly had none of it. Bent intently over her accounts, peering through her spectacles at columns of figures, she was nothing but a little old woman alone in an immense room. It seemed impossible that she could really have any family pride, any pride of any sort. She looked round at me over her spectacles, across her shoulder.

===Chinese===
当年造起这些房间的人，必定身怀某种在场的特质，才能在仆从、听差的眼里显得魁伟——倘若他们当真显得过的话。也许正是那份产业主的意识，给了他们必需的威望。我的老姑母不过是个临时房客，当然一丝一毫也没有。她专注地伏在账目上，隔着眼镜细看一栏栏数字，不过是个独坐巨大房间里的瘦小老妇人。她当真还能有什么家族的自尊、任何一种自尊，这似乎是不可能的事。她从肩膀上方、隔着眼镜回头看了看我。

===Original===
“Ah﻿ ﻿… Etchingham,” she said. She seemed to be trying to carry herself back to England, to the England of her land-agent and her select visiting list. Here she was no more superior than if we had been on a desert island. I wanted to enlighten her as to the woman she was sheltering﻿—wanted to very badly; but a necessity for introducing the matter seemed to arise as she gradually stiffened into assertiveness.

===Chinese===
“啊……埃钦厄姆，”她说。她似乎正竭力把自己带回英国去，带回那个有她的土地代理人、有她精选的拜客名单的英国。在这里，她并不比我们同处荒岛时更高人一等。我想让她认清她收留在屋檐下的那个女人——非常想；可是，随着她渐渐绷紧、摆出说一不二的架势，这件事倒似乎非得挑开不可了。

===Original===
“My dear aunt,” I said, “the woman.﻿ ﻿…” The alien nature of the theme grew suddenly formidable. She looked at me arousedly.

===Chinese===
“亲爱的姑母，”我说，“那个女人……”这个话题的异己性质陡然变得令人生畏。她激动地望着我。

===Original===
“You got my note then,” she said. “But I don’t think a woman *can* have brought it. I have given such strict orders. They have such strange ideas here, though. And Madame﻿—the *portière*﻿—is an old retainer of M. de Luynes, I haven’t much influence over her. It is absurd, but.﻿ ﻿…” It seems that the old lady in the lodge made a point of carrying letters that went by hand. She had an eye for gratuities﻿—and the police, I should say, were concerned. They make a good deal of use of that sort of person in that neighbourhood of infinitesimal and unceasing plotting.

===Chinese===
“这么说，便条你收到了，”她说。“可我想，一个女人总不至于送来吧。我吩咐得那么严。不过这地方的人想法古怪得很。还有那位太太——门房（portière）——是德·吕伊纳先生家的老仆，我说不上能左右她多少。荒唐得很，可是……”看来，门房里那位老太太认定了一桩事：凡靠人手传递的信件，都由她经手来送。她对赏钱有眼力——而且，我该说，警察也与此有关。在那片琐屑而无休止的密谋之地里，这类人是大有用场的。

===Original===
“I didn’t mean that,” I said, “but the woman who calls herself my sister.﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“我不是说这个，”我说，“我是说那个自称我姐妹的女人……”

===Original===
“My dear nephew,” she interrupted, with tranquil force, as if she were taking an arranged line, “I cannot﻿—I absolutely cannot be worried with your quarrels with your sister. As I said to you in my note of this morning, when you are in this town you must consider this house your home. It is almost insulting of you to go to an inn. I am told it is even﻿ ﻿… quite an unfit place that you are stopping at﻿—for a member of our family.”

===Chinese===
“亲爱的侄儿，”她打断我，带着一种平静的强硬，仿佛在照着一条排定好的路线行事，“我不能——我绝对不能让你和你姐妹的争吵来烦扰我。正像我今天早晨便条里对你说的，你在这座城里，就得把这所宅子当你的家。你跑去住客栈，简直近乎侮辱。我听说，你眼下落脚的那个地方，甚至……相当不合适——对我们家族的一员来说。”

===Original===
I maintained for a few seconds a silence of astonishment.

===Chinese===
我惊愕得沉默了好几秒。

===Original===
“But,” I returned to the charge, “the matter is one of importance. You must understand that she.﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“可是，”我重新发动攻势，“这件事非同小可。您必须明白，她……”

===Original===
My aunt stiffened and froze. It was as if I had committed some flagrant sin against etiquette.

===Chinese===
老姑母绷紧了，冷了下来。仿佛我犯下了某种明目张胆的失礼大罪。

===Original===
“If I am satisfied as to her behaviour,” she said, “I think that you might be.” She paused as if she were satisfied that she had set me hopelessly in the wrong.

===Chinese===
“我既然对她的举止感到满意，”她说，“我想你也大可以满意。”她停了停，仿佛对于已把我断送进无望翻身的理亏之地感到满意。

===Original===
“I don’t withdraw my invitation,” she said. “You must understand I *wish* you to come here. But your quarrels you and she must settle. On those terms.﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“我并不收回我的邀请，”她说。“你要明白，我真心愿意你到这儿来。不过你和你那位姐妹之间的争吵，得由你们自己去解决。条件就是如此……”

===Original===
She had the air of conferring an immense favour, as if she believed that I had, all my life through, been waiting for her invitation to come within the pale. As for me, I felt a certain relief at having the carrying out of my duty made impossible for me. I did not *want* to tell my aunt and thus to break things off definitely and for good. Something would have happened; the air might have cleared as it clears after a storm; I should have learnt where I stood. But I was afraid of the knowledge. Light in these dark places might reveal an abyss at my feet. I wanted to let things slide.

===Chinese===
她带着一副赏赐天大恩典的神气，仿佛相信我这一辈子都在等她的邀请，好跨进那个体面的圈子。至于我，眼见履行义务之路被堵死，倒觉出几分轻松。我并不想把事情告诉老姑母，从而把一切干脆利落、一劳永逸地断掉。本来是会闹出点什么的；空气也许会像暴雨过后那样豁然开朗；我也会知道自己站在何处了。可我害怕知道。这些暗处一旦透进光，也许会照出我脚下的深渊。我只想听任事情就这么滑下去。

===Original===
My aunt had returned to her accounts, the accounts which were the cogwheels that kept running the smooth course of the Etchingham estates. She seemed to wish to indicate that I counted for not very much in the scheme of things as she saw it.

===Chinese===
老姑母已经回到她的账目上去了——那些账目是一副副齿轮，维系着埃钦厄姆田产的顺当运转。她似乎有意示意：在她眼中的万事格局里，我这个人无足轻重。

===Original===
“I should like to make your better acquaintance,” she said, with her head still averted, “there are reasons.﻿ ﻿…” It came suddenly into my head that she had an idea of testamentary dispositions, that she felt she was breaking up, that I had my rights. I didn’t much care for the thing, but the idea of being the heir of Etchingham was﻿—well, was an idea. It would make me more possible to my pseudo-sister. It would be, as it were, a starting-point, would make me potentially a somebody of her sort of ideal. Moreover, I should be under the same roof, near her, with her sometimes. One asks so little more than that, that it seemed almost half the battle. I began to consider phrases of thanks and acceptance and then uttered them.

===Chinese===
“我倒愿意同你进一步相知，”她说，头仍偏着，“这里面有些缘故……”我突然想到，她是在盘算遗嘱上的处分，她觉得自己行将溃散，而我自有我的权利。这档子事我本不甚在意，可是，做埃钦厄姆的继承人这个念头——怎么说呢——到底是个念头。它会让我在那位假姐妹眼里更成为可能。它将是一个起点，可以说是起点，会让我有潜质成为她那一类理想中的一个人物。再者，我将住进同一个屋檐底下，在她近旁，有时同她在一起。人所求的比这多不了多少，于是这似乎几乎就是成功的一半了。我开始斟酌致谢与接受的措辞，随后便说了出来。

===Original===
I never quite understood the bearings of that scene; never quite whether my aunt really knew that my sister was not my sister. She was a wonderfully clever woman of the unscrupulous order, with a sangfroid and self-possession well calculated to let her cut short any inconvenient revelations. It was as if she had had long practice in the art, though I cannot say what occasion she can have had for its practice﻿—perhaps for the confounding of wavering avowers of Dissent at home.

===Chinese===
我始终不曾十分明白那一幕的种种关节；也始终不曾明白，老姑母究竟知不知道我的姐妹并非我的姐妹。她是个精明绝顶的女人，属于不择手段的那一类，那份冷静与自持，恰足以让她把任何不便的揭发半途掐断。那情形仿佛她在这门艺术上久经研习，虽然我说不出她能有什么研习的场合——也许是用来震慑家乡那些摇摆不定的非国教（Dissent）信奉者吧。

===Original===
I used to think that she knew, if not all, at least a portion; that the weight that undoubtedly was upon her mind was nothing else but that. She broke up, was breaking up from day to day, and I can think of no other reason. She had the air of being disintegrated, like a mineral under an immense weight﻿—quartz in a crushing mill; of being dulled and numbed as if she were under the influence of narcotics.

===Chinese===
我一向以为她是知道的，就算不全知，至少也知道一部分；她心上那份无可置疑的重负，不是别的，正是这件事。她在溃散，正一天天溃散下去，此外我想不出别的缘故。她有一种正被碾散的神气，像巨压之下的矿石——碎矿机里的石英；有一种昏沉麻木的样子，仿佛受了麻醉品的影响。

===Original===
There is little enough wonder, if she actually carried that imponderable secret about with her. I used to look at her sometimes, and wonder if she, too, saw the oncoming of the inevitable. She was limited enough in her ideas, but not too stupid to take that in if it presented itself. Indeed they have that sort of idea rather grimly before them all the time﻿—that class.

===Chinese===
倘若她当真随身携着那个无形的秘密，这也就没什么可怪的了。我有时望着她，纳闷她是否也看见了那不可避免者的逼近。她的见识够有限的了，可还不至于蠢到这个摆在眼前也领悟不了。的确，那一类人——那个阶级——是自始至终冷冷地把那种念头摆在眼前的。

===Original===
It must have been that that was daily, and little by little, pressing down her eyelids and deepening the quivering lines of her impenetrable face. She had a certain solitary grandeur, the pathos attaching to the last of a race, of a type; the air of waiting for the deluge, of listening for an inevitable sound﻿—the sound of oncoming waters.

===Chinese===
想必正是那个东西，日复一日、一点一点地压下她的眼皮，加深她那张莫测高深的脸上微微颤动的纹路。她自有一种孤独的庄严，一种系于一个种族、一种类型之末路的哀婉；一副坐等洪水临头的神气，一副谛听着某个无可回避的声响的神气——大水涌来的声响。

===Original===
It was weird, the time that I spent in that house﻿—more than weird﻿—deadening. It had an extraordinary effect on me﻿—an effect that my “sister,” perhaps, had carefully calculated. She made pretensions of that sort later on; said that she had been breaking me in to perform my allotted task in the bringing on of the inevitable.

===Chinese===
我在那座宅子里度过的那段时光很怪诞——不止怪诞——是叫人麻木的。它对我产生了一种异乎寻常的影响——一种我的“姐妹”也许早已精心算定了的影响。她后来果然以此自命；说她一直在驯练我，好叫我在促成那不可避免之事的过程中，尽我分派的职分。
===Original===
I have nowhere come across such an intense solitude as there was there, a solitude that threw one so absolutely upon one’s self and into one’s self. I used to sit working in one of those tall, panelled rooms, very high up in the air. I was writing at the series of articles for the *Bi-Monthly*, for Polehampton. I was to get the atmosphere of Paris, you remember. It was rather extraordinary, that process. Up there I seemed to be as much isolated from Paris as if I had been in﻿—well, in Hampton Court. It was almost impossible to write; I had things to think about: preoccupations, jealousies. It was true I had a living to make, but that seemed to have lost its engrossingness as a pursuit, or at least to have suspended it.

===Chinese===
我在别处从未遇见过像那里那样浓重的孤独，一种把人如此彻底地抛回自身、掷入内心的孤独。我惯常坐在那些高大的镶板房间中的一间里工作，高高凌在半空。我在为《双月刊》、为波尔汉普顿写那组文章。你要记得，我的差事就是把巴黎的气氛摄取下来。那个过程相当离奇。在那么高的地方，我与巴黎隔绝之深，仿佛我身处——唔，身处汉普顿宫（Hampton Court）。写作几乎不可能；我有心事要想：种种牵挂，种种醋意。不错，我是要谋生的，可谋生作为一门营生，似乎已失去了它引人入胜的力量，或者至少是暂时搁下了。

===Original===
The panels of the room seemed to act as a sounding-board, the belly of an immense cello. There were never any noises in the house, only whispers coming from an immense distance﻿—as when one drops stones down an unfathomable well and hears ages afterward the faint sound of disturbed waters. When I look back at that time I figure myself as forever sitting with uplifted pen, waiting for a word that would not come, and that I did not much care about getting. The panels of the room would creak sympathetically to the opening of the entrance-door of the house, the faintest of creaks; people would cross the immense hall to the room in which they plotted; would cross leisurely, with laughter and rustling of garments that after a long time reached my ears in whispers. Then I should have an access of mad jealousy. I wanted to be part of her life, but I could not stand that Salon of suspicious conspirators. What could I do there? Stand and look at them, conscious that they all dropped their voices instinctively when I came near them?

===Chinese===
房间的镶板仿佛起着共鸣板的作用，像一把大提琴的琴腹。宅子里从来没有任何声响，只有从极远处传来的低语——像人往一口深不可测的井里丢下石子，隔了许久许久，才听见搅动的水那微弱的声音。回首那段时光，我总把自己想成永远举着笔坐在那里，等一个不肯来的字眼——那个字眼，我也并不怎么在乎等得着等不着。宅子大门一开，房间的镶板便应和着吱呀一响——最轻微的一声吱呀；人们穿过那巨大的厅堂，往他们密谋的房间去；从容地穿过去，人语与衣料的窸窣，要过好一阵才以低语的形式传到我耳中。这时我便要发作一阵疯狂的醋意。我想成为她生活的一部分，可我受不了那个满是可疑阴谋家的沙龙。我在那儿能做什么呢？站着看他们，同时心里明白，他们一见我走近，便都本能地压低了嗓音？

===Original===
That was the general tone of that space of time, but, of course, it was not always that. I used to emerge now and then to breakfast sympathetically with my aunt, sometimes to sit through a meal with the two of them. I danced attendance on them singly; paid depressing calls with my aunt; calls on the people in the Faubourg; people without any individuality other than a kind of desiccation, the shrivelled appearance and point of view of a dried pippin. In revenge, they had names that startled one, names that recalled the generals and flaneurs of an impossibly distant time; names that could hardly have had any existence outside the memoirs of Madame de Sévigné, the names of people that could hardly have been fitted to do anything more vigorous than be reflected in the mirrors of the *Salle des Glaces*. I was so absolutely depressed, so absolutely in a state of suspended animation, that I seemed to conform exactly to my aunt’s ideas of what was desirable in me as an attendant on her at these functions. I used to stand behind chairs and talk, like a good young man, to the assorted *Pères* and *Abbés* who were generally present.

===Chinese===
那段时期大体上就是这个调子，当然，也不尽然。我偶尔露一露面，满怀同情地陪老姑母进早餐，有时也同她们两个一起坐完一顿饭。我分别随侍着她们两位；陪老姑母出去作些叫人丧气的拜访——拜访圣日耳曼区那些人家；那些人除了一种干缩之外再无个性，外貌和见解都像一颗干瘪起皱的皮平苹果（pippin）。作为补偿，他们的名字倒叫人一惊，让人想起一个遥远得不可想象的时代里的将军们和浪荡子（flaneurs）；那些名字几乎不可能在塞维涅夫人（Madame de Sévigné）的书简之外存在，那些名字的主人，几乎安派不上任何比映在镜厅（Salle des Glaces）的镜子里去更费力气的用场。我是那么彻底地意气消沉，那么彻底地处于一种假死状态，以至于竟似乎恰恰合乎老姑母的心意——在她眼里，我随她出席这类场合，要的正是这么一个随侍。我惯常站在椅子背后，像个好青年似的，同通常在场的那些形形色色的神父（Pères）、司铎（Abbés）攀谈。

===Original===
And then I used to go home and get the atmospheres of these people. I must have done it abominably badly, for the notes that brought Polehampton’s cheques were accompanied by the bravos of that gentleman and the assurances that Miss Polehampton liked my work﻿—liked it very much.

===Chinese===
然后我便回家去，摄取这些人的气氛。我一定写得糟透了，因为随波尔汉普顿的支票一同寄来的短简里，总伴着那位先生的喝彩，还有波尔汉普顿小姐喜欢我的文字——非常喜欢——的种种保证。

===Original===
I suppose I exhibited myself in the capacity of the man who knew﻿—who could let you into a thing or two. After all, anyone could write about students’ balls and the lakes in the Bois, but it took *someone* to write “with knowledge” of the interiors of the barred houses in the Rue de l’Université.

===Chinese===
我想，我是在以“知情之人”的身份展示自己——一个能让你窥见一二内幕的人。毕竟，学生舞会、布洛涅林园（the Bois）的湖水，谁都写得出；可要“内行地”写出大学街（Rue de l’Université）那些装着铁栅的宅邸的内部，那就得是个人物才行了。

===Original===
Then, too, I attended the more showy entertainments with my sister. I had by now become so used to hearing her styled “your sister” that the epithet had the quality of a name. She was “*mademoiselle votre soeur*,” as she might have been Mlle. Patience or Hope, without having anything of the named quality. What she did at the entertainments, the charitable bazaars, the dismal dances, the impossibly bad concerts, I have no idea. She must have had some purpose, for she did nothing without. I myself descended into fulfilling the functions of a rudimentarily developed chaperon﻿—functions similar in importance to those performed by the eyes of a mole. I had the maddest of accesses of jealousy if she talked to a man﻿—and *such* men﻿—or danced with one. And then I was forever screwing my courage up and feeling it die away. We used to drive about in a coupé, a thing that shut us inexorably together, but which quite as inexorably destroyed all opportunities for what one calls making love. In smooth streets its motion was too glib, on the *pavé* it rattled too abominably. I wanted to make love to her﻿—oh, immensely, but I was never in the mood, or the opportunity was never forthcoming. I used to have the wildest fits of irritation; not of madness or of depression, but of simple wildness at the continual recurrence of small obstacles. I couldn’t read, couldn’t bring myself to it. I used to sit and look dazedly at the English newspapers﻿—at any newspaper but the *Hour*. De Mersch had, for the moment, disappeared. There were troubles in his elective grand duchy﻿—he had, indeed, contrived to make himself unpopular with the electors, excessively unpopular. I used to read piquant articles about his embroglio in an American paper that devoted itself to matters of the sort. All sorts of international difficulties were to arise if de Mersch were ejected. There was some other obscure prince of a rival house, Prussian or Russian, who had desires for the degree of royalty that sat so heavily on de Mersch.
