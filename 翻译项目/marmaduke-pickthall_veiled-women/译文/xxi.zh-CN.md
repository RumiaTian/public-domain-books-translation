## XXI / 二十一
===Original===
The idea of seeing Mrs. Cameron again was quite intolerable. She therefore wrote that lady a brief note, an asp for venom, designed to terminate acquaintance and to rankle, and plunged into the harem pleasures with sensations of defiance.

One morning, as she lounged upon her cushioned window-seat, smoking her narghile and listening to the voices wafted with the sunlight through her lattice, Fatûmah came and with a grin announced that Hamdi Bey desired an audience of her Honour. She gave the word, and in came Hamdi, knuckling his two eyes.

“O day of pitch!” he cried. “O vile nefarious day! O my beloved sister, hide me, save me! My father has enforced my mother’s harsh command. I am to be married today to that unholy child of dogs﻿—against my will. I wished to wait a thousand years. Ghandûr is waiting at this minute to conduct me to the bath.”
===Chinese===
再见到卡梅伦夫人，这念头实在叫人无法忍受。于是她给那位夫人写去一封短信——毒如一条蝰蛇，专为断绝这段交情、教那毒口隐隐溃痛；写罢，便带着一股挑战的情绪，一头扎进内宅的种种欢愉。

一天早晨，她正斜倚在铺着垫子的窗座上，抽着水烟筒，听人语声随着阳光从窗棂间飘进来，法图麦进来了，咧着嘴禀报说哈姆迪·贝伊求见夫人。她吩咐传见，哈姆迪走了进来，两只拳头揉着两只眼睛。

“哦，漆黑如沥青的日子！”他叫道。“哦，可恶歹毒的日子！哦，我亲爱的姐姐，把我藏起来，救救我！我父亲执行了我母亲的无情严令。今天我就要娶那个不圣洁的狗崽子——这违背我的心意。我本想再等上一千年。这会儿甘杜尔正在外头等着，要领我去浴场呢。”
===Original===
As if in confirmation of his words, the voice of Ghandûr shouted in the street without: “Make haste, O Hamdi! Lo, the sun is high! The shadow is already on the stone thou fixedst for a limit when I let thee enter.”

“Thou hearest,” snuffled Hamdi, “how they hound me? He has my wedding garments in a bundle﻿—O my hatred! Guests have been bidden﻿—may their fathers perish! Go to my mother (she will hear thee); plead that I may be allowed a few months’ respite. It is Na’imah who, through her mother, hastens on the match. She would destroy my newfound freedom and torment me.”

Barakah could not help laughing, though she uttered words of comfort. Na’imah was a very pretty girl, she pointed out, and not ill-natured, though a great coquette. He would have none of it, but shook his head with ominous frowns.
===Chinese===
像是特地应验他的话似的，甘杜尔的声音随即在外面街上喊了起来：“快点，哈姆迪！瞧，太阳已经老高了！你进来时给自己定作界线的那块石头，影子已经爬上去了！”

“你听见了，”哈姆迪抽着鼻子说，“他们是怎样追逼我的？他把我成亲的衣裳打成了一包——哦，我恨哪！客人都请过了——叫他们的爹都死绝才好！去找我母亲（她会听你的），求她许我缓上几个月。是纳伊玛通过她娘催紧了这门亲事。她要毁掉我新近到手的自由，好折磨我。”

巴拉卡忍不住笑出声来，嘴上还是说着宽慰的话。她说，纳伊玛是个十分标致的姑娘，虽说爱卖弄风情，心肠并不坏。他哪里肯听，只连连摇头，眉头拧出满脸不祥。
===Original===
“I hate her!” he declared. “And knowest thou? I have a mind to drown myself this morning at the bath.”

Then, as Ghandûr’s calls became insistent, he left the room with slow, reluctant steps.

The wedding was a small affair, the parties being children of one house, and their betrothal (which is legal marriage) having taken place in infancy. The bride, enthroned, showed none of the reluctance felt by Hamdi. A bright-eyed and determined little maiden, she was wreathed in smiles; and when Barakah inquired if she were truly happy, replied, “The praise to Allah!” with decision.
===Chinese===
“我恨她！”他宣称。“你可知道？我今天早上有心在浴场里把自己淹死算了。”

随后，甘杜尔越催越紧，他才拖着迟缓而不情愿的脚步离开了房间。

婚礼办得很简省，因为新人原是同一宅门的孩子，订亲（那在教法上即是成婚）早在襁褓中便已行过。新娘高踞上座，没有半点哈姆迪那样的不情愿。她是个眼睛明亮、极有主见的小姑娘，满面堆着笑；巴拉卡问她是否真心快活，她一口答道：“赞美归于安拉！”
===Original===
Next day the house was full of smothered laughter. Hamdi was completely changed. He and his bride were now the fondest pair. The lady Fitnah, who had always held that matrimony was a panacea for the crotchets of young people, male and female, rendered praise where praise was due. For many days, through shame, the bridegroom hid from Barakah, and from everyone else to whom he had proclaimed his dread of marriage.

When she told Gulbeyzah of the case as of a kind of miracle, the Circassian answered:

“I perceive no cause for wonder. The bridegroom had not thought of her before in that relation, had not truly known her﻿—that is all. Love is a blessing that brings gratitude as surely as the Nile makes plants to grow.”
===Chinese===
第二天，宅子里到处是憋着的窃笑。哈姆迪彻底变了样。他和新娘成了顶顶恩爱的一对。菲特娜夫人素来主张婚姻是治少男少女种种怪脾气的万灵药，这回功劳该归谁就归了谁。一连许多天，新郎臊得躲着巴拉卡，也躲着一切他曾向其宣扬过惧怕婚姻的人。

她把这桩事当作一桩奇事讲给古尔贝雅扎听，那切尔克斯女子答道：

“我看不出有什么可怪的。新郎从前不曾往那层关系上想过她，不曾真正认识过她——仅此而已。爱是一种恩赐，必然带来感戴，正如尼罗河必然使草木生长。”
===Original===
Gulbeyzah and Bedr-ul-Budûr﻿—nay, all her friends﻿—viewed love, apart from any individual man, as a material boon. Bred up to it and ripened for it cunningly, they were ready to adore the man who gave it, however unattractive from a European standpoint. This view of love, when realized, explained to Barakah the happiness which every girl of her acquaintance seemed to find in marriage, even where, as in Gulbeyzah’s case, the husband was a greybeard thrice her age. Those who possessed it were content and virtuous. In those who had it not, or were deprived of it, all amorous crime was reckoned pardonable.

Gulbeyzah and Bedr-ul-Budûr explained all this to Barakah in thrilling tones, as if they uttered truths divine.
===Chinese===
古尔贝雅扎和白德尔-布杜尔——不，她所有的朋友——都把爱本身看作一件实实在在的恩惠，与任何一个具体的男人无干。她们自幼为此受调教，又为此被巧妙地催熟，随时乐于崇拜那个把爱赐下来的人，无论以欧洲的眼光看他是多么乏味无吸引力。这套爱的观念一旦领会，便向巴拉卡解释了她相识的每个姑娘何以似乎都能在婚姻里找到幸福——哪怕像古尔贝雅扎那样，丈夫是个年齿三倍于己的老翁。拥有爱的人知足而贞静；没有爱或被夺了爱的人，则一切情罪皆可宽宥。

古尔贝雅扎和白德尔-布杜尔把这番道理用激动的声调讲给巴拉卡听，仿佛口宣的是神启的真理。
===Original===
“Behold the wisdom of our Faith,” they said, “which grants to every woman this delight in secret. Women can never truly be the friends of men; their soul is different. If thrown with men for long, they feel fatigue. They ask of men one thing﻿—the gift of love. Here we consort with women, true companions, all day long; and in the night the bridegroom comes, and we are blest. Is not this better than the way of Europe, which sets at nought apparent truths﻿—as that most men love more than one of us, whereas most women need but love itself, the hope of children?”

That was one of the occasions when Barakah would have given anything to have an Englishwoman present, and to watch her face. Another came a few days later when she called upon Gulbeyzah. Alighting from her carriage at the palace door, she saw a baby’s coffin being carried out, and thought at once of turning home again. But already smiling eunuchs stood before her bidding welcome, beseeching her to deign to follow them to the haremlik. Gulbeyzah met her with a kiss on either cheek.
===Chinese===
“瞧瞧我们信仰中的智慧，”她们说，“它暗中赐给每个女人这份欢愉。女人永远做不成男人真正的朋友；她们的灵魂不同。与男人长久厮混，她们只觉得疲乏。她们向男人求的只有一样东西——爱的馈赠。在这里，我们同女人、同真正的同伴整日相伴；到了夜里新郎来了，我们便蒙受福泽。这岂不比欧洲的道路强？那路子把明摆着的道理视若无物——比如：大多数男人爱着的不止我们一个，而大多数女人所要的不过是爱本身，加上生儿育女的指望。”

这是巴拉卡情愿付出任何代价、也想有个英国女人在场看看她神色的时刻之一。几天后又碰上一回：她去拜访古尔贝雅扎，在府门前下车时，恰看见一口婴儿的小棺材正被抬出来，她当即就想掉头回家。可是满面堆笑的阉人已经迎上来道欢迎，恳请她赏光随他们进哈雷姆利克去。古尔贝雅扎迎上来，在她两颊上各亲了一吻。
===Original===
“Come, help us to console Nasîbah,” she exclaimed. “Her baby died this night. She is distracted.”

She drew her friend into a chamber where the childless mother lay, face downward, moaning, while the others tried to soothe her.

“It is no matter,” was the burden of their consolations. “It is not as if thou wert left altogether desolate. Are we not one, we four? Thou hast two children left, since ours are thine, and in a day or two Gulbeyzah will present thee with a third, inshallah!”
===Chinese===
“来，帮我们劝劝纳西芭（Nasîbah），”她叫道。“她的婴儿昨夜死了。她都痛得神魂失守了。”

她把朋友领进一间屋子，那位失了孩子的母亲正脸朝下躺着呻吟，其余的人在旁劝慰。

“这算不得什么，”她们劝慰的话翻来覆去总是这一句。“又不是把你撇得孤苦无依。我们四个不是一体的吗？你还有两个孩子呢——我们的就是你的——过一两天，古尔贝雅扎还要送你第三个，若安拉意欲！”
===Original===
“Inshallah!” cried Gulbeyzah. “And it shall be thine entirely. Directly it is born it shall be sent to thee to nurse. I will forget it. And when it is thy turn again, thou wilt repay me. Is not that a good idea?”

Oh that English people, who regard polygamy as something dreadful, could have witnessed that small scene! The wish, escaping Barakah at unawares, begot a heartache, as she realized that all she saw and heard for their instruction was thwarted of its natural vent forevermore.
===Chinese===
“若安拉意欲！”古尔贝雅扎叫道。“而且它整个儿归你。一落生就送到你怀里奶。我会忘掉它。往后再轮到你，你再还我。这主意不好么？”

唉，那些把多妻制看作洪水猛兽的英国人，若能亲眼见到这一幕就好了！这心愿从巴拉卡心头不防溜出，随即生出一阵心痛——她明白，自己为他们长见识而看到听到的一切，从此永远失去了天然的出口。
===Original===
She told herself that she was happy in this life; and so she was upon the surface, where she kept her thoughts, not daring to pry down into the depths. In the early days she had desired more knowledge of the Muslim faith, and a woman learned in religion had been hired to teach her. But the fury of that faith, the scathing nature of its truths, appalled her, awaking recollections of a creed more sentimental, with distressing doubts. She very soon gave up her lessons, closed the eyes of her intelligence, and resolutely sought her pleasure in the passing hour.

Still there were moments when vague fears oppressed her. When, in the third year of her marriage, she brought forth a stillborn child, frightful abysses seemed to yawn around her, and for days she was afflicted with a kind of nightmare of misgiving, derived from recollection of the “zâr” and other horrors.
===Chinese===
她对自己说，这样的日子她是快活的；就表面而论也确实如此——她把心思都留在表面上，不敢往下窥探深处。起初她曾想多了解些穆斯林信仰，宅里还雇了一位精通教门的妇人来做她的教师。可那信仰的烈焰、那些真道的灼人严酷吓住了她，唤起她对一种更缠绵的信条的记忆，伴着令人苦恼的疑虑。没过多久她便放弃了功课，闭上灵智的眼睛，打定主意只在眼前的时辰里寻欢取乐。

然而仍有些时刻，无名的恐惧压上她的心头。婚后第三年，她产下一个死婴，那时可怕的深渊仿佛在她四周张开大口，一连数日，她被一种疑惧的梦魇折磨着——那是从对“扎尔”仪式及种种别的恐怖的回忆里生出来的。
===Original===
The Eastern ladies were so calm and strong compared with her; they flinched at nothing except impropriety. The slaughter of a thousand sheep at Curban Bairam, turning the kitchen court into a shambles, caused them no disgust. It was ordained of God, they told her, and it fed the poor. They had no horror of disease or death or filthy persons, and, though most cleanly, looked on vermin philosophically. The Turks and the Circassians, with their grand ideals, appeared more dreadful than the Africans, whose faith was childlike. Barakah preferred the latter. Her pleasure was in feasts and little outings, in storytellers, dancers, and musicians who beguile the time; her only rapture was in adoration of her small Muhammad.

Her hidden yearnings and beliefs clung round the boy. She dwelt in longing for the days when he should be her friend. He was her hope, the product of both parts of her divided life; giving it sense and sequence, and, in the end perhaps, if Allah willed, consistency. She dreamt of a great future for him, to astonish Europe. But in the meanwhile, being sometimes dull, she felt the need of an intelligent, discreet companion.
===Chinese===
东方的夫人们与她相比是那样镇定、那样坚强；除了失礼之事，没有什么能使她们畏缩。宰牲节（Curban Bairam）上宰一千头羊，把厨房院子变成屠宰场，也不曾引得她们半点憎厌。她们告诉她，这是真主所定的，又能周济穷人。她们对疾病、死亡和腌臜的人毫无恐惧，自己虽然极其爱洁，看待虱虫之属却达观得很。突厥人和切尔克斯人抱着一套宏大的理想，在她看来反倒比非洲人更可怖，因为后者的信仰是孩子一般的。巴拉卡更喜欢后者。她的乐趣在宴饮和小小的出游，在说书的、跳舞的、奏乐的那些消磨光阴的人；她唯一的狂喜，在于疼爱她的小穆罕默德。

她深藏的渴望与信念，都萦系在这个男孩身上。她一心渴盼他长成她朋友的那一天。他是她的希望，是她被分成两半的生活两半共同的结晶，赋予这生活以意义和连贯，或许到头来——若安拉意欲——还能赋予它以一贯。她梦想着他有一个伟大的前程，好教欧洲吃惊。可在那之前，日子有时烦闷，她感到需要一个聪慧而口风紧的伴侣。
