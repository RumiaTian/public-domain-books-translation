## XIX / 十九

===Original===

Never in her life had Barakah seen so many strange old women. There were always four or five of them within her chamber, squatting on mats along the wall, conversing in low tones, ready at a breath to rearrange her pillows, or fetch some posset that was ordered for her. They were all of apelike ugliness, and, going barefoot, moved as noiselessly as ghosts.

The Frankish doctor﻿—an Italian﻿—had pronounced her much too frail to nurse her baby﻿—a decision which excited such dependants of the house as were eligible for the post of foster-mother. This was a great prize, kinship by milk, among the Muslims, being esteemed as genuine and binding as by blood. The wet-nurse thus became a near relation of the family, and all her race had claims upon its bounty. Barakah felt jealous of the woman who usurped her function, till she heard from Fitnah Khânum that the choice had fallen on the wife of her old friend Ghandûr. The girl, a former slave of the harem, was then presented to her, the baby in her arms; and won her heart by her excessive gratitude. She was touched, too, by the transports of Ghandûr, who sang thanksgiving to her lattice in his simple way. His chant was something in this manner:

===Chinese===

巴拉卡有生以来，从没见过这么多古怪的老太婆。她房里总坐着四五个，蹲在沿墙的席子上低声交谈，一声招呼便随时替她重新摆好枕头，或去端为她配好的热奶饮。她们个个丑得像猿猴，赤着脚，走动起来悄无声息，如同鬼魅。

法兰克医生——一位意大利人——判定她身子太弱，哺育不了自己的婴孩——这一裁断，让宅子里够格竞争乳母之位的仆妇们好一阵激动。这可是份厚赏：在穆斯林中间，乳汁结下的亲缘，被看得同血亲一样真切、一样有约束力。乳母就此成了这家亲近的亲戚，她全族的人都有权沾这家的恩泽。巴拉卡起初还嫉妒那个僭夺了她职分的女人，直到听菲特娜·哈努姆说，人选落在她老朋友甘杜尔的妻子身上。那姑娘原是内宅的一个奴婢，随即被领来见她，怀里抱着婴孩；她那过分的感激赢得了巴拉卡的心。甘杜尔欣喜若狂的样子也叫她感动，他用他那质朴的方式，对着她的窗棂唱起了谢恩歌。他唱的大致是这样：

===Original===

She liked to hear him, his voice so near at hand produced a sense of true devotion and security. Missing his chant upon the following day, she inquired what had become of him. She was informed that, consequent upon his wife’s preferment, he had been appointed to a small position in the Government. It made her sad.

Her son was given over to the harem midwives to fulfil no end of ceremonies destined to frustrate the powers of evil. For a week he was not left alone or in the dark a single second. They carried him in a procession through the house, his future kingdom, and as each door was opened, sprinkled salt mixed with the seven seeds to exorcize the jinn who lurked within. Soon after birth his face had been defiled with certain powders, which Barakah could not persuade the women to wash off. It was a necessary precaution, they assured her, against jealous powers of darkness who, if they had an inkling of his beauty, would destroy him. Chief among these was the discarded wife of Adam, alluded to as El Carînah (the companion), the cause of man’s first fall, who hates Eve’s daughters and resents their great fertility. Where a child seems lovely and the mother shows delight in its appearance, she attacks them both; if, on the contrary, she sees it ugly and hears words of disappointment, she lets it live to spite the seed of Adam. For one so powerful, she must be very stupid to be taken in by such pretences, Barakah remarked; but they cried out that such was not the case, but Allah in His mercy had set limits to her sight and hearing. Each day the infant was the central figure in some ancient rite believed essential to its welfare.

===Chinese===

她喜欢听他唱；他的声音就在近旁，令人生出一种真切的虔敬与安稳之感。第二天没听见他的歌声，她便打听他去了哪里。有人告诉她，随着他妻子的高升，他已被安插进政府里的一个小职位。这让她难过。

她的儿子被交给内宅的接生婆们，去履行没完没了的仪式，专为挫败邪魔之力。整整一个星期，没有一秒钟让他独处，也没有一秒让他待在黑暗里。她们抱着他巡行全宅——他未来的王国——每开一扇门，便撒上掺了七种籽实的盐，驱逐潜伏在门内的恶灵。出生后不久，他的脸便被抹上某些粉末，巴拉卡怎么也劝不动那些女人给洗掉。她们向她担保，这是必要的防备，防的是好妒的黑暗之力——它们若嗅出他的俊美，就会毁了他。其中的头一号，便是亚当被休弃的发妻，人称埃尔·卡里娜（El Carînah，“同伴”之意），人类最初堕落的根由；她恨夏娃的女儿们，忌恨她们旺盛的生育。哪里的孩子看着可爱、做母亲的又面露得意，她就对母子俩一齐下手；反之，她若见孩子生得丑、又听得失望的话，便放他活着，好气一气亚当的子孙。巴拉卡说，这样厉害的角色，竟会被这点伎俩瞒过，想必蠢得很；她们却叫嚷起来，说绝非如此，而是安拉大发慈悲，给她的眼和耳设了限度。每一天，这婴孩都是某项古老仪式的中心人物，那些仪式被信为他的福祉所系。

===Original===

As Barakah lay in bed and watched the pattern of the lattice, her whole existence passing like a dream before her, she sought to reconcile her former English with her present Eastern life. Her son was a fine boy, they all assured her. It saddened her that she had no relations of her own to take a pride in him. In this mood she asked Yûsuf to write a little note to Mrs. Cameron entreating her to come one day and see the baby. He did so, and the answer was that she would come with pleasure.

Elated by the prospect of this visit, Barakah wished to have her offspring made presentable; but when she gave command to wash his face and wrap him in nice clothes, the goodies screamed aloud, and fetched the lady Fitnah to remonstrate with her. She gave way, perforce; and Mrs. Cameron beheld the infant at his worst.

===Chinese===

巴拉卡躺在床上，望着窗棂的格影，自己的一生如一场梦从眼前掠过，她想把自己前半生的英国旧我，同眼前的东方生活调和起来。她的儿子是个漂亮男孩——她们全都这样向她担保。难过的是，她自己没有亲族，不能为他骄傲。正是在这种心绪下，她请优素福写了张小便条给卡梅伦夫人，恳求她哪天过来瞧瞧婴儿。他写了，回话说她乐意前来。

一想到这次造访，巴拉卡喜不自胜，想把孩子拾掇得体面些；可她一下令给孩子洗脸、裹上漂亮的衣裳，那班老妈子便高声尖叫起来，搬来菲特娜夫人劝阻她。她只得让步；卡梅伦夫人看到的，便是这婴孩最不堪的模样。

===Original===

The visitor was very kind in her address to Barakah; but, when she held the baby for a minute, looking down at it, the latter, watching keenly, saw upon her face a quiver of extreme disfavour mixed with pity. The whiteness of her hands and face showed the child yellow; Barakah had thought him white as snow till then. A flush of anger and humiliation reached her brain.

“His face is dirty, the poor little one! Our Lord preserve him!” the visitor remarked in Arabic as she returned the baby to his nurse; at which there was an outburst of applause from the onlookers. They called down blessings on the lady’s head, desiring she might have herself a thousand children, not like this one, puny and unpleasant, but most beautiful.

Barakah, consumed with rage, murmured hoarsely in response to Mrs. Cameron’s farewell. The moment she was gone she burst out weeping.

===Chinese===

访客对巴拉卡的言辞极尽亲切；可当她把婴儿抱了片刻、低头端详时，一旁冷眼紧盯的巴拉卡，看见她脸上掠过一丝极度的嫌恶，混着怜悯。她手和脸的白皙，衬得孩子发黄；在那之前，巴拉卡一直当他白得像雪。一股愤怒与屈辱的热血直冲上头。

“他的脸脏兮兮的，可怜的小家伙！愿我们的主保佑他！”访客用阿拉伯语说着，把婴孩交还给他的乳母；这话博得旁观众人一阵喝彩。她们连声为夫人祝祷，愿她自己生上一千个孩子，个个不像这一个——又羸弱又不讨喜——而是漂亮极了。

巴拉卡强压怒火，哑着嗓子含混地应答卡梅伦夫人的道别。她一走，巴拉卡便放声大哭。

===Original===

“She did not like the child! She scorned my son, because he is not altogether white as she is.”

“Thou mistakest, O my dear! Be comforted!” cried Fitnah Khânum, while the other women round her exchanged pitying glances.

“Thou art not yet perfect in our prudent customs; but thy friend, though not a Muslimah, has learnt them, having been much longer in the land. Hast thou forgotten my instructions touching El Carînah? Nor is she alone to be redoubted, since Allah Himself abhors a boastful spirit, and dishonours those who make too much of any creature.﻿ ﻿…”

“O Lord! I know all that!” wailed Barakah. “But she disliked my child, despised him! I﻿—I saw it!”

===Chinese===

“她不喜欢这孩子！她看轻我的儿子，就因为他不像她那样浑身白。”

“你弄错了，哦，亲爱的！想开些！”菲特娜·哈努姆喊道，周围的其余女人交换着怜悯的眼色。

“你还未尽通我们这些谨慎的风俗；可你的朋友虽非穆斯林女子（Muslimah），却也学会了，因为她在这片土地上住得久得多。你可忘了我关于埃尔·卡里娜的那番叮嘱？何况要忌惮的还不止她一个，因为安拉自己也厌恶自夸的心，贬辱那些把任何造物夸得太甚的人。……”

“哦，主啊！这些我都知道！”巴拉卡哭喊道。“可她不喜欢我的孩子，看轻他！我——我亲眼看见的！”

===Original===

Conviction that the portion of the human race from which she sprang beheld her son as little better than a monkey, tortured Barakah. She had looked upon him as a mediator, but now sought revenge. Hot, feverish dreams of hate disturbed her rest; and when a spell of khamsîn weather robbed the world of energy she grew as weak and fretful as her thoughts were wild.

Already Barakah had kept her bed a fortnight longer than any Eastern woman would have dreamt of doing after childbirth. The lady Fitnah, seeing she did not gain strength, believed that some debilitating vile afrît was in her. The Frankish doctor said there was no cause for fear. She called him fool and worse, in her own circle; since by his disregard or ignorance of pious formulas he had left the door ajar for evil spirits. Resolved to stop the mischief, when no man was by she hung a plant of garlic in the room, burnt potent odours till its air grew suffocating, and dosed the patient with a paste compounded of the dust of mummies mixed with human milk. When these means failed to drive away the enemy, she sat down in despair among her cronies, and braced herself to try the last resort of all.

===Chinese===

她由此确信，自己出身的那一族人，把她儿子看得比猴子好不了多少；这念头折磨着巴拉卡。她本把他当作联结两边的中间人，如今却一心复仇。炽热狂乱的仇恨之梦搅扰她的安眠；待到一阵喀姆辛（khamsîn）热风天气夺去全世界的元气，她便愈发虚弱烦躁，恰如她的思绪一般狂乱。

巴拉卡产后卧床，早已比任何东方女人梦中所敢想的多躺了半个月。菲特娜夫人见她不见起色，认定她体内盘着某个蚀人元气的下作阿弗里特妖灵。法兰克医生说没有可惧之处。她在自己的圈子里骂他是蠢货，还有更难听的；因为他无视或不懂敬神的仪轨，等于给邪灵留了半掩的门。她决心堵住这祸患，趁没有男人在旁，在屋里挂起一整棵大蒜，焚烧浓烈的香气，直到满屋空气令人窒息，又给病人灌下一种用木乃伊粉末调着人乳做成的膏糊。这些法子都赶不走那仇敌，她便绝望地在老姐妹们中间坐下，横下心来试那万不得已的最后一招。

===Original===

This was the “zâr”﻿—a very awful ceremony, of which she was exceedingly afraid. Her wish to hold it in the house﻿—risking the Pasha’s favour, and her life through terror﻿—was proof of her devoted love for Barakah. The dear one must be healed at any price.

Accordingly, she summoned negresses of those who hold familiar intercourse with demons, bought a kid and several fowls alive, and made arrangements to secure the sickroom to herself and her confederates for two good hours upon a certain afternoon.

这就是扎尔——一场可怖至极的仪式，她对它怕得要命。她偏要在这宅子里行它——冒着失掉帕夏恩宠、因恐惧而送命的风险——正证明了她对巴拉卡的挚爱。这位心爱的人儿，无论如何也要治好。

于是她唤来几个黑人女子——正是那类与魔鬼过从甚密的——买了一只小山羊和几只活鸡，又安排妥当，要在某个下午把病房包占下来，归她和她的同伙们足足用上两个钟头。

Barakah was roused out of a troubled sleep by women moving out the furniture from both bedchamber and salon, and covering the floor with worn-out linen and the cheapest matting. They went, and she lay wondering, when Fitnah Khânum came and bade her have no fear. The ceremony she was going to witness was a potent medicine, well calculated to restore her health completely. Many servants, female children, and familiars of the household trooped in with noiseless feet and squatted down along the wall. Then came a group of half a dozen negresses, fantastically dressed in rags of finery, with ringing anklets; one of whom embraced a struggling little goat, while the others bore live chickens by the feet. Bold-eyed and with a swaggering gait, they marched up to the bed, and seemed to offer up the fowls and kid to Barakah, who could not understand the words they uttered in a screeching chant. They then danced back to the adjoining room, of which the door stood open. Upon the threshold madness seemed to seize them. They fell upon the kid with cries of glee. The creature, bleating piteously, was flung into an earthen bowl placed there in readiness. Amid mad laughter knives were brandished and brought down, hands helping to extract the creature’s life. The fowls were likewise gashed and torn asunder; the matting round grew foul with steaming entrails. Another minute and the slayers reappeared, their black arms purpled to the elbow, dripping blood, their faces and their lips defiled with it; and then began a devilish dance of self-abandonment, all the more horrible for its approach to beauty. The sleek skin of the dancers caught blue lights; their fixed eyes gleamed enormous, like those painted on the lids of mummies. Barakah believed herself in hell, forever lost; it was as if an iron hand compressed her throat. Her heart beat wildly. One of the women, the most shameless, lurched towards her, stretching out a bloodstained hand. Her heart gave one tremendous beat and then stood still.

巴拉卡从纷乱的睡梦中惊醒，女人们正把家具从卧室和客厅里搬出去，往地上铺破旧的亚麻布和最便宜的草席。她们去了，她躺着纳闷，菲特娜·哈努姆便走来，嘱咐她别怕。她即将亲眼目睹的这场仪式，是一剂猛药，保管能让她的身子完全康复。宅子里许多仆人、女童和常来常往的熟人蹑着脚鱼贯而入，沿墙蹲坐。随后来了六个黑人女子，穿着绫罗的破片扮出的奇装异服，脚镯叮当作响；其中一个怀里搂着一只挣扎的小山羊，其余的手里倒提着活鸡。她们眼神泼辣，步态张扬，一路走到床前，仿佛是把鸡和羊献上给巴拉卡，口中尖声唱着什么，她一句也听不懂。接着她们舞回隔壁的房间，那边的门敞开着。一上门槛，疯狂似乎攫住了她们。她们欢呼着扑向那小山羊。那生灵哀哀地叫着，被扔进一只预先备好的陶盆。疯狂的笑声中，刀子挥起又落下，一双手也上来帮着结果它的性命。那些鸡同样被剁开、撕碎；四周的草席被冒着热气的肠肚弄得污秽不堪。又过了一分钟，杀生者们重新露面，黑色的手臂直到肘弯都染成了紫红，滴着血，脸上唇上尽是血污；随后开始了一场自弃的魔舞，因其逼近于美而愈发骇人。舞者们光洁的皮肤映出幽蓝的光；她们僵直的眼睛瞪得灼灼极大，就像画在木乃伊眼睑上的那些。巴拉卡只当自己落进了地狱，永世沉沦；仿佛一只铁手扼住了她的喉咙。她的心狂跳。其中一个最不知羞的女人，踉跄着朝她走来，伸出一只血污的手。她的心猛跳了一下，随即停住了。

“Breathe not a word to Yûsuf or our lord the Pasha. Deny by Allah that thou sawest anything. If not, the afrît which we have with pains extracted will return and kill thee.”
