## Endnotes / 尾注
===Original===

1. In a treatise on boxing by Captain Edgeworth Johnstone, just published, I read, “In the days of the prize-ring, fights lasted for hours; and the knockout blow was unknown.” This statement is a little too sweeping. The blow was known well enough. A veteran prizefighter once described to me his first experience of its curious effect on the senses. Only, as he had thirty seconds to recover in instead of ten, it did not end the battle. The thirty seconds made the knockout so unlikely that the old pugilists regarded it as a rare accident, not worth trying for. The glove fighter tries for nothing else. Nevertheless knockouts, and very dramatic ones too (Mace by King, for example), did occur in the prize-ring from time to time. Captain Edgeworth Johnstone’s treatise is noteworthy in comparison with the earlier Badminton handbook of sparring by Mr. E. B. Michell (one of the Queensberry champions) as throwing over the old teaching of prize-ring boxing with mufflers, and going in frankly for glove fighting, or, to put it classically, cestus boxing. ↩︎

===Chinese===

1. 在刚出版的埃奇沃思·约翰斯通上尉的拳击论著里，我读到：“在拳击场的年代，一场打斗可持续几个钟头；击倒一击则无人知晓。”这话稍稍说得太满。这一击是人人皆知的。一位老职业拳手曾向我描述他头一回领教它对感官的奇妙作用。只不过，那时他有三十秒而不是十秒恢复，所以一击并不终结战斗。那三十秒使击倒变得如此难得，以致老拳师们把它看作帘见的意外，不值得特意去求。而戴拳套的拳手所求的别无其他。尽管如此，拳击场年代也确实不时出现击倒，而且颇具戏剧性（如金击倒梅斯一例）。埃奇沃思·约翰斯通上尉的论著之所以值得注意，在于同早先 E.B.米切尔先生（昆斯伯里规则时代的冠军之一）那本巴德明顿拳击手册相比，它抛弃了戴护具的拳击场旧法，坦率地转向拳套格斗——用古典的话说，即皮质拳击。 ↩︎
