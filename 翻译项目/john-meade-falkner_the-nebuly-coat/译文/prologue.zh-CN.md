===Original===

Sir George Farquhar, Baronet, builder of railway-stations, and institutes, and churches, author, antiquarian, and senior partner of Farquhar and Farquhar, leant back in his office chair and turned it sideways to give more point to his remarks. Before him stood an understudy, whom he was sending to superintend the restoration work at Cullerne Minster.
“Well, goodbye, Westray; keep your eyes open, and don’t forget that you have an important job before you. The church is too big to hide its light under a bushel, and this Society-for-the-Conservation-of-National-Inheritances has made up its mind to advertise itself at our expense. Ignoramuses who don’t know an aumbry from an abacus, charlatans, amateur faddists, they *will* abuse our work. Good, bad, or indifferent, it’s all one to them; they are pledged to abuse it.”
His voice rang with a fine professional contempt, but he sobered himself and came back to business.
“The south transept roof and the choir vaulting will want careful watching. There is some old trouble, too, in the central tower; and I should like later on to underpin the main crossing piers, but there is no money. For the moment I have said nothing about the tower; it is no use raising doubts that one can’t set at rest; and I don’t know how we are going to make ends meet, even with the little that it is proposed to do now. If funds come in, we must tackle the tower; but transept and choir-vaults are more pressing, and there is no risk from the bells, because the cage is so rotten that they haven’t been rung for years.
“You must do your best. It isn’t a very profitable stewardship, so try to give as good an account of it as you can. We shan’t make a penny out of it, but the church is too well known to play fast-and-loose with. I have written to the parson﻿—a foolish old fellow, who is no more fit than a lady’s-maid to be trusted with such a church as Cullerne﻿—to say you are coming tomorrow, and will put in an appearance at the church in the afternoon, in case he wishes to see you. The man is an ass, but he is legal guardian of the place, and has not done badly in collecting money for the restoration; so we must bear with him.”

===Chinese===

乔治·法夸尔爵士（Sir George Farquhar），从男爵，火车站、会馆和教堂的建造者，作家、古物学家、法夸尔-法夸尔事务所的资深合伙人，靠在办公椅里，把椅子侧过来，好叫他的训话更有分量。他面前站着一个下级，正要被派去主持卡勒恩大教堂的修复工程。

「喏，再会，韦斯特雷；眼睛放亮些，别忘了你面前是件要紧差事。这座教堂太大，藏不住自己的光；那个『国家遗产保护协会』又打定了主意要拿我们的开销给自己做广告。连圣器龛和柱头都分不清的浑人，走江湖的骗子，业余的时髦分子，他们*就是要*糟蹋我们的工作。好的、坏的、不好不坏的，在他们全一样；他们是发了誓要骂的。」

他的嗓音里响着一种漂亮的专业性的轻蔑；随即他收了收神，回到正事上。

「南耳堂的屋顶和唱诗班的穹顶要仔细盯。中央塔里也有一处老毛病；我本想往后给交叉部的主柱墩做托换基础，可是没钱。眼下关于塔我一个字也没提；挑起一个平息不了的疑心没有用处；何况连现在打算做的这一点点，我还不知道钱怎么凑。经费来了，我们必须对付塔；但耳堂和唱诗班穹顶更要紧；钟倒没有风险，钟笼烂得厉害，多年没敲过了。

「你必须尽力。这差事油水不多，所以尽量把它交代得漂亮些。咱们一个便士也赚不到；可这座教堂名气太大，玩忽不得。我已写信给那牧师——一个蠢老头，把卡勒恩这样一座教堂交给他，还不如交给一个贴身女仆——说你明天到，下午会到教堂露面，他若想见你，正好。这人是头驴，可他是这地方的法定监护人，为修复集款也集得不坏；所以咱们得容他。」
