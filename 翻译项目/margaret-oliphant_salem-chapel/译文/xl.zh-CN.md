“I hope, sir, as I haven’t said anything to give offence?﻿—it was far from my meaning,” said Tozer; “not as the﻿—person﻿—is a church-member, being only a seat-holder for one sittin’, as is down in the books. “I’ll walk with you, sir, if I ain’t in the way,” said the deacon, accompanying the young man’s restless steps downstairs. “They tell me Miss is a deal better, and all things is going on well. I wouldn’t be meddlesome, Mr. Vincent, not of my own will; but when matters is settling, sir, if you’d but hear reason! There can’t nothing but harm come of more explanations. I never had no confidence in explanations, for my part; but pleasant looks and the urns a-smoking, and a bit of green on the wall, as Phoebe and the rest could put up in no time! and just a speech as was agreeable to wind up with﻿—a bit of an anecdote, or poetry about friends as is better friends after they’ve spoke their minds and had it out﻿—that’s the thing as would settle Salem, Mr. Vincent. I don’t speak, not to bother you, sir, but for your good. There ain’t no difficulty in it; it’s easier a deal than being serious and opening up all things over again; and as for them as would like to dictate﻿—”
“I am not thinking of Salem,” said the minister; “I have many other things to distract me; for heaven’s sake, if you have any pity, leave me alone today.”
“But you’ll give in to make it a tea-meetin’?” said the anxious butterman, pausing at his own door.

“但愿我没有说出什么得罪您的话吧，先生？——我决没有那个意思，”托泽说；“至于那位——人——也不是领圣餐的会友，不过是个包一个座位的坐客，簿子上记着的。要不是一心想试试能不能劝您回心转意、依了今晚定下的那场大会，我才不会这么老早过来呢，文森特先生。这里面没有我的私利——论钱一利没有，那一类的事一概没有。我心里惦的是您。您若肯点头把它改成茶会，这笔开销我情愿自家腰包里出。您什么都不用作，只消入座主持、和颜悦色就成。余下的事，我和布朗同女眷们自会张罗。那会成为一场叫人惊喜的乐事，准是的，”这位好心的卖黄油人说；“菲比她们几个立马就能过去收拾：卡灵福德的糕和甜面包管够，文森特先生，只要您肯上点心、点个头。”

“我要出门，”文森特说；“我——有点事；别耽搁我，托泽。今天早晨我必须独自待着。”

“我陪您走一程，先生，若不碍事的话，”执事说着，跟上年轻人不安的脚步下了楼。“听说小姐好多了，一切都顺顺当当。我不是爱管闲事的人，文森特先生，本心并不想管；可是到了该了结的时候，先生，您只要肯听劝！再解释来解释去，只会越描越黑。解释这东西，我从来没信过；可一张张笑脸、一把把冒气的茶汤壶，墙上再点缀点青绿——菲比她们转眼就能挂上！临了再来一篇叫人舒坦的讲话——一段小趣闻，或者一首咏朋友的诗，说说朋友把话说开了、掏尽了，反倒更是朋友——这才是能把塞勒姆了结平息的东西啊，文森特先生。我说这些，不是要聒噪您，先生，是为您好。这又不费什么难；比板起脸、把一切再从头抖搂一遍容易多了；至于那些想发号施令的人嘛——”

“我没有想塞勒姆，”牧师说；“我有许许多多别的事分着心；看在上天份上，您若还有半分怜悯，今天就让我一个人待着。”

“可您肯点这个头，把大会改成茶会了吧？”焦虑的卖黄油人立在自己门口问。

Tozer did not make out the minister’s reply. It is difficult to distinguish between a nod and a shake of the head, under some circumstances﻿—and Vincent did not pause to give an articulate answer, but left his champion to his own devices. It seemed to Vincent to be a long time since Fordham left his house﻿—and he was possessed with a fever of impatience to see for himself what was being transacted down yonder in the sunshine, where the spire of St. Roque’s appeared in the distance through the ruddy morning haze. The bells had ceased, and all was quiet enough in Grange Lane. Quite quiet﻿—a few ordinary passengers in the tranquil road, nursemaids and children﻿—and the calm green doors closing in the concealed houses, as if no passion or agitation could penetrate them. The door of Lady Western’s garden was ajar. The minister crossed over and looked in with a wistful, despairing hope of seeing something that would contradict his conclusion. The house was basking in the spring sunshine﻿—the door open, some of the windows open, eager servants hovering about, an air of expectation over all. With eyes full of memories, the minister looked in at the half-open door, which one time and another had been to him the gate of paradise. Within, where the red geraniums and verbenas had once brightened all the borders, were pale crocuses and flowers of early spring﻿—the limes were beginning to bud, the daisies to grow among the grass. The winter was over in that sheltered and sunny place; Nature herself stood sweet within the protecting walls, and gathered all the tenderest sweets of spring to greet the bride in the new beginning of her life. It was but a glance, but the spectator, in the bitterness of his heart, did not lose a single tint or line; and just then the joy-bells burst out once more from St. Roque’s. Poor Vincent drew back from the door as the sudden sound stung him to the heart. Nothing had any pity for him﻿—all the world, and every voice and breath therein, sided with the others in their joy. He went on blindly, without thinking where he was going, with a kind of dull, stubborn determination in his heart, not to turn back in his wretchedness even from the sight of the happy procession which he knew must be advancing to meet him. A pang more or less, what did it matter?

托泽没有听清牧师的答复。有些情形之下，点头同摇头是难分辨的——文森特也没有停下来给一个清楚的说法，把他的守护人撂在原地自想办法。在文森特感觉里，福德姆离开他家已经很久了——他心里烧着一阵急火，要亲眼去看看那边太阳底下正在办着什么事；那边，圣罗克的尖塔远远地从红彤彤的晨霭里露出来。钟声停了，格兰奇巷里静得很。十分安静——那条平静的路上只有几个寻常的行人，保姆和孩子——一扇扇绿门把深藏的宅子关得严严实实，仿佛什么激情与骚动也钻不进去。韦斯顿夫人家园子的门虚掩着。牧师穿过街，向里张望，怀着一线怅惘而绝望的希望，想看见一点什么，推翻他的结论。宅子正沐在春日的阳光里——门开着，有几扇窗也开着，急切的仆人们进进出出，满宅笼着一层等候的气象。牧师含着满怀旧忆，向那扇半开的门里望去——这扇门，曾一度又一度，是他进天堂的门。里面，天竺葵和马鞭草曾经映亮全部花境的地方，如今是苍白的番红花和早春的花朵——菩提树正在发芽，雏菊在草间生长。在那个背风向阳的园子里，冬天过完了；大自然亲自立在护墙之内，甜甜地集齐春天最娇嫩的芬芳，来迎新娘人生新的开端。这只是匆匆一瞥，可这旁观的人，在心里那阵苦楚里，一色一线也没有漏掉；正在这时，圣罗克的喜钟又迸响了。可怜的文森特从门前猛地退回来，那骤然的声音刺进他的心。没有一样东西可怜他——全世界，和世间每个声音每口气息，都站在欢乐的他们那一边。他盲目前行，不想自己走向哪里，心里带着一股沉闷而倔强的决意：哪怕撞上他知道必会迎面而来的欢乐的行列，也不在这凄苦里回头。多一记痛，少一记痛，又算得了什么？

And for the last time he would look on Her who was nothing in the world to him now﻿—who never could have been anything﻿—yet who had somehow shed such streams of light upon the poor minister’s humble path, as no reality in all his life had ever shed before. He paused on the edge of the road as he saw the carriage coming. It was one of those moments when a man’s entire life becomes apparent to him in long perspective of past and future, he himself and all the world standing still between. The bells rang on his heart, with echoes from the wheels and the horses’ feet coming up in superb pride and triumph. Heaven and earth were glad for her in her joy. He, in his great trouble, stood dark in the sunshine and looked on.
It was only a moment, and no more. He would have seen nothing but the white mist of the veil which surrounded her, had not she in her loveliness and kindness perceived him, and bent forward in the carriage with a little motion of her hand calling the attention of her unseen bridegroom to that figure on the way. At sight of that movement, the unhappy young man started with an intolerable pang, and went on heedless where he was going. He could not control the momentary passion. She had never harmed him﻿—never meant to dazzle him with her beauty, or trifle with his love, or break his heart. It was kind as the sunshine, this sweet bridal face leaning out with that momentary glance of recognition. She would have given him her kind hand, her sweet smile as of old, had they met more closely﻿—no remorseful consciousness was in *her* eyes; but neither the bells, nor the flowers, nor the sunshine, went with such a pang to poor Vincent’s heart as did that look of kindness. It was all unreal then﻿—no foundation at all in it? not enough to call a passing colour to her cheek, or to dim her sweet eyes on her bridal day? He went down the long road in the insensibility of passion﻿—seeing nothing, caring for nothing﻿—stung to the heart. No look of triumph, no female dart of conscious cruelty, could have given the poor minister so bitter a wound. All her treasured looks and smiles﻿—the touch of her hand﻿—her words, of which he had scarcely forgotten one﻿—did they mean nothing after all? nothing but kindness? He had laid his heart at her feet; if she had trodden on it he could have forgiven her; but she only went on smiling, and never saw the treasure in her way.

他要最后看她一眼——她如今于他已是世上无关的人——本来也永远不可能是别的——可她不知怎么，曾把那样的光流，泼在可怜的牧师卑微的路上，是他一生中任何真实从不曾泼下过的。望见马车驶来，他在路边停住。那是这样的一刻：一个人整个的一生，在过往与未来的长景里一齐显了形，他本人同全世界都静止在中间。钟在他的心上敲着，车轮与马蹄的回声，挟着堂皇的骄傲与凯旋迎面而来。天与地都为她的欢乐而欢喜。他，在他巨大的苦难里，黑黑地立在阳光中望着。

只是一瞬，仅此而已。若不是她，以她的可爱与好心，望见了他，在车里俯身向前，小小说了句什么，一只手微微一动，把那路边的人影指给她看不见的新郎——他本会什么也看不见，只有罩着她的那片面纱的白雾。见了那一动，这不幸的年轻人被一阵难忍的剧痛惊得跳起，顾不得脚下的路，径自走去。他管不住那一阵骤起的激情。她从不曾伤害他——从不曾有意用美貌炫惑他、拿他的爱情耍弄、碎他的心。这张甜蜜的新娘的脸探出车窗、带着那一瞬认出他的顾盼，是和阳光一样的好心。倘若他们挨得更近，她会照旧把好心的手伸给他、把甜甜的微笑给他——她的眼睛里没有半点愧疚的自觉；可是钟声也好，花也好，阳光也好，都不及那一眼的好心，更尖锐地刺进可怜的文森特的心。那么这一切全是虚的——全无一点根底？不值到叫她脸上添一抹转瞬的红、不值到叫她甜蜜的眼睛在婚礼这天蒙一层雾？他沿着长路走去，处在激情的麻木里——什么也看不见，什么都不在乎——心上扎着刺。没有任何得胜的眼色、没有任何女子自觉残忍的一掷，能给这可怜的牧师这样苦的伤。她所有那些珍藏的眼色与微笑——她的手的触碰——她的话，他几乎没有一句忘得掉——到头来竟什么都不是？只是好心而已？他曾把他的心放在她脚边；她若踩了它，他倒还能饶恕她；可她只是照旧微笑着，从没有看见路上这份珍宝。

And this was the end. The unfortunate young man could not give way to any outbreak of the passion that consumed him; he could but go on hotly﻿—on past St. Roque’s, where flowers still lay in the porch, and the open doors invited strangers, to the silent country, where the fields lay callow under the touch of spring. Spring! everlasting mockery of human trouble! Here were the hedgerows stirring, the secret grain beginning to throb conscious in the old furrows; but life itself standing still﻿—coming to a sudden end in this heart which filled the young man’s entire frame with pulsations of anguish. All his existence had flowed towards this day, and took its termination here. His love﻿—heaven help him! he had but one heart, and had thrown it away; his work﻿—that too was to come to nothing, and be ended; all his traditions, all his hopes, were they to be buried in one grave? and what was to become after of the posthumous and nameless life?

而这便是结局了。这不幸的年轻人，发作不出吞噬他的那场激情；他只能灼灼地往前走——走过圣罗克，门廊里还堆着花，敞开的大门迎着外客——走进寂静的乡野，那里的田地在春的抚触下还是光秃新嫩。春天！对人间苦难永久的嘲弄！这里树篱在萌动，古老的犁沟里，沉睡的种子开始搏动着苏醒；而生命本身却停住了——在这颗心里戛然而止，这颗心以苦痛的搏动，涨满了年轻人的全副身躯。他整个的存在都朝这一天流来，也在这里到了尽头。他的爱——上天可怜他！他只有一颗心，却把它抛掉了；他的事业——那也要落空、要完了；他全部的信念、全部的希望，难道也要埋进同一座坟？往后，那身后无名的日子，又怎么过？
