## XVII The Tenant of the Palanquin / XVII 轿中占卜师

===Original===
The Marquis called on me next day. My late breakfast was still upon the table.

He had come, he said, to ask a favour. An accident had happened to his carriage in the crowd on leaving the ball, and he begged, if I were going into Paris, a seat in mine﻿—I was going in, and was extremely glad of his company. He came with me to my hotel; we went up to my rooms. I was surprised to see a man seated in an easy chair, with his back towards us, reading a newspaper. He rose. It was the Count de St. Alyre, his gold spectacles on his nose; his black wig, in oily curls, lying close to his narrow head, and showing, like carved ebony over a repulsive visage of boxwood. His black muffler had been pulled down. His right arm was in a sling. I don’t know whether there was anything unusual in his countenance that day, or whether it was but the effect of prejudice arising from all I had heard in my mysterious interview in his park, but I thought his countenance was more strikingly forbidding than I had seen it before.

I was not callous enough in the ways of sin to meet this man, injured at least in intent, thus suddenly, without a momentary disturbance.

He smiled.

===Chinese===
第二天，侯爵来拜访我。我那顿迟开的早饭还摆在桌上。

他说，他是来求个人情的。昨晚离开舞会时，他的马车在人群里出了岔子，所以他想求个座位——如果我进巴黎的话。我正要进城，极愿与他同行。他随我到了旅馆；我们上楼进了我的房间。我吃了一惊：安乐椅里坐着一个人，背朝着我们，正在读报。那人站起身来。是圣阿利尔伯爵，鼻梁上架着金丝眼镜；黑色假发打着油亮的卷儿，紧贴着他那窄窄的头颅，看上去就像乌木雕饰底下露出一张黄杨木刻的可憎面孔。黑围巾拉到了下巴底下。右臂吊着绷带。我说不清那天他的脸是否真有什么异样，也不知是不是夜里在他园林中那场神秘会谈、听了那许多话之后先入为主的成见作祟，只觉得他那副面相比我从前所见的更加阴森拒人。

在罪孽的门道上，我还没有练出足够硬的心肠，骤然见到这个至少在意图上受过我伤害的人，心头不由一阵慌乱。

他微微一笑。

===Original===
“I called, Monsieur Beckett, in the hope of finding you here,” he croaked, “and I meditated, I fear, taking a great liberty, but my friend the Marquis d’Harmonville, on whom I have perhaps some claim, will perhaps give me the assistance I require so much.”

“With great pleasure,” said the Marquis, “but not till after six o’clock. I must go this moment to a meeting of three or four people, whom I cannot disappoint, and I know, perfectly, we cannot break up earlier.”

“What am I to do?” exclaimed the Count, “an hour would have done it all. Was ever contretemps so unlucky!”

“I’ll give you an hour, with pleasure,” said I.

“How very good of you, Monsieur, I hardly dare to hope it. The business, for so gay and charming a man as Monsieur Beckett, is a little *funeste*. Pray read this note which reached me this morning.”

It certainly was not cheerful. It was a note stating that the body of his, the Count’s cousin, Monsieur de St. Amand, who had died at his house, the Château Clery, had been, in accordance with his written directions, sent for burial at Père La Chaise, and, with the permission of the Count de St. Alyre, would reach his house (the Château de la Carque), at about ten o’clock on the night following, to be conveyed thence in a hearse, with any member of the family who might wish to attend the obsequies.

===Chinese===
「我登门拜访，贝克特先生，正盼着您在这儿，」他哑着嗓子说，「我盘算着的，恕我冒昧，是一桩大大的叨扰；可我的朋友德·哈蒙维尔侯爵——我于他或许还有几分情分——大约肯给我眼下急需的帮衬。」

「非常乐意，」侯爵说，「不过得等到六点以后。我这刻就要去赴一个三四人的聚会，那些人我万不能爽约，而且我清楚得很，散会不会早。」

「那我可怎么办？」伯爵嚷道，「一个钟头本来就能把事全办完。天底下还有比这更倒霉的意外（contretemps）么！」

「我给您一个钟头，乐意效劳。」我说。

「先生真是太好心了，我简直不敢奢望。这桩差事，对贝克特先生这样风流有趣的人来说，未免有点*凶煞*（funeste）。请读读今早送到我手中的这张便条。」

这便条确实不轻松。上面说，伯爵的表亲德·圣阿芒先生（Monsieur de St. Amand），在他家克莱里城堡（Château Clery）病故，遵其书面遗嘱，遗体已启运拉雪兹神父公墓安葬；经圣阿利尔伯爵许可，灵柩将于次晚十点前后抵达他家（拉卡尔克城堡），再从那里由灵车载往墓地，家族中人愿送葬者可随行。

===Original===
“I did not see the poor gentleman twice in my life,” said the Count, “but this office, as he has no other kinsman, disagreeable as it is, I could scarcely decline, and so I want to attend at the office to have the book signed, and the order entered. But here is another misery. By ill luck, I have sprained my thumb, and can’t sign my name for a week to come. However, one name answers as well as another. Yours as well as mine. And as you are so good as to come with me, all will go right.”

Away, we drove. The Count gave me a memorandum of the Christian and surnames of the deceased, his age, the complaint he died of, and the usual particulars; also a note of the exact position in which a grave, the dimensions of which were described, of the ordinary simple kind, was to be dug, between two vaults belonging to the family of St. Amand. The funeral, it was stated, would arrive at half-past one o’clock a.m. (the next night but one); and he handed me the money, with extra fees, for a burial by night. It was a good deal; and I asked him, as he entrusted the whole affair to me, in whose name I should take the receipt.

“Not in mine, my good friend. They wanted me to become an executor, which I, yesterday, wrote to decline; and I am informed that if the receipt were in my name it would constitute me an executor in the eye of the law, and fix me in that position. Take it, pray, if you have no objection, in your own name.”

This, accordingly, I did.

“You will see, by-and-by, why I am obliged to mention all these particulars.”

===Chinese===
「这位可怜的先生，我生平统共没见过两面，」伯爵说，「可这桩差事，既然他再没有别的亲眷，虽说不情愿，我实在推辞不得。所以我要亲自到办事处去，把簿册签了，文书登了。可是又有一桩苦处：倒霉，我扭伤了拇指，往后一个礼拜签不得自己的名字。好在签谁的名字都是一样。您的同我的一样。既然先生肯赏光陪我走一遭，一切就都顺了。」

我们驱车而去。伯爵交给我一份备忘，写着死者的教名和姓氏、年龄、死因，以及照例应载的项目；还有一份说明，写明墓穴的确切位置——那墓的尺寸也有描述，是寻常朴素的一种——要挖在圣阿芒家（St. Amand）两座墓窖之间。丧仪据载将于（后天夜里）凌晨一点半抵达；他又把款项交到我手里，含着加倍的规费，为的是夜间下葬。数目不小；他把整个事情都托付给了我，我便问他，收据该开在谁的名下。

「不要开我的，我的好朋友。他们本要我做遗嘱执行人，我昨天已去信辞了；而且听说，收据若开了我的名字，在法律眼里就等于立我为执行人，把我就钉在那个位子上了。请您行个好，若不见怪，就开您自己的名字。」

我于是照办了。

「往后您自会明白，我为什么不得不把这些细节一一交代。」

===Original===
The Count, meanwhile, was leaning back in the carriage, with his black silk muffler up to his nose, and his hat shading his eyes, while he dozed in his corner; in which state I found him on my return.

Paris had lost its charm for me. I hurried through the little business I had to do, longed once more for my quiet room in the Dragon Volant, the melancholy woods of the Château de la Carque, and the tumultuous and thrilling influence of proximity to the object of my wild but wicked romance.

I was delayed some time by my stockbroker. I had a very large sum, as I told you, at my banker’s, uninvested. I cared very little for a few days’ interest﻿—very little for the entire sum, compared with the image that occupied my thoughts, and beckoned me with a white arm, through the dark, toward the spreading lime-trees and chestnuts of the Château de la Carque. But I had fixed this day to meet him, and was relieved when he told me that I had better let it lie in my banker’s hands for a few days longer, as the funds would certainly fall immediately. This accident, too, was not without its immediate bearing on my subsequent adventures.

===Chinese===
一路上，伯爵仰在车厢里，黑绸围巾一直掩到鼻子，帽子遮着眼睛，缩在角落里打盹；我回来时，他还是这副模样。

巴黎于我已失了魅力。我匆匆办完手头那几件小事，一心只盼回到飞龙旅馆那间安静的屋子，回到拉卡尔克城堡愁惨的树林，回到我那桩狂热而不光彩的罗曼司的对象近旁——那份近在咫尺的、骚动而令人战栗的魔力。

证券经纪人又耽搁了我一阵。如我告诉过你的，我在银行里存着一大笔款子，尚未投资。几天的利息，我全不放在心上——同占据我心头的那幅影像相比，整笔款子又算得了什么；那影像正伸出雪白的手臂，穿过黑暗，朝着拉卡尔克城堡舒展的菩提树和栗树向我招手。可我约的正是今天同他见面；他对我说，款项不妨再在银行里搁上几天，因为公债行情眼看要跌——这话叫我松了口气。这桩小小的意外，日后同我的奇遇也正有着直接的干系。

===Original===
When I reached the Dragon Volant, I found, in my sitting-room, a good deal to my chagrin, my two guests, whom I had quite forgotten. I inwardly cursed my own stupidity for having embarrassed myself with their agreeable society. It could not be helped now, however, and a word to the waiters put all things in train for dinner.

Tom Whistlewick was in great force; and he commenced almost immediately with a very odd story.

He told me that not only Versailles, but all Paris, was in a ferment, in consequence of a revolting, and all but sacrilegious, practical joke, played off on the night before.

The pagoda, as he persisted in calling the palanquin, had been left standing on the spot where we last saw it. Neither conjuror, nor usher, nor bearers had ever returned. When the ball closed, and the company at length retired, the servants who attended to put out the lights, and secure the doors, found it still there.

It was determined, however, to let it stand where it was until next morning, by which time, it was conjectured, its owners would send messengers to remove it.

===Chinese===
回到飞龙旅馆，我在起居室里发现了我的两位客人——着实懊恼，我竟把他们忘了个干净。我心里暗骂自己糊涂，竟让这两位好同伴搅了局。事已至此，无法可想，吩咐侍者一声，晚饭诸事便都安排停当。

汤姆·惠斯尔威克精神大振，几乎立刻就讲起一桩天大的怪事。

他告诉我，不但凡尔赛，连整个巴黎都沸腾了，为的是前一夜有人开了一个骇人听闻、几近亵渎的恶作剧。

那座「宝塔」——他咬定了要这么称呼那顶轿辇——一直搁在我们最后见到它的地方。术士也好，引座人也好，轿夫也好，一个都没有回来。舞会散场、宾客终于尽退之后，留下来熄灯锁门的仆役们，发现它还在那里。

于是决定，姑且让它立在原处，等第二天早晨；料想届时主人自会打发人来搬走。

===Original===
None arrived. The servants were then ordered to take it away; and its extraordinary weight, for the first time, reminded them of its forgotten human occupant. Its door was forced; and, judge what was their disgust, when they discovered, not a living man, but a corpse! Three or four days must have passed since the death of the burly man in the Chinese tunic and painted cap. Some people thought it was a trick designed to insult the Allies, in whose honour the ball was got up. Others were of opinion that it was nothing worse than a daring and cynical jocularity which, shocking as it was, might yet be forgiven to the high spirits and irrepressible buffoonery of youth. Others, again, fewer in number, and mystically given, insisted that the corpse was bona fide necessary to the exhibition, and that the disclosures and allusions which had astonished so many people were distinctly due to necromancy.

===Chinese===
谁也没来。仆役们这才奉命把它抬走；它那出奇的沉重，头一回叫人想起里面那位被遗忘了的活人。门被撬开了；想想他们恶心到什么地步——里面不是活人，而是一具死尸！那个穿中国袍、戴彩绘帽的魁梧汉子，死了总有三四天了。有人以为，这是一个存心侮辱同盟国的把戏——这场舞会本是为向同盟国致敬而办的。另一些人认为，这不过是放肆而玩世不恭的一手恶谑，虽则骇人，念在年轻人的血气和压不住的浑劲儿，尚可原谅。还有一些人，数目更少，心思偏向神秘一途，一口咬定这具尸体于这场展演实实在在（bona fide）必不可少，那些叫无数人惊诧的揭底和暗语，分明出自死灵法术。

===Original===
“The matter, however, is now in the hands of the police,” observed Monsieur Carmaignac, “and we are not the body we were two or three months ago, if the offenders against propriety and public feeling are not traced, and convicted, unless, indeed, they have been a great deal more cunning than such fools generally are.”

I was thinking within myself how utterly inexplicable was my colloquy with the conjuror, so cavalierly dismissed by Monsieur Carmaignac as a “fool;” and the more I thought the more marvellous it seemed.

“It certainly was an original joke, though not a very clear one,” said Whistlewick.

“Not even original,” said Carmaignac. “Very nearly the same thing was done, a hundred years ago or more, at a state ball in Paris; and the rascals who played the trick were never found out.”

In this Monsieur Carmaignac, as I afterwards discovered, spoke truly; for, among my books of French anecdote and memoirs, the very incident is marked, by my own hand.

While we were thus talking, the waiter told us that dinner was served; and we withdrew accordingly; my guests more than making amends for my comparative taciturnity.

===Chinese===
「不过，这案子如今已到警察手里，」卡马尼亚克先生说，「我们也不是两三个月前那个班子了；伤风败俗、搅扰公愤的家伙，若不能查出来定罪——除非他们比这类蠢货通常的情形狡猾得多。」

我心里正想着，我同那术士的一场对答，究竟如何地无从解释——卡马尼亚克先生却轻飘飘一句「蠢货」打发了；我越想越觉得神妙。

「这玩笑确属别开生面，只是不大明白。」惠斯尔威克说。

「连别开生面也谈不上，」卡马尼亚克说。「一百多年前，巴黎的一场宫廷舞会上，就有人干过几乎一模一样的事；那帮捣鬼的流氓，始终没有查出来。」

这一点，卡马尼亚克先生说的，我后来发觉属实；因为在我那些法国掌故和回忆录的书里，恰好记着这桩逸事，还是我亲手做的记号。

我们正这么说着，侍者来禀报晚饭已齐；我们便入席；两位客人的健谈，绰绰有余地补足了我相对的沉默。
