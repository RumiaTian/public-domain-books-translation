## XXIII A Cup of Coffee / XXIII 一杯咖啡

===Original===
The room was carpetless. On the floor were a quantity of shavings, and some score of bricks. Beyond these, on a narrow table, lay an object, which I could hardly believe I saw aright.

I approached and drew from it a sheet which had very slightly disguised its shape. There was no mistake about it. It was a coffin; and on the lid was a plate, with the inscription in French:

> Pierre de la Roche St. Amand.
> Agée de XXIII ans.

I drew back with a double shock. So, then, the funeral after all had not yet left! Here lay the body. I had been deceived. This, no doubt, accounted for the embarrassment so manifest in the Countess’s manner. She would have done more wisely had she told me the true state of the case.

I drew back from this melancholy room, and closed the door. Her distrust of me was the worst rashness she could have committed. There is nothing more dangerous than misapplied caution. In entire ignorance of the fact I had entered the room, and there I might have lighted upon some of the very persons it was our special anxiety that I should avoid.

===Chinese===
这房间没有铺地毯。地板上是一堆刨花，还有二十来块砖。在这些东西那头，一张窄桌上躺着一件东西，我几乎不敢相信自己的眼睛。

我走上前去，从它上面掀开一层仅仅稍稍掩住其形状的布单。没有看错。那是一口棺材；盖板上钉着一块铭牌，刻着法文的碑铭：

> 皮埃尔·德·拉·罗什·圣阿芒（Pierre de la Roche St. Amand）。
> 享年二十三岁（Agée de XXIII ans）。

我带着双重的震惊退了回来。这么说，送葬的队伍终究还没有出发！遗体就停在这里。我被骗了。这无疑解释了伯爵夫人神态间那般明显的局促。她若把实情告诉我，才是更聪明的做法。

我从这个阴惨惨的房间退出来，关上了门。她对我的不信任，是她所能犯下的最糟的轻率。没有什么比用错了地方的谨慎更危险。我全然不知实情，闯进了那个房间，在那儿我极可能撞上几个恰恰是我们最担心要避开的人。

===Original===
These reflections were interrupted, almost as soon as begun, by the return of the Countess de St. Alyre. I saw at a glance that she detected in my face some evidence of what had happened, for she threw a hasty look towards the door.

“Have you seen anything—anything to disturb you, dear Richard? Have you been out of this room?”

I answered promptly, “Yes,” and told her frankly what had happened.

“Well, I did not like to make you more uneasy than necessary. Besides, it is disgusting and horrible. The body *is* there; but the Count had departed a quarter of an hour before I lighted the coloured lamp, and prepared to receive you. The body did not arrive till eight or ten minutes after he had set out. He was afraid lest the people at Père la Chaise should suppose that the funeral was postponed. He knew that the remains of poor Pierre would certainly reach this tonight although an unexpected delay has occurred; and there are reasons why he wishes the funeral completed before tomorrow. The hearse with the body must leave this in ten minutes. So soon as it is gone, we shall be free to set out upon our wild and happy journey. The horses are to the carriage in the porte-cochère. As for this *funeste* horror (she shuddered very prettily), let us think of it no more.”

===Chinese===
这些念头刚起，便被德·圣阿利尔伯爵夫人的归来打断了。我一眼便看出，她从我脸上察觉了事情的一些痕迹，因为她朝那扇门仓促地瞥了一眼。

"你看见什么了吗——什么让你不安的东西，亲爱的理查德？你出过这间屋子吗？"

我立刻答了声"是"，并把发生的事坦白告诉了她。

"唉，我不想让你担不必要的更多心事。再说，这事又恶心又可怕。遗体确实在那里；可是在我点亮彩色灯、准备接你进来之前一刻钟，伯爵就已经动身走了。遗体是在他出发之后八九分钟才送到的。他怕拉雪兹神父公墓那边的人以为葬礼改期了。他知道可怜的皮埃尔的遗骸今晚必定会送到，尽管出了个意料之外的耽搁；而他自有缘故，希望葬礼赶在明天之前办完。载遗体的灵车十分钟后必须离开这里。它一走，我们就可以动身，踏上我们这场狂放而快乐的旅程了。马已经套上车，停在门廊车廊里了。至于这桩凶煞事（funeste）的恐怖"（她颤得煞是动人），"我们再别去想它了。"

===Original===
She bolted the door of communication, and when she turned, it was with such a pretty penitence in her face and attitude, that I was ready to throw myself at her feet.

“It is the last time,” she said, in a sweet sad little pleading, “I shall ever practise a deception on my brave and beautiful Richard—my hero? Am I forgiven?”

Here was another scene of passionate effusion, and lovers’ raptures and declamations, but only murmured, lest the ears of listeners should be busy.

At length, on a sudden, she raised her hand, as if to prevent my stirring, her eyes fixed on me, and her ear toward the door of the room in which the coffin was placed, and remained breathless in that attitude for a few moments. Then, with a little nod towards me, she moved on tiptoe to the door, and listened, extending her hand backward as if to warn me against advancing; and, after a little time, she returned, still on tiptoe, and whispered to me, “They are removing the coffin—come with me.”

I accompanied her into the room from which her maid, as she told me, had spoken to her. Coffee and some old china cups, which appeared to me quite beautiful, stood on a silver tray; and some liqueur glasses, with a flask, which turned out to be noyau, on a salver beside it.

===Chinese===
她闩上连通两屋的门，转过身来时，脸上和姿态里带着那样楚楚动人的悔意，我几乎要拜倒在她脚下。

"这是最后一次，"她用甜甜的、忧伤的口吻小声恳求道，"我再也不同我勇敢英俊的理查德——我的英雄——耍半点欺瞒了？我能得到原谅吗？"

接着又是一幕激情奔涌的场面，恋人间的狂喜与剖白，只是都压在唇间喃喃低语，生怕隔墙有耳。

终于，她蓦地举起手，像是要止住我的动作，眼睛紧盯着我，耳朵偏向停放着棺材的那间房的门，屏住呼吸保持着这个姿势，片刻不动。然后，她朝我微微颔首，踮着脚走到门边听了听，一只手向后伸着，仿佛警告我别上前；过了一小会儿，她仍踮着脚回来，对我悄声道："他们在抬棺材了——跟我来。"

我随她走进那个房间——据她说，她的女仆方才就是在那儿同她说话的。一只银托盘上摆着咖啡和几只旧瓷杯，在我看来美极了；旁边另一只托盘上是几只利口酒杯和一只细颈瓶，后来知道盛的是努瓦约酒（noyau）。

===Original===
“I shall attend you. I’m to be your servant here; I am to have my own way; I shall not think myself forgiven by my darling if he refuses to indulge me in anything.” She filled a cup with coffee, and handed it to me with her left hand, her right arm she fondly, passed over my shoulder, and with her fingers through my curls caressingly, she whispered, “Take this, I shall take some just now.”

It was excellent; and when I had done she handed me the liqueur, which I also drank.

“Come back, dearest, to the next room,” she said. “By this time those terrible people must have gone away, and we shall be safer there, for the present, than here.”

“You shall direct, and I obey; you shall command me, not only now, but always, and in all things, my beautiful queen!” I murmured.

My heroics were unconsciously, I daresay, founded upon my ideal of the French school of lovemaking. I am, even now, ashamed as I recall the bombast to which I treated the Countess de St. Alyre.

“There, you shall have another miniature glass—a fairy glass—of noyau,” she said, gaily. In this volatile creature, the funereal gloom of the moment before, and the suspense of an adventure on which all her future was staked, disappeared in a moment. She ran and returned with another tiny glass, which, with an eloquent or tender little speech, I placed to my lips and sipped.

===Chinese===
"我来侍候你。这一回我做你的仆人；一切要依我的意思；我的心肝若不肯样样纵容我，我就不认为自己得到了原谅。"她斟了一杯咖啡，用左手递给我，右手臂柔情地搭上我的肩，手指插进我的鬈发间轻抚着，悄声说："把它喝了，我马上也喝一点。"

咖啡好极了；我喝完后，她又递来利口酒，我也喝了。

"回隔壁房间去吧，最亲爱的，"她说，"这会儿那伙可怕的人必定已经走了，眼下我们在那边要比在这里安全。"

"你来指点，我来服从；你只管命令我，不单是现在，而是永远，在一切事情上，我美丽的王后！"我喃喃道。

我敢说，我的这套英雄腔，是无意间师法了我理想中法国式的谈情路数。即使现在，一想起自己对德·圣阿利尔伯爵夫人发过的那通浮夸言辞，我还臊得慌。

"来，再给你一小杯——一小杯精灵杯里的努瓦约酒，"她欢快地说。在这个轻灵活泼的人身上，方才那阵丧仪的阴郁，和一场押上她全部前程的冒险的悬心，顷刻间都无影无踪了。她跑开去又端来一小杯，我伴着一句动听而温柔的小话，把它举到唇边啜了一口。

===Original===
I kissed her hand, I kissed her lips, I gazed in her beautiful eyes, and kissed her again unresisting.

“You call me Richard, by what name am I to call my beautiful divinity?” I asked.

“You call me Eugenie, it is my name. Let us be quite real; that is, if you love as entirely as I do.”

“Eugenie!” I exclaimed, and broke into a new rapture upon the name.

It ended by my telling her how impatient I was to set out upon our journey; and, as I spoke, suddenly an odd sensation overcame me. It was not in the slightest degree like faintness. I can find no phrase to describe it, but a sudden constraint of the brain; it was as if the membrane in which it lies, if there be such a thing, contracted, and became inflexible.

“Dear Richard! what is the matter?” she exclaimed, with terror in her looks. “Good Heavens! are you ill? I conjure you, sit down; sit in this chair.” She almost forced me into one; I was in no condition to offer the least resistance. I recognised but too truly the sensations that supervened. I was lying back in the chair in which I sat without the power, by this time, of uttering a syllable, of closing my eyelids, of moving my eyes, of stirring a muscle. I had in a few seconds glided into precisely the state in which I had passed so many appalling hours when approaching Paris, in my night-drive with the Marquis d’Harmonville.

Great and loud was the lady’s agony. She seemed to have lost all sense of fear. She called me by my name, shook me by the shoulder, raised my arm and let it fall, all the time imploring of me, in distracting sentences, to make the slightest sign of life, and vowing that if I did not, she would make away with herself.

These ejaculations, after a minute or two, suddenly subsided. The lady was perfectly silent and cool. In a very businesslike way she took a candle and stood before me, pale indeed, very pale, but with an expression only of intense scrutiny with a dash of horror in it. She moved the candle before my eyes slowly, evidently watching the effect. She then set it down, and rang a hand-bell two or three times sharply. She placed the two cases (I mean hers containing the jewels) and my strong box, side by side on the table; and I saw her carefully lock the door that gave access to the room in which I had just now sipped my coffee.

我吻了她的手，吻了她的唇，凝望她美丽的眼睛，又一次吻了她，她顺从地任我亲吻。

"你唤我理查德，那我该用什么名字称呼我美丽的女神呢？"我问。

"你就唤我欧仁妮，这是我的名字。让我们完全以真心相待；当然，前提是你爱得同我一样彻底。"

"欧仁妮！"我叫出声来，对着这个名字又生出一阵新的狂喜。

最后，我对她说，我多么迫不及待地想踏上我们的旅程；说话间，一种奇异的感觉突然攫住了我。它丝毫也不像晕眩。我找不到词句形容它，只觉得脑中猛地一阵拘紧；仿佛包裹着大脑的那层膜——如果真有这么一层膜——缩紧了，变得僵直。

"亲爱的理查德！怎么了？"她叫道，眼里满是惊怖。"天哪！你病了吗？我求你坐下；坐在这把椅子上。"她几乎是强把我按进椅子里；我已无力作丝毫反抗。接着袭来的那些感觉，我认得太真切了。我仰靠在椅子里，这时已发不出一个音节，阖不上眼皮，转不动眼球，动不了一丝肌肉。几秒钟之间，我已滑入了那种状态——同我随德·哈蒙维尔侯爵夜车驶近巴黎时熬过那许多骇人时光的状态，一模一样。

这位女士的悲恸激烈而喧腾。她似乎全然忘了恐惧。她喊着我的名字，摇我的肩，举起我的手臂又任它落下，一面用撕心裂肺的话句句恳求我，求我作出最微小的一丝生命迹象，并发誓若我理也不理，她就自寻短见。

这阵呼天抢地，过了一两分钟便骤然平息。这位女士完全安静下来，冷静得很。她拿起一支蜡烛，用十足办公事的架势站在我面前——脸确实白，白得很，但那表情只是凝神细致的审视，掺着一丝惊骇。她让蜡烛在我眼前缓缓移动，分明在观察效果。然后她放下蜡烛，把一只手铃急促地摇了两三下。她把两只匣子（我是说她装珠宝的那只）同我的钱箱并排放在桌上；我还看见她仔细锁上了通向那间房——我方才在其中啜饮咖啡的那间——的门。
