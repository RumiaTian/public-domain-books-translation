## IV Monsieur Droqville / IV 德罗克维尔先生

===Original===
Full of this exciting hope, I sauntered out, upon the steps of the Belle Étoile. It was now night, and a pleasant moonlight over everything. I had entered more into my romance since my arrival, and this poetic light heightened the sentiment. What a drama, if she turned out to be the Count’s daughter, and in love with me! What a delightful﻿—*tragedy*, if she turned out to be the Count’s wife!

In this luxurious mood, I was accosted by a tall and very elegantly-made gentleman, who appeared to be about fifty. His air was courtly and graceful, and there was in his whole manner and appearance something so distinguished, that it was impossible not to suspect him of being a person of rank.

===Chinese===
满怀着这个令人心痒难耐的希望，我信步走到美星旅馆门前的台阶上。这时已是夜晚，一片宜人的月光洒在万物之上。自打到了这里，我愈发沉浸在我的罗曼史里，而这诗意的月光又把这份情怀烘托得更浓。倘若她原来是伯爵的女儿，而且爱着我，那将是怎样一出戏剧！倘若她原来是伯爵的妻子，那又是何等美妙的——*悲剧*！

我正处在这般陶然的心境里，一位身材颀长、体态极为优雅的绅士上来同我搭话。他看上去约莫五十岁，气度雍容潇洒，整个举止和仪表都带着那么一种出众不凡的味道，叫人不能不疑心他是位有身份的人物。

===Original===
He had been standing upon the steps, looking out, like me, upon the moonlight effects that transformed, as it were, the objects and buildings in the little street. He accosted me, I say, with the politeness, at once easy and lofty, of a French nobleman of the old school. He asked me if I were not Mr. Beckett? I assented; and he immediately introduced himself as the Marquis d’Harmonville (this information he gave me in a low tone), and asked leave to present me with a letter from Lord R﻿—﻿—, who knew my father slightly, and had once done me, also, a trifling kindness.

This English peer, I may mention, stood very high in the political world, and was named as the most probable successor to the distinguished post of English Minister at Paris.

I received it with a low bow, and read:

> “My Dear Beckett,
> “I beg to introduce my very dear friend, the Marquis d’Harmonville, who will explain to you the nature of the services it may be in your power to render him and us.”

===Chinese===
他原本就站在台阶上，和我一样，眺望着月色的奇效——那月光仿佛把小街上的景物和屋宇都改换了模样。我说，他上来同我攀谈时，带着旧派法国贵族那种既从容又高贵的礼貌。他问我可是贝克特先生？我应了是；他随即自报家门，说是德·哈蒙维尔侯爵（这一节他是压低了嗓音告诉我的），并请求允许他转交给我一封R——勋爵的信——这位勋爵与我父亲有过些微交往，从前也曾对我有过一点小小的好处。

不妨说一句，这位英国贵族在政界地位极高，已被点名为英国驻巴黎公使这一显赫职位最有希望的继任者。

我深深鞠了一躬，接过信来，读道：

> 「亲爱的贝克特：
> 「谨为我至交好友德·哈蒙维尔侯爵作介，你或有能力为他、为我们效些劳，其性质他自会向你说明。」

===Original===
He went on to speak of the Marquis as a man whose great wealth, whose intimate relations with the old families, and whose legitimate influence with the court rendered him the fittest possible person for those friendly offices which, at the desire of his own sovereign, and of our government, he has so obligingly undertaken.

It added a great deal to my perplexity, when I read, further﻿—

> “By the by, Walton was here yesterday, and told me that your seat was likely to be attacked; something, he says, is unquestionably going on at Domwell. You know there is an awkwardness in my meddling ever so cautiously. But I advise, if it is not very officious, your making Haxton look after it, and report immediately. I fear it is serious. I ought to have mentioned that, for reasons that you will see, when you have talked with him for five minutes, the Marquis﻿—with the concurrence of all our friends﻿—drops his title, for a few weeks, and is at present plain Monsieur Droqville.
> “I am this moment going to town, and can say no more.
> “Yours faithfully,
> “R﻿—﻿—.”

===Chinese===
信上接着谈到这位侯爵，说他家资巨万，与旧日世家过从甚密，在宫廷中又有正当的影响力，实为承担那些友好使命的最佳人选——这些使命是应他本国君主与我国政府之请，他慨然应承下来的。

而读到下面这几行，更叫我如坠云雾——

> 「顺便说一句，沃尔顿昨天来过，告诉我你的议席恐怕要受人攻讦；他说，多姆韦尔那头无疑正在搞些什么名堂。你知道，我哪怕再小心翼翼地过问，也有不便之处。不过我仍要冒昧奉劝一句：让哈克斯顿去料理此事，并即刻回报。我怕事情不小。我还早该说明：出于你同他谈上五分钟便会明白的原因，侯爵——经我们诸位朋友一致同意——暂时收起他的头衔几个星期，目前只是平平凡凡的德罗克维尔先生。
> 「我这就进城，言不尽意。
> 「你忠实的，
> 「R——。」

===Original===
I was utterly puzzled. I could scarcely boast of Lord ———’s acquaintance. I knew no one named Haxton, and, except my hatter, no one called Walton; and this peer wrote as if we were intimate friends! I looked at the back of the letter, and the mystery was solved. And now, to my consternation﻿—for I was plain Richard Beckett﻿—I read﻿—

> “To George Stanhope Beckett, Esq., M.P.”

===Chinese===
我全然摸不着头脑。我与R———勋爵的交情实在不敢自夸。我不认识什么哈克斯顿，除了我的帽商，也不认识什么沃尔顿；而这位贵族写起信来，倒仿佛我们是至亲密友！我看看信封背面，谜底揭开了。这一看非同小可——因为我不过是个普普通通的理查德·贝克特——只见上面写着——

> 「乔治·斯坦霍普·贝克特先生，国会议员收」

===Original===
I looked with consternation in the face of the Marquis.

“What apology can I offer to Monsieur the Mar﻿—to Monsieur Droqville? It is true my name is Beckett﻿—it is true I am known, though very slightly to Lord R﻿—﻿—; but the letter was not intended for me. My name is Richard Beckett﻿—this is to Mr. Stanhope Beckett, the member for Shillingsworth. What can I say, or do, in this unfortunate situation? I can only give you my honour as a gentleman, that, for me, the letter, which I now return, shall remain as unviolated a secret as before I opened it. I am so shocked and grieved that such a mistake should have occurred!”

I dare say my honest vexation and good faith were pretty legibly written in my countenance; for the look of gloomy embarrassment which had for a moment settled on the face of the Marquis, brightened; he smiled, kindly, and extended his hand.

===Chinese===
我惊愕地望着侯爵的脸。

「我该向侯爵先生——向德罗克维尔先生致以怎样的歉意呢？我的确姓贝克特——我也的确与R——勋爵有一面之识，虽说交情极浅；可这封信不是写给我的。我叫理查德·贝克特——这信是写给斯坦霍普·贝克特先生的，希灵斯沃思选区的议员。处在这等尴尬境地，我还能说什么、做什么呢？我只能以绅士的名誉担保：这封信，我此刻奉还，在我之后，它将同我拆看之前一样，是个不曾泄露的秘密。发生这样的差错，我真是又震惊又难过！」

我想，我那老实的懊恼与诚恳，想必清清楚楚写在了脸上；因为侯爵脸上一时笼起的阴沉尴尬之色开朗起来；他和善地微笑着，向我伸出手来。

===Original===
“I have not the least doubt that Monsieur Beckett will respect my little secret. As a mistake was destined to occur, I have reason to thank my good stars that it should have been with a gentleman of honour. Monsieur Beckett will permit me, I hope, to place his name among those of my friends?”

I thanked the Marquis very much for his kind expressions. He went on to say﻿—

“If, Monsieur, I can persuade you to visit me at Claironville, in Normandy, where I hope to see, on the 15th of August, a great many friends, whose acquaintance it might interest you to make, I shall be too happy.”

I thanked him, of course, very gratefully for his hospitality. He continued:

===Chinese===
「我毫不怀疑贝克特先生会为我保守这桩小小的秘密。差错既然命中注定要发生，我自有理由感谢我的福星：它偏偏落在一位有荣誉感的绅士身上。希望贝克特先生肯赏光，容我把他的名字列在我的朋友之列？」

我对侯爵的这番美意深表感谢。他接着说——

「先生，倘若我能说动您到诺曼底的克莱罗维尔来看我——我希望八月十五日那天在那里见到许多朋友，结识他们想必会让您觉得有趣——那我就再高兴不过了。」

对他的盛情款待，我自然感激不尽地道了谢。他继续说道：

===Original===
“I cannot, for the present, see my friends, for reasons which you may surmise, at my house in Paris. But Monsieur will be so good as to let me know the hotel he means to stay at in Paris; and he will find that although the Marquis d’Harmonville is not in town, that Monsieur Droqville will not lose sight of him.”

With many acknowledgments I gave him the information he desired.

“And in the meantime,” he continued, “if you think of any way in which Monsieur Droqville can be of use to you, our communication shall not be interrupted, and I shall so manage matters that you can easily let me know.”

===Chinese===
「眼下，出于您可能猜测到的原因，我还不能在巴黎的宅邸里会见朋友。但请先生行个好，告诉我您打算下榻巴黎的哪家旅馆；您会发现，德·哈蒙维尔侯爵虽然不在城里，德罗克维尔先生却不会同您断了音讯。」

我千恩万谢，把我要住的旅馆告诉了他。

「在此期间，」他接着说，「倘若您想出什么德罗克维尔先生能为您效劳之处，我们之间的联络不会中断，我会把事情安排妥当，让您随时都能方便地通知到我。」

===Original===
I was very much flattered. The Marquis had, as we say, taken a fancy to me. Such likings at first sight often ripen into lasting friendships. To be sure it was just possible that the Marquis might think it prudent to keep the involuntary depository of a political secret, even so vague a one, in good humour.

Very graciously the Marquis took his leave, going up the stairs of the Belle Étoile.

===Chinese===
我大大地受宠若惊。用我们的话说，这位侯爵看中了我。这种一见如故的好感，往往能酿成终身的友谊。当然，也完全有这种可能：侯爵是觉得把一个无意间成了政治秘密——哪怕是如此含糊的一桩——的保管人哄得高高兴兴，方为稳妥之计。

侯爵风度翩翩地道了别，登上美星旅馆的楼梯去了。

===Original===
I remained upon the steps, for a minute lost in speculation upon this new theme of interest. But the wonderful eyes, the thrilling voice, the exquisite figure of the beautiful lady who had taken possession of my imagination, quickly reasserted their influence. I was again gazing at the sympathetic moon, and descending the steps, I loitered along the pavements among strange objects, and houses that were antique and picturesque, in a dreamy state, thinking.

In a little while, I turned into the innyard again. There had come a lull. Instead of the noisy place it was, an hour or two before, the yard was perfectly still and empty, except for the carriages that stood here and there. Perhaps there was a servants’ table-d’hôte just then. I was rather pleased to find solitude; and undisturbed I found out my ladylove’s carriage, in the moonlight. I mused, I walked round it; I was as utterly foolish and maudlin as very young men, in my situation, usually are. The blinds were down, the doors, I suppose, locked. The brilliant moonlight revealed everything, and cast sharp, black shadows of wheel, and bar, and spring, on the pavement. I stood before the escutcheon painted on the door, which I had examined in the daylight. I wondered how often her eyes had rested on the same object. I pondered in a charming dream. A harsh, loud voice, over my shoulder, said suddenly﻿—

===Chinese===
我在台阶上又站了一分钟，沉浸在对这桩新鲜事的琢磨里。但是，那位把我想象力据为己有的美丽夫人的奇妙眼睛、动人嗓音、曼妙身姿，很快又重新占了上风。我又一次凝望起那善解人意的月亮，走下台阶，梦悠悠地沿着人行道闲逛，置身于种种新奇的景物和古雅如画的屋舍之间，一路胡思乱想。

不多时，我又转回客店院子。喧闹已经平息。一两个钟头之前这里还是个闹哄哄的所在，此刻却全然寂静空旷，只剩几辆马车零零落落停在各处。也许是仆役们正赶上吃公共餐桌的晚饭。我倒庆幸得了这份清静；无人打扰，我在月光下找到了我心上人的马车。我出神冥想，围着它转来转去；我十足地痴傻伤感，正像我这种处境里的毛头小子惯常的那副样子。车帘都放下了，车门想必也锁上了。皎洁的月光照亮一切，把车轮、车杠、弹簧那又锐又黑的影子投在石板地上。我站在车门上绘着盾徽的地方——白天我曾细细端详过它。我不知道她的眼睛曾多少次落在这同一件东西上。我正沉在一场美妙的梦境里遐想，一个刺耳的大嗓门忽然在我肩后响起——

===Original===
“A red stork﻿—good! The stork is a bird of prey; it is vigilant, greedy, and catches gudgeons. Red, too!﻿—blood red! Ha! ha! the symbol is appropriate.”

I had turned about, and beheld the palest face I ever saw. It was broad, ugly, and malignant. The figure was that of a French officer, in undress, and was six feet high. Across the nose and eyebrow there was a deep scar, which made the repulsive face grimmer.

===Chinese===
「一只红鹳鸟——好哇！鹳鸟是猛禽；它警觉、贪馋，专捉鲦鱼吃。还是红的！——血一样红！哈！哈！这徽记起得恰当。」

我转过身，看见了一张我平生所见最苍白的脸。那张脸又宽又丑，透着凶气。那身形是位法国军官，穿着便服，身高六英尺。鼻梁和眉毛之间横着一道深深的疤痕，把那张本就吓人的脸衬得愈发狰狞。

===Original===
The officer elevated his chin and his eyebrows, with a scoffing chuckle, and said﻿—“I have shot a stork, with a rifle bullet, when he thought himself safe in the clouds, for mere sport!” (He shrugged, and laughed malignantly). “See, Monsieur; when a man like me﻿—a man of energy, you understand, a man with all his wits about him, a man who has made the tour of Europe under canvas, and, *parbleu!* often without it﻿—resolves to discover a secret, expose a crime, catch a thief, spit a robber on the point of his sword, it is odd if he does not succeed. Ha! ha! ha! Adieu, Monsieur!”

He turned with an angry whisk on his heel, and swaggered with long strides out of the gate.

===Chinese===
那军官扬起下巴和眉毛，嗤笑一声，说道——「我用步枪子弹打下来过一只鹳鸟，那时它还自以为躲在云彩里万无一失呢——纯粹为了取乐！」（他耸耸肩，恶狠狠地笑。）「瞧着吧，先生；当像我这样一个人——一个有魄力的人，你懂吗，一个脑子从不闲置的人，一个靠着军帐走遍欧洲、*见鬼*（parbleu）！还常常连军帐都没有的人——下定决心要探出一个秘密、揭发一桩罪行、捉住一个窃贼、用剑尖串上一个强盗时，他要是不成，那才怪呢。哈！哈！哈！再会，先生！」

他怒冲冲地一旋脚跟转身，迈开大步，大摇大摆地出了院门。
