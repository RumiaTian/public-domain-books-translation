## III Death and Love Together Mated / III 死神与爱神结成一对

===Original===
When the day drags, when a man is solitary, and in a fever of impatience and suspense; when the minute-hand of his watch travels as slowly as the hour-hand used to do, and the hour-hand has lost all appreciable motion; when he yawns, and beats the devil’s tattoo, and flattens his handsome nose against the window, and whistles tunes he hates, and, in short, does not know what to do with himself, it is deeply to be regretted that he cannot make a solemn dinner of three courses more than once in a day. The laws of matter, to which we are slaves, deny us that resource.

But in the times I speak of, supper was still a substantial meal, and its hour was approaching. This was consolatory. Three-quarters of an hour, however, still interposed. How was I to dispose of that interval?

===Chinese===
当日子拖拖拉拉，当一个人形单影只，处在急不可耐、悬心吊胆的焦灼之中；当怀表的分针走得同从前的时针一般慢，而时针几乎失了可辨的移动；当他打哈欠，急躁地用手指敲出烦人的笃笃声，把自己俊俏的鼻子压扁在窗玻璃上，吹着他所讨厌的曲子，总之不知道拿自己怎么办才好——这时候，一个人竟不能一天吃上不止一顿正正经经的三道菜正餐，实在是莫大的憾事。我们都是物质规律的奴隶，它不许我们享有这项排遣。

但在我所说的那个年代，晚饭仍是一顿实实在在的正餐，而且饭点眼看就要到了。这是可堪告慰的。然而中间还隔着三刻钟。这段光阴教我如何打发？

===Original===
I had two or three idle books, it is true, as travelling-companions; but there are many moods in which one cannot read. My novel lay with my rug and walking-stick on the sofa, and I did not care if the heroine and the hero were both drowned together in the water-barrel that I saw in the innyard under my window.

===Chinese===
不错，我身边带着两三本消遣的书权充旅伴；可是人的许多心境是读不进书的。我的小说同毯子和手杖一起躺在沙发上；那男女主人公就是双双淹死在我窗外客店院子里那只水桶中，我也毫不在意。

===Original===
I took a turn or two up and down my room, and sighed, looking at myself in the glass, adjusted my great white “choker,” folded and tied after Brummel, the immortal “Beau,” put on a buff waistcoat and my blue swallow-tailed coat with gilt buttons; I deluged my pocket handkerchief with eau de cologne (we had not then the variety of bouquets with which the genius of perfumery has since blessed us); I arranged my hair, on which I piqued myself, and which I loved to groom in those days. That dark-brown chevelure, with a natural curl, is now represented by a few dozen perfectly white hairs, and its place﻿—a smooth, bald, pink head﻿—knows it no more. But let us forget these mortifications. It was then rich, thick, and dark-brown. I was making a very careful toilet. I took my unexceptionable hat from its case, and placed it lightly on my wise head, as nearly as memory and practice enabled me to do so, at that very slight inclination which the immortal person I have mentioned was wont to give to his. A pair of light French gloves and a rather club-like knotted walking-stick, such as just then came into vogue, for a year or two again in England, in the phraseology of Sir Walter Scott’s romances, “completed my equipment.”

===Chinese===
我在屋里来回踱了一两趟，叹了口气，对着镜子端详自己，整了整我那条雪白的大领巾（choker）——照着不朽的「花花公子」布鲁梅尔的样式折好系上的——穿上米黄色背心和那件缀着镀金纽扣的蓝燕尾服；我把手帕用古龙水浇了个透（那时还没有后来香水业的天才们赐予我们的那许多花露香型）；然后梳理头发——这是我颇为自负、当年也最爱打理的地方。那头深棕色的头发，带着天然鬈曲，如今只剩几十根全白的毛发充当代表了，而它的故地——一颗光溜溜、粉红色的秃头——已不再认得它。不过，这些丢人的事就不提了吧。那时它可是又浓、又厚、又是深棕色。我正在进行一场一丝不苟的梳妆。我从帽盒里取出我那无可挑剔的帽子，轻轻戴到我那颗聪明的头颅上，凭记忆和练习之所及，尽量斜出那么一点点——正是方才提到的那位不朽人物惯于给自己的帽子的那种斜度。一副轻薄的法国手套，一根颇为粗重多节的手杖——那种手杖当时正开始时兴，后来在英国又风行了一两年——用沃尔特·司各特爵士传奇小说里的话说，「装束至此大功告成」。

===Original===
All this attention to effect, preparatory to a mere lounge in the yard, or on the steps of the Belle Étoile, was a simple act of devotion to the wonderful eyes which I had that evening beheld for the first time, and never, never could forget! In plain terms, it was all done in the vague, very vague hope that those eyes might behold the unexceptionable getup of a melancholy slave, and retain the image, not altogether without secret approbation.

As I completed my preparations the light failed me; the last level streak of sunlight disappeared, and a fading twilight only remained. I sighed in unison with the pensive hour, and threw open the window, intending to look out for a moment before going downstairs. I perceived instantly that the window underneath mine was also open, for I heard two voices in conversation, although I could not distinguish what they were saying.

===Chinese===
这一切讲究仪表的功夫，不过是为着到院子里、或美星旅馆门前的台阶上随便站一站，实则是献给那双奇妙眼睛的一份虔敬之礼——那天晚上我才初次见到它们，就再也不能、永远不能忘怀！说白了，这一切都是抱着一种渺茫的、极其渺茫的希望：但愿那双眼睛能看见一个忧郁的奴仆这身无可挑剔的行头，并把这形象留在心里，而且并非全然没有暗暗的赞许。

我打扮停当时，天光也暗了；最后一抹平射的夕阳消失不见，只剩渐渐消褪的暮色。我应和着这沉思时分的气氛叹了口气，推开窗子，打算下楼前望一小会儿。我立刻察觉，我楼下的那扇窗也开着，因为我听见两个声音在交谈，虽然听不清说的是什么。

===Original===
The male voice was peculiar; it was, as I told you, reedy and nasal. I knew it, of course, instantly. The answering voice spoke in those sweet tones which I recognised only too easily. The dialogue was only for a minute; the repulsive male voice laughed, I fancied, with a kind of devilish satire, and retired from the window, so that I almost ceased to hear it.

The other voice remained nearer the window, but not so near as at first.

===Chinese===
那个男声很特别；如我告诉过你的，又尖又带鼻音。我当然一下子就听出来了。应答的那个声音用的是那种甜美的音调，我也实在太容易辨认。对话只有一分钟；那讨人嫌的男声笑了——我觉得那笑里带着一种魔鬼似的讥诮——随即从窗边退开，我几乎听不见它了。

另一个声音还留在窗边，只是不像起先那么近了。

===Original===
It was not an altercation; there was evidently nothing the least exciting in the colloquy. What would I not have given that it had been a quarrel﻿—a violent one﻿—and I the redresser of wrongs, and the defender of insulted beauty! Alas! so far as I could pronounce upon the character of the tones I heard, they might be as tranquil a pair as any in existence. In a moment more the lady began to sing an odd little chanson. I need not remind you how much farther the voice is heard *singing* than speaking. I could distinguish the words. The voice was of that exquisitely sweet kind which is called, I believe, a semi-contralto; it had something pathetic, and something, I fancied, a little mocking in its tones. I venture a clumsy, but adequate translation of the words:﻿

===Chinese===
那不是争吵；这场对谈显然毫无令人兴奋之处。我什么代价不肯付出，只求那是一场口角——一场激烈的口角——而我是冤屈的昭雪者、受辱美人的保护人！唉！单凭我听到的音调判断，他们俩兴许是天下最安谧的一对。过了一会儿，那位夫人唱起一支古怪的小歌（chanson）。用不着我提醒你，*唱*出来的声音比说话传得远多少。词句我听得分明。那嗓音属于那种绝顶甜美的类型，我想是叫作次女低音；音调里有些哀婉，我觉得还有些微嘲讽的意味。我斗胆把歌词试译如下，译文虽笨拙，尚能达意——

===Original===
> “Death and Love, together mated,

 Watch and wait in ambuscade;

 At early morn, or else belated.

 They meet and mark the man or maid.
> “Burning sigh, or breath that freezes,

 Numbs or maddens man or maid;

 Death or Love the victim seizes,

 Breathing from their ambuscade.”

===Chinese===
> 「死神与爱神，配作一双，

 埋伏着守候，暗中张望；

 或在清晨，或已夜深，

 一朝相逢，便盯住儿郎或姑娘。
> 「灼热的叹息，凝冻的气息，

 教儿郎或姑娘麻木癫狂；

 不是死神、便是爱神攫住猎物，

 从那埋伏中吐出这一息。」

===Original===
“Enough, Madame!” said the old voice, with sudden severity. “We do not desire, I believe, to amuse the grooms and hostlers in the yard with our music.”

The lady’s voice laughed gaily.

“You desire to quarrel, Madame!” And the old man, I presume, shut down the window. Down it went, at all events, with a rattle that might easily have broken the glass.

===Chinese===
「够了，夫人！」那苍老的声音突然严厉起来，「我想，我们并不打算用我们的音乐，给院子里的马夫马倌们解闷。」

那位夫人的嗓音快活地笑了。

「您是想吵架，夫人！」于是那老头——我猜是他——把窗子放下了。无论如何，窗子哗啦一声落了下去，那动静险些震碎了玻璃。

===Original===
Of all thin partitions, glass is the most effectual excluder of sound. I heard no more, not even the subdued hum of the colloquy.

What a charming voice this Countess had! How it melted, swelled, and trembled! How it moved, and even agitated me! What a pity that a hoarse old jackdaw should have power to crow down such a Philomel! “Alas! what a life it is!” I moralized, wisely. “That beautiful Countess, with the patience of an angel and the beauty of a Venus and the accomplishments of all the Muses, a slave! She knows perfectly who occupies the apartments over hers; she heard me raise my window. One may conjecture pretty well for whom that music was intended﻿—ay, old gentleman, and for whom you suspected it to be intended.”

===Chinese===
在一切单薄的隔断之中，玻璃隔绝声音的本事最大。我什么也听不见了，连那压低了的谈话嗡嗡声也听不见了。

这位伯爵夫人有一副何等迷人的嗓音！它怎样地柔转、高涨、颤动！它怎样地打动我，甚至搅得我心神不宁！可惜啊，一只嘶哑的老寒鸦，居然有本事把这样一只菲洛墨拉（Philomel，夜莺）压下去！「唉！这是什么日子哟！」我满腹智慧地感慨道，「那位美丽的伯爵夫人，有天使的忍耐、维纳斯的美貌、九位缪斯的全副才艺，竟是个奴隶！她清清楚楚知道住在她楼上的是谁；她听见我推开窗子了。那音乐是唱给谁听的，不难猜个八九不离十——可不是嘛，老先生，还有您疑心它是唱给谁听的，也同样不难猜。」

===Original===
In a very agreeable flutter I left my room, and descending the stairs, passed the Count’s door very much at my leisure. There was just a chance that the beautiful songstress might emerge. I dropped my stick on the lobby, near their door, and you may be sure it took me some little time to pick it up! Fortune, nevertheless, did not favour me. I could not stay on the lobby all night picking up my stick, so I went down to the hall.

I consulted the clock, and found that there remained but a quarter of an hour to the moment of supper.

Everyone was roughing it now, every inn in confusion; people might do at such a juncture what they never did before. Was it just possible that, for once, the Count and Countess would take their chairs at the table-d’hôte?

===Chinese===
我心头荡漾着一种极为受用的悸动，出了房门，下得楼来，从伯爵的门前慢慢悠悠地走过。那位美丽的歌姬未始没有露一露面的可能。我把手杖掉在门厅里他们门边，你尽可相信，我捡起来着实花了些工夫！无奈命运不曾眷顾我。我总不能整夜守在门厅里捡手杖，于是便下楼到大厅去了。

我看了看钟，离晚饭开席只剩一刻钟了。

如今人人都在将就度日，家家客栈乱作一团；在这种时节，人们会做出从前从不曾做过的事来。有没有那么一线可能，伯爵和伯爵夫人这一回竟肯屈尊坐上公共餐桌（table d’hôte）的席面呢？
