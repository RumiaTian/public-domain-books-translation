## Endnotes / 尾注

===Original===
1. “*Audire est operae pretium … [laborent].”* It is worth your while, ye that do not wish well to adulterers, to hear how they are hampered on all sides. —Lib. i. *Satires* i. 2, 37 ↩︎

===Chinese===
1. “*Audire est operae pretium … [laborent].*”（值得尔等一听，尔等这班不愿善待奸夫的人——听他们如何四面受阻。）——卷一《讽刺诗集》第 2 首，第 37 行 ↩︎

===Original===
2. “*[Haec] metuat … deprensa.*” The woman fears for her dowry, if she should be caught. —Lib. i. *Satires* i. 2, 131 ↩︎

===Chinese===
2. “*[Haec] metuat … deprensa.*”（那女子若被拿住，便要为她的嫁妆担惊。）——卷一《讽刺诗集》第 2 首，第 131 行 ↩︎

===Original===
3. “In the vain joys … sight.” A reference is here intended to the various shows which were common in London at this time. ↩︎

===Chinese===
3. “In the vain joys … sight.”此处意在指涉当时伦敦盛行的种种游艺演出。 ↩︎

===Original===
4. “Though senseless … quaff.” That is, the well-dressed barbarians know Congreve’s name and power, such is his compelling art, although they are insensible to mirth except when they laugh and feel wise only when they have drunk to a surfeit. ↩︎

===Chinese===
4. “Though senseless … quaff.”意谓：那些衣冠楚楚的蛮子也知晓康格里夫的名号与威力——其艺术慑人竟至于此——尽管他们对谐趣无所感，唯笑出声时方觉有乐，也唯痛饮至醉方自觉聪明。 ↩︎

===Original===
5. “Arabella.” A generic name for the ladies who inspire the lyrical name of Congreve. ↩︎

===Chinese===
5. “Arabella.”（阿拉贝拉）泛指那些激发康格里夫抒情诗情的女士们。 ↩︎

===Original===
6. “William glorious in the strife.” The allusion is to Congreve’s “To the King, on the Taking of Namour.” ↩︎

===Chinese===
6. “William glorious in the strife.”此语暗指康格里夫的《呈国王：攻克那慕尔》。 ↩︎

===Original===
7. “In her own nest … changeling-kind.” An allusion to the fact that the cuckoo lays her eggs in the nest of another bird. ↩︎

===Chinese===
7. “In her own nest … changeling-kind.”暗指杜鹃把蛋下在别的鸟巢里这一事实。 ↩︎

===Original===
8. “The squire … undone.” “Buttered still,” that is, always heaped with loathsome flattery. ↩︎

===Chinese===
8. “The squire … undone.”「Buttered still（黄油涂个不停）」，意即：备受令人作呕的谀词浇灌。 ↩︎

===Original===
9. “declares for a friend and ratafia.” Ratafia was a liqueur flavoured with fruits. The term “friend” as here used indicates a man friend with whom one’s relations were not entirely unquestionable. ↩︎

===Chinese===
9. “declares for a friend and ratafia.”拉塔菲亚是一种以果子调香的甜酒。「朋友」一词在此处的用法，指一位与之关系未必全然清白的男友。 ↩︎

===Original===
10. “continued in the state of nature.” Gone on in a natural course. ↩︎

===Chinese===
10. “continued in the state of nature.”照着自然的进程继续下去。 ↩︎

===Original===
37. “Quarles and Prynne.” Francis Quarles was a writer of sacred poems, author of *Divine Emblems*, one of the most popular works of the age. William Prynne was a lawyer and pamphleteer, author of *Hisiriomastix*, a savage attack upon the stage in the time of Charles I. ↩︎

===Chinese===
37. “Quarles and Prynne.”弗朗西斯·夸尔斯（Francis Quarles）是圣诗作家，著有《神圣的象征》，为该时代最风行的作品之一。威廉·普林（William Prynne）是律师兼小册子作家，著有《戏子之鞭》（*Histriomastix*），是对查理一世时代舞台的一场猛烈抨击。 ↩︎

===Original===
38. “The Short View of the Stage.” The full title is *A Short View of the Profaneness and Immorality of the English Stage*. This righteous attack on the abuses of the stage by Jeremy Collier caused a flutter among the playwrights and in time brought about a modicum of reform. Congreve was especially censured. ↩︎

===Chinese===
38. “The Short View of the Stage.”全题为《英国舞台之亵渎与伤风败俗一瞥》。杰里米·科利尔（Jeremy Collier）对舞台种种弊端的这场义愤讨伐，在剧作家中激起一阵慌乱，并终究促成了些许革新。康格里夫尤受弹劾。 ↩︎

===Original===
39. “Robin from Locket’s.” One of the drawers or waiters at Locket’s ordinary in Charing Cross. ↩︎

===Chinese===
39. “Robin from Locket’s.”查令十字的洛克特馆（Locket’s）是一家餐馆，罗宾是那里的跑堂之一。 ↩︎

===Original===
40. “like a Long-lane penthouse.” Long-lane from West Smithfield to Barbican was occupied by the sellers of old clothes. A penthouse was here a species of continuous shed or arcade, covering the walk. ↩︎

===Chinese===
40. “like a Long-lane penthouse.”朗街——从西史密斯菲尔德直抵芭比肯——两侧尽是旧衣商贩。此处的 penthouse 指一种连绵不断的棚檐或骑楼，罩着人行道。 ↩︎

===Original===
41. “the million lottery.” A lottery the prizes of which amounted to a million pounds in the advertisements. ↩︎

===Chinese===
41. “the million lottery.”一种彩票，其各项奖金照广告所称总计达一百万镑。 ↩︎

===Original===

===Chinese===
42. “the whole court upon a birthday.”因为按习俗，寿辰之日礼数不可少。 ↩︎

===Original===
43. “Ludgate … Blackfriars … old mitten.” Ludgate was one of the better debtors’ prisons. It abutted on the precinct of Blackfriars. To angle with a mitten refers to the custom of imprisoned debtors who begged alms of passersby through a grating. Here doubtless a string was let down from an upper window with a mitten in which the benevolent passerby might put his farthing, subsequently to be drawn up. ↩︎

===Chinese===

===Original===
44. “has a month’s mind.” To have an inclination to a thing. ↩︎

===Chinese===

===Original===

===Chinese===
45. “passe-partout.”万能钥匙。 ↩︎

===Original===
46. “any chemist upon the day of projection.” The culmination of an experiment in alchemy, when the metals were supposed to be transmuted into gold was called a projection. ↩︎

===Chinese===

===Original===
