## VI / 六

He Chats Over a Hedge / 他隔着树篱攀谈

### I / 一

===Original===
Left to myself, I began to retrace my steps. Solitude had mitigated my craving for tobacco in a surprising manner; indeed, a casual observer might have thought it completely forgotten, for I walked with curious leisure. When I had come again to the box-hedge my pace had degenerated, a little by a little, into an aimless lounge. Mr. Robert Etheridge Townsend was rapt with admiration of the perfect beauty of the night.

Followed a strange chance. There was only the mildest breeze about; it was barely audible among the leaves above; and yet—so unreliable are the breezes of still summer nights—with a sudden, tiny and almost imperceptible outburst, did this treacherous breeze lift Mr. Townsend’s brand-new straw hat from his head, and waft it over the hedge of trim box-bushes. This was unfortunate, for, as has been said, the hedge was a tall and sturdy hedge. So I peeped over it, with disconsolate countenance.

===Chinese===
剩下我独自一个，我便循着原路往回走。独处竟以惊人的效力缓释了我的烟瘾；说实在的，随便哪个漫不经心的路人都会当我把它忘了个一干二净，因为我走得闲散得出奇。待我又走回那道黄杨树篱跟前，我的步子已经一点一点地堕落成一种漫无目的的闲荡。罗伯特·埃瑟里奇·汤森（Robert Etheridge Townsend）先生正心醉神迷，叹赏着这夜色十全十美的光华。

随后便发生了一桩离奇的巧事。四下里本只有一缕最轻最柔的微风，在头顶的枝叶间勉强可闻；然而——夏日静夜的风偏偏就是这样靠不住——这阵促狭的风忽然小小地、几乎无从察觉地发作起来，竟把汤森先生那顶崭新的草帽从头上掀起，一路飘送过了那道修剪齐整的黄杨树篱。这可大为不妙，因为——如前所述——那是一道又高又结实的树篱。于是我满脸怏怏，从篱顶上探头张望。

### II / 二

===Original===
“Beastly awkward,” said I, as meditatively; “I’d give a great deal to know how I’m going to get my hat back without breaking through the blessed hedge, and rousing the house, and being taken for a burglar, may be—”

“It is terrible,” assented a quite tranquil voice; “but if gentlemen *will* venture abroad on such terrible nights—”

“Eh?” said I. I looked up quickly at the moon; then back toward the possessor of the voice. It was peculiar I had not noticed her before, for she sat on a rustic bench not more than forty feet away, and in full view of the street. It was, perhaps, the strangeness of the affair that was accountable for the great wonder in my soul; and the little tremor which woke in my speech.

“—so windy,” she complained.

===Chinese===
「真是别扭透顶，」我作沉思状地说；「我情愿破费一大笔，只消知道我究竟怎样才能把帽子取回来——既不必撞破这该死的树篱，也不必惊动这一家子，更不至于被人当成个夜盗，没准儿真会当成夜盗——」

「是可怕呀，」一个十分安闲的声音附和道；「只是绅士们偏要挑这种可怕的夜晚出门游荡——」

「呃？」我说。我急忙抬头望了望月亮；又回过头去望那嗓音的主人。说来奇怪，我先前竟不曾留意到她，因为她分明坐在一张粗木长椅上，离我至多四十英尺，而且正对着街面，一望即知。也许，正是这桩事情本身的离奇，才害得我灵魂里生出那般大大的惊异，也害得我的话音里醒来一丝轻轻的颤动。

「——这风刮得也实在厉害，」她抱怨道。

===Original===
“Er—ah—yes, quite so!” I agreed, hastily.

“I am really afraid that it must be a tornado. Ah,” she continued, emotion catching at her voice, “heaven help all poor souls at sea! How the wind must whistle through the cordage! how the marlinspikes must quiver, and the good ship reel on such a night!” She looked up at a cloudless sky, and sighed.

“Er h’m!” I observed.

For she had come forward and had held out my hat toward me, and I could see her very plainly now; and my mouth was making foolish sounds, and my heart was performing certain curious and varied gymnastics which could not, by any stretch of the imagination, be included among its proper duties, and which interfered with my breathing.

===Chinese===
「呃——啊——对，正是正是！」我慌忙附和。

「我真怕这准是一场龙卷风。啊，」她接着说，激动得嗓音发哽，「上天保佑海上一切可怜的魂灵吧！这样的夜里，风准在船索间呜呜地尖啸！索针怕不都要簌簌打颤，好端端的船也要踉跄打晃了！」她抬头望着一碧无云的夜空，叹了口气。

「呃哼！」我评了一句。

原来她已走上前来，正把我的帽子朝我递来，此刻我能把她看得清清楚楚了；我的嘴里正发出种种傻气之声，而我的心呢，正在表演一套古怪而花样繁多的体操——这套动作任凭怎样穿凿附会，也归不进它的正当职责里去——而且还妨碍起我的呼吸来。

### III / 三

===Original===
“Didn’t I know it—didn’t I know it?” I demanded of my soul, and my pulses sang a paean; “I knew, with that voice, she couldn’t be a common actress—a vulgar, raddled creature out of a barn! You not a gentlewoman! Nonsense! Why—why, you’re positively incredible! Oh, you great, wonderful, lazy woman, you are probably very stupid, and you certainly can’t act, but your eyes are black velvet, and your voice is evidently stolen from a Cremona, and as for your hair, there must be pounds of it, and, altogether, you ought to be set up on a pedestal for men to worship! There is just one other woman in the whole wide world as beautiful as you are; and she is two thousand years old, and is securely locked up in the Louvre, and belongs to the French Government, and, besides, she hasn’t any arms, so that even there you have the advantage!”

Indeed, Miss Annabelle Alys Montmorenci was of much the same large, placid type as the Venus of Milo, nor were the upper portions of the two faces dissimilar. Miss Montmorenci’s lips, however, were far more curved, more buxom, and were, at the present moment, bordered by an absolutely bewildering assemblage of dimples which the statue may not boast.

===Chinese===
「我岂不是早就知道么——岂不是早就知道么？」我质问着自己的灵魂，周身的脉搏齐声唱起一曲凯歌；「我早知道，有那样的嗓音，她绝不可能是寻常的女戏子——绝不是谷仓里滚出来的那种粗俗的、脂残粉褪的货色！你不是大家闺秀？胡说！哎呀——哎呀，你简直叫人难以置信！哦，你这高大、奇妙、慵懒的女人，你多半蠢得很，你也笃定不会演戏，可你的眼睛是黑丝绒，你的嗓音分明是从一把克雷莫纳（Cremona）名琴上偷来的，至于你的头发，怕不有论磅称的分量——总而言之，你早该被供上基座，好教男人们顶礼膜拜！普天之下，统共只有另外一个女人同你一样美；而她已经是两千岁的人了，被牢牢锁在卢浮宫（Louvre）里，归法国政府所有；再说，她连两条胳膊都没有——这样看来，即便在这一点上，你也占着上风！」

说真的，安娜贝尔·艾莉丝·蒙特莫伦西小姐（Annabelle Alys Montmorenci），正是同米洛的维纳斯（Venus of Milo）一模一样的那种丰腴安详的类型，两张脸的上半部也无甚分别。只是蒙特莫伦西小姐的嘴唇，曲线远为弯浓，远为丰润，而且此刻唇边正环拥着一簇足以令人神魂颠倒的笑靥——这却是那座雕像所不能夸口的。

### IV / 四

===Original===
“I really think,” said Miss Montmorenci, judicially, “that it would be best for you to seek some shelter from this devastating wind. It really is not safe, you know, in the open. You might be swept away, just as your hat was.”

“The shelter of a tree—” I began, looking doubtfully into the garden, which had any number of trees.

“The very thing,” she assented. “There is a splendid oak yonder, just half a block up the street.” And she graciously pointed it out.

I regarded it with disapproval. “Such a rickety old tree,” I objected, sulkily.

===Chinese===
「我当真觉得，」蒙特莫伦西小姐俨然裁事一般地说，「你顶好还是找个地方躲避躲避这场摧枯拉朽的大风。要知道，露天里实在不安全。弄不好你会被卷走的，就跟你的帽子一个样。」

「找棵树遮一遮——」我开口道，一面迟疑地朝园子里张望，那园中多的是树。

「正是呢，」她赞同道。「那边就有一棵极好的橡树，沿街往上不过半条街的路程。」她还落落大方地指给我看。

我满不以为然地打量着它。「这么一棵摇摇欲坠的老树，」我赌气似的提出异议。

===Original===
Followed a silence. She bent her head to one side, and looked up at me. She was now grave with a difference. “A strolling actress isn’t supposed to be very particular, is she?” asked Miss Montmorenci. “She wouldn’t object to a man’s coming by night and trying to scrape acquaintance with her—a man who wouldn’t think of being seen with her by day? She would like it, probably. She—she’d probably be accustomed to it, wouldn’t she?” And Miss Montmorenci smiled.

And I, on a sudden, was abjectly ashamed of myself. “Why, you can’t think that of me!” I babbled. “I—oh, don’t think me that sort, I beg of you! I’m not—really, I’m not, Miss Montmorenci! But I admired you so much tonight—I—oh, of course, I was very silly and very presumptuous, but, really, you know—”

I paused for a little. This was miles apart from the glib talk I had designed.

“My name is Robert Townsend,” I then continued; “I am staying at Mr. Charteris’s place, just outside of Fairhaven. And I am delighted to meet you, Miss Montmorenci. So now, you see, we have been quite properly introduced, haven’t we? And, by the way,” I suggested, after a moment’s meditation, “there is a very interesting old college here—old pictures, records, historical association and suchlike. I would like to inspect it, vastly. Can’t I call for you in the morning. We can do it together, if you don’t mind, and if you haven’t already seen it. Won’t you, Miss Montmorenci? You really ought to see King’s College, you know; it is quite famous, because I was educated there, and no end of other interesting things have happened within its venerable confines.”

===Chinese===
接着是一阵沉默。她把头偏向一侧，抬眼望着我。此刻她的庄重已是另一种庄重。「照说，一个流动剧团的女演员，总不至于太挑剔吧，是不是？」蒙特莫伦西小姐问道。「一个男人夜里跑来，想方设法同她攀谈搭讪——一个白天决计不肯让人瞧见他同她走在一起的男人——她总不至于见怪吧？她多半还乐意呢。她——她多半早就习以为常了，对不对？」说罢，蒙特莫伦西小姐莞尔一笑。

而我，霎时间羞愧得无地自容。「哎呀，你怎么能这么看我！」我语无伦次地说。「我——哦，千万别把我当成那种人，求求你了！我不是——真的，我不是，蒙特莫伦西小姐！只是今晚我实在太过倾慕你了——我——哦，当然，我又蠢又放肆透了，可是，真的，你要知道——」

我稍稍顿了顿。这同我预先打好腹稿的那套伶牙俐齿，相差不啻十万八千里。

「我的名字叫罗伯特·汤森，」接着我继续说道；「我眼下正住在查特里斯先生府上，就在费尔黑文城外。蒙特莫伦西小姐，能结识你，我真是高兴极了。这么着，你瞧，咱们这也算得上经过体体面面的正式引见了，不是吗？对了，」我沉吟片刻，又提议道，「这儿有一所非常有意思的老学堂——古画啦，档案啦，历史掌故啦，诸如此类。我巴不得去瞻仰瞻仰呢，想得不得了。明儿上午我去接你，咱们一道去逛逛，行不行？只要你不嫌弃，又还没去看过的话。去不去嘛，蒙特莫伦西小姐？说真的，你理该去看看国王学院；它是大大有名呢——一来我本人就是在那儿受的教育，二来在那座古意盎然的院墙之内，还发生过数不清的趣事。」

===Original===
She had drawn close to the hedge. “You really mean it?” she asked. “You would walk through the streets of this Fairhaven with me—with a barnstormer, with a strolling actress? You’d be afraid!” she cried, suddenly; “oh, yes, you talk bravely enough, but you’d be afraid, of course, when the time came! You’d be afraid!”

I had taken the hat, but my head was still uncovered. “I don’t think,” said I, reflectively, “that I am afraid of many things, somehow. But of one thing I am certainly not afraid, and that is of mistaking a good woman for—for anything else. Their eyes are different somehow,” I haltingly explained, as to myself; then I smiled. “Shall we say eleven o’clock?”

Miss Montmorenci laid one hand upon the hedgetop and slowly twisted off four box-leaves what while I waited. “I—I believe you,” she said, in meditation; “oh, yes, I believe you, somehow, Mr. Townsend. But we rehearse in the morning, and there is a matinee every day, you know, and—and there are other reasons—” She paused, irresolutely. “No,” said Miss Montmorenci, “I thank you, but—good night.”

“Oh, I say! am I never to see any more of you?”

===Chinese===
她已挨近了树篱。「你是当真的？」她问道。「你肯陪我走过这费尔黑文的大街小巷——陪一个跑码头的戏子，陪一个流动剧团的女演员？你会害怕的！」她忽然叫道；「哦，是啊，你话说得够英勇，可真到了那个时辰，你自然会害怕的！你会害怕的！」

帽子我已经接了过来，头上却还空着。「我想，」我若有所思地说，「不知怎么，我害怕的东西并不多。可有一样我笃定不怕：那就是把一个好女人错认成——错认成别的任何什么。她们的眼睛总有些不一样，」我吞吞吐吐地解释着，像是自言自语；随后我笑了。「咱们就定在十一点钟，如何？」

蒙特莫伦西小姐把一只手搭上篱顶，在我等候的当口，慢条斯理地拧下了四片黄杨树叶。「我——我相信你，」她沉吟着说；「哦，是的，不知怎么，我信你，汤森先生。只是我们上午要排戏，每天午后还有一场日场，你是知道的，再说——再说还有别的缘故——」她踌躇不决地顿住了。「不，」蒙特莫伦西小姐说，「多谢你了，可是——晚安。」

「哦，喂！这么说，我就再也见不着你了？」

===Original===
A century or so of silence now. Her deliberation seemed endless.

At last: “Matinees and rehearsal keep us busy by day. But I am boarding here for the week, and—and I rest here in the garden after the evening performance. It is cool, it—it is like a glass of water after taking rather bitter medicine. And you aren’t a bad sort, are you? No; you look too big and strong and clean, Mr. Townsend. And, besides, you’re just a boy—”

“In that case,” cried Mr. Townsend, “I shall say goodnight with a light heart.” And I turned to go.

“A moment—” said she.

“An eternity,” I proffered.

===Chinese===
这回是约莫一个世纪的沉默。她的斟酌仿佛没有穷尽。

她终于开口：「日场和排戏把白天都占满了。可我这个礼拜就在这儿搭伙寄宿，而且——而且每晚散了戏，我总要到这园子里歇一歇。这儿凉爽，这——这就像服下一剂相当苦的药之后，再喝上一杯清水。你也不算个坏家伙，是不是？不坏；你瞧着太高大、太结实、太干净了，汤森先生。再说，你不过是个大孩子——」

「既然如此，」汤森先生喊道，「我便可以怀着轻松的心情道晚安了。」说罢我便转身要走。

「且慢——」她说。

「等上一个永世也行，」我慨然应道。

===Original===
“Promise me,” she said, “that you will not come again this week to the Opera House.”

My brows were raised a trifle. “I adore the drama,” I pleaded.

“And I loathe it. And I act very badly—hopelessly so,” said Miss Montmorenci, with an indolent shrug; “and, somehow, I don’t want you to see me do it. Why did you mind my calling you a boy? You *are*, you know.”

So I protested I had not minded it at all; and I promised. “But at least,” I said, triumphantly, “you can’t prevent my remembering Juliet!”

===Chinese===
「答应我，」她说，「这个礼拜，你可别再到歌剧院（Opera House）来了。」

我的眉梢微微扬起。「我酷爱戏剧，」我央告道。

「可我厌恶它。而且我演得糟透了——糟得无可救药，」蒙特莫伦西小姐懒懒地耸了耸肩；「而且，不知怎么，我不想让你看见我演成那样。我管你叫大孩子，你何必介意呢？你本来就是个孩子，你知道的。」

于是我分辩说自己毫不介意；并且应承了下来。「不过至少，」我得意扬扬地说，「你总拦不住我惦念朱丽叶（Juliet）吧！」

===Original===
She said of course not, only I was not to be silly.

“And therefore,” quoth I, “Juliet shall be remembered always.” I smiled and waved my hand. “Au revoir, Signorina Capulet,” said I.

And I took my departure. My blood rejoiced, with a strange fervor, in the summer moonlight. It was good to be alive.

===Chinese===
她说自然拦不住，只是不许我犯傻。

「因此之故，」我道，「朱丽叶必将被我永志不忘。」我微笑着挥了挥手。「再会了，卡普莱特小姐（Signorina Capulet），」我说。

于是我便动身离去。我的血液在这夏夜的月色里，怀着一种奇异的狂热欢欣鼓舞。活着，真好。
