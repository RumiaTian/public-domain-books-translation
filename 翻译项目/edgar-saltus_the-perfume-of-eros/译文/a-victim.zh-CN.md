## X A Victim / X 受害者

===Original===
Sylvia had gone from Newport. She was then at Lenox. It was there the previous autumn that her interest in Annandale had begun. The interest had so deepened that she gave him her heart. Never before had she given that to anyone. Annandale had taken it and then, one night, he had so bruised it that she thought it broken. He had written that he had not meant to. His letter had been full of regrets, of protestations, of bad grammar. Such things may palliate, but they do not cure. Only time can do that.

Time is a strange emollient. In its mysterious potency it softens without our knowledge. Suddenly a whisper, a breeze that passes, shows that it has done its work. With Sylvia time was having its will. Furtively she had found herself wondering, as Annandale had wondered, how it should fare with her, and how with him, could the past be effaced and the old days renewed. But those days were gone, she decided. Though into that decision a doubt would creep, not indeed concerning the departure, but concerning her attitude and the justice of it.

===Chinese===
西尔维娅已离开了纽波特。她那时正在莱诺克斯（Lenox）。头年秋天，正是在那里，她对安南代尔起了意。这份情意愈陷愈深，她终于把自己的心交给了他。从前她从未把心交给过任何人。安南代尔把它接了过去，随后，某夜，他竟把它磋磨得伤痕累累，她以为它已碎了。他写信说，他并非有意如此。那封信满是懊悔、满是辩白、满是语病。这些东西可以减轻痛楚，却不能治愈。唯有时间能治愈。

时间是一种奇异的抚慰剂。它那神秘的药力在我们不知不觉中施展软化之功。忽然，一声低语、一阵掠过的微风，便显露出它已奏效。在西尔维娅这里，时间正在自行其是。她暗自发现，自己竟像安南代尔所曾想的那样，琢磨起倘若往昔可以抹去、旧日可以重来，她自己结局将如何、他结局又将如何。可那段日子毕竟过去了，她这样断定。只是这个断定里会悄悄爬进一丝疑虑——倒不是对那份离去本身，而是对她自己的态度、以及这态度是否公道。

===Original===
Annandale had sinned. He had sinned wantonly, grievously. From an atmosphere of vice—an atmosphere from which, under pain of her displeasure, she had distinctly warned him—he had staggered to her, its pollution about him, reeking with drink, talking abundantly about nothing imaginable, and at her just remonstrance had become instantly irritable, refusing almost to leave the house.

So had his condition and the spectacle of it shocked her that, for awhile, memory of him and of it was repellent. In her own eyes she felt degraded. That men drank, she knew. But in her sphere of life they drank either moderately or else in haunts invisible to her. And it was precisely from such a haunt he had come, a shameless haunt, one that sullied her even to know of.

===Chinese===
安南代尔是犯了错的。他犯得放肆、犯得严重。从一处风月场的氛围里——一种她曾明白警告过他、若再涉足必惹她不悦的氛围里——他踉跄着来到她跟前，一身那地方的污浊，酒气冲天，滔滔不绝地说着些难以想象的胡话，待她正当地责备了几句，便立刻恼怒起来，几乎不肯从屋里离开。

他的那副模样、那副光景，把她惊得——有一阵子，关于他、关于那一幕的记忆都令她作呕。她觉得自己受了玷辱。男人喝酒，这她知道。可是在她那个圈子里，他们要么喝得有节制，要么就在她看不见的地方喝。而他正是从那样一个去处出来的，一个无耻的所在，单是晓得它的存在，便已脏了她。

===Original===
Yes, he had sinned, wantonly, grievously, almost unforgivably. Almost, she reflected, but perhaps not quite. In his letter of protest and regret he had told her that he remembered nothing, nothing whatever, absolutely nothing at all, save one vague, brief vision of herself. The rest, the beginning, the end, the inter-spaces were, he assured her, blank. At first she had thought that sheer nonsense. But, later, the earnest way in which it was put impressed her. Then on the heels of that communication there had followed one from Orr, endorsing what Annandale said, declaring that it was all quite possible, adding that, in certain temperaments, memory when influenced by toxics will play tricks stranger than the average mind can comfortably credit.

These letters she had not answered. Logically she could not admit the validity of the statements which they contained. But the heart has logic which logic does not know. Then, too, is there not that within us that prompts us to believe less what we should than what we wish? Sylvia’s reason, guided by her inexperience, refused at first to accept the idea that any sane man could act as Annandale had and afterward be oblivious of it. That remorse there should be was only natural, but that there should be no memory of anything whatever seemed to her absurd.

===Chinese===
是的，他犯了错，放肆地、严重地、几乎不可饶恕地犯了错。几乎，她思忖，但或许并不全然不可饶恕。在那封辩白与懊悔并陈的信里，他告诉她，他什么都不记得，什么都记不得，绝对什么都不记得，只除了一个模糊的、短暂的关于她的影子。其余的一切，开头、结尾、当中的间隙，据他保证，全是一片空白。起初她以为这纯属胡扯。可后来，他那恳切的语气打动了她。紧接着这封信之后，奥尔也来了一封信，证实安南代尔所言不虚，宣称这一切大有可能，并补充说，在某些气质的人身上，受了毒物影响的记忆会耍出一些比寻常头脑所能轻易采信的更为离奇的花招。

这些信她都没有回。从逻辑上讲，她无法承认信中所言的有效性。然而人心自有它一套逻辑，是逻辑本身所不晓得的。再者，我们心中岂不是有那么一种东西，怂恿我们不肯相信我们理应相信的，反倒去相信我们情愿相信的？西尔维娅的理性，受了她的涉世未深的指引，起初拒绝接受这种想法：任何一个神志清醒的男人怎能做出安南代尔所做之事、事后又一无所知。懊悔自是应有之义，可竟至对一切都毫无记忆，在她看来未免荒谬。

===Original===
But there was her cousin’s assurance to the contrary. Then imperceptibly, little by little, that assurance, filtering through the saddened girl, took possession of her, insisting on recognition, telling her that, though her lover had erred, yet, in erring, he was more to be pitied than condemned. Dominated by drink, which, Orr added, he had promised to renounce, he had gone to that haunt and, contaminated there, knew not what he did. But she, instead of realizing that, she who was to have been his in sickness and health, for better, for worse, she, in her pride, had dismissed him.

He had erred, Sylvia told herself, deeply, grievously, but so, too, had she. She had condemned when she should have condoned; she had spurned him when it was her solicitude that he needed.

===Chinese===
然而她表兄对此却作了相反的保证。于是，不知不觉地、一点一滴地，那份保证渗透过这伤心的姑娘，占有了她，非要她承认不可，告诉她：她的恋人虽有错，然而其错之中，他之堪怜甚于堪咎。奥尔又补了一句，他已答应戒酒；可是在酒的支配下，他去了那处所在，在那儿被沾染，不知自己做了什么。而她呢——她本该是他的，无论疾病健康、无论顺境逆境——可她非但没有看穿这一点，反倒出于骄傲，把他逐走了。

他犯了错，西尔维娅对自己说，错得深、错得重，可她自己又何尝不是。她本该宽宥，却施以了定罪；她本该给他关怀，却施以了鄙弃。

===Original===
At the sure cognition of that, it was as though from her eyes a bandage had fallen. Then at once in her tender conscience she beheld herself, detestable in pride, a girl without a heart, one of whom he, no doubt, was well rid of.

It was during the process of this awakening that the conflagration at Narragansett Pier occurred. Sylvia read of it. She read, too, of certain prowesses which the dismissed had displayed.

The account, very inexact as such accounts always are, was also highly colored, spun out for space purposes for much more than the space was worth. Had you not known better you would have taken it for granted that the heroism of Annandale was on a par with that of Leonidas at Thermopylae and even of Roosevelt at San Juan. It quite stirred you.

===Chinese===
一旦确确实实认识到这一点，便仿佛有一条蒙眼布从她眼前落下。随即，在她那柔嫩的良知里，她看见了自己——骄傲得可憎，一个没有心的姑娘，而他，毫无疑问，摆脱了她倒是件幸事。

正是在这一觉来的过程中，纳拉甘西特码头（Narragansett Pier）的那场大火发生了。西尔维娅读到了消息。她也读到了那个被她辞退的人所表现的若干英勇事迹。

那篇报道，正如这类报道向来那样，极不准确，而且渲染得花团锦簇，为了凑篇幅而注的水，远比那点版面所值的多。若非你知根知底，你定会想当然地认为，安南代尔的英勇足可与温泉关的列奥尼达（Leonidas at Thermopylae）比肩，甚至不亚于圣胡安（San Juan）的罗斯福（Roosevelt）。它确实叫你心潮澎湃。

===Original===
It stirred Sylvia. The paper fell from her. But the past returned. At once it seemed to her that it might be mended and the old days renewed. The hero of whom the paper told she knew now that she had wholly loved, and she knew, too, that wholly she still loved him.

Time had done its work ridiculously, inopportunely, yet effectively at last. But the gates of life are double. On one stands written “Too Soon.” On the other “Too Late.” It is unfortunate to get wedged between them. Of that fact Sylvia became rapidly aware. On the morrow she began a letter to Annandale. Before it was finished there came one from Fanny, announcing that she was to be Annandale’s wife.

===Chinese===
它搅动了西尔维娅。报纸从她手中滑落。可往昔回来了。一时间，她觉得它或许还能弥补，旧日或许还能重来。报上所说的那位英雄，她如今才明白自己曾全心全意地爱过他，她同时也明白，自己至今仍全心全意地爱着他。

时间完成了它的工作——办得荒唐、办得不是时候，可终究办成了。然而生命之门有两扇。一扇上写着「太早」。另一扇上写着「太迟」。被卡在两扇之间是不幸的。这一层西尔维娅很快便觉察到了。第二天她动笔给安南代尔写信。信尚未写完，范妮的信先到了，宣告她将与安南代尔成婚。

===Original===
In certain crises of the emotions there is a certain sense of unreality. Even as Sylvia read what Fanny said she could not grasp it. When presently she did, she could not believe it. But there it was. Then immediately she experienced the agony which comes when we battle in dream with the intangible and the dread, when we know it is dream and yet feel it is death.

“It is all my fault,” she cried. She found but that. At the moment she was in that condition which precedes the great commotion of tears, when the strangulation of agony is subsiding and contracted nerves distend. But the tears did not come. The pain was infinite. There was a weight which she felt not without but within, a weight so heavy that she thought she could not bear it. It racked her. Only her mind was active. “It is my fault,” she repeated. Then she added, “And my cross.”

===Chinese===
在某些情感的紧要关头，会有某种不真实之感。西尔维娅读着范妮的话时，竟一时无法领会。待领会过来，又不能置信。可那信就在那里。随即她尝到了那种痛苦——我们在梦中与那无形与可怖之物搏斗时所尝到的痛苦，明知是梦，却感到它是死亡。

「这都是我的错，」她叫道。她只找到这一句话。此刻她正处在泪潮大作之前的那种状态——痛苦的窒息感正退去，紧缩的神经正鼓胀开来。可眼泪并没有来。痛却是无穷无尽的。她感到一种分量，不在身外，而在身内，那分量如此沉重，她以为自己受不住了。它折磨着她。唯有她的心思还在动。「是我的错，」她重复道。然后又添了一句，「也是我的十字架。」

===Original===
From a crisis such as this, in a nature such as hers, the soul issues as from an orgy. It has supped on sorrow. It is fed. It ceases to look back. It looks forward, marveling indeed that it should look at all, yet looking. Life’s burdens are more bearable than the despairful think. Until the eyes are closed and the heart no longer beats, in some way, somehow, they can be carried.

Sylvia took up her cross. It was leaden. But in the effort she was aided. Pride helped her. The assistance of pride may be poor, yet is it not better than none? To Sylvia it was useful. It enabled her to answer Fanny’s letter.

“You have my congratulations, Fanny dear,” she wrote, “all of them, my best and warmest, and so has Arthur too. Please say so to him and tell him that, in marrying by dearest friend, he and I must be dear friends also.”

Then the tears did come, swiftly, like the ripple of the rain. On the table where she sat she put her head down and sobbed, paroxysmally, as sobs a child.

===Chinese===
像她这样的性情，经过这样一场危机之后，灵魂走出来时，犹如从一场纵欲里走出来。它已饱饮了悲伤。它餍足了。它不再回首。它向前望去——自己居然还能望，都觉得惊奇，可到底还在望。生命的重担，比绝望者所想的更可承受。只要双目未合、心脏未停，总有什么办法、总有什么路子，可以把它扛下去。

西尔维娅背起了她的十字架。它是铅铸的。可她在这桩苦功中得了助力。骄傲助了她。骄傲的助力或许寒酸，可总好过没有吧？对西尔维娅，它是有用的。它使她得以回复范妮的信。

「我祝贺你，亲爱的范妮，」她写道，「把全部的祝贺都给你，我最衷心、最热切的那一份；阿瑟也有。请你转告他，并告诉他，他娶了我最亲爱的朋友，那么他和我从此也必是挚友。」

随后眼泪果然来了，来得飞快，像雨的涟漪。她把头伏在面前那张桌上抽噎起来，一阵阵地，像个孩子那样抽噎。
