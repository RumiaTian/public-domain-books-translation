## IV / 四

===Original===
But Lucius did not sleep. Now that he was alone, he felt the agony of his suffering and affliction. He drew a sandal from a little casket, a woman’s blue-leather sandal adorned with gold relief and small, for all that Uncle Catullus was pleased to say. It was the only trace that Ilia had left behind her. And he kissed the sandal and groaned and stretched himself out impotently and clenched his fists and lay like that, staring before him without moving.

He lay lost in thought. And suddenly he struck the gong and summoned Tarrar, who entered nimbly and respectfully:

“Find Caleb and bring him here to me.”

The little slave returned in a short time and ushered in Caleb, who approached with graceful salaams. Tarrar left his master alone with the Sabaean.

“Caleb,” said Lucius, “sit down and listen. I need your advice.”

“Your faithful servant is listening, my lord,” said Caleb, sitting down on a chair.

“Caleb,” continued Lucius, “I have not come to Egypt merely to see the things of interest which this country supplies. I have another object. There are mysterious oracles in Egypt; there are prophets and sibyls, so I am told, living in the desert. I want to know something. I want to know where someone is, one who is dear to me and far away. I want to consult the oracles and the prophets and sibyls. You must conduct me, without saying a word to my uncle or my tutor, because they do not approve of the attempts which I wish to make to find this person whom I love. Be my guide, Caleb, and I will reward you.”

===Chinese===
卢基乌斯却没有睡。如今独自一人，他尝尽了痛苦煎熬的滋味。他从一只小匣里取出一只凉鞋，一只蓝皮革的女式凉鞋，饰着金浮雕，小小的——随卡图卢斯叔父爱怎么说去吧。这是伊利娅留下的唯一痕迹。他吻着那只凉鞋，呻吟着，无可奈何地伸展四肢，攥紧拳头，就那样躺着，一动不动地瞪视着前方。

他躺着，陷在沉思里。突然他敲响铜锣，唤来塔拉尔；小奴麻利而恭顺地进来：

“去找卡莱布，把他带到我这儿来。”

小奴不多时便回来了，引进卡莱布；卡莱布一路打着优雅的躬身礼走近前来。塔拉尔把主人单独留给了那位萨巴人。

“卡莱布，”卢基乌斯说，“坐下，听着。我需要你的主意。”

“您忠心的仆人听着呢，老爷，”卡莱布说着，在一张椅子上坐下。

“卡莱布，”卢基乌斯接着说，“我到埃及来，不只是为了看这个国家供给的种种有趣东西。我另有目的。埃及有神秘的谕示；听说沙漠里还住着先知和女巫。我想知道一件事。我想知道一个人在哪里，一个离我遥远、为我所爱的人。我要去请教那些谕示、先知和女巫。你必须带我去，一个字也不要对我叔父或我教师讲，因为他们不赞成我想方设法去寻这位我所爱的人。做我的向导吧，卡莱布，我会赏你。”

===Original===
“I will be your guide, my lord,” replied Caleb, “and this very night I will conduct you. …”

“Where?”

“To the sibyl of Rhacotis, an old sorceress who knows everything.”

“We will go by ourselves, in secret.”

“Very well, my lord; no one shall accompany us. … What do you think: would you not like a cool sherbet after your rest? And divert yourself with looking at the goods which the travelling merchants from distant foreign lands, who happen today to be staying in the diversorium, have to offer for sale? I will have the sherbet prepared and the merchants informed, my lord. And tonight I will lead you through Rhacotis: we will go by ourselves, my lord, and no one shall know anything of our nocturnal expedition.”

Caleb went away; Tarrar drew the curtains aside. Beyond the bedchamber was a pillared portico; and the green shadow of the palm-garden outside fell within doors. Uncle Catullus was still asleep, but Thrasyllus already sat reading his Egyptian guidebooks at a table under a palm-tree. The wonderful, fantastic stories of Herodotus charmed the old tutor’s mind, which was not disinclined to fantasy; but Thrasyllus also took pleasure in the more succinct descriptions of the learned Eratosthenes, Ptolemy Evergetes’ librarian, who lived three centuries before and was a noted astronomer, philosopher and geographer. Thrasyllus loved to consult his splendid maps, which had never yet been bettered and which lay spread in heavy parchment on the table before him; and the tutor followed the cinnabar-traced Nile on these maps down to Ethiopia and the mysterious sources of the sacred stream.

===Chinese===
“我愿做您的向导，老爷，”卡莱布答道，“就在今夜，我就带您去。……”

“去哪儿？”

“去拉科提斯的女巫那儿，一个无所不知的老巫婆。”

“就我们自己，偷偷地去。”

“很好，老爷；谁也不跟着。……您看怎么样：歇过之后，来一杯冰凉的果子露如何？再看看那些远方外邦来的行商今天恰好住在客栈里、拿出来发卖的货色，解解闷？我去叫人备果子露、通知商人，老爷。今夜我就领您穿行拉科提斯：就我们两个人去，老爷，咱们的夜行探险，谁也不会知道半点。”

卡莱布退下了；塔拉尔把帷幔拉开。卧房外面是一道柱廊；外面棕榈园的绿荫投进门里。卡图卢斯叔父还睡着，特拉叙卢斯却已坐在一株棕榈树下的桌旁，读他的埃及旅行指南。希罗多德（Herodotus）那些奇妙而荒诞的故事让老教师的心听得入迷——他本不排斥奇幻；但特拉叙卢斯也乐于读博学的埃拉托色尼那些较为简明的记述——他是托勒密·欧尔格特斯的图书馆长，生活在三百年前，是著名的天文学家、哲学家和地理学家。特拉叙卢斯爱查看他那几幅出色的地图——至今无人超越——厚厚的羊皮地图摊在他面前的桌上；教师顺着地图上朱砂描出的尼罗河，一路看到埃塞俄比亚和那条圣河神秘的源头。

===Original===
Yes, Eratosthenes was the most respectable guide. When he went blind, in his eighty-second year, he starved himself. Thrasyllus honoured him as a martyr of science. But the tutor also consulted Artemidorus and Hypsicrates, for he wished to be well-informed about the country which he was about to visit, that mysterious country of age-old history and colossal art, while also he did not despise the quite modern writings of his contemporary, Strabo: what a contemporary told about a country over the whole of which he had travelled was perhaps most important of all, because of its practical utility and also because of the freshness of the new impressions.

So Thrasyllus sat under his palm-tree at a table strewn with unrolled papyri; more scrolls stood in a case by his side; and his fingers followed the cinnabar-traced Nile. Lucius in the portico smiled in kindly approval. But the travelling merchants, led by Caleb, arrived through the garden. They were Indians, Sabaeans, Arabs, Phoenicians; and their slaves toiled under their heavy bales of merchandise, which were slung on pliant sticks over their shoulders. The merchants bent low in salaams before the wealthy Roman, bowed down to the earth, kissed the ground which his foot had trodden, all eager to sell their exotic wares at a profit above the ordinary to so distinguished a traveller. The Phoenicians made their slaves spread out Damascus tapestry, but Lucius looked at it with scorn and the Phoenicians at once rolled up their inferior tapestry. Then, however, they displayed embroidery from Nineveh and Tyre; and Lucius turned a little pale, because he thought of Ilia. It was all very beautiful in hue and very curious in pattern.

“Call Uncle Catullus here,” he said to Tarrar, who was squatting beside him like a faithful little monkey.

===Chinese===
是的，埃拉托色尼是最可敬的向导。他八十二岁那年双目失明，便绝食自尽。特拉叙卢斯尊他为科学的殉道者。但教师也参看阿尔特米多鲁斯（Artemidorus）和希普西克拉特斯（Hypsicrates）的著述，因为他想把自己将要游历的这个国家摸个透彻——那个有着古老历史与巨大艺术的神秘国度；同时他也不轻视同时代人斯特拉波那些相当时新的著述：一位同时代人讲述他亲身游历过全境的国度，也许最要紧，因为实用，也因为那些新印象的鲜活。

特拉叙卢斯就这样坐在他的棕榈树下，桌摊着展开的纸草卷；更多的卷轴立在手边的书箱里；他的手指顺着朱砂描出的尼罗河抚过去。柱廊里的卢基乌斯微笑着，嘉许地望着。这时行商们在卡莱布引领下穿过园林来了。他们是印度人、萨巴人、阿拉伯人、腓尼基人；他们的奴隶扛着沉重的货物包，负荷在韧性的扁担上，压弯了肩。商人们在那位富裕的罗马人面前深深打躬，一躬到地，亲吻他的脚踏过的尘土，个个急着想把自己异域的货品以高出寻常的利润卖给这样一位显赫的旅人。腓尼基人让奴隶铺开大马士革织锦，卢基乌斯却轻蔑地扫了一眼，腓尼基人立刻卷起他们次等的织锦。可接着他们展示出尼尼微和推罗的刺绣；卢基乌斯微微变了脸色，因为他想起了伊利娅。那些刺绣色彩艳丽，纹样奇巧。

“去把卡图卢斯叔父叫来，”他对塔拉尔说；小家伙正蹲在他身边，像一只忠心的小猴子。

===Original===
Tarrar hastened to Catullus, who thereupon arrived, sleepily rubbing his eyes, in a wide silk indoor simar; his grey hair stood in a tangle around his bald skull.

“Uncle,” said Lucius, aside, “look here, if you please. Those embroideries from Tyre and Nineveh: I want them. Bargain for them.”

For Uncle Catullus knew how to bargain. He began by turning up his nose at the embroideries; and the merchants uttered loud cries of protest and lifted their hands and invoked all the gods. But Uncle Catullus scornfully shook his head and said:

“No, I won’t buy that trash. Show me other things.”

Then the Phoenicians showed gold vessels from Tartessus, but the Arabs offered perfumes and aromatics from Jeddah and Zebid. The Sabaeans displayed wonderful amulets, which bring luck and blissful dreams: the Indians showed tame, trained snakes, as domestic pets: the snakes had a small sardonyx encrusted in their heads, where it had grown into their scaly skin, and they danced on the tips of their tails, to the piping of the Indians’ flutes. They were attractive little creatures and did not cost more than one stater apiece, with the ebony casket in which they were kept; and Lucius impatiently bought them at once, partly because Tarrar found them so attractive and grinned where he squatted and looked on while the snakes danced and twisted one among another.

===Chinese===
塔拉尔飞跑去找卡图卢斯；叔父随即就来了，睡眼惺忪地揉着眼，穿着一件宽大的丝绸家常长袍；灰发乱蓬蓬地支棱在秃顶四周。

“叔父，”卢基乌斯偏过头低声说，“劳驾，瞧这儿。推罗和尼尼微来的那些刺绣：我要。你去讲价。”

因为讲价是卡图卢斯叔父的拿手好戏。他先对那些刺绣嗤之以鼻；商人们大声抗议，举起双手，把满天神佛都请了出来。可卡图卢斯叔父轻蔑地摇头，说：

“不，这种破烂我不买。拿别的给我看。”

于是腓尼基人捧出塔特苏斯的金器，阿拉伯人献上吉达和兹比德的香水和香料。萨巴人展示神奇的护身符，能带来好运和美梦：印度人展出驯养调教过的蛇，当家庭宠物：蛇头里嵌着一小块缟玛瑙，长进了鳞甲皮里；它们随着印度人的笛声，用尾巴尖跳舞。这些小生灵招人喜欢，连装它们的乌木匣子算在内，每条不过一个斯塔特；卢基乌斯不耐烦地立刻全买了下来，一半也是因为塔拉尔觉得它们太好玩了，蹲在一边看着蛇一条缠着一条地扭摆起舞，咧着嘴直乐。

===Original===
But at last a Mongolian merchant arrived, with a pale-yellow face and narrow eyes, which looked as though they were closed, and his hair, around his shaven head, ended in a pigtail of purple silk, with a tassel to it. This merchant offered little black balls, to be smoked in peculiar pipes; he asked Lucius to accept a pipe and a couple of little black pills in a yellow-silk bag, without payment, and to smoke them when he had the opportunity. The intoxication which they produced was something very peculiar, said the merchant.

Meanwhile Uncle Catullus had duly succeeded in acquiring the embroideries from Tyre and Nineveh at a really laughable price and presented them to his nephew, who of course paid for them in his stead. But, when Lucius held them in his hands—they were narrow strips embroidered with Assyrian lions and strange unicorns—he grew sad and said:

“What use are they to me, after all? Time was when I should have given them to Ilia as a border for her stola. Tarrar, put the pretty embroideries away, with the Mongolian pills and all the other rubbish which I have bought without wanting to: the little gold vases and the Sabaean amulets.”

“And the dear little snakes, my lord?” asked Tarrar, with glittering eyes.

“You may keep them … to play with,” said Lucius, carelessly.

Meanwhile Caleb had had the cups of sherbet handed round. Uncle Catullus thought it particularly good and considered that Lucius’ cook ought really to write down this Egyptian recipe; but Lucius gave his to Tarrar, who scooped up the sherbet greedily with his black fingers.

===Chinese===
最后来了一位蒙古商人，一张淡黄的脸，眼睛细得像闭着似的，剃光的头四周留的头发在脑后编成一条紫丝辫子，缀着流苏。这商人拿出一些黑色小球，要用特制的烟斗抽吸；他请卢基乌斯无偿收下一杆烟斗和两颗小黑药丸，装在一只黄绸袋里，得便时抽一抽。他说，它们引起的醉意非常特别。

与此同时，卡图卢斯叔父果然以一个简直叫人笑话的价钱把推罗和尼尼微的刺绣弄到了手，呈给侄儿——钱当然由侄儿替他付。可卢基乌斯把它们捧在手里——那是几条窄幅绣带，绣着亚述狮子和奇异的独角兽——忽然难过起来，说：

“这些到底对我有什么用呢？从前我会把它们送给伊利娅，给她的斯托拉（stola）镶边。塔拉尔，把这几条漂亮刺绣收起来，跟蒙古药丸和我并不想要却买了的那些破烂一起：小金瓶啊，萨巴护身符啊，都收起来。”

“还有那两条亲爱的小蛇呢，老爷？”塔拉尔眼睛亮晶晶地问。

“就归你了……拿去玩吧，”卢基乌斯漫不经心地说。

这其间卡莱布已让人把一杯杯果子露端了上来。卡图卢斯叔父觉得味道特别好，认为卢基乌斯的厨子真该把这个埃及方子记下来；卢基乌斯却把自己那杯给了塔拉尔，小家伙用黑手指贪婪地舀着吃。
