### I / 一
===Original===

There are certain things that I feel, as I look through this bundle of manuscript, that I must say. The first is that of course no writer ever has fulfilled his intention and no writer ever will; secondly, that there was, when I began, another intention than that of dealing with my subject adequately, namely that of keeping myself outside the whole of it; I was to be, in the most abstract and immaterial sense of the word, a voice, and that simply because this business of seeing Russian psychology through English eyes has no excuse except that it *is* English. That is its only interest, its only atmosphere, its only motive, and if you are going to tell me that any aspect of Russia psychological, mystical, practical, or commercial seen through an English medium is either Russia as she really is or Russia as Russians see her, I say to you, without hesitation, that you don’t know of what you are talking.
Of Russia and the Russians I know nothing, but of the effect upon myself and my ideas of life that Russia and the Russians have made during these last three years I know something. You are perfectly free to say that neither myself nor my ideas of life are of the slightest importance to anyone. To that I would say that anyone’s ideas about life are of importance and that anyone’s ideas about Russian life are of interest﻿ ﻿… and beyond that, I have simply been compelled to write. I have not been able to help myself, and all the faults and any virtues in this story come from that. The facts are true, the inferences absolutely my own, so that you may reject them at any moment and substitute others. It is true that I have known Vera Michailovna, Nina, Alexei Petrovitch, Henry, Jerry, and the rest﻿—some of them intimately﻿—and many of the conversations here recorded I have myself heard. Nevertheless the inferences are my own, and I think there is no Russian who, were he to read this book, would not say that those inferences were wrong. In an earlier record, to which this is in some ways a sequel,1 my inferences were, almost without exception, wrong, and there is no Russian alive for whom this book can have any kind of value except as a happy example of the mistakes that the Englishman can make about the Russian.
But it is over those very mistakes that the two souls, Russian and English, so different, so similar, so friendly, so hostile, may meet.﻿ ﻿…

===Chinese===

翻检这一捆稿子，有几件事我觉得必须说。头一件：当然，从来没有作家实现过他的意图，将来也不会有；第二件：动笔时我还有另一个意图，除了把题材处理周全之外——那就是把自己置身于整个故事之外。我本来只该做——就这个词最抽象、最无形的意义说——一个声音，而这仅仅因为，用英国的眼睛看俄国的灵魂这件事，若说还有什么理由，那理由就是它是英国的。那是它全部的趣味、全部的气氛、全部的动机；你要是想对我说，透过一个英国中介看到的俄国的任何一面——心理的、神秘的、实际的或商业的——要么是俄国本来的样子，要么是俄国人眼中的俄国，那我毫不迟疑地对你说：你根本不知道自己在说什么。
俄国和俄国人，我一无所知；可这三年来，俄国和俄国人加在我本人和我的生活观念上的影响，我知道一些。你完全有自由说，我和我的生活观念对谁都无足轻重。对此我要说：任何人的生活观念都举足轻重，任何人关于俄国生活的观念都有趣味……再说，我简直是被迫非写不可。我管不住自己，这个故事里一切的毛病和任何的好处都来源于此。事实是真的，推论全然是我自己的，所以你随时可以拒收，另换一套。我确实认识薇拉·米哈伊洛夫娜、妮娜、阿列克谢·彼得罗维奇、亨利、杰里和其余的人——其中几位过从甚密——这里记录的许多谈话是我亲耳听见的。尽管如此，推论仍是我自己的，而且我想，随便哪个俄国人读了这本书，都会说那些推论错了。在先前那一部记录里——本书在某些方面是它的续篇——我的推论几乎无一例外都错了；对今天活着的任何一个俄国人，这本书都谈不上任何价值，除了作为一个愉快的例证，证明英国人关于俄国人能犯多大的错。
可恰恰是隔着这些错，这两个灵魂，俄国的和英国的，那么不同、那么相似、那么友好、那么敌对，才得相会。……

===Original===

And in any case the thing has been too strong for me. I have no other defence. For one’s interest in life is stronger, God knows how much stronger, than one’s discretion, and one’s love of life than one’s wisdom, and one’s curiosity in life than one’s ability to record it. At least, as I have said, I have endeavoured to keep my own history, my own desires, my own temperament out of this, as much as is humanly possible.﻿ ﻿…
And the facts are true.

===Chinese===

再 说，这件事对我来说实在太强了。此外我拿不出别的辩护。人对生活的兴味，天知道比人的分寸强多少，人对生活的爱比人的智慧强，人对生活的好奇比人记录生活的本事强。至少，如我所说，我已尽力把我自己的历史、我自己的欲望、我自己的脾气，从这本书里排除出去，尽了人力所能达到的限度。……
而事实是真的。
