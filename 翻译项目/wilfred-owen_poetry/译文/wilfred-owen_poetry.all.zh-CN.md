## Introduction / 引言

===Original===
In writing an Introduction such as this it is good to be brief. The poems printed in this book need no preliminary commendations from me or anyone else. The author has left us his own fragmentary but impressive Foreword; this, and his Poems, can speak for him, backed by the authority of his experience as an infantry soldier, and sustained by nobility and originality of style. All that was strongest in Wilfred Owen survives in his poems; any superficial impressions of his personality, any records of his conversation, behaviour, or appearance, would be irrelevant and unseemly. The curiosity which demands such morsels would be incapable of appreciating the richness of his work.

===Chinese===
写这样一篇引言，贵在简短。收入本书的诗，无需我或任何人预先揄扬。作者已给我们留下他自己的那篇虽残缺却有力的序言；有了它，再加上他的诗，尽足以为他代言——有他步兵经历的权威作后盾，有高贵而独创的风格作支撑。威尔弗雷德·欧文（Wilfred Owen）身上一切最坚强的东西都活在他的诗里；至于对他个性的浮光掠影的印象，关于他言谈、举止、容貌的种种记述，都无关宏旨，也不得体。那种非要打听这类琐屑的好奇心，根本无缘领会他作品的丰厚。

===Original===
The discussion of his experiments in assonance and dissonance (of which *Strange Meeting* is the finest example) may be left to the professional critics of verse, the majority of whom will be more preoccupied with such technical details than with the profound humanity of the self-revelation manifested in such magnificent lines as those at the end of his *Apologia pro Poemate Meo*, and in that other poem which he named *Greater Love*.

===Chinese===
关于他在谐音（assonance）与不谐音（dissonance）上的实验（《奇异的会面》（Strange Meeting）便是其中最精妙的例子），不妨留给职业的诗歌批评家去讨论；他们中的多数人，对这类技术细节的牵挂，将远过于对深沉人性的关注——那人性袒露在壮丽的诗行所自陈的心迹之中，如《为我诗作一辩》（Apologia Pro Poemate Meo）结尾的几行，如他命名为《更大的爱》（Greater Love）的那另一首诗。

===Original===
The importance of his contribution to the literature of the War cannot be decided by those who, like myself, both admired him as a poet and valued him as a friend. His conclusions about War are so entirely in accordance with my own that I cannot attempt to judge his work with any critical detachment. I can only affirm that he was a man of absolute integrity of mind. He never wrote his poems (as so many war-poets did) to make the effect of a personal gesture. He pitied others; he did not pity himself. In the last year of his life he attained a clear vision of what he needed to say, and these poems survive him as his true and splendid testament.

===Chinese===
他对这场战争的文学所作贡献之重大，不能由像我这样的人来评断——我既倾慕这位诗人，又珍重这位朋友。他对战争的结论与我的想法如此完全一致，以致我无法带着任何批评的超然去评判他的作品。我只能断言：他是个内心绝对正直的人。他从不（像那么多战争诗人那样）为做出某种个人姿态而写诗。他悲悯他人，却不自怜。在生命的最后一年，他清晰地看到了自己必须说出什么；这些诗在他身后留存下来，便是他真实而辉煌的遗篇。

## The Sentry / 哨兵

===Original===
We’d found an old Boche dug-out, and he knew,
And gave us hell, for shell on frantic shell
Hammered on top, but never quite burst through.
Rain, guttering down in waterfalls of slime
Kept slush waist high, that rising hour by hour,
Choked up the steps too thick with clay to climb.
What murk of air remained stank old, and sour
With fumes of whizz-bangs, and the smell of men
Who’d lived there years, and left their curse in the den,
If not their corpses.…
There we herded from the blast
Of whizz-bangs, but one found our door at last.
Buffeting eyes and breath, snuffing the candles.
And thud! flump! thud! down the steep steps came thumping
And splashing in the flood, deluging muck—
The sentry’s body; then his rifle, handles
Of old Boche bombs, and mud in ruck on ruck.
We dredged him up, for killed, until he whined
“O sir, my eyes—I’m blind—I’m blind, I’m blind!”
Coaxing, I held a flame against his lids
And said if he could see the least blurred light
He was not blind; in time he’d get all right.
“I can’t,” he sobbed. Eyeballs, huge-bulged like squids
Watch my dreams still; but I forgot him there
In posting next for duty, and sending a scout
To beg a stretcher somewhere, and floundering about
To other posts under the shrieking air.

Those other wretches, how they bled and spewed,
And one who would have drowned himself for good—
I try not to remember these things now.
Let dread hark back for one word only: how
Half-listening to that sentry’s moans and jumps,
And the wild chattering of his broken teeth,
Renewed most horribly whenever crumps
Pummelled the roof and slogged the air beneath—
Through the dense din, I say, we heard him shout
“I see your lights!” But ours had long died out.

===Chinese===
我们寻到一处德国佬（Boche）的旧掩蔽部，他竟也知道，
于是叫我们吃足了苦头：疯狂的炮弹一发接一发
锤打在顶上，却始终没能完全炸穿。
雨水裹着黏泥，像瀑布般直泻而下，
积起的烂泥齐腰深，一小时一小时上涨，
淤死台阶，泥浆稠得无法攀爬。
残存的浑浊空气里是陈年的酸臭，
混着咻轰弹（whizz-bang）的硝烟味，混着人的气味——
那些人在这儿一住经年，把咒骂留在了这窝穴里，
若不是把尸首留在这儿。……
在那里我们挤作一团，躲避着
咻轰弹的爆风，可终于有一发找上了门。
它扑打人的眼睛和呼吸，扇灭了烛火。
咚！噗隆！咚！——顺着陡阶一路捶滚下来，
溅进积水里，泼得污泥四泻——
是哨兵的身子；随后是他的步枪，还有
德国佬旧炸弹的弹柄，烂泥一叠叠压上来。
我们把他捞上来，只当他死了，直到他哀叫：
「长官啊，我的眼睛——我瞎了——我瞎了，我瞎了！」
我哄着他，把一簇火苗凑近他的眼皮，
说只要他还看得见最模糊的一点光，
他就没有瞎；过些时候自会复明。
「我看不见，」他抽泣道。那双像鱿鱼般暴凸的眼球
至今仍盯着我的梦；可在那儿我竟忘了他——
忙着按次序换岗查哨，忙着派一名侦察兵
去什么地方讨一副担架，又跌跌撞撞
赶往别的哨位，头顶炮声凄厉。

其余那些可怜人，他们怎样流血、怎样呕吐，
还有一个一心想把自己淹死拉倒的——
这些事我如今竭力不去回想。
且让惊惧只追回一句话：那时
我们半听着那哨兵的呻吟与抽动，
听着他磕碎的牙齿疯狂打颤，
那打颤便一次比一次可怖地重起，每当重炮弹
捶打屋顶、痛殴底下的空气——
我说，透过那稠密的喧嚣，我们听见他喊：
「我看见你们的光了！」可我们的灯早就熄了。

## The Chances / 机运

===Original===
I mind as ’ow the night afore that show
Us five got talking—we was in the know,
“Over the top to-morrer; boys, we’re for it,
First wave we are, first ruddy wave; that’s tore it.”
“Ah well,” says Jimmy—an’ ’e’s seen some scrappin’—
“There ain’t more nor five things as can ’appen;
Ye get knocked out; else wounded—bad or cushy;
Scuppered; or nowt except yer feeling mushy.”

One of us got the knock-out, blown to chops.
T’other was hurt, like, losin’ both ’is props.
An’ one, to use the word of ’ypocrites,
’Ad the misfortoon to be took by Fritz.
Now me, I wasn’t scratched, praise God Almighty
(Though next time please I’ll thank ’im for a blighty),
But poor young Jim, ’e’s livin’ an’ ’e’s not;
’E reckoned ’e’d five chances, an’ ’e’s ’ad;
’E’s wounded, killed, and pris’ner, all the lot—
The ruddy lot all rolled in one. Jim’s mad.

我记得，开演前那天夜里，
咱们五个凑一块儿唠——消息咱是内行，
「明儿个翻出战壕顶；弟兄们，咱躲不掉，
头一波就是咱，头一波他妈的；这下完蛋。」
「唉，」吉米说——他可是见过真仗阵的——
「天底下能摊上的事儿，不出五样：
要么当场报销；要么挂彩——有轻有重；
要么交代了；要么屁事没有，就是心里发虚。」

咱们里头一个当场报销，炸成了碎肉。
另一个挂了彩，两条腿都给撂了。
还有一个，用假道学的话说，
时运不济，叫弗里茨（Fritz）给逮了去。
说到咱，皮都没破一块，赞美全能的上帝
（不过下回，求他老人家赏咱一处能回英国老家（Blighty）养伤的轻伤），
可怜的小吉米，他活着又没活着；
他掰着指头数了五种下场，五种全领受了；
挂彩、送命、当俘虏，一样不落——
他妈的全套滚成一团，砸在他一个人身上。吉米疯了。

## Anthem for Doomed Youth / 命定青年的颂歌

===Original===
What passing-bells for these who die as cattle?
Only the monstrous anger of the guns.
Only the stuttering rifles’ rapid rattle
Can patter out their hasty orisons.
No mockeries for them; no prayers nor bells,
Nor any voice of mourning save the choirs—
The shrill, demented choirs of wailing shells;
And bugles calling for them from sad shires.

What candles may be held to speed them all?
Not in the hands of boys, but in their eyes
Shall shine the holy glimmers of goodbyes.
The pallor of girls’ brows shall be their pall;
Their flowers the tenderness of patient minds,
And each slow dusk a drawing-down of blinds.

===Chinese===
有什么丧钟，为这些像牲口般成群死去的人敲响？
只有炮群那兽性的愤怒。
只有打结的步枪急促的嗒嗒轰响
能嗒出一篇他们仓促的祷文。
不要为他们行虚假的仪式；没有祷词，没有钟声，
也没有任何哀悼的声音，除了唱诗班——
那尖啸的、疯狂的、炮弹哀号的唱诗班；
还有军号，从哀伤的郡土召唤他们。

有什么烛光，可以为他们的亡灵全体举起来送行？
不在少年们的手里，而在他们的眼睛里，
将闪亮那神圣的、诀别的微光。
姑娘们额头的苍白将是他们的裹尸布；
他们的花圈，是坚忍心灵的柔情，
而每一寸迟缓的暮色，都是一次窗帘的垂落。

## Apologia Pro Poemate Meo / 为我诗作一辩

===Original===
I, too, saw God through mud—
The mud that cracked on cheeks when wretches smiled.
War brought more glory to their eyes than blood,
And gave their laughs more glee than shakes a child.

Merry it was to laugh there—
Where death becomes absurd and life absurder.
For power was on us as we slashed bones bare
Not to feel sickness or remorse of murder.

I, too, have dropped off fear—
Behind the barrage, dead as my platoon,
And sailed my spirit surging, light and clear
Past the entanglement where hopes lay strewn;

And witnessed exultation—
Faces that used to curse me, scowl for scowl,
Shine and lift up with passion of oblation,
Seraphic for an hour; though they were foul.

I have made fellowships—
Untold of happy lovers in old song.
For love is not the binding of fair lips
With the soft silk of eyes that look and long,

By Joy, whose ribbon slips—
But wound with war’s hard wire whose stakes are strong;
Bound with the bandage of the arm that drips;
Knit in the welding of the rifle-thong.

I have perceived much beauty
In the hoarse oaths that kept our courage straight;
Heard music in the silentness of duty;
Found peace where shell-storms spouted reddest spate.

Nevertheless, except you share
With them in hell the sorrowful dark of hell,
Whose world is but the trembling of a flare,
And heaven but as the highway for a shell,

You shall not hear their mirth:
You shall not come to think them well content
By any jest of mine. These men are worth
Your tears: You are not worth their merriment.

November 1917.

===Chinese===
我也曾在泥泞中看见上帝——
那泥泞，苦人一笑就在脸颊上龟裂。
战争映入他们眼中的，血少荣光多，
战争给他们笑声的欢畅，胜过逗孩子的一颤。

在那里欢笑是快事——
在那里，死变得荒诞，生更荒诞。
因为一股伟力加在我们身上，当我们砍得白骨毕露，
竟不觉恶心，也没有杀戮的悔恨。

我也把恐惧丢在了身后——
在弹幕后面，死得像我的全排，
任我的灵魂鼓浪而航，轻盈而澄澈，
驶过那希望散落一地的铁丝网障；

也见证了狂喜——
那些从前咒骂我的脸，一张张横眉相对，
如今焕发昂扬，怀着奉献的激情，
一时圣洁如炽天使；纵然他们满身污秽。

我结成了情谊——
古老歌谣里的快活爱侣从未唱过。
因为爱不是把秀唇轻缚，
用那顾盼生情的眼波的软丝，

凭的是喜乐——可她的丝带会滑脱——
爱是用战争的硬铁丝缠捆，桩柱牢牢；
用滴血手臂上的绷带绑扎；
在步枪皮带扣死的焊结里织牢。

我领悟了许多美，
在那些撑直我们勇气的沙哑咒骂里；
在恪尽职守的静默中听见了音乐；
在炮弹风暴喷出最赤红洪流处觅得安宁。

然而，除非你也与他们同担
地狱里地狱的悲暗——
他们的世界不过是一星照明弹的颤动，
天堂不过是供炮弹飞驰的公路——

你便听不见他们的欢笑：
你也决想不到他们安然自足，
任凭我怎样打趣。这些人值得
你的眼泪：你却不值得他们的欢畅。

一九一七年十一月。

## Wild with All Regrets / 悔恨交加

===Original===
(Another Version of *A Terre*.)

To Siegfried Sassoon

My arms have mutinied against me—brutes!
My fingers fidget like ten idle brats,
My back’s been stiff for hours, damned hours.
Death never gives his squad a Stand-at-ease.
I can’t read. There: it’s no use. Take your book.
A short life and a merry one, my buck!
We said we’d hate to grow dead old. But now,
Not to live old seems awful: not to renew
My boyhood with my boys, and teach ’em hitting,
Shooting and hunting—all the arts of hurting!
—Well, that’s what I learnt. That, and making money.
Your fifty years in store seem none too many;
But I’ve five minutes. God! For just two years
To help myself to this good air of yours!
One Spring! Is one too hard to spare? Too long?
Spring air would find its own way to my lung,
And grow me legs as quick as lilac-shoots.

Yes, there’s the orderly. He’ll change the sheets
When I’m lugged out, oh, couldn’t I do that?
Here in this coffin of a bed, I’ve thought
I’d like to kneel and sweep his floors for ever—
And ask no nights off when the bustle’s over,
For I’d enjoy the dirt; who’s prejudiced
Against a grimed hand when his own’s quite dust—
Less live than specks that in the sun-shafts turn?
Dear dust—in rooms, on roads, on faces’ tan!
I’d love to be a sweep’s boy, black as Town;
Yes, or a muckman. Must I be his load?
A flea would do. If one chap wasn’t bloody,
Or went stone-cold, I’d find another body.

Which I shan’t manage now. Unless it’s yours.
I shall stay in you, friend, for some few hours.
You’ll feel my heavy spirit chill your chest,
And climb your throat on sobs, until it’s chased
On sighs, and wiped from off your lips by wind.

I think on your rich breathing, brother, I’ll be weaned
To do without what blood remained me from my wound.

5th December 1917.

一只跳蚤就够了。只要那个人身上没血污，
或者已经凉透，我可以再找一个身子。

这如今我是办不到了。除非用你的身子。
我要在你里面待上几个钟头，朋友。
你会觉出我沉重的魂魄冰着你的胸口，
又踩着呜咽爬上你的喉咙，直到它被
叹息逐走，被风从你唇边拭去。

我想，靠着你绵长的呼吸，兄弟，我会断奶，
学会不再需要我伤口里剩下的那点血。

一九一七年十二月五日。

## The End / 终结

===Original===
After the blast of lightning from the east,
The flourish of loud clouds, the Chariot throne,
After the drums of time have rolled and ceased
And from the bronze west long retreat is blown,

Shall Life renew these bodies? Of a truth
All death will he annul, all tears assuage?
Or fill these void veins full again with youth
And wash with an immortal water age?

When I do ask white Age, he saith not so—
“My head hangs weighed with snow.”
And when I hearken to the Earth she saith
“My fiery heart sinks aching. It is death.
Mine ancient scars shall not be glorified
Nor my titanic tears the seas be dried.”

===Chinese===
东方炸过闪电之后，
喧云翻涌过后，宝座的战车过后，
时间的战鼓擂罢、息止之后，
铜色的西天吹响悠长的撤退之后，

生命可会更新这些躯壳？果真，
他会取消一切的死，抚平一切的泪？
还是把这些空瘪的血管重新灌满青春，
用一种不朽的水洗掉衰老？

我去问白发的高龄，他答说不然——
「我的头颅垂着，压满了雪。」
我再侧耳听大地，她说：
「我火热的心沉坠作痛。这是死亡。
我古老的伤疤不会得着荣耀，
我巨人般的泪，大海也吸不干。」

## Dulce Et Decorum Est / 为国捐躯，甘美而荣光

===Original===
Bent double, like old beggars under sacks,
Knock-kneed, coughing like hags, we cursed through sludge,
Till on the haunting flares we turned our backs,
And towards our distant rest began to trudge.
Men marched asleep. Many had lost their boots,
But limped on, blood-shod. All went lame, all blind;
Drunk with fatigue; deaf even to the hoots
Of gas-shells dropping softly behind.

Gas! **Gas!** Quick, boys!—An ecstasy of fumbling
Fitting the clumsy helmets just in time,
But someone still was yelling out and stumbling
And flound’ring like a man in fire or lime.—
Dim through the misty panes and thick green light,
As under a green sea, I saw him drowning.

In all my dreams before my helpless sight
He plunges at me, guttering, choking, drowning.

If in some smothering dreams, you too could pace
Behind the wagon that we flung him in,
And watch the white eyes writhing in his face,
His hanging face, like a devil’s sick of sin,
If you could hear, at every jolt, the blood
Come gargling from the froth-corrupted lungs
Obscene as cancer, bitter as the cud
Of vile, incurable sores on innocent tongues—
My friend, you would not tell with such high zest
To children ardent for some desperate glory,
The old Lie: *Dulce et decorum est*
*Pro patria mori.*

===Chinese===
人人弯成对折，像顶着麻袋的老乞丐，
罗圈着腿，咳嗽得像老巫婆，我们一路咒骂着趟过泥浆，
直到把那鬼火般的照明弹甩在背后，
朝着远处的宿营地开始跋涉。
人们打着瞌睡行军。许多人的靴子早丢了，
仍蹒跚向前，脚上是血做的鞋。全都跛了；全都瞎了；
疲惫得像烂醉；连毒气炮弹
在身后轻柔坠落的呜呜声也听不见。

毒气！**毒气！**快，弟兄们！——一阵狂乱的摸索，
总算及时戴好笨拙的头盔，
可还有人叫喊着、跌撞着，
像陷在烈火或石灰里一样扑腾。——
隔着起雾的镜片和浓绿的幽光，隐约地，
仿佛沉在一片绿海底下，我看见他淹死。

在我一切无能为力的眼前的梦里，
他总朝我扑来，摇曳着，呛噎着，溺毙着。

倘若在某种窒息的梦里，你也能跟在
那辆我们把他扔上去的马车后面走，
看那张脸上翻滚的白眼，
那张下垂的脸，像恶鬼厌透了罪恶，
倘若你听得见，每一次颠簸，血
从起沫溃烂的肺里汩汩涌上来，
龌龊如癌瘤，苦得像反刍的
秽恶而不治的疮、烂在无辜的舌头上——
朋友，你便不会那样起劲地宣讲，
对那些渴求殊死荣光的孩子们，
宣讲那古老的谎言：*为国捐躯，
甘美而荣光（Dulce et decorum est / Pro patria mori）。*
## Insensibility / 麻木

### I / 一

===Original===
Happy are men who yet before they are killed
Can let their veins run cold.
Whom no compassion fleers
Or makes their feet
Sore on the alleys cobbled with their brothers.
The front line withers,
But they are troops who fade, not flowers
For poets’ tearful fooling:
Men, gaps for filling
Losses who might have fought
Longer; but no one bothers.

===Chinese===
幸福啊，那些还来得及在被杀之前
让自己血管冷却的人。
任谁的怜悯也讥笑不到他们，
也硌不疼他们的脚，在那
用亲兄弟铺成的巷路上。
前线凋敝了，
可他们是渐渐隐没的队伍，不是供诗人
泪汪汪煽情的那些花：
他们是人，是等着填补的空缺，
是原本还能再打
下去的伤耗；只是无人在意。

### II / 二

===Original===
And some cease feeling
Even themselves or for themselves.
Dullness best solves
The tease and doubt of shelling,
And Chance’s strange arithmetic
Comes simpler than the reckoning of their shilling.
They keep no check on Armies’ decimation.

===Chinese===
有些人连感觉都熄了，
对自己、为自身都再无感觉。
麻木最能消解
炮击的撩弄与惶惑，
而命运那古怪的算术，
比清点他们那枚先令军饷还简单。
大军把他们十扣去一，他们也从不去核账。

### III / 三

===Original===
Happy are these who lose imagination:
They have enough to carry with ammunition.
Their spirit drags no pack.
Their old wounds save with cold can not more ache.
Having seen all things red,
Their eyes are rid
Of the hurt of the colour of blood for ever.
And terror’s first constriction over,
Their hearts remain small drawn.
Their senses in some scorching cautery of battle
Now long since ironed,
Can laugh among the dying, unconcerned.

===Chinese===
幸福啊，这些丢掉了想象力的人：
他们要背的东西，连同弹药已经够多。
他们的灵魂不拖行囊。
他们的旧伤安分于寒冷，再不会作痛。
既然看什么都是一片红，
他们的眼睛便永远
摆脱了血这种颜色的刺痛。
而恐怖最初的紧攥过去后，
他们的心只剩缩紧的一小团。
他们的感官，在某场战事的灼烙里
早已烫平，
能在垂死者中间谈笑，无动于衷。

### IV / 四

===Original===
Happy the soldier home, with not a notion
How somewhere, every dawn, some men attack,
And many sighs are drained.
Happy the lad whose mind was never trained:
His days are worth forgetting more than not.
He sings along the march
Which we march taciturn, because of dusk,
The long, forlorn, relentless trend
From larger day to huger night.

===Chinese===
幸福啊，那回国的士兵，浑然不知
每一个破晓，在某处，总有人在进攻，
多少叹息被抽干。
幸福啊，那脑子从没受过训的小伙子：
他的日子，与其记住不如忘掉。
行军时他一路唱歌，
同一条路我们默默走，因为暮色，
因为那漫长、凄凉、不由分说的趋势：
从更大的白昼走向更巨的黑夜。

### V / 五

===Original===
We wise, who with a thought besmirch
Blood over all our soul,
How should we see our task
But through his blunt and lashless eyes?
Alive, he is not vital overmuch;
Dying, not mortal overmuch;
Nor sad, nor proud,
Nor curious at all.
He cannot tell
Old men’s placidity from his.

===Chinese===
我们这些聪明人，一个念头就把血
泼污了整个灵魂，
我们又怎能看懂自己的任务，
除了透过他那一双莽钝、无睫的眼？
活着，他没有多少生气；
死时，也算不上多么致命；
不悲，不傲，
也全无好奇。
他分不清
老人们的安恬和自己的有什么两样。

### VI / 六

===Original===
But cursed are dullards whom no cannon stuns,
That they should be as stones.
Wretched are they, and mean
With paucity that never was simplicity.
By choice they made themselves immune
To pity and whatever mourns in man
Before the last sea and the hapless stars;
Whatever mourns when many leave these shores;
Whatever shares
The eternal reciprocity of tears.

===Chinese===
可诅咒啊，那些麻木的蠢物，大炮都震不醒他们，
他们竟活成了石头。
他们不幸，而且鄙吝，
那份寒贫从来不是简朴。
他们甘愿让自己免疫于
怜悯，免疫于人心中一切哀恸者——
在最后的海与无告的星辰之前的哀恸者；
免疫于众人离岸时的哀恸；
免疫于一切分担着
眼泪的永恒往还的东西。

## Conscious / 苏醒

===Original===
His fingers wake, and flutter up the bed.
His eyes come open with a pull of will,
Helped by the yellow may-flowers by his head.
A blind-cord drawls across the window-sill…
How smooth the floor of the ward is! what a rug!
And who’s that talking, somewhere out of sight?
Why are they laughing? What’s inside that jug?
“Nurse! Doctor!” “Yes; all right, all right.”

But sudden dusk bewilders all the air—
There seems no time to want a drink of water.
Nurse looks so far away. And everywhere
Music and roses burnt through crimson slaughter.
Cold; cold; he’s cold; and yet so hot:
And there’s no light to see the voices by—
No time to dream, and ask—he knows not what.

===Chinese===
他的手指醒了，沿着被单翩然上移。
他的眼睛凭一股意志的拉力睁开，
床头那束黄色的山楂花帮了忙。
一根百叶窗绳懒懒地横过窗台……
病房的地板多光滑啊！这地毯真好！
谁在说话，在看不见的什么地方？
他们笑什么？那壶里装的是什么？
「护士！医生！」「哎；好了，没事了。」

可骤然的昏暗搅乱了整个空气——
仿佛来不及想要一口水喝。
护士显得那么遥远。而四面八方，
音乐和玫瑰烧穿了绯红的屠戮。
冷；冷；他冷；却又那么热：
又没有一点光亮，照着认一认那些声音——
没时间做梦了，也没时间问——问什么，他不知道。

## Strange Meeting / 奇异的会面

===Original===
It seemed that out of the battle I escaped
Down some profound dull tunnel, long since scooped
Through granites which Titanic wars had groined.
Yet also there encumbered sleepers groaned,
Too fast in thought or death to be bestirred.
Then, as I probed them, one sprang up, and stared
With piteous recognition in fixed eyes,
Lifting distressful hands as if to bless.
And by his smile, I knew that sullen hall;
With a thousand fears that vision’s face was grained;
Yet no blood reached there from the upper ground,
And no guns thumped, or down the flues made moan.
“Strange, friend,” I said. “Here is no cause to mourn.”
“None,” said the other, “Save the undone years,
The hopelessness. Whatever hope is yours,
Was my life also; I went hunting wild
After the wildest beauty in the world,
Which lies not calm in eyes, or braided hair,
But mocks the steady running of the hour,
And if it grieves, grieves richlier than here.
For by my glee might many men have laughed,
And of my weeping something has been left,
Which must die now. I mean the truth untold,
The pity of war, the pity war distilled.
Now men will go content with what we spoiled.
Or, discontent, boil bloody, and be spilled.
They will be swift with swiftness of the tigress,
None will break ranks, though nations trek from progress.
Courage was mine, and I had mystery;
Wisdom was mine, and I had mastery;
To miss the march of this retreating world
Into vain citadels that are not walled.
Then, when much blood had clogged their chariot-wheels
I would go up and wash them from sweet wells,
Even with truths that lie too deep for taint.
I would have poured my spirit without stint
But not through wounds; not on the cess of war.
Foreheads of men have bled where no wounds were.
I am the enemy you killed, my friend.
I knew you in this dark; for so you frowned
Yesterday through me as you jabbed and killed.
I parried; but my hands were loath and cold.
Let us sleep now…”2

===Chinese===
仿佛是从那场战斗中，我逃脱了，
顺着一道幽深而沉闷的隧道向下，它久已被凿空，
穿过诸巨神之战拱成的花岗岩。
然而那里也有负重的睡眠者在呻吟，
陷进思想或死亡太深，唤不醒、动不得。
接着，当我探看他们时，有一个霍然跃起，凝视着，
僵直的眼里含着凄然的相认，
举起痛苦的手，仿佛要祝福。
凭他的笑，我认出了那阴沉的大厅；
那张幻象的脸，因千种恐惧而布满刻痕；
然而没有血从上面的地面流到那里，
也没有炮声捶击，或顺着烟道呜咽而下。
「奇怪，朋友，」我说，「这里没有哀恸的理由。」
「没有，」那一位说，「除了未及完成的岁月，
除了绝望。你怀有的无论什么希望，
也曾是我的生命；我发狂似的追逐
世上最狂野的美，
它并不安卧于眼中，或编起的发辫里，
却嘲弄时辰刻板的运行，
它若悲哀，也悲哀得比这里更丰饶。
因为凭我的欢愉，许多人本可以发笑，
而我的哭泣，也留下了一些东西，
它们如今必须死去。我是说那未及说出的真相，
战争的悲悯，战争蒸馏出的悲悯。
如今人们将满足于我们所毁掉的一切，
或者不满，血淋淋地沸腾，然后泼洒掉。
他们将带着母虎的迅疾而迅疾，
无人会脱离队伍，哪怕各个民族背离文明而跋涉。
勇气曾属我，我还拥有玄秘；
智慧曾属我，我还得其三昧；
竟错过了这溃退世界的行军，
退进那些虚妄而不设防的堡垒。
那时，等他们的血厚厚地堵住车轮，
我本会走上前去，用甜井的水把它们洗净，
就用那些深得无从玷污的真相来洗。
我本会毫不吝惜地倾出我的灵魂，
却不透过伤口；也不倾在战争的粪池上。
人的额头，曾在无伤处流血。
我是你杀死的敌人，我的朋友。
我在这黑暗中认出了你；昨天
你刺杀我的时候，正是这样皱着眉。
我也招架了；只是我的手又冷，又不情愿。
现在让我们睡吧……」2

## Arms and the Boy / 武器与少年

===Original===
Let the boy try along this bayonet-blade
How cold steel is, and keen with hunger of blood;
Blue with all malice, like a madman’s flash;
And thinly drawn with famishing for flesh.

Lend him to stroke these blind, blunt bullet-heads
Which long to muzzle in the hearts of lads.
Or give him cartridges of fine zinc teeth,
Sharp with the sharpness of grief and death.

There lurk no claws behind his fingers supple;
And God will grow no talons at his heels,
Nor antlers through the thickness of his curls.

===Chinese===
让这少年沿着这把刺刀的刀刃摸一摸，

淬满歹毒的蓝，像疯子眼里的一道凶光；

## The Dead-Beat / 垮掉的人

===Original===
He dropped—more sullenly than wearily,
Lay stupid like a cod, heavy like meat,
And none of us could kick him to his feet;
Just blinked at my revolver, blearily;
—Didn’t appear to know a war was on,
Or see the blasted trench at which he stared.
“I’ll do ’em in,” he whined. “If this hand’s spared,
I’ll murder them, I will.”

A low voice said,
“It’s Blighty, p’raps, he sees; his pluck’s all gone,
Dreaming of all the valiant, that *aren’t* dead:
Bold uncles, smiling ministerially;
Maybe his brave young wife, getting her fun
In some new home, improved materially.
It’s not these stiffs have crazed him; nor the Hun.”

We sent him down at last, out of the way.
Unwounded;—stout lad, too, before that strafe.
Malingering? Stretcher-bearers winked, “Not half!”

Next day I heard the Doc.’s well-whiskied laugh:
“That scum you sent last night soon died. Hooray!”

===Chinese===
他倒下了——与其说疲惫，不如说阴沉，
躺着发呆，像条鳕鱼，死沉，像一块肉，
我们谁也没法把他踢起来；
他只朝我的左轮枪眨眼，眯迷糊糊；
——好像不知道眼下正打着仗，
也看不见他盯着的这条挨炸的战壕。
「我要宰了他们，」他哼哼唧唧，「只要这只手还留着，
我就杀了他们，我真杀。」

一个低低的声音说：
「他八成是盼着挨一处能回英国老家（Blighty）的轻伤；胆气全泄了，
梦见的净是那些好样的，偏偏*没有*死：
威风的叔伯们，一脸体谅的官笑；
说不定还有他年轻的媳妇，正寻她的快活，
在什么新宅子里，家当样样翻新。
弄疯他的不是这些死鬼；也不是德国鬼子（Hun）。」

最后我们把他送下了后方，眼不见为净。
没挂彩；——本来也是条壮实的小伙，在那场炮击之前。
装病逃差？担架兵挤挤眼：「可不，全是装的！」

第二天我听见军医饱蘸威士忌的笑声：
「你们昨晚送来的那个废物，很快就断气了。好哇！」

## S.I.W. / 自伤

===Original===
“I will to the King,
And offer him consolation in his trouble,
For that man there has set his teeth to die,
And being one that hates obedience,
Discipline, and orderliness of life,
I cannot mourn him.”

> W. B. Yeats

Patting goodbye, doubtless they told the lad
He’d always show the Hun a brave man’s face;
Father would sooner him dead than in disgrace—
Was proud to see him going, aye, and glad.
Perhaps his Mother whimpered how she’d fret
Until he got a nice, safe wound to nurse.
Sisters would wish girls too could shoot, charge, curse,…
Brothers—would send his favourite cigarette,
Each week, month after month, they wrote the same,
Thinking him sheltered in some Y. M. Hut,
Where once an hour a bullet missed its aim
And misses teased the hunger of his brain.
His eyes grew old with wincing, and his hand
Reckless with ague. Courage leaked, as sand
From the best sandbags after years of rain.
But never leave, wound, fever, trench-foot, shock,
Untrapped the wretch. And death seemed still withheld
For torture of lying machinally shelled,
At the pleasure of this world’s Powers who’d run amok.

He’d seen men shoot their hands, on night patrol,
Their people never knew. Yet they were vile.
“Death sooner than dishonour, that’s the style!”
So Father said.

One dawn, our wire patrol
Carried him. This time, Death had not missed.
We could do nothing, but wipe his bleeding cough.
Could it be accident?—Rifles go off…
Not sniped? No. (Later they found the English ball.)

It was the reasoned crisis of his soul.
Against the fires that would not burn him whole
But kept him for death’s perjury and scoff
And life’s half-promising, and both their riling.

With him they buried the muzzle his teeth had kissed,
And truthfully wrote the Mother “Tim died smiling.”

## The Show / 演出

===Original===
My soul looked down from a vague height with Death,

与世无争地分享着草场与阵雨。
柔雨会抚到我——像从前那样抚到我，
除了太阳，再没有什么能叫我惊动。
你的炮尽管在我四周炸。我听不见；
或者缩一下，也不会知道自己缩了。
别把我灵魂这点可怜的安慰，当成你的笑料。
士兵变成蕨叶之后，或许会长出灵魂，
可眼下这东西，最好留在家里，托给朋友。

我的灵魂是一小团悲苦，攀住你的胸口，
踩着呜咽往上爬你的喉咙；轻易就会被
别的叹息赶跑，被更新的风拭去。

载着我啼哭的灵魂吧，直到它断了奶，
学会不再需要这些伤口里剩下的血。

## The Send-Off / 送行

===Original===
Down the close, darkening lanes they sang their way
To the siding-shed,
And lined the train with faces grimly gay.

Their breasts were stuck all white with wreath and spray
As men’s are, dead.

Dull porters watched them, and a casual tramp
Stood staring hard,
Sorry to miss them from the upland camp.
Then, unmoved, signals nodded, and a lamp
Winked to the guard.

So secretly, like wrongs hushed-up, they went.
They were not ours:
We never heard to which front these were sent.

Nor there if they yet mock what women meant
Who gave them flowers.

Shall they return to beatings of great bells
In wild trainloads?
A few, a few, too few for drums and yells,
May creep back, silent, to still village wells
Up half-known roads.

## Mental Cases / 精神病人

===Original===
Who are these? Why sit they here in twilight?
Wherefore rock they, purgatorial shadows,
Drooping tongues from jaws that slob their relish,
Baring teeth that leer like skulls’ tongues wicked?
Stroke on stroke of pain—but what slow panic,
Gouged these chasms round their fretted sockets?
Ever from their hair and through their hand palms
Misery swelters. Surely we have perished
Sleeping, and walk hell; but who these hellish?

—These are men whose minds the Dead have ravished.
Memory fingers in their hair of murders,
Multitudinous murders they once witnessed.
Wading sloughs of flesh these helpless wander,
Treading blood from lungs that had loved laughter.
Always they must see these things and hear them,
Batter of guns and shatter of flying muscles,
Carnage incomparable and human squander
Rucked too thick for these men’s extrication.

Therefore still their eyeballs shrink tormented
Back into their brains, because on their sense
Sunlight seems a bloodsmear; night comes blood-black;
Dawn breaks open like a wound that bleeds afresh
—Thus their heads wear this hilarious, hideous,
Awful falseness of set-smiling corpses.
—Thus their hands are plucking at each other;
Picking at the rope-knouts of their scourging;
Snatching after us who smote them, brother,
Pawing us who dealt them war and madness.

===Chinese===
这些是谁？他们为什么坐在暮色里？
为什么摇晃着，这些炼狱的影子，
垂着舌头，颌间淌着涎水，
龇着牙，像骷髅的舌头一样邪恶地冷笑？
一击又一击的疼痛——可那是怎样缓慢的惊惶，
在他们焦灼的眼窝四周，凿出这些沟壑？
永远，从他们的头发里，从他们的掌心里，
苦难在蒸腾。想必我们已经死了，
死在睡梦里，此刻正走在地狱中；可这些地狱般的是什么人？

——这些是人，是心智被死者们劫掠过的人。
记忆在他们头发里翻检，根根都是屠戮，
那些他们曾亲眼目睹的、数不尽的屠戮。
蹚着肉的泥沼，这些无助者游荡，
脚下踩出血来，来自那些曾热爱欢笑的肺。
他们必须永远看见这些、听见这些，
大炮的捶打，纷飞肌肉的迸裂，
无可比拟的杀场和人命的抛掷，
堆叠得太厚，这些人再也拔不出身。

因此他们的眼球至今痛苦地
缩回脑子里去，因为对他们的感官来说，
阳光像一抹血污；夜来时血一般黑；
黎明绽开，像一道重新流血的伤口
——于是他们的头颅上戴着这种欢腾的、狰狞的、
可怖的假面——僵笑的死尸的假面。
——于是他们的手彼此撕扯；
抠着抽打他们的那道道绳鞭伤痕；
追抓着我们这些击打他们的人，兄弟，
撕扯着我们这些发给他们战争与疯狂的人。

## Exposure / 暴露

### I / 一

===Original===
Our brains ache, in the merciless iced east winds that knife us…
Wearied we keep awake because the night is silent…
Low drooping flares confuse our memory of the salient…
Worried by silence, sentries whisper, curious, nervous,
But nothing happens.

Watching, we hear the mad gusts tugging on the wire.
Like twitching agonies of men among its brambles.
Northward incessantly, the flickering gunnery rumbles,
Far off, like a dull rumour of some other war.
What are we doing here?

The poignant misery of dawn begins to grow…
We only know war lasts, rain soaks, and clouds sag stormy.
Dawn massing in the east her melancholy army
Attacks once more in ranks on shivering ranks of gray,
But nothing happens.

Sudden successive flights of bullets streak the silence.
Less deadly than the air that shudders black with snow,
With sidelong flowing flakes that flock, pause and renew,
We watch them wandering up and down the wind’s nonchalance,
But nothing happens.

===Chinese===
我们的脑袋疼得发木，在刀子般割人的、无情凛冽的东风里……
疲惫中我们强撑着不睡，因为夜太安静……
低垂的照明弹扰乱了我们对突出部的记忆……
被寂静搅得不安，哨兵们低语着，好奇，紧张，
可什么也没有发生。

瞭望时，我们听见狂风疯扯着铁丝网，
像缠在荆棘里的男人们抽搐的苦痛。
北面，炮火闪闪烁烁，连绵地隆隆滚过，
远得像另一场战争模糊的谣传。
我们在这儿做什么？

破晓那锥心的悲惨开始生长……
我们只知道战争在继续，雨在浸透，云阴沉欲雨。
黎明在东边集结着她忧郁的大军，
又一次以横列，攻击着颤抖的灰色横列，
可什么也没有发生。

骤然，一连串的子弹划破寂静。
不及那黑雪打颤的空气致命，
斜飞的雪片成群涌来，停一停，又续上，
我们看着它们上上下下，游荡在风的漠然里，
可什么也没有发生。

### II / 二

===Original===
Pale flakes with lingering stealth come feeling for our faces—
We cringe in holes, back on forgotten dreams, and stare, snow-dazed,
Deep into grassier ditches. So we drowse, sun-dozed,
Littered with blossoms trickling where the blackbird fusses.
Is it that we are dying?

Slowly our ghosts drag home: glimpsing the sunk fires glozed
With crusted dark-red jewels; crickets jingle there;
For hours the innocent mice rejoice: the house is theirs;
Shutters and doors all closed: on us the doors are closed—
We turn back to our dying.

Since we believe not otherwise can kind fires burn;
Nor ever suns smile true on child, or field, or fruit.
For God’s invincible spring our love is made afraid;
Therefore, not loath, we lie out here; therefore were born,
For love of God seems dying.

To-night, His frost will fasten on this mud and us,
Shrivelling many hands and puckering foreheads crisp.
The burying-party, picks and shovels in their shaking grasp,
Pause over half-known faces. All their eyes are ice,
But nothing happens.

===Chinese===
苍白的雪片迟迟疑疑、蹑手蹑脚，摸索上我们的脸——
我们缩在坑里，退回被遗忘的梦里，瞪着眼，雪盲了一般，
深深望进更青翠的沟渠。就这样我们打着盹，晒着太阳昏昏沉沉，
身上散落着花，那是有乌鸫扑腾的地方漏下的。
是我们正在死去吗？

我们的鬼魂缓缓往家拖：瞥见炉膛里闷烧的火
结着暗红的硬痂，像宝石；蟋蟀在那儿鸣叫；
无辜的老鼠欢腾了几个钟头：房子归了它们；
百叶窗和门都关了：对我们，门也关上了——
我们折返自己的垂死。

因为我们不信，不这样炉火便烧不旺；
也不信太阳会真心对着孩子、田野或果实微笑。
为了上帝那挡不住的春天，我们的爱反生了恐惧；
所以，并不勉强，我们躺卧在这里；所以生下来，
因为对上帝的爱，仿佛正在死去。

今夜，祂的严霜将攫住这泥泞和我们，
蔫缩多少只手，把多少额头冻出褶皱。
掩埋队攥着发抖的铁镐与铁锹，
在认不太清的脸上停顿。所有的眼睛都是冰，
可什么也没有发生。

## Spring Offensive / 春季攻势

===Original===
Halted against the shade of a last hill,
They fed, and, lying easy, were at ease
And, finding comfortable chests and knees
Carelessly slept. But many there stood still
To face the stark, blank sky beyond the ridge,
Knowing their feet had come to the end of the world.

Marvelling they stood, and watched the long grass swirled
By the May breeze, murmurous with wasp and midge,
For though the summer oozed into their veins
Like the injected drug for their bones’ pains,
Sharp on their souls hung the imminent line of grass,
Fearfully flashed the sky’s mysterious glass.

Hour after hour they ponder the warm field—
And the far valley behind, where the buttercups
Had blessed with gold their slow boots coming up,
Where even the little brambles would not yield,
But clutched and clung to them like sorrowing hands;
They breathe like trees unstirred.

Till like a cold gust thrilled the little word
At which each body and its soul begird
And tighten them for battle. No alarms
Of bugles, no high flags, no clamorous haste—
Only a lift and flare of eyes that faced
The sun, like a friend with whom their love is done.
O larger shone that smile against the sun—
Mightier than his whose bounty these have spurned.

So, soon they topped the hill, and raced together
Over an open stretch of herb and heather
Exposed. And instantly the whole sky burned
With fury against them; and soft sudden cups
Opened in thousands for their blood; and the green slopes
Chasmed and steepened sheer to infinite space.

Of them who running on that last high place
Leapt to swift unseen bullets, or went up
On the hot blast and fury of hell’s upsurge,
Or plunged and fell away past this world’s verge,
Some say God caught them even before they fell.

But what say such as from existence’ brink
Ventured but drave too swift to sink.
The few who rushed in the body to enter hell,
And there out-fiending all its fiends and flames
With superhuman inhumanities,
Long-famous glories, immemorial shames—
And crawling slowly back, have by degrees
Regained cool peaceful air in wonder—
Why speak they not of comrades that went under?

===Chinese===
在最后一座山丘的荫蔽下停住，
他们进食，躺着舒展，一身轻快，
胸口和膝盖找到了舒服的姿势，
便稀里糊涂睡去。可也有许多人站着不动，
面对山脊外那赤裸、空茫的天，
知道自己的双脚已走到世界的尽头。

他们站着惊叹，看长草被
五月的风旋起，草间嗡涌着黄蜂与蠓虫，
因为夏天虽已渗进他们的血管，
像一剂注入的药，医治骨头的酸痛，
草线的迫近仍锋利地悬在他们的灵魂上，
天空那面神秘的镜子，骇人地闪着光。

一个钟头接一个钟头，他们对望着暖热的田野——
还有身后遥远的山谷，那里的毛茛
曾以金色，祝福他们缓缓上行的军靴，
在那里，连小小的悬钩子也不肯让路，
只顾抓着他们、攀着他们，像悲戚的手；
他们呼吸着，像纹丝不动的树。

直到那道命令，短短几个字，像一股冷风
穿过——每个身躯连同它的灵魂
都为战斗束紧、绷紧。没有
号角的警报，没有高扬的旗帜，没有喧嚷的急迫——
只有一次抬眼，眼中燃起光焰，望向
太阳，像望着一位情分已尽的旧友。
哦，那迎着太阳的笑容，照得比太阳更亮——
比那位被他们抛却了馈赠的太阳更强。

就这样，他们很快翻过山丘，一起
奔过一片开阔的草卉与石楠，
全然暴露。刹那间，整片天空燃起
对他们的狂怒；柔软而骤开的杯盏，
千万只为他们的血而张开；青绿的山坡
裂成深渊，陡直地跌向无穷的虚空。

他们之中，有人奔跑在那最后的高地上，
纵身迎向迅疾而无形的子弹，或者
随着地狱喷薄而出的炽风与烈怒腾空而起，
或者一头栽下，坠出这个世界的边沿，
有人说，上帝在他们落地之前就接住了他们。

可那些人又怎么说呢——他们从存在的
边缘一跃，冲得太疾，反沉不下去。
那几个人，以血肉之躯冲进了地狱，
在那里比一切恶魔与烈焰更凶恶魔祟，
以超人的不人道，
以久负盛名的荣光、太古流传的羞耻——
又缓缓爬了回来，一点一点地，
在惶惑中重获清凉平静的空气——
为什么他们绝口不提那些陷落下去的战友？

## Smile, Smile, Smile / 微笑，微笑，微笑

===Original===
Head to limp head, the sunk-eyed wounded scanned
Yesterday’s Mail; the casualties (typed small)
And (large) Vast Booty from our Latest Haul.
Also, they read of Cheap Homes, not yet planned;
For, said the paper, “When this war is done
The men’s first instinct will be making homes.
Meanwhile their foremost need is aerodromes,
It being certain war has just begun.
Peace would do wrong to our undying dead—
The sons we offered might regret they died
If we got nothing lasting in their stead.
We must be solidly indemnified.
Though all be worthy Victory which all bought,
We rulers sitting in this ancient spot
Would wrong our very selves if we forgot
The greatest glory will be theirs who fought,
Who kept this nation in integrity.”
Nation?—The half-limbed readers did not chafe
But smiled at one another curiously
Like secret men who know their secret safe.
This is the thing they know and never speak,
That England one by one had fled to France
(Not many elsewhere now save under France).
Pictures of these broad smiles appear each week,
And people in whose voice real feeling rings
Say: How they smile! They’re happy now, poor things.

23rd September 1918.

===Chinese===
无力的头挨着头，眼窝深陷的伤员们翻看
昨天的《每日邮报》（Mail）；阵亡名单（小号铅字排的），
还有（大字标题）「我军最新战果、空前战利品」。
另外他们也读到「廉价住宅」，虽然图纸八字还没一撇；
因为报纸说：「等这场仗打完，
军人们的第一本能便是成家置业。
眼下他们最急需的是飞机场，
反正这场仗无疑才刚刚开打。
停战将会对不起我们虽死犹生的亡者——
我们献出去的这些儿子，倘若换不来
任何持久的东西，只怕死也要悔恨。
我们必须拿到实实在在的赔款。
纵然这胜利人人当得起、是人人出价买下的，
可我们这些端坐在古老之地的统治者，
若是忘了这一点，便对不起我们自己：
最伟大的荣耀将属于那些打仗的人，
属于保全了这个国家完整的人。」
国家？——那些缺胳膊少腿的读者并不愠怒，
只是彼此古怪地相视一笑，
像揣着秘密的人，深知那秘密万无一失。
这正是他们心知肚明、却从不出口的事：
英格兰的儿郎早已一个接一个投奔了法兰西，
（如今除了长眠法兰西土下，别处剩下的已不多。）
这些咧开的笑脸，照片每周都登，
而那些话音里响着真切感情的人们
便说：瞧他们笑得多欢！他们现在快活了，可怜的家伙们。

一九一八年九月二十三日。

## Preface1 / 序言

===Original===
This book is not about heroes. English Poetry is not yet fit to speak of them. Nor is it about deeds or lands, nor anything about glory, honour, dominion or power,

except War.

Above all, this book is not concerned with Poetry.

The subject of it is War, and the pity of War.

The Poetry is in the pity.

Yet these elegies are not to this generation,

This is in no sense consolatory.

They may be to the next.

All the poet can do to-day is to warn.

That is why the true Poets must be truthful.

If I thought the letter of this book would last,

I might have used proper names; but if the spirit of it survives Prussia—my ambition and those names will be content; for they will have achieved themselves fresher fields than Flanders.

===Chinese===
这本书写的不是英雄。英格兰的诗，还不足以谈论他们。它写的也不是战功或疆土，不是任何关于荣耀、荣誉、统治或权势的东西，

所以真正的诗人，必须真实。
