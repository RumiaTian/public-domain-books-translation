## V / 第五章

===Original===
Capwell Chapel﻿—it bore the pork merchant’s name as an eternal memorial to him﻿—was as impressive inside as out. The stained-glass windows had been made by a famous New York firm; the altar had been designed by an even more famous sculptor. The walls, quite improperly, were adorned with paintings of former presidents, but the largest painting of all﻿—it was fairly Gargantuan﻿—was of the pork merchant, a large, ruddy gentleman, whom the artist, a keen observer, had painted truly﻿—complacently porcine, benevolently smug.

===Chinese===
卡普韦尔礼拜堂（Capwell Chapel）——它冠着那位猪肉商的大名，作为他永久的纪念碑——堂内同堂外一样令人印象深刻。彩绘玻璃窗出自纽约一家著名的作坊；祭坛的设计者是一位名气更大的雕塑家。四壁张挂着历任校长的画像，这实在有失体统；而其中最大的一幅——那尺寸简直够得上高康大（Gargantuan）——画的正是那位猪肉商：一位身材魁伟、面色红润的绅士。画家是个敏锐的观察者，把他画得分毫不爽——一副怡然自得的猪相，一脸和善可亲的自负。

===Original===
The seniors and juniors sat in the nave, the sophomores on the right side of the transept, the freshmen on the left. Hugh gazed upward in awe at the dim recesses of the vaulted ceiling, at the ornately carved choir where gowned students were quietly seating themselves, at the colored light streaming through the beautiful windows, at the picture of the pork merchant. The chapel bells ceased tolling; rich, solemn tones swelled from the organ.

===Chinese===
四年级学生和三年级学生坐在中殿，二年级学生坐在翼廊右侧，新生们坐在左侧。休满怀敬畏地仰望拱顶幽深的穹窿，仰望雕饰华美的唱诗席——身着长袍的学生们正悄然入座——仰望美丽的窗子间倾泻而下的彩色光线，仰望那幅猪肉商的画像。礼拜堂的钟声停了；管风琴涌起浑厚庄严的和鸣。

===Original===
President Culver in cap and gown, his purple hood falling over his shoulders, entered followed by his faculty, also gowned and hooded. The students rose and remained standing until the president and faculty were seated. The organ sounded a final chord, and then the college chaplain rose and prayed﻿—very badly. He implored the Lord to look kindly “on these young men who have come from near and far to drink from this great fount of learning, this well of wisdom.”

===Chinese===
卡尔弗校长（President Culver）头戴方帽、身着长袍，紫色垂布披在肩头，缓步而入，身后跟着全体教员，同样长袍垂布。学生们起立恭候，直到校长和教员们落座。管风琴奏出最后一组和弦，随后校牧起身祷告——祷告得糟糕透了。他恳求主垂怜“这些从四面八方前来，就饮于这座伟大学问之泉、这一口智慧之井的年轻人”。

===Original===
The prayer over, the president addressed the students. He was a large, erect man with iron-gray hair and a rugged intelligent face. Although he was sixty years old, his body was vigorous and free from extra weight. He spoke slowly and impressively, choosing his words with care and enunciating them with great distinctness. His address was for the freshmen: he welcomed them to Sanford College, to its splendid traditions, its high ideals, its noble history. He spoke of the famous men it numbered among its sons, of the work they had done for America and the world, of the work he hoped future Sanford men, they, the freshmen, would some day do for America and the world. He mentioned briefly the boys from Sanford who had died in the World War “to make the world safe for democracy,” and he prayed that their sacrifice had not been in vain. Finally, he spoke of the chapel service, which the students were required to attend. He hoped that they would find inspiration in it, knowledge and strength. He assured them that the service would always be nonsectarian, that there would never be anything in it to offend anyone of any race, creed, or religion. With a last exhortation to the freshmen to make the most of their great opportunities, he ended with the announcement that they would rise and sing the sixty-seventh hymn.

===Chinese===
祷告既毕，校长向学生们致辞。他身材高大，腰背挺直，一头铁灰色的头发，一张粗犷而聪慧的脸。虽已年届六十，身子骨依然矫健，没有一两赘肉。他说话缓慢而有分量，字斟句酌，吐字极其清晰。这篇致辞是说给新生的：他欢迎他们来到桑福德学院（Sanford College），来到它辉煌的传统、它崇高的理想、它高贵的史册。他谈到学院门下养育的名人，谈到他们为美国和世界建立的功业，谈到他期望未来的桑福德人——也就是他们，这些新生——有朝一日能为美国和世界建立的功业。他简略提了提那些在世界大战中“为民主创造一个安全的世界”而捐躯的桑福德子弟，并祈愿他们的牺牲不曾付诸东流。最后，他讲到礼拜仪式，学生们一律必须出席。他希望他们能从中觅得启迪，觅得知识与力量。他向他们保证，礼拜仪式将永远不分教派，其中绝不会有任何开罪任何种族、任何信条、任何宗教之人的东西。临了，他最后一次劝勉新生们善用他们了不起的机遇，随即宣布全体起立，齐唱第六十七首赞美诗。

===Original===
Hugh was deeply impressed by the speech but disturbed by the students. From where he sat he got an excellent view of the juniors and seniors. The seniors, who sat in the front of the nave, seemed to be paying fairly good attention; but the juniors﻿—many of them, at least﻿—paid no attention at all. Some of them were munching apples, some doughnuts, and many of them were reading “The Sanford News,” the college’s daily paper. Some of the juniors talked during the president’s address, and once he noticed four of them doubled up as if overcome by laughter. To him the service was a beautiful and impressive occasion. He could not understand the conduct of the upper-classmen. It seemed, to put it mildly, irreverent.

===Chinese===
这篇演讲令休深深感动，可那些学生令他心神不宁。从他的座位望去，三年级生和四年级生一览无余。坐在中殿前排的四年级生，看模样听得还算用心；可三年级生——至少他们中间的一大半——压根儿就没在听。有几个在大嚼苹果，有几个嚼着炸面圈，还有许多人在读《桑福德新闻》，学院的日报。校长致辞的时候，几个三年级生自顾自讲话，有一回他还看见四个笑得直不起腰来，像乐不可支的样子。在他看来，这场仪式是一个美丽而庄严的时刻。他没法理解高年级学生的行径。说轻些，那未免太不虔敬了。

===Original===
Everyone, however, sang the doxology with great vigor, some of the boys lifting up a “whisky” tenor that made the chapel ring, and to which Hugh happily added his own clear tenor. The benediction was pronounced by the chaplain, the seniors marched out slowly in twos, while the other students and the faculty stood in their places; then the president, followed by the faculty, passed out of the great doors. When the back of the last faculty gown had disappeared, the underclassmen broke for the door, pushing each other aside, swearing when a toe was stepped on, yelling to each other, some of them joyously chanting the doxology. Hugh was caught in the rush and carried along with the mob, feeling ashamed and distressed; this was no way to leave a church.

===Chinese===
不过，唱起《三一颂》来人人都劲头十足，有几个男孩拔起一股“威士忌”男高音，震得满堂嗡嗡回响，休也欢欢喜喜地把自己清亮的男高音加了进去。校牧念了祝福文，四年级生两人一排，缓缓鱼贯而出，其余学生和教员们则站在原处不动；随后校长领头，教员们随后，从大门里走了出去。最后一件学袍的背影消失之后，低年级学生便一齐扑向门口，你推我搡，踩了脚就骂，彼此大呼小叫，有几个还兴高采烈地哼唱着《三一颂》。休被卷进这股人流，身不由己地随众而去，又是羞愧又是难过；离开教堂可不该是这个样子。

===Original===
Once outside, however, he had no time to think of the chapel service; he had five minutes in which to get to his first class, and the building was across the campus, a good two minutes’ walk. He patted his cap to be sure that it was firmly on the back of his head, clutched his notebook, and ran as hard as he could go, the strolling upper-classmen, whom he passed at top speed, grinning after him in tolerant amusement.

===Chinese===
不过一到外面，他就顾不上想礼拜仪式了；他只有五分钟赶到第一堂课，而那座楼在校园另一头，足足要走两分钟。他拍了拍帽子，确信它牢牢扣在后脑勺上，攥紧笔记本，撒腿飞奔，能跑多快跑多快。那些闲庭信步的高年级学生被他一阵风似的一一掠过，望着他的背影咧嘴直乐，又觉有趣，又怀宽容。

===Original===
Hugh was the first one in the classroom and wondered in a moment of panic if he was in the right place. He sat down dubiously and looked at his watch. Four minutes left. He would wait two, and then if nobody came he would﻿—he gasped; he couldn’t imagine what he would do. How could he find the right classroom? Maybe his class didn’t come at this hour at all. Suppose he and Carl had made a mistake. If they had, his whole schedule was probably wrong. “Oh, golly,” he thought, feeling pitifully weak, “won’t that be hell? What can I do?”

===Chinese===

===Original===

Hugh had one terrifying experience before he finally got settled to his work. It occurred the second day of classes. He was comfortably seated in what he thought was his English class﻿—he had come in just as the bell rang﻿—when the instructor announced that it was a class in French. What was he to do? What would the instructor do if he got up and left the room? What would happen if he didn’t report at his English class? What would happen to him for coming into his English class late? These questions staggered his mind. He was afraid to stay in the French class. Cautiously he got up and began to tiptoe to the door.

===Chinese===
在终于安下心来念书之前，休还经历过一场惊魂。那发生在上课的第二天。他舒舒服服地坐在他以为是英文课的教室里——他恰好踏着铃声进门——任课教师却宣布这是一堂法文课。他该怎么办？要是他起身走出教室，教师会怎样？要是他不去英文课报到，会怎样？要是他上英文课迟到了，又会怎样？这些问题把他弄得晕头转向。他不敢留在法文课上，小心翼翼地站起身，踮着脚尖朝门口挪去。

===Original===
“Wrong room?” the instructor asked pleasantly.

===Chinese===
“走错教室了？”教师和颜悦色地问。

===Original===
Hugh flushed. “Yes, sir.” He stopped dead still, not knowing what to do next.

===Chinese===
休涨红了脸。“是的，先生。”他当场钉住，不知道下一步如何是好。

===Original===
He was a typical rattled freshman, and the class, which was composed of sophomores, laughed. Hugh, angry and humiliated, started for the door, but the instructor held up a hand that silenced the class; then he motioned for Hugh to come to his desk.

===Chinese===
他是那种典型的慌了神的新生；满教室二年级生哄堂大笑。休又气又臊，抬脚便朝门口走，可教师举起一只手，教室顿时鸦雀无声；随后他示意休到他的讲台跟前去。

===Original===
“What class are you looking for?”

===Chinese===
“你要找哪门课的教室？”

===Original===
“English One, sir, Section Seven.” He held out his schedule card, reassured by the instructor’s kindly manner.

===Chinese===
“英语一（English One），先生，第七班。”他递出选课卡，教师和善的态度让他安下心来。

===Original===
The instructor looked at the card and then consulted a printed schedule.

===Chinese===
教师看了看卡片，又查了一张印好的课表。

===Original===
“Oh,” he said, “your adviser made a mistake. He got you into the wrong group list. You belong in Sanders Six.”

===Chinese===
“哦，”他说，“你的导师出了个错。他把你填进了错的分组名单。你该在桑德斯楼六号（Sanders Six）。”

===Original===
“Thank you, sir.” Hugh spoke so softly that the waiting class did not hear him, but the instructor smiled at the intensity of his thanks. As he left the room, he knew that everyone was looking at him; his legs felt as if they were made of wood. It wasn’t until he had closed the door that his knee-joints worked naturally. But the worst was still ahead of him. He had to go to his English class in Sanders 6. He ran across the campus, his heart beating wildly, his hands desperately clenched. When he reached Sanders 6, he found three other freshmen grouped before the door.

===Chinese===
“谢谢您，先生。”休说得很轻，等候上课的全班都没有听见；可从他感激之情的深重上，教师微笑了。走出教室时，他知道人人都在看他；两条腿像木头做的。直到掩上门，他的膝关节才恢复自然。但最难堪的还在前头。他得去桑德斯楼 6 号上英文课。他狂奔着穿过校园，心怦怦乱跳，双手死死攥紧。赶到桑德斯楼 6 号，他发现门口已经聚着另外三个新生。

===Original===
“Is this English One, Section Seven?” one asked tremulously.

===Chinese===
“这儿是英语一第七班吗？”其中一个声音发颤地问。

===Original===
“I think so,” whispered the second. “Do you know?” he asked, turning to Hugh.

===Chinese===
“我想是，”第二个压低嗓音说。“你知道么？”他转向休问。

===Original===
“Yes; I am almost sure.”

===Chinese===
“知道；我差不多能肯定。”

===Original===
They stood there looking at each other, no one quite daring to enter Sanders 6, no one quite daring to leave. Suddenly the front door of the building slammed. A bareheaded youth rushed up the stairs. He was a repeater; that is, a man who had failed the course the preceding year and was taking it over again. He brushed by the scared freshmen, opened the door, and strode into Sanders 6, closing the door behind him.

===Chinese===
他们站在那里面面相觑，谁也不敢太冒失地进桑德斯楼 6 号，谁也不敢抬腿就走。突然，楼的前门砰的一响。一个光着脑袋的青年蹬蹬蹬冲上楼梯。他是个重修生；也就是说，是头一年这门课没及格、这年再修一遍的人。他从吓坏了的新生们身边擦身而过，推开门，大步跨进桑德斯楼 6 号，随手把门带上了。

===Original===
The freshmen looked at each other, and then the one nearest the door opened it. The four of them filed in silently.

===Chinese===
新生们面面相觑，随后离门最近的那个把门推开。四人鱼贯而入，鸦雀无声。

===Original===
The class looked up. “Sit in the back of the room,” said the instructor.

===Chinese===
全班抬起头来。“坐到教室后排去，”教师说。

===Original===
And that was all there was to that. In his senior year Hugh remembered the incident and wondered at his terror. He tried to remember why he had been so badly frightened. He couldn’t; there didn’t seem to be any reason at all.

===Chinese===
事情就这么完了，再无下文。到了四年级，休想起这桩事，诧异于自己当初的惊惶。他试图想起自己何以吓成那样。想不起来了；那惊惶仿佛根本没有任何来由。
