## XXXVI Once Upon a Time, When the Days Were Long and Hot / 第36章 从前，日子漫长而炎热

===Original===
Next day was Sunday﻿—a blazing one it was too. I proposed that in the afternoon some of us should go to church. Father sat upon the idea as a mad one. Walk two miles in such heat for nothing! as walk we would be compelled to do, horseflesh being too precious in such a drought to fritter it away in idle jaunts. Surprising to say, however, Harold, who never walked anywhere when he could get any sort of a horse, uttered a wish to go. Accordingly, when the midday dinner was over, he, Stanley, and I set out. Going to church was quite the event of the week to the residents around Possum Gully. It was a small Dissenting chapel, where a layman ungrammatically held forth at 3 p.m. every Sunday; but the congregation was composed of all denominations, who attended more for the sitting about on logs outside, and yarning about the price of butter, the continuance of the drought, and the latest gossip, before and after the service, than for the service itself.

===Chinese===
第二天是星期天——而且是个火烧火燎的大热天。我提议下午我们几个人去做礼拜。父亲一听便把这主意断为疯念：这样的暑天，白白走上两英里！何况我们非靠两条腿走不可——大旱年头马力金贵，犯不上为闲逛糟蹋牲口。然而说来出奇，哈罗德——那个但凡弄得到一匹马、再蹩脚也决不肯走路的人——竟说他想去。于是正午的饭一吃完，他便同斯坦利和我一道上了路。在袋熊谷左近的居民看来，上教堂实在是当周的一件大事。那是一座小小的非国教礼拜堂，每逢星期日下午三点，由一位平信徒文法不通地宣讲一番；可会众各宗各派的人都有，他们上这儿来，与其说是为了礼拜本身，毋宁说是为了礼拜前后能在外头的圆木上坐一坐，讲讲黄油的价钱、旱情还要拖多久，再交换些最新的闲话。

===Original===
I knew the appearance of Harold Beecham would make quite a miniature sensation, and form food for no end of conjecture and chatter. In any company he was a distinguished-looking man, and particularly so among these hard-worked farmer-selectors, on whose careworn features the cruel effects of the drought were leaving additional lines of worry. I felt proud of my quondam sweetheart. There was an unconscious air of physical lordliness about him, and he looked such a swell﻿—not the black-clothed, clean-shaved, great display of white collar-and-cuffs swell appertaining to the office and city street, but of the easy sunburnt squatter type of swelldom, redolent of the sun, the saddle, the wide open country﻿—a man who is a man, utterly free from the least suspicion of effeminacy, and capable of earning his bread by the sweat of his brow﻿—with an arm ready and willing to save in an accident.

===Chinese===
我知道哈罗德·比彻姆这一露面，准会引起一场小小的轰动，招来没完没了的揣测与嚼舌。无论在什么人堆里，他都是个仪表堂堂的人物，在这群操劳过度的选地农中间尤其如此——旱灾的酷烈，正在他们愁苦的面容上添刻一道道忧虑的纹路。我为昔日的情郎感到骄傲。他周身有一种不自觉的体魄上的王者气派，看上去好一派时髦人物的丰采——不是那种黑呢衣装、脸刮得精光、白领白袖口大张旗鼓、属于写字楼与通衢大街一路的时髦，而是舒展从容、晒得黢黑的大牧场主型时髦，浑身散发着阳光、马鞍和辽阔旷野的气息——一个顶天立地的男子汉，全然不带一丝娘娘腔的嫌疑，凭额头的汗水挣得自己的面包——还有一条臂膀，遇险时随时乐意出手相救。

===Original===

===Chinese===

===Chinese===

===Original===

===Chinese===
“哎呀，才不是呢！我引见给你。”

===Original===
I did so, and watched him as they made remarks about the heat and drought. There was nothing of the cad or snob about him, and his short season of adversity had rubbed all the little crudities off his character, leaving him a man that the majority of both sexes would admire: women for his bigness, his gentleness, his fine brown moustache﻿—and for his wealth; men, because he was a manly fellow.

===Original===
