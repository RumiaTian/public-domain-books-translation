## V Disjointed Sketches and Grumbles / 第5章 零散速写与牢骚

===Original===
It was my duty to “rare the poddies.” This is the most godless occupation in which it has been my lot to engage. I did a great amount of thinking while feeding them﻿—for, by the way, I am afflicted with the power of thought, which is a heavy curse. The less a person thinks and inquires regarding the why and the wherefore and the justice of things, when dragging along through life, the happier it is for him, and doubly, trebly so, for her.
===Chinese===
我的差事是“拉扯手喂的牛犊”。在我命里干过的行当当中，这是顶顶不敬神的一种。喂牛的时候我想了许许多多的心思——顺便说一句，我天生受着思想力的折磨，那是一桩沉重的诅咒。一个人拖着日子在世上熬，对万事万物的缘由、所以然与公道想得越少、问得越少，他就活得越快活；换成她，还要加倍、加三倍地快活。

===Original===
Poor little calves! Slaves to the greed of man! Bereft of the mothers with which Nature has provided them, and compelled to exist on milk from the separator, often thick, sour, and icy cold.
===Chinese===
可怜的小牛犊！做了人类贪心的奴隶！被夺走了老天爷给它们备下的母亲，被迫靠分离机里出来的奶过活——那奶常常又稠、又酸、又冰。

===Original===
Besides the milking I did, before I went to school every morning, for which I had to prepare myself and the younger children, and to which we had to walk two miles, I had to feed thirty calves and wash the breakfast dishes. On returning from school in the afternoon, often in a state of exhaustion from walking in the blazing sun, I had the same duties over again, and in addition boots to clean and home lessons to prepare for the morrow. I had to relinquish my piano practice for want of time.
===Chinese===
除了每天清早上学前要挤的奶——上学得先把我自己和弟弟妹妹们拾掇停当，还得走上两英里——我还要喂三十头牛犊，洗早饭的碗碟。下午放学回来，往往叫毒日头晒得脱了力，同一套差事还得从头再来一遍，外加上擦靴子、预备第二天的功课。练琴的事，只好因为腾不出工夫撂下了。

===Original===
Ah, those short, short nights of rest and long, long days of toil! It seems to me that dairying means slavery in the hands of poor people who cannot afford hired labour. I am not writing of dairy-farming, the genteel and artistic profession as eulogized in leading articles of agricultural newspapers and as taught in agricultural colleges. I am depicting practical dairying as I have lived it, and seen it lived, by dozens of families around me.
===Chinese===
啊，那些短短、短短的安歇之夜，那些长长、长长的劳苦白昼！在我看来，奶牛业在雇不起帮工的穷人家手里，就意味着奴役。我写的可不是农业报纸社论里颂扬的、农学院里讲授的那种养奶牛的雅致艺术行当。我描画的是实打实的奶牛营生——我自己亲身熬过的，也是眼见周围几十户人家熬着的那种。

===Original===
It takes a great deal of work to produce even one pound of butter fit for market. At the time I mention it was 3d. and 4d. per lb., so it was much work and small pay. It was slaving and delving from morning till night﻿—Sundays, weekdays, and holidays, all alike were workdays to us.
===Chinese===
哪怕只做出一磅拿得上市场的黄油，也得下天大的工夫。我说的那个年头，黄油一磅才卖三便士、四便士，所以是活计重、进项薄。从早到晚当牛做马、刨地挖土——礼拜天、平常日、节假日，对我们来说一概都是做工的日子。

===Original===
Hard graft is a great leveller. Household drudgery, woodcutting, milking, and gardening soon roughen the hands and dim the outside polish. When the body is wearied with much toil the desire to cultivate the mind, or the cultivation it has already received, is gradually wiped out. Thus it was with my parents. They had dropped from swelldom to peasantism. They were among and of the peasantry. None of their former acquaintances came within their circle now, for the iron ungodly hand of class distinction has settled surely down upon Australian society﻿—Australia’s democracy is only a tradition of the past.
===Chinese===
苦活重役是个顶了不起的扯平器。家务苦差、砍柴、挤奶、侍弄园子，很快就磨粗了手，磨暗了外表那层光鲜。身子叫重活累乏了，修养心灵的愿望——连同心灵已经受过的一点教养——便渐渐被磨蚀干净。我的父母就是这样。他们从阔气人家跌进了庄稼人的行列。他们混在农民之中，也成了农民中的一员。旧日的相识如今没有一个踏进他们的圈子，因为阶级分野那只铁铸的、无法无天的手，已经稳稳压上了澳大利亚社会——澳大利亚的民主，不过是往昔的一段传说罢了。

===Original===
I say naught against the lower life. The peasantry are the bulwarks of every nation. The life of a peasant is, to a peasant who is a peasant with a peasant’s soul, when times are good and when seasons smile, a grand life. It is honest, clean, and wholesome. But the life of a peasant to me is purgatory. Those around me worked from morning till night and then enjoyed their well-earned sleep. They had but two states of existence﻿—work and sleep.
===Chinese===
我对这种下层生活并无半句贬词。农民是每一个国家的柱石。对于一个揣着农民灵魂的庄稼人，赶上年景好、四季和顺的时节，庄稼日子是很了不起的日子——诚实、干净、康健。可庄稼日子于我却是炼狱。我周围的人从早干到晚，然后享受那挣来的酣睡。他们的存在只有两种状态——干活，睡觉。

===Original===
There was a third part in me which cried out to be fed. I longed for the arts. Music was a passion with me. I borrowed every book in the neighbourhood and stole hours from rest to read them. This told upon me and made my physical burdens harder for me than for other children of my years around me. That third was the strongest part of me. In it I lived a dream-life with writers, artists, and musicians. Hope, sweet, cruel, delusive Hope, whispered in my ear that life was long with much by and by, and in that by and by my dream-life would be real. So on I went with that gleaming lake in the distance beckoning me to come and sail on its silver waters, and Inexperience, conceited, blind Inexperience, failing to show the impassable pit between it and me.
===Chinese===
我身体里还有第三个部分，哭着喊着要吃食。我渴慕艺术。音乐于我是一场痴狂。我把左近能借到的书借了个遍，又从歇息里偷出钟点来读。这在我身上见了效，叫我身子的担子，比周围同我一般年纪的孩子更沉。那第三部分是我身上顶强壮的部分。在它里头，我同作家、画家、音乐家们过着一种梦里的生活。希望，甜美、残忍、骗人的希望，在我耳边低语，说日子还长，往后有的是将来，在那将来里，我的梦里的生活会变成真的。我便这样一路走下去，远处那片波光潋滟的湖招着手，唤我去它银色的水上泛舟；而“无知”——自负的、瞎眼的无知——却不肯指给我看，那湖与我之间横着一道跨不过去的深坑。

===Original===
To return to the dairying.
===Chinese===
还是回到奶牛营生上来吧。

===Original===
Old and young alike we earned our scant livelihood by the heavy sweat of our brows. Still, we *did* gain an honest living. We were not ashamed to look day in the face, and fought our way against all odds with the stubborn independence of our British ancestors. But when 1894 went out without rain, and ’95, hot, dry, pitiless ’95, succeeded it, there came a time when it was impossible to make a living.
===Chinese===
老老小小，我们全凭额头上沉甸甸的汗水，挣得一点微薄的嚼裹。话虽如此，我们*实实在在*挣的是一份清白的生计。我们问心无愧，敢抬头正视白日，凭着不列颠祖先传下的倔强自立，百般艰难里杀出一条活路。可是 1894 年没等来一场雨就过完了，接着来的是 95 年——火热、干燥、无情的 95 年——终于到了生计再也挣不出来的那一天。

===Original===
The scorching furnace-breath winds shrivelled every blade of grass, dust and the moan of starving stock filled the air, vegetables became a thing of the past. The calves I had reared died one by one, and the cows followed in their footsteps.
===Chinese===
火炉喷息一般的灼风把每一茎草都烤得枯卷，尘土和饿牲口的哀号充满了空气，青菜成了一件旧事。我一手拉扯大的牛犊一头接一头地死了，奶牛也跟着它们的脚印去了。

===Original===
I had left school then, and my mother and father and I spent the days in lifting our cows. When our strength proved inadequate, the help of neighbours had to be called in, and father would give his services in return. Only a few of our more well-to-do neighbours had been able to send their stock away, or had any better place to which to transfer them. The majority of them were in as tight a plight as ourselves. This cow-lifting became quite a trade, the whole day being spent in it and in discussing the bad prospect ahead if the drought continued.
===Chinese===
那时我已离开了学堂，父亲、母亲和我成天把日子花在扶牛上。自家的力气不够了，就得请邻居来搭手，父亲便也出力去回报人家。只有几户家道稍殷实的邻舍，才来得及把牲口送走，或是另有较好地方可以转移。大多数人家的境况都同我们一样窘。扶牛竟成了一门正经行当，一整天搭进去不算，还要议论旱灾再这么拖下去，前头的前景可怎么好。

===Original===
Many an extra line of care furrowed the brows of the disheartened bushmen then. Not only was their living taken from them by the drought, but there is nothing more heartrending than to have poor beasts, especially dairy cows, so familiar, valued, and loved, pleading for food day after day in their piteous dumb way when one has it not to give.
===Chinese===
那时候，多少灰了心的丛林汉子，眉头上又添了一道一道的愁纹。旱灾不光夺走了他们的生计；更叫人心碎欲裂的是，眼看着那些可怜的牲口——尤其是奶牛，那样熟悉、那样贵重、那样叫人疼爱的奶牛——一天又一天，用它们那可怜的、说不出话的法子讨食吃，而你手里偏偏没有可以给的。

===Original===
We shore ourselves of all but the bare necessaries of life, but even they for a family of ten are considerable, and it was a mighty tussle to get both ends within cover of meeting. We felt the full force of the heavy hand of poverty﻿—the most stinging kind of poverty too, that which still holds up its head and keeps an outside appearance. Far more grinding is this than the poverty inherited from generations which is not ashamed of itself, and has not as an accompaniment the wounded pride and humiliation which attacked us.
===Chinese===
我们把家计剪剃得只剩光板板的活命必需，可就连这些，搁在十口之家也是一笔大数，要叫两头碰得上头，实在是一场天大的角力。贫穷那只沉沉的手，我们结结实实领教了——还是顶扎人的那一种穷：明明穷着，却还昂着头，还撑着外头那层体面。这远比祖祖辈辈传下来的穷更磨人——那种穷自己并不害臊，也不曾带着刺伤我们自尊的那种屈辱做陪嫁。

===Original===
Some there are who argue that poverty does not mean unhappiness. Let those try what it is to be destitute of even one companionable friend, what it means to be forced to exist in an alien sphere of society, what it is like to be unable to afford a stamp to write to a friend; let them long as passionately as I have longed for reading and music, and be unable to procure it because of poverty; let poverty force them into doing work against which every fibre of their being revolts, as it has forced me, and then see if their lives will be happy.
===Chinese===
有些人偏说，穷并不等于不快活。那就叫他们尝尝，连一个说得来的朋友也没有，是个什么滋味；尝尝被逼着活在社会上一个格格不入的圈层里，算个什么意思；尝尝连买一张邮票给朋友写信的钱都拿不出，是副什么光景。叫他们像我一样，发疯似地渴着书读、渴着音乐，却因为穷弄不到手；叫穷逼着他们去干那种浑身上下每一根纤维都起而反抗的活计——像它逼我这样——然后再看看，他们的日子还快活不快活。

===Original===
My school life had been dull and uneventful. The one incident of any note had been the day that the teacher, better known as old Harris, “stood up” to the inspector. The latter was a precise, collar-and-cuffs sort of little man. He gave one the impression of having all his ideas on the subjects he thought worthy of attention carefully culled and packed in his brainpan, and neatly labelled, so that he might without fluster pounce upon any of them at a moment’s warning. He was gentlemanly and respectable, and discharged his duties punctiliously in a manner reflecting credit on himself and his position, but, comparing the mind of a philanthropist to the Murrumbidgee in breadth, his, in comparison, might be likened to the flow of a bucket of water in a dray-rut.
===Chinese===
我的学堂生涯乏味得很，没出过一桩事。唯一算得上事件的，便是那位教师——大家更常叫他老哈里斯（old Harris）——跟督学“顶牛”的那一天。督学是个一丝不苟、讲究硬领硬袖口的小个子男人。他给人的印象是：凡是他认为值得留意的问题，见解都早已细细拣净，码进脑壳，还齐齐整整贴好了标签，只消一声招呼，便可不慌不忙地扑上去取出任何一条。他有绅士气派，体面规矩，办事谨严，为他自己和他的职位增光；只是，若把一位慈善家的胸怀比作马兰比吉河（Murrumbidgee）的宽阔，那么他的胸怀比起来，只好比一桶水在大车辙里淌过的那点水流。

===Chinese===

### A Drought Idyll / 旱灾牧歌

===Original===
“Sybylla, what are you doing? Where is your mother?”
===Chinese===
“西比拉，你在干什么？你妈呢？”

===Original===
“I’m ironing. Mother’s down at the fowl-house seeing after some chickens. What do you want?”
===Chinese===
“我在熨衣裳。妈在下面鸡舍里照看小鸡呢。你有什么事？”

===Original===
It was my father who addressed me. Time, 2 o’clock p.m. Thermometer hung in the shade of the veranda registering 105½ degrees.
===Chinese===
同我说话的是父亲。时间，下午两点。游廊荫里挂着的温度计，指着 105½ 度。

===Original===
“I see Blackshaw coming across the flat. Call your mother. You bring the leg-ropes﻿—I’ve got the dogleg. Come at once; we’ll give the cows another lift. Poor devils﻿—might as well knock ’em on the head at once, but there might be rain next moon. This drought can’t last forever.”
===Chinese===
“我瞧见布莱克肖穿过平地过来了。去叫你妈。你去拿腿绳——狗腿架我带了。赶紧来；咱们再把奶牛扶一回。可怜的畜生——倒不如趁早一棒敲死了爽快，不过下个月兴许有雨。这旱灾总不能没完没了。”

===Original===
I called mother, got the leg-ropes, and set off, pulling my sunbonnet closely over my face to protect my eyes from the dust which was driving from the west in blinding clouds. The dogleg to which father had referred was three poles about eight or ten feet long, strapped together so they could be stood up. It was an arrangement father had devised to facilitate our labour in lifting the cows. A fourth and longer pole was placed across the fork formed by the three, and to one end of this were tied a couple of leg-ropes, after being placed round the beast, one beneath the flank and one around the girth. On the other end of this pole we would put our weight while one man would lift with the tail and another with the horns. New-chum cows would sulk, and we would have great work with them; but those used to the performance would help themselves, and up they’d go as nice as a daisy. The only art needed was to draw the pole back quickly before the cows could move, or the leg-ropes would pull them over again.
===Chinese===
我叫了母亲，拿上腿绳便动身，把遮阳帽紧紧拉下来罩住脸，好护着眼睛，躲那从西边一阵阵卷来的、糊得人睁不开眼的尘土。父亲说的那副狗腿架，是三根八到十英尺长的杆子，捆在一起，能立得起来。那是父亲琢磨出来的一个装置，为的是扶牛省些力气。第四根更长的杆子横担在三根杆子形成的叉口上，等两条腿绳兜上了牛身——一条兜在胁腹底下，一条兜在肚围上——便把它们拴在这杆子的一头。我们全体再压上杆子的另一头，一个人拽尾巴，一个人掰牛角。初来乍到的生牛会犯犟闹脾气，我们得费老鼻子劲；可惯了这一套的牛自己会使劲，起来得利利索索、漂漂亮亮。唯一要紧的手艺，是趁牛还没挪步，赶紧把杆子抽回来，不然腿绳又会把它们拽倒。

===Original===
On this afternoon we had six cows to lift. We struggled manfully, and got five on their feet, and then proceeded to where the last one was lying, back downwards, on a shadeless stony spot on the side of a hill. The men slewed her round by the tail, while mother and I fixed the dogleg and adjusted the ropes. We got the cow up, but the poor beast was so weak and knocked about that she immediately fell down again. We resolved to let her have a few minutes’ spell before making another attempt at lifting. There was not a blade of grass to be seen, and the ground was too dusty to sit on.
