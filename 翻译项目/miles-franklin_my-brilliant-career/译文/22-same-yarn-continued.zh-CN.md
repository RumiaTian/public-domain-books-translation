## XX Same Yarn (Continued) / 第20章 故事续篇

===Original===
Every station hand from Five-Bob, male and female, had gone to the ball at Yabtree. Harold and his overseer had to attend to the horses, while the jackeroos started a fire in the kitchen, opened windows and doors which had been locked all day, and saw to the comfort of the gentlemen guests.

===Chinese===
五博丘（Five-Bob）的牧场人手，不论男女，全都上亚布特里（Yabtree）赴舞会去了。哈罗德（Harold）只得同他的监工（overseer）亲自照料马匹，几个牧场学徒（jackeroo）则在厨房里生起火，打开闩了一整天的门窗，把男宾们的舒妥一一打点停当。

===Original===
Aunt Helen and I shared the one bedroom. As we had not fresh dresses to put on we had to make the best of our present toilet.

===Chinese===
海伦姨妈（aunt Helen）和我合住同一间卧室。我们没有新衣裳可换，只好把手头这副妆束将就着拾掇到最好。

===Original===
I unplaited my hair (shook the dust out of it) and wore it flowing. We washed and dusted ourselves, and wore as adornment﻿—roses. Crimson and cream roses paid the penalty of peeping in the window. Aunt Helen plucked some of them, which she put in my hair and belt, and pinned carefully at my throat, and then we were ready. Miss Beecham assured us there was nothing to be done, as the maids had set the table and prepared the viands for a cold meal before leaving in the morning, so we proceeded to the drawing-room to await the arrival of the other visitors. They soon made their appearance. First, two stout old squatters with big laughs and bigger corporations, then Miss Augusta Beecham, next Joe Archer the overseer, and the two other jackeroos. After these appeared a couple of governesses, Mr., Mrs., and Miss Benson, a clergyman, an auctioneer, a young friend of Harold’s from Cootamundra, a horse-buyer, a wool-classer, Miss Sarah Beecham, and then Miss Derrick brought herself and her dress in with great style and airs. She was garbed in a sea-green silk, and had jewellery on her neck, arms, and hair. Her self-confident mien was suggestive of the conquest of many masculine hearts. She was a big handsome woman. Beside her, I in my crushed white muslin dress was as overshadowed as a little white handkerchief would be in comparison to a gorgeous shawl heavily wrought in silks and velvet. She was given the best seat as though she were a princess. She sat down with great indifference, twirled a bracelet round her wrist, languidly opened her fan, and closed her eyes as she wafted it slowly to and fro.

===Chinese===
我解开辫子（抖净发间的尘土），让一头长发披泻而下。我们洗了洗，掸了掸尘，拿玫瑰作了装饰——绯红的、乳白的玫瑰，只因探进窗来窥望，便付出了代价。海伦姨妈摘下几朵，簪进我的头发，插上我的腰带，又细心地别在我喉前，我们便妆扮停当了。比彻姆小姐叫我们不必操劳：女仆们清晨动身前已摆好餐桌、备下冷餐酒馔，于是我们移步客厅，静候其余宾客到来。他们很快就到了。先来了两位上了年纪的大牧场主（squatter），身躯粗壮，笑声洪亮，肚皮更洪亮；接着是奥古斯塔·比彻姆小姐（Miss Augusta Beecham），随后是监工乔·阿彻（Joe Archer）和另外两名牧场学徒；这批人之后，又来了两位家庭女教师，本森先生、本森太太和本森小姐（Mr., Mrs., and Miss Benson）一家，一位牧师，一位拍卖商，哈罗德一位从库塔曼德拉（Cootamundra）来的年轻朋友，一位贩马客，一位羊毛分级员，莎拉·比彻姆小姐（Miss Sarah Beecham）；最后，德里克小姐（Miss Derrick）连人带那身衣裳，派头十足、架子十足地款款而入。她一身海绿色绸衫，颈上、臂上、发间都缀着珠光宝气。她那副自信满满的气派，叫人想到被她攻陷的男人心不知凡几。她是个高大俊美的妇人。在她旁边，我裹着一身揉皱的白细布裙，就像一方小小的白手帕，同一条满绣丝绒、华丽厚重的披巾摆在了一处，那样黯然失色。她得了上座，俨然一位公主。她大模大样地落座，捻着手腕上的镯子，懒洋洋地打开折扇，阖上双眼，把扇儿缓缓地摇来摇去。

===Original===
“By Jove, isn’t she a splendid creature?” enthusiastically whispered a gentleman sitting beside me.

===Chinese===
“老天作证，她岂不是个光彩照人的尤物？”坐在我身旁的一位先生热情地悄声说。

===Original===
I looked at her critically. She was very big, and in a bony stiff way was much developed in figure. She had a nice big nose, and a long well-shaped face, a thin straight mouth, and empty light eyes. If my attention had not been called to her I would not have noticed her one way or the other, but being pointed to as a beauty, I weighed her according to my idea of facial charm, and pronounced her one of the most insipid-looking people I had set eyes upon.

===Chinese===
我挑剔地打量她。她生得十分高大，一身嶙峋僵直的骨架，身段却发育得颇为可观。她有一只端正的大鼻子，一张修长周正的脸，一张薄而直的嘴，一双空空洞洞的浅色眼睛。若不是有人特地指给我看，我是好是歹根本不会留意她；可既被人指为美人，我便拿自己对姿色的那套标准来掂量她，断她是平生所见的面孔里最索然寡味的一张。

===Original===
She was the kind of woman with whom men become much infatuated. She would never make a fool of herself by letting her emotions run away with her, because she had no emotions, but lived in a sea of unruffled self-consciousness and self-confidence. Any man would be proud to introduce her as his wife to his friends whom he had brought home to dinner. She would adorn the head of his table. She would never worry him with silly ideas. She would never act with impropriety. She would never become a companion to her husband. Bah, a man does not want his wife to be a companion! There were myths and fables in the old day; so there are now. The story that men like a companion as well as a wife is an up-to-date one.

===Chinese===
她正是那种男人会为之神魂颠倒的女人。她绝不会任感情牵着跑、闹出丢人现眼的蠢事，因为她压根儿没有感情，只活在一片波澜不兴的自我意识与自我信心里。随便哪个男人把这样的妻子引见给请到家吃饭的朋友，都与有荣焉。她装点得起他餐桌的首席。她绝不用傻念头去烦扰他。她绝无失礼的举止。她也绝不会成为丈夫的知己。呸，男人根本不要妻子做知己！古时有神话寓言，如今照样有。说男人除妻子外还喜欢个知己，不过是个时新的传说罢了。

===Original===
This train of thought was interrupted by our host, who appeared in the doorway, clad from sole to neck in white. We steered for the dining-room﻿—twenty-two all told﻿—thirteen men and nine representatives of the other sex.

===Chinese===
这串思绪被我们的东道打断了。他出现在门口，从脚板到脖颈裹着一身雪白。我们朝饭厅鱼贯而去——男女合计二十二位——男人十三个，另一性的代表九位。

===Original===
Aunt Helen got one seat of honour near the head of the table and Miss Derrick another. I drifted to the foot among the unimportant younger fry, where we had no end of fun and idle chatter. We had to wait on ourselves, and as all formality was dispensed with, it was something like a picnic.

===Chinese===
海伦姨妈得了靠近上首的一个荣座，德里克小姐得了另一个。我随波逐流，漂到桌子末尾，混在一群无足轻重的毛孩子中间——在那儿，我们的耍笑闲磕牙没了完。我们得自己伺候自己，又全免了客套，倒像是一场野餐。

===Original===
The heat was excessive. Every window and door were open, and the balmy, almost imperceptible, zephyrs which faintly rustled the curtains and kissed our perspiration-beaded brows were rich with many scents from the wide old flower-garden, which, despite the drought, brought forth a wealth of blossom.

===Chinese===
暑气逼人。门窗尽数敞着，一缕缕温软得几乎察觉不出的微风，轻轻拂响帘幔，吻着我们沁满汗珠的额头，饱含着那座开阔的老花园里飘来的种种香气——虽是大旱年头，它依旧捧出满园繁花。

===Original===
When done eating we had to wash the dishes. Such a scamper ensued back and forwards to the kitchen, which rang with noise, and merriment. Everyone was helping, hindering, laughing, joking, teasing, and brimming over with fun and enjoyment. When we had completed this task, dancing was proposed. Some of the elderly and more sensible people said it was too hot, but all the young folks did not care a rap for the temperature. Harold had no objections, Miss Derrick was agreeable, Miss Benson announced herself ready and willing, and Joe Archer said he was “leppin’ ” to begin, so we adjourned to the dancing-room and commenced operations.

===Chinese===
吃罢饭，我们还得洗盘子。于是乎一阵大折腾，人人在厨房里跑进跑出，厨房里喧声震耳，笑闹连天。人人都在帮忙，也都在帮倒忙，笑着、逗着、闹着，欢畅得从心里往外溢。这桩差事收了场，便有人提议跳舞。几位年长些、明白些的说天太热，可年轻人对这温度连一个子儿也不放在心上。哈罗德没有异议，德里克小姐深表赞同，本森小姐宣称自己随时奉候、满心愿意，乔·阿彻说他“急得脚底板直蹦跶”，恨不能立刻开场，于是我们移进舞厅，拉开了阵仗。

===Original===
I played the piano for the first quadrille, and aunt Helen for the second dance. It was most enjoyable. There was a table at one end of the room on which was any amount of cherries, lollies, cake, dainties, beers, syrups, and glasses, where all could regale themselves without ceremony or bother every time the inclination seized them. Several doors and windows of the long room opened into the garden, and, provided one had no fear of snakes, it was delightful to walk amid the flowers and cool oneself between dances.

===Chinese===
头一支四方舞由我弹琴，第二支舞归海伦姨妈弹。实在是快活极了。屋子一头摆着一张桌子，上头的樱桃、糖果、糕点、各色细巧吃食、啤酒、果子露、杯盏，应有尽有；谁兴头上来，随时可以不拘礼数、不费周章地自取自享。长厅的好几道门窗都开向花园，只要不怕蛇，舞与舞之间到花丛里走走、凉快凉快，再可心不过。

===Original===
A little exertion on such a night made us very hot. After the third dance the two old squatters, the horse-buyer, the clergyman, and Mr. Benson disappeared. Judging from the hilarity of their demeanour and the killing odour of their breaths when they returned an hour or so later, during their absence they must have conscientiously sampled the contents of every whisky decanter on the dining-room sideboard.

===Chinese===
这样的夜晚，稍一动弹便热得够呛。第三支舞过后，那两位上了年纪的大牧场主、贩马客、牧师和本森先生都不见了踪影。约莫一个钟头后他们回来，瞧那副乐陶陶的做派，闻那股冲鼻子的酒气，可知他们不在的这一阵，准是把饭厅餐具柜上每一只威士忌细颈壶里的存货，都尽心尽责地逐一品鉴过了。

===Original===
I could not dance, but had no lack of partners, as, ladies being in the minority, the gentlemen had to occasionally put up with their own sex in a dance.

===Chinese===
我不会跳舞，舞伴却从不短少——女客占少数，先生们只得偶尔凑合着同自己的性别共舞一场。

===Original===
“Let’s take a breeze now and have a song or two, but no more dancing for a while,” said some of them; but Harold Beecham said, “One more turn, and then we will have a long spell and a change of programme.”

===Chinese===
“咱们这会儿出去透透风，唱一两支歌，舞且停一停吧。”他们中间有几位这么说。哈罗德·比彻姆（Harold Beecham）却说：“再转一圈，然后我们好好歇上一场，换一换节目。”

===Original===
He ordered Joe Archer to play a waltz, and the floor soon held several whirling couples. Harold “requested the pleasure” of me﻿—the first time that night. I demurred. He would not take a refusal.

===Chinese===
他吩咐乔·阿彻弹一支圆舞曲，舞场上很快旋起了好几对人儿。哈罗德向我“敬请赏光”——当晚这是头一回。我推辞。他不容回绝。

===Original===
“Believe me, if I felt competent, Mr. Beecham, I would not refuse. I cannot dance. It will be no pleasure to you.”

===Chinese===
“请相信我，比彻姆先生，我若跳得来，是不会拒绝的。我不会跳舞。同我跳，您得不着半点乐趣。”

===Original===
“Allow me to be the best judge of what is a pleasure to me,” he said, quietly placing me in position.

===Chinese===
“什么于我是乐趣，请容我自己来裁断。”他说着，不动声色地把我安放进舞步的位置。

===Original===
He swung me once round the room, and then through an open window into the garden.

===Chinese===
他拥着我绕场旋转了一遭，随即从一扇敞开的窗子转进了花园。

===Original===
“I am sorry that I haven’t had more time to look after you today. Come round into my room. I want to strike a bargain with you,” were his words.

===Chinese===
“很抱歉，今天我没多腾出工夫照应你。到我屋里来一趟吧。我要同你做成一笔交易。”他这样说。

===Original===
I followed him in the direction of a detached building in the garden. This was Harold’s particular domain. It contained three rooms﻿—one a library and office, another an arsenal and deed-room, and the third, into which he led me, was a sort of sitting-room, containing a piano, facilities for washing, a table, easy-chairs, and other things. As we entered I noticed the lamp, burning brightly on the table, gleamed on the face of a clock on the wall, which pointed to half past ten.

===Chinese===
我跟着他朝花园里一座独立的屋子走去。这是哈罗德的自家天地。里头有三间房——一间是书房兼办公室，一间是兵器房兼契据房，第三间便是他领我进去的那间，算是间起居室，屋里有一架钢琴、盥洗的家什、一张桌子、几张安乐椅，还有些别物。进门时我注意到，桌上亮堂堂燃着的灯光，正映在墙上一面钟的钟面上——钟指着十点半。

===Original===
We stood beside the table, some distance apart, and, facing me, he said:

===Chinese===
我们站在桌旁，隔着一段距离，他面朝着我，说道：

===Original===
“It is no use of me making a long yarn about nothing. I’m sure you know what I want to say better than I do myself. You always are wonderfully smart at seeing through a fellow. Tell me, will it be yes or no?”

===Chinese===
“我犯不上没事找事，绕一大篇闲话。我想说什么，你保准比我自己还清楚。你向来有本事一眼把一个男人看穿。告诉我，答应，还是不答应？”

===Original===
This was an experience in love. He did not turn red or white, or yellow or green, nor did he tremble or stammer, or cry or laugh, or become fierce or passionate, or tender or anything but just himself, as I had always known him. He displayed no more emotion than had he been inviting me to a picnic. This was not as I had pictured a man would tell his love, or as I had read of it, heard of it, or wished it should be. A curious feeling﻿—disappointment, perhaps﻿—stole over me. His matter-of-fact coolness flabbergasted me.

===Chinese===
这便是一场求爱的经历。他不变红，不变白，也不发青发绿；不颤抖，不结巴；不哭，不笑；不激烈，不痴狂，也不温柔——什么都不是，只是他自己，同我素来认得的他一般无二。他表露的情愫，不比邀我去野餐时多一丝一毫。这同我想象中一个男人吐露爱情的模样全不相干，同我书上读来的、耳朵听来的、心里盼着的也全不相干。一种古怪的感觉——兴许是失望——悄悄袭上我的心头。他这副就事论事的冷静，把我惊得瞠目结舌。

===Original===
“Is this not rather sudden? You have given me no intimation of your intentions,” I stammered.

===Chinese===
“这岂不太仓促了些？您的意向，我从没得到过半点风声。”我讷讷地说。

===Original===
“I didn’t think it wise to dawdle any longer,” he replied. “Surely you have known what I’ve been driving at ever since I first clapped eyes on you. There’s plenty of time. I don’t want to hurry you, only I want you to be engaged to me for safety.”

===Chinese===
“我觉着再磨蹭下去就不聪明了。”他答道。“打我头一眼见着你，我打的什么主意，你必定早知道了。时间有的是。我不想催你，只是想叫你先同我订下婚约，图个稳妥。”

===Original===
He spoke as usual in his slow twangy drawl, which would have proclaimed his Colonial nationality anywhere. No word of love was uttered to me and none requested from me.

===Chinese===
他照旧操着那副慢吞吞、拖腔拖调的土腔说话——这口音无论走到哪里，都会替他亮明殖民地人的出身。没有一句情话对我说出口，也没有向我讨一句。

===Original===
I put it down to his conceit. I thought that he fancied he could win any woman, and me without the least palaver or trouble. I felt annoyed. I said aloud, “I will become engaged to you;” to myself I added, “Just for a little while, the more to surprise and take the conceit out of you when the time comes.”

===Chinese===
我把这归咎于他的自负。我想，他准以为自己不费半句殷勤、半点周章，便能赢得随便哪个女人——连我也不例外。我心头着恼。我出声说道：“我愿意同你订婚。”心里又补上一句：“不过订一小阵子，好等时候一到，加倍地叫你吃惊，煞尽你的自负。”

===Original===
Now that I understand his character I know that it was not conceit, but just his quiet unpretending way. He had meant all his actions towards me, and had taken mine in return.

===Chinese===
如今我懂得了他的性子，才知道那并不是自负，只是他那安静而不事张扬的做派罢了。他待我的一举一动都是当真的，也把我回敬的举动一一当了真。

===Original===
“Thank you, Sybylla, that is all I want. We will talk about the matter more some other time. I will go up to Caddagat next Sunday. You have surprised me nearly out of my wits,” here he laughed. “I never dreamt you would say yes so easily, just like any other girl. I thought I would have a lot of trouble with you.”

===Chinese===
“谢谢你，西比拉，我要的就是这一句。这件事我们改日再细谈。下个礼拜天我就动身到卡达加特去。你险些把我惊得魂儿都出了窍。”说到这里他笑了。“我做梦也不曾想到，你会这么轻易地就答应了，同随便哪个姑娘一模一样。我原打算在你身上费好一番周折呢。”

===Original===
He approached me and was stooping to kiss me. I cannot account for my action or condemn it sufficiently. It was hysterical﻿—the outcome of an overstrung, highly excitable, and nervous temperament. Perhaps my vanity was wounded, and my tendency to strike when touched was up in arms. The calm air of ownership with which Harold drew near annoyed me, or, as Sunday-school teachers would explain it, Satan got hold of me. He certainly placed a long strong riding-whip on the table beneath my hand! As Harold stooped with the intention of pressing his lips to mine, I quickly raised the whip and brought it with all my strength right across his face. The instant the whip had descended I would have smashed my arm on the doorpost to recall that blow. But that was impossible. It had left a great weal on the healthy suntanned skin. His moustache had saved his lips, but it had caught his nose, the left cheek, had blinded the left eye, and had left a cut on the temple from which drops of blood were rolling down his cheek and staining his white coat. A momentary gleam of anger shot into his eyes and he gave a gasp, whether of surprise, pain, or annoyance, I know not. He made a gesture towards me. I half expected and fervently wished he would strike. The enormity of what I had done paralysed me. The whip fell from my fingers and I dropped on to a low lounge behind me, and placing my elbows on my knees crouchingly buried my face in my hands; my hair tumbled softly over my shoulders and reached the floor, as though to sympathetically curtain my humiliation. Oh, that Harold would thrash me severely! It would have infinitely relieved me. I had done a mean unwomanly thing in thus striking a man, who by his great strength and sex was debarred retaliation. I had committed a violation of self-respect and common decency; I had given a man an ignominious blow in the face with a riding-whip.
