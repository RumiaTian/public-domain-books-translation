## XXVII My Journey / 第27章 我的旅程

===Original===
The coach was a big vehicle, something after the style of a bus, the tilt and seats running parallel with the wheels. At the rear end, instead of a door, was a great tailboard, on the principle of a spring-cart. This was let down, and, after we scrambled over it into our seats, it was fixed half-mast, all the luggage piled thereon, and firmly roped into position. When this was completed, to anyone on the ground only the heads of passengers were visible above the pile. Had the coach capsized we would have been in a nice fix, as the only means of exit was by crawling up through the back of the box-seat, which rose breast-high﻿—an awkward feat.

===Chinese===
那辆长途马车是个大家伙，样式有几分像公共马车，车篷和座席顺着车轮平行排开。车尾不设车门，只有一块大尾板，原理同弹簧轻马车一个样。板子先放下来，等我们手脚并用地翻过它、爬进各自的座位，再把它支到半截，全部行李都码在上头，用绳索牢牢捆定。这一套完毕，站在地上的人望去，乘客们便只剩一颗颗脑袋露在行李堆上头。马车若是翻了车，我们的处境可就“妙”了——唯一的出口是打车夫座背后爬出去，那座儿升得齐胸高——一桩别扭的功夫。

===Original===
Frank Hawden and I parted good friends. I leant out and waved my handkerchief, until a bend of the road hid him from sight.

===Chinese===
弗兰克·霍登同我和和气气地分了手。我探出身子挥动手帕，直到道路一个弯儿把他遮出我的视线。

===Original===
It was noon, the thermometer registered 112 degrees in the shade, and the dust was simply awful. It rose in such thick grey clouds that often it was impossible to discern the team of five which pulled us, and there was danger of colliding with passing vehicles. We were very much crowded, there being sixteen passengers. When we settled down and got started, I discovered that I was the only representative of my sex, and that I was sandwiched between a perky youth in his teens and a Chinaman, while a black fellow and a man with a red beard sat opposite. A member of Parliament, farther up the seat, who had been patronizing New Year’s Day races in a portion of his electorate, bawled loudly to his companion about “the doin’s of the ’Ouse.” In the perky youth I discovered a professional jockey; and when he found that I was a daughter of Dick Melvyn, the onetime great horse-breeder, he became very friendly. He gave me a couple of apples out of his tin box under the seat, from whence he also produced his whip for my inspection, and was good enough to say:

===Chinese===
时当正午，背阴处的温度计指着华氏 112 度，尘土更是糟得没话说。它腾起一团团浓灰的云，常常连拉着我们走的那五匹马都辨不出来，还有同迎面车辆相撞的危险。车上挤得很，一共十六名乘客。待大家坐定、车子启动，我才发现自己是本性别在车上唯一的代表，被夹在一个十几岁、神气活现的小子和一个中国人中间，对面则坐着一个土著黑人和一个红胡子男人。座位再往上头是一位议员，他方才到选区的一处地方去光临了元旦赛马会，此刻正扯着嗓门向同伴大讲“‘议院’里干的那些事儿”。那个神气活现的小子，我发觉是个职业骑师；待他得知我是迪克·梅尔文的女儿——当年那位鼎鼎大名的养马人——便对我亲热起来。他从座位底下的铁皮匣子里掏出两只苹果给我，又从同一处取出他的马鞭来请我鉴赏，还大发好心地说：

===Original===
“If you can’t stand the stink of that bloomin’ chow, miss, just change seats with me. I’ve knocked about, so that I can easy stand some tough smells without much inconvenience.”

===Chinese===
“小姐，你要是受不住那该死中国佬的臭味，只管同我换座儿。我在外头闯荡惯了，多呛人的味儿也受得住，没多大相干。”

===Original===
I cautioned him to talk lower for fear of hurting the Chinaman’s feelings: this amused him immensely. He laughed very much, and, leaning over to the red-bearded man, repeated the joke:

===Chinese===
我嘱咐他说话放低声些，怕伤了那中国人的心：这话把他逗得乐不可支。他哈哈大笑，又凑向那红胡子男人，把笑话重讲了一遍：

===Original===
“I say, this young lady is afraid I might hurt the chow’s feelin’s. Golly! Fancy a bloomin’ chow havin’ any!”

===Chinese===
“我说，这位小姐怕我伤了那中国佬的心呢。老天爷！亏你想得出——一个该死的中国佬，居然还有心！”

===Original===
The other man also thought it a great joke. I changed seats with the jockey, which put me beside a young gentleman of a literary turn of mind, with whom I had some conversation about books when the dust, rumble of wheels, and turf talk of my other neighbour permitted. They were all very kind to me﻿—gave me fruit, procured me drinks of water, and took turns in nursing a precious hat, for which, on account of the crush, no safe place could be found among the other luggage.

===Chinese===
那一位也把这当成一桩绝妙的笑话。我同骑师换了座位，于是便挨着了一位雅好文学的青年绅士；只要尘土、车轮的轰隆和我另一侧邻座的赛马经容许，我就同他谈谈书。他们待我全都非常和气——给我水果，替我张罗水喝，还轮流看护着我那顶宝贝帽子——车里挤成这样，其余行李当中实在寻不出一处安放它的稳妥地方。

===Original===
Before we had gone half our journey the horses knocked up. All the men were forced to walk up hills for miles and miles in the dust and heat, which did not conduce to their amiability, and many and caustic were the remarks and jokes made upon the driver. He wore out two whips upon his team, until the labour and excessive heat sent the perspiration rolling in rivulets down his face, leaving muddy tracks in the thick coating of dust there. The jockey assisted with his loaded instrument of trade, some of the passengers thrashed with sticks, and all swore under their breath, while a passing bullock-driver used his whip with such deadly effect, that the sweat which poured off the poor beasts was mingled with blood.

===Chinese===
路程未走一半，马匹先垮了下来。上坡时男人们全被迫下车，顶着尘土和酷热一英里又一英里地走，这对他们的和气大有损减；冲着车夫发的议论和笑话又多又刻毒。他在那队马身上抽断了两条鞭子，劳苦加酷热，汗珠子顺着他的脸一道道往下淌，在脸上厚厚的尘垢里冲出一道道泥沟。骑师操起他吃饭的家伙——那柄灌了铅的鞭子——助阵，几位乘客拿棍子抽，人人压着嗓子咒骂；一个过路的赶牛车人也抡起鞭子，下手狠到那几匹可怜的牲口身上淌下的汗都和了血。

===Original===
“Why the deuce don’t you have proper horses?” demanded the red-bearded passenger.

===Chinese===
“你干吗他妈的不配几匹像样的马？”红胡子乘客质问道。

===Original===
The man explained that a ministerial party had chartered his best team to go on a tour of inspection to a mine; a brother coachman had been “stuck up” for horses, and borrowed a couple from him, whereupon he was forced to do with animals which had been turned out for a spell, and the heat and overloading accounted for a good part of the contretemps. However, we managed to catch our train, but had to rush for it without waiting for refreshments. Nice articles we looked﻿—our hair grey with dust, and our faces grimy. The men took charge of me as carefully as though I had been specially consigned to their care. One procured my ticket, another secured me a seat, while a third took charge of my luggage; and they were just as thoughtful when we had to change trains. Off we went. Grannie had packed me quite a large box full of dainties. I produced it, the men provided drinks, and we had quite a pleasant picnic, with all the windows down to catch a little air.

===Chinese===
车夫解释说，一位部长的一行人包下了他顶好的一班马，去视察一座矿井；又有一位同行车夫遭人“拦路打劫”失了马，从他这里借走两匹，于是他只好将就着用那几匹歇了好一阵子工的牲口，再者这般酷热加上超载，也是这场岔子的大半缘由。好在我们总算赶上了火车，只是茶点也顾不得等，拔腿就狂奔。我们这批“货色”的模样真够体面——头发叫尘土蒙得灰白，脸上乌一道泥一道。男人们照管我照管得细心极了，倒像我是一件专门托运给他们、责成他们照料的行李。一位替我买车票，一位给我占座位，第三位照管我的行李；到得换车，他们照样想得这样周到。车开了。外婆给我装了满满一大匣子精美的吃食。我把它拿将出来，男人们供上酒水，我们竟痛痛快快野餐了一场，车窗全放下来，好透一点风。

===Original===
I love the rush and roar of the train, and wished on this occasion that it might go on and on for over, never giving me time to think or stop. But, alas, at 1:20 we pulled up at Yarnung, where a man came inquiring for a young lady named Melvyn. My fellow passengers collected my belongings, and I got out.

===Chinese===
我爱火车的疾驰与轰鸣，这一回更巴望它就这么一直开下去、永远开下去，永远不给我留出思考或停顿的工夫。可是，唉，1:20 我们便在雅农（Yarnung）停了车，一个人上车来，寻问一位名叫梅尔文的年轻小姐。同车的乘客们替我收拢了行李，我下了车。

===Original===
“Goodbye, gentlemen; thank you very much for your kindness.”

===Chinese===
“再会，先生们；多谢你们的好意。”

===Original===
“Goodbye, miss; you’re welcome. Some of us might meet again yet. Ta-ta!”

===Chinese===
“再会，小姐；不用谢。我们这伙人往后兴许还能再碰上呢。回头见！”

===Original===
A shriek, a jerk, and the great train rushed on into the night, leaving me there on the insignificant little platform, feeling how lonely and unhappy, no one knew or cared.

===Chinese===
一声尖啸，车身一挫，那庞然的大火车便一头冲进夜色里去了，把我撂在那个不起眼的小站台上，只觉着心里那份孤寂、那份愁苦有多深——没有人知道，也没有人在乎。

===Original===
Mr. M’Swat shouldered most of my luggage, I took the remainder, and we trudged off in the dark without a word on either side. The publican had given M’Swat the key, so that we might enter without disturbing the household, and he escorted me to a bedroom, where I tumbled into bed with expedition.

===Chinese===
麦斯沃特先生（Mr. M'Swat）扛起我大部分的行李，我拿着剩下的，两人摸着黑一路跋涉，彼此一言不发。旅店老板已把钥匙交给了麦斯沃特，好教我们进门时不必惊动他家里的人；他把我送到一间卧室，我便以麻利至极的身手一头栽上了床。
