## XXII Sweet Seventeen / 第22章 芳龄十七

===Original===
Monday arrived﻿—last day of November and seventeenth anniversary of my birth﻿—and I celebrated it in a manner which I capitally enjoyed.

===Chinese===
星期一到了——十一月最后的一天，我降生后的第十七个周年——我用一种自己顶顶痛快的法子把它庆祝了一番。

===Original===
It was the time of the annual muster at Cummabella﻿—a cattle-station seventeen miles eastward from Caddagat﻿—and all our men were there assisting. Word had been sent that a considerable number of beasts among those yarded bore the impress of the Bossier brand on their hides; so on Sunday afternoon uncle Jay-Jay had also proceeded thither to be in readiness for the final drafting early on Monday morning. This left us manless, as Frank Hawden, being incapacitated with a dislocated wrist, was spending a few weeks in Gool-Gool until he should be fit for work again.

===Chinese===
正赶上卡马贝拉（Cummabella）一年一度的牲口大清点（muster）——那是卡达加特（Caddagat）以东十七英里的一处牧牛场——我们全部的人手都在那边帮工。有话传来，说围场里的牲口中有一大批，皮上烙着博西耶（Bossier）家的印记；于是礼拜天下午，杰杰舅舅（uncle Jay-Jay）也动身赶了过去，好从礼拜一一早便伺候着最后的分栏拣选。这一来家里就没了男人——弗兰克·霍登（Frank Hawden）腕骨脱臼干不得活，正在古尔古尔（Gool-Gool）将养几个礼拜，等养好了再回来上工。

===Original===
Uncle had not been gone an hour when a drover appeared to report that twenty thousand sheep would pass through on the morrow. Grass was precious. It would not do to let the sheep spread and dawdle at their drovers’ pleasure. There was not a man on the place; grannie was in a great stew; so I volunteered my services. At first she would not hear of such a thing, but eventually consented. With many injunctions to conduct myself with proper stiffness, I started early on Monday morning. I was clad in a cool blouse, a holland riding-skirt, and a big straw hat; was seated on a big bay horse, was accompanied by a wonderful sheepdog, and carried a long heavy stock-whip. I sang and cracked my stock-whip as I cantered along, quite forgetting to be reserved and proper. Presently I came upon the sheep just setting out for their day’s tramp, with a black boy ahead of them, of whom I inquired which was the boss. He pointed towards a man at the rear wearing a donkey-supper hat. I made my way through the sheep in his direction, and asked if he were in charge of them. On being answered in the affirmative, I informed him that I was Mr. Bossier’s niece, and, as the men were otherwise engaged, I would see the sheep through.

===Chinese===
舅舅走了还不到一个钟头，便来了个赶牲口人（drover），报告说有两万只羊明天要打这儿过。牧草金贵，断不能由着羊群散开去，随赶羊人的性子磨蹭。宅子里一个男人也没有；外婆（grannie）急得团团转；我便自告奋勇。起初她说什么也不肯依，末了还是应了。她千叮咛万嘱咐，要我举止端端庄庄、拿足架子，我礼拜一一早便动身了。我穿一件凉快的罩衫、一条荷兰麻布骑马裙，戴一顶大草帽；骑一匹高大的骝色马，带着一条顶出色的牧羊犬，手里拎一条又长又沉的牧鞭（stock-whip）。我一路催马小跑，唱着歌，把牧鞭甩得噼啪响，什么端庄架子，早忘到九霄云外去了。不多时我便赶上了那群羊，它们正要动身做一天的跋涉，前头走着个土著男孩，我便问他谁是头儿。他朝队尾一个戴“驴晚饭”帽的男人一指。我从羊群里朝那个人挤过去，问他可是管事的。他应了一声是，我便告诉他，我是博西耶先生的外甥女，男人们都有差事绊住了脚，由我来把这群羊送过境。

===Original===
“That’s all right, miss. I will look out that you don’t have much trouble,” he replied, politely raising his hat, while a look of amusement played on his face.

===Chinese===
“没要紧的，小姐。我会留神，叫您不致碰上多少麻烦。”他答道，彬彬有礼地举了举帽，脸上浮起一丝觉得有趣的神情。

===Original===
He rode away, and shouted to his men to keep the flock strictly within bounds and make good travelling.

===Chinese===
他骑马走开，朝他的伙计们扬声喊话，叫他们把羊群严格圈在界内，赶出个痛快的脚程来。

===Original===
“Right you are, boss,” they answered; and returning to my side he told me his name was George Ledwood, and made some remarks about the great drought and so on, while we rode in the best places to keep out of the dust and in the shade. I asked questions such as whence came the sheep? whither were they bound? and how long had they been on the road? And having exhausted these orthodox remarks, we fell a-talking in dead earnest without the least restraint. I listened with interest to stories of weeks and weeks spent beneath the sun and stars while crossing widths of saltbush country, mulga and myall scrubs, of encounters with blacks in Queensland, and was favoured with a graphic description of a big strike among the shearers when the narrator had been boss-of-the-board out beyond Bourke. He spoke as though well educated, and a gentleman﻿—as drovers often are. Why, then, was he on the road? I put him down as a scapegrace, for he had all the winning pleasant manner of a ne’er-do-well.

===Chinese===
“遵命，头儿！”他们应道。他回到我身边，告诉我他名叫乔治·莱德伍德（George Ledwood），又扯了些大旱年景之类的闲话；我们拣着尘土最少、荫凉最多的好位置并辔而行。我问了些诸如此类的照例话：羊是从哪里来的？要往哪里去？在路上走了多久了？这几句照例的客套一说完，我们便正正经经攀谈起来，没有半点拘束。我听得津津有味：他们一连几礼拜头顶烈日、夜宿星空，穿越一片片滨藜（saltbush）荒原、穆尔加（mulga）和相思木（myall）灌丛；在昆士兰（Queensland）同土著黑人打交道；还有幸听他绘声绘色讲了一场剪毛工大罢工——那时叙述者正在伯克（Bourke）城外当剪毛台领班（boss-of-the-board）。他谈吐像个受过良好教育的人，像个绅士——赶牲口人里头往往颇有这样的。那么，他怎么还在路上奔波？我断定他是个出门胡混的浪荡子：他浑身上下那副讨人喜欢的和气劲儿，正是那些不成器的败家子共有的。

===Original===
At noon﻿—a nice, blazing, dusty noon﻿—we halted within a mile of Caddagat for lunch. I could have easily ridden home for mine, but preferred to have it with the drovers for fun. The men boiled the billy and made the tea, which we drank out of tin pots, with tinned fish and damper off tin plates as the completion of the menu, Mr. Ledwood and I at a little distance from the men. Tea boiled in a billy at a bush fire has a deliciously aromatic flavour, and I enjoyed my birthday lunch immensely. Leaving the cook to collect the things and put them in the spring-cart, we continued on our way, lazily lolling on our horses and chewing gum-leaves as we went.

===Chinese===
正午——一个妙极了、火辣辣、灰扑扑的正午——我们在离卡达加特不到一英里的地方停下打尖。我原可以轻轻松松赶回家去吃饭，却宁可图个新鲜，同赶牲口人们搭伙。伙计们烧上比尔壶（billy）沏了茶，我们用锡罐喝，就着锡盘里的罐头鱼和篝火面饼（damper），这席面便告圆满——莱德伍德先生同我坐得离伙计们略远些。在灌木篝火上的比尔壶里烧出来的茶，有一股芬芳馥郁的香味，我这一顿生日午餐吃得痛快极了。我们把收拾家什、装上弹簧轻马车的活计留给厨子，继续赶路，懒洋洋地歪在马背上，一路嚼着桉树叶。

===Original===
When the last of the sheep got off the Caddagat run it was nearing two o’clock.

===Chinese===
等最后一只羊走出卡达加特的放牧地，已快两点钟了。

===Original===
Mr. Ledwood and I shook hands at parting, each expressing a wish that we might meet again some day.

===Chinese===
莱德伍德先生同我握手道别，彼此都说，但愿日后有缘再会。

===Original===
I turned and rode homewards. I looked back and saw the drover gazing after me. I waved my hand; he raised his hat and smiled, displaying his teeth, a gleam of white in his sun-browned face. I kissed my hand to him; he bowed low; I whistled to my dog; he resumed his way behind the crawling sheep; I cantered home quickly and dismounted at the front gate at 2:30 p.m., a dusty, heated, tired girl.

===Chinese===
我拨转马头往家走。回头一望，见那赶牲口人正目送着我。我朝他挥手；他举帽微笑，露出一口牙齿，在他晒成褐色的脸上一道白光。我吻了吻自己的手，向他挥去；他深深一躬；我吹哨唤我的狗；他重新跟在蠕蠕而行的羊群后面上了路；我一路小跑赶回家，下午 2:30 在前门口下了马——一个满身尘土、热烘烘、乏透了的女孩子。

===Original===
Grannie came out to question me regarding the sex, age, condition, and species of the sheep, what was their destination, whether they were in search of grass or were for sale, had they spread or eaten much grass, and had the men been civil?

===Chinese===
外婆迎出来盘问：那群羊的公母、口齿、膘情、品类，它们要往哪里去，是寻草场还是待售的，散没散开、吃掉了多少草，还有那些人客气不客气？

===Original===
When I had satisfactorily informed her on all these points, she bade me have something to eat, to bathe and dress, and gave me a holiday for the remainder of the day.

===Chinese===
我把这些事一一回禀周全，她便吩咐我吃点东西，洗个澡换身衣裳，又赏了我半天假，这天剩下的时辰随我玩乐。

===Original===
My hair was grey with dust, so I washed all over, arrayed myself in a cool white dress, and throwing myself in a squatter’s chair in the veranda, spread my hair over the back of it to dry. Copies of Gordon, Kendall, and Lawson were on my lap, but I was too physically content and comfortable to indulge in even these, my sworn friends and companions. I surrendered myself to the mere joy of being alive. How the sunlight blazed and danced in the roadway﻿—the leaves of the gum-trees gleaming in it like a myriad gems! A cloud of white, which I knew to be cockatoos, circled over the distant hilltop. Nearer they wheeled until I could hear their discordant screech. The thermometer on the wall rested at 104 degrees despite the dense shade thrown on the broad old veranda by the foliage of creepers, shrubs, and trees. The gurgling rush of the creek, the scent of the flower-laden garden, and the stamp, stamp of a horse in the orchard as he attempted to rid himself of tormenting flies, filled my senses. The warmth was delightful. Summer is heavenly, I said﻿—life is a joy.

===Chinese===
我的头发叫尘土蒙得灰白，便周身洗了个遍，换上一袭凉快的白裙，往游廊上一张牧场主宽椅（squatter's chair）里一躺，把头发披散在椅背上晾干。戈登（Gordon）、肯德尔（Kendall）、劳森（Lawson）几卷诗集就摊在我膝头，可我浑身舒坦、通体熨帖，连这几位誓同生死的朋友伙伴也懒怠去亲近，只一味把自己交给单单活着的欢喜。阳光在大路上烧得多旺、舞得多欢——桉树的叶子在光里闪烁，像千万粒宝石！远处山顶上盘旋着一团白影，我知道那是凤头鹦鹉。它们兜着圈子越飞越近，近得我听得见那不谐和的尖叫。墙上温度表停在 104 度，尽管藤蔓、花木、乔木的浓荫遮着这宽阔的老游廊。溪水汩汩奔流的声响，满园花气熏人，果园里一匹马为甩开恼人的苍蝇不停刨着蹄子——咚，咚——这一切灌满了我的感官。这暖意真叫人受用。夏天是天堂，我说——人生是欢喜。

===Original===
Aunt Helen’s slender fingers looked artistic among some pretty fancywork upon which she was engaged. Bright butterflies flitted round the garden, and thousands of bees droned lazily among the flowers. I closed my eyes﻿—my being filled with the beauty of it all.

===Chinese===
海伦姨妈（aunt Helen）纤细的手指埋在一幅精巧的绣活里，看着真有画意。明媚的蝴蝶在园中翻飞，千万只蜜蜂在花间懒懒地嗡鸣。我阖上眼——满心满眼都被这一派美填满了。

===Original===

===Original===
Grannie’s voice faded on my ears, my thoughts ran on uncle Jay-Jay. He had promised to be home in time for my birthday spread, and I was sure he had a present for me. What would it be?﻿—something nice. He would be nearly sure to bring someone home with him from Cummabella, and we would have games and fun to no end. I was just seventeen, only seventeen, and had a long, long life before me wherein to enjoy myself. Oh, it was good to be alive! What a delightful place the world was!﻿—so accommodating, I felt complete mistress of it. It was like an orange﻿—I merely had to squeeze it and it gave forth sweets plenteously. The stream sounded far away, the sunlight blazed and danced, grannie’s voice was a pleasant murmur in my ear, the cockatoos screamed over the house and passed away to the west. Summer is heavenly and life is a joy, I reiterated. Joy! Joy! There was joy in the quit! quit! of the green-and-crimson parrots, which swung for a moment in the rosebush over the gate, and then whizzed on into the summer day. There was joy in the gleam of the sun and in the hum of the bees, and it throbbed in my heart. Joy! Joy! A jackass laughed his joy as he perched on the telegraph wire out in the road. Joy! joy! Summer is a dream of delight and life is a joy, I said in my heart. I was repeating the one thing over and over﻿—but ah! it was a measure of happiness which allowed of much repetition. The cool murmur of the creek grew far away, I felt my poetry books slip off my knees and fall to the floor, but I was too content to bother about them﻿—too happy to need their consolation, which I had previously so often and so hungrily sought. Youth! Joy! Warmth!

===Chinese===

===Chinese===
