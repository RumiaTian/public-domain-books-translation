## III My Boyhood and Youth / 我的少年与青年时代

===Original===
Early Employment﻿—Slave-Life﻿—Food, Lodging, Clothing﻿—Amusements﻿—Gleams of Sunshine﻿—My Knight-Errantry﻿—Become an Overseer and General Superintendent.
My earliest employments were, to carry buckets of water to the men at work, and to hold a horse-plough, used for weeding between the rows of corn. As I grew older and taller, I was entrusted with the care of master’s saddle-horse. Then a hoe was put into my hands, and I was soon required to do the day’s work of a man; and it was not long before I could do it, at least as well as my associates in misery.
The everyday life of a slave on one of our Southern plantations, however frequently it may have been described, is generally little understood at the North; and must be mentioned as a necessary illustration of the character and habits of the slave and the slaveholder, created and perpetuated by their relative position. The principal food of those upon my master’s plantation consisted of cornmeal, and salt herrings; to which was added in summer a little buttermilk, and the few vegetables which each might raise for himself and his family, on the little piece of ground which was assigned to him for the purpose, called a truck patch.
In ordinary times we had two regular meals in a day:﻿—breakfast at twelve o’clock, after laboring from daylight, and supper when the work of the remainder of the day was over. In harvest season we had three. Our dress was of tow-cloth; for the children nothing but a shirt; for the older ones a pair of pantaloons or a gown in addition, according to the sex. Besides these, in the winter a round jacket or overcoat, a wool hat once in two or three years, for the males, and a pair of coarse shoes once a year.

早期的活计——奴隶的生活——衣食住——消遣——几线阳光——我的游侠行径——当上监工和总管。
我最早的活计，是给干活的人们送水桶，以及扶一匹马拉的锄犁，在玉米行间除草。我渐渐长大长高，便被托付照管主人的坐骑。随后一把锄头塞进我手里，不久就要我做成年男子一整天的活；没过多久我就做得了，至少不比我那些受苦的伙伴们差。
我们南方种植园里奴隶的日常生活，无论被描述过多少回，在北方通常还是很少有人真正了解；而为了说明奴隶与奴隶主因彼此地位而养成并延续的品性与习惯，这又是必须提及的。我主人家种植园上人的主食，是玉米面和咸鲱鱼；夏天再加上一点酪乳，以及各人在分给自己的一小块地上自种自吃的少量菜蔬——那块地叫作菜园地（truck patch）。
平常时节，我们一天吃两顿正餐：从天亮劳动到 12 点吃早饭，当天余下的活干完吃晚饭。收获季节吃三顿。我们穿的是粗麻布：小孩子只有一件衬衫；大孩子按性别添一条裤子或一件长袍。此外，冬天男人们有一件圆领短外套或大衣，两三年一顶毡帽，一年一双粗鞋。

We lodged in log huts, and on the bare ground. Wooden floors were an unknown luxury. In a single room were huddled, like cattle, ten or a dozen persons, men, women and children. All ideas of refinement and decency were, of course, out of the question. There were neither bedsteads, nor furniture of any description. Our beds were collections of straw and old rags, thrown down in the corners and boxed in with boards; a single blanket the only covering. Our favorite way of sleeping, however, was on a plank, our heads raised on an old jacket and our feet toasting before the smouldering fire. The wind whistled and the rain and snow blew in through the cracks, and the damp earth soaked in the moisture till the floor was miry as a pigsty. Such were our houses. In these wretched hovels were we penned at night, and fed by day; here were the children born and the sick﻿—neglected.
Notwithstanding this system of management I grew to be a robust and vigorous lad. At fifteen years of age there were few who could compete with me in work or sport. I was as lively as a young buck, and running over with animal spirits. I could run faster, wrestle better, and jump higher than anybody about me, and at an evening shakedown in our own or a neighbor’s kitchen, my feet became absolutely invisible from the rate at which they moved. All this caused my master and my fellow slaves to look upon me as a wonderfully smart fellow, and prophecy the great things I should do when I became a man. My vanity became vastly inflamed, and I fully coincided in their opinion. Julius Caesar never aspired and plotted for the imperial crown more ambitiously than did I to out-hoe, out-reap, out-husk, out-dance, out-everything every competitor; and from all I can learn he never enjoyed his triumph half as much. One word of commendation from the petty despot who ruled over us would set me up for a month.
I have no desire to represent the life of slavery as an experience of nothing but misery. God be praised, that however hedged in by circumstances, the joyful exuberance of youth will bound at times over them all. Ours is a lighthearted race. The sternest and most covetous master cannot frighten or whip the fun out of us; certainly old Riley never did out of me. In those days I had many a merry time, and would have had, had I lived with nothing but moccasins and rattlesnakes in Okafenoke swamp. Slavery did its best to make me wretched; I feel no particular obligation to it; but nature, or the blessed God of youth and joy, was mightier than slavery. Along with memories of miry cabins, frosted feet, weary toil under the blazing sun, curses and blows, there flock in others, of jolly Christmas times, dances before old massa’s door for the first drink of eggnog, extra meat at holiday times, midnight visits to apple orchards, broiling stray chickens, and first-rate tricks to dodge work. The God who makes the pup gambol, and the kitten play, and the bird sing, and the fish leap, was the author in me of many a lighthearted hour. True it was, indeed, that the fun and freedom of Christmas, at which time my master relaxed his front, was generally followed up by a portentous back-action, under which he drove and cursed worse than ever; still the fun and freedom were fixed facts; we had had them and he could not help it.

我们住的是圆木小屋，睡的是光地。木地板是不曾见识的奢侈。一间屋子里，像牲口一样挤着十个八个人，男女老幼都有。一切体面和廉耻的观念，自然无从谈起。没有床架，也没有任何家具。我们的床是一堆堆稻草和破布，扔在屋角，用木板围一围；一条毯子是唯一的铺盖。不过我们最爱的睡法是睡在一块木板上，头枕一件旧外套，脚朝着将熄的火烤着。风在呼啸，雨雪从缝隙里灌进来，潮湿的地气浸得地上泥泞得像猪圈。我们的屋子便是这样。夜里我们被圈在这些悲惨的窝棚里，白天在这里喂食；孩子们在这里出生，病人在这里——无人照管。
尽管有这样一套管束，我还是长成了一个壮健旺盛的小伙子。十五岁上，无论干活还是玩耍，能同我较量的没有几人。我欢蹦乱跳像头小鹿，浑身是使不完的精气神。我跑得比谁都快，摔跤比谁都行，跳得比谁都高；晚上在我们自己或邻居家的厨房里跳一场即兴舞，我的两只脚快得简直看不见。这一切使主人和我的奴隶伙伴们都把我看作一个聪明绝顶的家伙，预言我长大后必成大器。我的虚荣心大为膨胀，也完全赞同他们的看法。尤利乌斯·凯撒觊觎谋划那顶皇冠，也未必比我觊觎锄地、割谷、剥壳、跳舞以及样样都压倒对手的心思更热切；而且据我所知，他得意的快乐还不及我的一半。我们那位小小的暴君夸我一句，够我神气一个月。
我无意把奴隶的生活说成只有苦难、别无其他。感谢上帝，青春那欢快的丰盈，无论被环境怎样围困，总有时会一跃而过这一切。我们是个天性快活的种族。最严厉、最贪婪的主人，也吓不走、打不掉我们的乐趣；当然，老赖利也从没能打掉我的。在那些日子里，我有过许多快活时光；就算住在奥凯芬诺基沼泽（Okafenoke swamp），整日与麂子和响尾蛇为伴，我也会照样快活。奴隶制竭力使我痛苦；我并不觉得欠它什么情；但天性，或者说那主宰青春与欢乐的蒙福的上帝，比奴隶制更强大。同泥泞的小屋、冻伤的脚、烈日下的苦役、咒骂和鞭打一起涌来的记忆，还有别的：快活的圣诞节，在老主子门前跳舞讨头一口蛋奶酒，节庆日额外的肉食，半夜溜进苹果园，烤拾来的鸡，还有躲懒逃工的高明把戏。那位使小狗撒欢、小猫嬉戏、鸟儿歌唱、鱼儿跳跃的上帝，也在我心里造出过许多轻松愉快的时辰。的确，圣诞节——那时主人会松一松脸——的欢乐与自由，随后照例要来一番凶险的反攻倒算，他赶我们、骂我们比平时更凶；但欢乐与自由终归是铁打的事实；我们享受过了，他也无可奈何。

Besides these pleasant memories I have others of a deeper and richer kind. I early learned to employ my spirit of adventure for the benefit of my fellow-sufferers. The condition of the male slave is bad enough; but that of the female, compelled to perform unfit labor, sick, suffering, and bearing the peculiar burdens of her own sex unpitied and unaided, as well as the toils which belong to the other, is one that must arouse the spirit of sympathy in every heart not dead to all feeling. The miseries which I saw many of the women suffer often oppressed me with a load of sorrow. No *white* knight, rescuing white fair ones from cruel oppression, ever felt the throbbing of a chivalrous heart more intensely than I, a *black* knight, did, in running down a chicken in an out-of-the way place to hide till dark, and then carry to some poor overworked black fair one, to whom it was at once food, luxury, and medicine. No Scotch borderer, levying black mail or sweeping off a drove of cattle, ever felt more assured of the justice of his act than I of mine, in driving a mile or two into the woods a pig or a sheep, and slaughtering it for the good of those whom Riley was starving. I felt good, moral, heroic. The beautiful combination of a high time and a benevolent act﻿—the harmonious interplay of nature and grace﻿—was absolutely entrancing. I felt then the excellency of a sentiment I have since found expressed in a hymn:
> “Religion was never designed,
 

 To make our pleasures less.”
Was this wrong? I can only say in reply, that, at this distance of time, my conscience does not reproach me for it. Then I esteemed it among the best of my deeds. It was my training in the luxury of doing good, in the divinity of a sympathetic heart, in the righteousness of indignation against the cruel and oppressive. There and then was my soul made conscious of its heavenly original. This, too, was all the chivalry of which my circumstances and condition in life admitted. I love the sentiment in its splendid environment of castles, and tilts, and gallantry; but having fallen on other times, I love it also in the homely guise of Sambo as Paladin, Dinah as outraged maiden, and old Riley as grim oppressor.

除了这些愉快的记忆，我还有更深沉、更丰厚的一种。我很早就学会把我那股冒险劲头用在受苦同伴的身上。男奴隶的境遇已经够苦；女奴隶呢——被迫干不适合的活，病着、苦着，独自承受她性别的特别重负，无人怜、无人助，还要加上男人们的劳苦——这境遇必能在任何一颗心尚未全死的人心里唤起同情。我眼见许多妇人受的苦，常常压得我心头沉重。任何*白*骑士从残暴的压迫中搭救白人美人，其侠义之心的跳动，从未比我这个*黑*骑士更激烈——我追一只鸡到偏僻地方藏到天黑，再送给哪个被役使过度的可怜黑美人，那鸡于她既是饭食，又是珍馐，还是药。任何苏格兰边境人在勒索保护金或赶走一群牛时对自己行为正义性的笃信，从未胜过我——我把一口猪或一只羊赶出一两英里赶进树林，宰了给赖利正在饿着的那些人充饥。我觉得自己善良、有德、英雄。痛快时光与慈善行为的美妙结合——天性与恩典的和谐互动——实在令人心醉。那时我便体会到一个道理的妙处，后来我见它写在一首圣诗里：
「宗教从来不是要设计，
来减少我们的欢乐。」
这难道错了吗？我只能这样回答：事到如今，隔了这么远，我的良心并不为此责备我。那时我把它算在我最好的行为之中。那是我受的训练：学着享受行善之乐，学着体察同情之心的神性，学着对残忍与压迫之义愤的正直。就在那时那地，我的灵魂意识到了它上天的来处。这也正是我的境遇和地位所能容得下的全部骑士精神。我爱这情操置身于城堡、比武和殷勤献媚的华丽环境中的样子；但它既逢别的时代，我也爱它换上家常的装束：桑博（Sambo）作游侠，黛娜（Dinah）作受辱的佳人，老赖利作狰狞的暴君。

By means of the influence thus acquired, the increased amount of work thus done upon the farm, and by the detection of the knavery of the overseer, who plundered his employer for more selfish ends, and through my watchfulness was caught in the act and dismissed, I was promoted to be superintendent of the farm work, and managed to raise more than double the crops, with more cheerful and willing labor, than was ever seen on the estate before.
Yes, I was now practically overseer. My pride and ambition had made me master of every kind of farm work. But like all ambition its reward was increase of burdens. The crops of wheat, oats, barley, potatoes, corn, tobacco, all had to be cared for by me. I was often compelled to start at midnight with the wagon for the distant market, to drive on through mud and rain till morning, sell the produce, reach home hungry and tired, and nine times out of ten reap my sole reward in curses for not getting higher prices. My master was a fearful blasphemer. Clearly as he saw my profitableness to him, he was too much of a brute, and too great a fool through his brutality, to reward me with kindness or even decent treatment. Previous to my attaining this important station, however, an incident occurred which produced so powerful an influence on my intellectual development, my prospect of improvement in character, as well as condition, my chance of religious culture, and in short, on my whole nature, body and soul, that it deserves especial notice and commemoration. This, however, requires another chapter.

靠这样得来的威望，靠农场上由此多出的活计，也靠揭穿了那监工的自私舞弊——他为一己之私侵吞雇主，经我留心察访，当场拿住，被打发走了——我被提拔为农场工务总管，经营出的收成比庄园历来所见多出一倍还多，出力的人们也更为乐意、更为欢畅。
是的，如今我实际上已是监工。我的自尊和雄心使我样样农活都拿得起。但一切雄心的报偿一样是负担的加重。小麦、燕麦、大麦、马铃薯、玉米、烟草，样样都得我照管。我常被迫半夜套车上远处的集市，在泥泞和风雨里赶到天亮，卖掉货物，回到家又饿又累，十回有九回，唯一的报赏是挨一顿骂，怪我卖价不够高。我主人是个可怕的咒骂者。他明明看得出我于他有利可图，可他太是个畜生，又因他的畜生性太是个蠢货，不肯以善待、甚至体面的待遇来酬报我。然而，在我升上这个要职之前，发生了一件事，它对我的智识发展、我品性与境遇上进的前途、我受宗教熏陶的机会，总之对我的整个天性、身体和灵魂，都产生了极为有力的影响，值得特别记一笔。不过这须得另起一章。
