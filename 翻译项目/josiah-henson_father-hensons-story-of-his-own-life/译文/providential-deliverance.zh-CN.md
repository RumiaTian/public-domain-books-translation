## XI Providential Deliverance / 天意的解救

===Original===
Offered for Sale﻿—Examined by Purchasers﻿—Plead with My Young Master in Vain﻿—Man’s Extremity, God’s Opportunity﻿—Good for Evil﻿—Return North﻿—My Increased Value﻿—Resolve to Be a Slave No Longer.
In a few days after this trying crisis in my life, we arrived at New Orleans. The little that remained of our cargo was soon sold, the men were discharged, and nothing was left but to dispose of me, and break up the boat, and then Master Amos would take passage on a steamboat, and go home. There was no longer any disguise about the disposition which was to be made of me. Master Amos acknowledged that such were his instructions, and he set about fulfilling them. Several planters came to the boat to look at me; I was sent on some hasty errand that they might see how I could run; my points were canvassed as those of a horse would have been; and, doubtless, some account of my various faculties entered into the discussion of the bargain, that my value as a domestic animal might be enhanced. Master Amos had talked, with apparent kindness, about getting me a good master, who would employ me as a coachman, or as a house-servant; but as time passed on I could discern no particular effort of the kind.
In our intervals of leisure I tried every possible means to move his heart. With tears and groans I besought him not to sell me away from my wife and children. I dwelt on my past services to his father, and called to his remembrance a thousand things I had done for him personally. I told him about the wretched condition of the slaves I had seen near Vicksburg. Sometimes he would shed tears himself, and say he was sorry for me. But still I saw his purpose was unchanged. He now kept out of my way as much as possible, and forestalled every effort I made to talk with him. His conscience evidently troubled him. He knew he was doing a cruel and wicked thing, and wanted to escape from thinking about it. I followed him up hard, for I was supplicating for my life. I fell down and clung to his knees in entreaties. Sometimes when too closely pressed, he would curse and strike me. May God forgive him. And yet it was not all his fault. He was made so by the accursed relation of slavemaster and slave. I was property﻿—not a man, not a father, not a husband. And the laws of property and self-interest, not of humanity and love, bore sway.
At length everything was wound up but this single affair. I was to be sold the next day, and Master Amos was to set off on his return, in a steamboat, at six o’clock in the afternoon. I could not sleep that night; its hours seemed interminably long, though it was one of the shortest of the year. The slow way in which we had come down had brought us to the long days and heats of June; and everybody knows what the climate of New Orleans is at that period of the year.

被摆出来出售——受买主验看——向少主人哀求无效——人的绝境，上帝的机会——以德报怨——北归——我涨了身价——决心不再为奴。
我一生中这场严酷的危机过去没几天，我们便到了新奥尔良。船上所剩无几的货很快卖掉了，水手们被遣散，剩下的只是把我脱手、把船拆卖，然后阿莫斯主人便搭汽船回家。至于我的处置，再没有任何遮掩了。阿莫斯主人承认他领受的吩咐正是如此，并着手照办。几个种植园主到船上来相看我；我被支使去干一桩急差，好让他们看看我的脚力；我的各处优点被一一评头论足，像相一匹马那样；而且，毫无疑问，对我各项才干的某些介绍也进入了讨价还价，好抬高我这头家畜的身价。阿莫斯主人曾带着显然的好意谈论过，要给我寻个好主人，雇我做车夫或室内仆役；但随着时日过去，我看不出这方面有什么特别的动静。
一有空闲，我便想尽一切办法打动他的心。我流着泪、呻吟着哀求他，不要把我从妻子儿女身边卖走。我细数我从前对他父亲的效力，唤他记起我为他本人做过的千百件事。我告诉他我在维克斯堡（Vicksburg）附近见到的奴隶们的悲惨境况。有时他自己也落泪，说他为我难过。但我看他的主意不变。如今他尽量躲着我，我每次想同他说话都被他预先避开。他的良心显然不安。他知道自己干的是一件残忍邪恶的事，想逃开不去想它。我却紧追不放，因为我是在为性命哀告。我扑倒在地，抱住他的膝头哀求。有时被逼得太紧，他便咒骂我、打我。愿上帝饶恕他。而这也不全是他的过错。是奴隶主与奴隶那该诅咒的关系把他造成了这样。我是财产——不是人，不是父亲，不是丈夫。而且当道的是财产法和自利之法，不是人道与爱。
最后，一切都料理清了，只剩这一件事。第二天我就要被卖掉，阿莫斯主人下午 6 点便要乘汽船启程回家。那一夜我不能成眠；几个钟头仿佛长得没有尽头，虽然那是一年里最短的夜之一。我们一路耽搁而来，已到了 6 月的长昼与酷暑；新奥尔良那个时节的气候如何，人人知道。

And now occurred one of those sudden, marked interpositions of Providence, by which in a moment the whole current of a human being’s life is changed; one of those slight and, at first, unappreciated contingencies, by which the faith that man’s extremity is God’s opportunity is kept alive. Little did I think, when a little before daylight Master Amos called me and told me he felt sick, how much my future was bound up in those few words. His stomach was disordered, and I advised him to lie down again, thinking it would soon pass off. Before long he felt worse, and it was soon evident that the river fever was upon him. He became rapidly ill, and by eight o’clock in the morning was utterly prostrate. The tables were now turned. I was no longer property, no longer a brute beast to be bought and sold, but his only friend in the midst of strangers. Oh, how different was his tone from what it had been the day before! He was now the supplicant. A poor, terrified object, afraid of death, and writhing with pain, there lay the late arbiter of my destiny. How he besought me to forgive him. “Stick to me, Sie! Stick to me, Sie! Don’t leave me, don’t leave me. I’m sorry I was going to sell you.” Sometimes he would say he had only been joking, and never intended to part with me. Yes, the tables were utterly turned. He entreated me to dispatch matters, sell the flatboat in which we had been living, and get him and his trunk, containing the proceeds of the trip, on board the steamer as quick as possible. I attended to all his requests, and by twelve o’clock that day he was in one of the cabins of the steamer appropriated to sick passengers.
O, my God! how my heart sang jubilees of praise to Thee, as the steamboat swung loose from the levee and breasted the mighty tide of the Mississippi! Away from this land of bondage and death! Away from misery and despair! Once more exulting hope possessed me. This time if I do not open my way to freedom, may God never give me chance again!
Before we had proceeded many hours on our voyage, a change for the better appeared in my young master. The change of air in a measure revived him; and well it was for him that such was the case. Short as his illness had been, the fever had raged like a fire, and he was already near death. I watched and nursed him like a mother; for all remembrance of personal wrong was obliterated at sight of his peril. His eyes followed me in entreaty wherever I went. His strength was so entirely gone that he could neither speak nor move a limb, and could only indicate his wish for a teaspoonful of gruel, or something to moisten his throat, by a feeble motion of his lips. I nursed him carefully and constantly. Nothing else could have saved his life. It hung by a thread for a long time. We were as much as twelve days in reaching home, for the water was low at that season, particularly in the Ohio river; and when we arrived at our landing he was still unable to speak, and could only be moved on a sheet or a litter. Something of this sort was soon fixed up at the landing, on which he could be carried to the house, which was five miles off; and I got a party of the slaves belonging to the estate to form relays for the purpose. As we approached the house, the surprise at seeing me back again, and the perplexity to imagine what I was bringing along, with such a party, were extreme; but the discovery was soon made which explained the strange appearance; and the grief of father and mother, and brothers and sisters, made itself seen and heard. Loud and long were the lamentations over poor Amos; and when the family came a little to themselves, great were the commendations bestowed upon me for my care of him and of the property.

就在这时，发生了那种突然而分明的天意干预——人生整条水流会因它顷刻改道；那种起初微细而不为人重的偶然，正靠它，「人的绝境是上帝的机会」这一信心才得常存不灭。天快亮时，阿莫斯主人叫醒我，说他觉得不舒服；我哪里想得到，我的未来竟系在他这寥寥几个字上。他的胃不舒服，我劝他再躺下，想着很快就会过去。不久他更难受了，很快就看得出是河热病上了身。他病势急转直下，到早晨 8 点已完全起不来了。局面翻过来了。我不再是财产，不再是任人买卖的畜生，而是他在生人堆里唯一的朋友。哦，他的口气同前一天多么不同啊！如今他是哀求的人了。这个可怜、惊恐、怕死、痛得翻腾的人，躺在那里的，正是昨天我命运的主宰者。他怎样求我饶恕他啊。「别离开我，赛！别离开我，赛！别丢下我，别丢下我。对不起，我本要卖你的。」有时他又说他不过是说着玩的，从没真想同我分开。是的，局面彻底翻转了。他求我快些了结事务，把我们一直住着的平底船卖掉，尽快把他本人和他那口装着此行货款的箱子弄上汽船。他吩咐的事我都一一办妥；当天 12 点，他已躺在汽船上一间拨给病客的舱房里了。
哦，我的上帝！当汽船解开缆索离开堤岸、迎着密西西比浩浩的洪流时，我的心怎样向你唱着欢腾的赞美啊！离开这奴役与死亡之地！离开苦难与绝望！欢欣的希望重新占据了我。这一次我若不闯出一条自由的路来，愿上帝不再给我机会！
航行没过几个钟头，我这位少主人便有了起色。换了的空气多少使他振作起来；他幸好如此。他的病虽短，热势却如火燎原，已经濒临死亡。我像母亲一样看护他；一见他的危险，个人恩怨的一切记忆都烟消云散了。无论我走到哪里，他的眼睛都恳求似的追着我。他的气力衰得完全说不出话、动不了肢体，只能靠嘴唇微弱地翕动，表示想喝一茶匙稀粥，或润润喉咙的什么东西。我细心而不断地护理他。不这样便救不了他的命。有好一阵子，他的命悬于一发。我们走了足足十二天才到家，因为那个季节水浅，俄亥俄河上尤其如此；到达我们的码头时，他还不能说话，只能用一块床布或一副担架抬着。码头上很快备好了一副这类的东西，好把他抬到五英里外的宅子里；我又请了庄园上的一队奴隶，轮流抬送。我们走近宅子时，人们见我又回来了，又猜不透我带着这样一队人抬的是什么，又惊又惑，到了极点；但谜底很快揭开，一切怪状都有了着落；父亲母亲、兄弟姐妹的悲伤，便显于形、闻于声。为可怜的阿莫斯发出的哀哭又高又长；等这一家人稍定下神来，我因看护他、看护财物而得的称许，也就滔滔而来。

Although we reached home by the tenth of July, it was not until the middle of August that Master Amos was well enough to leave his chamber. To do him justice, he manifested strong gratitude towards me. Almost his first words after recovering his strength sufficiently to talk, were in commendation of my conduct. “If I had sold him I should have died.” On the rest of the family no permanent impression seemed to have been made. The first few words of praise were all I ever received. I was set at my old work. My merits, whatever they were, instead of exciting sympathy or any feeling of attachment to me, seemed only to enhance my market value in their eyes. I saw that my master’s only thought was to render me profitable to himself. From him I had nothing to hope, and I turned my thoughts to myself and my own energies.
Before long I felt assured another attempt would be made to dispose of me. Providence seemed to have interfered once to defeat the scheme, but I could not expect such extraordinary circumstances to be repeated; and I was bound to do everything in my power to secure myself and my family from the wicked conspiracy of Isaac and Amos Riley against my life, as well as against my natural rights, and those which I had acquired, under even the barbarous laws of slavery, by the money I had paid for myself. If Isaac would only have been honest enough to adhere to his bargain, I would have adhered to mine, and paid him all I had promised. But his attempt to kidnap me again, after having pocketed three-fourths of my market value, in my opinion absolved me from all obligation to pay him any more, or to continue in a position which exposed me to his machinations.

我们虽然 7 月 10 日就到了家，直到 8 月中旬，阿莫斯主人才好得起得了房间。凭良心说，他对我表现出深切的感激。他气力恢复到能说话时，开口的头几句，几乎就是称许我的行为。「我若卖了他，我就死了。」至于这一家其余的人，似乎没有留下什么持久的印象。起初那几句夸奖，便是我的全部所得。我又被派上旧日的活计。我的种种好处，无论是什么，非但引不起他们对我的同情或眷恋，似乎只在他们眼里抬高了我的市价。我看透了，主人唯一的心思是使我于他有利可图。从他身上我无所指望，我便把心思转到自己和自己的一份力气上来。
不久我便确信，处置我的企图还会再来一次。天意似乎干预过一回，挫败了这个计谋；但这样的奇缘我不能指望重演；我必须竭尽自己的全力，保全自己和家人，摆脱艾萨克·赖利和阿莫斯·赖利那既害我性命、又夺我天赋权利的毒计——连那些我在野蛮的奴隶法之下、用我付过的血汗钱挣得的权利，也在被夺之列。艾萨克只要诚实得肯守他的约，我也会守我的约，把我答应的钱如数付清。但他收进我市价四分之三的钱之后，还想再把我绑票一次；在我看来，这已解除我再付他分文的一切义务，也解除了我继续留在这个任他算计的位置上的一切义务。
