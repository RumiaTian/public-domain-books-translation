## XXII Visits to the Ragged Schools / 走访贫民学校

===Original===
Speech at Sunday School Anniversary﻿—Interview with Lord Grey﻿—Interview with the Archbishop of Canterbury, and Dinner with Lord John Russell, the Great Events of My Life.
While in England I was frequently called upon to speak at public meetings of various kinds. I was deeply interested in the Ragged School enterprise, and frequently addressed the schools, and also public meetings held in their behalf. I spent two months of May in that country, and attended many of the great anniversaries, and was called upon to speak at many of them. On several occasions I did what I could to make known the true condition of slaves, in Exeter Hall and other places. On one occasion, I recollect, an eminent man from Pennsylvania, was addressing the anniversary of a Sabbath School Union, who boasted of the great benefits of Sunday Schools in the United States, and asserted that all classes indiscriminately enjoyed their blessings. I felt bound to contradict him, and after putting to the speaker a few questions which he stammeringly answered, I told the immense meeting that in the Southern States, the great body of the colored people were almost entirely neglected, and in many places they were excluded altogether; and that even in the most of the Northern States, the great mass of the colored children were not sought out and gathered into Sunday Schools. This created some little storm, but my own personal observation and experience carried conviction to the people.
Being thus introduced to the public, I became well acquainted with many of the leading men of England. Lord Grey made a proposition to me, which, if circumstances had permitted, I should have been glad to attempt. It was to go to India and there superintend some great efforts made by the government to introduce the culture of cotton on the American plan. He promised to me an appointment to an office, and a good salary. Had it not been for my warm interest in my Canadian enterprise, I should have accepted his proposal.

在主日学周年会上演讲——谒见格雷勋爵——谒见坎特伯雷大主教，与约翰·罗素勋爵共餐——我生平的大事。
在英格兰期间，我常被请在各种公共集会上演讲。我对贫民学校的事业深为关切，常到各校演说，也常在为它们募款的公众集会上讲话。我在那个国度度过了两个五月，参加了许多盛大的周年纪念会，多次应邀演讲。有几回，我在埃克塞特会堂（Exeter Hall）等地，尽力把奴隶的真实境况公之于众。记得有一回，一位从宾夕法尼亚来的显赫人物正在主日学联合会的周年会上演讲，夸耀主日学在美国的种种好处，断言各阶层不分彼此地共享其福。我觉得不能不驳他；向演讲人提了几个问题，他讷讷地答了；我便告诉那盛大的集会：在南方各州，有色人的主体几乎全被弃置不顾，许多地方干脆一概排除在外；即便在北方大多数州，有色儿童的大多数也无人寻访、无人收进主日学。这话掀起了一场小风波，但我亲身的观察与经历，使人们信服了。
经此一番公开露面，我结识了英格兰许多头面人物。格雷勋爵（Lord Grey）向我提议一事——倘若环境许可，我本很愿意一试——就是去印度，督办政府正在那里进行的一项大规模事业：按美国的方案引种棉花。他答应给我一个官职和一份可观的薪俸。若不是我热切挂念着我在加拿大的事业，我本会接受他的提议。

One of the most pleasing incidents for me now to look back upon, was a long interview which I was permitted to enjoy with the Archbishop of Canterbury. The elevated social position of this man, the highest beneath the crown, is well known to all those acquainted with English society. Samuel Gurney, the noted philanthropist, introduced me, by a note and his family card, to his grace, the archbishop. He received me kindly in his palace. I immediately entered upon a conversation with him, upon the condition of my people, and the plans I had in view. He expressed the strongest interest in me, and after about a half hour’s conversation he inquired, “At what university, Sir, did you graduate?” “I graduated, your grace,” said I in reply, “at the university of adversity.” “The university of adversity,” said he, looking up with astonishment; “where is that?” I saw his surprise, and explained. “It was my lot, your grace,” said I, “to be born a slave, and to pass my boyhood and all the former part of my life as a slave. I never entered a school, never read the Bible in my youth, and received all of my training under the most adverse circumstances. This is what I meant by graduating in the university of adversity.” “I understand you, Sir,” said he. “But is it possible that you are not a scholar?” “I am not,” said I. “But I should never have suspected that you were not a liberally educated man. I have heard many negroes talk, but have never seen one that could use such language as you. Will you tell me, Sir, how you learned our language?” I then explained to him, as well as I could, my early life; that it had always been my custom to observe good speakers, and to imitate only those who seemed to speak most correctly. “It is astonishing,” said the archbishop. “And is it possible that you were brought up ignorant of religion? How did you attain to the knowledge of Christ?” I explained to him, in reply, that a poor ignorant slave mother had taught me to say the Lord’s Prayer, though I did not know then how, truly, to pray. “And how were you led to a better knowledge of the Saviour?” I answered that it was by the hearing of the Gospel preached. He then asked me to repeat the text, and to explain all the circumstances. I told him of the first sermon I heard, of the text, “He, by the grace of God, tasted death for every man.” “A beautiful text was that,” said the archbishop, and so affected was he by my simple story that he shed tears freely.
I had been told by Samuel Gurney that perhaps the archbishop would give me an interview of a quarter of an hour; I glanced at the clock and found that I had already been there an hour and a half, and arose to depart. He followed me to the door, and begged of me if ever I came to England to call and see him again; and shaking hands affectionately with me, while the tears trembled in his eyes, he put into my hands graciously five golden sovereigns, (about twenty-five dollars,) and bade me adieu. I have always esteemed him as a warmhearted Christian.
Thus ended the interview with the venerable Archbishop of England. On my second visit to England, I had an invitation, in company with a large number of Sabbath School Teachers, to spend a day on the beautiful grounds of Lord John Russell, then Prime Minister of England. His magnificent park, filled with deer, of all colors, and from all climes, and sleek hares, which the poet Cowper would have envied, with numberless birds, whose plumage rivalled the rainbow in gorgeous colors, together with the choicest specimens of the finny tribe, sporting in their native element, drew from me the involuntary exclamation: “O, how different the condition of these happy, sportive, joyful, creatures, from what was once my own condition, and what is now the lot of millions of my colored brethren in America!” This occupancy of the elegant grounds of England’s Prime Minister, for the day, by a party of Sabbath School Teachers, was what we should call, in America, a picnic, with this difference, that, instead of each teacher providing his own cakes, and pies, and fruit, they were furnished by men and women, who were allowed to come on to the grounds, with every variety of choice eatables for sale. After strolling over these charming grounds, enjoying the beautiful scenery, and the happy gambols of the brute creation, and the conversation of the many intelligent men and women, with whom we came in contact, we were most unexpectedly, at five o’clock, sent for to visit the elegant mansion of the proprietor. There we found what I will call a surprise party, or at any rate, we were taken by surprise, for we were ushered, three hundred of us at least, into a spacious dining hall, whose dimensions could not have been less than one hundred feet by sixty, and here were tables, groaning under every article of luxury for the palate, which England could supply, and to this bountiful repast we were all made welcome. I was invited to take the head of the table; I never felt so highly honored. The blessing was invoked by singing the two following verses.

如今回想起来最令我愉快的一件事，是我获准同坎特伯雷大主教（Archbishop of Canterbury）的一次长谈。此人在英国社会中位极人臣——王座之下第一人——凡熟悉英国社会的人无不知晓。著名的慈善家塞缪尔·格尼用一封便笺和他家的名帖，把我引见给大主教阁下。大主教在他的府邸亲切地接待了我。我随即同他谈起我族人的境况和我心里的种种计划。他对我表现出最强烈的关切；约莫谈了半小时，他问道：「先生，您是哪个大学毕业的？」「我是从逆境大学毕业的，阁下。」我答道。「逆境大学，」他惊异地抬眼说，「在哪儿？」我看出他的惊讶，便解释道：「我生来就是个奴隶，阁下，少年和此前的大半生都过着奴隶的日子。我从未进过学校，年轻时从未读过圣经，所受的一切陶冶都是在最逆境的境况之下。这便是我说的从逆境大学毕业的意思。」「我明白您了，先生，」他说，「可您竟不是个读书人，这可能吗？」「我不是。」我说。「那我怎么也想不到您没受过博雅教育。我听过许多黑人讲话，却从没见过一个能像您这样遣词的。先生，可否告诉我，您怎样学会我们的语言？」我便尽力向他讲述我的早年生活；说我的习惯向来是留心好的演说者，只模仿那些讲话似乎最纯正的人。「真是惊人，」大主教说，「那么您竟是在对宗教一无所知中长大的？您又是怎样认识基督的呢？」我回答说，是一个贫穷无知的奴隶母亲教我念了主祷文，虽然那时我还不知道怎样才是真正的祷告。「那您又是怎样更进一步认识救主的呢？」我回答说，是借着听人传讲福音。他便请我把那节经文背出来，把种种情形讲给他听。我告诉他我听的头一篇道，和那节经文：「他因着上帝的恩，为人人尝了死味。」「好一节美的经文。」大主教说。我朴素的叙述深深打动了他，他簌簌地落了泪。
塞缪尔·格尼原先告诉我，大主教也许会给我一刻钟的接见；我瞥一眼钟，发觉已经谈了一个半钟头，便起身告辞。他送我到门口，恳切嘱咐我，日后再到英格兰务必再来看他；他热诚地同我握手，眼中噙着泪，亲切地把五枚金镑（约合二十五元）放进我手里，同我道别。我一直敬他为一颗温暖的基督徒之心。
同英国这位令人尊敬的大主教的会面就这样结束了。我第二次到英格兰时，受邀同大批主日学教员一起，到约翰·罗素勋爵——时任英国首相——美丽的园林里消磨一天。他那壮丽的园苑里，蓄着各色各样、来自各气候带的鹿，和毛色光润的野兔——诗人考珀见了也要眼红——还有数不清的禽鸟，羽毛的华彩赛过彩虹，再加上鱼类中的上品，在它们本来的水域里嬉游；我不由得叹道：「哦，这些快乐、活泼、欢腾的生灵的境况，同我当年的境况，同我千百万有色同胞今日的命运，是何等不同啊！」英格兰首相这处雅致的园林，这一天由一班主日学教员占用，用美国的话说，便是一次野餐会；只是有一点不同：不是各人自备糕点馅饼水果，而是有小贩获准入园，摆出各式精美的吃食售卖。我们在那迷人的园子里漫步，赏玩美景，观看走兽飞禽的欢跳，同接触到的许多聪明男女交谈；到了 5 点钟，出人意料地被请去参观园主的华宅。在那里等着我们的，我只好称之为一个惊喜会——无论如何我们是被惊喜了一回：至少我们三百人被引进一座宽敞的宴厅，长宽决不下于一百英尺乘六十英尺；厅内一张张餐桌，压满了英格兰所能供应的一切珍馐美馔；这丰盛的筵席，请我们随意享用。我被邀坐上首席；我从未感到如此殊荣。饭前的祝谢，唱的是下面两节：

After dinner, various toasts were proposed, on various subjects, and in my humble way I offered the following:
> “First to England. Honor to the brave, freedom to the Slave, success to British emancipation. God bless the Queen!”

饭后，大家就各种题目轮番祝酒；我也以我卑微的方式，献上如下一盏：
「第一杯，敬英格兰。荣耀归于勇者，自由归于奴隶，愿英国的解放事业成功。上帝保佑女王！」

Cheers and laughter followed the reading of this toast, succeeded by the usual English exclamations, “*Up, up, up again!*” I again arose and gave, To our most Sovereign Lady, the Queen:
> “May she have a long life, and a happy death. May she reign in righteousness, and rule in love!”
And to her illustrious consort, Prince Albert:

这盏祝酒词一念完，满堂欢呼和笑声，接着便是英国人惯常的喝彩：「*再来，再来，再来！*」我又站起来，为至尊的女王陛下祝酒：
「愿她长寿，善终；愿她以正义治国，以仁爱临民！」

> “May he have peace at home, pleasure abroad, love his Queen, and serve the Lord!”
Among the distinguished persons who made speeches on this joyous occasion, I will mention the names of Rev. William Brock, Hon. Samuel M. Peto, and a Mr. Bess, brother-in-law of Mr. Peto, with his accomplished and beautiful lady. Thus ended one of the pleasantest days of my life.

在这个欢乐场合发表演说的显要人物中，我要提一提威廉·布鲁克牧师（Rev. William Brock）、塞缪尔·M·皮托阁下（Hon. Samuel M. Peto），还有一位贝斯先生（Mr. Bess）——皮托先生的连襟——和他那位娴雅美丽的夫人。我生平最愉快的日子之一，就这样过去了。
