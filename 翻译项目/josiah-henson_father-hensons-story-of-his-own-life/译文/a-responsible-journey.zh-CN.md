## VI A Responsible Journey / 一趟责任重大的旅程

===Original===
My Marriage﻿—Marriage of My Master﻿—His Ruin﻿—Comes to Me for Aid﻿—A Great Enterprise Undertaken﻿—Long and Successful Journey﻿—Incidents by the Way﻿—Struggle Between Inclination and Duty﻿—Duty Triumphant.
When I was about twenty-two years of age, I married a very efficient, and, for a slave, a very well-taught girl, belonging to a neighboring family, reputed to be pious and kind, whom I first met at the religious meetings which I attended. She has borne me twelve children, eight of whom still survive and promise to be the comfort of my declining years.
Things remained in this condition for a considerable period; my occupations being to superintend the farming operations, and to sell the produce in the neighboring markets of Washington and Georgetown. Many respectable people, yet living there, may possibly have some recollection of “Siah,” or “Sie,” (as they used to call me,) as their market-man; but if they have forgotten me, I remember them with an honest satisfaction.
After passing his youth in the manner I have mentioned in a general way, and which I do not wish more particularly to describe, my master, at the age of forty-five, or upwards, married a young woman of eighteen, who had some little property, and more thrift. Her economy was remarkable, and was certainly no addition to the comfort of the establishment. She had a younger brother, Francis, to whom Riley was appointed guardian, and who used to complain﻿—not without reason, I am confident﻿—of the meanness of the provision made for the household; and he would often come to me, with tears in his eyes, to tell me he could not get enough to eat. I made him my friend for life, by sympathising in his emotions and satisfying his appetite, sharing with him the food I took care to provide for my own family. He is still living, and, I understand, one of the wealthiest men in Washington city.

我二十二岁上下时，娶了一个非常能干、而且就奴隶而言受过很好教育的姑娘，她属于邻近一户人家，那家人素有虔敬仁厚之名；我最初是在常去参加的宗教聚会上遇见她的。她为我生了十二个孩子，其中八个如今还在世，有望成为我暮年的安慰。
这样的境况延续了相当一个时期；我的职务是督理农事，并把农产品运到邻近的华盛顿和乔治敦（Georgetown）市场去售卖。许多如今还在那里、受人尊敬的人，或许还记得他们的市场伙计「赛亚」（Siah）或「赛」（Sie）——他们从前就这样叫我；即便他们忘了我，我也怀着老实的欣然记得他们。
我的主人像我一笔带过的那样度过了青年时代——那情形我不愿更细地描述——之后，在四十五岁上下娶了一个十八岁的年轻女子，她有点儿财产，更有算计。她的节俭出了名，也确实没有给这个家添什么舒适。她有个弟弟叫弗朗西斯（Francis），赖利被指定为他的监护人。这孩子常常抱怨——我确信并非无因——家里给他的用度何等刻薄；他常含着眼泪来找我，说他吃不饱。我同情他的情绪，又满足他的胃口，把我特意为自家筹办的食物分给他吃，就此交了他这个终生的朋友。他如今还在世，据我所知，是华盛顿城里最有钱的人之一。

After a time, however, continual dissipation was more than a match for domestic saving. My master fell into difficulty, and from difficulty into a lawsuit with a brother-in-law, who charged him with dishonesty in the management of property confided to him in trust. The lawsuit was protracted enough to cause his ruin of itself.
Harsh and tyrannical as my master had been, I really pitied him in his present distress. At times he was dreadfully dejected, at others crazy with drink and rage. Day after day would he ride over to Montgomery Court House about his business, and every day his affairs grew more desperate. He would come into my cabin to tell me how things were going, but spent the time chiefly in lamenting his misfortunes and cursing his brother-in-law. I tried to comfort him as best I could. He had confidence in my fidelity and judgment, and partly through pride, partly through that divine spirit of love I had learned to worship in Jesus, I entered with interest into all his perplexities. The poor, drinking, furious, moaning creature was utterly incapable of managing his affairs. Shiftlessness, licentiousness and drink had complicated them as much as actual dishonesty.
One night in the month of January, long after I had fallen asleep, he came into my cabin and waked me up. I thought it strange, but for a time he said nothing and sat moodily warming himself at the fire. Then he began to groan and wring his hands. “Sick, massa?” said I. He made no reply but kept on moaning. “Can’t I help you any way, massa?” I spoke tenderly, for my heart was full of compassion at his wretched appearance. At last, collecting himself, he cried, “Oh, Sie! I’m ruined, ruined, ruined!” “How so, massa?” “They’ve got judgment against me, and in less than two weeks every nigger I’ve got will be put up and sold.” Then he burst into a storm of curses at his brother-in-law. I sat silent, powerless to utter a word. Pity for him and terror at the anticipation of my own family’s future fate filled my heart. “And now, Sie,” he continued, “there’s only one way I can save anything. You can do it; won’t you, won’t you?” In his distress he rose and actually threw his arms around me. Misery had levelled all distinctions. “If I can do it, massa, I will. What is it?” Without replying he went on, “won’t you, won’t you? I raised you, Sie; I made you overseer; I know I’ve abused you, Sie, but I didn’t mean it.” Still he avoided telling me what he wanted. “Promise me you’ll do it, boy.” He seemed resolutely bent on having my promise first, well knowing from past experience that what I agreed to do I spared no pains to accomplish. Solicited in this way, with urgency and tears, by the man whom I had so zealously served for over thirty years, and who now seemed absolutely dependent upon his slave﻿—impelled, too, by the fear which he skilfully awakened, that the sheriff would seize everyone who belonged to him, and that all would be separated, or perhaps sold to go to Georgia, or Louisiana﻿—an object of perpetual dread to the slave of the more Northern States﻿—I consented, and promised faithfully to do all I could to save him from the fate impending over him.
At last the proposition came. “I want you to run away, Sie, to your master Amos in Kentucky, and take all the servants along with you.” I could not have been more startled had he asked me to go to the moon. Master Amos was his brother. “Kentucky, massa? Kentucky? I don’t know the way.” “O, it’s easy enough for a smart fellow like you to find it; I’ll give you a pass and tell you just what to do.” Perceiving that I hesitated, he endeavored to frighten me by again referring to the terrors of being sold to Georgia.

然而过了一段时期，不断的挥霍便压倒了家内的节省。主人陷入困境，又由困境陷入与内弟的一场官司；那内弟指控他在受托管理财产时行为不诚实。这场官司拖得够久，单是它本身就足以叫他破产。
主人尽管一向残暴专横，我在他此刻的困厄中却真可怜他。他时而垂头丧气到可怕的地步，时而借酒发狂、暴怒如雷。他日复一日骑马前往蒙哥马利县城（Montgomery Court House）办事，而他的境况一天比一天绝望。他会走进我的小屋，告诉我事情如何如何，但时间多半花在哀叹自己的不幸和咒骂内弟上。我尽力安慰他。他信得过我的忠心和见识；一半出于自尊，一半出于我在耶稣身上学会敬奉的那种神圣的爱人之心，我便怀着关切投入他全部的烦恼。这可怜的、酗酒的、狂暴呻吟的人，全然没有料理自己事务的能力。无能、荒淫和酗酒，把他的事务搅得一团糟，不亚于真正的不诚实。
一月里的一个夜晚，我睡下很久之后，他走进我的小屋把我叫醒。我觉得蹊跷，但他有一会儿什么也不说，只阴郁地坐在火边烤火。随后他开始呻吟、搓手。「病了，老爷？」我说。他不答，只是不停地哼。「我就不能帮您点什么吗，老爷？」我柔声说，他那凄惨的样子使我满心怜悯。终于，他定下神来，喊道：「唉，赛！我完了，完了，完了！」「怎么会呢，老爷？」「他们判我败诉了，不出两个星期，我手下的每个黑鬼都要被摆出来卖掉。」接着他对内弟破口大骂。我坐在那里默然无语。对他的怜悯，和对自家妻儿未来命运的恐惧，一齐塞满我的心。「如今，赛，」他接着说，「只有一条路能保住点东西。你能做到；你肯不肯，肯不肯？」他在苦痛中站起身来，竟然伸出双臂搂住我。苦难抹平了一切尊卑界限。「只要我做得到，老爷，我就做。什么事？」他不回答，只顾说下去：「你肯不肯，肯不肯？是我把你养大的，赛；是我叫你当上监工；我知道我亏待过你，赛，可我不是有意的。」他还是避着不说他要什么。「答应我你一定做，孩子。」他似乎拿定主意非要我先许诺不可；他从过去的经验深知，凡我答应要做的事，我必不辞辛劳去办成。这个我热心伺候了三十多年的人，如今似乎全然要依靠他的奴隶了——他又巧妙地唤醒我的恐惧：法警会把属于他的每个人都抓走，大家将被拆散，或许被卖往佐治亚或路易斯安那——那是较北各州奴隶永远的祸根——他这样恳求着，又急切又带泪；我终于应允，并且忠实地许诺：必竭尽全力救他脱离悬在头上的厄运。
最后他终于说出了他的请求。「我要你跑一趟，赛，到你肯塔基的主人阿莫斯那里去，把所有的仆人都带上。」哪怕他叫我去月亮上，我也不会比这更吃惊。阿莫斯主人是他的兄弟。「肯塔基，老爷？肯塔基？我不认得路啊。」「噢，像你这样伶俐的家伙，找条路还不容易；我给你一张通行证，告诉你该怎么办。」见我犹豫，他又搬出被卖到佐治亚去的可怕，想吓我就范。

For two or three hours he continued to urge the undertaking, appealing to my pride, my sympathies, and my fears, and at last, appalling as it seemed, I told him I would do my best. There were eighteen negroes, besides my wife, two children and myself, to transport nearly a thousand miles, through a country about which I knew nothing, and in midwinter﻿—for it was the month of February, 1825. My master proposed to follow me in a few months, and establish himself in Kentucky.
My mind once made up, I set earnestly about the needful preparations. They were few and easily made. A one-horse wagon, well stocked with oats, meal, bacon, for our own and the horse’s support, was soon made ready. My pride was aroused in view of the importance of my responsibility, and heart and soul I became identified with my master’s project of running off his negroes. The second night after the scheme was formed we were under way. Fortunately for the success of the undertaking, these people had long been under my direction, and were devotedly attached to me in return for the many alleviations I had afforded to their miserable condition, the comforts I had procured them, and the consideration I had always manifested for them. Under these circumstances no difficulty arose from want of submission to my authority. The dread of being separated, and sold away down South, should they remain on the old estate, united them as one man, and kept them patient and alert.
We started from home about eleven o’clock at night, and till the following noon made no permanent halt. The men trudged on foot, the children were put into the wagon, and now and then my wife rode for a while. On we went through Alexandria, Culpepper, Fauquier, Harper’s Ferry, Cumberland, over the mountains on the National Turnpike, to Wheeling. In all the taverns along the road were regular places for the droves of negroes continually passing along under the system of the internal slave trade. In these we lodged, and our lodging constituted our only expense, for our food we carried with us. To all who asked questions I showed my master’s pass, authorizing me to conduct his negroes to Kentucky, and often was the encomium of “smart nigger” bestowed on me, to my immense gratification.
At the places where we stopped for the night, we often met negro-drivers with their droves, who were almost uniformly kept chained to prevent them from running away. The inquiry was often propounded to me by the drivers, “Whose niggers are those?” On being informed, the next inquiry usually was, “Where are they going?” “To Kentucky.” “Who drives them?” “Well, I have charge of them,” was my reply. “What a smart nigger!” was the usual exclamation, with an oath. “Will your master sell you? Come in and stop with us.” In this way I was often invited to pass the evening with them in the barroom; their negroes, in the meantime, lying chained in the pen, while mine were scattered around at liberty.

他一连劝了两三个钟头，一会儿打动我的自尊，一会儿打动我的怜悯，一会儿又打动我的恐惧；最后，尽管这事看上去骇人，我还是告诉他：我愿尽力而为。除了我妻子、两个孩子和我本人之外，还有十八个黑人，要运到将近一千英里之外，途经一片我全然陌生的地域，而且正值隆冬——那时是 1825 年 2 月。我的主人打算过几个月跟过去，在肯塔基安家。
主意一定，我便认真地着手必要的准备。准备不多，也容易办。一辆单马篷车，装足燕麦、玉米粉和腌肉，供我们一路和马匹的食用，很快就备齐了。想到自己责任重大，我的自豪之情油然而生；我全心全意地投进了主人这项把他的黑人运走的计划。计议成立的第二个夜晚，我们就上路了。所幸这项事业的成功有个凭借：这些人久在我的管领之下，又因为我在他们悲惨的境遇中多少有所宽解、为他们弄到些舒适、并始终体恤他们，他们也死心塌地地依恋我。在这种情形下，我的权威没有遇到半点不服管束的困难。唯恐留在老庄园会被拆散、被卖到南方深处去，这层恐惧把他们团结得像一个人，使他们既耐心又警觉。
我们大约夜里十一点从家里动身，直到第二天正午才作较长的停留。男人们步行，孩子们放进篷车，我妻子不时也坐一段。我们一路经过亚历山德里亚（Alexandria）、卡尔佩珀（Culpepper）、福基尔（Fauquier）、哈珀斯渡口（Harper's Ferry）、坎伯兰（Cumberland），取道国道收费公路（National Turnpike）翻山越岭，抵达惠灵（Wheeling）。沿途所有的客栈里，都有专供一队队黑奴歇宿的固定处所——在国内奴隶贸易制度之下，黑奴队伍不绝于途。我们就在这些地方投宿，宿钱是我们唯一的花费，因为食物是随身携带的。凡有人盘问，我就出示主人的通行证，上面授权我把他的黑人带往肯塔基；人们常夸我一声「伶俐的黑鬼」，令我大为得意。
在歇夜的地方，我们常遇见赶着成群黑奴的奴隶贩子；那些黑奴几乎一例戴着镣铐，以防逃跑。贩子们常这样问我：「这些是谁家的黑鬼？」得知之后，接着必问：「往哪儿去？」「去肯塔基。」「谁押的队？」「呃，是我管着他们。」我这样答。「多伶俐的黑鬼！」这是惯常的惊叹，还夹着咒骂。「你家主人卖不卖你？进来跟我们一起歇吧。」就这样，我常被邀去同他们在酒馆里消磨一个晚上；他们的黑人此时锁在栅栏里，而我的那些人却四下散坐，自由自在。

Arriving at Wheeling, in pursuance of the plan laid down by my master, I sold the horse and wagon, and purchased a large boat, called in that region a yawl. Our mode of locomotion was now decidedly more agreeable than tramping along day after day, at the rate we had kept up ever since leaving home. Very little labor at the oars was necessary. The tide floated us steadily along, and we had ample leisure to sleep and recruit our strength.
A new and unexpected trouble now assailed me. On passing along the Ohio shore, we were repeatedly told by persons conversing with us, that we were no longer slaves, but free men, if we chose to be so. At Cincinnati, especially, crowds of colored people gathered round us, and insisted on our remaining with them. They told us we were fools to think of going on and surrendering ourselves up to a new owner; that now we could be our own masters, and put ourselves out of all reach of pursuit. I saw the people under me were getting much excited. Divided counsels and signs of insubordination began to manifest themselves. I began, too, to feel my own resolution giving way. Freedom had ever been an object of my ambition, though no other means of obtaining it had occurred to me but purchasing myself. I had never dreamed of running away. I had a sentiment of honor on the subject. The duties of the slave to his master as appointed over him in the Lord, I had ever heard urged by ministers and religious men. It seemed like outright stealing. And now I felt the devil was getting the upper hand of me. Strange as all this may seem, I really felt it then. Entrancing as the idea was, that the coast was clear for a run for freedom, that I might liberate my companions, might carry off my wife and children, and some day own a house and land, and be no longer despised and abused﻿—still my notions of right were against it. I had promised my master to take his property to Kentucky, and deposit it with his brother Amos. Pride, too, came in to confirm me. I had undertaken a great thing; my vanity had been flattered all along the road by hearing myself praised; I thought it would be a feather in my cap to carry it through thoroughly; and had often painted the scene in my imagination of the final surrender of my charge to master Amos, and the immense admiration and respect with which he would regard me.
Under the influence of these impressions, and seeing that the allurements of the crowd were producing a manifest effect, I sternly assumed the captain, and ordered the boat to be pushed off into the stream. A shower of curses followed me from the shore; but the negroes under me, accustomed to obey, and, alas! too degraded and ignorant of the advantages of liberty to know what they were forfeiting, offered no resistance to my command.
Often since that day has my soul been pierced with bitter anguish at the thought of having been thus instrumental in consigning to the infernal bondage of slavery so many of my fellow-beings. I have wrestled in prayer with God for forgiveness. Having experienced myself the sweetness of liberty, and knowing too well the after misery of numbers of many of them, my infatuation has seemed to me the unpardonable sin. But I console myself with the thought that I acted according to my best light, though the light that was in me was darkness. Those were my days of ignorance. I knew not the glory of free manhood. I knew not that the title-deed of the slave-owner is robbery and outrage.
What advantages I may have personally lost by thus throwing away an opportunity of obtaining freedom, I know not; but the perception of my own strength of character, the feeling of integrity, the sentiment of high honor, I thus gained by obedience to what I believed right, these advantages I do know and prize. He that is faithful over a little, will alone be faithful over much. Before God, I tried to do my best, and the error of judgment lies at the door of the degrading system under which I had been nurtured.

到了惠灵，遵照主人定下的计划，我卖掉马和车，买了一条大船——那地方管这种船叫约尔艇（yawl）。我们的行进方式这下比一天天徒步赶路舒服多了，自离家以来我们一直是那个速度。划桨用不了多少力气。水流平稳地载着我们前行，我们有充裕的闲暇睡觉养神。
这时一个始料未及的新麻烦缠上了我。沿着俄亥俄河岸行进时，同我们搭话的人一再告诉我们：我们已经不是奴隶，只要愿意，就是自由人了。在辛辛那提尤其是这样，成群的有色人围住我们，执意挽留我们同他们住下。他们说，还想着往前走、把自己交给一个新主人，简直是傻瓜；说如今我们可以做自己的主人，可以躲到谁也追不到的地方。我看出我手下的人激动得很。意见分歧和不服管束的苗头开始显露。我自己也开始感到决心动摇。自由素来是我心志所向，只是除赎买自身之外，我从没想到过别的门路。我做梦也不曾想过逃跑。在这件事上我有一种道义上的廉耻感。奴隶对其奉上帝之命管辖他的主人应尽本分——这是我素来听牧师和虔敬之人宣讲的。这样做简直像是明抢。而这时我觉得魔鬼正在占我的上风。这一切如今看来何等奇怪，可我当时确实这样觉得。这念头迷人已极：通往自由的路已经敞开，我可以解放我的同伴，可以带着妻子儿女远走高飞，有朝一日置起房屋田地，再不受人轻贱欺凌——然而我的是非之心仍然反对这样做。我已答应主人把他的财产带到肯塔基，交到他兄弟阿莫斯手里。自尊心也来给我打气。我承担的是一件大事；一路上听着人家夸我，我的虚荣心早被捧得飘飘然；我想，把它彻头彻尾办妥，将是我莫大的荣耀；我还常在想象中描画那最后一幕——把我押送的人马交给阿莫斯主人，他将怀着何等的惊叹与敬重看待我。
在这些想法的作用下，又见人群的诱惑正在产生明显的影响，我便板起面孔摆出船长的威严，下令把船撑进河心。岸上跟着泼来一阵咒骂；但我手下的黑人惯于服从，而且唉！他们太堕落、太不识自由的宝贵，不知道自己正失去什么，对我的命令竟毫无抗拒。
自那日以后，一想到自己竟这样亲手把那么多同胞交进奴隶制那地狱般的枷锁，我的灵魂常常被刺得剧痛。我曾在祷告中与上帝角力，求他赦免。自从亲尝了自由的甘美，又深知他们之中许多人日后的苦难，我的那段痴迷在我看来简直像不可赦免的罪。但我以此自慰：我当时是照自己最好的见识行事的，尽管我里面的光原是黑暗。那是我蒙昧无知的年月。我不知道自由人格的光荣。我不知道奴隶主的产权凭证就是抢劫与暴行。
这样扔掉一个获得自由的机会，我个人究竟损失了什么好处，我不知道；但因听从了我认定为对的事而得到的那些益处——对自己性格力量的自觉，正直之感，高尚的道义心——这些我却知道，而且珍视。在小事上忠心的人，在大事上才靠得住。在上帝面前，我已尽了全力；那判断上的过失，要归罪于养育我的那堕落的制度。
