## V Waiting / 五 等待
===Original===
What I saw when I cautiously peered over the rim of that little hollow was (beginning at the top) a vast expanse of pale-blue sky, with fleecy clouds down near the horizon already tinged with pink reflections from the sunset far off behind my back. Then came a shining glimpse of the North Sea; then a rim of green islands, rising on the right to high heather hills; then the landlocked waters and the grey ships now getting blurred and less distinct; then some portions of the green land that sloped up to where I lay; and among these fields, and not far away from me, the steep roof and gable-top of a grey, old-fashioned house. It was the parish manse, the pacific abode of the professional exponent and exemplar of peace﻿—the parish minister; and yet, curiously enough, it was that house which my eyes devoured.
The single ship had now ceased firing and anchored with her consorts, the fleet had grown too indistinct to note anything of its composition, and there was nothing to distract my attention from the house. I looked at it hard and long and studied the lie of the ground between it and me, and then I lay down on a couch of soft heather and began to think.
So far as I could see I had done nothing yet to draw suspicion to this particular spot, for no one at all seemed to have seen me, but it was manifest that there would be a hard and close hunt for the mysterious motorcyclist on the morrow. I began to half regret that I had cut that telegraph wire and advertised myself so patently for what I was. Now it was quite obvious that for some days to come motorcycling would be an unhealthy pastime in these islands. Even at night how many ears would be listening for my “phut-phut-phut,” and how many eyes would be scanning the dark roads? A few judiciously placed and very simple barricades﻿—a mere bar on two uprights, with a sentry beside each﻿—and what chance would I have of getting back to that distant bay, especially as I had just been seen so near it?
“However,” I said to myself, “that is looking too far ahead. It was not my fault I brought this hornet’s nest about my ears. Just bad luck and a clumsy sailor!”

我小心翼翼地从那个小洼地的边沿探头张望时，看到的（从顶上数起）是：一大片淡蓝色的天空，贴近地平线处的絮状云朵已染上粉色，那是远远照在我身后的落日返照。接着是北海闪亮的一角；接着是一环绿色的岛屿，在右侧升起为高高的石楠山冈；接着是那片闭锁的水域和灰色的舰船，此刻已模糊难辨；接着是几片向上斜到我卧处的绿色陆地；而在这片田野间，离我不远，是一座灰色老式房屋的陡顶和山墙尖。那是本教区的牧师住宅，和平的职业阐释者与化身——教区牧师——那座安宁的居所；然而说来奇怪，恰恰是那座房子，让我目光贪馋地盯住不放。
那艘单独的军舰已停止射击，随同它的伙伴们下了锚，舰队已模糊得看不出编成的细节，再没有什么能分散我对那座房子的注意了。我久久地紧紧盯住它，研究它与我这之间的地势，随后往一榻柔软的石楠上躺下，开始思考。
依我看，我还没有做过什么引人怀疑这处地点的事，因为似乎根本没有人见到过我；但显而易见，明天必将对这个神秘的骑摩托车者展开一场严密紧逼的搜捕。我开始有点后悔剪断了那根电报线，把自己是何等人物张扬得那样明白。事到如今已很清楚，往后好些天里，在这些岛上骑摩托车将是一种不利于健康的消遣。即便在夜里，会有多少只耳朵谛听着我的“噗噗噗”，多少双眼睛扫视着黑暗中的道路？几处布置得当、构造简单的路障——不过是两根立柱架一根横木，每处再站一名哨兵——我还能有什么机会赶回那遥远的海湾，何况我刚刚又是在离它那么近的地方被人看见？
“不过，”我对自己说，“这想得太远了。把这一窝马蜂招到耳边，不是我的过错。只能怪运气不好，加上一个笨手笨脚的水兵！”

===Original===
Just then I heard something approaching on the road below me, and in a minute or two it became unmistakably the sound of a horse and trap. At one place I could catch a glimpse of this road between the hummocks of heather, and I raised myself again and looked out. In a moment the horse and trap appeared and I got a sensation I shall not soon forget. Not that there seemed to the casual passerby anything in the least sensational about this equipage. He would merely have noticed that it contained, besides the driver, a few articles of luggage and a gentleman in a flat-looking felt hat and an overcoat﻿—both of them black. This gentleman was sitting with his back to me (he was in a small wagonette), but I could scarcely doubt who it was. But only arriving tonight!
Curiosity and anxiety so devoured me that I ran a little risk. Getting out of my hollow, I crawled forward on my hands and knees till I could catch a glimpse of the side road leading to that house; and there I lay flat on my face and watched.
Down the steep hill the horse proceeded at a walk, and what between my impatience to make sure, and my consciousness of my own rashness in quitting even for a moment my sheltered hollow, I passed a few very uncomfortable minutes. The light by this time was failing fast, but it was quite clear enough to see (or be seen), and at last I caught one more glimpse of that horse and trap﻿—turning off the road just where I expected. And then I was crawling back with more haste than dignity.
It was “him”! And he had only arrived tonight. If it had not been for my accident, in what a nice dilemma I should have been landed! Never did I bless anyone more fervently than that awkward sailor who had let my cycle slip, and as for the wave of salt water which wet it, it seemed to have sprung from the age of miracles.
The trouble of my discovery and its possible consequences still remained, but I thought little enough of that now, so thankful did I feel for what had *not* happened. And then I stretched myself out again on the heather, waiting with all the patience I could muster for the falling of night.

就在那时，我听见下面的路上有什么东西驶近，一两分钟后，那声音已明白无误是马车的动静。有一处地方，我能从石楠丛的土丘间瞥见这段路，我便又撑起身子望出去。马车很快就出现了，我得到一种不会轻易忘却的感觉。倒不是这辆车在过路人眼里有什么丝毫耸动之处。他只会注意到，车上除了赶车的，还有几件行李和一位戴扁平毡帽、穿大衣的先生——两样都是黑色。那位先生背朝我坐着（他坐的是一辆小型四轮马车），可我几乎不会怀疑他是谁。但他竟今晚才到！
好奇心和焦灼如此吞噬着我，以致我稍稍冒了点险。出了洼地，我手脚并用地匍匐向前，爬到能瞥见通向那座房子的支路的地方，便伏在地上观望。
那匹马一步一步走下陡坡，我既急切地想看个分明，又意识到自己哪怕片刻离开那处隐蔽的洼地也是孟浪，两下夹攻，度过了很难受的几分钟。这时天光正迅速暗下去，但要看见（或被看见）还是绰绰有余的，我终于又瞥见了那辆马车——恰在我料想的地方拐下了大路。随后我往回爬去，顾不得体面，只求快些。
正是“他”！而他今晚才到。倘若不是我这桩意外，我会陷入怎样的尴尬境地！我从未如此热诚地感念过谁，除了那个让我的车滑落的笨拙水兵；至于打湿车子的那股咸水，简直像出自奇迹迭现的年代。
我被发觉的麻烦及其可能的后果依然存在，但此刻我几乎不去想它了，因为没发生的事已让我感激不尽。随后我又在石楠丛中舒展开身子，拿出全部的耐心，等待夜幕降临。
