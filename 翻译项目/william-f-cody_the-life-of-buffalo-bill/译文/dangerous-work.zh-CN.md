## XXXI Dangerous Work / 第三十一章 凶险差事
===Original===

One evening while we were in camp on the Yellowstone at the mouth of Powder River, I was informed that the commanding officers had selected Louis Richard, a half breed, and myself to accompany General Mills on a scouting expedition on the steamer *Far West*, down the Yellowstone as far as Glendive Creek. We were to ride on the pilot house and keep a sharp lookout on both sides of the river for Indian trails that might have crossed the stream. The idea of scouting on a steamboat was indeed a novel one to me, and I anticipated a pleasant trip.
At daylight next morning we reported on board the steamer to General Mills, who had with him four or five companies of his regiment. We were somewhat surprised when he asked us where our horses were, as we had not supposed that horses would be needed if the scouting was to be done on the steamer. He said we might need them before we got back, and thereupon we had the animals brought on board. In a few minutes we were booming down the river, at the rate of about twenty miles an hour.
The steamer *Far West* was commanded by Captain Grant Marsh, whom I found to be a “brick.” I had often heard of him, for he was and is yet one of the best known river captains in the country. He it was who, with his steamer the *Far West*, transported the wounded men from the battle of the Little Big Horn to Fort Abraham Lincoln on the Missouri River, and on that trip he made the fastest steamboat time on record. He was a skillful and experienced pilot, handling his boat with remarkable dexterity.
While Richard and myself were at our stations on the pilot house, the steamer with a full head of steam went flying past islands, around bends, over sand bars, at a rate that was exhilarating. Presently I thought I could see horses grazing in a distant bend of the river and I reported the fact to General Mills, who asked Captain Marsh if he could land the boat near a large tree which he pointed out to him.
“Yes, sir; I can land her there, and make her climb the tree if necessary,” said he.
On reaching the spot designated, General Mills ordered two companies ashore, while Richard and myself were ordered to take our horses off the boat and push out as rapidly as possible to see if there were Indians in the vicinity.

一天傍晚，当我们在保德河（Powder River）河口附近的黄石河（Yellowstone）畔扎营时，我接到上级通报：指挥官们挑中了混血儿路易斯·理查德（Louis Richard）与我，随同米尔斯将军（General Mills）搭乘“远西号”（Far West）轮船执行一次侦察任务，顺黄石河直下直到格兰戴夫溪（Glendive Creek）。我们的职责是在舵手室顶上放哨，密切监视大河两岸，搜寻可能横渡过河的印第安人（Indians）踪迹。乘着轮船去侦察，对我而言委实新鲜，我也满心以为这会是一趟惬意的差事。
次日破晓，我们登上轮船向米尔斯将军报到，他随行带着麾下军团的四五个连队。将军开口询问我们的战马何在，这倒让我们颇感诧异，因为我们原以为既然坐船侦察，自然用不上马匹。将军却说在返程前很可能会用上马，于是我们便把牲口牵上了船。不过几分钟工夫，轮船便开足马力顺流疾驰，时速约达20英里。
“远西号”轮船由格兰特·马什船长（Captain Grant Marsh）掌舵，我发现他当真是个豪爽过硬的汉子。我早就久仰他的大名，因为无论当年还是眼下，他都是举国闻名的大河领航巨擘。正是这位船长，曾驾驶着他的“远西号”，把小大角（Little Big Horn）战役中的伤员一路运往密苏里河（Missouri River）畔的亚伯拉罕·林肯堡（Fort Abraham Lincoln），并且在那次航行中创下了有史以来最快的汽船航速纪录。他是一位经验老到、技艺绝伦的舵手，驾驭起轮船来游刃有余、精妙无比。
当理查德与我各就各位伫立在舵手室顶上时，轮船加足马力，劈波斩浪掠过一座座江心岛屿、绕过一道道险弯、冲过片片浅滩沙洲，其疾如飞的势头令人心旷神怡。未几，我隐约瞧见远处河湾处有一群马正在吃草，便立即向米尔斯将军报告。将军指着岸边一棵大树，询问马什船长能否在那儿靠岸。
“没问题，长官；我能把船靠过去，真有必要的话，我还能让这艘船爬到树上去！”船长答道。
抵运指定地点后，米尔斯将军下令两个连队登岸，理查德与我则奉命把马匹牵下船，火速向外围推进，探查附近是否有印第安人出没。

While we were getting ashore, Captain Marsh remarked that if there was only a good heavy dew on the grass he would shoot the steamer ashore and take us on the scout without the trouble of leaving the boat.
It was a false alarm, however, as the objects we had seen proved to be Indian graves. Quite a large number of braves who had probably been killed in some battle, had been buried on scaffolds, according to the Indian custom, and some of their clothing had been torn loose from the bodies by the wolves and was waving in the air.
On arriving at Glendive Creek we found that Colonel Rice and his company of the Fifth Infantry, who had been sent there by General Mills, had built quite a good little fort with their trowel-bayonets﻿—a weapon which Colonel Rice was the inventor of, and which is, by the way, a very useful implement of war, as it can be used for a shovel in throwing up entrenchments and can be profitably utilized in several other ways. On the day previous to our arrival, Colonel Rice had a fight with a party of Indians, and had killed two or three of them at long range with his Rodman cannon.
The *Far West* was to remain at Glendive over night, and General Mills wished to send dispatches back to General Terry at once. At his request I took the dispatches and rode seventy-five miles that night through the bad lands of the Yellowstone, and reached General Terry’s camp next morning, after having nearly broken my neck a dozen times or more.
There being but little prospect of any more fighting, I determined to go East as soon as possible to organize a new “Dramatic Combination,” and have a new drama written for me, based upon the Sioux war. This I knew would be a paying investment as the Sioux campaign had excited considerable interest. So I started down the river on the steamer Yellowstone en route to Fort Beauford. On the same morning Generals Terry and Crook pulled out for Powder River, to take up the old Indian trail which we had recently left.
The steamer had proceeded down the stream about twenty miles when it was met by another boat on its way up the river, having on board General Whistler and some fresh troops for General Terry’s command. Both boats landed, and almost the first person I met was my old friend and partner, Texas Jack, who had been sent out as a dispatch carrier for the *New York Herald*.

正当我们涉水登岸之际，马什船长打趣道，草地上哪怕只要有一层浓重的晨露，他就能一口气把轮船开上岸去，省得我们费劲下船跑路，直接载着我们去侦察。
不过这只是一场虚惊，我们先前瞅见的目标原来是印第安人的坟茔。相当多大概是在某场战斗中阵亡的印第安勇士，按照部落习俗被安放在脚手架式的树葬台上收殓；野狼撕扯烂了裹尸的衣物，残破的布条正迎风飘摇。
抵达格兰戴夫溪后，我们发现米尔斯将军先前派驻此地的赖斯上校（Colonel Rice）与其麾下第五步兵团的连队，已经用他们的镘刀刺刀（trowel-bayonets）筑起了一座颇为像样的小堡垒——顺便提一句，这种武器正是赖斯上校发明的，在行军作战中极其好使，既能充当铲子挖掘掩体战壕，还能派上许多其他用场。就在我们到达的前一天，赖斯上校还与一伙印第安人交了火，并用他的罗德曼加农炮（Rodman cannon）进行远程轰击，当场击毙了两三名敌手。
“远西号”轮船当晚在格兰戴夫停泊过夜，米尔斯将军希望能立即派人把快报送回给特里将军（General Terry）。应将军之请，我怀揣公文连夜跨马飞奔，在黄石河沿岸崎岖荒地中穿行了75英里，一路上险些折断脖子十几次，终于在次日清晨赶到了特里将军的营地。
眼见短期内已无大规模激战的苗头，我决定尽早赶往东部，组建一支新的“巡回戏剧联合剧团（Dramatic Combination）”，并请人以眼下的苏族战争（Sioux war）为题材为我量身定做一部新戏。我心知这定是一桩稳赚不赔的好买卖，毕竟苏族战役在全美激起了轰动一时的极大关注。于是我登上了“黄石号”（Yellowstone）轮船顺流而下，准备取道前往博福德堡（Fort Beauford）。就在同一个清晨，特里将军与克鲁克将军（General Crook）亦率部拔营前往保德河，沿我们不久前追踪过的印第安旧踪追寻下去。
轮船顺水航行了约20英里，迎面遇上了另一艘逆流而上的船只，船上载着惠斯勒将军（General Whistler）以及增援特里将军部属的新生部队。两船靠岸停泊，我下船见到的第一批人中，竟就有我的老搭档兼老朋友“德克萨斯·杰克”（Texas Jack），他此行正受聘担任《纽约先驱报》（New York Herald）的随军通信速递员。

General Whistler, upon learning that General Terry had left the Yellowstone, asked me to carry to him some important dispatches from General Sheridan, and although I objected, he insisted upon my performing this duty, saying that it would only detain me a few hours longer; as an extra inducement he offered me the use of his own thoroughbred horse, which was on the boat. I finally consented to go, and was soon speeding over the rough and hilly country towards Powder River; and I delivered the dispatches to General Terry that same evening. General Whistler’s horse, although a good animal, was not used to such hard riding, and was far more exhausted by the journey than I was.
After I had taken a lunch, General Terry asked me if I would carry some dispatches back to General Whistler, and I replied that I would. Captain Smith, General Terry’s aide-de-camp, offered me his horse for the trip, and it proved to be an excellent animal; for I rode him that same night forty miles over the bad lands in four hours, and reached General Whistler’s steamboat at one o’clock. During my absence the Indians had made their appearance on the different hills in the vicinity, and the troops from the boat had had several skirmishes with them. When General Whistler had finished reading the dispatches, he said:
“Cody, I want to send information to General Terry concerning the Indians who have been skirmishing around here all day. I have been trying all the evening long to induce someone to carry my dispatches to him, but no one seems willing to undertake the trip, and I have got to fall back on you. It is asking a great deal, I know, as you have just ridden eighty miles; but it is a case of necessity, and if you’ll go, Cody, I’ll see that you are well paid for it.”
“Never mind about the pay,” said I, “but get your dispatches ready, and I’ll start at once.”
In a few minutes he handed me the package, and mounting the same horse which I had ridden from General Terry’s camp, I struck out for my destination. It was two o’clock in the morning when I left the boat, and at eight o’clock I rode into General Terry’s camp, just as he was about to march﻿—having made one hundred and twenty miles in twenty-two hours.

General Terry, after reading the dispatches, halted his command, and then rode on and overtook General Crook, with whom he held a council; the result was that Crook’s command moved on in the direction which they had been pursuing, while Terry’s forces marched back to the Yellowstone and crossed the river on steamboats. At the urgent request of General Terry I accompanied the command on a scout in the direction of the Dry Fork of the Missouri, where it was expected we would strike some Indians.
The first march out from the Yellowstone was made in the night, as we wished to get into the hills without being discovered by the Sioux scouts. After marching three days, a little to the east of north, we reached the buffalo range, and discovered fresh signs of Indians, who had evidently been killing buffaloes. General Terry now called on me to carry dispatches to Colonel Rice, who was still camped at the mouth of Glendive Creek, on the Yellowstone﻿—distant about eighty miles from us.
Night had set in with a storm, and a drizzling rain was falling when, at ten o’clock, I started on this ride through a section of country with which I was entirely unacquainted. I traveled through the darkness a distance of about thirty-five miles, and at daylight I rode into a secluded spot at the head of a ravine where stood a bunch of ash trees, and there I concluded to remain till night; for I considered it a dangerous undertaking to cross the wide prairies in broad daylight﻿—especially as my horse was a poor one.
I accordingly unsaddled my animal, and ate a hearty breakfast of bacon and hard tack which I had stored in the saddle-pockets; then, after taking a smoke, I lay down to sleep, with my saddle for a pillow. In a few minutes I was in the land of dreams.
After sleeping some time﻿—I can’t tell how long﻿—I was suddenly awakened by a roaring, rumbling sound. I instantly seized my gun, sprang to my horse, and hurriedly secreted him in the brush. Then I climbed up the steep side of the bank and cautiously looked over the summit; in the distance I saw a large herd of buffaloes which were being chased and fired at by twenty or thirty Indians. Occasionally a buffalo would drop out of the herd, but the Indians kept on until they had killed ten or fifteen. They then turned back, and began to cut up their game.

特里将军拆阅文书后下令部队原地驻扎，随后亲自策马赶往前头追上克鲁克将军，两人举行了紧急磋商。磋商达成的方案是：克鲁克麾下的部队沿着原先既定路线继续推进，特里将军的人马则折返黄石河并搭乘汽船渡河。在特里将军的殷切请求下，我随同部队一同向密苏里河干岔口（Dry Fork）方向前出侦察，军方估计我们将在那里与一伙印第安人正面相撞。
从黄石河出发的第一程行军选在夜间展开，以便队伍能神不知鬼不觉地扎入丘陵腹地而不被苏族的斥候哨兵察觉。大军朝东北偏北方向行军了3天后，抵运了野牛群生息的牧野地带，沿途发现了印第安人留下的新鲜痕迹，显然对方刚刚在此地猎杀过野牛。此时特里将军又找上我，让我携带加急文书送交给赖斯上校，彼时上校仍驻扎在黄石河畔格兰戴夫溪的河口——距离我们足有约80英里之遥。
入夜后狂风呼啸，细雨绵绵。夜间10点整，我孤身一人策马冲入这片我素未涉足的全然陌生地界。我冒着浓重的夜色疾驰了大约35英里，待黎明破晓时分，我策马来到一处深谷源头的僻静隐蔽处，此处生长着一丛白蜡树。我思忖再三，决定在此潜伏挨到天黑；毕竟在我看来，在大白天毫无掩护地横穿辽阔大平原委实是一场九死一生的冒险，更何况我的坐骑脚力相当平庸。
于是我卸下马鞍，就着马鞍口袋里随身携带的腌肉和干硬饼干饱餐了一顿早点；抽过一袋烟后，我头枕马鞍沉沉睡去。不出几分钟，我便已沉入梦乡。
睡了不知多久——我实在辨不清到底过了多少时辰——一阵轰隆隆滚雷般的剧烈巨响猛然将我惊醒。我刹那间一把抓起身旁的枪支，飞身冲到战马旁，急匆匆将马匹拉进茂密灌木丛中藏匿妥当。紧接着我手脚并用攀上陡峭土坡，小心翼翼探出头向坡顶外张望：只见远方大平原上一大群野牛正狂暴狂奔，身后紧追着20或30名边追边射的印第安人。时不时有野牛中弹扑倒掉队，但这伙印第安人穷追不舍，直到猎杀了10到15头野牛才勒马停手。随后他们掉转马头折返回来，开始动手切割宰剥猎获的猎物。

I saddled my horse and tied him to a small tree where I could reach him conveniently in case the Indians should discover me by finding my trail and following it. I then crawled carefully back to the summit of the bluff, and in a concealed position watched the Indians for two hours, during which time they were occupied in cutting up the buffaloes and packing the meat on their ponies. When they had finished this work they rode off in the direction whence they had come and on the line which I had proposed to travel. It appeared evident to me that their camp was located somewhere between me and Glendive Creek, but I had no idea of abandoning the trip on that account.
I waited till nightfall before resuming my journey, and then I bore off to the east for several miles, and by making a semicircle to avoid the Indians, I got back on my original course, and then pushed on rapidly to Colonel Rice’s camp, which I reached just at daylight.
Colonel Rice had been fighting Indians almost every day since he had been encamped at this point, and he was very anxious to notify General Terry of the fact. Of course I was requested to carry his dispatches. After remaining at Glendive a single day I started back to find General Terry, and on the third day out I overhauled him at the head of Deer Creek while on his way to Colonel Rice’s camp. He was not, however, going in the right direction, but bearing too far to the east, and I so informed him. He then asked me to guide the command and I did so.
On arriving at Glendive I bade goodbye to the General and his officers and took passage on the steamer *Far West*, which was on her way down the Missouri. At Bismarck I left the steamer, and proceeded by rail to Rochester, New York, where I met my family.
Mr. J. Clinton Hall, manager of the Rochester Opera House, was very anxious to have me play an engagement at his theatre. I agreed to open the season with him as soon as I had got my drama written; and I did so, meeting with an enthusiastic reception.
My new drama was arranged for the stage by J. V. Arlington, the actor. It was a five-act play, without head or tail, and it made no difference at which act we commenced the performance. Before we had finished the season several newspaper critics, I have been told, went crazy in trying to follow the plot.

我迅速备好马鞍，把坐骑拴在一棵顺手就能牵到的小树上，防备印第安人若循着我的蹄印追查过来时好随时策马脱身。随后我轻手轻脚爬回断崖顶端，在隐蔽的藏身处足足观察了这伙印第安人2小时。在此期间，他们忙着大卸野牛骨肉，把沉重的鲜肉捆扎在各家的矮马背上。待忙完这桩营生后，他们便调转马头朝原路折返而去——而那恰恰是我计划前行的行军方向。我心里很清楚，他们的宿营大寨必定就横亘在我与格兰戴夫溪之间的必经之路上，但我绝无半点因噎废食放弃使命的打算。

抵运格兰戴夫后，我向将军及其随行军官们挥手作别，随即登上了正顺密苏里河南下的“远西号”轮船。行至俾斯麦（Bismarck）后我离船上岸，改乘火车一路奔赴纽约州罗切斯特（Rochester, New York），与我的家人团聚。
罗切斯特歌剧院（Rochester Opera House）的经理J·克林顿·霍尔（J. Clinton Hall）先生极其热切地盼望我能登上他的剧场登台献艺。我答应他只要新戏本一脱稿便率先来他的剧院开启巡演演季；剧目落成后我如约履诺，首演当即收获了全城观众雷鸣般的狂热追捧。
