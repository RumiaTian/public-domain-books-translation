## XXIII Administering Justice / 第二十三章 伸张正义
===Original===

On returning to Fort McPherson we found that Brevet Major General W. H. Emory, Colonel of the Fifth Cavalry, and Brevet Brigadier General Thomas Duncan, Lieutenant Colonel of the regiment, had arrived there during our absence. General Emory had been appointed to the command of the District of the Republican, with headquarters at Fort McPherson. As the command had been continually in the field, it was generally thought that we were to have a long rest; and it looked as if this post was to be my home and headquarters for some time to come. I accordingly sent to St. Louis for my wife and daughter to join me there. General Emory promised to build a house for me, but before the building was completed my family arrived.
During the fall of 1869 there were two or three scouting expeditions sent out; but nothing of very great importance was accomplished by them. I found Fort McPherson to be a lively and pleasant post to be stationed at, especially as there was plenty of game in the vicinity, and within a day’s ride there were large herds of deer, antelope and elk.
During the winter of 1869﻿–﻿70 I spent a great deal of time in pursuit of game, and during the season we had two hunting parties of Englishmen there; one party being that of Mr. Flynn, and the other that of George Boyd Houghton, of London﻿—the well known caricaturist. Among their amusements were several horse races, which I arranged, and in which Tall Bull and Powder Face were invariably the winners. Tall Bull by this time had such a reputation as a running horse, that it was difficult to make a race for him. I remember one however, in which he ran against a horse in Captain Spaulding’s Company of the Second Cavalry.
This race was rather a novel affair. I had made a bet that Tall Bull would beat the Second Cavalry horse around a one mile track, and, during the time that he was running, I would jump off and on the horse eight times. I rode the horse bareback; seized his mane with my left hand, rested my right on his withers, and while he was going at full speed, I jumped to the ground, and sprang again upon his back, eight times in succession. Such feats I had seen performed in the circus and I had practiced considerably at it with Tall Bull, so that I was certain of winning the race in the manner agreed upon.

Emory）与该团副团长、名誉准将托马斯·邓肯（Brevet Brigadier General Thomas Duncan）已在我们出征期间抵运该哨所。埃默里将军被任命为里帕布利肯军区（District of the Republican）司令官，指挥部设在麦克弗森堡。由于部队此前常年征战在野外前线，全军上下普遍认为大家即将迎来一段漫长的休整期；看起来这座哨所将在今后相当长一段岁月里成为我的常驻大本营与安乐窝。我随即打发人前往圣路易斯，接我的妻子与女儿前来要塞与我团聚。埃默里将军承诺亲自为我修建一座宅邸，但在房屋竣工落成之前，我的家眷便已风尘仆仆抵运要塞。
1869年秋，要塞派出过两三支前哨侦察支队，但均未建树什么重大战果。我发现驻防麦克弗森堡是一段极其惬意欢快的岁月，尤其是哨所周边野生猎物异常充裕，只消策马骑行一天的路程，便能撞见大群的鹿、叉角羚与麋鹿。
1869至1870年冬，我把大把大把的时光倾注在狩猎荒原上。那个季节里，有两批英国贵族狩猎旅行团远道而来造访此地；一批由弗林先生（Mr. Flynn）带队，另一批则是伦敦赫赫有名的讽刺漫画家乔治·博伊德·霍顿（George Boyd Houghton）一行。在他们的诸多消遣娱乐项目中，包括好几场由我亲自组织的赛马盛会，在这些赛马中，我的名驹“高牛”（Tall Bull）与“扑面火药”（Powder Face）毫无悬念地场场夺冠。此时此刻，高牛作为平原飞毛腿的神驹盛名早已威震四方，以至于很难再给它物色到旗鼓相当的对手。但我仍清晰记得其中一场精彩角逐，当时它的对手是第二骑兵团斯波尔丁上尉（Captain Spaulding）连里的一匹顶尖快马。
这场赛马相当新颖刺激。我立下赌注：高牛不仅能在一英里环形跑道上跑赢第二骑兵团的战马，而且在它全速狂飙奔驰期间，我还要在马背上连续翻上跃下整整八次。我光着马背跨骑其上，左手死死抓住马鬃，右手紧按在马肩隆上；就在战马疾驰如电、全速爆发之际，我纵身跃向地面，双脚点地后腾空弹起重新跃上马背，如此一连利落地完成了整整八次。这等绝活我过去只在马戏团特技表演里见过，平日里我骑着高牛苦练过许多次，因此成竹在胸，深信自己必定能按约定规矩一举夺魁。

Early one morning, in the spring of 1870, the Indians, who had approached during the night, stole some twenty-one head of horses from Mr. John Burke﻿—a Government contractor﻿—Ben Gallagher and Jack Waite. They also ran off some horses from the post; among the number being my pony Powder Face. The commandant at once ordered out Lieutenant Thomas with Company I of the Fifth Cavalry, and directed me to accompany them as trailer. We discovered the trail after some little difficulty, as the Indians were continually trying to hide it, and followed it sixty miles, when darkness set in.
We were now within about four miles of Red Willow Creek and I felt confident the Indians would camp that night in that vicinity. Advising Lieutenant Thomas to halt his company and “lay low” I proceeded on to the creek, where, moving around cautiously, I suddenly discovered horses feeding in a bend of the stream on the opposite side. I hurried back to the troops with the information, and Lieutenant Thomas moved his company to the bank of the creek, with the intention of remaining there until daylight, and then, if possible, surprise the Indians.
Just at break of day we mounted our horses, and after riding a short distance we ascended a slight elevation, when, not over one hundred yards distant, we looked down into the Indian camp. The Indians, preparing to make an early start, had driven up their horses and were in the act of mounting, when they saw us charging down upon them. In a moment they sprang upon their ponies and dashed away. Had it not been for the creek, which lay between us and them, we would have got them before they could have mounted their horses; but as it was rather miry, we were unexpectedly delayed. The Indians fired some shots at us while we were crossing, but as soon as we got across we went for them in hot pursuit. A few of the redskins had not had time to mount and had started on foot down the creek toward the brush. One of these was killed.
A number of our soldiers, who had been detailed before the charge to gather up any of the Indian horses that would be stampeded, succeeded in capturing thirty-two. I hurriedly looked over them to see if Powder Face was among them; but he was not there. Starting in pursuit of the fugitives I finally espied an Indian mounted on my favorite, dashing away and leading all the others.

1870年春的一个清晨，趁着夜色掩护潜行摸近的一伙印第安人，盗走了军方承包商约翰·伯克先生（Mr. John Burke）、本·加拉格尔（Ben Gallagher）与杰克·韦特（Jack Waite）的大约21匹马。他们还从哨所马厩里顺手卷走了几匹战马，我那匹心爱的小马“扑面火药”赫然也在被盗之列。要塞司令官当即责令托马斯中尉（Lieutenant Thomas）率领第五骑兵团I连紧急出动，并指派我作为随军追踪向导。经过一番周折侦察，我们终于摸清了这伙盗贼的足迹——印第安人沿途费尽心机企图掩盖行踪——我们一路穷追了60英里，夜幕已然深沉降临。
此时我们距离红柳溪（Red Willow Creek）仅约4英里，我笃信印第安人当晚必定会在那一带依水扎营。我建议托马斯中尉叫停全连就地“隐蔽待命”，自己则孤身一人悄悄潜行摸向小溪；在一阵极为谨慎的四下巡查后，我冷不防窥见对岸的一处溪流弯道草地上，大群马匹赫然正在悠闲低头吃草。我疾步飞奔回大队通报军情，托马斯中尉随即率部悄无声息推进至溪岸边，打算隐蔽潜伏到天明拂晓，而后出其不意打敌人一个措手不及。
东方刚泛起鱼肚白，全军便悄然翻身上马；策马推进了一小段距离后，我们悄悄登上一处平缓小高地，在相距不足100码的正下方，印第安人的营寨赫然展现在眼前。印第安人正打算起早拔营赶路，刚刚把马群赶拢过来，正准备翻身上马；冷不防望见官兵如猛虎下山般俯冲杀下。刹那间，他们仓皇跳上马背四散狂奔。若非我们与敌营之间横亘着一条泥泞难行的溪流，我们本可以在他们翻身上马前将他们一网打尽；然而溪滩淤泥极深，导致我们的冲锋意料之外地受阻耽搁了片刻。在我们涉水渡溪时，印第安人朝我们胡乱放了几枪，但我们一跃过溪流，便立刻纵马如旋风般展开亡命穷追。有几个印第安凶徒来不及跳上马背，只能徒步连滚带爬顺着小溪向灌木丛里逃窜，其中一人被当场开枪击毙。
此前在发起冲锋前受命负责收拢受惊奔散印第安马匹的几名士兵，成功截获缴获了32匹良驹。我心急如焚地在马群里一通乱找，想看看扑面火药是否混在其中，然而却不见它的踪影。我随即拍马全速追赶溃逃的敌骑，终于远远瞥见一名印第安勇士正跨骑着我那匹心爱的小马狂奔飞驰，一马当先跑在所有逃敌的最前面。

We continued the chase for two or three miles, overtaking a couple who were mounted upon one horse. Coming up behind them I fired my rifle, when about thirty feet distant; the ball passed through the backs of both, and they fell headlong to the ground; but I made no stop however just then, for I had my eye on the gentleman who was riding Powder Face. It seemed to be fun for him to run away from us, and run away he did, for the last I saw of him was when he went over a divide, about three miles away. I bade him adieu. On my way back to the Indian camp I stopped and secured the war bonnets and accoutrements of the pair I had killed, and at the same time gently “raised their hair.”
We were feeling rather tired and hungry, as we had started out on the trail thirty-six hours before without a breakfast or taking any food with us; but not a murmur or complaint was heard among the men. In the abandoned Indian camp, however, we found enough dried buffalo meat to give us all a meal, and after remaining there for two hours, to rest our animals, we started on our return to Fort McPherson, where we arrived at night, having traveled 130 miles in two days.
This being the first fight Lieutenant Thomas had ever commanded in, he felt highly elated over his success, and hoped that his name would be mentioned in the special orders for gallantry; sure enough when we returned both he, myself and the whole command received a complimentary mention in a special order. This he certainly deserved for he was a brave, energetic, dashing little officer. The war bonnets which I had captured I turned over to General Carr, with the request that he present them to General Augur, whose daughters were visiting at the post at the time.
Shortly after this, another expedition was organized at Fort McPherson for the Republican River country. It was commanded by General Duncan, who was a jolly, blustering old fellow, and the officers who knew him well, said that we would have a good time, as he was very fond of hunting. He was a good fighter, and one of the officers said that an Indian bullet never could hurt him, as he had been shot in the head with a cannon ball which had not injured him in the least; another said the ball glanced off and killed one of the toughest mules in the army.

我们纵马穷追了两三英里，追上了合骑在一匹马上的两个印第安人。我策马从后方疾驰迫近，在距离他们约30英尺处举起步枪果断扣动扳机；飞出的铅弹瞬间洞穿了两个人的后背，二人一头栽下马鞍翻滚在地气绝身亡。但我当时脚下片刻未停，因为我的双眼死死盯着那个骑着扑面火药的狂徒。对我方骑兵的追赶他似乎浑然当作儿戏，策马狂奔而去；我最后望见他时，他已然翻过了大约3英里外的一道山脊分水岭，彻底消失得无影无踪。我只能在心底向我的爱驹依依道别。在折返回印第安营地的路上，我在那两个被我击毙的敌人尸首旁勒马停步，收缴了他们的战冠羽冠与全套行头，顺手极其利落地“揭下了他们的头皮”。
全队将士此刻感到极其困顿饥饿，因为早在36个小时之前我们便在未进早餐、未带半点口粮的情况下轻装急进追击敌踪；然而全军上下没有一个人发出哪怕半句怨言。在敌人丢弃的营寨里，我们搜出了足够的风干野牛肉，供全连饱餐了一顿扎实的野味大餐；在原地休整了两个小时让牲口恢复体力后，我们踏上归途班师返回麦克弗森堡，于当晚顺利进抵要塞——在整整两天的时间里，我们长途奔袭了整整130英里。
由于这是托马斯中尉平生第一次独立指挥战斗，他对所建树的战果感到无比自豪欢欣，期盼着自己的名字能在表彰英勇功绩的特别嘉奖令上榜上有名；果不其然，归队之后，他、我本人以及全连将士均在特别嘉奖令中荣获了通报表彰。这份殊荣托马斯中尉当之无愧，因为他是一位骁勇善战、雷厉风行、英姿勃发的小个子军官。我缴获的那两顶华丽战冠被我呈交给了卡尔将军，恳请他转赠给奥格尔将军（General Augur），当时奥格尔将军的千金小姐们正好在哨所做客探访。
此后不久，麦克弗森堡又针对里帕布利肯河流域组织了一次新的远征军。这支远征军由邓肯将军统帅，他是一个生性豁达开朗、脾气暴躁粗豪的老牌军官；熟知他的军官们私下说这趟远征大家一定能玩得痛快淋漓，因为老将军平生酷爱打猎。他打起仗来同样骁勇凶悍，一位军官开玩笑说印第安人的子弹根本伤不了他分毫，因为早年一颗实心炮弹曾正中他的脑门，却连他的皮都没擦破半点；另一位军官则打趣说，那颗炮弹其实是被他的硬脑壳给弹开了，当场反弹砸死了全军最壮实结实的一头骡子。

The Pawnee scouts who had been mustered out of service, during the winter of 1869 and ’70, were reorganized to accompany this expedition. I was glad of this, as I had become quite attached to one of the officers, Major North, and to many of the Indians. The only white scout we had at the post, besides myself at that time, was John Y. Nelson, whose Indian name was Cha-Sha-Cha-Opoyeo,3 which interpreted means Red-Willow-Fill-the-Pipe. This man is a character in his way; he has a Sioux squaw for a wife, and consequently a half-breed family. John is a good fellow, though as a liar he has but few equals and no superior.
We started out from the post with the regimental band playing the lively air of “The Girl I Left Behind Me.” We made but a short march that day, and camped at night at the head of Fox Creek. Next morning General Duncan sent me word by his orderly that I was to bring up my gun and shoot at a mark with him; but I can assure the reader that I did not feel much like shooting anything except myself, for on the night before, I had returned to Fort McPherson and spent several hours in interviewing the sutler’s store, in Company with Major Brown. I looked around for my gun, and found that I had left it behind. The last I could remember about it was that I had it at the sutler’s store. I informed Major Brown of my loss, who said that I was a nice scout to start out without a gun. I replied that that was not the worst of it, as General Duncan had sent for me to shoot a match with him, and I did not know what to do; for if the old gentleman discovered my predicament, he would very likely severely reprimand me.
“Well, Cody,” said he, “the best you can do is to make some excuse, and then go and borrow a gun from some of the men, and tell the General that you lent yours to some man to go hunting with today. While we are waiting here, I will send back to the post and get your rifle for you.”
I succeeded in obtaining a gun from John Nelson, and then marching up to the General’s headquarters I shot the desired match with him, which resulted in his favor.

This was the first scout the Pawnees had been out on under command of General Duncan, and in stationing his guards around the camp he posted them in a manner entirely different from that of General Carr and Colonel Royal, and he insisted that the different posts should call out the hour of the night thus:
“Post No. 1, nine o’clock, all is well! Post No. 2, nine o’clock, all is well!” etc.
The Pawnees, who had their regular turns at standing upon guard, were ordered to call the hour the same as the white soldiers. This was very difficult for them to do, as there were but few of them who could express themselves in English. Major North explained to them that when the man on post next to them should call out the hour, they must call it also as near like him as possible. It was very amusing to hear them do this. They would try to remember what the other man had said on the post next to them. For instance, a white soldier would call out: “Post No. 1, half-past nine o’clock, all is well!” The Indian standing next to him knew that he was bound to say something in English, and he would sing out something like the following:
“Poss number half pass five cents﻿—go to —﻿—﻿—! I don’t care!”
This system was really so ridiculous and amusing that the General had to give it up, and the order was accordingly countermanded.
Nothing of any great interest occurred on this march, until one day, while proceeding up Prairie Dog Creek,4 Major North and myself went out in advance of the command several miles and killed a number of buffaloes. Night was approaching, and I began to look around for a suitable camping ground for the command. Major North dismounted from his horse and was resting, while I rode down to the stream to see if there was plenty of grass in the vicinity. I found an excellent camping spot, and returning to Major North told him that I would ride over the hill a little way, so that the advance guard could see me. This I did, and when the advance came in sight I dismounted and laid down upon the grass to rest.
Suddenly I heard three or four shots, and in a few moments Major North came dashing up towards me, pursued by eight or ten Indians. I instantly sprang into my saddle, and fired a few shots at the Indians, who by this time had all come in sight, to the number of fifty.

这场妙趣横生的别致婚礼至此圆满礼成，随后便是此等大喜之日少不了的欢歌笑语与饕餮盛宴。大家对我以如此高贵典雅、雄辩滔滔的神来之笔主持并缔结这一良缘佳偶赞不绝口。
1871年夏，耶鲁大学的马什教授（Professor Marsh）率领一大批青年学子专程来到麦克弗森堡，计划在荒原开展科学考察打猎并搜寻远古化石。马什教授此前听闻波尼人在尼奥布拉拉河流域（Niobrara）发掘出过巨型巨兽骨骼化石，此番专程前来寻觅该古生物化石以及其他骨骼遗迹。他随即出资聘请弗兰克·诺斯少校与波尼侦察连担任武装护卫队。军方原计划也指派我随这支“化石搜寻队”同行，若非就在此时我接到紧急调令、奉命率领一支前哨侦察小队火速出击追剿印第安人，我本定会随教授一同策马远行。
就在教授抵达哨所的前一天，我与几名同袍在北普拉特河北岸的波尼泉（Pawnee Springs）附近狩猎，冷不防遭到了一伙印第安人的突然伏击，我们队里的约翰·韦斯特（John Weister）不幸中弹负伤。我们顽强就地阻击逼退了敌人，韦斯特咬牙还击亲手击毙了一名敌兵扯平了战果。然而印第安人人多势众，最终我们被迫奋力突围亡命飞奔。我们幸运地突围成功，毫发无损地平安撤回了哨所。要塞司令官严令必须对这伙敢于犯境的凶徒展开武装追剿，表示手头兵力吃紧，绝抽调不出我去陪同马什教授寻骨。
不过，我还是有幸结识了这位声名卓著的知名教授，发现他不仅学富五车、博古通今，更是一位极富幽默感与亲和力的谦谦君子。他深入浅出地为我系统讲解了当地的地质变迁史；指点我在哪些具体地层与区域最易发掘出古生物化石；并用许多深奥玄妙的科学典故开阔了我的眼界，其中某些理论对像我这样的边疆大老粗而言未免显得过于晦涩高深、不可思议，然而在他脑海中却条理清晰、如数家珍。在他率队启程踏上寻骨征途之际，我策马陪同他送行了数英里，那段交谈伴行令我感到无比惬意开怀。

His party had been provided with Government transportation and his students were all mounted on Government horses.

While the fossil-hunters were out on their expedition, we had several lively little skirmishes with the Indians. After having been absent some little time Professor Marsh and his party came back with their wagons loaded down with all kinds of bones, and the Professor was in his glory.
