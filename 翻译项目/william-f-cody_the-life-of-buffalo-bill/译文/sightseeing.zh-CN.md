## XXVI Sightseeing / 第二十六章 东部观光
===Original===

General Ord, commanding the Department of the Platte at the time, and who had been out on the Alexis hunt, had some business to attend to at Fort McPherson, and I accepted his invitation to ride over to the post with him in an ambulance. On the way thither he asked me how I would like to have an officer’s commission in the regular army. He said that General Sheridan and himself had had some conversation about the matter, and if I wanted a commission, one could easily be procured for me. I thanked General Ord for his kindness, and said that although an officer’s commission in the regular army was a tempting prize, yet I preferred to remain in the position I was then holding. He concluded by stating that if at any time I should wish a commission, all that I would have to do to secure it would be to inform him of my desire.
Having determined to visit New York, I acted upon General Sheridan’s suggestion and wrote to General Stager, from whom in a few days I received my railroad passes. Obtaining thirty days’ leave of absence from the department, I struck out for the East. On arriving in Chicago, in February, 1872, I was met at the depot by Colonel M. V. Sheridan, who said that his brother, the General, had not yet returned, but had sent word that I was to be his and the Colonel’s guest, at their house, while I remained in Chicago.
I spent two or three days very pleasantly in the great city of the West, meeting several of the gentlemen who had been out on the Sheridan hunt in September﻿—General Stager, Colonel Wilson, editor of the *Journal*; Mr. Sam Johnson, General Rucker and others﻿—by all of whom I was most cordially received and well entertained. I was introduced to quite a number of the best people of the city, and was invited to several “swell” dinners. I also accompanied General Sheridan﻿—who meantime had returned to the city﻿—to a ball at Riverside﻿—an aristocratic suburb.
On this occasion I became so embarrassed that it was more difficult for me to face the throng of beautiful ladies, than it would have been to confront a hundred hostile Indians. This was my first trip to the East, and I had not yet become accustomed to being stared at. And besides this, the hundreds of questions which I was called upon to answer further embarrassed and perplexed me.

当时统领普拉特军区（Department of the Platte）并刚刚陪同阿列克谢大公围猎的奥德将军（General Ord），恰好有要务需前往麦克弗森堡（Fort McPherson）处置；我便欣然应允他的邀请，同乘一辆野战救护车前往要塞。在途中的交谈中，他亲切地询问我，是否有意在正规军中担任委任军官。他透露说，谢里登将军曾同他就此事交换过意见，只要我有意从军入伍，为我谋取一份正规军军官军衔可谓易如反掌。我由衷感谢奥德将军的厚爱与美意，但我坦言：尽管正规军军官的军衔是一份极具诱惑力的殊荣，然而就我眼下的志向而言，我更倾向于坚守并维系目前自由自在的侦察兵职位。将军最后爽朗地总结道：日后无论何时只要我改变主意渴望出任正规军官，只需向他修书通报一声，他自会全力促成。
由于早已决意前往纽约一游，我便遵照谢里登将军的提议给斯泰格将军（General Stager）修书一封，短短数日之后，我便如期收到了他寄来的全程铁路免费乘车证。在军区顺利获批了为期30天的带薪长假之后，我便打点行装，毅然踏上了开往繁华东部的列车。1872年2月抵达芝加哥时，M·V·谢里登中校（Colonel M. V. Sheridan）已在火车站月台迎候多时；中校转达说，他的胞兄谢里登将军因公务尚未返抵芝加哥，但已特意带信吩咐：在我逗留芝加哥期间，务必下榻在将军与中校的私邸做客。
在这座西部第一大都会里，我惬意快活地度过了两三天的美好时光，重逢了多位曾在去年9月陪同谢里登将军参加过大围猎的政商名流——包括斯泰格将军、《芝加哥日报》（Journal）主编威尔逊上校（Colonel Wilson）、萨姆·约翰逊先生（Mr. Sam Johnson）、鲁克尔将军（General Rucker）等人——所有人皆对我给予了最热烈诚挚的迎候与款待。在他们的引荐下，我结识了芝加哥当地相当一批上流社会显贵，接到了多场极其奢华的名流晚宴邀约。其间，我还陪同此时已返抵城中的谢里登将军，一同出席了在河滨区（Riverside）——一处极其高雅尊贵的上流富人郊区——举办的盛大名流舞会。
在那场盛大舞会上，我显得异常局促不安、窘迫难堪：坦白讲，面对眼前成群结队、衣香鬓影的绝色佳人与名媛贵妇，那种手足无措的紧张感，比在荒原上真刀真枪面对100名凶神恶煞的敌对印第安人还要让我感到心惊胆战。这是我生平头一遭涉足东部繁华世界，我还很不习惯被人像稀世珍禽一般众目睽睽地上下打量；不仅如此，贵客们如连珠炮般抛向我的数百个好奇问题，更是让我晕头转向、窘态百出。

According to the route laid out for me by General Stager, I was to stop at Niagara Falls, Buffalo and Rochester on my way to New York, and he provided me with all the necessary railroad passes. Just as I was about to leave Chicago I met Professor Henry A. Ward, of Rochester, for whom during the previous year or two I had collected a large number of specimens of wild animals. He was on his way to Rochester, and kindly volunteered to act as my guide until we reached that point. We spent one day in viewing the wonders of Niagara, and I stopped one day at Rochester and was shown the beauties of that handsome city by Professor Ward, and I had the honor of receiving an invitation to dine with the Mayor.
On arriving at New York I was met at the depot by Mr. J. G. Hecksher, who had been appointed as “a committee of one” to escort me to the Union Club, where James Gordon Bennett, Leonard W. Jerome and others were to give me an informal reception, and where I was to make my headquarters during my visit in the great metropolis. I had an elegant dinner at the club rooms, with the gentlemen who had been out on the September hunt, and other members of the club.
After dinner, in company with Mr. Hecksher﻿—who acted as my guide﻿—I started out on the trail of my friend, Ned Buntline, whom we found at the Brevoort Place Hotel. He was delighted to see me, and insisted on my becoming his guest. He would listen to no excuses, and on introducing me to Messrs. Overton & Blair, proprietors of the Brevoort, they also gave me a pressing invitation to make my home at their house. I finally compromised the matter by agreeing to divide my time between the Union Club, the Brevoort House, and Ned Buntline’s quarters.
The next few days I spent in viewing the sights of New York, everything being new and startling, convincing me that as yet I had seen but a small portion of the world. I received numerous dinner invitations, as well as invitations to visit different places of amusement and interest; but as they came in so thick and fast, I soon became badly demoralized and confused. I found I had accepted invitations to dine at half a dozen or more houses on the same day and at the same hour.

依照斯泰格将军为我周密规划的旅行路线，在奔赴纽约的途中，我将在尼亚加拉瀑布（Niagara Falls）、布法罗（Buffalo）以及罗切斯特（Rochester）驻足游历，他为我备齐了沿途所需的所有铁路乘车证。正当我准备动身告别芝加哥之际，恰好邂逅了来自罗切斯特的亨利·A·沃德教授（Professor Henry A. Ward）——在过去一两年里，我曾为这位博物学家在荒原上捕获并搜集了大量珍贵的野生动物标本。他此时正准备返程回罗切斯特，便极其热忱地主动请缨充当我的向导，一路结伴同行直至他的家乡。我们在尼亚加拉瀑布流连忘返地饱览了一整天世界奇观的磅礴气象；随后在罗切斯特驻足了一天，在沃德教授的陪同下尽情领略了这座秀美都市的风土人情，并在彼处荣幸接获了罗切斯特市长的亲切邀约，与市长阁下共进盛大晚餐。
抵达到终点站纽约时，J·G·赫克歇尔先生（Mr. J. G. Hecksher）作为官方指定的“单人特派委员会”已在车站迎候，专程护送我前往联合俱乐部（Union Club）——詹姆斯·戈登·贝内特（James Gordon Bennett）、伦纳德·W·杰罗姆（Leonard W. Jerome）等诸位大亨已在彼处筹备好了一场非正式的盛大欢迎会，而这里也将是我在此次造访世界大都会期间的下榻大本营。在俱乐部富丽堂皇的宴会厅里，我与去年9月参加过大平原围猎的一众老友以及俱乐部的其他尊贵会员，共享了一顿无比丰盛华美的晚宴。
晚宴过后，在自告奋勇充当向导的赫克歇尔先生陪同下，我顺着线索去寻访我的挚友内德·邦特兰（Ned Buntline）的踪迹；我们最终在布雷沃特广场酒店（Brevoort Place Hotel）找到了他。邦特兰见到我惊喜万分、喜出望外，执意要让我搬去当他的贴身贵客。他根本不容我推脱推辞，并当场将我引荐给了布雷沃特酒店的两位大老板奥弗顿与布莱尔先生（Messrs. Overton & Blair），二位店东亦极其诚恳迫切地恳请我把他们的酒店当作自己在纽约的家。最终，在我的折中调解下达成了一致协议：将我的驻留时间平分，分别轮流下榻在联合俱乐部、布雷沃特酒店以及内德·邦特兰的寓所。
随后的数日里，我全情投入在纽约的走马观花观光见闻中。目之所及的一切皆是闻所未闻的崭新与震撼，让我深刻清醒地领悟到：以往的自己不过是在旷野一隅坐井观天，见识到的世界实在小得可怜。大批晚宴请帖与各类名胜剧院的游览邀约如雪片般纷纷飞来；然而由于请柬来得实在太密集、太迅猛，我很快便陷入了手忙脚乱的混乱与晕头转向之中。不多时我惊骇地发现：自己竟然在同一天的同一时刻，糊里糊涂答应了半打（6处）甚至更多名流府邸的晚宴邀约！

James Gordon Bennett had prepared a dinner for me, at which quite a large number of his friends were to be present, but owing to my confusion, arising from the many other invitations I had received, I forgot all about it, and dined elsewhere. This was “a bad break,” but I did not learn of my mistake until next day, when at the Union Club House several gentlemen, among them Lawrence Jerome, inquired “where in the world I had been,” and why I had not put in an appearance at Bennett’s dinner. They said that Bennett had taken great pains to give me a splendid reception, that the party had waited till nine o’clock for me, and that my non-arrival caused considerable disappointment. I apologized as well as I could, by saying that I had been out on a scout and had got lost, and had forgotten all about the dinner; and expressed my regret for the disappointment I had created by my forgetfulness. August Belmont, the banker, being near said:
“Never mind, gentlemen, I’ll give Cody a dinner at my house.”
“Thank you, sir,” said I; “I see you are determined that I shall not run short of rations while I am in the city. I’ll be there, sure.”
Both Mr. Jerome and Mr. Hecksher told me that I must not disappoint Mr. Belmont, for his dinners were splendid affairs. I made a note of the date, and at the appointed time I was promptly at Mr. Belmont’s mansion, where I spent a very enjoyable evening.
Mr. Bennett, who was among the guests, having forgiven my carelessness, invited me to accompany him to the Liederkranz masked ball, which was to take place in a few evenings, and would be a grand spectacle. Together we attended the ball, and during the evening I was well entertained. The dancers kept on their masks until midnight, and the merry and motley throng presented a brilliant scene, moving gracefully beneath the bright gaslight to the inspiriting music. To me it was a novel and entertaining sight, and in many respects reminded me greatly of an Indian war-dance.
Acting upon the suggestion of Mr. Bennett, I had dressed myself in my buckskin suit, and I naturally attracted considerable attention; especially when I took part in the dancing and exhibited some of my backwoods steps, which, although not as graceful as some, were a great deal more emphatic.

詹姆斯·戈登·贝内特特意为我筹备了一场极其隆重的盛大晚宴，邀请了他在纽约的一大批名流显贵出席；然而，由于被此前接踵而至的无数应酬搞得昏头涨脑，我竟然把这场至关重要的晚宴忘得一干二净，反而跑到别处去赴宴了。这着实是一场“天大的失礼洋相”，而我本人对此却浑然不知，直至次日我漫步走进联合俱乐部，好几位绅士——其中包括劳伦斯·杰罗姆（Lawrence Jerome）——迎面惊愕地追问我“昨天究竟跑到天涯海角去了”，为何在贝内特的盛大晚宴上不见踪影。他们告知我：贝内特为了这场欢迎盛宴煞费苦心，满堂宾客在席间苦苦等候我直至深夜9点，我的缺席让大家感到了极大的扫兴与失落。我只得满脸通红、竭尽所能地诚挚告罪自嘲道：自己在繁华都会里执行了一趟“前哨侦察”，结果不幸迷失了方向，脑子里竟把晚宴忘得精光；并对因自己的健忘而带给大家的扫兴深表歉疚痛悔。当时恰好伫立在旁的著名银行家奥古斯特·贝尔蒙特（August Belmont）朗声解围道：
“诸位先生，不必介怀，改日由我亲自在寒舍为科迪摆下一桌盛宴！”
“由衷感激您，先生，”我赶忙借坡下驴打趣道，“我看诸位是横下一条心，绝不容许我在这座大都会里断绝行军口粮啊。这回我定会准时赴约，万无一失！”
杰罗姆先生与赫克歇尔先生双双悄悄叮嘱我：这一回可千千万万绝不能再辜负贝尔蒙特先生，因为贝尔蒙特家的家宴在纽约堪称首屈一指的顶级盛事。我郑重在随身笔记本上记下了赴宴日期；到了约定时刻，我分秒不差准时踏入了贝尔蒙特先生的奢华府邸，在彼处度过了一个极其舒心惬意的美好夜晚。
贝内特先生也是当晚的座上宾之一，他宽宏大量地原谅了我的粗心大意，并力邀我随他一同出席即将于数日后隆重上演的利德克兰茨假面舞会（Liederkranz masked ball）——那必将是一场轰动全城的豪华视觉盛宴。当晚我们并肩出席了舞会，我在现场大开眼界、享尽了欢愉。狂欢的舞客们一直将面具佩戴至午夜时分才肯揭下，五光十色、光怪陆离的欢乐人潮构成了一幅美轮美奂的绚丽画卷，在璀璨明亮的瓦斯吊灯下，伴随着振奋人心的悠扬乐曲翩翩起舞。对我而言，这是一出见所未见、妙趣横生的奇观，在许多细节与神韵上，竟不可思议地让我想起了印第安人在旷野篝火旁狂跳的战舞！
在贝内特先生的极力建议下，我当晚特意换上了自己那套标志性的全套鹿皮软甲战袍，理所当然在满堂锦衣华服中吸引了全场无数惊艳与好奇的目光；尤其是当我欣然步入舞池中央、即兴展示了一段我们在偏远边荒山林里独创的粗犷舞步时，虽然动作不如东部绅士那般柔美娴熟，却远比任何舞姿都更具铿锵有力的大地力量与野性律动。

But when I undertook to do artistic dancing, I found I was decidedly out of place in that crowd, and I accordingly withdrew from the floor.
I occasionally passed an evening at Niblo’s Garden, viewing the many beauties of *The Black Crook*, which was then having its long run, under the management of Jarrett & Palmer, whose acquaintance I had made, and who extended to me the freedom of the theater.
Ned Buntline and Fred Maeder had dramatized one of the stories which the former had written about me for the *New York Weekly*. The drama was called *Buffalo Bill, the King of Border Men*. While I was in New York it was produced at the Bowery Theater; J. B. Studley, an excellent actor, appearing in the character of “Buffalo Bill,” and Mrs. W. G. Jones, a fine actress, taking the part of my sister, a leading role. I was curious to see how I would look when represented by someone else, and of course I was present on the opening night, a private box having been reserved for me. The theater was packed, every seat being occupied as well as the standing-room. The drama was played smoothly, and created a great deal of enthusiasm.
The audience, upon learning that the real “Buffalo Bill” was present, gave several cheers between the acts, and I was called on to come out on the stage and make a speech. Mr. Freleigh, the manager, insisted that I should comply with the request, and that I should be introduced to Mr. Studley. I finally consented, and the next moment I found myself standing behind the footlights and in front of an audience for the first time in my life. I looked up, then down, then on each side, and everywhere I saw a sea of human faces, and thousands of eyes all staring at me. I confess that I felt very much embarrassed﻿—never more so in my life﻿—and I knew not what to say. I made a desperate effort, and a few words escaped me, but what they were I could not for the life of me tell, nor could anyone else in the house. My utterances were inaudible even to the leader of the orchestra, Mr. Dean, who was sitting only a few feet in front of me. Bowing to the audience, I beat a hasty retreat into one of the canyons of the stage. I never felt more relieved in my life than when I got out of the view of that immense crowd. That evening Mr. Freleigh offered to give me five hundred dollars a week to play the part of “Buffalo Bill” myself.

在纽约逗留期间，我曾数度光顾尼勃罗花园剧场（Niblo’s Garden），尽情饱览了名噪一时的经典巨作《黑魔杖》（The Black Crook）的诸多绝美精髓——该剧在贾勒特与帕默公司（Jarrett & Palmer）的掌管经营下正在彼处进行旷日持久的连演；因我与两位剧场巨头结下了深厚友谊，他们慷慨向我授予了终身免费随意出入剧场的最高特权。
内德·邦特兰与弗雷德·梅德（Fred Maeder）当时刚刚将前者此前在《纽约周刊》（New York Weekly）上为我量身撰写的连载小说改编成了同名话剧。这部舞台传奇大戏定名为《野牛比尔，边疆人之王》（Buffalo Bill, the King of Border Men）。就在我造访纽约期间，该剧恰逢在鲍厄里剧院（Bowery Theater）隆重首演；声名显赫的著名话剧名伶J·B·斯塔德利（J. B. Studley）在剧中亲自挑大梁出演“野牛比尔”一角，而才华横溢的名演员W·G·琼斯夫人（Mrs. W. G. Jones）则出演我剧中的胞妹——一个举足轻重的主角戏份。我打心眼里极度好奇，若由旁人在舞台上扮演我自己究竟会呈现出何等光景；首演之夜我自然盛装出席，剧场特意为我在贵宾包厢保留了绝佳席位。整座剧场座无虚席、盛况空前，不仅所有座位被抢购一空，甚至连过道上的站票位置都挤得水泄不通。整场戏剧演出流畅通顺自然，在全场掀起了雷鸣般的狂热狂澜。
