## XI A Soldier / 第十一章 戎马生涯
===Original===

In the fall of 1861 I made a trip to Fort Larned, Kansas, carrying military dispatches, and in the winter I accompanied George Long through the country, and assisted him in buying horses for the government.
The next spring, 1862, an expedition against the Indians was organized, consisting of a volunteer regiment, the Ninth Kansas, under Colonel Clark. This expedition, which I had joined in the capacity of guide and scout, proceeded to the Kiowa and Comanche country, on the Arkansas River, along which stream we scouted all summer between Fort Lyon and Fort Larned, on the old Santa Fe trail. We had several engagements with the Indians, but they were of no great importance.
In the winter of 1862, I became one of the “Red Legged Scouts,”﻿—a company of scouts commanded by Captain Tuff. Among its members were some of the most noted Kansas Rangers, such as Red Clark, the St. Clair brothers, Jack Harvey, an old pony express-rider named Johnny Fry, and many other well known frontiersmen. Our field of operations was confined mostly to the Arkansas country and southwestern Missouri. We had many a lively skirmish with the bushwhackers and Younger brothers, and when we were not hunting them, we were generally employed in carrying dispatches between Forts Dodge, Gibson, Leavenworth, and other posts. Whenever we were in Leavenworth we had a very festive time. We usually attended all the balls in full force, and “ran things” to suit ourselves. Thus I passed the winter of 1862 and the spring of 1863.
Subsequently I engaged to conduct a small train to Denver for some merchants, and on reaching that place in September, I received a letter stating that my mother was not expected to live. I hastened home, and found her dangerously ill. She grew gradually worse, and at last, on the 22nd of November, 1863, she died. Thus passed away a loving and affectionate mother and a noble, brave, good and loyal woman. That I loved her above all other persons, no one who has read these reminiscences can for a moment doubt.
Previous to this said event my sister Julia had been married to a gentleman named J. A. Goodman, and they now came to reside at our house and take charge of the children, as my mother had desired that they should not be separated. Mr. Goodman became the guardian of the minor children.

===Chinese===

1861年秋，我前往堪萨斯州的拉尼德堡（Fort Larned）递送军用公文；入冬后，我陪同乔治·朗（George Long）走遍乡间，协助他为联邦政府采办军马。
次年春，即1862年，军方组织了一支讨伐印第安人的远征军，由克拉克上校（Colonel Clark）麾下的堪萨斯第九志愿团组成。我以向导兼侦察兵的身份加入了这支远征军，部队开拔进抵阿肯色河沿岸的凯奥瓦人（Kiowa）与科曼奇人（Comanche）聚居地。整个夏天，我们都在古老的圣菲小道（Santa Fe trail）上，在里昂堡与拉尼德堡之间的河谷地带执行巡逻侦察。期间我们与印第安人发生过几次交火，但规模都不大，无关大局。
到了1862年冬季，我成为了“红腿侦察队”（Red Legged Scouts）的一员——这是由塔夫上尉（Captain Tuff）指挥的一支侦察连队。队中云集了多位声名显赫的堪萨斯游骑兵，诸如红发克拉克（Red Clark）、圣克莱尔兄弟（St. Clair brothers）、杰克·哈维（Jack Harvey）、昔日的小马快递骑手约翰尼·弗莱（Johnny Fry），以及许多威震边疆的老拓荒者。我们的作战区域主要局限在阿肯色河流域与密苏里州西南部。我们屡次与南方丛林游击队员（bushwhackers）以及扬格兄弟（Younger brothers）匪帮发生激烈遭遇战；在不搜捕他们的时候，我们通常受雇在道奇堡（Fort Dodge）、吉布森堡（Fort Gibson）、莱文沃思堡（Fort Leavenworth）以及其他军事哨所之间传递紧急公文。每逢进驻莱文沃思，我们都会尽情狂欢作乐。我们通常全员出动出席所有舞会，随心所欲地“主宰一切”。我便是这样度过了1862年的冬天与1863年的春天。
后来，我受雇为几位商人护送一支小型货运大车队前往丹佛。在同年9月抵达丹佛时，我收到了一封家信，信中说我母亲病危，恐已不久于人世。我日夜兼程赶回家中，发现她已病入膏肓。她的病情日渐恶化，最终在1863年11月22日溘然长逝。一位慈爱仁厚、高贵英勇、善良忠诚的伟大母亲就这样离我们而去。我对她的至深爱戴超越了世间所有人，读过这些回忆录的人绝不会对此抱有丝毫怀疑。
在此不幸变故之前，我的姐姐朱莉娅（Julia）已嫁给了一位名叫J·A·古德曼（J. A. Goodman）的绅士；由于母亲生前极愿孩子们不要骨肉分离，他们夫妇便搬到我们家中居住并照料弟弟妹妹。古德曼先生成为了尚未成年的弟妹们的法定监护人。

I soon left the home now rendered gloomy by the absence of her whom I had so tenderly loved, and going to Leavenworth I entered upon a dissolute and reckless life﻿—to my shame be it said﻿—and associated with gamblers, drunkards, and bad characters generally. I continued my dissipation about two months, and was becoming a very “hard case.” About this time the Seventh Kansas regiment, known as “Jennison’s Jay-hawkers,” returned from the war, and re-enlisted and reorganized as veterans. Among them I met quite a number of my old comrades and neighbors, who tried to induce me to enlist and go south with them. I had no idea of doing anything of the kind; but one day, after having been under the influence of bad whisky, I awoke to find myself a soldier in the Seventh Kansas. I did not remember how or when I had enlisted, but I saw I was in for it, and that it would not do for me to endeavor to back out.
In the spring of 1864 the regiment was ordered to Tennessee, and we got into Memphis just about the time that General Sturgis was so badly whipped by General Forrest. General A. J. Smith reorganized the army to operate against Forrest, and after marching to Tupalo, Mississippi, we had an engagement with him and defeated him. This kind of fighting was all new to me, being entirely different from any in which I had ever before engaged. I soon became a noncommissioned officer, and was put on detached service as a scout.
After skirmishing around the country with the rest of the army for some little time, our regiment returned to Memphis, but was immediately ordered to Cape Girardeau, in Missouri, as a confederate force under General Price was then raiding that state. The command of which my regiment was a part hurried to the front to intercept Price, and our first fight with him occurred at Pilot Knob. From that time for nearly six weeks we fought or skirmished every day.
I was still acting as a scout, when one day I rode ahead of the command, some considerable distance, to pick up all possible information concerning Price’s movements. I was dressed in gray clothes, or Missouri jeans, and on riding up to a farmhouse and entering, I saw a man, also dressed in gray costume, sitting at a table eating bread and milk. He looked up as I entered, and startled me by saying:
“You little rascal, what are you doing in those ‘secesh’ clothes?”

失去了深爱的母亲，昔日温馨的家如今变得凄凉压抑，不久我便离开了家门前往莱文沃思。令人汗颜的是，我在那里陷入了一段纸醉金迷、放浪形骸的堕落生活，整日与赌徒、酒鬼以及各类三教九流的无赖之徒厮混在一起。这种放纵荒唐的生活持续了大约两个月，我正迅速沦为一个不可救药的“浑蛋混混”。就在此时，被誉为“詹尼森兜头鸟”（Jennison’s Jay-hawkers）的堪萨斯第七骑兵团从前线征战归来，作为老兵团重新招募并重组部队。在他们中间我偶遇了许多昔日的同袍与邻里故旧，他们力劝我入伍参军，跟随他们一同开赴南方作战。我原本毫无从军的打算；但有一天，在劣质威士忌的酒精麻醉下人事不省，等我清醒过来时，惊愕地发现自己竟已成了堪萨斯第七团的一名正式士兵。我全然记不得自己究竟是何时、又是如何签下的入伍契约，但我明白自己这回是彻底陷进去了，绝不可能再打退堂鼓。
1864年春，该团奉命开赴田纳西州。在我们进驻孟菲斯之际，恰逢斯图尔吉斯将军（General Sturgis）惨败于南方名将福雷斯特将军（General Forrest）手下。A·J·史密斯将军（General A. J. Smith）重新整编大军展开针对福雷斯特的反击行动；在进军密西西比州的图珀洛（Tupelo）后，我们与福雷斯特展开恶战并一举将其击溃。这种大规模正规军会战对我而言全然陌生，与我以往经历过的任何战斗都截然不同。不久我升任士官，并被抽调担任特遣侦察兵。
随大部队在当地展开了一阵围剿清剿后，我们团班师返回孟菲斯，但随即又接到紧急调令开赴密苏里州的吉拉多角（Cape Girardeau），因为此时普赖斯将军（General Price）率领的南军部队正席卷突袭该州。我所属的部队日夜兼程赶往前线拦截普赖斯，我们与他的首场血战在领航峰（Pilot Knob）爆发。自那之后近6个星期的时间里，我们几乎每天都在交火或发生激烈前哨战。
我当时依然充当尖兵侦察员。一天，我只身一人策马在部队前方很远处侦察前行，试图搜集有关普赖斯部动向的一切情报。当时我身着一身灰色便服，也就是密苏里粗斜纹粗布衣裳；当我策马来到一处农庄并推门而入时，赫然看见屋里坐着一名同样身着灰衣的汉子，正坐在桌旁就着牛奶吃面包。我进门时他抬起头来，脱口而出的一句话吓了我一跳：
“你这个小兔崽子，身上穿着这身‘南部分裂分子’的行头是打算干什么？”

Judge of my surprise when I recognized in the stranger my old friend and partner, Wild Bill, disguised as a Confederate officer.
“I ask you the same question, sir,” said I without the least hesitation.
“Hush! sit down and have some bread and milk, and we’ll talk it all over afterwards,” said he.
I accepted the invitation and partook of the refreshments. Wild Bill paid the woman of the house, and we went out to the gate where my horse was standing.
“Billy, my boy,” said he, “I am mighty glad to see you. I haven’t seen or heard of you since we got busted on that St. Louis’ horse-race.”
“What are you doing out here?” I asked.
“I am a scout under General McNiel. For the last few days I have been with General Marmaduke’s division of Price’s army, in disguise as a southern officer from Texas, as you see me now,” said he.
“That’s exactly the kind of business that I am out on today,” said I; “and I want to get some information concerning Price’s movements.”
“I’ll give you all that I have;” and he then went on and told me all that he knew regarding Price’s intentions, and the number and condition of his men. He then asked about my mother, and when he learned that she was dead he was greatly surprised and grieved; he thought a great deal of her, for she had treated him almost as one of her own children. He finally took out a package, which he had concealed about his person, and handing it to me he said:
“Here are some letters which I want you to give to General McNiel.”
“All right,” said I as I took them, “but where will I meet you again?”
“Never mind that,” he replied; “I am getting so much valuable information that I propose to stay a little while longer in this disguise.” Thereupon we shook hands and parted.
It is not necessary to say much concerning Price’s raid in general, as that event is a matter of recorded history. I am only relating the incidents in which I was personally interested either as one of the actors or as an observer.
Another interesting and I may say exciting episode happened to me a day or two after my unexpected meeting with Wild Bill. I was riding with the advance guard of our army, and wishing a drink of water, I stopped at a farmhouse. There were no men about the premises, and no one excepting a very fine and intellectual looking lady and her two daughters.

不难想象我有多么震惊——我立刻认出眼前这位不速之客不是别人，正是我昔日的老友兼老搭档“狂野比尔”（Wild Bill），他此刻正伪装成一名南部邦联军官。
“我也正想问您同样的问题呢，先生。”我毫不迟疑地反驳道。
“嘘！快坐下吃点面包喝口牛奶，待会儿咱们再详谈。”他压低声音说道。
我欣然落座，一同享用了这顿便餐。狂野比尔掏钱付给了女主人，随后我们俩一同走到屋外我的马匹拴着的院门口。
“比利，我的好兄弟，”他说，“真高兴能见到你。自从咱们在圣路易斯那场赛马赌局上输得精光之后，我就再也没听过你的音讯了。”
“你跑到这荒郊野岭来干什么？”我好奇地问。
“我现在是麦克尼尔将军（General McNiel）手下的侦察兵。过去这几天，我一直伪装成一名来自得克萨斯的南军军官潜伏在普赖斯大军的马默杜克将军（General Marmaduke）师部里，正如你现在看到的模样。”他答道。
“我今天执行的任务恰好也是这一路，”我说，“正想打探普赖斯部的兵力调动情报呢。”
“我把我掌握的全都交给你。”接着他竹筒倒豆子般将自己所知晓的有关普赖斯全盘意图、部队兵力与补给状况的情报一五一十告诉了我。随后他问起我母亲的近况，当听说她老人家已经故去时，他震惊万分、悲痛难抑；他生前对我母亲无比敬重爱戴，因为母亲待他几乎就像亲生骨肉一般。最后，他从贴身衣物里摸出一个密件包递到我手中，嘱咐道：
“这里有几封密信，务必亲手交给麦克尼尔将军。”
“包在我身上，”我接过信件说道，“但咱们下回在哪儿碰头？”
“这个先别操心，”他回答，“我正在源源不断获取极其珍贵的核心情报，我打算顶着这身伪装再多潜伏一阵子。”说完，我们互道珍重握手作别。
关于普赖斯突袭战役的全局走向无需在此赘述，正史中早已记载详实。我在此仅仅记录自己作为亲历者或目击者直接卷入的生动片段。
与狂野比尔意外重逢一两天后，我又经历了一场堪称惊心动魄的难忘轶事。当时我正随我军先遣警戒前哨向前推进，口渴难耐想找口水喝，便在路旁一座农舍前勒马停步。宅子里没有任何成年男子，只有一位举止端庄文雅的贵妇和她的两个女儿。

They seemed to be almost frightened to death at seeing me﻿—a “yank”﻿—appear before them. I quieted their fears somewhat, and the mother then asked me how far back the army was. When I told her it would be along shortly, she expressed her fears that they would take everything on the premises. They set me out a lunch and treated me rather kindly, so that I really began to sympathize with them; for I knew that the soldiers would ransack their house and confiscate everything they could lay their hands on. At last I resolved to do what I could to protect them. After the generals and the staff officers had passed by, I took it upon myself to be a sentry over the house. When the command came along some of the men rushed up with the intention of entering the place and carrying off all the desirable plunder possible, and then tearing and breaking everything to pieces, as they usually did along the line of march.
“Halt!” I shouted; “I have been placed here by the commanding officer as a guard over this house, and no man must enter it.”
This stopped the first squad; and seeing that my plan was a success, I remained at my post during the passage of the entire command and kept out all intruders.
It seemed as if the ladies could not thank me sufficiently for the protection I had afforded them. They were perfectly aware of the fact that I had acted without orders and entirely on my own responsibility, and therefore they felt the more grateful. They urgently invited me to remain a little while longer and partake of an excellent dinner which they said they were preparing for me. I was pretty hungry about that time, as our rations had been rather slim of late, and a good dinner was a temptation I could not withstand, especially as it was to be served up by such elegant ladies. While I was eating the meal, I was most agreeably entertained by the young ladies, and before I had finished it the last of the rearguard must have been at least two miles from the house.
Suddenly three men entered the room, and I looked up and saw three double-barreled shotguns leveled straight at me. Before I could speak, however, the mother and her daughters sprang between the men and me.
“Father! Boys! Lower your guns! You must not shoot this man,” and similar exclamations, were the cry of all three.

一见到我——一个货真价实的“联邦北方佬”（yank）——突兀地闯入院中，母女三人吓得几乎魂飞魄散。我好言安抚，平复了她们的恐慌情绪；随后那位母亲颤声询问我北方大军距此还有多远。当我告诉她大部队片刻即至时，她满面愁容地表达了自己的恐惧，生怕大军一到会将庄园里的一切洗劫一空。她们端出了一顿便餐招待我，态度颇为温和友善，以至于我心底油然而生同情之意；因为我深知大队行军官兵必然会把整座宅子翻个底朝天，把目所能及的物资抢夺殆尽。最终我横下一条心，决定竭尽所能护她们周全。等各路将军及其幕僚参谋们策马走过之后，我擅自做主在宅院门口站起岗来充当卫兵。当大部队后续行军纵队开过来时，果然有几拨士兵气势汹汹地冲上前来，企图闯入宅院搜刮一切能带走的战利品，并像他们沿途素来所做的那样把一切砸得稀烂。
“站住！”我厉声断喝，“我奉最高指挥官严令在此驻守警戒此宅，任何人不得擅入一步！”
这声怒喝当场喝退了第一批散兵游勇；眼见狐假虎威的计策奏效，我在整个大军纵队过境期间始终坚守在哨位上，将所有企图趁火打劫的闯入者尽数挡在大门之外。
对于我施以的庇护之恩，母女几人感激涕零、千恩万谢。她们心知肚明我完全是在没有上级命令的情况下擅自承担风险仗义相助，因此心中的感激更深一层。她们极力挽留我多盘桓片刻，盛情邀请我享用一顿她们特意为我精心烹制的丰盛大餐。当时我饥肠辘辘，因为部队近来口粮供给十分匮乏，面对如此丰盛可口的美食与几位优雅女士的殷勤款待，这等诱惑我委实难以抗拒。用餐期间，几位年轻小姐谈笑风生将我招待得极其周到惬意，等我吃完这顿大餐时，后卫部队的殿后尾巴恐怕早已开拔开出宅院两英里开外了。
冷不防，房门砰的一声被推开，三个大汉杀气腾腾闯入房中；我猛一抬头，赫然看见三支黑洞洞的双管猎枪正死死瞄准着我的胸膛。还没等我来得及拔枪或开口，那位母亲和两个女儿便发疯般飞身扑了上来，用血肉之躯死死挡在我与枪口之间。
“爹！弟弟！快把枪放下！千万不能开枪打这位先生！”母女三人惊恐万状地齐声尖叫起来。

The guns were lowered, and then the men, who were the father and brothers of the young ladies, were informed of what I had done for them. It appeared that they had been concealed in the woods nearby while the army was passing, and on coming into the house and finding a Yankee there, they determined to shoot him. Upon learning the facts, the old man extended his hand to me, saying:
“I would not harm a hair of your head for the world; but it is best that you stay here no longer, as your command is some distance from here now, and you might be cut off by bushwhackers before reaching it.”
Bidding them all goodbye, and with many thanks from the mother and daughters, I mounted my horse and soon overtook the column, happy in the thought that I had done a good deed, and with no regrets that I had saved from pillage and destruction the home and property of a confederate and his family.
Our command kept crowding against Price and his army until they were pushed into the vicinity of Kansas City, where their further advance was checked by United States troops from Kansas; and then was begun their memorable and extraordinary retreat back into Kansas.
While both armies were drawn up in skirmish line near Fort Scott, Kansas, two men on horseback were seen rapidly leaving the Confederate lines, and suddenly they made a dash towards us. Instantly quick volleys were discharged from the Confederates, who also began a pursuit, and some five hundred shots were fired at the flying men. It was evident that they were trying to reach our lines, but when within about a quarter of a mile of us, one of them fell from his horse to rise no more. He had been fatally shot. His companion galloped on unhurt, and seven companies of our regiment charged out and met him, and checked his pursuers. The fugitive was dressed in Confederate uniform, and as he rode into our lines I recognized him as Wild Bill, the Union scout. He immediately sought Generals Pleasanton and McNiel, with whom he held a consultation. He told them that although Price made a bold showing on the front, by bringing all his men into view, yet he was really a great deal weaker than the appearance of his lines would indicate; and that he was then trying to cross a difficult stream four miles from Fort Scott.

枪口终于垂了下来，三名男子——正是两位小姐的父亲与兄弟——随后听闻了我刚才冒死护宅的义举。原来，大军过境时他们一直荷枪实弹潜伏在邻近的密林中，等大军走后进屋猛然撞见一名落单的北方兵，便决意立刻开枪取其性命。获悉原委之后，老父亲主动走上前来向我伸出双手，动情地说道：
“就算给我全世界，我也绝不会动您一根汗毛；但您眼下绝不能在此久留了，贵军大部队早已走远，您孤身一人极可能会在追赶归队途中遭到丛林游击队的截杀。”
在母女们千恩万谢的感激声中，我向全家人拱手道别，跃上马背飞驰而去，很快便追赶上了大军行军纵队。一想到自己行了一桩善事，拯救了一个南方邦联家庭的家园与财产免遭劫掠和焚毁，我心头满溢着坦然与欣慰，毫无半分悔意。
我军对普赖斯所部的合围压迫持续步步紧逼，直至将残敌一路压缩赶到了堪萨斯城（Kansas City）郊外，敌人的进一步进犯遭到驻扎堪萨斯各要塞的联邦主力严密遏阻；随后，南军便拉开了其历史上那场惊心动魄、狼狈万状的大溃退序幕，一路仓皇退回堪萨斯境内。
就在两军在堪萨斯州的斯科特堡（Fort Scott）附近拉开散兵线对峙之际，只见南军防线中突然飞驰出两名策马狂奔的骑手，风驰电掣般径直朝我们北方阵营冲杀过来。南军阵地顿时枪声大作，密集的排枪如暴雨般倾泻而出，大队南军骑兵紧随其后穷追不舍，足有500多发子弹呼啸射向这两名逃亡者。显而易见他们正拼死企图冲入我军阵地，但当冲到距离我方防线仅约四分之一英里处时，其中一人中弹栽下马鞍，再也没能站起来，当场气绝身亡。他的同伴则毫发无伤地策马继续狂飙，我团7个连队当即纵马杀出防线迎上前去，猛烈开火阻击打退了追敌。这位逃脱死劫的骑手身穿南军制服，等他策马冲入我军战线时，我一眼认出他正是联邦神级侦察兵狂野比尔。他立刻直奔普莱森顿将军（General Pleasanton）与麦克尼尔将军的大营召开紧急军情密商。他向将军们报告：普赖斯虽在正面战场上倾巢而出摆出一副虚张声势的进攻姿态，但其实际战力远比表面防线虚弱得多；此刻敌人正企图在距离斯科特堡4英里处抢渡一条水流湍急的难渡河流。

===Original===

It was late in the afternoon, but General Pleasanton immediately ordered an advance, and we charged in full force upon the rear of Price’s army, and drove it before us for two hours.
If Wild Bill could have made his successful dash into our lines earlier in the day, the attack would have been made sooner, and greater results might have been expected. The Confederates had suspected him of being a spy for two or three days, and had watched him too closely to allow an opportunity to get away from them sooner. His unfortunate companion who had been shot, was a scout from Springfield, Missouri, whose name I cannot now remember.
From this time on, Wild Bill and myself continued to scout together until Price’s army was driven south of the Arkansas River and the pursuit abandoned. We then returned to Springfield, Missouri, for a rest and for supplies, and Wild Bill and myself spent two weeks there in “having a jolly good time,” as some people would express it.
