## XXX I Return to the Plains / 第三十章 重返大平原
===Original===

We closed our theatrical season earlier than usual in the spring of 1876, because I was anxious to take part in the Sioux war which was then breaking out. Colonel Hills had written me several letters saying that General Crook was anxious to have me accompany his command, and I promised to do so, intending to overtake him in the Powder River country. But when I arrived at Chicago, on my way West, I learned that my old regiment, the gallant Fifth Cavalry, was on its way back from Arizona to join General Crook, and that my old commander, General Carr, was in command. He had written to military headquarters at Chicago to learn my whereabouts, as he wished to secure me as his guide and chief of scouts. I then gave up the idea of overtaking General Crook, and hastening on to Cheyenne, where the Fifth Cavalry had already arrived, I was met at the depot by Lieutenant King, adjutant of the regiment, he having been sent down from Fort D. A. Russell for that purpose by General Carr, who had learned by a telegram from military headquarters at Chicago that I was on the way. I accompanied the Lieutenant on horseback to the camp, and as we rode up, one of the boys shouted, “Here’s Buffalo Bill!” Soon after there came three hearty cheers from the regiment. Officers and men all were glad to see me, and I was equally delighted to meet them once more. The General at once appointed me his guide and chief of scouts.
The next morning the command pulled out for Fort Laramie, and on reaching that post we found General Sheridan there, accompanied by General Frye and General Forsyth, en route to Red Cloud Agency. As the command was to remain here a few days, I accompanied General Sheridan to Red Cloud and back, taking a company of cavalry as escort.
The Indians having recently committed a great many depredations on the Black Hills road, the Fifth Cavalry was sent out to scout the country between the Indian agencies and the hills. The command operated on the South Fork of the Cheyenne and at the foot of the Black Hills for about two weeks, having several small engagements with roving bands of Indians during the time. General Wesley Merritt﻿—who had lately received his promotion to the Colonelcy of the Fifth Cavalry﻿—now came out and took control of the regiment. I was sorry that the command was taken from General Carr, because under him it had made its fighting reputation.

1876年春，我们的剧场演出季比往年提前收官，因为我急切地渴望奔赴前线，投身当时正全面爆发的苏族战争（Sioux war）。希尔斯上校（Colonel Hills）此前接连给我写了几封信，说克鲁克将军（General Crook）极盼我能随军参战，我也慨然应允，打算前去粉河一带追赶他的部队。然而在西行途中抵达芝加哥时，我得知我的老部队——英勇无畏的第五骑兵团（Fifth Cavalry）正从亚利桑那兼程赶回，准备前去与克鲁克将军会师，而执掌该团帅印的正是我的老长官卡尔将军（General Carr）。卡尔将军已向芝加哥军区司令部发函打听我的下落，希望招揽我继续出任他的向导兼首席侦察兵。于是我打消了直接去追赶克鲁克将军的念头，日夜兼程赶赴夏延（Cheyenne）。此时第五骑兵团已先期抵达那里，卡尔将军收到芝加哥司令部告知我正在路上的电报后，特派团副金中尉（Lieutenant King）从D·A·拉塞尔堡（Fort D. A. Russell）专程赶往火车站接我。我随中尉策马前往军营，刚一靠近，营里便有名士兵高喊：“野牛比尔（Buffalo Bill）来啦！”紧接着，全团官兵爆发出一阵又一阵由衷的三声欢呼。全体军官与士兵无不为重逢欢欣鼓舞，而能与老战友们再次并肩作战，我也同样喜不自胜。将军当场任命我为他的向导兼首席侦察兵。
翌日清晨，大队人马开拔奔赴拉勒米堡（Fort Laramie）。抵达到该哨所后，我们见到了谢里登将军（General Sheridan），同行的还有弗莱将军（General Frye）和福赛思将军（General Forsyth），他们正取道前往红云代办所（Red Cloud Agency）。由于大部队需在此休整驻留数日，我便率领一个骑兵连护送谢里登将军前往红云代办所，随后又平安返回。
鉴于印第安人近期在黑山（Black Hills）沿线道路上频频劫掠作乱，第五骑兵团奉命开赴印第安各代办所与黑山之间的广大区域执行巡逻侦察。部队在夏延河南汊（South Fork of the Cheyenne）及黑山脚下持续巡弋了约两周时间，其间与游荡流窜的印第安小股武装打了几次小规模遭遇战。不久，晋升为第五骑兵团上校的韦斯利·梅里特将军（General Wesley Merritt）赶赴前线，接掌了全团指挥权。指挥权从卡尔将军手中移交出去，我心中颇感惋惜，毕竟这支部队威震四方的赫赫威名，正是在卡尔将军麾下一刀一枪打出来的。

===Original===

However, upon becoming acquainted with General Merritt, I found him to be an excellent officer.
The regiment, by continued scouting, soon drove the Indians out of that section of the country, as we supposed, and we had started on our way back to Fort Laramie, when a scout arrived at the camp and reported the massacre of General Custer and his band of heroes on the Little Big Horn, on the 25th of June, 1876; and he also brought orders to General Merritt to proceed at once to Fort Fetterman and join General Crook in the Big Horn country.
Colonel Stanton, who was with the Fifth Cavalry on this scout, had been sent to Red Cloud Agency two days before, and that same evening a scout arrived bringing a message from him that eight hundred Cheyenne warriors had that day left the Red Cloud Agency to join Sitting Bull’s hostile forces in the Big Horn region. Notwithstanding the instructions to proceed immediately to join General Crook by the Way of Fort Fetterman, Colonel Merritt took the responsibility of endeavoring to intercept the Cheyennes, and as the sequel shows he performed a very important service.
He selected five hundred men and horses, and in two hours we were making a forced march back to Hat, or War-Bonnet Creek﻿—the intention being to reach the main Indian trail running to the north across that creek before the Cheyennes could get there. We arrived there the next night, and at daylight the following morning, July 17th, 1876, I went out on a scout, and found that the Indians had not yet crossed the creek. On my way back to the command I discovered a large party of Indians, which proved to be the Cheyennes, coming up from the south, and I hurried to the camp with this important information.
The cavalrymen quietly mounted their horses, and were ordered to remain out of sight, while General Merritt, accompanied by two or three aides and myself, went out on a little tour of observation to a neighboring hill, from the summit of which we saw that the Indians were approaching almost directly towards us. Presently fifteen or twenty of them dashed off to the west in the direction from which we had come the night before; and upon closer observation with our field glasses, we discovered two mounted soldiers, evidently carrying dispatches for us, pushing forward on our trail.

然而在与梅里特将军熟悉相处之后，我发现他着实是一位极其出色的职业军官。
全团经过连日不懈的巡逻搜索，很快便将那一带的印第安人悉数肃清——至少我们当时是这般认为的。我们正启程踏上返回拉勒米堡的归途，一名侦察兵飞驰而至抵营报信，带来了1876年6月25日卡斯特将军（General Custer）及其麾下一众英雄在小大角河（Little Big Horn）全军覆没的惨烈噩耗；信使同时传达了给梅里特将军的指令，命他火速开拔前往费特曼堡（Fort Fetterman），赴大角山区与克鲁克将军会师。
在此次巡逻侦察中随第五骑兵团同行的斯坦顿上校（Colonel Stanton），在两天前已被派往红云代办所。就在这天傍晚，又有一名侦察兵飞马送来他发出的紧急军情：当天有800名夏延勇士（Cheyenne warriors）离开红云代办所，企图北上汇合大角地区坐牛（Sitting Bull）的反叛敌军。尽管上级严令全团立即取道费特曼堡驰援克鲁克将军，梅里特上校却毅然担起重任，当机立断决定全力截击这批夏延人；事后的战局发展充分证明，他这一决断立下了居功至伟的奇功。
他精选了500名精骑，短短两小时内全军便已拔营，顶着夜色强行军杀回帽溪（Hat Creek），即战冠溪（War-Bonnet Creek）——我们的企图是在夏延人抵达前，抢先封堵住向北横跨该溪流的印第安主要迁徙通道。我们在次日夜晚赶到了目的地。1876年7月17日拂晓，我单骑前出侦察，探明印第安人尚未渡溪。在返回营地的途中，我赫然发现一大群印第安人正自南面蜂拥逼近，后经证实这正是夏延大队人马；我火速策马回营通报这一极其关键的情报。
骑兵们悄无声息地翻鞍上马，奉命隐蔽待命；梅里特将军在两三名副官以及我的陪同下，悄悄登上一座临近的山丘进行前哨瞭望。从山顶举目望去，只见大批印第安人正几乎径直向我们的方向迎面扑来。不多时，其中约有15到20名骑手突然调头疾奔向西，朝我们前一晚行军经过的路线猛扑过去；我们举起望远镜凝神细看，只见两名骑马的士兵正沿着我们的马蹄足迹疾驰前行，显而易见是奉命为我们送达紧急军情的通信兵。

The Indians were evidently endeavoring to intercept these two men, and General Merritt feared that they would accomplish their object. He did not think it advisable to send out any soldiers to the assistance of the couriers, for fear that would show to the Indians that there were troops in the vicinity who were waiting for them. I finally suggested that the best plan was to wait until the couriers came closer to the command, and then, just as the Indians were about to charge, to let me take the scouts and cut them off from the main body of the Cheyennes, who were coming over the divide.
“All right, Cody,” said the General, “if you can do that, go ahead.”
I rushed back to the command, jumped on my horse, picked out fifteen men, and returned with them to the point of observation. I told General Merritt to give us the word to start out at the proper time, and presently he sang out:
“Go in now, Cody, and be quick about it. They are going to charge on the couriers.”
The two messengers were not over four hundred yards from us, and the Indians were only about two hundred yards behind them. We instantly dashed over the bluffs, and advanced on a gallop towards the Indians. A running fight lasted several minutes, during which we drove the enemy some little distance and killed three of their number. The rest of them rode off towards the main body, which had come into plain sight, and halted, upon seeing the skirmish that was going on. We were about half a mile from General Merritt, and the Indians whom we were chasing suddenly turned upon us, and another lively skirmish took place. One of the Indians, who was handsomely decorated with all the ornaments usually worn by a war chief when engaged in a fight, sang out to me, in his own tongue:
“I know you, Pa-he-haska; if you want to fight, come ahead and fight me.”
The chief was riding his horse back and forth in front of his men, as if to banter me, and I concluded to accept the challenge. I galloped towards him for fifty yards and he advanced towards me about the same distance, both of us riding at full speed, and then, when we were only about thirty yards apart, I raised my rifle and fired; his horse fell to the ground, having been killed by my bullet.
Almost at the same instant my own horse went down, he having stepped into a hole. The fall did not hurt me much, and I instantly sprang to my feet.

那些印第安人显然企图半路拦截这两名通信兵，梅里特将军深恐他们得逞。然而将军认为派出士兵出营援救并非良策，生怕此举会打草惊蛇，让印第安人察觉附近有大队主力潜伏，一时间左右权衡，委决不下。说话间，印第安人已经逼近到距离两名信使不足300码之处，距离我们也仅有200码之遥；他们猫腰潜入了一条干涸狭窄的小溪河床，伺机守株待兔，只等信使跨过溪流，便一拥而上将其生擒活剥。
“将军，”我请命道，“您只要拨给我15名精兵，由我带队，我保证把他们带到敌人看不见的隐蔽阵位；一旦印第安人准备向信使发起冲锋，我们就突然杀出，切断他们的退路！”
“好，科迪，”将军当机立断，“若是你有把握，立刻行动！”
我如旋风般奔回部队，纵身跃上马背，火速挑出15名精悍战士，带着他们悄然折返到那处瞭望哨位。我严令所有人保持绝对肃静，全员屏息凝神静候冲锋命令。
“出击，科迪，动作要快！他们就要向信使动手了！”
此时那两名信使距离我们已不过400码，而印第安人尾随在他们身后仅约200码。我向部下一声断喝下达冲锋令，大伙儿策马凌空跃入河床沟壑，连连开火射击。印第安人原本以为四周除那两名落单信使外别无敌情，冷不丁遭到这记当头棒喝，顿时被打得魂飞魄散，仓皇调头四散奔逃；但在他们逃出有效射程之前，我们当场干净利落地击毙了3人。
顺着小溪下游行进的其余印第安主力闻声赶到现场，随即向我们倾泻下密集凶猛的弹雨。面对数倍于己的敌群重重包围，我们拼尽全力死守阵地，麾下战士们个个浴血奋战，表现得无比英勇剽悍。一名相貌英俊俊朗的年轻印第安人，头戴象征部落酋长威仪的斑斓羽冠（war-bonnet），手握一柄温彻斯特连发步枪（Winchester rifle），率领几名勇士径直朝我们迅猛冲杀过来，其余印第安人则在远处游弋策应。当这位年轻酋长冲至近前辨认出我时，竟操着颇为流利的英语冲我大喊：
“我认得你，帕赫斯卡（Pa-he-haska，意为‘长发者’）！你要是有胆量决斗，就放马过来同我一战！”
酋长在部众阵前策马疾驰来回逡巡，神态张狂尽显挑衅戏谑，我当即横下一条心接下了这场生死挑战。我大步跨出队伍，朝他迎面怒吼道：
“成全你，红皮魔鬼！放马过来，咱们俩一对一单挑定生死！”
我们双双拍马对冲而上，当逼近到相距仅约30码时，几乎同时端枪怒射。他的子弹呼啸着擦过，击穿了我外套的翻领；而我的枪弹则不偏不倚，将他胯下的坐骑当场击毙。
几乎就在同一瞬间，我自己的坐骑也因前蹄踏入洞穴轰然栽倒在地。这一跤并未让我受什么重伤，我敏捷如豹地一跃而起，双脚稳稳站定在地上。

The Indian had also recovered himself, and we were now both on foot, and not more than twenty paces apart. We fired at each other simultaneously. My usual luck did not desert me on this occasion, for his bullet missed me, while mine struck him in the breast. He reeled and fell, but before he had fairly touched the ground I was upon him, knife in hand, and had driven the keen-edged weapon to its hilt in his heart. Jerking his war-bonnet off, I scientifically scalped him in about five seconds.
The whole affair from beginning to end occupied but little time, and the Indians, seeing that I was some little distance from my company, now came charging down upon me from a hill, in hopes of cutting me off. General Merritt had witnessed the duel, and realizing the danger I was in, ordered Colonel Mason with Company K to hurry to my rescue. The order came none too soon, for had it been given one minute later I would have had not less than two hundred Indians upon me. As the soldiers came up I swung the Indian chieftain’s topknot and bonnet in the air, and shouted: “*The first scalp for Custer.*”
General Merritt, seeing that he could not now ambush the Indians, ordered the whole regiment to charge upon them. They made a stubborn resistance for a little while, but it was of no use for any eight hundred, or even sixteen hundred Indians to try and check a charge of the gallant old Fifth Cavalry, and they soon came to that conclusion and began a running retreat towards Red Cloud Agency. For thirty-five miles we drove them; pushing them so hard that they were obliged to abandon their loose horses, their camp equipage and everything else. We drove them into the agency, and followed in ourselves, notwithstanding the possibility of our having to encounter the thousands of Indians at that point. We were uncertain whether or not the other agency Indians had determined to follow the example of the Cheyennes and strike out upon the warpath; but that made no difference with the Fifth Cavalry, for they would have fought them all if necessary. It was dark when we rode into the agency, where we found thousands of Indians collected together; but they manifested no disposition to fight.
While at the agency I learned the name of the Indian Chief whom I had killed in the morning; it was Yellow Hand; a son of old Cut-nose﻿—a leading chief of the Cheyennes.

那印第安人也已翻身跃起，此时我们双双徒步对峙，彼此相距不过20步之遥。我们几乎在同一弹指间同时扣动扳机。上苍赐予我的一贯好运在这一生死攸关的当口再度眷顾了我：他的子弹射偏掠过，而我的子弹却结结实实洞穿了他的胸膛。他身形摇晃轰然倾倒，然而还没等他完全瘫倒在尘土中，我已飞身扑上前去，拔出雪亮的利刃，将冰冷的锋刃齐根没入了他的心脏。我猛力扯下他的战冠羽饰，手起刀落，仅仅用了大约5秒钟，便纯熟精准地剥下了他的头皮。
这场决斗从头至尾不过瞬息之间，四周的印第安人眼见我孤身一人落单在队伍前沿，纷纷从山丘上狂呼策马向下冲杀，企图将我合围绞杀。梅里特将军在远方亲眼目睹了这场决斗，察觉到我身陷重围命悬一线，当即命令梅森上校（Colonel Mason）率领K连火速驰援接应。这道命令下达得千钧一发，哪怕只要再耽搁哪怕一分钟，我必将被不少于200名印第安人彻底吞没。当大队骑兵如狂潮般策马赶到近前时，我高高扬起那名印第安酋长的发辫与战冠，在风中振臂高呼：“*为卡斯特报仇的第一张头皮！（The first scalp for Custer.）*”
梅里特将军眼见伏击奇袭的战机已过，索性下令全团发动排山倒海般的大冲锋。印第安人起初还负隅顽抗了一阵子，但莫说是800人，纵使有1600名印第安狂徒，也绝无可能阻挡声名赫赫的功勋第五骑兵团的铁蹄洪流；他们很快便痛苦地意识到这一点，开始沿着通往红云代办所的方向一路溃退溃逃。我们乘胜追击，策马足足追杀驱赶了35英里；攻势之凌厉迅猛，迫使他们沿途狼狈丢弃了未套挽具的备用马匹、宿营辎重以及随身携带的一切家当。我们一路将他们狠狠驱赶进代办所辖区，并紧随其后长驱直入，哪怕明知在该据点可能会撞上数以千计的当地印第安人，也毫不畏缩。我们当时尚无法断定代办所辖区内的其余印第安部族是否已决意效仿夏延人走上全面反叛的战争之路；但这对于悍勇绝伦的第五骑兵团来说毫无二致——若有必要，哪怕与全境所有敌寇决一死战，全团官兵也万死不辞。夜幕低垂之际，我们策马驰入代办所，只见成千上万的印第安人黑压压地聚集在一起；然而面对我们的森严铁骑，他们已全然丧失了任何抵抗应战的意志。
在代办所驻留期间，我打听到了上午被我亲手击毙的那位印第安酋长的大名：他名叫黄手（Yellow Hand），是夏延族大酋长——老断鼻（old Cut-nose）的亲生儿子。

Cut-nose, having learned that I had killed his son sent a white interpreter to me with a message to the effect that he would give me four mules if I would turn over to him Yellow Hand’s war-bonnet, guns, pistols, ornaments, and other paraphernalia which I had captured. I sent back word to the old gentleman that it would give me pleasure to accommodate him, but I could not do it this time.
The next morning we started to join General Crook, who was camped near the foot of Cloud Peak in the Big Horn Mountains; awaiting the arrival of the Fifth Cavalry, before proceeding against the Sioux, who were somewhere near the head of the Little Big Horn﻿—as his scouts informed him. We made rapid marches and reached General Crook’s camp on Goose Creek about the 3rd of August.
At this camp I met many old friends, among whom was Colonel Royal, who had received his promotion to the Lieutenant Colonelcy of the Third Cavalry. He introduced me to General Crook, whom I had never met before, but of whom I had often heard. He also introduced me to the General’s chief guide, Frank Grouard, a half breed, who had lived six years with Sitting Bull, and knew the country thoroughly.
We remained in this camp only one day, and then the whole troop pulled out for the Tongue River, leaving our wagons behind, but taking with us a large pack train. We marched down the Tongue River for two days, thence in a westerly direction over to the Rosebud, where we struck the main Indian trail, leading down this stream. From the size of the trail, which appeared to be about four days old, we estimated that there must have been in the neighborhood of seven thousand Indians who had made the broad trail.
At this point we were overtaken by Jack Crawford, familiarly known as “Captain Jack, the Poet Scout of the Black Hills,” and right here I will insert the following lines, written by him, just after the “Custer Massacre,” upon receiving from me the following dispatch:

我高高伫立在山头，怀着极大的兴味打量着眼前这出令人忍俊不禁的调兵遣将：区区单枪匹马的一介凡人，竟能让一整支装备精良的正规大军全军肃立、严阵以待摆开阵地决战，这支部队的士气必定已被惊吓到了草木皆兵的地步。待我居高临下欣赏够了这出好戏之后，我挥舞着帽子策马飞驰疾奔下山，直扑他们的散兵线。当冲至距大军约一百码处时，第七骑兵团的韦尔上校（Colonel Weir）飞马出阵前来迎候。他一眼便认出了我，陪同我穿过严密的警戒防线；随后他扯开嗓门冲全军放声大呼：“弟兄们，这位就是野牛比尔！咱们队伍里的老兵没有不认识他的，大伙儿给他欢呼喝彩！”话音未落，全团官兵爆发出响彻云霄的三声雷鸣欢呼，欢呼声随即如巨浪般沿着整条战线此起彼伏经久不息。
韦尔上校将我郑重引荐给特里将军。在回答将军的焦急询问时，我向他明确报告说，此前所谓遭遇印第安大军的警报纯属虚惊一场，他的斥候所看见的滚滚烟尘，其实是克鲁克将军麾下的正规大军在开进。

General Terry thereupon rode forward to meet General Crook, and I accompanied him at his request. That night both commands went into camp on the Rosebud. General Terry had his wagon train with him, and everything to make life comfortable on an Indian campaign. He had large wall tents and portable beds to sleep in, and large hospital tents for dining-rooms. His camp looked very comfortable and attractive, and presented a great contrast to that of General Crook, who had for his headquarters only one small fly tent; and whose cooking utensils consisted of a quart cup﻿—in which he made his coffee himself﻿—and a stick, upon which he broiled his bacon. When I compared the two camps, I came to the conclusion that General Crook was an Indian fighter; for it was evident that he had learned that, to follow and fight Indians, a body of men must travel lightly and not be detained by a wagon train or heavy luggage of any kind.
That evening General Terry ordered General Miles to take his regiment, the Fifth Infantry, and return by a forced march to the Yellowstone, and proceed down that river by steamboat to the mouth of Powder River, to intercept the Indians, in case they attempted to cross the Yellowstone. General Mills made a forced march that night of thirty-five miles, which was splendid traveling for an infantry regiment through a mountainous country.
Generals Crook and Terry spent that evening and the next day in council, and on the following morning both commands moved out on the Indian trail. Although General Terry was the senior officer, he did not assume command of both expeditions, but left General Crook in command of his own troops, although they operated together. We crossed the Tongue River to Powder River, and proceeded down the latter stream to a point twenty miles from its junction with the Yellowstone, where the Indian trail turned to the southeast in the direction of the Black Hills. The two commands now being nearly out of supplies, the trail was abandoned, and the troops kept on down Powder River to its confluence with the Yellowstone, and remained there several days. Here we met General Mills, who reported that no Indians had as yet crossed the Yellowstone. Several steamboats soon arrived with a large quantity of supplies, and once more the “Boys in Blue” were made happy.

特里将军随即策马向前与克鲁克将军会晤，并应其邀请由我随行陪同。当晚，两路大军在玫瑰花蕾溪畔各自安营扎寨。特里将军随军带着庞大的辎重车队，凡征讨印第安人战役中能让行军生活舒坦惬意的一应用度，无不一应俱全。他配备了宽敞高大的直壁帐篷和可折叠的行军便携床铺，甚至用巨大的野战医院帐篷作为官兵就餐的餐厅。他的营地看起来舒适优雅、赏心悦目，与克鲁克将军的简陋营地形成了极其强烈的鲜明反差：克鲁克将军的司令部仅仅是一顶狭小的遮阳单棚篷；他的全部炊具不过是一个一品脱装的小铁杯——他总是在里面亲手烹煮自己的咖啡——外加一根用来插着培根在篝火上炙烤的树枝。每当我把这两座营地暗自作比时，我便深切认定克鲁克将军才是真正千锤百炼的征剿印第安人行家里手；显而易见，他早已用血与火的经验领悟到：若想在这片荒原上追猎并彻底击溃印第安人，一支部队必须轻装简从、迅捷如风，绝不可被沉重的辎重车队或任何繁重行李所拖累羁绊。
当天傍晚，特里将军命令迈尔斯将军（General Miles，原文亦作 Mills）率领他的第五步兵团（Fifth Infantry）以强行军杀回黄石河，随后搭乘蒸汽轮船顺流而下直奔粉河口，以切断并拦截可能企图横渡黄石河逃遁的印第安人。迈尔斯将军在当夜创下了强行军35英里的骄人战绩，对于一支跋涉在崇山峻岭崎岖地形中的步兵团而言，这样的推进速度堪称奇迹神速。
克鲁克将军与特里将军在当晚及次日闭门举行了多次军事会议；到了第三天清晨，两路大军再度沿着印第安人留下的踪迹大举开拔追击。特里将军资历与军衔虽高，却并未统揽两军的总指挥大权，而是让克鲁克将军继续独立统辖自己的本部人马，双方保持协同作战。我们跨过唐河挺进粉河，并沿着粉河顺流而下，推进至距离其与黄石河交汇处约20英里的地界；在此处，印第安人的足迹突然折向东南，朝黑山方向延伸而去。此时两支大军的粮草给养几乎消耗殆尽，不得不放弃顺藤摸瓜的追踪，大部队继续沿粉河下行直至与黄石河的交汇处，并在彼处驻扎休整了数日。在这里我们见到了迈尔斯将军，他汇报称迄今尚无任何印第安人能够渡过黄石河。不多时，几艘满载大批补给物资的蒸汽轮船相继靠岸，饱受艰辛的“蓝衫军健儿们（Boys in Blue）”终于再次欢欣鼓舞、喜笑颜开。
