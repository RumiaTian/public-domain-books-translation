## XXIV Hunting Expedition / 第二十四章 围猎远征
===Original===

Early in the month of September, 1871, information was received at Fort McPherson that General Sheridan and a party of invited friends were coming out to the post to have a grand hunt in the vicinity, and to explore the country from McPherson to Fort Hays, in Kansas. On the morning of September 22nd they arrived in a special car at North Platte, a station on the Union Pacific, distant eighteen miles from Fort McPherson.
The party consisted of General Sheridan, Lawrence R. Jerome, James Gordon Bennett, of the *New York Herald*; Leonard W. Jerome, Carroll Livingston, Major J. G. Hecksher, General Fitzhugh, General H. E. Davies, Captain M. Edward Rogers, Colonel J. Scuyler Crosby, Samuel Johnson, General Anson Stager, of the Western Union Telegraph Company; Charles Wilson, editor of the *Chicago Evening Journal*; General Rucker, Quartermaster-General, and Dr. Asch﻿—the two last-named being of General Sheridan’s staff. They were met at the station by General Emory and Major Brown, with a cavalry company as escort and a sufficient number of vehicles to carry the distinguished visitors and their baggage.
A brisk drive of less than two hours over a hard and smooth road brought them to the fort, where they found the garrison, consisting of five companies of the Fifth Cavalry, under the command of General Carr, out on parade awaiting their arrival. The band played some martial music, and the cavalry passed very handsomely in review before General Sheridan. The guests were then most hospitably received, and assigned to comfortable quarters.
Lieutenant Hayes, the quartermaster of the expedition, arranged everything for the comfort of the party. One hundred cavalry under command of Major Brown were detailed as an escort. A train of sixteen wagons was provided to carry the baggage, supplies, and forage for the trip; and, besides these, there were three four-horse ambulances in which the guns were carried, and in which members of the party who became weary of the saddle might ride and rest. At General Sheridan’s request I was to accompany the expedition; he introduced me to all his friends, and gave me a good send-off.

车队在平整坚实的道路上轻快飞驰了不到2小时便抵达要塞，驻军由卡尔将军（General Carr）统领的第五骑兵团（Fifth Cavalry）5个连组成，此刻已列队肃立在校场恭候大驾。军乐队奏响激昂的进行曲，骑兵部队英姿飒爽地从谢里登将军面前接受检阅。随后宾客们受到了极其热忱的款待，并被妥善安置在舒适的下榻处。
远征军军需官海斯中尉（Lieutenant Hayes）事无巨细地为贵宾们安排好了一切起居保障。由布朗少校亲自统领的100名骑兵受命全程护驾。一支由16辆大车组成的车队专司运载全队的行李、给养与草料；除此之外，还配备了3辆四马双蓬客运救护车，车上装载着猎枪，供骑马疲惫的宾客登车乘坐歇息。应谢里登将军的亲切指名，我受命随远征军全程担任向导；将军郑重将我引荐给他的所有挚友，并为我送上了极高的赞誉。

During the afternoon and evening the gentlemen were all entertained at the post in a variety of ways, including dinner and supper parties, and music and dancing; at a late hour they retired to rest in their tents at the camp which they occupied outside the post﻿—named Camp Rucker in honor of General Rucker.
At five o’clock next morning a cavalry bugle sounded the reveille, and soon all were astir in the camp, preparatory to pulling out for the first day’s march. I rose fresh and eager for the trip, and as it was a nobby and high-toned outfit which I was to accompany, I determined to put on a little style myself. So I dressed in a new suit of light buckskin, trimmed along the seams with fringes of the same material; and I put on a crimson shirt handsomely ornamented on the bosom, while on my head I wore a broad sombrero. Then mounting a snowy white horse﻿—a gallant stepper﻿—I rode down from the fort to the camp, rifle in hand. I felt first-rate that morning, and looked well.
The expedition was soon under way. Our road for ten miles wound through a wooded ravine called Cottonwood Canyon, intersecting the high ground, or divide, as it is called, between the Platte and Republican Rivers. Upon emerging from the canyon we found ourselves upon the plains. First in the line rode General Sheridan, followed by his guests, and then the orderlies. Then came the ambulances, in one of which were carried five greyhounds, brought along to course the antelope and rabbit. With the ambulances marched a pair of Indian ponies belonging to Lieutenant Hayes﻿—captured during some Indian fight﻿—and harnessed to a light wagon, which General Sheridan occasionally used. These little horses, but thirteen hands high, showed more vigor and endurance than any other of the animals we had with us. Following the ambulances came the main body of the escort and the supply wagons.
We marched seventeen miles the first day, and went into camp on Fox Creek, a tributary of the Republican. No hunting had as yet been done; but I informed the gentlemen of the party that we would strike the buffalo country the next day. A hundred or more questions were then asked me by this one and that one, and the whole evening was spent principally in buffalo talk, sandwiched with stories of the plains﻿—both of war and of the chase. Several of the party, who were good vocalists, gave us some excellent music.

在下午和傍晚，要塞举办了丰富多彩的娱乐活动款待各位绅士，包括午宴、晚宴、音乐演奏和欢快舞蹈；深夜时分，贵宾们方才移步要塞营外专为他们搭设的帐篷营地就寝——为向鲁克将军致敬，该营地被命名为鲁克营（Camp Rucker）。
次日清晨5点整，骑兵嘹亮的军号声吹响了起床号，营地里顿时一片人声鼎沸，全员紧张有序地为第一天的拔营开拔做准备。我起得格外早，精神抖擞、摩拳擦掌；既然此番陪同的是这样一支高贵显赫、风头无两的贵胄队伍，我暗忖自己也绝不能跌了份儿，非得好好拾掇装扮一番不可。于是我换上了一身簇新的浅色鹿皮猎装，衣缝与裤缝两旁缀满了同材质的鹿皮流苏；内穿一件胸前绣有华美刺绣图案的深红衬衫，头顶一顶宽檐墨西哥阔边毡帽。随后我翻身跃上一匹雪白如霜的矫健骏马，手中横端猎枪，气宇轩昂地从要塞驰入大营。那天清晨我自觉神采飞扬、威风凛凛，卖相极佳。
远征大队很快启程上路。我们前行了10英里，穿过一条名为棉木峡谷（Cottonwood Canyon）的茂密林壑深谷，横切过普拉特河与共和河（Republican River）之间的高地分水岭。一跨出峡谷，苍茫辽阔的大平原便豁然展现在眼前。行进纵队的最前方由谢里登将军领骑，紧随其后的是随行贵宾，接着是勤务护卫兵。随后便是几辆客运车，其中一辆车上载着5条格力灵缇犬，专门带去在荒原上追猎羚羊与野兔。与车辆并排行进的是属于海斯中尉的一对印第安小马——那是早先在某次与印第安人（Indian）交战中缴获的战利品——套拉着一辆轻便马车，谢里登将军偶有乘坐。这两匹小马体高不过13手高（约52英寸），却展现出比全队任何牲口都要旺盛的体魄与耐力。走在车队后方的，则是护航骑兵主力部队与辎重大车队。
第一天我们行军了17英里，在共和河的支流福克斯溪（Fox Creek）畔安营扎寨。当天尚未正式开猎，但我胸有成竹地告知各位绅士，明天一早我们将正式踏入野牛繁盛的猎场。这一下引发了大伙儿极大的兴致，这个问、那个提，抛出了上百个关切问题；整个夜晚的营火长谈几乎完全围绕着野牛这一主题，间杂着平原征战与荒原围猎的惊险传奇。宾客中有几位嗓音极佳的歌唱好手，为全营献上了多曲动听悦耳的乐章。

We closed the evening by christening the camp, naming it Camp Brown, in honor of the gallant officer in command of the escort.
At three o’clock next morning the bugle called us to an early start. We had breakfast at half-past four, and at six were in the saddle. All were eager to see and shoot the buffaloes which I assured them we would certainly meet during the day. After marching five miles, the advance guard, of which I had the command, discovered six buffaloes grazing at a distance of about two miles from us. We returned to the hunters with this information, and they at once consulted with me as to the best way to attack the “enemy.”
Acting upon my suggestions, Fitzhugh, Crosby, Lawrence Jerome, Livingston, Hecksher and Rogers, accompanied by myself as guide, rode through a convenient canyon to a point beyond the buffaloes, so that we were to the windward of the animals. The rest of the party made a detour of nearly five miles, keeping behind the crest of a hill. We charged down upon the buffaloes, at full gallop, and just then the other party emerged from their concealment and witnessed the exciting chase. The buffaloes started off in a line, single file. Fitzhugh, after a lively gallop, led us all and soon came alongside the rear buffalo, at which he fired. The animal faltered, and then with another shot Fitzhugh brought him to the ground. Crosby dashed by him and leveled another of the herd, while Livingston dropped a third. Those who were not directly engaged in the hunt now came up and congratulated the men upon their success, and Fitzhugh was at once hailed as the winner of the buffalo cup; while all sympathized with Hecksher, whose chance had been the best at the start, but who lost by reason of his horse falling and rolling over him.
The hunt being over, the column moved forward on its march passing through a prairie-dog town, several miles in extent. These animals are found throughout the plains, living together in a sort of society; their numberless burrows in their “towns” adjoin each other, so that great care is necessary in riding through these places, as the ground is so undermined as often to fall in under the weight of a horse.

我们以命名大营为当晚的盛会画上句号，将此地定名为布朗营（Camp Brown），以向统领护卫部队的英勇军官致敬。
次日凌晨3点，嘹亮的军号便催促我们提前拔营启程。我们在4点半吃过早饭，6点整已全员翻身上鞍。人人跃跃欲试，迫不及待想要亲眼目睹并猎杀我承诺当天必定会撞见的野牛大群。行军了5英里后，由我亲自率领的前锋斥候哨便在前方约2英里处发现了6头正在悠闲吃草的野牛。我们迅速折返向猎手们通报了这一大好消息，大伙儿当即聚拢过来同我商议围剿这群“猎物敌人”的最佳战法。
按照我的部署提议，菲茨休、克罗斯比、劳伦斯·杰罗姆、利文斯顿、赫克歇尔和罗杰斯在我的向导引领下，策马穿过一条地形隐蔽的峡谷绕行至野牛群侧后方，确保我们始终处于野牛的上风向。其余人员则沿着山脊背风处迂回兜抄了近5英里。部署就绪后，我们拍马全速冲杀向野牛群，正当此时，潜伏在掩体后的另一支观摩队伍也策马跃出，尽情饱览这场扣人心弦的追猎大戏。6头野牛受惊排成一字长队落荒而逃。菲茨休纵马疾驰一马当先，很快便并排追上了队尾的那头野牛，抬手一枪将其击中。中弹的野牛脚步踉跄，菲茨休紧接着补上一枪将其当场击毙。克罗斯比风驰电掣般从旁掠过，放倒了牛群中的另一头；利文斯顿亦手起枪落撂倒了第三头。未直接参战的旁观者们纷纷策马围拢上前向凯旋的猎手们热烈祝贺，菲茨休当场被大家推戴为斩获野牛杯的首魁英雄；而大家也无不对赫克歇尔报以深深的同情——他原本起步时占尽先机、胜算最大，不料座骑中途失蹄摔倒，战马翻滚狠狠砸在他身上，致使他痛失夺冠良机。
追猎告一段落后，全军继续列队向前推进，穿过了一处绵延数英里之巨的草原犬鼠聚居区。这种小动物遍布平原各地，群居生活在某种井然有序的微型社会中；它们错综复杂的洞穴在地下彼此贯通成连绵的“市镇”，以至于骑马穿行其间必须打起十二分小心，因为地表已被彻底掏空，常常会在战马重压之下轰然塌陷。

Around the entrance to their holes the ground is piled up almost a foot high; on these little elevations the prairie-dogs sit upon their hind legs, chattering to each other and observing whatever passes on the plains. They will permit a person to approach quite near, but when they have viewed him closely, they dive into their dens with wonderful quickness. They are difficult to kill, and if hit, generally succeed in crawling underground before they can be captured. Rattlesnakes and small owls are generally found in great numbers in the prairie-dog towns, and live in the same holes with the dogs on friendly terms. A few of the prairie-dogs were killed, and were found to be very palatable eating.
A short distance beyond the dog town we discovered a settlement of five white men, who proved to be the two Clifford brothers, Arthur Ruff, Dick Seymour and John Nelson﻿—the latter already referred to in these pages. Each of them had a squaw wife and numerous half-breed children, living in tents of buffalo skins. They owned a herd of horses and mules and a few cattle, and had cultivated a small piece of land. Their principal occupation was hunting, and they had a large number of buffalo hides, which, they had tanned in the Indian manner.
Upon reaching Pleasant Valley, on Medicine Creek, our party divided into two detachments﻿—one hunting along the bank of the stream for elk or deer, and the other remaining with the main body of the escort. The elk hunters met with no success whatever, but the others ran across plenty of buffaloes, and nearly everybody killed one or more before the day was over. Lawrence Jerome made an excellent shot; while riding in an ambulance he killed a buffalo which attempted to cross the line of march.
At about four o’clock p.m., we arrived at Mitchell’s Fork of the Medicine, having traveled thirty-five miles during that day, and there we went into camp﻿—calling it Camp Jack Hayes, in honor of Lieutenant Hayes.
On the next morning, the 25th, we moved out of camp at eight o’clock. The party was very successful through the day in securing game, Hecksher, Fitzhugh, Livingston and Lieutenant Hayes; and in fact all did good shooting.
Lawrence Jerome persuaded me to let him ride Buckskin Joe, the best buffalo horse in the whole outfit, and on his back he did wonders among the buffaloes.

在洞口四周，泥土被它们堆积成近1英尺高的隆起土包；草原犬鼠平时就端坐在这些小土包上，后肢着地直立，一边彼此唧唧喳喳交头接耳，一边警觉打量着平原上过往的一切动静。它们允许生人走近到相当近的距离，但只要仔细瞧清来人面孔，便会以惊人的神速一头扎进洞穴深处。它们极难被击毙，即便中弹，通常也能在被捕获前拼尽最后一口气挣扎爬入地底深处。响尾蛇与穴鸮等小型猫头鹰在犬鼠市镇里随处可见，它们竟能在同一洞穴中与犬鼠和平共处。战士们顺手击毙了几只犬鼠，剥皮烤熟后品尝，发现滋味竟格外鲜美可口。
越过犬鼠城镇不远，我们撞见了一处由5名白人拓荒者组成的定居点，经查验正是克利福德两兄弟（Clifford brothers）、亚瑟·拉夫（Arthur Ruff）、迪克·西摩（Dick Seymour）与约翰·纳尔逊（John Nelson）——后者在本书前文已有详述。他们每个人都娶了印第安妇人为妻，繁育了众多混血子女，全家人居住在野牛皮缝制的帐篷里。他们拥有一群马匹、骡子和少量耕牛，开垦了一小方土地。他们的生计主要依赖打猎，手头积攒了大量按照印第安传统工艺鞣制精良的野牛皮张。
行抵梅迪辛溪（Medicine Creek）畔的欢乐谷（Pleasant Valley）后，远征队兵分两路——一路沿溪岸搜寻麋鹿与鹿群踪迹，另一路则护送辎重大队稳步推进。猎鹿小分队整日一无所获，而大队人马沿途却撞见了数不胜数的野牛大群，天黑前几乎人人都猎杀了一头或数头野牛。劳伦斯·杰罗姆上演了一记技惊四座的绝杀：当时他正舒适地坐在救护车里颠簸前行，端枪隔窗一发击毙了一头胆敢横穿行军列队的莽撞野牛。
下午4点左右，大队抵达成梅迪辛溪的米切尔岔道（Mitchell’s Fork），全天行军里程达35英里；全军就地扎营——为表彰海斯中尉的辛劳，大营被命名为杰克·海斯营（Camp Jack Hayes）。
次日清晨25日，全队于8点整拔营启程。这一整天众人的猎运亨通，赫克歇尔、菲茨休、利文斯顿与海斯中尉皆有丰硕斩获，事实上几乎人人都展现了精湛枪法。
劳伦斯·杰罗姆百般央求我，想借骑我的战马“鹿皮乔”——全营公认最顶尖的猎牛战驹；换乘我的宝马后，他在野牛群中所向披靡，出尽了风头。

Leonard Jerome, Bennett and Rogers also were very successful in buffalo hunting.
Our camp of this night was named Camp Asch to commemorate our surgeon, Dr. Asch. The evening was pleasantly spent around the camp fires in relating the adventures of the day.
Upon crossing the Republican River on the morning of the 26th, we came upon an immense number of buffaloes scattered over the country in every direction, as far as the eye could reach and all had an opportunity to do as much hunting as they wished. The wagons and troops moved slowly along in the direction of the next camp, while the hunters went off separately, or by twos and threes, in different directions, and all were rewarded with abundant success. Lawrence Jerome, however, had his career suddenly checked. He had dismounted to make a steady and careful shot, and thoughtlessly let go of the bridle. The buffalo failing to take a tumble, as he ought to have done, started off at a lively gait, followed by Buckskin Joe﻿—the horse being determined to do some hunting on his own account﻿—the last seen of him, he was a little ahead of the buffalo, and gaining slightly, leaving his late rider to his own reflections and the prospect of a tramp; his desolate condition was soon discovered and another horse warranted not to run under any provocation, was sent to him. It maybe stated here that three days afterwards, as I subsequently learned, Buckskin Joe, all saddled and bridled, turned up at Fort McPherson.
We pitched our tents for the night in a charming spot on the bank of Beaver Creek. The game was so abundant that we remained there one day. This stopping place was called. Camp Cody, in honor of the reader’s humble servant.

当我们班师返回麦克弗森堡时，遇上了罗亚尔·巴克先生（Mr. Royal Buck），他的父亲与全家拓荒小队此前在比弗溪不幸惨遭波尼杀手（Pawnee Killer）率领的印第安凶徒灭门屠戮。他手持军区司令长官的亲笔特批函件，请求军方派遣护航部队协助他搜寻父亲及遇难亲属的骸骨遗物。驻军抽调了两个骑兵连随行护送，我受命担任向导引路。由于我们早先俘获并审讯的那名印第安老妇——本书前文已有详述——无法精准指认小队在比弗溪遇害的确切地点，我们全队在茫茫荒野上漫山遍野地地毯式搜寻了整整2天，起初未发现遇害者的任何蛛丝马迹。但工夫不负有心人，我们的艰辛搜寻最终迎来了突破。我们先是搜寻到了大车的破碎残骸，接着从散落的杂物中辨认出一两封泛黄的旧家信，巴克先生一眼便认出那正是家父苍劲熟悉的亲笔字迹。随后我们寻获了部分遇难同胞的骸骨并郑重予以安葬，除此之外别无所获。此时时令已步入深秋，我们遂拔营收兵返回麦克弗森堡。
此后不久，第五骑兵团奉命换防调往亚利桑那——那可绝不是一处适合当兵服役的安乐福地。我同该团军官朝夕相处并肩征战已逾3年之久，彼此间结下了深厚无比的袍泽之情，原本已下定决心随他们一同赴任；然而就在此时，要塞收到了谢里登将军发给驻军指挥官的亲笔手令，明令“切勿带走科迪（Cody）”，指示我必须坚守岗位、留任原职。
