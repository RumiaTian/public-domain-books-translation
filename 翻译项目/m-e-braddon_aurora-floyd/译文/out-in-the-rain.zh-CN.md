## XVIII Out in the Rain / 十八 雨中
===Original===

The second dinner-bell rang five minutes after the “Softy” had left Aurora, and Mr. John Mellish came out upon the lawn to look for his wife. He came whistling across the grass, and whisking the roses with his pocket-handkerchief in very gaiety of heart. He had quite forgotten the anguish of that miserable morning after the receipt of Mr. Pastern’s letter. He had forgotten all but that his Aurora was the loveliest and dearest of women, and that he trusted her with the boundless faith of his big, honest heart. “Why should I doubt such a noble, impetuous creature?” he thought; “doesn’t every feeling and every sentiment write itself upon her lovely, expressive face in characters the veriest fool could read? If I please her, what bright smiles light up in her black eyes! If I vex her﻿—as I do, poor awkward idiot that I am, a hundred times a day﻿—how the two black arches contract over her pretty impertinent nose, while the red lips pout defiance and disdain! Shall I doubt her because she keeps one secret from me, and freely tells me I must forever remain ignorant of it; when an artful woman would try to set my mind at rest with some shallow fiction invented to deceive me? Heaven bless her! no doubt of her shall ever darken my life again, come what may.”
It was easy for Mr. Mellish to make this mental vow, believing fully that the storm was past, and that lasting fair weather had set in.
“Lolly darling,” he said, winding his great arm round his wife’s waist, “I thought I had lost you.”
She looked up at him with a sad smile.
“Would it grieve you much, John,” she said in a low voice, “if you were really to lose me?”
He started as if he had been struck, and looked anxiously at her pale face.
“Would it grieve me, Lolly!” he repeated; “not for long; for the people who came to your funeral would come to mine. But, my darling, my darling, what can have made you ask this question? Are you ill, dearest? You have been looking pale and tired for the last few days, and I have thought nothing of it. What a careless wretch I am!”
“No, no, John,” she said; “I don’t mean that. I know you would grieve, dear, if I were to die. But suppose something were to happen which would separate us forever﻿—something which would compel me to leave this place never to return to it﻿—what then?”
“What then, Lolly?” answered her husband, gravely.

血色从约翰·梅利什的脸上褪尽了。
「北门房！」他说。「你去北门房了？」
「我去的是*北门房那个方向*。」奥罗拉答道，把这几个字咬得冷嘲而重。「你的情报分毫不差，鲍威尔太太；只是我不知道，你有这等雅兴，监视我的行动。」
梅利什先生像是没有听见。他看看妻子，又看看妻子的女伴，神情半是茫然——一种新醒的疑心、一种挣扎不明的困惑——看着叫人心里发疼。
「北门房！」他重复道；「你在北门房做什么，奥罗拉？」
「你要我穿着湿衣裳站在这里讲么？」梅利什太太问，那双大黑眼睛燃起愤傲的光。「若是为叫鲍威尔太太满意，我可以就在这里说明；若只为你一个人，上楼说也一样。」
她朝门口走去，湿披肩拖在身后，哪怕一身滴水，也减不了半分王后气；塞米拉米德和克娄巴特拉，想必也淋过雨。门洞里她站住，回头望了丈夫一眼。
「明天你要带我去伦敦，梅利什先生。」她说。
