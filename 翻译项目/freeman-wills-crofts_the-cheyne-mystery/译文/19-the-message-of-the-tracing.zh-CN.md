## XIX The Message of the Tracing / 十九 描图的讯息

===Original===
Inspector French spread the photograph on his desk, and Cheyne and Price having drawn up chairs, all three gazed at it as if expecting that in the light of their great idea its message would have become obvious.

===Chinese===
弗伦奇探长把照片摊开在办公桌上，切恩和普赖斯拉过椅子凑上前，三个人一同凝视着它，仿佛指望经他们那个高明主意一照，照片的讯息便会豁然显明。

===Original===
But in this they were disappointed. The suggestion did not seem in any way to help either French or Cheyne, and Price, who of course had not seen the document before, was satisfactorily mystified. Granted that the thing was a map, granted even that it was a map of England, its meaning remained just as provokingly hidden as ever.

===Chinese===
但这一回他们失望了。那个设想似乎无论如何也帮不上弗伦奇和切恩的忙，而普赖斯——他先前当然从未见过这份文书——则被它弄得满头雾水，这效果倒也“令人满意”。就算这东西是幅地图，就算它甚至是幅英国地图，它的含义依旧同先前一样，藏得叫人干着急。

===Original===
Presently Price gave vent to an exclamation. “Hang it all!” he cried irritably, and then: “I suppose those numbers couldn’t be soundings? Could they give depths at the circles?”

===Chinese===
过了一会儿，普赖斯发出一声惊叹。“见鬼！”他烦躁地喊道，接着又说：“我想，那些数字总不会是测深读数吧？会不会是标出各个圆圈处的水深？”

===Original===
“That’s an idea,” Cheyne cried, but French shook his head.

===Chinese===
“这倒是个思路。”切恩喊道，但弗伦奇摇了摇头。

===Original===
“I think there’s more in it than that,” he observed. “If you examine those numbers you’ll find that they’re consecutive, they run from one to thirty-six. Soundings wouldn’t lend themselves to such an arrangement. You may be right, Mr. Price, and we must keep your idea in view, but I don’t see it working out for the moment.”

===Chinese===
“我看这里头的名堂不止于此。”他说道。“细看那些数字您就会发现，它们是连续的，从一排到三十六。测深读数可排不成这种阵势。您也许说得对，普赖斯先生，这个想法我们得记在心上，不过眼下我看它行不通。”

===Original===
Silence reigned for a few moments, then Price sat back from the table and spoke again.

===Chinese===
一时无人作声，随后普赖斯从桌上直起身子，往椅背上一靠，又开口了。

===Original===
“Look here, Inspector,” he said, knocking the ashes out of his pipe and beginning to fill it with his strong, black mixture, “you said something just now I didn’t quite follow. Let’s get your notion clear. You talked of this thing beginning with the sea﻿—at Schulz, and ending with the sea﻿—at *L’Escaut*, and Schulz’s message being a map. Just what was in your mind?”

===Chinese===
“听着，探长，”他说着，在烟斗上磕掉烟灰，动手往里装他那浓烈的黑色板烟，“您刚才说的话我没能完全听懂。咱们把您的想法摆摆明白。您说这件事以海起头——在舒尔茨那里，又以海收尾——在莱斯考号上，还说舒尔茨的遗言是一幅地图。您心里究竟是怎么想的？”

===Original===
“Only the obvious suggestion that if you leave a message which provokes an expedition, you must also convey in your message the destination of that expedition, and a map seems the simplest way of doing it. But on second thoughts I question my first conclusion. There must be an explanation of the secret as well as a direction of how to profit by it, and it would seem to me doubtful that such an explanation could be covered by a map.”

===Chinese===
“无非是那个浅显的念头：假如你留下的一份讯息会招来一趟远征，你就必须在讯息里交代那趟远征的目的地，而地图似乎是办这件事最省事的法子。不过再一细想，我又怀疑起自己最初的结论了。这秘密既须有个说明，又须有一条如何从中取利的指引；这样一份说明能否被一幅地图涵盖，我看靠不住。”

===Original===
“Sounds all right, that,” Price admitted. “Have you any idea what the secret might be? Sounds like treasure or salvage or something of that kind.”

===Chinese===
“这话在理。”普赖斯承认道。“这秘密可能是什么，您心里有数吗？听上去像是宝藏啦、打捞啦一类的名堂。”

===Original===
“I scarcely think salvage,” French answered. “The *L’Escaut* is not a salvage boat, and a boat not specially fitted for the purpose would be of little use. But I thought of treasure all right. This Schulz might have robbed his ships﻿—there would always be money aboard, and even during the war many women traveled with jewelry. The man might easily have made a cache of valuables somewhere round the coast.”

===Chinese===
“打捞我看不像。”弗伦奇答道。“莱斯考号不是打捞船，一条不是专门为这行装备起来的船派不上多大用场。不过宝藏嘛，我确实想到了。这个舒尔茨没准劫掠过他经手的船——船上总归有钱，大战年月里连许多妇女都随身带着珠宝。这家伙满可以沿岸找个地方，窖藏下一批值钱之物。”

===Original===
“Easily,” Cheyne intervened, “or he might have learned of some valuable deposit in some out of the way cove round the coast, like those chaps in that clinking tale of Maurice Drake’s, *WO2*.”

===Chinese===
“容易得很，”切恩插话道，“再不然，他也可能是打听到了沿岸哪个偏僻小湾里藏着一宗值钱的东西，就像莫里斯·德雷克（Maurice Drake）那篇顶呱呱的小说《WO2》里那帮家伙一样。”

===Original===
“As at Terneuzen?” said French. “I read that book﻿—one of the best I ever came across. It’s a possibility, of course.”

===Chinese===
“就像泰尔讷曾（Terneuzen）那种地方？”弗伦奇说。“那本书我读过——是我读过的最好读物之一。这当然是一种可能。”

===Original===
The talk here became somewhat rambling, Price not having read *WO2* and wanting to know what it was about, but French soon reverted to his photograph. He reminded his hearers that they were all interested in its elucidation. Miss Merrill’s safety, his own professional credit, Cheyne’s peace of mind, and Price’s fortune, all were at stake.

===Chinese===
谈话到这里有些跑了野马——普赖斯没读过《WO2》，想知道它讲的是什么——但弗伦奇很快又回到他的照片上。他提醒两位听客：把这张照片琢磨透，与他们个个利害攸关。梅里尔小姐的安危、他自己的职业体面、切恩的心神安宁、普赖斯的身家富贵，全都押在上面了。

===Original===
“We have,” he went on, “evolved the idea that perhaps this tracing may be a map of England. On further thought that suggestion does not seem promising, but as we have no other let us work on it. Assume it is a map of England, and let us see if it leads us anywhere.” There were murmurs of assent from his hearers, and he continued: “Now it seems to me the first thing to do is to try if we can fit these circles and lines into the map of England. Is there anything corresponding to them in English geography?”

===Chinese===
“我们刚才，”他接着说，“琢磨出了这样一个想法：这张描图也许是幅英国地图。再想想，这设想不见得有多大指望，可咱们手头别无线索，不妨就顺着它做。姑且假定它是幅英国地图，看看能不能把咱们引到哪里。”两位听客低声应和，他便继续说道：“眼下我看，头一件事是试试能不能把这些圆圈和线条套进英国地图。英国地理里有没有跟它们对得上的东西？”

===Original===
No one being able to answer this query, French went on:

===Chinese===
这个问题谁也答不上来，弗伦奇便接着往下说：

===Original===
“I think we must distinguish between the letter circles on the one hand and those of the numbers and lines on the other. The position of the former was not altered in the faked copy; that of the latter was. From this may we not assume that the message lies in the numbers and lines only? Possibly the letters were added as a blind, as we have already assumed the words ‘expects every man to do his duty’ were added as a blind to ‘England.’ Suppose at all events that we eliminate the letter circles and concentrate on the others for our first effort?”

===Chinese===
“我看咱们得把字母圆圈跟数字、线条圆圈区分开来。前者的位置在伪造件里没有变动；后者的位置动过了。由此咱们何不推断：讯息只在数字和线条里？那些字母也许是外加的障眼法，就像我们已经认定‘期望人人恪尽职守’那几个词是加在‘英格兰’上的障眼法一样。无论如何，第一步先把字母圆圈撇开，专攻其余那些，怎么样？”

===Original===
“That sounds all right.”

===Chinese===
“听着没毛病。”

===Original===
“Good. Then let us go a step further. Have you noticed the distribution of the numbers, letters and lines? The numbers are bunched, roughly speaking, towards the center, the letters round the edge, and the irregular lines between the two. Does this central mass give us anything?”

===Chinese===
“好。那就再进一步。数字、字母和线条的分布，你们留意过没有？大体说来，数字抱团在中部，字母环在边缘，不规则线条夹在两者之间。居中这一大团，能给我们点什么吗？”

===Original===
“I get you,” Price replied. He had risen and begun to pace the room, but now he returned to the table and stood looking down at the photograph. “You know, as a matter of fact,” he went on slowly, “if, as you say, you take that central part which contains numbers only, the shape of the thing is not so very unlike England after all. Suppose the numbers represent land and the letters sea. Then this patch of letters in the top left-hand corner might be the Irish Sea, and this larger patch to the right the North Sea. And look, the letter circles form a band across the bottom. What price that for the English Channel?”

===Chinese===
“我明白您的意思。”普赖斯答道。他本已起身在屋里踱步，这时回到桌旁，站着俯视那张照片。“您知道，说实在的，”他缓缓地接着说，“照您说的，单取当中那个只含数字的部分，这东西的形状其实并不那么不像英国。假定数字代表陆地、字母代表海。那么左上角这一片字母就是爱尔兰海，右边这片大些的就是北海。再看，字母圆圈在底部连成一带。拿这一带当英吉利海峡，诸位说值不值？”

===Original===
French crossed the room, and taking a small atlas from a shelf, opened it at the map of England and laid it down beside the photograph. With a rising excitement all three compared them. Then Cheyne burst out irritably:

===Chinese===
弗伦奇走到房间另一头，从架子上取下一本小地图册，翻到英国那页地图，摊在照片旁边。三个人怀着节节升高的兴奋把它们比来比去。随后切恩烦躁地嚷了起来：

===Original===
“Confound the thing! It’s like it and it’s not like it. Let’s draw a line round those number circles and see if it makes anything like the shape.” He seized the photograph and took out a pencil.

===Chinese===
“这劳什子！说像又不像。咱们沿那些数字圆圈画条线，看看画出来像不像那个形状。”他抓过照片，掏出一支铅笔。

===Original===
But just as in the scientific and industrial worlds discoveries and inventions seldom come singly, so among these three men the begetting of ideas begot more ideas. Scarcely had Cheyne spoken when French made a little gesture of comprehension.

===Chinese===
不过，正如在科学界和实业界，发现与发明很少单独降临，在这三个人中间，念头的孕育也会催生更多念头。切恩话音未落，弗伦奇已做了一个恍然的小手势。

===Original===
“I believe I have it at last,” he said quietly but with ill-concealed eagerness in his tones. “Those irregular lines in certain of the circles are broken bits of the coast line. See here, those two between 8 and *U* are surely the Wash, and that below *H* is Flamborough Head. Let’s see if we can locate correspondingly shaped outlines on the atlas, and fill in between those on the photograph with pencil.”

===Chinese===
“我相信我终于把它抓住了。”他语气平静，声调里却掩不住急切。“某些圆圈里那些不规则线条，是海岸线的碎段。瞧这儿，8 和 U 之间那两条准是沃什湾（the Wash），H 下面那道是弗兰伯勒角（Flamborough Head）。咱们看看能不能在地图册上找到形状相应的轮廓，再用铅笔把照片上这些地方之间的海岸线补上。”

===Original===
A few seconds’ examination only were needed. Opposite, but slightly above the projection which French suggested as Flamborough Head was an angled line between *GU* and 31 which all three simultaneously pronounced St. Bee’s Head. Short double lines on each side of 24 showed two parts of the estuary of the Severn, and projections along the bottom near *X* and 27 were evidently St. Alban’s Head and Selsey Bill.

===Chinese===
只消几秒钟的查验。在弗伦奇认作弗兰伯勒角的那道突出部的对面、略微偏上处，GU 和 31 之间有一道折线，三个人不约而同断定是圣比斯角（St. Bee’s Head）。24 两侧的短短双线显示出塞文河（the Severn）河口的两个部分，底部靠近 X 和 27 的几道突出部，则显然是圣奥尔本斯角（St. Alban’s Head）和塞尔西角（Selsey Bill）。

===Original===
That they were on the right track there could now no longer be any doubt, and they set themselves with renewed energy to the problem still remaining﻿—the meaning of the circles and the numbers they contained.

===Chinese===
走对了路子，这一点如今再无可疑。于是他们抖擞精神，扑向尚未解决的难题——圆圈及圈中数字的含义。

===Original===
“We can’t locate the blessed things this way,” French pointed out. “We’ll have to rule squares on the atlas to correspond. Then we can pencil in the coast line accurately, and see just where the circles lie.”

===Chinese===
“这么着没法把这些劳什子定准。”弗伦奇指出。“得在地图册上相应地打出方格。然后就能用铅笔把海岸线描准，看清圆圈究竟落在哪里。”

===Original===
For a time measuring and the drawing of lines were the order of the day. And then at last the positions of the circles were located. They were all drawn round towns.

===Chinese===
有一阵子，量尺寸、画线条成了正经活计。最后，各个圆圈的位置终于定了下来。它们全都是圈着城镇画的。

===Original===
“Towns!” Price exclaimed. “Guess we’re getting on.”

===Chinese===
“城镇！”普赖斯叫出声来。“依我看，咱们有进展了。”

===Original===
“Towns!” Cheyne echoed in his turn. “Then you must have been right, Inspector, about those letters being merely a blind.”

===Chinese===
“城镇！”切恩也跟着应了一声。“那么探长，您说那些字母纯属障眼法，必定是说对了。”

===Original===
“I think so,” French admitted. “Look at it in this way. If only the towns and coast were marked, the shape of England would show too clearly. But adding those letter circles disguises the thing﻿—prevents the shape becoming apparent. Now, I may be wrong, but I am beginning to question very much if this map has anything to do with indicating a position﻿—I mean directly. I am beginning to think it is merely a cipher. Let us test this at all events. Let us write down the names of the towns in the order of the numbers and see if that gives us anything.”

===Chinese===
“我也这么想。”弗伦奇承认道。“这么看吧。假如标出来的只有城镇和海岸，英国的形状就未免太清楚了。可加上那些字母圆圈，就把这东西伪装起来——叫形状露不出形迹。当然，我或许错了，可我已开始十分怀疑，这幅地图究竟同标示位置有没有关系——我是说直接标示。我渐渐觉得它只是一份密码。无论如何咱们来验证一番。把城镇名称按数字顺序写下来，看看能得出什么。”

===Original===
He took a sheet of paper, while Price found No. 1 on the photograph and Cheyne identified its position with that of a town on the atlas map.

===Chinese===
他取过一张纸；普赖斯在照片上找出 1 号，切恩把它的位置同地图册上的某座城镇一一对应。

===Original===
“No. 1,” said Cheyne, “is Salisbury.”

===Chinese===
“1 号，”切恩说，“是索尔兹伯里（Salisbury）。”

===Original===
French wrote down: “1, Salisbury.”

===Chinese===
弗伦奇写下：“1，索尔兹伯里。”

===Original===
“No. 2,” went on Cheyne, “is Immingham.”

===Chinese===
“2 号，”切恩接着说，“是伊明厄姆（Immingham）。”

===Original===
“2, Immingham,” wrote French, as he remarked, “Salisbury﻿—Immingham: *S*﻿—*I*. That goes all right so far.”

===Chinese===
“2，伊明厄姆。”弗伦奇一面写一面说，“索尔兹伯里——伊明厄姆：S——I。到这一步还顺。”

===Original===
The next three towns were Liverpool, Uttoxeter, and Reading, and though none of the men could see where **silur** was leading, it was at least pronounceable.

===Chinese===
接下来三座城是利物浦、厄托克塞特（Uttoxeter）和雷丁（Reading）。三个人虽然都看不出 **silur** 会引向何方，但至少拼得出口。

===Original===
But when the next three letters were added French gave a mighty shout of victory. No. 6 was Ipswich, No. 7 Andover, and No. 8 Nottingham. **ian** added to **silur** made Silurian.

===Chinese===
可等接下来三个字母一添上，弗伦奇便迸出一声胜利的大吼。6 号是伊普斯威奇（Ipswich），7 号是安德沃（Andover），8 号是诺丁汉（Nottingham）。**ian** 加在 **silur** 之后，拼出了 Silurian（西卢里亚号）。

===Original===
“*Silurian!*” French cried, striking the table a mighty blow with his clenched fist. “*Silurian!* That begins to show a light!”

===Chinese===
“西卢里亚号！”弗伦奇喊道，攥起的拳头在桌上重重一击。“西卢里亚号！这下开始透出光亮了！”

===Original===
The others stared.

===Chinese===
另外两人瞪着他。

===Original===
“Don’t you recognize the name?” went on French. “The *Silurian* was a big Anchor liner, and she was torpedoed on her way to the States with two and a half millions in gold bars aboard!”

===Chinese===
“你们不认得这个名字吗？”弗伦奇接着说。“西卢里亚号是安卡班轮公司（Anchor Line）的一条大邮轮，她载着 250 万英镑的金条在开往美国途中被鱼雷击沉了！”

===Original===
The others held their breath and their eyes grew round.

===Chinese===
另外两人屏住呼吸，眼睛都瞪圆了。

===Original===
“Any of it recovered?”

===Chinese===
“金子打捞上来过吗？”

===Original===
“None: it was in mid-Atlantic.”

===Chinese===
“没有：那是在大西洋中部。”

===Original===
“But,” stammered Cheyne at last, “I don’t follow﻿—”

===Chinese===
“可是，”切恩终于结结巴巴地说，“我还是跟不上——”

===Original===
“I don’t follow myself,” French returned briskly, “but when the cipher which leads to a maritime expedition begins with a wreck with two and a half millions aboard, well then, I say it is suggestive. Come along, let’s read the rest of the thing. We’ll know more then.”

===Chinese===
“我自己也还没想通。”弗伦奇干脆利落地答道，“不过，凡是引向一次海上远征的密码，开篇便是一条载着 250 万英镑的沉船——那么我说，这就大有意味。来，把余下的读完。到时候咱们就知道得更多了。”
