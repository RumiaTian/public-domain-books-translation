## LX / 六十

===Original===

The Blue Knight Gathers Men and Departs from Brookside

Wore the days then till the winter came upon them, and though the season was not hard, yet was there but little coming and going about the countryside, that is to say for long journeys; but even so the Blue Knight had his hands full of business in seeing to the gathering of men and stuff for the hosting of the Barons’ League. But when March was at hand, and the roads were dry, there was no need of further message to him, and he let it be known to all and several that on the very first day of the month he would depart before sunrise. And this he told to the Maiden specially, and by this time she had got to look upon it as a thing already done, so that the news thereof took not much from her cheer, which, to say sooth, was but little.

Mighty was the hubbub and toil of their getting ready; but when the morning was come all was in good order, and the men and their wains and whatnot were all drawn up in array down on the little plain before the bridge, and they looked as if nothing might overthrow them, so stalwart they were each man, and so well learned to move as though they were one. The sun was not yet up ere there came a knock on the Maiden’s door, and she, who was fully clad, and had been looking out of her window (whence she could see all the array) for a good while, went to the door and opened, and lo! it was Sir Mark, fully armed save his head. She put out her hands to him and said: “Thou hast come to say farewell to me. See, I have saved thee the pain of saying that word; soon may it be that I shall have to say Welcome back!”

He took her hands and kissed her face many times, and she suffered him. Then he said: “O my thanks to thee! Yet hearken: If I come not back at all, when it is known for sure here that I am dead, then I rede thee make as little delay as thou mayest, but get thee gone at once, thou and thy nurse, from the pleasant house of Brookside, and go straight to the house of the Grey Sisters, which thou hast seen from without many a time, and which lieth betwixt wood and water a seven miles down the river, and tell them that I have sent you and bid them to cherish you; then will they see to thy matters in the best way they know. Much more might I say, and I know that thou wouldst hearken me, but I must forbear, lest I soften my heart overmuch for this day and this hour.”

蓝骑士聚集人马，辞别溪畔

此后一天天过去，冬天临到了他们头上；那季节虽不算严酷，乡间却甚少往来行走——这是就长途旅程而言。纵然如此，蓝骑士也忙得不可开交：为着男爵同盟的会师，他须照管征聚人马、筹办物资的种种事务。及至三月将近，道路干燥，便无须再给他传什么音信了；他向众人逐一传告：三月头一日，他将于日出之前启程。这话他还特意说与少女听；到这时，她已把此事看作既定之事，故而这消息也不曾减去她多少欢怀——说句实话，她的欢怀本就无多。

他们备装启程，忙乱与操劳好不厉害；但清晨一到，一切已安排得井井有条：人众、大车以及诸般物事，尽数在桥前那片小平原上列成阵势；他们望去仿佛天下没有什么能将他们摧翻，因为人人壮健剽悍，又素来操练得行动如一人一般。太阳尚未升起，便有人来叩少女的房门；她早已衣装齐整，在窗前张望了好一阵子（从窗口可将全阵尽收眼底），便走去开了门——看啊！竟是马克爵士，周身披挂俱全，只差头上未戴盔。她向他伸出双手，说道：“你是来向我道别的。瞧，我已免了你开口说那个字眼的苦楚；兴许用不了多久，我便得对你说一声‘欢迎回来’了！”

他握住她的双手，把她亲了又亲，她也由他。随后他说道：“啊，多谢你！然而且听我说：倘若我竟一去不返，待此地确知我已身死，那时我劝你万万不可迁延，须即刻动身——你同你的乳母，离开溪畔堡这座可悦的宅子，径直投灰衣修女的会院去。那院子你从外面已望见过许多回，就在沿河下行七英里、林木与流水之间。你告诉她们，是我打发你们去的，并吩咐她们眷顾你们；她们自会以她们所知的至善之法，替你料理诸事。我本还有许多话可说，我也知道你必肯听；但我必须忍住，唯恐在这个日子、这个时辰，我的心肠软得过了分。”

Then he turned and went, but came back in a twinkling while she still stood at the door, and said to her: “I tell thee it needeth but a little but that I should do off this weed of war and abide at home while my men wend to battle.” Then he turned again and was gone.

But the Maiden went to the window weeping thus to lose her friend, and the Carline came to her there, and they looked forth, and beheld the Knight ride down to his men. And then all the array shook and clashed, as they shouted for joy that their captain was come amongst them; and there were the two young squires, gay and bright in their broidered surcoats, and they fell into their places beside the lord, and Roland bore the wavy banner. Then arose the sun, and Sir Mark drew forth his sword and waved it aloft, and Roland shook the banner loose and displayed it in the clear air. The horns blew up, and the whole band of them got on to the bridge and went their ways toward the place where the road to the south and the east turned off from the northern road. Even so departed that glorious piece of ordered might; and when they were quite gone those two turned away from the window, and the days which were next to come seemed empty and dull.

But the Maiden told the Carline all that the Blue Knight had said to her about fleeing straightway to the Grey Sisters if he himself should fall in the war; and the two looked at each other a while, and each knew the thoughts which were in the other’s heart, and which each left unspoken; to wit that Sir Mark feared his mother’s pride and malice, what she might do if he were no longer there to refrain it; yea, and she seeking some outlet to her grief and solace for it in wrath and cruelty.

说罢他转身而去；但她仍立在门边未动，转眼间他又回来了，对她说：“我告诉你，只差那么一点，我便要卸下这一身战袍，安居家中，任我的人马自行赴战了。”说罢他重又转身，去了。

少女就这样失了她的良友，含泪走回窗前；老妪也来到她身旁，两人凭窗外望，只见那骑士策马而下，去到他的人马中间。于是全阵撼动，甲兵铿锵，人人欢呼，喜他们的统领来到队中；那两名年轻的扈从也在，绣纹罩袍鲜丽夺目，各自就位，侍于主人身侧，罗兰则擎着那面波浪纹的大旗。随即太阳升起，马克爵士拔剑出鞘，向高空挥舞；罗兰把大旗抖开，在晴空之下高高舒展。号角齐鸣，全队人马上了桥，迤逦行去，往南行与东行的大道自北路分岔之处去了。那一支齐整有序的堂堂雄师，便这样离去了；待他们去得远了，两人才从窗前转过身来，往后的日子，似乎都显得空落而无味。

其后，少女把蓝骑士对她说的那番话——倘若他殁于战阵，便须即刻逃往灰衣修女那里——一五一十告诉了老妪。两人彼此对望了一阵，各人都知道对方心里怀着什么念头，也都知道彼此都把这念头留住不说：便是马克爵士畏惧他母亲的骄横与刻毒，唯恐他一旦不在、再无人约束时，她会做出怎样的事来；不但如此，她还要在暴怒与残忍之中，为自己那份悲伤寻个发泄的门径，觅一点慰藉。
