## LVI / 五十六

===Original===

The Blue Knight Talks with the Maiden by the Way

The Blue Knight rode beside the Maiden, and it could be seen that in all ways he would take care of her and give her honour; but he was few-spoken at first, nor for a while had she much mind to speak. But after a little she looked on him aside, and seemed to think that he would be fain were she to cast a word to him. And she herself was grown of good cheer now, for she deemed herself delivered from captivity; and, however it were, she trusted in this man’s good faith and kindness. So she asked him some simple question about the way, and he started when he heard her voice, but turned and answered her frankly, and seemed as if he had liked it better if he might have made more of it. Then she said: “Fair sir, thou hast not yet told me whither we be going.”

“Nay,” he said, “that is true, and heedless it was of me, and I pray thee pardon me. We be bound for the Castle of Brookside, which is my chiefest manor house, though no great thing. But we shall not be there tonight, nor for many nights. Now if thou ask me what we shall find there, I shall tell thee that beside the serving-men and a few men-at-arms and sergeants, and three squires, thou shalt find little save my mother there, for I am unwedded as yet.”

蓝骑士途中与少女交谈

蓝骑士骑行在少女身旁，看得出他处处留心照护她、敬重她；只是起初他寡言少语，她也有好一阵子不大想开口。可过了一小会儿，她斜眼觑他，觉得他倒像是很乐意她抛一句话给他。而她自己如今心情也已宽舒，只道自己已脱了囚困；再说无论如何，她信得过这人的忠厚与善良。于是她拣了一句关于路途的闲话问他；他听见她的声音，微微一怔，却回过头来坦然作答，那神气倒像是恨不得把这话说得更长久些才好。于是她说道：“好爵士，你还不曾告诉我，咱们这是要往哪里去呢。”

“是啊，”他说，“这话不假，是我疏忽了，求你恕我。咱们此去是溪畔堡，那是我头一份的庄园宅子，虽说算不得什么了不得的地方。可咱们今夜到不了那里，往后好几夜也到不了。你若问我在那儿能见到什么人，我便告诉你：除了家仆、几名甲士和军士、再有三名侍从之外，你差不多只能见到我的母亲，因为我至今尚未娶亲。”


===Original===

At that word the Maiden fell silent again, for she was wondering what like would be the Knight’s mother, and what days she was like to make for her. But presently she set all that aside, and fell to ask the Knight of other matters, such as the fashion of the countryside and the ways of the folk round about his castle, and freely he answered to everything; and so at last began to ask her concerning her land and folk, and her way of life, and she told him of all freely. But no word did she say to him of the man whom she loved; nay, when the talk seemed drawing near to such a point that it seemed he must be told of presently, she would break off and hold her peace straightway; neither did the Knight say aught, nor ask her wherefore she went not on with her tale, but let speech be till the spring thereof began to run again of its own will.

Thus then they wore the day, riding through a fair country of husbandry, not very thickly housed. None meddled with them, till at sunset they came to a goodly grange walled and moated; and the Blue Knight said: “If we take not harbour here we shall have to lie out in the field, for we shall fall in with no other house till the night is well deep.” Therewith he rode up to the door and lighted down, and so did they all; and there came forth a tall and somewhat goodly man of some fifty winters and bade Welcome, Sir Mark! And without more ado they entered the hall, which was fair and big and well-plenished. There presently they were feasted by the goodman and his sons and his folk, for Sir Mark the Blue Knight was well known to the said goodman. In due time withal the Maiden was shown to a fair chamber well hung and with a good bed therein, wherein she slept sweet without dreams. So was the ending of that day better than the beginning. They took to their road betimes on the morrow, and two of the goodman’s sons and three of his men rode with them, well armed; for though this was a peopled part, yet whiles reivers rode therein. But on the way the Blue Knight excused him to the Maiden for suffering this eking of his army, and he said: “Seest thou, lady, were I with my two lads here, or even were I riding birdalone, I would have bidden these five good fellows abide at home; but I fear for thee, lest the fewness of our company should draw on this rascaile to come within smiting distance, and then who knows what might betide? For a chance stroke might do all the scathe at once, and make me an unhappy man till the end of my days.”

She smiled on him friendly and said: “Sir Knight, there is no need to excuse thee; trust me I am nowise greedy of battle, and thank thee heartily for thinking of me.”

听了这话，少女又沉默下来，因为她心里在琢磨：骑士的母亲是怎样一个人，往后会给她什么样的日子过。但她随即把这一切撇开，转而问起骑士别的种种，比如乡野的风物、他城堡左近一带人们的习俗；他样样都答得爽快。到后来，他倒问起她的土地和亲人、她的度日之道来，她便也无甚隐瞒，尽数告诉了他。只是关于她所爱的那人，她对他一字不提；不，每当话头仿佛快要逼到那一步、眼看转眼就要说到那人时，她便立即打住，当下缄口；骑士也一言不发，也不问她为何不把话讲下去，只由着话头搁着，直到那话的泉源自己重新流淌起来。

他们就这样消磨过这一天，一路骑过一片美好的农耕之乡，只是人烟不算稠密。无人前来搅扰，直到日落时分，他们来到一座很好的庄寨前，有围墙环护、壕沟围绕；蓝骑士说：“咱们若不在此投宿，便只得露宿野地里了，因为夜色深透之前，再撞不上一处人家。”说着他便驰到门前，翻身下马，众人也都下了马；里面出来一个身材高大、颇为体面的男子，约摸五十个冬天，口称“欢迎，马克爵士！”众人不再多礼，径自进了厅堂；那厅堂宽敞漂亮，陈设齐备。当下庄主便同他的儿子们、他的仆从们设宴款待他们，因为蓝骑士马克爵士是这位庄主素来相识的。到了时辰，少女也被引到一间上好的内室，壁上挂饰齐整，屋里有一张舒坦的床，她在那床上睡得香甜，一夜无梦。这一天，结局倒比开头来得好。次日他们一早登程，庄主的两个儿子和三名仆从披挂齐整，随他们同行；因为这一带虽有人烟，却时而也有抄掠之徒骑行出没。路上，蓝骑士为容自己这支队伍添了几个人，向少女告罪，说道：“小姐你瞧，若只是我带着身边这两个小伙子，甚或是我独自单骑，我原会叫这五个好汉子守在家里；可我为你担着心，惟恐咱们人马单薄，引得那些流窜匪徒凑到兵刃够得着的地方来，那时谁知道会出什么事？因为一记乱下的打击，兴许一下子便把祸事做全了，叫我做个伤心人，直到我老死那一天。”

她对他友好地嫣然一笑，说道：“骑士先生，你大可不必告罪；信我，我绝非贪恋厮杀之人，倒要真心谢谢你为我着想。”


===Original===

The Knight made as if he would have said something which would not come forth out of his mouth, and he turned very red, and so rode, but presently drew rein, and bade the others ride on and he would catch up with them. So they went on, and the Maiden would have ridden on also, but he said: “I beseech thee to abide with me, for I have a word or two to say to thee before we get on with this day’s journey.” She looked on him wonderingly, and was somewhat abashed, but turned to hearken to him, and he said, not speaking very glibly: “Thou thankest me for thinking of thee, but meseems I have nowise thought of thee enough. I have told thee that we be riding to my house of Brookside, but now I will ask thee if thou hast will to go thither?”

“Why not?” she said; “I deem not by thy looks and thy speech that thou wilt be hard or cruel with me, or do me wrong in any wise, or suffer others to do so.”

“Nay, by Allhallows,” said he; “but this I ask. Tell me right out if thou hast any will to go back to thine old home in the Dale. I beseech thee to tell me thy mind hereon; and if thou longest to go back, then will we turn bridle at once and seek to the stead where thou wert born and bred, and there will I say farewell to thee. For what! it may not be forever; I shall ride to see thee once and again, I promise thee.”

===Chinese===

骑士做出一副要说什么的样子，那话却出不了口；他脸涨得通红，只得闷头骑马，可不久便勒住缰绳，嘱咐其他人先行，说他自会赶上去。于是众人往前去了，少女也要跟着前行，他却说：“我求你留在我身边，趁今日的旅程还未继续，我有一两句话要对你说。”她诧异地望着他，微微有些羞窘，却还是回身听他说。他说得不甚利索：“你谢我为你着想，可依我看，我想你想得还万万不够。我告诉过你，咱们这是往我溪畔的宅子去；如今我要问你一句：你可愿意到那里去？”

“为什么不愿意？”她说，“看你的容貌，听你的言语，我不觉得你会对我严苛狠戾，会丝毫亏待我，或是容许旁人来亏待我。”

“绝不会，凭诸圣起誓，”他说，“可我要问的是这一件：请你直截了当告诉我，你可有一丝念头，想回谷地里你的老家去。我恳求你把心思明白告诉我；你若渴想着回去，那咱们这就拨转马头，径直寻你生身长成的那处庄舍去，我就在那里同你道别。这算得了什么！又不是今生今世永不再见；我答应你，我会一次又一次骑马来看你。”


===Original===

Now the Maiden flushed red and the tears gathered in her eyes, and she looked piteous-kind on him; but she said: “Thou art kind indeed; but that farewell in the Dale needeth not to be, for I have no will to go back home. Such an errand is laid on me that hath made me homeless now; for I must go seeking that which is lost, it may be, wide over the world; and if thou wilt shelter me a while in Brookside Castle I shall thank thee and bless thee as scarce a man hath yet been thanked since earth was new.”

The Knight hung down his head, but presently he raised it, and heaved a sigh as if a weight were lifted from his heart, and he said: “Let each of us take what content may be in the passing days.” Then he shook his rein, and they both sped on together till they caught up with their company.

That night they harboured at a husbandman’s cot, where was no room save for the two women, and the men lay out under the bare heaven, but all was done that might be for the easement of the Maiden. The franklin’s folk rode on with them on the morrow, and whereas they must needs wend a somewhat thick wood the more part of the day, they rode close, and had the Maiden in their midst, while the Blue Knight went the foremost of their company, and was as wary as might be. So whatever strong-thieves might have been lurking under cover of the thicket, they adventured them not against so stout and well-ordered a company, and they all came safely through the wood into a fair grassy valley some little time before sunset. But though the pasture was good there and the land well watered, there were no houses within sight, for it was overnigh to the wood for folk to venture their goods, yea and their lives, by dwelling in neighbourhood to such ill men as haunted the thickets of the forest. Wherefore this night all the company, women as well as men, must needs forego lying under rafters: albeit they dight some kind of tent within what cloths they had for the Maiden and her fosterer.

这时少女脸上泛起红晕，眼泪涌上了眼眶，她凄然又温情地望着他；可她说：“你实在是好心；可谷地里那场道别是用不着的，因为我全然不想回家去。一桩差事落在我身上，如今已叫我没了家；因为我必须去寻找那失落的，兴许要寻遍天涯；你若肯收留我在溪畔堡将息些时日，我便会感激你、祝福你——自天地初开以来，怕还没有几个男子受过这样的感激和祝福呢。”

骑士垂下头去，可不久又抬了起来，长叹一声，仿佛心头卸下了一副重担，说道：“这流逝的日子里能得几分安稳，咱们便各自领受几分吧。”说罢他一抖缰绳，两人并辔疾驰，直到赶上他们的队伍。

这一夜他们宿在一户庄稼人的茅舍，那里除了两个女人再腾不出地方，男人们便露宿在光天之下；但凡能使少女安适的法子，都用尽了。次日，庄主家的人仍旧随他们同行；因为这一天大半路程都得穿行一片颇密的树林，他们便骑得紧，让少女居在队伍当中，蓝骑士则走在全队最前头，警觉得了不得。这样，任凭林莽掩护之下潜伏着什么样的剧盗，也不敢来招惹这般齐整雄壮的一队人马；日落前不多的时辰，他们全都安然穿过树林，进到一片美丽的青草谷地。只是那地方牧草虽好、水泽丰盈，放眼望去却无一户人家：因为它离树林太近，谁也不肯拿身家财物、乃至性命，去住得离那班出没林间的恶徒这般近。因此这一夜，全队人等，连女人带男人，都只得舍弃屋檐下的栖宿：不过他们还是拿手头所有的布幔，为少女和她的养母张设了一座简易的篷帐。

The fourth day, as they rode the grassy fair valley, as it was noon, they saw somewhat aloof the riding of another company, which they deemed to be more than they. So they looked to their weapons and rode on steadily, but without haste, lest the others might deem they were fleeing them. So the others, when they had well espied their demeanour, passed on without meddling with them; and well-nigh the whole valley could be ridden, so there was nought to drive them to meet side by side in a strait road, wherefore they came not very nigh, but yet nigh enough to know the newcomers for such as would be evil way-fellows to any whom they feared not. As it was, the Blue Knight and his drew rein and turned a little toward them as they went by, to show that they feared them not, and Sir Mark rode forward before his folk and abode them with a sword in fist. But the newcomers did nought but set up a yelling and jeering, and rode on their way not over slowly.

Three hours thereafter they saw, a little mile aloof, a fair white house garnished with towers on a knoll, round about which ran a little river; so the Maiden, who was now again riding close beside the Blue Knight, asked him if that were Brookside, and he smiled and said: “Nay, my house is still five days’ ride away, but this house, which hight Warding Knowe, is the house of a friend, and there shall we have good guesting, whereof I rejoice for thy sake.” Then he was silent a while, and said thereafter: “Tell me, lady, dost thou wish those five days over?”

“Nay,” she said, “it is little matter to me where I am, and to say sooth, this riding through the fair land likes me well.”

第四天，他们正骑行在那片青青美丽的谷地里，到了正午，望见稍远处另有一队人马在行进，看样子人数比他们还多。于是他们检视兵刃，从容前进，却不慌不忙，惟恐对方当他们是在逃走。对方把他们的这副气度瞧了个清楚，便自顾过去了，并不来搅扰；再说那谷地几乎处处可骑，没有什么逼得两队在窄路上并肩相撞，故此双方离得不很近，却又近得足以叫人认出来：这些新来的人，正是那种凡他们所不惧怕者，同他们打一路便决无好事的凶徒。当时的情形是：蓝骑士一行勒住缰绳，在对方经过时稍稍朝他们转了转，以示无所畏惧；马克爵士更纵马抢在自家人马之前，手握长剑，迎候他们。而那伙新来的人什么也没做，只发出一片吼叫嘲弄，不紧不慢地赶他们的路去了。

三个钟头之后，他们望见一英里开外，一座小冈上有一所漂亮的白房子，缀着几座角楼，一条小河绕着它流转；这时少女又骑到了蓝骑士近旁，问他那可是溪畔么，他微笑道：“不是，我的宅子离这里还有五天的骑程；不过这座名唤守望冈的宅子，是一位朋友的居所，咱们在那里会受到好好的款待——我为你欢喜。”随后他沉默了一阵，又说道：“告诉我，小姐，你可盼着这五天早些过完么？”

“不盼，”她说，“我在哪里都无甚要紧，说句实话，这样骑行在美好的国土上，我很是喜欢。”

He sighed and said, yet slowly: “Well, for my part I would that the five days were fifty.” “Why?” she said heedlessly. He reddened and said: “I must needs tell thee since thou askest me. It is because I have got used to seeing these men and thy Carline about thee; neither does it irk me to see the folk that give us guesting gazing on thee or speaking to thee. But when we come to Brookside it will be all other than that; for there will be the folk all about, and some belike will make friends with thee; and there will be my mother. And look you, all and each of these folk shall have as much part and lot in thee as I shall have. Now, art thou angry that I have said this?”

“Nay,” she said, and knew not what more to say. And she looked at him covertly and saw grief and torment in him, and she was sorry for him. But within herself she said, Woe’s me! and how long it shall be belike ere I meet my beloved!

他叹了口气，缓缓说道：“唉，至于我，倒宁愿这五天是五十天。”“为什么？”她随口问道。他红了脸，说道：“你既然问我，我便不能不告诉你。那是因为我已看惯了这些汉子和你那位老妪围在你身边；款待咱们的人家望着你、同你说话，我看在眼里也不难受。可等咱们到了溪畔，情形便全然两样了：那里四下里都是人，其中多半有些人会同你结好；还有我的母亲。你要知道，这些人个个都在你身上有一份，那一份额，都不下于我的一份。怎么样，我说了这话，你恼了么？”

“不恼，”她说，此外便不知再说什么好。她悄悄觑他，见他满心愁苦煎熬，心里为他难过。可她心中暗道：苦啊我！离我与我心爱之人相会，只怕还不知要多久呢！
