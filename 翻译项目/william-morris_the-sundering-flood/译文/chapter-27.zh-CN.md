## XXVII / 二十七

===Original===

They Parley from the Walls

Therewith he was gone and Osberne entered the town after his fellows, and the Baron of Deepdale was brought to Sir Medard in the great tower. There they would have served him with all honour, but he was not yet come out of that trance; so they laid him to rest in Sir Medard’s own bed, and had warders both within the chamber and without; and Osberne sat talking with Sir Medard in the said chamber till dawn was, when the Baron awoke really and fairly, and called for drink. And Sir Medard brought it unto him with his own hand, and the Baron stared at him and said: “Art thou of the service tonight? I know thee not.” Quoth Sir Medard: “And yet we have been near enough together ere this, Lord Baron; thou shouldst know me, meseems.” The Baron looked hard on him and then round about the chamber, and cried out: “Holy Mary! ’tis Medard the carle-leader. Where am I, and where is the evil beast of a minstrel? Hath he beguiled me?” Said Medard: “Lord, at this present thou art in a chamber of my poor house in Eastcheaping. Doubtless tomorrow, after we have had some talk together, thou and I and the Porte, thou mayst go back home to Deepdale, or abide here to see how we can feast, we carle-warriors, and to be holden in all honour.”

Now came forth Stephen the Eater and said: “Lord, lo here the evil beast of a minstrel who hath verily beguiled thee; but, Baron, it is to thy gain and not thy loss. For tomorrow shall the war be ended, and thou shalt be free to go back again to the fair women of Deepdale whom thou lovest so much, and shalt save thy men-at-arms, and thy weapons and tents and timber, and victuals and drink a great heap; and all this I deem, and more maybe, wouldst thou have lost hadst thou gone on sitting perversely before Eastcheaping all for nought. So I will not say pardon me, but make friends with me rather for being good to me.” And therewith he reached out his great hand to the Baron; but Osberne drew him back by the girdle, and chid him for mocking a captive, while the Baron turned his face to the wall and covered up his head with the bedclothes.

But ye may judge if there were riding and running in the leaguer next morning when they could find the Baron nowhere; and one said this and the other that; and he cried Kill and slay, and he cried Flee ere we all come to like end; and great was the doubt and the turmoil. Amidst of which comes Sir Medard on to the battlement of the northwest tower, and beside him a squire bearing a white banner, and a herald with a trumpet, which herald presently blew a loud blast, but such an one as sounded not of war but of parley. So when the captains and leaders heard the said blast and saw the white banner of peace, they deemed that new tidings were toward, and a half score of them crossed over their dyke bearing a white banner with them, and came close under the tower whereon stood Sir Medard; and the chiefest of them, an old hoar man and very wise, hight Sir Degore, stood before the others all unhelmed and said: “Is it Sir Medard that standeth up there?”

“Yea verily,” said the Knight; “and what art thou? Art thou a leader of the host that sitteth about us?” Said the other: “I am Sir Degore, of whom thou wilt have heard; under my lord the Baron of Deepdale I am the leader of this host, and I have come to ask what thou wouldst of us.” Said Sir Medard: “I would see the Baron of Deepdale.”

城头会谈

说罢他便去了；奥斯伯恩随着同伴们进了城，深谷男爵则被带到大塔里梅达德爵士面前。众人本要按全副礼数侍奉他，他却尚未从那昏迷中醒转；于是众人便让他躺上梅达德爵士自己的床，房里房外都派了看守；奥斯伯恩就在那间屋里陪着梅达德爵士说话，直到天亮时分，男爵才算真真切切地醒了过来，开口讨酒水。梅达德爵士亲手端给他，男爵瞪眼打量着他，说道：“今晚是你当值么？我不认得你。”梅达德爵士道：“可是，男爵爵爷，咱们先前彼此离得够近的了；照我想，你该认得我才是。”男爵死死盯住他，又环顾屋内四壁，失声叫道：“圣母玛利亚！这不是汉子头子梅达德么。我这是在哪儿？那恶兽乐师又上哪儿去了？他可是诓了我？”梅达德道：“爵爷，眼下你是在东集我寒舍的一间屋子里。明日你我同市府好生谈过一番之后，你无疑便可以回深谷家里去，或者留在此地，瞧瞧我们这些汉子战士怎样摆宴欢庆，并受全般的礼敬。”

这时饕客斯蒂芬走上前来，说道：“爵爷，瞧啊，那恶兽乐师正在这儿，他当真诓了你；只是，男爵，这于你有利，而非有损。因为明日战事便告终结，你便可以脱身回去，回到你那般疼爱的深谷美人儿们身边，又保全了你的兵士、你的兵器帐篷木料，还有好大一堆的粮秣酒水；这些，依我看，兴许还不止这些——倘若你执意顽抗、白白在东集城外干耗下去，可就都要折损了。所以我不说‘请你饶我’，倒要说：我待你这么好，你该同我交个朋友才是。”说着他向男爵伸出那只大手；奥斯伯恩却一把扯住他的腰带拉了回来，责备他不该嘲弄被俘之人；男爵则把脸转向墙壁，拿被褥把头蒙了起来。

可想而知，第二天早晨营里四下寻不着男爵时，那里头是何等的驰逐奔忙；这人这么说，那人那么说；有人喊“杀呀，斩呀”，有人喊“快逃罢，免得咱们都落得一样的下场”；一时疑惧纷纭，乱作一团。正乱着，梅达德爵士登上西北塔的城堞，身旁一名扈从执着白旗，一名传令官带着号角；那传令官当即吹响一声高亢的长号，却不是杀伐之音，而是邀谈之调。众统领头目听得这号声，又望见那面和平的白旗，料想必有新的消息，便有十名统领越过壕沟，也带着一面白旗，走到梅达德爵士所立的塔楼底下；为首一人是个须发如霜、十分练达的老者，名唤狄戈尔爵士，他不戴头盔立在众人之前，说道：“上头站着的可是梅达德爵士？”

“正是，”骑士答道；“你又是何人？是围在我们四周这支大军的统领么？”那边答道：“我是狄戈尔爵士，你想必也听人说起过；我在我家主公深谷男爵麾下统领这支大军，特来请问你要我们怎样。”梅达德爵士道：“我要见深谷男爵。”

“He is sick this morning,” said Sir Degore, “and may not rise; but if thou wouldst render the town and the castle unto him, it is all one, thou mayst make me serve thy turn; I know his mind full well.”

Sir Medard laughed: “Nay,” said he, “we will wait for that till we may see the Baron himself. But tell me, Sir Knight, what is all this stir and hubbub in thine host this morning?”

Said Sir Degore, without tarrying the word one moment: “There is a great aid and refreshment come to us out of the East country, both of victual and men, and our folk be welcoming the men and sharing the victual.”

“There is nothing in this, then, that we have heard, that ye cannot find your Duke, and are seeking him up and down?”

“Nay, nothing,” said the greybeard, wagging his head. But the folk that were with him looked on each other and thought within themselves how wise the old man was. And Sir Medard spake when he might for his laughter: “Sir, thy lord did well to make thee captain under him, for thou art a wise and ready liar. But so it is that thou speakest with one who knoweth the tale better than thou. Ho ye, bring forward my lord.”

“他今晨身上不好，”狄戈尔爵士说，“起不得身；不过你若肯把城池堡垒献给他，那全是一样，尽可以拿我来当他的替身；他的心思我了然得很。”

梅达德爵士笑了：“不，”他说，“这事且等着，等我们见得着男爵本人再说。只是请告诉我，骑士先生，今晨你军中这般喧腾骚扰，却是为何？”

狄戈尔爵士答话，半刻也不迟疑：“有一大队接济自东方国度来到我们这里，人马粮草都有，我们的弟兄正欢迎来人、分派粮草呢。”

“那么，我们听说的那桩事——你们寻不着你们的公爵，正四处上下找他——也是没有的事么？”

“没有的事，全没有的事，”白胡子老儿摇着头说。可他身后的人们面面相觑，心里暗暗赞叹这老人何等练达。梅达德爵士好容易忍住笑，才开口道：“先生，你家主公派你做他麾下的统领，真是派对了人，因为你是个出口成章、应付裕如的说谎老手。只是如今同你说话的这个人，对这段故事知道得比你还清楚。喂，来人，把我家爵爷带上来。”

Straightway came two squires, who led a lean dark man between them, unarmed and clad in a long furred black gown. He took off his hat, and thereupon Sir Degore and all they below knew him for their lord. He spake at once and said: “Sir Degore and ye others, my lords and captains, can ye hear me?”

“Yea, lord,” said Sir Degore.

Then said the Baron: “This then is my word and commandment, that ye give leave to all our folk-in-arms to depart each one to his own house, and to bear away with him his weapons and armour and three horses if he be of the knighthood, and one if he be of the sergeantry; but the others, archers and villeins, may take one horse between three to bear their baggage and ease them on the journey. But the flour and wheat and wine, and all the neat and sheep, ye shall leave behind; for the folk of this countryside and the good town have occasion for them. But as to mine own matters which are of mine own person, as arms and raiment and jewels and the like, ye shall bring them unto me here in the good town, where I am minded to abide two or three days that I may hold counsel about weighty matters with the Porte and the Burgreve. Moreover, I would have thee, Sir Degore, and a five of my counsellors and a half score of my servants, come hither to me to abide with me for my aid and service while I tarry in Eastcheaping. Now this is my will and pleasure, and I shall be no wiser later on; wherefore do thou, Sir Degore, go straightway and tell my will to the captains and sergeants and the knights, so that the hosts may presently break up.”

Ye may deem how Sir Degore and the other Deepdalers were abashed when they knew that their lord was a captive in the hands of the foemen; yet they seemed to think that the terms of the good town were not so hard as might have been looked for, since they had gotten this so great advantage.

Now Sir Degore spake and said: “Sir Medard, wilt thou suffer me to come to thee, so that I may speak with my lord privily?” “To what end,” said Sir Medard, “since thou hast heard thy lord’s commandment? wilt thou not obey him?” “Yea,” said Degore, “if I have heard his last word; nevertheless were I fain to come up and speak with him.” “Come up then,” said Sir Medard; “yet I must warn thee that it may be easier for thee to come in to Eastcheaping today than to go out therefrom. Moreover, bethink ye if ye dally how it would be were we to open our gates and fall upon you with all ours, and ye disarrayed and leaderless.”

立时上来两名扈从，中间押着一名清瘦黧黑的汉子，未带兵器，身上穿一袭裘皮的黑色长袍。他摘下帽子，狄戈尔爵士和下面众人当即认出正是自家主公。他随即开口说道：“狄戈尔爵士，还有你们诸位、列位大人与统领们，听得见我么？”

“听得见，主公，”狄戈尔爵士道。

于是男爵说道：“这便是我的谕令：着即准我们全军武装之人各自散归本家，随身带走各自的兵器甲胄；骑士身份的每人带走三匹马，军士身份的每人一匹；其余的弓手与庄户，每三人合领一匹马，好驮运行李、纾解路途劳顿。至于面粉、麦子、酒，连同所有的牛羊，都须留下；因为此间乡民和这座良城都用得着它们。至于纯属我个人之身的私物，如兵器衣物珠宝之类，你们须给我送到这良城里来；我打算在此盘桓两三天，好与市府和城守会商几件要务。此外，我要你狄戈尔爵士，连同五名谋臣、十名仆从，上我这里来，在我滞留东集期间随侍左右，供我驱使。这便是我的意旨与心愿，日后决无更改；所以，狄戈尔爵士，你即刻去把我的意旨传告众统领、军士和骑士们，好教各路人马即刻解散。”

可以想见，狄戈尔爵士和其他深谷人得知自家主公已落在敌人手中时，是何等的愕然失色；不过他们似乎也觉得，既已让对方占了这么大的便宜，这座良城开出的条款倒不像原先料想的那般苛刻。

于是狄戈尔爵士开口道：“梅达德爵士，你可容我上你那儿去，让我与我家主公私下说几句话？”“何必呢，”梅达德爵士道，“你既已听得你家主公的谕令，还不肯遵从么？”“遵从自然遵从，”狄戈尔说，“只要这确是他最后的话；然而我还是很想上去同他谈一谈。”“那就上来罢，”梅达德爵士说；“只是我须提醒你：今日进东集怕是要比日后再出去容易些。再者，你们若拖延迁延，也想想看：我们若大开城门，倾尽城中人马扑将出来，而你们阵脚散乱、无人统领，那会是何等光景。”

Therewith he gave word to open the postern to Sir Degore, who entered and was brought to the top of the tower, and there he went up to the Baron and bent the knee to him and might not refrain his tears; but the Baron laughed, yet somewhat hardly. So they two went aside into an ingle of the tower toward the town, while sir Medard and his stood aloof a while. Then turned back Sir Degore to them of Eastcheaping, and said: “Sir Medard, I pray thee leave to depart to my host, that I may do after the bidding of my lord.”

“Yea, go,” said Sir Medard; “yet I would have thee remember that I pray for a long life for the Baron of Deepdale, since he hath become so good a friend to our town, and that thou wilt be in the wrong if thou do aught to shorten it.”

So Degore went his ways, and he and those counsellors and leaders went back sadly to the leaguer, and fell to work to undo all they had done the six months past. And it was no long time ere the stout men-at-arms of Deepdale began to flow away from before Eastcheaping, and the men of the town held good watch all the while; and ere it was evening, divers bands of them went out-of-gates in good order to see that none of the Deepdalers abode in array in the leaguer, and found nothing there which they had cause to dread. And they took much spoil of that which the Baron’s host must needs leave behind. Meanwhile, Sir Medard and his made what cheer they might to the Baron; and Sir Medard showed Osberne unto him, and told him all the tale of the wolves and the slaying of Hardcastle, and did him to wit that much of the valiancy which they of Eastcheaping had shown in the war came of this lad of Wethermel. And the Baron marvelled, and looked upon Osberne and said: “Well, lad, if ever thou art hard bestead, come thou to Deepdale, and we shall find somewhat for thee to do; and I bid thee thrive hale and well!”

Howbeit Sir Medard told not to the Baron that Osberne had been one of them that bore him off the last night. Yet somehow he came to know it in time to come; I wot not through whom or how.

说罢他吩咐开边门放狄戈尔爵士进来；狄戈尔进了城，被带上塔顶，走到男爵面前屈膝下跪，忍不住落下泪来；男爵倒笑了，只是笑得颇有些勉强。于是他们二人踱到塔上朝城那面一处炉角的僻静所在，梅达德爵士和他的人则退开站了一会儿。随后狄戈尔爵士转身回到东集众人跟前，说道：“梅达德爵士，请你准我回营中去，好让我依我家主公的吩咐行事。”

“去罢，”梅达德爵士说；“只是望你记住：我祈愿深谷男爵福寿绵长，因为他已成了我们城的大大的好友；你若做什么折损他寿数的事，那便是你的不是了。”

于是狄戈尔自去了；他和那些谋臣头领黯然回到营中，动手把过去六个月里经营起来的一应工事尽数拆撤。没过多久，深谷那些剽悍的甲士便开始从东集城外陆陆续续地流退而去；城中人自始至终严加守望；天未黑，城中几队人马便出得城去，队列严整，查看再没有深谷人留在营中列阵的，却见那里已无一物足以令他们忌惮。男爵大军不得不留下的东西，他们掳得了许多。这其间，梅达德爵士和他的人尽力承欢，让男爵开怀；梅达德又把奥斯伯恩引见给他，把群狼的事和诛杀哈德卡斯尔的始末一一讲给他听，并使他知道，东集人在这次战事中表现的许多英勇，都是出自韦瑟梅尔的这个少年。男爵惊叹不已，看着奥斯伯恩说道：“好孩子，往后你若遇了窘难，只管到深谷来，我们总有事情给你做；我祝你康健兴旺！”

不过梅达德爵士并没有告诉男爵，昨夜把他劫走的人里头就有奥斯伯恩。然而日后不知怎的，他终究得知了此事；经由何人、如何得知，我便不得而知了。
