## XXXV / 三十五

===Original===

Osberne Seeks Counsel of Steelhead

Wear the days hereafter into summer, and Osberne is at Wethermel, and doth what work cometh to hand no worser than heretofore; yet folk marvel that his sorrow over the man-fall of the Cloven battle seemeth to wear off him but little, though he is mild and kind in speech to all men. Much he sat talking with Stephen the Eater, who in these days was growing whole of his hurts, and it is thought he learned some hidden lore from him, for many deemed that Stephen was wise therein. Every third day he went all alone to the Bight of the Cloven Knoll, and sat there long through the day; but never had he any tidings of Elfhild, nor forsooth did he look for any such. He learned from over the water that there was no newcomer at Hartshaw Knolls, and that the house and garth lay waste, and so was like to abide.

Now when it lacked but three nights of Midsummer, Osberne, after he had spoken long with Stephen, set some victual in his scrip, and went afoot in the evening-tide up the bent and over it among the mountain-necks, and so into that same little dale where he had first met Steelhead. There he sat him down on the grass by the brookside and ate his meat, and then, when it darkened so much as it would that June night, he laid him down and slept in all trust of safekeeping. He awoke at the end of dawn and washed him in the brook, and then clad him and sat down to abide sunrise. Then even as the sun arose it smote a beam of light from some bright thing overtopping the crown of the hillside before him, and Osberne knew that there was come his friend Steelhead, in such guise as he had first beheld him there: which was in sooth the very thing which he desired.

So Osberne stood up to greet him and Steelhead came to him and put his arms about him and kissed and embraced him, and Osberne wept for pity and hope of his life. Then said Steelhead: “I know why thou art come to me; a while agone I laid my hands upon thee that I might make thy body stark for all adventure, and now thou wouldst have me do the like for the soul of thee. Herein will I do what I may, but first we will eat of the increase of Wethermel, that thou mayest see how much I love thee and the land that bred thee.”

So Osberne bestirred him, and kindled the cooking-fire and made ready the meat, and they ate together in all content and friendliness. But when they were full Steelhead spake: “Now whether wouldst thou tell me all thy tale, or whether wouldst thou be silent thereof, knowing that I know it without words spoken?”

奥斯伯恩向钢首问计

此后时日渐渐磨入夏季，奥斯伯恩守在韦瑟梅尔，手头有什么活计便做什么，做得不比往日差；只是众人暗暗称奇：裂丘一战折损了那许多人，他心头的哀痛却似久久不肯褪去，尽管他对人人言辞都温厚和善。他常陪着饕客斯蒂芬闲坐长谈——斯蒂芬这些日子伤势渐愈——据人揣想，他从斯蒂芬那里学得了些隐秘的学问，因为不少人都认定斯蒂芬于此道颇有智慧。每隔三日，他便独自一人去裂丘湾，在那里一坐便是大半日；却始终得不到埃尔弗希尔德的半点音信，说实在的，他也本不指望会有。他从对岸打听得来：哈特肖丘并无新来的人，那座宅院连同场圃一片荒废，看来还要这般荒下去。

且说离仲夏只差三夜，奥斯伯恩与斯蒂芬长谈了一番之后，往挎囊里装了些吃食，趁傍晚徒步登上草坡，翻过坡顶，穿行于山颈之间，一路走进他初次遇见钢首的那座小谷。他在溪畔草地上坐下用饭，待到那六月之夜黑到它所能黑的程度，便躺下安睡，一心信托自己必得护佑。破晓时分他醒来，在溪中洗浴了，随即穿衣束带，坐待日出。恰在旭日升起之际，日光打在一件明晃晃的物事上，从前方山坡顶高处投下一道光芒，奥斯伯恩便知他的朋友钢首到了，来的正是他当初在此见到的那副形貌：这实在正合他的心愿。

于是奥斯伯恩起身相迎，钢首走到他跟前，伸出双臂将他搂住，又吻他又拥抱他；奥斯伯恩落下泪来，一半出于怜惜，一半出于生平的指望。钢首便道：“我知道你为何来找我。前些时候我把双手按在你身上，为的是教你筋骨强健，足以担当一切艰险；如今你要我也照样医你的心灵。这事我必尽力而为，只是先咱们要吃一顿韦瑟梅尔的出产，好教你看出我多么爱你，也爱这养育你的土地。”

于是奥斯伯恩忙活起来，生起炊火，整治肉食，两人一同进餐，尽欢尽睦。待到吃饱，钢首开口道：“如今你是想把你的事原原本本讲给我听呢，还是自知我不待言语早已了然，就此缄默不提？”

Quoth Osberne: “I would tell it.”

“There is yet time,” said Steelhead, smiling kindly on him, “so make no tarrying.”

Then Osberne began straightway, and spared not words overmuch, but herein he used the most when he told of Elfhild, what she was like in those latter days, and how his heart enfolded her, and how sweet was her converse with him; and when he was done Steelhead said: “What is in thy mind concerning dwelling in the Dale amidst thine own folk?” Said Osberne: “My mind it is to live and die here, and do all that is due to the folk of my fathers.” Said Steelhead: “Then thou must be healed of this trouble; that is, thou must forget thy love and thy longing, or at the least thou must think more of other matters than of this. For I will not have it that thou my fosterling shouldst be a killjoy among men of the kindred; wherefore ill-luck will come of it.”

Said Osberne, knitting his brows: “I will not be healed in this way. For do I not know that she also is wrapped in sorrow and tormented by longing. Shall I leave her, therefore, as the dastard leaves a wounded friend before the oncoming foeman?”

Steelhead smiled on him. Quoth he: “Thou wilt not be healed? So be it; then mayst thou not abide in the Dale amongst the kindred, but carry thy trouble to the lands of the aliens, where there is none to remember the joyous face of thee before the trouble was.”

奥斯伯恩道：“我愿意讲。”

“时候还来得及，”钢首朝他慈祥地微笑道，“那便不要再耽搁。”

于是奥斯伯恩当即讲了起来，辞句并不冗蔓，唯独讲到埃尔弗希尔德时用情最深——讲她后来那些日子是何等模样，讲他的心如何把她珍藏在怀，讲她与他对谈何等甜美。他讲完了，钢首便道：“住在谷地、置身亲族之间，你心里作何打算？”奥斯伯恩道：“我的心愿是生于斯、死于斯，把对先祖宗族应尽的本分一一尽到。”钢首道：“那么你这桩苦情必须得医；也就是说，你必须忘掉你的爱与渴念，或者至少，你要多惦念旁的事，少惦念这一桩。因为我断不容我抚育的孩子在族人当中做个败人清兴的人，那样只会招来厄运。”

奥斯伯恩皱眉道：“我不肯这样被医好。难道我不知道她也被愁苦包裹，受着渴念的煎熬么？难道我便因此丢下她，像那懦夫在强敌压境之际丢下受伤的伙伴一样么？”

钢首对他莞尔一笑，说道：“你不肯被医好？也罢；那你便不得留在谷地亲族中间，只得把你的苦楚带到异邦他乡去，那里没有人记得你受苦之前那张欢喜的脸。”

“This may I do,” said Osberne, “and even so it shall be since it is thy will. But hast nought else to say to enhearten me in my travel?”

“This I have thereto,” quoth Steelhead, “that though the world be wide there are many ways about it, and meseemeth that there is somewhere a way whereon thy feet and Elfhild’s may draw toward one another.” Said Osberne: “May all good hap go with thee for thy word. Dost thou not see how my face is already gladdened thereby?”

Said Steelhead: “This is hope, my son, that flareth up swiftly and fadeth soon; but now this I shall give to thee, as I deem I may, that never shalt thou lack hope so long as thou hast deeds to do. Call to mind what thou thyself saidst unto Elfhild, that the only way to bridge the Sundering Flood is for one of you, or both, to wander wide in the world. But now tell me, what hast thou in thy mind to do in these days that pass?” Said Osberne: “I have been thinking of it, that when the Midsummer Feast is over I shall say farewell to my folk and to ride to Eastcheaping to find Sir Medard; for meseems he is the man whom I know out in the world who will put me in the way of deeds.” Said Steelhead: “And wilt thou go alone, or hast thou a mind to take any with thee? Suppose it were Stephen the Eater, who is a man of lore, and as I do thee to wit moreover, a friend of our own?” “Dost thou command me to have him with me, lord?” said Osberne. “Nay,” said Steelhead, “I but ask thee of thy mind in the matter.”

Said Osberne: “Then I shall tell thee that my mind is to go all birdalone. I would take no part of Wethermel with me, lest I soften towards the Dale, and turn back some fair day of summer and fall to nursing my sorrow therein. Moreover I know of Stephen that he is both a wise man and a champion, and I deem it were well to leave such an one to uphold the good days of Wethermel; so that whether I do that which I would, and come back in joy and honour; or do it not, and die away from my place, not without honour it may be, I shall yet know of the thriving of my kindred and the pleasures of Wethermel, which shall yet be glorious on the earth, even as it were a very living creature and mine own true friend. Many a time shall I think of it, in good hap and in ill hap, in grief and in joy.”

“Hail to thy word, son and stout-heart!” said Steelhead, “for herein thou thinkest of it as my very heart would that thou shouldst. Now I see that I have indeed sown the seed of hope in thee, and I call it the lack of fear.”

“这个我做得到，”奥斯伯恩说，“既然是你的意旨，事情便这样定了。但除了这些，你可还有别的话，好教我旅途上提起胆气？”

“这我倒是有一句，”钢首道，“世界虽广，环世界而行的路却有好多条；依我看来，天下总有一条路，教你的脚和埃尔弗希尔德的脚得以渐渐走近。”奥斯伯恩道：“愿你这句话得百般好运相随。你难道看不出，我这张脸已经因此欢喜起来了么？”

钢首道：“我儿，这便是希望了——它燃起得快，消褪得也快；不过如今有一样东西我却可以给你，我自觉给得起：只要你还有事功可为，你便永远不会缺少希望。你记不记得你自己对埃尔弗希尔德说过的话——要在分离洪流上架起桥来，唯一的法子便是你们两人中的一个，或者两个一起，去世界上远远地闯荡一番。但现在你告诉我，眼下这些流逝的日子里，你打算做些什么？”奥斯伯恩道：“我正想着这事：等仲夏节的宴会一过，我便向家人辞行，骑马去东集投梅达德爵士；因为依我看来，我在世上认识的人里头，惟有他能引我上建立事功的路。”钢首道：“那你打算独自去呢，还是有意带个什么人同往？譬如带上饕客斯蒂芬如何？他是个有学问的人，而且——这我也让你知道——还是咱们自家的朋友。”“爵爷，你是吩咐我带他同去么？”奥斯伯恩说。“不是，”钢首道，“我不过问问你在这事上的心意。”

奥斯伯恩道：“那么我告诉你：我的心意是孤身只影上路。我不愿从韦瑟梅尔带走一物一事，只怕自己心肠一软，恋起谷地来，将来某个晴美的夏日打马回头，跌回那片土地上，只顾抚育自己的哀伤。再者，我深知斯蒂芬既有学问，又是一员健将，我以为留下这样一个人撑持韦瑟梅尔的好日子，乃是上策。这样，无论我如愿以偿，欢欢喜喜、载誉而归；还是功业未成，死在远离家园之地——纵然如此，或许也不失体面——我总还能得到音信，知道我宗族的兴旺、韦瑟梅尔的安乐。这庄园日后必在世间焕发光彩，宛如一个活生生的灵物、我真正的挚友。好运厄运、悲伤欢喜之中，我都要常常记起它。”

“向你这句话致敬，我的儿，好一条硬心汉！”钢首说，“因为在这事上，你想得正合我心底所愿你如此。如今我算看明白了，我确实已在你心里播下了希望的种子——我管它叫做无所畏惧。”

And now he brought the talk on other matters, and was as kind and friendly as might be, and Osberne deemed it was a great thing for him that he had so won the love of this noble wight and great-heart. So in all pleasure the day sped, and when it was hard on sunset Steelhead spake: “Now must I get me back to my house and home of old time, and thou shalt go home to Wethermel the dear; and now I see of thee that thou shalt hold a cheerful countenance there, and depart when needs must in honour and well-liking of all men.”

So they stood up, but ere Osberne turned his face to the west he said: “And when shall I see thee again, lord?”

“Who knows?” said Steelhead; “maybe when thou lookest least for me: on the lonely marsh maybe, or in the thick of the forest; or in the midst of the fierce battle, or on thy very deathbed; or it may not be at all in thine earthly life.”

“And that house whereto thou art now going, shall I ever see thee there?” said Osberne.

“Surely I deem that thou shalt; and yet most surely not till thine earthly days are over. But now farewell, and my heart goes with thee.” Therewith he turned and was gone, and Osberne went his ways to Wethermel without looking after him. And now it seemed to him as if he had been fain not to have gone back to that well-beloved stead, but to have gone on east at all adventure; and he looked toward the day when he must depart at last as a sad and sore time, when hope would be dimmed by mere sorrow and trouble.

随后他把话头引到别的事情上去，和蔼亲密，无可挑剔；奥斯伯恩自忖，自己竟能赢得这位高贵人物、这条伟烈心肠如此的眷爱，实是一桩莫大的幸事。这一天便在满襟欢愉中飞驰而过；眼看快到日落，钢首开口道：“如今我得回我古时的家园居所去了，你也该回你亲爱的韦瑟梅尔去。我如今看你，你必能在那里持一张欢颜，待到不得不走时，也必体体面面、为众人所爱重地辞别而去。”

于是两人站起身来；奥斯伯恩还未掉脸向西，先说道：“爵爷，我何时才能再见你呢？”

“谁知道呢？”钢首道，“多半在你最想不到我的时候：也许在寂寂的泽地，也许在密林深处；也许在鏖战的正中间，也许就在你的临终榻前；也或许，在你尘世的这一生里，压根儿就见不着。”

“还有你如今要回去的那座居所，我往后能到那里见到你么？”奥斯伯恩问。

“我深信你必有见到的一天；然而同样确然的是，那必得等你尘世的岁月终了之后。但如今别过了，我的心随你同去。”说罢他转身便去了；奥斯伯恩自回韦瑟梅尔，并不回头望他。这时他心中只觉，仿佛自己本不情愿回到那深爱的地方，倒宁愿径直向东、放胆闯荡去；他望向那终须动身的末日，只觉那将是一段愁惨酸辛的时辰，到那时，希望会被纯然的哀伤与烦难所遮暗。
