## LIII / 五十三

===Original===

Strangers Come to Wethermel
Now turns the tale to Wethermel, and tells how that on the morrow of Midsummer, five years to the day since Osberne had bidden them farewell, the folk once more sat without-doors about the porch in the cool of the evening; neither was there any missing of the settled folk of those to whom he had said farewell. For all had thriven there that while. There sat the goodman, more chieftain-like than of old; there sat the goodwife, as kind as ever, and scarce could she be kinder; there sat Bridget, not much aged in the five years; forever she deemed it a certain thing that her nursling would come back to her. Lastly, there sat Stephen the Eater, wise of aspect and thoughtful, as if he were awaiting something that should happen which should change much in him; and there were the carles and the queans (with some few children amongst them who had not been there five years ago) who had been familiar to Osberne ere he left the Dale for warfare. It was growing late now, and the twilight was creeping up under a cloudless sky, when those folk saw newcomers wending the lane betwixt the outbowers, and making straight for the house-porch. They were but three, and as they drew nigh it could be seen that they were hooded and cloaked despite the warm night; and one was tall and seemed a stalwarth man, and another was jimp and went daintily, as if it were a young woman, and the third, who forsooth had her face but little hidden, seemed a carline of some three score years and ten.
None of the folk stirred save Stephen the Eater, who rose up as if to welcome the guests; and the tall man spake in a strange high voice that seemed as if it came from the back of his head: “May we three wayfarers be here tonight? For we saw this stead from afar, and it seemed a plenteous house, and we deem it guest-kind.” Quoth Stephen: “A free and fair welcome to you; ye shall eat of our dish, and drink of our cup, and lie as the best of us do. Ho, ye folk! now were we best within doors; for our guests shall be both weary and hungry belike.”
So into the hall they wended, and the three were shown to a good place amidmost thereof, so that all might see them; and there they sat, the tall man innnermost, nighest to the dais, the young woman by him and the carline outermost. Then came in the meat, which was both plenteous and good, and when all were fulfilled the drink was brought in, and the tall man arose and called a health on Wethermel, and that it might thrive ever. But some men thought that, as he lifted his hand to put the cup to his lips, a gleam of something bright came from under his wayfarer’s cloak. And Stephen the Eater called a health on the wayfarers; and then one drank to one thing, one to another, and men waxed merry and gleeful.

生客来到韦瑟梅尔
如今话头转回韦瑟梅尔。且说仲夏次日——奥斯伯恩向众人道别，恰恰五年，一日不差——一家老小又趁傍晚的凉气，坐到门廊四周的露天里；当年他道别时安住庄中的那些人，如今一个也不曾缺少。这些年里，阖家都过得兴旺。庄主坐在那里，比往昔更有一派首领气象；女当家坐在那里，和善一如既往，再和善也难以更甚了；布里奇特坐在那里，五年之间不见怎么见老；她始终笃信，她奶大的孩子必定会回到她身边。最后坐着饕客斯蒂芬，神色明睿而若有所思，仿佛正等待一件必将发生、且要令他改变许多的事；还有那些汉子们和妇人们（其中还夹着几个五年前尚不在人世的孩子），都是奥斯伯恩离开谷地从军之前厮熟的人。这时天色渐晚，无云的天空下暮色悄悄爬升，众人忽见有新客沿着外舍之间的小径走来，径直奔向这家的门廊。来者只有三人：近前看时，虽是暖夜，却个个兜帽罩头、斗篷裹身；一个是高个子，像是个魁伟的男人；一个身量纤秀，脚下轻巧，仿佛是个年轻女子；第三个其实脸面几乎不曾遮掩，看去是个年约七旬的老婆子。
众人中只有饕客斯蒂芬起身，像是要迎客；那高个子开口了，嗓音又高又怪，仿佛是从他后脑勺里发出来的：“我们三个旅人，今夜可在贵府借宿么？我们打远处望见这处地方，看去是个丰足之家，想必待客殷勤。”斯蒂芬答道：“敞怀奉迎，欢迎之至；你们将吃我们盘中的食，喝我们杯中的酒，睡处同我们中间最体面的人一样。喂，众人！我们还是进屋去的好；客人多半又乏又饿了。”
于是众人进了大厅，那三位被引到堂中一处好座上，人人都看得见他们；三人落座：高个子坐在最里首，紧挨着上首的高台，年轻女子挨着他，老婆子坐最外。随后饭食端上来，既丰盛又精美；大家用饱之后，酒又送了上来。高个子起身，举杯祝韦瑟梅尔，愿它长盛不衰。却有人瞧见，他抬手举杯就唇之际，旅人斗篷底下闪出一道亮光。饕客斯蒂芬也举杯祝旅人们；接着你为此事祝酒，我为那事举杯，众人渐渐欢畅快活起来。

But at last rose up Stephen the Eater and spake: “Meat and drink and lodging is free without price to every comer to Wethermel, and most oft, as here it is, our good will goes with it; yet meseemeth that since these friends of ours come belike from the outlands and countries where is more tidings than mostly befalleth here, it might please them to make us their debtors by saying us some lay, or telling us some tale; for we be not bustled to drink the voidee cup now, these nights of Midsummer, when night and day hold each other’s hands throughout the twenty-four hours.”
Then rose up the tall, high-voiced man and said: “It is my will that each one of we three should say something, be it long or short, to make the folk of Wethermel glad. For they have treated us wayfarers as though we were lords and kings, and their words go to their hearts. Now I will that thou, mother, begin, and that I make an end of this saying.”
Then he sat down, and the carline said: “I am all the more willing to this, as meseemeth I can tell you a tale such as ye have never heard the like of, and which will move every heart of you. And yet I must pray your patience, as belike it may be somewhat long for a tale of one night’s hall-glee: and on this night must the tale be begun and ended. Hearken then!”

末了，饕客斯蒂芬起身说道：“来到韦瑟梅尔的客人，饭食、酒水与宿处一概分文不取，而且十有八九——今晚正是如此——还奉上我们的一片好意；只是依我看来，这几位朋友既多半来自外邦异域，那里的音信比我们这僻乡惯常听到的多，那么若蒙他们为我们唱一支歌谣、讲一个故事，教我们欠下他们一份情，想必他们也是乐意的；何况眼下是仲夏时节的夜晚，昼和夜在整整二十四个钟头里手拉着手，我们还不用急着喝那饯别的杯酒。”
于是那高嗓门的高个子男人起身说道：“我们三人各须献上一段话，或长或短，好教韦瑟梅尔的众人欢喜——这是我的意愿。因为他们待我们这些旅人，竟如同待王侯一般，他们的话句句都说进人心坎里。老妈妈，请你先起头，由我来为这段说话作结。”
说罢他坐下了，那老婆子便道：“这件事我愈发情愿应承，因为依我看来，我能讲一个你们从未听过可与之相比的故事，一个能动你们每一颗心的故事。只是我还得求诸位耐一耐性子，只怕它对一个夜晚的堂中之欢来说略嫌长了些：而这故事，必须今夜起头，今夜讲完。那就都请听好！”
