## LXIV / 六十四

===Original===

The Carline Endeth Her Tale

When they arose on the morrow they began to think of departure, though they would have kept them in that guesthouse for many days; but both of the twain, and especially the Maiden, deemed that, if they might, they should be drawing nigh to that dwelling of the good Knight who had overthrown the League of the Barons; and they both deemed that thereabout, if anywhere, they should have tidings, even had they long to wait for them, of that new champion whom the wise Knight had gotten.

Now then the Carline did wisely, and she got to see the steward, and fell to talk with him, and did him to wit that, for all the simplicity of their raiment, they had both the will and the might to make a fair oblation to the Saint; and she took from the aforesaid necklace two sapphires and two emeralds, all great and very fair, and the steward’s eyes danced in the head of him at the sight, and he said: “This is a fair gift indeed, and if ye will come with me into the church I will show you to the Sub-prior, and if ye have any honest desire, as is like, since ye have such love of Holy Church, he and I between us will help you therein. And if not, nought is your time wasted in seeing our church, which is of itself worth a long journey to behold.”

So they went, well pleased, and when they were in the church they found that he had said nought but the sooth: so many pillars there were reaching up and toward the sky, so nobly wide it was, and as long as it should be. And there were many altars therein, all as well furnished as might be done; and long had it taken any lettered man to have told up the number of histories on the walls and in the windows, wherein they were all as if done with gemstones; and everywhere the fair stories told as if they were verily alive, and as if they who did them had seen them going on on the earth and in the heavens. So the two waited there ravished while the steward went to fetch the Sub-prior, and brought him presently, a kind and holy man, and humble of demeanour.

老妪讲完她的故事

次日她们起身之后，便开始计议启程之事，虽说她们本愿意在这客舍里再住上许多天；但她们两人都觉得——少女尤其是——倘若办得到，就该往那位击破了男爵同盟的好骑士的居处渐渐靠近；而且两人都认定，若要打听那位贤明骑士新得的英杰的消息，无论要等多久，除了那一带，别处是不会有的。

且说老妪行事自有智计，她设法见到总管，同他攀谈起来，使他知道：别看她们衣着朴素，她们却既有心愿、也有力量向圣者献上一份丰美的奉献；说着便从前述那条项链上取下两颗蓝宝石、两颗绿宝石，颗颗硕大，极为精美，总管一见，喜得眼珠儿在眼眶里直打转，说道：“这确是一份美礼。你们若肯随我进教堂，我便引你们去见副副院长；倘若你们有什么正当的祈求——看你们这样敬爱圣教会，想来是有的——他和我两人合力，必帮你们办成。即便没有，来看看我们的教堂，工夫也不算白费；单是这座教堂本身，便值得专程走一遭远路来观览。”

于是她们欢欢喜喜地去了。进了教堂，才知他说的句句是实话：立柱何其多，拔地而起，直指天空；堂庑何其宏敞，长短又合宜得体。堂中祭坛众多，无不陈设得尽善尽美；墙上和窗间那些历史故事画，任凭哪个识文断字之人去逐一数点，也须许久工夫，何况那些画恍如用宝石缀成；处处美妙的故事都仿佛真的活着，仿佛作画之人当真亲见那些事迹在地上、在天上一幕幕演过似的。于是两人在那里等候，看得心醉神驰；总管去请副副院长，不多时便领了来——是位和善圣洁之人，举止谦冲。

He spake to them and said: “My daughters, it is told me that ye need somewhat of our house in all honesty and holiness; now when ye have laid your gift on the altar, if ye will come with me and our steward here to the parlour, I will hearken to all ye have to say, and if the thing ye need of us can be done, done it shall be.” They thanked him humbly and went and made their oblation, and prayed, and the Sub-prior blessed them, and brought them out of the church into the parlour, and there they sat down together.

Then the Carline opened her budget, and told how they two had suffered from war and rapine, and when they had been delivered from a foul caitiff by a good Knight who had cherished them with all honours in his house, and all went well a while, it endured not long, for needs must he go to the wars, and there was he slain: how they, to escape the malice of the mother of the said Knight, who was a proud and hard woman, and now that her son was dead neither loved nor feared aught, must needs flee away. “But withal,” said the Carline, “even had that good and kind Knight lived and come back to us, needs must we have left his house, and his kindness ere long. For this I must do you to wit,” says she, “that we deem we have a weird and a fortune abiding us, and that through all trouble we shall be brought thereto in the end, and that the said Knight’s house of Brookside was over-far from it. This therefore we ask of you, since ye have shown such kindness unto us as the man of Samaria to him who fell amongst thieves.”

The Sub-prior smiled at her word and said: “Well, dame, neither the priest nor the Levite pass by the poor souls.”

他向她们说道：“我的女儿们，有人告诉我，你们怀着全然的诚实与虔敬，有事需我们这会院相助。等你们把礼物供上祭坛之后，若肯随我与此间的总管同到谈话间去，你们有什么话，我都愿细听；只要你们求我们的事办得到，就必定办到。”她们谦逊地道了谢，前去献了奉献，作了祈祷，副副院长为她们祝福，随后领她们出教堂，来到谈话间，于是一同坐下。

于是老妪便打开了她的锦囊，讲起她们二人如何遭了兵燹劫掠之苦；讲起她们如何被一位善良骑士从卑污的恶徒手里救出，在他家中备受荣礼相待；有一阵子诸事顺遂，只是好景不长，因为他必得奔赴战阵，便殁在了那里；又讲她们为躲避那位骑士之母的歹意——那是个骄横冷硬的妇人，儿子既死，对她们既不爱、也无可畏了——只得逃了出来。“不过话又说回来，”老妪道，“即便那位善良仁厚的骑士活着回到了我们身边，我们也迟早必得离开他的宅邸和他的恩义。因为我须叫你们知道，”她说，“我们自觉身前有一段命数与前程在等候，纵使历尽艰难，终究会被引到那里去；而那位骑士的溪畔堡，离那里是太远了。所以我们才求告你们，因为你们待我们的恩慈，恰如那撒玛利亚人待落在盗匪手中的人。”

副副院长听了她的话，微笑道：“好说，夫人；只是那祭司和利未人，也不从这些苦命人身边径直走过去呀。”

“Father,” she said, “thou and thy house, are ye foes or friends to the Knight of Longshaw?”

The Sub-prior smiled: “Friends forsooth,” said he, “so far as we may do him any good; but ye wot that we give him no carnal help with sword and spear, yea and little indeed might we give were we temporal lords, so far off as we be from Longshaw, and the river and the Wood Masterless lying all between us. And now indeed we begin to deem that the good Knight may yet come to his above, though ere he had given the Barons’ League that great overthrow things seemed much going awry with him. Moreover we have heard of a new champion whom he hath gotten, and who counted for much in that battle with the Barons, and well-nigh as wise in war is he as the Knight himself, say men. But now, my daughters, what would ye with the Knight of Longshaw?”

With that the Maiden took up the word, blushing red like a rose, and she said: “With the Knight of Longshaw it is perhaps little that we have to do, although we wish him all good, but it is rather with that one of whom ye have heard tell that he is a new-come champion of the Knight’s.” The Sub-prior smiled withal and said: “But what have ye to do with this champion?”

“神父，”她说，“您和您这会院，与朗肖骑士是敌是友？”

副副院长又笑了：“自然是友，”他说，“只要力所能及，我们都愿为他效劳；但你们知道，我们不会以刀枪予他俗世的助力；说真的，纵然我们是俗世的领主，像我们这样远离朗肖，中间又横着大河和无主林，也实在帮不上什么忙。不过如今我们确实开始相信，那位好骑士终有出头之日了，虽然在他给男爵同盟那一场大溃败之前，事情看着很是不妙。此外我们也听说他新得了一位英杰，在那场对男爵之战中举足轻重；人说，论兵机战法，此人几乎不亚于骑士本人。可是如今，我的女儿们，你们找朗肖骑士又有什么事呢？”

这时少女接过话头，脸红得像一朵玫瑰，说道：“同朗肖骑士，我们或许没有多少干系——虽说我们愿他万事顺遂——我们真正要找的，倒是您听说过的、那位骑士新来的英杰。”副副院长闻言微笑道：“可你们与这位英杰又有什么干系呢？”

The Maiden blushed no longer, but said: “I will tell you the story in as short a way as it may be told: I was a damsel living much all alone by the side of a terrible river, not lightly to be crossed, or indeed not at all. And on the other side of the said river was there a bold lad of about my years, and we fell into converse, speaking together very sweetly each from our own side of the water. And for a long time this seemed a no such evil fate for the two of us to endure; but time went on, and I grew into a woman and he grew into a man, and indeed as bold a champion as there is in our parts; and then indeed it seemed hard that, though we should meet in speech, yet never should mouth meet mouth or hand meet hand. But we lived on in hope, and trusted to what weird had wrought for us. And it seemed possibly not so unlike but that this bold and eager champion might go wide in the world, and somehow find out the country and the side of the river on which I was born and bred. And in the meantime was I determined above all things never to think of anyone else but this bold and beautiful champion, and even so it is with me now. And this good dame here, who is my very foster-mother, and is somewhat wise, though I would hope not more so than Holy Church alloweth, has always bidden me to hope to see my champion again, and even so I do. And we both know that it is only amongst the Knight of Longshaw and his men that he is to be found.”

Quoth the Sub-prior: “And when he is found, and ye let him know where ye are, will he come to you, think ye?”

“Even so we believe,” said the Maiden. “Well,” said the Sub-prior, “tell me what ye would have, and it shall be done for you.” Said the Carline: “We would come across the water and have guide and guards through the Wood Masterless to some place where we may dwell alone. Can ye do this much for us? And we shall be well willing to pay with suchlike gems as ye have already seen of ours for such a small house.”

少女不再脸红，说道：“我愿把这故事拣最短的讲法讲给你们听：我原是个少女，独自住在一条可怕的大河边上；那河不是轻易渡得的，实话说，根本就渡不过去。河的对岸有一个大胆的少年，年纪与我相仿，我们攀谈起来，各人站在各人的水边，说得十分甜蜜。有很长一段时间，这样的光景，在我们两人看来，似乎也算不得怎样的苦命。可是时光流转，我长成了妇人，他长成了男子汉，而且确是我们那一方数一数二的骁勇英杰；到那时才真觉得难堪：虽说我们还能隔着水交谈，却永远口不能就口、手不能执手。但我们始终怀着指望活下去，把一切信托给命运替我们作成的安排。况且这指望也并非全然无凭：这位勇猛热切的英杰，也许会在世间广行远游，机缘凑巧，寻到我生身长大、居住的那片乡土和那道河岸来。在此期间，我又拿定主意，无论如何，除了这位勇猛俊美的英杰，绝不把旁人放在心上；如今我的心也依然如此。而这里这位好夫人，是我的乳母，颇有些智慧——但愿不至超出圣教会所容许的分寸——她总是嘱咐我，要存着再见我英杰的指望，我也正是这样存着指望的。而且我们都知道，只有到朗肖骑士和他的麾下人马之中，才寻得着他。”

副副院长道：“那么，待寻着了他，你们又让他知道你们在哪里，你们想，他会来寻你们么？”

“我们信定是如此。”少女道。“好，”副副院长说，“告诉我你们想要什么，必为你们办到。”老妪道：“我们要过河去，要向导和护卫送我们穿过无主林，到一个我们能独自安居的地方。这一点你们能为我们办到么？这样一座小小的宅子，我们甘愿用你们已经见过的我们那种宝石来偿付。”

“Well,” said the Sub-prior, “that may well be, and tomorrow morn, if ye will take the whole thing on your own heads, I will send you down to the ferry that lieth betwixt us and a House of Friars on the further side of the water. At a writing from us these good brothers may find you some such dwelling in the Wood Masterless as ye seek, and will furnish you with way-beasts and guides thereto. But I leave it to you, Carline, whether ye do not risk greatly to take such a pearl with you into the place which is peopled by the worst of men.” Said the Carline: “To tell you the truth, Father, I have pieces of wisdom by which I can blind the eyes of foolish men, so that they will see nothing of the delicate beauty of my daughter here.” “Well,” said the Sub-prior, and smiled.

So the very next morning it was as the Sub-prior said. Two lay-brothers brought them down to the waterside, and at parting gave a writing into the hand of the Carline. And when they were safely over the mighty Flood, and landed on a pleasant strand where the water was shallow and the current none so swift, the ferryman spoke a word of them to one of the brotherhood who had stood watching the crossing of that boat. With a friendly greeting he turned and led the way to the Friary, a fair stone building, set with a wall both high and long. Here met the Carline and the Maiden with a kindly welcome, and were set in the guesthouse to rest that night. And, said the good brothers, their matter might be seen to, and they would send them on through the Wood Masterless; and that there was such a house as the Carline would have, which is in a good case, said they, though it may want here and there a nail or a plank.

And in the morning two of the brothers were bidden array themselves and take sumpter-horses and good horses for the women, and to lead them to within such distance of the Castle and Longshaw as might seem good to the Carline, and that forsooth was but some dozen miles.

“好，”副副院长说，“这事多半能成。明日一早，倘若你们肯把这事一概担在自己头上，我便差人送你们下渡口去；渡口在我们这边与对岸一所修士会院之间。凭我们一纸书信，那些好修士便能为你们在无主林中寻一处你们要的那种住处，并为你们备办行路牲口和向导。只是老妪啊，把这样一颗明珠带进那恶徒麇集之地，你们是不是担着绝大的风险，这就留给你自己去掂量了。”老妪道：“跟您说句实话，神父，我有几件智巧的本事，能蒙住愚人的眼睛，叫他们看不出我这里这位女儿的娇美来。”副副院长微笑道：“那就好。”

果真，次日一早，一切都如副副院长所言。两名在俗修士送她们下到水边，临别时把一纸文书交在老妪手里。待她们安然渡过那浩大的洪流，在一片可人的岸滩登了岸——那里水浅，水流也不那么湍急——渡船夫向弟兄会中一个一直望着这条船摆渡的人，说了一声她们的来历。那弟兄友善地招呼了一句，便转身引路，把她们带到修士会院：一座美观的石砌屋宇，围着一道又高又长的墙。老妪和少女在此受到亲切的欢迎，被安置在客舍里歇了一夜。好修士们说，她们的事自会有人照管，他们愿送她们取道无主林继续前行；又说，老妪想要的那种房屋是有一所的，他们说，屋况良好，只是这里那里也许缺一枚钉子、一块木板。

到了早晨，两名修士奉命整装，带上驮马，又为两位妇人备了好马，要把她们送到距那座城堡和朗肖城远近合适的地方去——远近以老妪看着合宜为准；老实说，也不过十二英里上下。

There then they rested; and from time to time the Carline would go on her errands, and would see folk who would give her tidings of how things went in the world. And ever she found that the tale was the same. For the Lord of Longshaw might not stretch out a hand without thriving; and ever with him at council, or at privy talk, or in the front of the battle, was this marvellous champion, whom it availed nought for any man to gainsay. At last the time began to seem long for the Maiden; and the Carline from time to time, when she did not know that she was nigh, heard her bewailing that her man came not, and she heard her say one day: If he come not before long, then will be perished some deal of that delicate beauty which I would above all things deliver into his keeping, so that he may know that it was no mere shadow of a woman with whom he gave and took in talk on the other side of the Sundering Flood. And in very sooth she began to peak and pine, and the Carline took her to task therefor, and said that she herself would try to set this right. Till on a day the Carline knew for sure that the champion had now turned his head from all his valiances, and was thinking of nothing but of how he might come across her with whom he had such merry days on the other side of the Great Water.

Short it is that is left to tell. The Carline knew of a certainty that he had been smitten in felony and grievously hurt, and that he had been carried to an hermitage and there healed; therefore she waylaid him on a time and brought him to the house wherein they dwelt. And there, whether it were by her planning or by mere chance-hap may scarce be told, but such a thing befell that the wrath of the champion blazed out in him, so that for some few minutes he might scarce tell what was before him. And then it was all over, and they two were sealed for one another for what yet abided them on the earth.

Now this is my tale, and belike it has been somewhat overlong, and therefore it scarce needs that ye bid this damsel tell a tale for her part, which were indeed better told by her casting to earth her grey cloak and showing her body fairly dight. For, indeed, this damsel belongeth to one who is your kinsman and dear friend: and seemly will she think it that she show her body so dight that it shall lack no fairness before you.

她们便在那里安顿下来；老妪时时出门办她的事务，会见种种能给她带来世间动静消息的人。而她听来的，每每是同一番话：朗肖之主但凡伸一伸手，无有不顺遂的；而无论议事之时、密谈之间，还是战阵之前，总有那位神乎其神的英杰在他身边，任凭何人同他抵牾，都是徒然。到后来，时日渐渐显得漫长起来；老妪时常趁自己不知道少女就在近旁的时候，听得她哀叹她的良人不来；有一日还听她说：他若再不快来，我那无论如何也要完好交到他手中保藏的娇美，便要消损几分了——须知那娇美，原是要好叫他知道：当年在分离洪流对岸同他言来语去的，并非一个女子的空影。说实在的，她渐渐憔悴瘦损起来，老妪为此责数她，并说自己要设法把这件事料理妥当。直到有一天，老妪确知那位英杰已把心思从一切武功勋业上转开，一心只想着怎样才能渡过大水，去到曾同他在对岸共度快活时日的她身边。

剩下要讲的不多了。老妪确知他曾遭恶徒毒手，身受重伤，被人送到一处隐士庐里疗愈了；于是她瞅准一个时机候着，把他带到她们居住的屋里。在那里——究竟是出于她的筹谋，还是纯粹出于偶然的机缘，殊难断言——竟有这样一件事发生：那英杰胸中的怒火轰然迸发，有好一阵子，他几乎辨不清眼前是什么。随后便一切都定了局，他们两人从此互相印定，共守此后在人间尚未度尽的岁月。

我的故事便是这样了，只怕讲得有些过长；因此你们大可不必再叫这位少女讲她那一份——她那故事，其实由她把灰色斗篷往地上一抛、显出她妆扮齐整的身姿来讲，才更好哩。因为这位少女，原是许身于你们一位亲族兼挚友的人；她也会觉得，这样妆扮停当地在你们眼前现身，不缺半分妍丽，方才合乎体统。
