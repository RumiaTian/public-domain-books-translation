## XLVII / 四十七

===Original===

The Battle in the Square

On the morrow’s morn, the leaders of the town met Osberne and his captains in council, and their rede was that they should do warily and not throw the helve after the hatchet. This they deemed best, that they should now, while they might, make strong with mound and wall their quarter of the town, since, until Sir Godrick was come to them, they might even now look to it to have much might against them. This reded the Red Lad nowise gainsaid, knowing well how valiant and stout these men would be behind walls; but he said: “Yet, my masters, the more leisure you have for this spade and mattock work, the better it shall be for you and the work. Wherefore my rede is that some of your chosen men go with the best of mine, and that we issue out of our quarter and fall upon the others, and make a good space clear of foes of the streets and carfaxes that march unto your quarter, which forsooth shall serve you as an outwork to your castle until Sir Godrick comes with a great host and fills up all that and more. And, sooth to say, now at once is the best time to do this, while the foe is all astonied at what befell last night.”

That seemed good to one and all; so when they had eaten and were duly arrayed they issued forth into the streets, and at first indeed wended those that were truly of their quarter, only on the day before they deemed them not big enough to hold all that; but now it was their mind to bring it within their defences. So the Red Lad and his rode on warily, taking heed that they should not be cut off by any at their backs. So at last they came unto a great carfax with a wide square round about it. There they drew up their folk in a long line with a wide face to the foe, well furnished of bows and other shot-weapons; for the townsmen were archers exceeding good.

广场之战

次日清晨，城中的首领们与奥斯伯恩及其众队长聚议军情，众人的计议是：行事务必谨慎，切莫丢了斧头，又把斧柄也搭上去。他们认为上策乃是：趁眼下还来得及，用土垒与墙垣把城中自己那一坊筑得坚固；因为在戈德里克爵士来到他们这里之前，他们随时都得提防有大股兵力压到自己头上。对这条方略，红衣少年绝无异议，因为他深知这些人凭墙据守时会如何骁勇坚劲；但他开口说道：“然而，诸位头领，这等锹镐挖掘的活计，你们手头越是宽裕，于你们自身、于这活计都越好。因此我的方略是：你们拣选一些精壮，同我最得力的弟兄一道，杀出我们这一坊，去扑杀其他几方的人，把通连你们那一坊的街巷和十字街口，清出好大一片没有敌人的地面；在戈德里克爵士率大军前来、把那一片以及更多地方统统填满之前，这片地面确乎可充你们这座堡寨的外围工事。而且说句实话，眼下正是动手的最好时机，因为敌人昨夜遭了那场变故，此刻还惊魂未定呢。”

这主意人人称善。于是众人用过饭食，装束齐整，便开出街去。一开初所走的，确实还都是本坊的街巷；只不过前一日他们还嫌这一坊不够大，容不下这一切；如今却存心要把这一带统统圈进自家防线之内。红衣少年一行于是谨慎骑行，时时提防有人从背后截断归路。末了，他们来到一处大十字街口，四周环着一片宽阔的广场。他们便在那里把人马列成一条长阵，宽宽的正面朝向敌人，弓弩和其他射远的家伙一应齐备；因为城中的百姓都是顶好的弓手。

There was nought in the square or on the carfax at first but themselves; but after a little there entered by the east way and the west a rout of archers, and fell to shooting at Osberne’s, and they back again. The archers of the Porte did not dare to show much face to the Red Lad, but were gathered together in plumps at each incoming into the square. Said the Red Lad to himself: Let us make an end of this folly. And he bade his men leave shooting, and then gave the word, and they rode at the carles right and left with spear and sword. Straightway the archers ran all they might, yet not so fast but that the Red Lad and his captains got amidst them ere they could take to the narrow byways, so that a many were slain. And this was a matter of but ten minutes. But when the horsemen had been along with the bowmen a little while, they heard great horns blowing from the south, and therewith great noise of horse, and presently a great rout of men-at-arms in the best of armour began to come in by the southern road, and the Red Lad’s men were all agog to fall on them straightway, but he made them forbear till they had filled the square overfull. They were not long about it, but meanwhile the townsmen shot all they might; and so nigh they were that, despite their armour, not a few fell, both of men and horses; yet did they fall not on till the square was full of them, so that it looked far bigger than might have been deemed. Then they thrust on, but so close that they might scarce handle their arms, and the Red Lad and his cried their cry, The Red Lad for Longshaw! and rushed forward, smiting and thrusting, till the front of the foemen began to try to turn about if they might; but scarce they could, though if they might not flee they might not fall. And they behind strove to get forward to smite, for they said they were many more than the others; but they could get but little done, for their forward men who had been overthrown were hindering them. Now also the carles of the town laid aside their bows and entered among them with short swords and axes, and hewed and slew and took none to mercy, and it seemed hard to know how that would end, save by all those men-at-arms falling in the place.

Now, as ye may deem, Osberne was more thrust forward than any other, and somewhat of a space he had cleared before him, and his yellow hair came down from under his basnet, and his long red surcoat streamed all rent and tattered in the wind, and Boardcleaver was bare and bloody in his fist, and his face was stern but not exceeding fierce; for he would the slaughter of the day were over. Now he hove up Boardcleaver, and before him was a tall man in gilded armour and a gay yellow surcoat of silk, and his armour was little rent and his sword unscathed in his hand; a stark man he was of aspect, but terror was come into his soul because of the slaughter of the press and that there was no escape therefrom. So when he saw Boardcleaver arising he cried out, “O Red Lad, Red Lad, O thou seeker, let me live, that I may tell thee what thou wouldst give many lives to know!”

Then Osberne restrained Boardcleaver and let him fall to his wrist, and stretched out his hand to the gilded man. But even therewith his hand was thrust aside, for many a man there was mad and drunk with the slaying: and a short, dark, long-armed man of the weavers’ craft, armed with nought else save a heavy short-sword cutting on the inner edge, drew him on to the gilded man’s horse, and brought his sword backhanded across his face and neck, and fell with him as he fell, and mangled him that he was more than dead, and then got up again amidst the horses and fell to work again. Then Osberne, when he saw the tale was done, groaned aloud; but none heeded him, for it was to them but as a cry of the wounded. Then he uphove Boardcleaver again and cried out shrilly: “The Red Lad, the Red Lad for Longshaw and the Crafts! On, on at them!” And that all heard, both his and theirs. And now they of the foemen began to cease pressing forward, and many fled without a stroke stricken, till there was somewhat more room for the rest to flee, but little leave, for even so was more room for the pursuers, and soon was the square clear of all but dead and sore hurt; and the chase endured all up and along the carfax, and mad-fierce it was, and that mostly at the hands of the townsmen, who deemed that they had much to pay back to the men of the King and the Porte.

起初，广场上、街口处除了他们自己，空无一人；可是不多时，便有一股股弓手从东西两面涌入，朝奥斯伯恩的人放起箭来，这边也回射过去。市府的弓手不敢在红衣少年面前露多少脸面，只在通入广场的各个路口，一丛一丛挤作堆儿。红衣少年心中自语：这等蠢事，我们来给它收场罢。他便命部下停止放箭，随即一声号令，众人挺矛举剑，向左右两边的汉子们驰杀过去。弓手们撒腿便逃，却终究不够快，红衣少年和他的众队长赶在他们钻进窄巷之前便已杀入队中，因此结果了不少人。而这前后不过十分钟光景。骑兵们与弓手周旋了不多时，便听得南面大号角齐鸣，接着是浩大的马蹄之声，不久，一大股甲仗精良的甲士开始从南路开进广场。红衣少年的人马个个跃跃欲试，要立时扑上去，他却教众人按捺不动，直等他们把广场挤得满满当当。他们填广场倒没费多久；这其间城中百姓拼命放箭，因双方相距甚近，任凭甲胄在身，倒下的仍复不少，人马皆有；可他们偏要等广场塞满了人，方才挥军来攻，于是那广场望去竟比人们所能料想的大出许多。随后他们挤压向前，却挤得几乎施展不开兵器；红衣少年一行高呼他们的战号：“红衣少年为朗肖！”便冲杀上前，挥砍刺击，直杀得敌阵前锋想法掉头；却几乎掉转不得——既逃不出去，便连倒也倒不下去。后面的人还拼命向前挤着要砍杀，自恃人多势众；却收效甚微，因为前面被砍翻的自家人正挡着他们的道。这时，城中的汉子们也撂下弓，提着短剑板斧杀入阵中，砍的砍，剁的剁，不留半分怜悯；看那光景，这事除却那些甲士尽数葬身原地，已难有别个了局。

如今，如你们所能想见，奥斯伯恩冲得比谁都靠前，身前已清出了一小片空地；他的金发从战盔底下披散下来，那件长长的红罩袍撕裂褴褛，在风中翻卷；断板剑裸着锋刃、血渍斑斑，握在他的拳头里；他面容严峻，却不十分狰狞，因为他但愿这一日的杀伐早些完结。这时他举起断板剑，只见面前立着一个高大的汉子，身披镀金的甲胄，外罩一领鲜亮的黄绸罩袍；那人甲胄少有破损，手中长剑也完好无损；论相貌原是个凛然剽悍之人，无奈这一场人堆里的屠戮、又兼身陷其中无从脱逃，早教他魂胆俱丧。他见断板剑扬起，便高声叫道：“红衣少年啊，红衣少年，你这苦苦寻索的人啊，饶我一条性命，我好告诉你一件你拿几条命去换也甘愿的事！”

奥斯伯恩于是收住断板剑，任它垂落腕边，向那镀金甲的汉子伸出手去。可恰在此时，他的手却被人拨开了，因为场中多少人早已杀得如痴如狂：一个织工行会的汉子，身材矮黑，长臂膀，除一柄沉甸甸、刃口开在内侧的短剑外别无兵器，抢上前将那镀金甲的汉子拖下马来，反手一剑横斩过他的脸和脖颈，又随着他一同栽倒，把他剁得死上加死，随后从群马之间爬起，接着又杀上前去。奥斯伯恩见这事已成了定局，不由出声呻吟；却无人理会他，因为在众人听来，那不过是伤者的哀号罢了。随后他再度高举断板剑，尖声高呼：“红衣少年，红衣少年为朗肖与小行会！上啊，向他们杀啊！”这一声，敌我双方全都听见了。于是敌军渐渐不再向前挤压，许多人一刀未交便逃了；余下的人固然多了些逃窜的空当，却难得逃出性命，因为追杀的人也同样多了空当。不多时，广场上除了死者和重伤之人，已空无一人；追杀沿着十字街口一路上下延续，凶狂已极，动手的多半是城中百姓——他们自道欠下国王和市府人马的旧账，正有许多要清算。

Now after this Osberne and his drew not back from the carfax, but by the rede of him the townsmen made trenches and walls to strengthen them right up to the said carfax. And for three days the King’s men durst not fall upon them there, save that they tried a little arrow-shot from afar, but did not much hurt thereby.

But the next day thereafter comes Sir Godrick with his host to the help of the townsmen, and rides into the North Gate amidst the joy of all men. And the next day they push on to their outworks and fall on. Three days of battle they have thereafter, wherein Sir Godrick will not suffer the Red Lad to deal: “For,” saith he, “it is thou that hath won, and now we have little to do, but as it were the woodwright’s and the carpenter’s work. Wherefore now I bid thee to rest.” Laughed Osberne, and tarried in the North quarter, while Sir Godrick and his with all deliberation set to work on clearing the quarters on that side of the river; and they were four days about the business albeit the men of the Porte and the King were scarce so stubborn and enduring as they looked to find them.

But Osberne did all he might to keep good order and good heart amongst his men, and they made their strongholds strong to the letter, and looked to it that all their forward places should be ready for battle at a moment’s notice.

这之后，奥斯伯恩一行不曾从十字街口后退一步；依照他的方略，城中百姓挖壕筑墙，把防线一路加固到那处街口。整整三天，国王的人马不敢前来攻打，只从远处放箭稍稍试探，却没造成多大伤害。

可是再下一天，戈德里克爵士便率大军来援城中百姓，在万众欢腾之中驰入北门。次日，他们便推进到外围工事，发起攻击。此后又打了三天，这三天里，戈德里克爵士说什么也不让红衣少年出手：“因为，”他说道，“功业已经是你立下的了，如今我们没多少事可做，不过是细木匠和木匠的活计罢了。因此如今我吩咐你歇息去。”奥斯伯恩哈哈一笑，便留在城北那一坊歇着；戈德里克爵士则率众从容不迫地动手清剿河那一边的各坊市区。这事他们费了四天工夫，尽管市府与国王的人马远不如他们预想的那般顽韧耐战。

而奥斯伯恩则竭尽全力，使部下之间保持严整的军纪与旺盛的士气；众人把各处壁垒修得坚固无比、丝毫不苟，并且留心做到：所有前沿阵地，一闻号令，顷刻间便可投入战斗。
