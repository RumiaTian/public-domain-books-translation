## XVIII / 十八

===Original===

Elfhild Hears of the Slaying

But two days after this was the tryst-day for Osberne to see his over-water friend, and he went soberly enough, and came to the waterside and found her over against him; and she asked of him tidings. “Tidings enough,” said he, “for now have I done a deed beyond my years, a deed unmeet for a child; to wit, I have slain a man.”

“O,” she said, “and didst thou sleep after the deed?” Said Osberne: “Yea, and dreamed never a deal. But I must tell thee I was in my right.” Said Elfhild: “What did he to thee that thou must slay him?” Osberne said: “He came swaggering into our house and would take all to him, and put all of us to the road or hold us in thraldom.” She said “But tell me, how didst thou slay him? Was he drunk or asleep?” “Nay,” said he, “I was champion for my grandsire, and the robber had a sword in his fist, and I another and we fought, and I overcame him.” Said the maiden: “But was he mannikin or a dastard, or unskilled in weapons?” Spake Osberne, reddening: “He was a stark carle, a bold man, and was said to be of all prowess.”

埃尔弗希尔德闻知杀人之事

这事过后不过两天，便到了奥斯伯恩与对岸友人相会的约期。他此去倒还沉静，来到水边，见她正立在对岸迎着他；她便向他探问消息。“消息多着呢，”他说，“因为我如今做成了一桩超出我年纪的事，一桩不合孩子身份的事：说来，我杀了一个人。”

“哦，”她说，“这桩事干完之后，你可还睡得着？”奥斯伯恩道：“睡得着，连一个梦也不曾做。只是我须告诉你，我占的是理。”埃尔弗希尔德道：“他对你做了什么，你便非杀他不可？”奥斯伯恩道：“他大摇大摆闯进我们家来，要把一切都据为己有，要把我们全家赶到路上去，再不然便把我们拘作奴仆。”她说：“可你告诉我，你是怎么杀他的？他可是醉了，还是睡着了？”“都不是，”他说，“我是替我祖父出战的斗士；那强盗拳头里攥着一柄剑，我手里也有一柄，我们交了手，我胜了他。”那少女道：“可他是个矮子呢，还是个懦夫，还是个不谙兵器之辈？”奥斯伯恩涨红了脸，说道：“他是一条剽悍的汉子，一个有胆勇之人，据说浑身是胆、武艺俱全。”

She said nothing a while, but stood pale and downcast. And he said: “What is this, playmate? I looked to have much praise from thee for my deed. Dost thou know that this man was as the pest to all the countryside, and that I have freed men of peace from a curse?” “Be not wrath with me, Osberne,” she said, “indeed I am somewhat downcast; for I see that now thou wilt be no playmate for me, but wilt be a man before thy time, and wilt be looking towards such things as men desire; and that tall maidens come to womanhood will be for thee, not quaint rags of children such as I be.”

“Now, Elfhild,” said he, “why wilt thou run to meet trouble halfway? Am I worser to thee than I was last time?” “Nay,” she said, “and indeed I deem thee glorious, and it is kind and kind of thee to come to me ever, and not to miss one of our trysts.”

“Now thou art dear,” said Osberne; “and wilt thou do something for my disport? wilt thou pipe thy sheep to thee?”

她半晌没有言语，只站在那里，脸色发白，垂头丧气。他便说道：“这是怎么了，玩伴？我原指望我这桩作为，能从你这里讨得许多夸赞呢。你可知道，这人在四乡八里就同瘟疫一般，我已把安分的良民从一场灾殃里解救出来了？”“奥斯伯恩，你别恼我，”她说，“我委实有些意兴萧索；因为我看得分明，往后你不会再做我的玩伴了，你要不到年纪便先做个大人了，心思要转到男人们想望的那些事情上去了；再说，日后配你的，将是长成了人的高挑少女，不会是我这样一个孩子家的稀罕破烂儿了。”

“好了，埃尔弗希尔德，”他说，“你何苦半路上跑去迎接烦恼呢？难道我待你比上一次坏些了么？”“没有，”她说，“而且说实在的，我看你光彩得很；你回回肯到我这里来，一次相会也不曾错过，真是千好万好的情分。”

“这会儿你可真亲，”奥斯伯恩说，“你可肯做点什么给我取个乐？可肯吹起笛子，把你的羊儿唤到你身边来？”

“Nay,” said she, “I will not; I will not skip like an antic, and show thee my poor little spindle legs. If I were a woman grown I should scarce show so much as the ankle of my foot. Besides, thou laughest at my hopping and jumping amongst these foolish woolly beasts, and I would not have thee laughing at me.”

“Elfhild my dear,” said he, “thou art wrong. When I have laughed, it was never in mockery of thee, but for pleasure of thy pretty ways and the daintiness of thy dancing, which is like to the linden leaves on a fresh summer morning.”

“But how am I to know that?” she said. “Well, at any rate, ask me not to dance today. But I will sit down and tell thee a very sweet tale of old times, which thou hast never erst heard. It is about the sea and ships, and of a sea-wife coming into the dwellings of men.” Quoth Osberne, “I were fain to look on the sea and to sail it.” “Yea,” said Elfhild, “but thou wilt take me with thee, wilt thou not?” “O yea,” said Osberne. And they both forgat the Sundering Flood, and how they should never meet, as they sat each side of the fearful water, and the tale and sweet speech sped to and fro betwixt them. So a fair ending had that day of tryst.

“不，”她说，“我不肯；我才不肯像个小丑似的又蹦又跳，把我那两条可怜的纺锤细腿露给你瞧。我若是个长成了的女人，只怕连脚脖子都不肯露出来。再说，我在这些蠢头蠢脑的毛团畜生中间蹦来跳去，你只管笑话我；我可不愿意叫你笑话。”

“亲爱的埃尔弗希尔德，”他说，“你错怪我了。我但凡笑时，从不曾存心讥笑你，只因喜欢你那娇憨的情态，喜欢你的舞步那般轻灵妙曼——恰似夏日清晨里鲜嫩的菩提叶一般。”

“可我怎好知道呢？”她说。“也罢，无论如何，今天你别叫我跳舞了。可我会坐下来，给你讲一个古时极好听的故事，是你从来不曾听过的。那故事讲的是大海和船只，还讲一位海妻来到凡人的居所。”奥斯伯恩道：“我倒巴望亲眼见一见大海，还想在海上行船呢。”“好啊，”埃尔弗希尔德说，“可你要带我同去，是不是？”“哦，那是自然，”奥斯伯恩说。于是他们两个都忘了分离洪流，忘了他们永无相见之日，各自坐在那可怕的大水两岸，故事同甜蜜的话语，便在他们之间飞快地来来往往。那一日的相会，便这样得了一个美满的收场。
