## VI / 六

===Original===

They Fare to the Cloven Mote

And now the days wore away to winter, and ever thereafter might Osberne do what he would, and go where he would, for as little a lad as he was; but he worked with a good will if he were uncompelled, and if he were suffered to wander at whiles as his will drave him. Forsooth, since he had no fellows of a like age to him, it was whiles that he found the open field or the waste gave him better fellowship than the older folk, yea even than the women.

Winter came, and the snow and the frost, which was not very hard in that land, as many would have been glad if it were, for then might the Sundering Flood have been laid with ice, which never betid. On the morning of Yule day, Osberne and his grandsire and grandam got under way long before daylight, that they might go to the Cloven Mote, and hear the Christmass in the church of Allhallows, which had been builded on the east side of the water to be the church of the Mote; but on the other side of the water was another church like to it in all ways, and under the same invocation, for the Western folk. This was the first time that Osberne had been boun to the Mote, and withal both the women were wont to stay at home: but this time nought would serve the goodwife but she must wend with her man, that she might show her darling and her champion to the neighbours. It was a matter of seven miles down the water to the Mote-stead, and they went aslant over the snow-covered fields, and hit the riverbank about halfway, and went thence along the very lip of the water. And by then it was pretty much daylight; and Osberne looked over the water and saw about a half mile off (for the day was clear) two little knolls rising from the field, and betwixt them and about them a shaw of small wood; and he asked his grandsire what that might be, for hitherto he had never been so far down the water; whereas before he slew the wolves, down the water was banned to him, and after that he had been busy about the houses and folds, or driving the sheep to the bents day by day.

赴裂丘大会

且说日子一天天捱到冬天，此后奥斯伯恩（Osberne）任凭还是个小娃娃，却想做什么便做什么，想去哪里便去哪里；只是若没人强逼，他干活倒也甘心乐意；若再容他时不时照着自己的性子出去游荡一番，那就更好。说实在的，他既没有年纪相仿的伙伴，有时倒觉得空旷的田野和荒原，比年长的人们更够交情，甚而至于比女眷们还强。

冬天来了，带来了雪和霜，那地方的霜雪并不严酷——尽管不少人倒巴不得它严酷些，因为那样分离洪流（the Sundering Flood）便可封上冰，只是这事从来不曾有过。岁末冬至（Yule）日的清早，天还远未亮，奥斯伯恩便同祖父祖母动身上路，要往裂丘大会去，在诸圣堂（Allhallows）里听圣诞弥撒；那教堂建在水东岸，本是大会的教堂；水对岸却另有一座教堂，处处与它一模一样，奉的也是同一圣名，是为西岸百姓而设。奥斯伯恩这是头一遭去赴大会；再说，往常那两位女眷照例是留守家里的：可是这一回，女当家怎么也拦不住，定要跟着她男人同去，好把她那心肝宝贝、她那大勇士指点给邻里们看。从那儿顺水下行到大会场，约有七里之遥；他们斜穿过盖雪的田野，约莫走到一半便上了河岸，从此沿着紧贴水滨的边沿行去。这时天色已差不多大亮；奥斯伯恩隔水望去，见约莫半里之外（那天天色晴朗）有两座小丘从田野间隆起，丘间丘周生着一片小树林；他便问祖父那是什么去处，因为他此前从未到过这么远的下游：杀狼之前，下游一带原是不许他去的；杀狼之后，他又忙着屋舍栏圈之间的活计，再不便是天天把羊赶上草坡。

So his grandsire answered him: “That is hight Hartshaw, and we are told that on the other side of the shaw and the knolls looking west is a stead with houses inhabited, and the whole place is hight Hartshaw Knolls.” Said Osberne: “I would we were there a while, for as I look at the stead it seemeth friendly to me, and I fare to feel that the folk thereof shall come into my life some day.” Answered the goodman: “We hear that little dwelleth there save a widow-woman and her one child, a little maiden. And as to thy one day, it shall be a long while coming; for long and long shall it be for anyone to encompass the Sundering Flood, save the Winter of Fear come upon us, and all the land be overlaid with ice, and the waters of the Flood be stayed; which may God and Allhallows forfend.”

The lad said nought for a while; and then he said: “Goodman, I would we had gone down to riverbank from out our own door, and gone all along the Flood-side to the Mote; for it were pleasant to have looked across the Flood, thinking of all there is on the other side, and wondering if we shall ever get there. Why did we not this, for on the very bank the going is better?” Said the carle: “We have come the shortest way this bitter morning; that is all.”

Herein he lied; for they had gone that slant-way to give the go-by to a certain place of the Flood-bank which the Dale-dwellers deemed perilous; but thereof he would not tell the little carle, now that he was become so masterful, deeming that if he heard of any peril toward he would be all agog to try the adventure thereof, as forsooth was true. Of this place, which lay now but just behind them, shall more be told hereafter.

于是祖父答道：“那地方唤作哈特肖（Hartshaw）；听说在小树林和丘冈的西面，还有一座有人居住的庄舍，整处地方便唤作哈特肖丘（Hartshaw Knolls）。”奥斯伯恩道：“但愿我们能上那儿待一阵子才好；我望着那庄舍，只觉它对我有情，我渐渐觉得，那地方的人有朝一日会走进我的生命里来。”庄主答道：“我们听说那儿住的人寥寥，只有一个寡妇带着她的独生孩子，一个小姑娘。至于你说的有朝一日，那日子可来得远呢：人要越过分离洪流，还须许久许久，除非畏惧之冬（the Winter of Fear）降到我们头上，遍地封满坚冰，洪流之水凝止不流——愿上帝和诸圣千万拦住这样的事。”

少年有一会儿没言语；然后他说道：“庄主，方才我们若从自家门口一直下到河岸，沿着洪流边上一直走到大会场，那该多好；因为那样便可以一路隔着洪流眺望，想着那边的一切光景，猜着我们几时才能到得那里。方才为什么不这样走？紧贴着河岸，路不是更好走么？”那汉子道：“这样苦寒的清早，我们抄的是最近的路；如此而已。”

他这话其实是撒谎；他们所以走那条斜路，为的是避开洪流岸边一处谷地居民素来认为凶险的地方；只是如今这小家伙变得如此自作主张，庄主便不肯说给他听——他心想，这孩子若听说有什么凶险临头，准会心痒难熬，非去闯那历险不可，而这倒也真是实情。至于这个地方——此刻已落在他们身后不远处——后文自有细讲。

Now they come to the Mote in good time when the sun was but just arisen, and there was already a throng; and at their coming the folk on the western side raised a shout, as the folk on either side were wont to welcome newcomers; but the very first man they hit upon was Surly John; and the goodwife, a soft, kind woman, hailed him friendly, and was fain to have someone whom she knew unto whom to tell her tale of the champion and the wolves. For indeed it needs must out to the very first comer, and out it came now, many worded, and folk, both men and women, gathered about the twain to hearken; for the goodwife told it all well and without hitch.

Surly John must needs abide the telling of it, but when it was done he said: “Well, dame, so it is that I always deemed the lad kenspeckle; and it has moreover turned out as I warned you, that you have got a new master over you.” And therewith he turned away; but of those others who heard the tale there were more than one or two who praised it much, and deemed it marvellous as might well be that a child should have faced and slain those three monsters who had put two stout men to flight. And one man made up this stave, which was presently sung all about the Eastern Mote, and went over the water with the tale to the Western one:

> To run and to fight
> Are deeds free to the wight,
> And John tried in battle
> Had heard the boards rattle,
> But needed to prove
> The race back to the stove;
> So his wightness he showed
> In way-wearing the road.
> While Osberne, who knew
> How the footrace to do,
> Must try the new game
> Where the battle-beasts came.
> Bairn for fight, but for running the strong man and tall,
> And all folk for the laughter when both are in hall.

他们到大会场时正是好时辰，太阳才刚刚升起，场上早已聚了一群人；他们一到，西岸的百姓便高声欢呼起来，因为两岸的人惯常都这样迎接新来的客人。可是他们头一个撞见的偏偏是乖戾约翰（Surly John）；女当家是个温软和善的女人，亲亲热热地招呼他，正巴不得有个相识，好对她讲她那勇士与恶狼的故事。说实在的，这故事她逢着头一个遇上的人也非吐出来不可，如今便千言万语地倒了出来；男女听众渐渐围拢了这两人，听她讲说——她讲得实在好，一点儿不打磕绊。

乖戾约翰不得不由她把故事讲完；讲完之后他说道：“好吧，主母，实情是这样：我一向就看这小子惹眼出众；而且果然不出所料，应了我早先提醒你们的话——你们头顶上如今添了一位新主子。”说罢他便转身走开了。可是在旁听到这故事的人里，不止一两个赞不绝口，都觉得一个孩童竟能直面那吓跑两条壮汉的三头恶兽，把它们尽数杀了，实在堪称奇事。于是有一个人编出了下面这支歌谣，不消片刻便唱遍了东岸大会，又随着这故事渡水传到了西岸大会：

> 奔逃与厮杀，
> 儿郎自由的事；
> 约翰临阵打仗，
> 听惯盾板震响，
> 只是尚待考验：
> 奔回炉灶的脚程；
> 他的英雄气概，
> 全显在磨破道路。
> 至于奥斯伯恩，他素知
> 赛跑该当怎样跑，
> 只得换试新把戏，
> 到战兽来处走一遭。
> 打仗用娃娃，奔逃用那高壮大汉，
> 待两人同处一堂，众人好一场大笑。

When Surly John heard this stave he cursed between his teeth, but said nought.

But now on either side, the churches fell to ringing to mass, and all folk fared to service. And Osberne sat in a good place amongst the carles, and forsooth he had both ears and eyes open, both then and all day. Mass over, the cooking-fires were lighted and tents were pitched on either side the water, and in a while they went to dinner; and thereafter, when they had sung a while, came the time of drinking, and folk were paired, men and women so far as might be, for more men there were than women. But whereas all men save Surly John were well with Osberne, there was gotten for his mate a fair young damsel of but seventeen winters, and Osberne, who had looked hard on all the women who were well-liking (for he had seen but very seldom any women save those two of his kinfolk), was amazed with joy when the dear maid pulled down her hood and pulled off her gloves. And whereas she was shy of him because of his doughtiness, for all that he was but a child, it was not until they had drunk a cup or two that he took heart to set his hand to her neck and kiss her cheeks and her mouth, whereat she blushed rosy red, and all they that were in the tent laughed and cheered. But thereafter they fell to sweet speech and talked much, and he held her hand when the end of the feast was done; which was after this wise, that folk stood on the very lip of the river in one long row, hand in hand, and the loving-cup went down each row, and they cried healths to each other, and then lifted up their voices and shouted all together, and so undid the Mote and parted. And this time (and it was dark save for the fires flaring behind them) it was the maid that kissed Osberne; neither needed she, a tall damsel though she was, to stoop much thereto, for right big and tall he was of his years.

So then all went back each to his own home. And the winter wore away at Wethermel with nought to tell of.

乖戾约翰听见这支歌谣，只咬着牙暗暗咒骂，嘴里却一言不发。

这时两岸的教堂纷纷鸣起钟来，召人赴弥撒，众人都望弥撒去了。奥斯伯恩在汉子们中间坐了个好位子，说实在的，他不独当时，连这一整日都耳目齐张。弥撒已毕，两岸生起了炊火，支起了帐篷；过不多时便入席用饭；饭罢又歌唱了一阵，便到了饮酒的时辰，男女尽可能双双配了对——因为男人比女人多。只是除乖戾约翰外，人人都与奥斯伯恩相好，给他配的伴当是一位年方十七个冬天的俊俏少女；奥斯伯恩原已把在场的端丽女子个个细细打量过（因为他除了自家那两位女眷，几乎不曾见过旁的女人），待那可爱的少女卸下兜帽、褪去手套，不由得喜出望外。那少女却因他的英勇而对他存着羞怯——虽说他不过是个孩子——直到两人对饮过一两盏，他才鼓起胆量，伸手抚上她的脖颈，吻了她的双颊和嘴唇；她登时羞得满面玫瑰红，帐中众人都大笑喝彩。此后两人便说起甜蜜话来，絮絮谈了许多；宴席散时，他还牵着她的手。散席的规矩是这样：众人手携着手，在紧贴水滨之处站成一列长排，友爱之杯顺排相传，彼此高呼互祝康健，随后齐声呐喊，大会就此而散，众人各自作别。而这一回（四下里已黑，惟有身后篝火熊熊）是那少女吻了奥斯伯恩；她虽是位高挑的少女，却也无须怎样俯身俯就，因为论年纪，他已生得十分高大魁伟了。

于是众人各自归家去了。这个冬天便在韦瑟梅尔（Wethermel）平平淡淡地捱了过去，没有一件可记的事。
