## LXVI / 六十六

===Original===

The Lip of the Sundering Flood

When it was the morning and the sun shone through the house at Wethermel, those two arose and took each other by the hand, and no word they spake together, but went straight to the Sundering Flood, and there they walked slowly and daintily along the very lip thereof; and the day was the crown of all midsummer days, and it seemed to Elfhild that never on the other side had the flowers looked so fair and beautiful. So they went on till they came to the Bight of the Cloven Knoll, and there they looked across a while and yet said nothing. And Elfhild looked curiously toward that cave wherein Osberne first espied her, and she said: “How would it be if there were another one there?” He laughed and said: “There is not another one.” But she said: “Dost thou remember that game I played with the shepherd’s pipe, how that the sheep came all bundling towards me?” “Dearly I remember it,” said Osberne. “Now,” she said, “I will tell thee a thing. I have got the said pipe in my bosom now. It were good game to have it forth and try whether it has lost its power.” He said: “Well, try it.” She said: “Be there sheep about?” And there were sheep at no great distance.

And she drew forth the pipe and let her lips to it and played, and there came from it that very same sweet old tune that had joyed him so much long aforetime. But when they looked to see what would happen to the sheep, lo and behold they stirred not at all for the sweetness of the tune, nor made as if they heard it. So they laughed, albeit each of them, and Elfhild in especial, was a little grieved that the power had departed from the pipe. And they looked down towards the water, and Elfhild half thought to see a little brown man sitting at the door of the cave. But there was nothing; only it seemed to them both that there came up from the water a sound that said, Give it me back again. And Osberne said: “Didst thou hear that?” “Yea,” she said, “I thought I heard something. What shall we do?” Said he: “Why should he have his pipe back again?” She said: “Let us see what will happen if we cast it down to him.” “Good,” said Osberne, and he took the pipe, and as deftly as he might he cast it towards the mouth of the cave, but it fell a long way short.

But lo, as it was on the very point of striking the water it seemed that it was wafted up to the cave’s mouth, and it vanished away into the cave no slower than might have been looked for. And a faint voice came up from the water and said, I am pleased; good luck go with you.

So they sat down and pondered on these things a while, till at last Elfhild said: “Now will I tell thee a tale as in old days.” And he said: “That is good.” Then she began a tale which was sweet and pleasant, and little like to those terrible things that had happened to those two since they were sundered by the Flood. And it lasted long, and the afternoon was hot, and they were fain for coolness’ sake to creep into the shadow of certain bushes that grew a little off the lip of the Sundering Flood. There they rested them, and when the shadows began to lengthen, they arose and went back hand in hand to Wethermel as they had come.

===Chinese===

分离洪流的水唇

待到清晨，阳光照进韦瑟梅尔的宅屋，那两人便起身，彼此携了手，相对一言不发，径直往分离洪流去；到了那里，便贴着那道水唇缓缓而行，步履轻盈小心。这一日堪称历来仲夏诸日之冠；她只觉得，从前隔水相望时，对岸的花儿从来不曾显得这般鲜妍美丽。两人就这样一路行去，直到裂丘湾，在那里朝对岸眺望了一阵，仍旧相对无言。埃尔弗希尔德好奇地望向那座山洞——当初奥斯伯恩正是在那里头一回望见她的——开口说道：“倘若那里头还住着另一个，不知会怎样？”他笑道：“并没有另一个。”她却道：“你可记得我从前用牧笛玩的那个把戏？那些羊儿怎样一股脑儿全朝我拥来？”奥斯伯恩道：“我可是记得真切。”“喏，”她道，“我告诉你一件事。那支笛子如今就揣在我怀里呢。把它取出来试一试，看它的法力还在不在，倒是个好玩的把戏。”他道：“好，那就试试。”她道：“这左近可有羊儿么？”不远处正有一群羊。

于是她取出笛子，把嘴唇凑上去吹奏起来；笛中流出的，正是很久以前令他那样欢喜的那支甜蜜老调子。可当他们去看羊儿有何动静时，你道怎的，那曲子虽然甜美，羊儿竟纹丝不动，也不像是听见了的模样。两人便都笑了，只是彼此心里——埃尔弗希尔德尤其如此——都因笛子的法力已然消散而微微有些怅然。他们朝下望向水面，埃尔弗希尔德恍惚间只道会看见一个棕色的小人坐在山洞门口。却什么也没有；只是两人都仿佛觉得，水里传上来一个声音，说：“把它还给我吧。”奥斯伯恩道：“你听见了么？”她道：“听见了，我像是听见了点什么。咱们该怎么办呢？”他道：“他凭什么要讨回自己的笛子？”她道：“那便把它抛下去给他，看看会怎样。”奥斯伯恩道声“好”，接过笛子，用尽巧劲朝洞口掷去，却还差着老大一截。

但见那笛子眼看着就要落到水面，却仿佛被一股风托起，飘飘直上洞口，转眼便没入洞中，那份迅捷竟不输人意所料。随即水中传上来一个细微的声音，说道：“我心满意足；愿好运与你们同在。”

于是两人坐下来，对着这些奇事思量了一阵；末了埃尔弗希尔德说道：“如今我要像旧日那样，给你讲一个故事。”他道：“那再好不过。”她便讲起一个故事来，那故事甜美悦人，同他们两人自被洪流隔开以来所经历的种种可怖之事，几乎没有一点相像。故事讲了许久；午后天气炎热，两人贪着凉快，便挪进离分离洪流的水唇不远的几丛灌木的荫翳里。两人在那里安歇了身子；待到日影渐渐拉长，便起身手携手回韦瑟梅尔去，一如来时。
