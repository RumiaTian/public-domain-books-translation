## XIII What May Be Heard in a Greenroom / 第十三章 后台听得见的

===Original===

One evening in November a new ballet was given at the Opéra. Its production had been heralded in the manner which has found most favor with Parisian impressarii. The dead walls of the capital were not adorned with colored lithographs. The advertising sheets held no notice of the coming performance. But for several weeks previous the columns of the liveliest journals had teemed with items and discreet indiscretions.

Through these measures the curiosity of the Tout-Paris had been coerced afresh, and, when the curtain, after falling on the second act of the *Favorite* parted again before the new ballet, there was hardly a vacant seat in the house.

The box which Mr. Incoul had taken for the season was on what is known as the grand tier. It was roomy, holding eight comfortably and twelve if need be. But Maida, who was adverse to anything that suggested crowding, was always disinclined to ask more than five or six to share it with her, and on the particular evening to which allusion is made she extended her hospitality to but four people: Mr. and Mrs. Wainwaring and their daughter, New Yorkers like herself, and the Duc de la Dèche, a nobleman who served as figurehead to the Cercle des Capucines, and who, so ran the gossip, was anxious to effect an exchange of his coroneted freedom for the possession of Miss Wainwaring and a bundle or two of her father’s securities.

===Chinese===

十一月的一个晚上，歌剧院（the Opéra）上演了一出新芭蕾。它的推出所用的宣传之道，正是最讨巴黎主办人们欢心的那一种。首都一面面无窗的空白墙上，并没有彩色石版画的装点；广告报页上，也登不出这场演出的消息。只是在此前好几个星期里，几家最热闹的报纸专栏早已塞满了种种短讯，以及点到即止的“无意泄露”。

靠这些手段，整个上流巴黎（*Tout-Paris*）的好奇心被重新吊了起来；当大幕在歌剧《宠姬》（*Favorite*）第二幕后落下、又在新芭蕾开幕前提起时，剧场里几乎寻不出一个空座。

因库尔先生为整个演出季包下的包厢，在所谓的“大包厢层”（grand tier）。包厢宽绰，坐八个人舒舒服服，必要时挤十二个也行。但玛伊达对一切让人想到拥挤的东西都心怀抵触，总不肯邀超过五六个的人来同坐；在所说的这个晚上，她招待的客人只有四位：韦恩韦林先生和太太以及他们的女儿——和她一样都是纽约人——还有德·拉·德什公爵。这位贵族是嘉布遣会俱乐部（Cercle des Capucines）挂名的招牌人物；据流言说，他正急于做成一笔交易：拿他这顶冠冕之下的自由身，换韦恩韦林小姐到手，外带她父亲的一两捆证券。

During the entr’acte that preceded the ballet the box was invaded by a number of visitors, young men who were indebted to Maida for a dinner or a cup of tea and by others who hoped that such indebtedness was still in store for them; there came, too, a popular artist who wished to paint Maida’s portrait for the coming Salon and an author who may have had much cleverness, but who never displayed it to anyone.

As the invasion threatened to continue Mr. Incoul went out in the corridor, where he was presently joined by the duke, who suggested that they should visit the foyer. They made their way down the giant stair and turning through the lobby passed on through the corridor that circles the stalls until they reached a door guarded from non-subscribers by a Suisse about whose neck there drooped a medallioned chain of silver. By him the door was opened wide and the two men passed on through a forest of side scenes till the *foyer de la danse* was reached.

It was a spacious apartment, well lighted and lined with mirrors; the furniture was meagre, a dozen or more chairs and lounges of red plush. It was not beautiful, but then what market ever is? To Mr. Incoul it was brilliant as a café, and equally vulgar. From dressing-rooms above and beyond there came a stream of willowy girls. Few among them were pretty, and some there were whose faces were repulsive, but the majority were young; some indeed, the rats, as they are called, were mere children. Here and there was a mother of the Mme. Cardinal type, armed with an umbrella and prepared to listen to offers. As a rule, however, the young ladies of the ballet were quite able to attend to any little matter of business without maternal assistance. The Italian element was easily distinguishable. There was the ultra darkness of the eye, the faint umber of the skin, the richer vitality, in fact, of which the anemic daughters of Paris were unpossessed. And now and then the Gothic gutturals of the Spanish were heard, preceded by a wave of garlic.

在芭蕾开演之前的那段幕间休息（*entr’acte*）里，包厢让一群访客给占了：有欠过玛伊达一顿晚餐或一杯茶的年轻人，也有巴望这份人情日后还会轮到自己头上的；来的还有一位走红画家，想为下一届沙龙（Salon）画玛伊达的肖像；以及一位作家——此君或许满腹聪明，只是从不曾向任何人展露分毫。

眼见这场“入侵”大有蔓延下去之势，因库尔先生便踱到走廊里去，公爵随即也跟了出来，提议两人到后台的休息室走走。他们走下那道巨大的阶梯，穿过前厅拐了个弯，沿环绕正厅座席的走廊一路走去，来到一扇门前。守门的是一名瑞士门房（*Suisse*），防的是没订正厅季票的人；他的脖子上垂着一条坠满徽牌的银链。他把门大大敞开，两个人穿行在侧幕的森林里，一直走到舞蹈休息室（*foyer de la danse*）。

那是一间宽敞的厅堂，灯火通明，四壁镶着镜子；家具却颇简陋，十几把红长毛绒的椅子和躺榻而已。它算不上美——可话说回来，哪个市场又美过？在因库尔先生看来，它像咖啡馆一样辉煌，也一样俗气。楼上下和尽头的化妆间里，源源走出一批批身条柔软的姑娘。她们中间漂亮的寥寥无几，有几张脸甚至惹人嫌恶，但大多数人都年轻；有几个——就是人称“小耗子”（rats）的那些——干脆还是孩子。间或也有一位卡迪纳尔太太（Mme. Cardinal）式的母亲，手里撑一把伞，随时预备听取开价。不过照例说来，芭蕾舞团的小姐们全然能够不用母亲帮忙，自行料理任何一桩小小的生意。意大利血统一眼便能认出：眼珠黑到极处，皮肤带一抹淡淡的赭色，总之具备巴黎那些贫血的女儿们所欠缺的更丰盈的元气。不时还可听见西班牙语那种哥特式的喉音，先遣开道的是一股大蒜的浪潮。

That night the subscribers to the stalls were out in full force. There were Jew bankers in plenty, there were detachments from the Jockey and the Mirletons, one or two foreign representatives, a few high functionaries, the Minister of the Interior, and he of the Fine Arts, a member of the imperial family of Russia, a number of stock brokers and an Arab Sheik flanked by an interpreter.

Before the curtain rose, battalions of ballerines formed on the stage, and after the performance began they were succeeded by others, the first contingent returning to the dressing-rooms or loitering in the foyer. In this way there was a constant coming and going accompanied by the murmur of the spectators beyond and the upper notes of the flute.

Mr. Incoul was growing weary; he would have returned to the box, but he was joined by acquaintances that he had made at the club, Frenchmen mainly, friends of his companion, and presently he found himself surrounded by a group of *viveurs*, men about town, who had their Paris at the end of their gloves, and to whom it held no secrets. They had dined and talked animatedly in ends and remnants of phrases in a sort of verbal telegraphy; an exclamation helped by a gesture sufficing as often as not for the full conveyance of their thought.

这天晚上，订正厅季票的先生们倾巢出动。犹太银行家多的是；有赛马俱乐部（Jockey）和米勒通俱乐部（Mirletons）来的小队人马，有一两位外国代表，几名高级官员，内政部长和美术部长各一位，一位俄国皇室成员，若干股票经纪人，还有一位阿拉伯酋长，旁边站着给他传话的译员。

开演之前，一队队舞女已在台上列阵；演出开始后，另一批接了上去，头一批人便回化妆间去，或在休息室里闲荡。就这样人来人往川流不息，伴着幕外观众的低语和笛声的高音。

因库尔先生渐渐腻了；他本想回包厢去，却被他在俱乐部结识的几个熟人缠住——多半是法国人，是他同伴的朋友——不多时，他发觉自己已被一群*浪荡子*（*viveurs*）围在中央。这帮城中闲人把巴黎把玩于手套指尖之上，巴黎对他们毫无秘密可言。他们吃过晚饭，正以一种言语电报，拿半截句子和字眼的残片谈得热火朝天；往往一声感叹，再帮上一个手势，便足以把意思传个十足。

Mr. Incoul spoke French with tolerable ease, but having nothing of moment to say, he held his tongue, contenting himself with listening to the words of those who stood about him. And as he listened, the name of Mirette caught his ear. The programme had already informed him that it was she who was to assume the principal role in the new ballet, consequently he was not unfamiliar with it, but of the woman herself he knew nothing, and he listened idly, indifferent to ampler information. But at once his interest quickened; his immediate neighbor had mentioned her in connection with one whom he knew.

“They came up from Biarritz together,” he heard him say. “She went there with Chose, that Russian.”

“Balaguine?”

“Precisely.”

因库尔先生法文说得相当流利，但因无要事可说，便三缄其口，只管听周围那些人讲话。听着听着，米蕾特的名字钻进了他的耳朵。节目单早已告诉他，新芭蕾的主角便由她担任，因此这名字他并非不熟；至于这女子本人，他却一无所知，只懒懒地听着，并不在意打听得更详。可他的兴味立刻提了起来：紧挨着他的那人提到她时，把她同他认识的一个名字连在了一起。

“他们是一起从比亚里茨来的，”他听见那人说。“她是跟某君（Chose）去的，就是那个俄国人。”

“巴拉金（Balaguine）？”

“正是。”

“Ah, as to that﻿—” And the first speaker shrugged his shoulders.

A rustle circled through the foyer, men stood aside and nodded affably. The lights took on a fairer glow. “Stay,” murmured the second speaker, “she is there.”

Through the parting crowd Mirette passed with a carriage such as no queen, save perhaps Semiramis, ever possessed. She moved from the hips, her body was erect and unswayed. It was the perfection of artificial grace. Her features were not regular, but there was an expression in them that stirred the pulse. “*Je suis l’Amour*,” she seemed to say, and to add “*prends garde à toi*.” As she crossed the room men moistened their lips, and when she had gone they found them still parched.

一阵窸窣声在休息室里绕行，男人们闪到一旁，和气地点头。灯光也添了一层更柔美的光辉。“慢着，”第二个说话的人低声道，“她在那儿呢。”

米蕾特穿过分开的人群，那步态仪容，除了塞弥拉弥斯（Semiramis），怕是任何王后都不曾有过。她走路从胯上发力，上身笔直，纹丝不摇。那是人工的优雅登峰造极之作。她的五官并不端正，可眉目间有一种叫脉搏加快的神情。“*Je suis l’Amour*（我是爱神），”她仿佛这样说，接着又补一句“*prends garde à toi*（你可要当心）。”她穿过厅堂时，男人们都舔了舔嘴唇；待她走远，他们发觉嘴唇还是干的。

Mr. Incoul followed her with his eyes. She had not left him unimpressed, but his impression differed from that of his neighbors. In her face his shrewdness had discerned nothing but the animal and the greed of unsatiated appetites. He watched her pass, and stepped from the group in which he had been standing that he might the better follow her movements.

From the foyer she floated on into a side scene, yet not near enough to the stage to be seen by the audience. A few machinists moved aside to let her pass, and as they did so Mr. Incoul saw Lenox Leigh. It was evident that he had been waiting there for her coming. There was a scarf about her neck, and as the young man turned to greet her, she took it off and gave it into his keeping. They whispered together. Beyond, Mr. Incoul could see the tulle of the ballet rising and subsiding to the rhythm of the orchestra. Then came a sudden blare of trumpets, the measure swooned, and as it recovered again the ballet had faded to the back of the stage. Abruptly, as though sprung from a trapdoor, a régisseur appeared, and at a signal from him Mirette, with one quick backward stroke to her skirt, bounded from the side scene and fluttered down to the footlights amid a crash and thunder of applause.

Mr. Incoul had heard and seen enough. His mind was busy. He felt the need of fresh air and of solitude. He turned into the corridor and from there went through the vestibule until he reached an outer door, which he swung open and passed out into the night. He was thinly clad, in evening dress, and the air was chilly, but he thought nothing of his dress nor of the warmth or chill of the air. He walked up and down before the building with his head bent and his hands behind his back. A *camelot* offered him a pack of transparent cards, a vender of programmes pestered him to buy, but he passed them unheeding. For fully half an hour he continued his walk, and when he reentered the box, Maida, who of late had given much attention to his moods, noticed that his face was flushed, and that about his lips there played the phantom of a smile.

因库尔先生目送着她。她并非没有给他留下印象，只是这印象与身边众人不同。凭着他的精明，他从她脸上只看出兽性，和永不餍足的胃口所生的贪求。他望着她走过去，便从站着的人群里跨出来，好把她的一举一动看得更真切。

她出了休息室，又飘进一处侧幕，那里离舞台尚远，观众看不见。几名置景工闪身让路；就在他们让开的一瞬，因库尔先生看见了伦诺克斯·利。他分明是在那里候她前来。她颈间围着一条纱巾；年轻人转身迎她时，她把纱巾解下，交给他保管。两人低低说着体己话。再远处，因库尔先生能望见芭蕾舞群的纱裙随乐队的节拍起落。忽然铜号齐鸣，乐句一阵晕厥；等它缓过神来，群舞已淡成舞台深处的背景。一位舞台监督（régisseur）像从活板门里弹出来似的蓦地现身；他一打手势，米蕾特向后迅捷一拂裙裾，从侧幕一跃而出，翩然飞落到脚灯前，掌声轰雷般炸响。

因库尔先生听得够了，也看得够了。他的心念转个不停。他需要新鲜空气，需要独自一人。他转身进了走廊，从那里穿过前厅，直到一扇临街的门前；他把门推开，走进夜色里。他衣衫单薄，只穿着晚礼服，夜气又寒，可他既不想身上的衣着，也不管空气的冷暖。他在剧院前来回踱步，垂着头，两手抄在背后。一个街头小贩（*camelot*）向他兜售一包透明纸牌，一个卖节目单的纠缠他购买，他都恍若无闻地走过。他足足踱了半个钟头；待回到包厢，近来对他的心境颇为留意的玛伊达，看出他脸上泛着红，唇边浮着一抹幽灵般的笑意。
