## XX Leaving the Schoolmaster, the Pythagorean Sets Up for Himself / 第二十章 告别校长先生（the Schoolmaster），毕达哥拉斯门徒（the Pythagorean）自立门户
===Original===

During the progress of the events which have hitherto occupied my narrative, I had become a graduate of my college. Willing for a while to defer the prosecution of more immediately professional studies, I cast about for some employment which for a year might engage a portion of my efforts, and leave, at the same time, a reasonable amount of leisure for private reading. As is the case with so many of our newly-fledged American alumni, my choice fell upon the assumption of the pedagogic purple. There were doubtless, somewhere in the States, candidates for induction into the mysteries of the Greek and Latin tongues﻿—some youths of promise who burned for an acquaintance with the arts of address, and who would not scorn to receive, even from the hands of an own countryman, the crumbs of literature which fall from the Gallic table. If my horoscope had not failed me, I could find them out.
Accordingly, at the bar of my college, whither petitions for instruction very frequently came in from the benighted world, I lodged an application for the most eligible situation of the kind above stated which should present itself. Before long a letter reached me, offering a post of teachership, situated somewhere between the Hudson and Fort Laramie, in a village glorified with some name of Epic valor, mighty in the appanage of ten dwelling-houses and a post-office, and, like all places sanctified by the presence of the educational genius, “refined, salubrious, and highly religious.” As to the first item in this latter statement, there was every reason to be satisfied of its truth, since the writer of the letter was evidently a gentleman, and, to judge from the size of the place, he was a very large integral portion of its population. Upon the second point it was rather more difficult to be assured, since any number of deaths possible to the dimensions of the village might have occurred there without their wave of agitation reaching the shore that acknowledges the jurisdiction of bills of mortality. Finally came the question of “highly-religiousness.” On this head, nothing could have given my doubts a more decisive quietus than the fact that the community wanted a teacher, since, just then looking through the Lorraine-glass of enthusiasm for a chosen occupation, I saw a peculiar force and beauty in the words, “Science, the handmaid of Religion.”

在迄今为止占据我叙述篇幅的种种事态演进之中，我已经从大学顺利毕业。鉴于我尚愿暂时搁置更具针对性的专业深造，我开始物色一份能在一年间占用我部分精力、同时又能为私人研读留出合理闲暇的工作。正如我们许多初出茅庐的美国大学毕业生一样，我的选择落在了披上教书育人的紫袍之上。在美利坚各州之中，毫无疑问正有众多渴望探求希腊语与拉丁语奥秘的莘莘学子——一些渴望通晓演说口才之术、且不耻于哪怕从同胞之手接纳自高卢餐桌上掉落的文学碎屑的有志青年。倘若我的星盘未曾欺骗我，我定能将他们寻得。

因此，在我的大学事务处——那里经常收到来自蒙昧世界的求教请愿——我针对上述类型的最合适职位递交了申请。不久，一封信函寄达我手中，向我提供了一份教席职位，地点位于哈德逊河（Hudson）与拉勒米堡（Fort Laramie）之间的某处，坐落在一座被赋予了某种史诗般英武之名的村庄里，坐拥十栋民宅与一处邮局的雄厚排场，正如所有被教育之神圣光所照耀的所在一样，号称“典雅、宜居且极具宗教虔敬”。关于最后这一声明中的第一项，完全有理由对其真实性感到满意，因为写信者显然是一位绅士，且从该村庄的规模来判断，他本人便构成了全村人口中极大的一部分。关于第二项，则相对较难确证，因为即便在村庄规模所允许的范围内发生过任何数量的死亡事件，其震荡波澜也未必能扩散触及到承认死亡公报管辖范围的文明彼岸。最后则是“极具宗教虔敬”的论断。关于这一点，最能令我的疑虑烟消云散的莫过于该社区亟需一名教师的事实——因为在彼时彼刻，透过对所选职业狂热激情的洛林镜（Lorraine-glass），我在“科学乃宗教之婢女”这一格言中看到了非凡的力量与美感。

Yet one thing there was which stood as a slight obstacle in the way of accepting the position. I had determined, for the year to come, to be independent for a support of all aid save my own exertions. Entire self-sustenance was a very dear project with me. Could I hope for it there?
My correspondent informed me that no very great pecuniary inducements could be offered, but seductively added that, to a young man of excellent principles, who desired to establish himself as a moral centre in the community, no opening could be more promising. As he did not go on to advise me whether, in his portion of the country, “moral centres” were gratuitously fed, lodged, and clothed, besides being generously presented, as a slight token of popular esteem, with their laundry-bills, fuel, lights, and stationery, I concluded not to close with his offer, thus forever losing, for the basest of earthly considerations, the priceless opportunity of radiating circular waves of an unctuous excellence through it is impossible to tell how large an area of uninhabited timber-region. Whether any sufficiently self-sacrificing incumbent has been found to fill the rejected vacancy, from want of data is uncertain; if not, the place with the Homeric name wanders in heathen ignorance to this day.
Another application which came to me, seeming in all points satisfactory, was accepted. In the town of W﻿—﻿—, in the State of New York, a cry had gone up for a teacher, who might be absorbent as well as radiant, and one, moreover, who might indulge the hope of moderate leisure for his own self-disciplinary purposes.
There, as I began arranging matters for my departure from home, I flattered myself that a stated occupation should absorb me from regrets over the loss of my old indulgence; quiet, books, and a regular life should create in me a new stimulus and energy. The department of pedagoguery over which I was to be installed was congenial to long-consolidated tastes﻿—the ancient and the English classics. Thus I should gradually emerge out of shadows into a being with new motives, and by moderate cares blunt the pains of progress. How far I harvested my hope the sequel will show.

然而，却有一件事构成了接纳该职位的些许阻碍。我已然下定决心，在未来的一年里，除了自身的辛劳之外彻底独立、不仰赖任何外在资助。完全的自给自足于我而言是一项极其珍视的夙愿。我能指望在那里实现它吗？

我的通信人告知我，对方无法提供多么优厚的薪酬诱惑，但却极其诱人地补充道：对于一位品行端正、渴望在社区中确立自身为“道德中心”的青年才俊而言，没有哪处机缘能比这里更为前途无量。由于他并未进一步向我阐明在他所在的那片国土上，“道德中心”在作为受人敬重的微薄表意而慷慨免除洗衣账单、取暖燃料、照明灯火与文具花销之外，是否能平白享有免费的食宿与衣着供给，我断然决定拒绝他的提议，从而仅仅出于凡俗最鄙陋的现实考量，便永远错失了向一片难以言喻其辽阔的无人荒莽林区辐射出一圈圈油然圣洁卓越之光的无价良机。由于缺乏史料数据，是否有某位怀具充分牺牲奉献精神的接替者填补了这一被拒的空缺尚不得而知；倘若未曾寻得，那座冠以荷马史诗般英名的所在，恐怕至今仍在一片蛮荒蒙昧中随波逐流。

寄给我的另一份求贤申请，因在各个维度上皆令人满意，被我慨然应允。在纽约州的W镇，传来了一阵对教师的渴求呼声——要求此人既能如海绵般汲取新知，亦能如明灯般启迪后学，此外，更能为自身的修身自律抱有一丝适度闲暇的期许。

在彼处，正当我开始筹备离家行装之际，我自欺欺人地坚信：一份规律的职业定能将我从对昔日旧癖丧失的懊悔中彻底抽离出来；宁静、典籍与井然有序的生活，定能在我的体内催生出崭新的动力与蓬勃的生机。我即将执掌的教席部门，恰好契合我根深蒂固的长久志趣——古希腊罗马与英格兰经典文学。就这样，我将逐步走出往昔的阴霾，蜕变为一个拥有全新动机的生灵，并通过适度的操劳来抚平前行阵痛中的创伤。我的这一期冀究竟收获了几何，随后的经历将昭示一切。

Having reached the scene of my labors, I found myself associated with a teacher who, like myself, was a newcomer, yet not, like myself, a neophyte in the profession, for he had grown venerable in the priesthood of Minerva, having, in all probability, during his previous life, offered up numerous hecatombs of youthful victims, both male and female, upon her altar. At the same time that I congratulated myself upon possessing the aid of his experience, I discovered that I must look elsewhere for congenial sympathies, since he was one of those persons whose metal is not annealed. In youth he had indulged a happy disposition, but now saw the folly of it. Through some fault of my own early training, I was unable to discover the necessary connection between sanctity and acridity, a heart like Enoch and a face like Sphinx.
Yet upon external sympathy I did not expect to be very dependent. The institution in which I was resident offered that invaluable advantage, a large and well-selected library, where I hoped to find all those choice attachments which from without my position might deny me.
In a good library how swiftly time melts away! Not merely in the sense of its rapid passage through our absorption in other interests, but as an element in any consideration, it becomes entirely neglected. In practical business the present is our only actuality; the past has been cast down like a ladder whose rounds have helped us up to a height whence we never again expect to descend. Among books, all temporal successions are obliterated; Plato and Coleridge walk arm-in-arm; genial Chaucer and loving Elia shake hands; with them, with all, we stand enraptured upon the same plane of time, in one age, the ceaseless age of the communion of souls. Well did Heinsius say, as he locked himself into the library of Leyden, *Nunc sum in gremio saeculorum!*﻿—“Now I am in the lap of eternity!”
But gradually the increasing pressure of duties connected with my new vocation more and more deprived me of leisure for enjoying any other literature than that of textbooks.

抵达我的履职之地后，我发现自己与另一位教师共事；他同我一样是初来乍到，但在这门职业中却绝非如我这般的生手，因为他在智慧女神密涅瓦（Minerva）的祭司祭坛前已然熬出了白发苍苍的资历，极大概率在他过往的岁月里，曾在女神的祭坛上献祭过无数男女青年的百牲祭品。在庆幸自己能借由他的老练阅历获益的同时，我亦发觉自己必须去别处寻觅志趣相投的共鸣，因为他是那种未经回火淬炼的顽固金石之徒。在早年他曾沉溺于快活随和的性情，如今却将其视作彻头彻尾的愚妄。或许由于我自己早期教养中的某种欠缺，我始终无法在那份神圣与刻薄之间、在如以诺（Enoch）般的心肠与如斯芬克斯（Sphinx）般的面孔之间，找出任何必然的因果关联。

然而，对于外部世界的同情相恤，我本就未曾指望过分依赖。我所任职的机构提供了无价的优渥馈赠——一座规模宏大、甄选精良的图书馆，我指望在其中寻觅到那些外在处境恐将剥夺我的一切至珍羁绊。

在藏书丰富的图书馆里，岁月消融得何其迅疾啊！不仅体现在因沉浸于其他兴致之中而浑然不觉其飞逝，而且作为任何考量中的一种维度，它被彻底抛诸脑后。在务实的事功中，唯有“当下”才是我们唯一的真实存在；而“过往”则宛若一架梯子般被弃置于地，其梯级曾助我们攀爬上了一处我们再也不指望跌落的高峰。在典籍之中，一切时间的先后序幕皆被荡涤抹平；柏拉图与柯勒律治挽手同行；风趣的乔叟与深情的伊利亚（Elia，查尔斯·兰姆）抚掌言欢；同他们、同所有的圣哲一道，我们在同一个时间层面上神魂颠倒地伫立在永恒的时代之中——那个灵魂交融神会的无休无止时代。海因修斯（Heinsius）在将自己反锁于莱顿大学图书馆时，由衷赞叹道：*Nunc sum in gremio saeculorum!*——“如今我正置身于万古的怀抱之中！”

然而渐渐地，与我的新教职相绑定的日益沉重的职责重压，愈发剥夺了我除教科书之外享受任何其他文学作品的闲暇。

Long after the last noisy foot had pattered down the front steps of the school building did my table groan with incorrigible exercises which demanded correction, one leaf of which, laid upon the grave of any worthy﻿—Molière, for instance﻿—who spoke the language which it assassinated, would have brought up as deep a groan from the depths below as when the mandrake is uprooted.
I had promised myself regular habits; but the wanderer who was so unfortunate or so eccentric as to be shelterless at that hour, might have seen, at two or three o’clock of almost every morning, the light of my lamp shining through one of the tall windows that looked upon the street. Not that I rose early, but that I retired early﻿—in the morning. It was not the mere sense of duty and responsibility which impelled me to such labors for the school, although, indeed, these had their just, perhaps their exorbitant weight with me. An element more selfish entered into the consideration﻿—the dread of being haunted on the morrow by unappeased ghosts of business. The accumulative nature of work distressed me; the slightest thing left unfinished at the close of one day added itself to the labors of the next, and it had grown mightily during the night. There are some people so constituted that they can not slur matters if they would. No one else may notice the mint, anise, and cummin which they have forborne to tithe, but they can no more themselves overlook the deficiency than if they had neglected the weightier matters of the law.
It will be easily understood that late hours, hard work, and an almost total cessation from bodily exercise were not the best means that could have been taken to restore tone and elasticity to a mind struggling with the horrors of an abandoned stimulus. Without cares of some kind, I had doubtless been at this time a most unhappy being; yet, under such pressure as I then felt, an overtasked mind had no opportunity to recover itself, but rather grew sensitive daily to the loss of its former support. Perhaps, however, even such a state of things was better than an absence of all absorbing employment; for, although I dreaded a return to hashish as an upright man dreads the violation of his most sacred oath, I had not reached a point at which I could utterly execrate the drug.

在最后一声喧闹的脚步声跑下校舍门前的台阶许久之后，我的案头依然在为堆积如山、亟待批改的顽劣习作而痛苦呻吟；只要从中抽出一张薄页，置于任何一位曾操持被其肆意践踏谋杀之语言的文豪——譬如莫里哀（Molière）——的墓碑之上，定能从九泉深处引来一声深沉至极的凄厉悲叹，恰如曼德拉草（mandrake）被连根拔起时发出的哀号。

我曾向自己许诺要保持规律的作息；然而凡不幸或因古怪脾性而在这个时刻无家可归的夜行人，几乎在每天清晨的两三点钟，皆能望见我的灯火正透过一扇俯瞰街道的高大窗户向外投射着光芒。这并非因为我起得早，而是因为我睡得早——在清晨就寝。促使我为学校如此废寝忘食劳作的，绝非仅仅是对职责与担当的责任感，尽管这些在我心中确实占据着合情合理、或许甚至是过度的分量。一种更为自私的要素潜入了这个考量之中——对次日被未竟事务的冤魂索命纠缠的深切恐惧。工作那不断累积滚雪球的特质令我寝食难安；一日终了之际遗留下的最微不足道之物，定会叠加进次日的重负之中，且在夜色中蓬勃滋长。世间有些人的天性便是如此：哪怕他们想敷衍了事也绝难做到。旁人或许根本察觉不到他们未曾献上十分之一的薄荷、茴香与莳萝，但他们自己却绝无法忽视这份缺失，恰如他们懈怠了律法中更为重大的关键纲领一般。

不难理解的是，通宵达旦、严酷劳作、以及几乎完全停止肉体锻炼，绝非让一颗正同戒断刺激物的恐怖纠缠搏斗的心灵重获健康基调与弹性的良策。倘若毫无某种操劳羁绊，在彼时彼刻我毫无疑问将成为一个极其悲惨的可怜虫；然而，在当时我所承受的这般万钧重压之下，过度透支的心智根本没有任何机会自我复原，反倒日益对丧失昔日的支撑变得更加敏感脆弱。然而，或许即便这般局面，也比全然缺乏吸引注意力的繁杂事务要好得多；因为，尽管我对重返大麻的畏惧恰如正人君子畏惧违背自身最神圣的誓言那般，但我却尚未达到能够彻底咒骂痛斥该药物的崇高境界。

The only feat of righteous indignation which was then possible was to think ill of it, as the lover of a faithless mistress whom he must abandon, or as the patriot of his fatherland, swayed by vile rulers, when, “fallen upon evil times,” he flies it in voluntary exile. Unemployed with daily and perplexing duties, I might have heard the former siren-voice floating into my careless quiet, and, step by step, have been almost unconsciously led back into the old snares.
As it was, the fascinations of the past were hard enough to resist. If ever for a moment I granted myself leisure to sit still and think﻿—if, especially, I resigned myself with closed eyes to the train of meditations set in motion by good music, I was infallibly borne back into the hashish world, and placed face to face with its now irretrievable glories. In quick flashes the old empurpled heights for a moment broke upon me, or amid cloud battalions in their rainbow armor I floated through a tremendous heaven. Or the far windings of some wondrous river allured me into the luxuriant shadows which trembled over its brink, and I sighed for an instant with an unutterable yearning as I thought that its waves were never more to upbear my shallop of gramarye. The embodied temptation of exquisite houris swam in ethereal dance down a garden of Gul: never more were their rosy arms to embrace me. Grand temples reared their spotless pediments into a sapphire sky; a lake that answered back in its own hue the look of heaven, kissed, dimpling with a fairy laughter, the steps that ascended to their portals﻿—portals eternally barred on me. And sometimes, more solemnly alluring than all these, for an instant I caught a view of that light wherein I had of old read the sublime secrets of things by unbearable apocalypse. At such a season, well﻿—oh! unspeakably well was it that hundreds of miles stretched between me and the nearest box of hashish, for, had I possessed the means, I should have rushed to the indulgence, though it were necessary to swim a whirlpool on the way.
I made acquaintances at W﻿—﻿— who could play cunningly upon an instrument, that universal one, the piano, especially. Knowing that there was no possibility of yielding to the allurement, I, often as possible, had them play for me, while I sat almost unconscious of anything outward, abandoning myself to music-inspired visions.

彼时唯一可行的义愤壮举，不过是对它心怀怨怼——宛如一个被迫抛弃背信弃义情妇的情郎，抑或一位身处恶君统治之下的爱国志士，当“生逢乱世”之际，唯有自愿流亡远遁。若非终日被繁冗复杂的教务所占据，我定会听闻昔日塞壬（siren）般的魅惑仙乐飘入我漫不经心的宁静之中，并在一阵不知不觉中，一步步被重新诱导入旧日的罗网陷阱之中。

即便在眼下的处境中，往昔的诱惑魔力亦已足够令人难以抵挡。每当我允许自己片刻闲暇静坐沉思——尤其是闭合双眸任由心神沉沦在优美音乐所唤起的一连串冥想遐思之中时，我定会被无可挽回地带回大麻的世界，并同它那如今已然无法挽回的万道荣光面面相对。在道道迅疾的闪光中，昔日的紫霞之巅瞬息间向我倾泻而下，抑或置身于身披虹彩战甲的云霄军旅之中，我在巍峨浩瀚的天宇间悠然漂游。抑或某条奇妙长河的蜿蜒远景诱惑我步入在其水畔震颤的浓郁树荫之中，而当我思索着它的波涛将永不再托举起我的魔法小舟（shallop of gramarye）时，我便在无可言状的渴慕中发出一声由衷的长叹。绝色仙女（houris）具象化的诱惑在波斯蔷薇园（garden of Gul）中跳着轻灵飘渺的舞蹈翩跹而过：她们那玫瑰色的双臂将永不再将我拥入怀中。巍峨的神庙将毫无瑕疵的三角楣山墙高耸插进蓝宝石般的苍穹；一片倒映着天容神韵的湖泊，伴随着仙子般的浅笑涟漪，轻吻着拾级而上通往殿门的层层石阶——而那些殿门，却已对我永恒闭锁。有时，比所有这些更为庄严肃穆、更为摄人心魄的，乃是我在瞬息之间窥见的那束光芒——往昔我曾在这束光中，借由令人难以承受的天启启示，通读了造化万物的崇高秘奥。在那些时刻，万幸——啊！委实是无可言状的万幸：在我与最近的一盒大麻之间，横亘着数百英里的千山万水；因为倘若我手头拥有药物，即便中途必须游过狂暴的漩涡，我也定会不顾一切地狂奔向那份沉沦。

有一天，为了修理窗扇，我曾坐在窗台上，身躯有一部分探出窗外。此后的睡梦便被一场关于一栋高耸入云超自然楼宇的异象所惊吓，我伫立在其最高层的窗台之上，一只手死死抠住突出的檐口，另一只手却试图去完成某项不可能达成的任务，从而无法协助另一只手支撑身体。而那檐口却依然在不断崩解碎裂。我伸手抓向一处崭新的凸起。那一处亦随之崩塌；又抓向另一处，再次在我的掌心断裂。这种危局通过几种途径被推向危机的高潮。有时凭借一股强烈的冲力我将身体荡回室内，梦境的轨迹随之变幻。有时，在我不知如何达成的情况下，异象彻底消散无踪。有一次，我所附身伫立的整栋楼宇从地基到顶石在刹那间陷入了一片熊熊烈火之中；为了避免更为残酷的火刑折磨，我纵身跃向了更为慈悲的坠亡之途，在惊醒之时，发觉自己正躺在地板上，陷入了我先前述及过的那种高烧滚烫状态之中。

那一夜我再也无法入眠。拂晓时分，我躺下身来经历了一个钟头纷乱不宁的浅睡，随后再次苏醒步入了一个同黑夜一样令人畏惧胆寒的白昼。
