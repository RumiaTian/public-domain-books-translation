## XVI An Oath in the Forum of Madness / 第十六章 疯狂法庭中的誓言
===Original===

Having been threatened many times with an utter isolation from human kind, it now became my practice, the moment that I began to feel the hashish change come over me, to run for sympathy to some congenial friend, and thus assure myself that the sentence had not yet been carried out. I entered his room. I told him of my state; and, before increasing delirium had any power to pervert my thoughts, pledged him to care for me, never to leave me, always to interest himself in my welfare to the end. Frequently this step prevented any undercurrent of horror from breaking up through my delightful tides of vision. Frequently, when I beheld the fearful Afreet invade the sanctity of my rejoicing with drawn cimeter, was that remembered pledge to me as the ring of Abdaldar, and straightway

> “There ceased his power; his lifted arm,

Suspended by the spell,

Hung impotent to strike.”

The penal renunciation of me by God and man was the grand prevailing shadow which now lowered about the horizon of my visions, and thrice happy was I when, in this way, I could keep it from blackening the whole sky. I mean more by this word “blackening” than mere metaphor, for, fully awake and at unclouded noonday, I have seen both heavens and air grow sable suddenly with a supernatural eclipse, and I walked by no beacon save that of fiery eyes which “glared upon me through the darkness.”
Yet the spell was not always powerful. There occurred seasons when I was beyond the power of man, and, as I thought, also an outcast from man’s league with God. Man could not, God would not, keep faith with me.
In the ecstasy of a serene uplifting I came one afternoon to the room of an acquaintance who had often expressed a wish to witness the hashish state in some walk with me while I was under the influence of the drug. By the pledge of sympathy I bound him, and felt assured﻿—doubly assured; for, as he prepared to accompany me out, without premonition there flashed into my mind that grand line of Festus,

> “ ’Tis not my will that evil be immortal.”

由于屡次受到与全人类彻底隔绝的威胁，如今每当我开始感到大麻的变幻袭来之时，我的惯常做法便是立刻奔向某位志趣相投的密友寻求同情安慰，以此向自己证实那道判决尚未真正付诸实施。我跨入他的房间。我将自己的处境如实相告；并且，在不断加深的谵妄尚无力扭曲我的思想之前，恳求他立誓照顾我、绝不离开我、始终关切我的安危直至最后一刻。这番举措往往能成功防范任何恐怖的暗流从我那令人心旷神怡的异象潮汐中破土而出。不知有多少次，当我望见狰狞的恶魔（Afreet）手执出鞘的弯刀侵入我欢乐的圣殿时，昔日那份誓言于我而言宛若阿卜杜勒达尔之戒（the ring of Abdaldar），刹那间

> “他的魔力烟消云散；他抬起的手臂，

因咒语的禁锢而悬在半空，

无力再落下劈砍。”

被上帝与世人施以惩罚性的唾弃抛弃，乃是如今笼罩在我异象地平线之上的主要阴霾；而每当能以此种方式阻止它将整片天空染黑之时，我便感到三生有幸。我对“染黑”一词的使用远非单纯的隐喻修辞，因为在完全清醒、万里无云的正午时分，我曾目睹诸天与空气在一场超自然的日食中骤然化作子夜般的漆黑，而我漫步前行唯有依凭那一双双“在黑暗中朝我凶狠瞪视”的灼热双眼充当向导。

然而，这道护身符并非每次都能奏效。有时我超越了凡人所能庇护的极限，且在我看来，亦成了被世人同上帝的盟约所逐出的弃儿。人不能救我，上帝亦不愿向我恪守信义。

在一次恬静出尘的狂喜升腾之中，一个午后我来到了某位熟人的房间，他曾多次表达过希望在某次散步中亲眼见证我处于大麻影响下的状态。凭借同情相助的承诺我同他缔结了誓约，心中感到笃定——双倍的笃定；因为正当他准备陪同我出门之际，毫无征兆地，我脑海中猛然闪过了《费斯图斯》（*Festus*）中那句壮丽的诗行：

> “让罪恶永存绝非我的本意。”

Not only did this line suggest to me a great future of good and happiness throughout my hashish eternity, but I saw the triumphant reign of right established forever among men. A sublime emancipation from the thraldom of the ages had been declared to earth, and in visible and audible joy Creation leaped and sang. Should I not, then, be happy, since God had pronounced it? I had no fears. Taking the arm of my friend, I passed into the open air.
We had hardly gone fifty feet when I heard the dreadful voice distinctly speak to me: “This is an imaginative man; if you are happy, he will powerfully sympathize with you; he will be fascinated, he will become like yourself a hashish-eater. To save him from this, it is necessary that you should become an exemplar of agony. Are you content?” Knowing well what should ensue, aware of the tortures that lay prepared in the intimate abyss of the hashish hell, could I, as aught less than a God, say “Yes?” Unable to bring myself to this height of superhuman heroism, I only forced my lips to murmur, “The will of God be done.”
Then the voice answered, “Horribly shalt thou suffer, suffer, suffer, more than tongue can tell, more than thou hast dreamed.”
I clenched my fists, I shut my teeth, I nerved my whole being for the flood of agony which was about to pour upward on me from the depths. I felt within me the prophecy of such pangs as would bring me to the very portals of nothingness.
The sentence began to be fulfilled. From the fence beside which we walked came hot blasts, as from a furnace, and, looking at its base, I saw fiery rifts in the ground whence the tornado issued. I withered to a parchment sack, which bound in my heart as the sensitive fuel for more torments.
And now through that heart glided a delicate saw, of innumerable blades, each sharpened to the ultimate thinness of steel, and each glowing with a red heat. Slowly as a marble-saw the dreadful engine passed back and forth, hissing through the writhing muscle, and, as I pressed my hand upon my breast, it was scorched by the intense heat of the laminae. From the walls of houses, black talons darted forth to clutch my skirts; they left a scar like the touch of moxa. And still I burned unquenchably.
For a while I kept silence, shutting my mouth with Promethean self-control.

这句诗行不仅向我昭示了在整个大麻永恒中充满至善与幸福的宏大未来，而且我目睹了正义的凯旋统治在世人中间得以永恒确立。向大地宣告了自万古奴役中得享崇高解放的纶音，在目之所见、耳之所闻的欢欣雀跃之中，整个受造世界欢腾高歌。既然上帝已然亲自宣告，我难道不该幸福欢愉吗？我毫无畏惧。挽着朋友的手臂，我迈步走入了户外的新鲜空气中。

我们前行了不过五十英尺，我便听见那个可怕的声音在我耳畔清晰地说道：“此人极富想象力；倘若你幸福欢畅，他将深深为你所感染共鸣；他将为之神魂颠倒，变得同你一样沦为食大麻者。为了拯救他免入此途，你必须成为受难酷刑的典范。你可愿意？”深知接下来将要发生何事，明晓在大麻地狱的最深渊底所筹备下的种种酷刑，身为凡人而非神明的我，怎能吐露出一声“愿意”？因无力将自己提升至这般超人英雄主义的高峰，我唯有强逼着双唇喃喃吐露出一句：“愿上帝的旨意成就。”

随即那声音回应道：“你将承受可怖的折磨，折磨，无尽的折磨，远超舌簧所能尽述，远超你梦想所能企及。”

我紧握双拳，咬紧牙关，全身紧绷迎接即将从深渊深处向我喷涌而来的无边痛苦洪流。我内心感受到了即将把我推向虚无边缘的剧痛预兆。

判决开始付诸执行。从我们身旁漫步经过的篱笆边，喷薄出了滚滚热浪，宛若出自熔炉；俯视其根基，我望见地面上裂开了一道道烈火翻滚的裂隙，风暴正从中呼啸喷涌。我枯萎成了一具羊皮纸般的皮囊，将我的心脏死死禁锢其中，充当承受更多折磨的敏感薪柴。

紧接着，一柄有着无数锯齿的精巧钢锯滑过了那颗心脏，每一道锯齿皆被磨砺至钢铁的至极纤薄，且每一道皆被烧至通红透亮。如同大理石切割锯般迟缓，那架可怖的机器来回拉锯，在抽搐痉挛的肌肉中发出咝咝的煎熬声；当我把手按在胸口时，手掌被那些薄刃的滚烫炽热生生灼伤。从房屋的墙壁上，伸出一只只漆黑的利爪撕扯我的衣角；它们留下了宛若艾灸（moxa）烙烫般的斑斑焦痕。而我依然在不可熄灭的烈焰中受煎熬。

有一阵子我保持着死寂的沉默，以普罗米修斯式的自控力紧闭双唇。

Not only did my acquired habit of suffering speechlessly restrain me, but my pride could not endure the thought of acknowledging to him who walked by my side the vengeful infliction which had fallen upon me, in place of the mantle of rapture which my promise had prepared him to see.
The voice then said, “Confess! confess!” In desperation, I set my lips like a vice, and in my soul replied, “No! I will not!”
“Wilt thou not confess?” wrathfully the voice returned.
“Thou shalt then know bitterer agonies.”
Now in my brain, moved by the same hellish machinery which was driving the saws through my heart, a murderous red-hot auger began turning round. Its speed increased, and with it a tremendous roar that shook my being. In every nerve I was agonized with an agony such as no martyr can ever have known. Head and heart both flaming, both riven by steel, the heavens looking wrathfully down, the earth opening up dreadful views of her demon-peopled deeps. Oh, here was a hell in which how could I live!
To the man at my side I whispered my confession. I told him all. I revealed to him the reasons of my punishment. I adjured him by all my own immortal tortures never to tamper with the insane spell.
And then, in piteous accents, I besought him to put out my fire.
To the first restaurant at hand we hastened. Passing in, I called for that only material relief which I have ever found for these spiritual sufferings﻿—something strongly acid. In the East the form in use is sherbet; mine was very sour lemonade. A glass of it was made ready, and with a small glass tube I drew it up, not being able to bear the shock of a large swallow. Relief came but very slightly﻿—very slowly. Before the first glass was exhausted I called most imperatively for another one to be prepared as quickly as possible, lest the flames should spread by waiting. In this way I kept a man busy with the composition of lemonade after lemonade, plunging my tube over the edge of the drained tumbler into the full one with a precipitate haste for which there were mortal reasons, until six had been consumed.
And now, almost entirely restored, I assured myself that I had expiated my full penal term, and passed out rejoicing. Baseless hope!

不仅我所习得的无言承受痛苦的习惯抑制着我，而且我的骄傲亦无法忍受向身旁同行之人承认那降临在我头顶的复仇天谴——我曾许诺要让他目睹的本该是一袭狂喜的华袍。

那声音随即说道：“认罪吧！招认吧！”在绝望之中，我的双唇如铁钳般紧闭，在灵魂深处回应道：“不！我绝不！”

“你当真不肯招认？”那声音愤怒地回敬道。

“那么你将领教更为苦涩的剧痛。”

此时此刻，在我的大脑之中，受驱动着锯条拉扯我心脏的同一套地狱机械装置的驱使，一柄凶残滚烫的烧红钻头开始飞速旋转起来。它的转速不断攀升，随之而来的是一阵震颤我整个存在的巍峨咆哮轰鸣。在周身的每一根神经上，我都饱受着世间任何殉道者皆未曾领教过的剧痛折磨。头颅与心脏皆在烈火中燃烧，皆被钢铁撕裂洞穿，诸天带着狂怒向下俯瞰，大地裂开露出了群魔盘踞的狰狞深渊。啊，置身于这般地狱之中，我究竟该如何苟活下去！

我向身旁的同伴耳语吐露了我的招认。我将一切和盘托出。我向他揭示了我遭受惩罚的缘由。我凭着自身不朽的酷刑折磨苦苦劝诫他，绝不可染指这疯狂的魔咒。

随后，我用凄惨哀怨的腔调，央求他为我扑灭身上的熊熊烈火。

我们匆匆赶往目之所及的第一家餐馆。一跨进门，我便索要起对于这些精神痛苦我所曾发现过的唯一物质缓解剂——某种极酸之物。在东方，通行的形式是水果冰糕饮料（sherbet）；而我所要的则是酸度极高的柠檬水。一杯柠檬水很快调配停当，我用一根纤细的玻璃吸管将其吮吸入口，因为我根本无法承受大口吞咽所带来的剧烈冲击。痛楚的缓解极其微弱——极其迟缓。在第一杯尚未见底之前，我便极其急迫地催促尽快备好另一杯，生怕因片刻的延误导致火势蔓延开来。就这样，我让侍者一刻不停地忙于调配一杯又一杯柠檬水，我将吸管从饮尽的空杯边缘急匆匆插入斟满的新杯之中，这慌乱的仓皇背后有着关乎生死的缘由，直至整整六大杯被我悉数饮尽。

此刻，身心近乎完全复原，我确信自己已然服满了全部的刑期，满心欢喜地迈步走了出去。虚妄的幻象啊！

In a moment my heart caught fire again, and now it was a huge cathedral organ wrapped in a garment of flame, and played upon mysteriously by the fingers of the element which was burning it up. Every stop that could sound like the despairing shrieks and groans of a human soul was open; nay, it was human; it lived in this slow and cruel death, and I felt its torture. A devil-choir sang anthems of mockery to its accompaniment, and I grew frenzied as I recognized the voices which ages back in the measureless past had blasphemed over my white-hot cradle and rocked me with the lullaby of hell. As we came along the broad terrace which extends before the colleges, I looked into heaven, and lo! upon rosy coursers serene angels were riding like an army, with incredible swiftness, upon some expedition of succor. Behind them trailed on winds that blew from the gates of Paradise resplendent garments of cloud ermine dotted with stars. In an ecstasy which upbore me above my demoniac pangs, I clasped my hands and shouted, “It is I whom they are coming to save!”
Just then a black hand parted the top of heaven and shook at me menacingly. Talk not to me of faces instinct with spiritual expression; that hand, slowly brandished and then withdrawn, held more expression than the most facile face. It told me all things of terror and of doom.
Until we arrived at the door of my entry I was speechless. Here my companion left me, and once more I gathered strength to burst into a bitter cry, “O God, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me.” I used the prayer of the Divine One with a most reverent soul, and hoping that the remembered words of his Son might move the heart of the Father.
I added also a promise, “Save me, and I will never take hashish again.” Once more the voice spoke and answered me, “Many a time hast thou promised this before. Speak! how hast thou kept thy vow?”
This was true. Repeatedly during the seasons of my suffering I had resolved, yes, sworn, that if I ever escaped with life and reason from the then present delirium, I would abjure the weed of madness forever.

On returning to the natural state I always recollected having made a promise, but regarded it as the act of an unphilosophic fear in irresponsible circumstances, and moved by a suffering which it was perfectly possible to prevent by sufficient attention to general health and spirits as elements materially modifying the effect of the drug. 在重返常态之后，我固然总能记起自己曾许下过承诺，却将其视作在毫无责任能力的情境下出于非理性恐惧的盲动举措，且是受某种完全可以通过充分调理整体健康与精神状态加以防范的痛苦所驱使——而这些要素原本能在实质上改善药物的药效。因此，我认为该诺言毫无约束力，便宛如对待在某场可怕噩梦中所许下的誓言那般将其随手打破。然而，每当大麻的痛苦将我带入同一个法庭、面对我上次经受类似审判时曾立下誓约的可畏审判席前，检控的话音总会指控我背信弃义，我的灵魂亲自宣判自己有罪，在此之后，痛苦便如复仇女神涅墨西斯（Nemesis）的利刃般变得极其尖锐刺骨。我所承受的痉挛并非源自折磨，而是源自刑罚。

然而，啊，难以承受！刹那间剧痛再次将我俘获，我呻吟呼喊道：“难道连神明的信义亦已荡然无存？上帝啊，难道连*你*也对我背信弃义了吗？”我未曾获得任何答复。在自己跨入的一间空荡荡的房间里，我手按痛苦挣扎的心脏来回踱步了片刻，感受着它强有力的搏动正同那魔鬼机械的震颤轰鸣交织共振。

At that door, tearing his hair, gnashing his teeth, smiling with a maniac smile of pain, stood once more myself!
The remainder of my personalities I was spared from seeing. One more would have driven me forever mad.
For the last time I cried to Heaven, “How shall I be saved?” I was now finally answered, “Thy goodness extendeth not to God. To man must thou repay thy fault in that thou hast sought to lift thyself above humanity. Go find a man who will believe thy promise and thou shalt be saved.”
Hard condition. So many of my friends had known the former vows, and seen how I had kept them, that I bitterly feared I should never be able to fulfill it.
But as in this lay my last hope, I rushed up the staircase to find the man who would accept my security. The first I met was at the top of the farthest stairs. There he was sitting, as if in anticipation that I should come, on the throne of a solemn tribunal.
Yet not a tribunal of severe and unrelenting justice. The courtly appanage of the scene which surrounded him was necessary for my very sense of security, since, in bringing my case to any other than the most august judication, I should have felt that I was trifling with immeasurable destiny.
