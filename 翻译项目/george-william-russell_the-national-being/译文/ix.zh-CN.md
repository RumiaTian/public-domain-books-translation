## IX / 九


===Original===
Often with sadness I hear people speak of industrial development in Ireland, for I feel they contemplate no different system than that which fills workers with despair in countries where it is more successfully applied. All these energetic people are conspiring to build factories and mills and to fill them with human labor, and they believe the more they do this the better it will be for Ireland. They talk of Ireland as if it was only admirable as a quantity rather than a quality. They express delight at swelling statistics and increased trade, but where do we hear any reflection on the quality of life engendered by this industrial development? Our civilization is to differ in no way from any other. No new ideal of life is suggested to differentiate us. We are to go on exploiting human labor. Our working classes are to increase and multiply and earn profits for an employing class, as labor has done from time immemorial in Babylon, in Nineveh, in Rome, and in London today. But a choice yet remains to us, because the character of our civilization is not yet fixed. It is mainly germinal. It fills the spirit with weariness to think of another nation following the old path, without thought or imagination of other roads leading to new and more beautiful life. Every now and then, when the world was still vast and full of undiscovered wonders, some adventurers would leave the harbor, and steer their galleys past the known coast and the familiar cities and over unraveled seas, seeking some new land where life might be freer and ampler than that they had known. Is the old daring gone? Are there not such spirits among us ready to join in the noblest of all adventures﻿—the building up of a civilization﻿—so that the human might reflect the divine order? In the divine order there is both freedom and solidarity. It is the virtue of the soul to be free and its nature to love; and when it is free and acts by its own will it is most united with all other life. Those planetary spirits who move in solemn motion about the heavens I do not conceive as the slaves of Deity but as its adorers. But that material nature in which the soul is embodied has the dividing quality of the prism, which resolves pure light into distinct rays; and so on earth we get the principle of freedom and the virtue of solidarity as separated ideals continually at warfare with each other, and the reconcilement on earth of these principles in man is the conquest of matter by the spirit. This dramatic sundering on earth of virtues in unison in the heavens explains the struggle between Protestantism and Catholicism, between nationality and imperialism, between individualist and socialist, between dynamic and static in philosophy. Indeed in the last analysis all human conflicts are the balancing on earth of the manifestation of divine principles which are one in the unmanifest spirit.

我常常怀着忧伤听人谈论爱尔兰的工业发展，因为我感到，他们心中所设想的制度，与那些在运用得更为成功的国度里令工人满怀绝望的制度并无二致。这些精力充沛的人们正同心协力地建造工厂与磨坊，再以人的劳动将其填满；他们相信，这件事做得越多，对爱尔兰就越好。他们谈论爱尔兰，仿佛它只因作为数量而非作为品质才值得称羡。他们为膨胀的统计数字与增长的贸易表示欣喜，可是我们在哪里听到过对这种工业发展所孕育的生活品质的半点反思？我们的文明将与其他任何文明毫无二致。没有任何新的生活理想被提出来，使我们区别于他人。我们将继续剥削人的劳动。我们的工人阶级将生养繁息，为一个雇佣阶级赚取利润，正如劳动自古以来在巴比伦、在尼尼微、在罗马所做的那样——今日又在伦敦如此。但我们仍握有选择的余地，因为我们文明的品格尚未定型：它大体上还处在胚芽之中。一想到又一个民族踏上旧路，而对通往新的、更美好的生活的其他道路既无思考亦无想象，精神便充满倦意。在世界尚且辽阔、满是未发现的奇迹的年代，每隔一段时日，总有一些冒险者驶离港口，驾着他们的桨帆船驶过已知的海岸与熟悉的城市，越过未经探明的海洋，去寻找某片新的土地——那里的生活，或许比他们所知的更自由、更宽广。那股古老的胆魄已经逝去了吗？我们中间难道没有这样的精神，随时准备加入一切冒险中最高贵的一种——文明的建构——好让人的世界映照出神圣秩序（the divine order）吗？在神圣秩序中，自由与团结（solidarity）并存。灵魂的美德在于自由，天性在于爱；而当它自由地依自己的意志行动时，它便与其余一切生命达至最深的合一。那些以庄严的运行环绕天穹的行星精灵（planetary spirits），我不把他们设想为神性的奴隶，而把他们设想为神性的礼拜者。但灵魂所寓居的物质自然，却有着棱镜那种分化离析的性质，把纯净的光析为一道道彼此分明的光线；于是在尘世，我们得到的自由原则与团结之德，是两个彼此分离的理想，无休止地相互争战；而这些原则在尘世之人身上的和解，便是精神对物质的征服。天上谐一无间的诸般美德在尘世发生的这种戏剧性分裂，解释了新教与天主教的斗争、民族性与帝国主义的斗争、个人主义者与社会主义者的斗争，以及哲学中动态与静态的斗争。的确，追根究底，一切人类的冲突，无非是种种神圣原则显现于尘世时的相互平衡——这些原则在未显之灵中本为一体。

The civilization we create, the social order we build up, must provide for essential freedom for the individual and for solidarity of the nation. Now essential freedom is denied to men if they are in their condition servile. Can we contemplate the permanent existence of a servile class in Ireland? For, disguise it how we will, our present industrial system is practically a form of slavery for the workers, differing in externals only from the ages when the serf had a collar round his neck. He has now freedom to change from master to master, and can even seek for a master in other countries; but he must, in any case, accept the relation of servant to master. The old slave could be whipped. In the new order the wage slave can be starved, and the fact that many of the rulers of industry use their power benevolently does not make the existing relation between employer and employed right, or the social order one whose permanence can be justified. Men will gladly labor if they feel that their labor conspires with that of all other workers for the general good; but there is something loathsome to the spirit in the condition of the labor market, where labor is regarded as a commodity to be bought and sold like soap or candles. For that truly describes how it is with labor in our industrial system: we can buy labor, which means we can buy human life and thought, a portion of God’s being, and make a profit out of it. By so selling himself the worker is enslaved and limited in a thousand ways. The power of dismissal of one person by another at whim acts against independence of character, or the free expression or opinion in thought, in politics, and in religion. The soul is stunted in its growth, and spiritual life made subordinate to material interests. To deny essential freedom to the soul is the greatest of all crimes, and such denial has in all ages evoked the deepest anger among men. When freedom has been threatened nations have risen up maddened and exultant, and the clang of martial arms has been heard and the stony kings of the past have been encountered in battle. In Ireland we shall have our greatest fight of all to gain this freedom: not alone material independence for man, but the freedom of the soul, its right to choose its own heroes and its own ideals without let or hindrance by other men.

我们所创造的文明、所建构的社会秩序，必须为个人保障本质的自由（essential freedom），为民族保障团结。而人若在处境上处于奴役状态，本质的自由便已被否定。我们能设想一个奴役阶级（servile class）在爱尔兰永久存在吗？因为，无论我们怎样遮掩，我们现行的工业制度对工人而言实际上就是一种奴役的形式，与农奴颈上还戴着项圈的时代只有外表上的差异。如今他有了从一个主人换到另一个主人的自由，甚至可以到别的国家去另寻一位主人；但无论何种情形，他都必须接受仆役对主人的关系。旧日的奴隶可能遭受鞭笞。在新秩序中，雇佣奴隶（wage slave）则可能被饿死；而产业的许多统治者行使其权力时心怀仁厚，这一事实并不能使雇主与受雇者之间现存的关系变为正当，也不能使这种社会秩序成为一种其永存可以辩护的东西。人们是乐于劳作的，只要他们感到自己的劳动与其余一切工人的劳动共相谋合，为着公共的善；但劳动力市场的境况里，却有着某种令精神憎厌的东西——在那里，劳动被当作一种商品，像肥皂或蜡烛一样被买卖。因为这确实道出了劳动在我们的工业制度中的处境：我们可以购买劳动，这就意味着我们可以购买人的生命与思想——上帝存在的一部分——并从中牟利。工人经由这样的自卖，便沦于奴役，在千百种方式中受到限制。一个人凭一时兴起便解雇另一个人的权力，与品格的独立相抗，也与思想、政治与宗教中自由表达见解的权利相抗。灵魂的成长受到压抑，精神生活沦为物质利益的从属。否定灵魂的本质自由，是一切罪行中最大的罪行；而这样的否定，在一切时代都曾唤起人们最深的愤怒。每当自由受到威胁，各民族便疯狂而欢腾地奋起，兵戈的铿锵随之可闻，与往昔那些石雕般的君王们兵戎相见。在爱尔兰，为赢得这种自由，我们将要进行我们一切战斗中最伟大的一场：不仅是为人赢得物质的独立，更是赢得灵魂的自由——它有权选择自己的英雄与自己的理想，不受他人的任何阻挠与妨碍。

We have many of the vices of a slave race, and we treat others as we have been treated. Our national aspirations were overborne by material power, and we in turn use cudgel and curse on our countrymen when they differ from us in opinion and policy. Men, when they cannot match their intellect against another’s, suppress him and howl him down, putting faith in their own brainlessness. I would make the most passionate plea for freedom in Ireland: freedom for all to say the truth they feel or know. What right have we to ask for ourselves what we deny to another? The bludgeon at meetings is a blow struck against heaven. Those who will not argue or reason are recreants against humanity, and are prowling back again on all fours in their minds to the brute. It matters not in what holy name men war with violence on freedom of thought, whether in the name of God or nation they are enemies of both. We are only right in controversy when we overcome by a superior beauty or truth. The first fundamental idea inspiring an Irish polity should be this idea of freedom in all spheres of thought, and it is most necessary to fight for this because the devil and hell have organized their forces in this unfortunate land in sectarian and secret societies, of which it might be written they love darkness rather than light for the old God-given reasons.

我们身上带着一个奴隶种族的许多恶习：我们怎样被对待，便怎样对待别人。我们的民族抱负曾被物质强权压倒，而我们反过来，又在同胞于意见与方针上与我们相左时，对他们施以棍棒与咒骂。人们一旦无法以自己的智识与对方的智识较量，便去压制他、吼倒他，对自己那副无脑深信不疑。我愿为爱尔兰的自由发出最热切的吁请：让所有人都自由地说出他们所感到或所知道的真理。我们自己不肯给予别人的东西，又有什么权利为自己索取？集会上的棍击，是对天的一击。那些不肯论辩、不肯讲理的人，是人性的叛徒；他们的心智正重新四肢着地，悄然爬回兽类。人们以何种神圣的名义用暴力向思想自由开战，无关紧要；无论假上帝之名还是假民族之名，他们都是这两者的敌人。唯有凭更胜一筹的美或真理压倒对方，我们在论争中才是站在正当的一方。激励一种爱尔兰政体的第一个根本观念，应当便是这种思想一切领域皆得自由的观念；为它而战极为必要，因为魔鬼与地狱已经在这片不幸的土地上，把它们的兵力组织进了教派会社与秘密会社之中——关于这些会社，人们不妨套用那古老的、上帝赐下的缘由写道：他们爱黑暗而不爱光明。
