## Biographical Notice / 生平简介

===Original===
E. Pauline Johnson (Tekahionwake) is the youngest child of a family of four born to the late G. H. M. Johnson (Onwanonsyshon), Head Chief of the Six Nations Indians, and his wife Emily S. Howells. The latter was of English parentage, her birthplace being Bristol, but the land of her adoption Canada.
Chief Johnson was of the renowned Mohawk tribe, being a scion of one of the fifty noble families which composed the historical confederation founded by Hiawatha upwards of four hundred years ago, and known at that period as the Brotherhood of the Five Nations, but which was afterwards named the Iroquois by the early French missionaries and explorers. For their loyalty to the British Crown they were granted the magnificent lands bordering the Grand River, in the County of Brant, Ontario, on which the tribes still live.
It was upon this Reserve, on her father’s estate, “Chiefswood,” that Pauline Johnson was born. The loyalty of her ancestors breathes in her prose, as well as in her poetic writings.
Her education was neither extensive nor elaborate. It embraced neither high school nor college. A nursery governess for two years at home, three years at an Indian day school half a mile from her home, and two years in the Central School of the city of Brantford, was the extent of her educational training. But, besides this, she acquired a wide general knowledge, having been through childhood and early girlhood a great reader, especially of poetry. Before she was twelve years old she had read Scott, Longfellow, Byron, Shakespeare, and such books as Addison’s *Spectator*, Foster’s *Essays* and Owen Meredith’s writings.

===Chinese===
E·波琳·约翰逊（E. Pauline Johnson，特卡希昂韦克/Tekahionwake）是已故的六族印第安人总酋长 G·H·M·约翰逊（昂瓦农希雄，Onwanonsyshon）与妻子埃米莉·S·豪厄尔斯（Emily S. Howells）四个子女中最年幼的一个。后者是英格兰血统，生于布里斯托尔，而她安身立命之地则是加拿大。
约翰逊酋长属于声名卓著的莫霍克部族，是组成那个历史性联盟的五十个高贵家族之一的后裔——那联盟由海华沙（Hiawatha）在四百多年前创立，当时称为“五族兄弟会”（Brotherhood of the Five Nations），后来被早期的法国传教士和探险家称为易洛魁（Iroquois）。由于对不列颠王室的忠诚，他们获赐安大略省布兰特县沿大河两岸的辽阔土地，诸部族至今仍居住在那里。
波琳·约翰逊就出生在这片保留地上她父亲的宅邸“酋长林”（Chiefswood）里。先人的忠贞之气，在她的散文与诗歌作品中皆有吐纳。
她所受的教育既不广博也不精深，既没有上过中学，也没有进过大学。在家里跟一位幼儿家庭女教师学习两年，在离家半英里的一所印第安走读学校上了三年学，又在布兰特福德市的中央学校读了两年——这便是她全部的教育经历。但除此以外，她博闻广识：从童年到少女时代，她一直是个酷爱读书的人，尤其钟爱诗歌。不到十二岁，她已读过司各特、朗费罗、拜伦、莎士比亚，以及艾迪生的《旁观者》（*Spectator*）、福斯特的《随笔》（*Essays*）和欧文·梅瑞狄斯的著作。

===Original===
The first periodicals to accept her poems and place them before the public were *Gems of Poetry*, a small magazine published in New York, and *The Week*, established by the late Prof. Goldwin Smith, of Toronto, the New York *Independent* and Toronto *Saturday Night*. Since then she has contributed to most of the high-grade magazines, both on this continent and England.
Her writings having brought her into notice, the next step in Miss Johnson’s career was her appearance on the public platform as a reciter of her own poems. For this she had natural talent, and in the exercise of it she soon developed a marked ability, joined with a personal magnetism, that was destined to make her a favourite with audiences from the Atlantic to the Pacific. Her friend, Mr. Frank Yeigh, of Toronto, provided for a series of recitals having that scope, with the object of enabling her to go to England to arrange for the publication of her poems. Within two years this aim was accomplished, her book of poems, *The White Wampum*, being published by John Lane, of the Bodley Head. She took with her numerous letters of introduction, including one from the Governor-General, the Earl of Aberdeen, and she soon gained both social and literary standing. Her book was received with much favour, both by reviewers and the public. After giving many recitals in fashionable drawing-rooms, she returned to Canada, and made her first tour to the Pacific Coast, giving recitals at all the cities and towns en route. Since then she has crossed the Rocky Mountains no fewer than nineteen times.
Miss Johnson’s pen had not been idle, and in 1903 the George Morang Co., of Toronto, published her second book of poems, entitled *Canadian Born*, which was also well received.
After a number of recitals, which included Newfoundland and the Maritime Provinces, she went to England again in 1906 and made her first appearance in Steinway Hall, under the distinguished patronage of Lord and Lady Strathcona. In the following year she again visited London, returning by way of the United States, where she gave many recitals. After another tour of Canada she decided to give up public work, to make Vancouver, BC, her home, and to devote herself to literary work.

最早接受她的诗作并将其公之于众的刊物，是纽约出版的小杂志《诗歌珍宝》（*Gems of Poetry*）、由多伦多已故的戈尔德温·史密斯教授创办的《一周》（*The Week*）、纽约的《独立》（*Independent*）与多伦多的《星期六之夜》（*Saturday Night*）。此后，本大陆及英国的一流杂志，她大多为之撰稿过。
她的作品使她渐受瞩目，约翰逊小姐生涯的下一步，便是作为一名朗诵自己诗歌的诵读者登上公共讲台。对此她有天生的才华，加之勤于演练，她很快便显露出出众的能力，并兼具一种个人的魅力，注定使她从大西洋到太平洋都深受听众喜爱。她的朋友、多伦多的弗兰克·耶先生为她安排了一系列以此为范围的朗诵会，目的是使她能前往英国安排自己诗集的出版。不出两年，这一目标便告达成：她的诗集《白色贝壳串珠》（*The White Wampum*）由博德利出版社（the Bodley Head）的约翰·莱恩出版。她随身携带了许多介绍信，其中包括总督阿伯丁伯爵的一封；她很快便在社交界和文学界都站稳了脚跟。她的诗集深受评论家和公众的欢迎。在许多时尚客厅举行朗诵会之后，她返回加拿大，并首次巡游太平洋沿岸，沿途各城镇皆举行朗诵会。此后，她翻越落基山脉的次数不下十九次。
约翰逊小姐的笔并未闲置。1903 年，多伦多的乔治·莫兰公司出版了她的第二部诗集，题为《加拿大人》（*Canadian Born*），同样广受好评。
在包括纽芬兰和滨海诸省在内的一系列朗诵会之后，她于 1906 年再度赴英，在斯特拉斯科纳勋爵与夫人的盛名赞助下，首次登台斯坦威音乐厅。翌年她再访伦敦，归途取道美国，在那里举行了多场朗诵会。又一次巡游加拿大之后，她决定放弃公开演出生涯，在不列颠哥伦比亚省的温哥华安家，潜心从事文学写作。

===Original===
Only a woman of remarkable powers of endurance could have borne up under the hardships necessarily encountered in travelling through Northwestern Canada in pioneer days as Miss Johnson did; and shortly after settling down in Vancouver the exposure and hardship she had endured began to tell on her, and her health completely broke down. For almost a year she has been an invalid, and as she is unable to attend to the business herself, a trust has been formed by some of the leading citizens of her adopted city for the purpose of collecting and publishing for her benefit her later works. Among these are the beautiful Indian Legends contained in this volume, which she has been at great pains to collect, and a series of boys’ stories, which have been exceedingly well received by magazine readers.
During the sixteen years Miss Johnson was travelling, she had many varied and interesting experiences. She travelled the old Battleford trail before the railroad went through, and across the Boundary country in British Columbia in the romantic days of the early pioneers. Once she took an eight hundred and fifty mile drive up the Cariboo trail to the gold fields. She has always been an ardent canoeist, and has run many strange rivers, crossed many a lonely lake, and camped in many an unfrequented place. These venturesome trips she made more from her inherent love of Nature and adventure than from any necessity of her profession.

在拓荒年代穿越加拿大西北部，必然历经艰辛，唯有耐力过人的女子才能像约翰逊小姐那样支撑下来；在温哥华定居后不久，她一路承受的风霜劳顿开始在她身上显露后果，她的健康彻底垮了。近一年来她一直缠绵病榻；由于她已无力亲自打理自己的事务，她所归化的城市里的一些主要市民为她成立了一个信托，以便为她收集并出版她的后期作品，使其受益。其中包括本卷所收的这些优美的印第安传说——她曾不辞辛劳地搜集它们——以及一系列少年故事，这些故事深受杂志读者欢迎。
在约翰逊小姐四处旅行的十六年间，她有过许多五花八门、趣味盎然的经历。她曾在铁路贯通之前走过古老的巴特尔福德古道，也在早期拓荒者充满浪漫色彩的日子里穿越不列颠哥伦比亚的边界地带。有一次，她乘马车沿卡里布古道向金矿区行进了八百五十英里。她始终是个热忱的独木舟手，泛舟过许多陌生的河流，横渡过许多寂寞的湖泊，在许多人迹罕至之处露营过。这些冒险的旅行，与其说是出于职业的需要，不如说是出于她对自然与冒险天生的热爱。

Miss Pauline Johnson died in Vancouver on March 7, 1913. In accordance with her last wish her ashes were buried in Stanley Park within sight and sound of Siwash Rock, where the main driveway round the park, coming from the English Bay entrance, divides east and west﻿—the western branch sloping down towards the rock and the eastern going to the Big Tree. An editorial in the *Vancouver Daily Province* of March 8 said:

> The keynote of her whole disposition was a generous charity towards everything and everybody with whom she came in contact. There was no trouble too great for her to take, no detail too small for her to neglect when it was a matter of giving happiness to others. She was one of those great souls who would starve themselves on the trail, work unwearyingly for her companions, cheer them ever onwards through good times and bad, and rejoice with them when the goal was achieved. She loved life with a passionate devotion that was almost pathetic in its intensity. In spite of all her travelling, all her experiences, which were by no means easy, Pauline Johnson never lost her capacity for getting the best out of life. She was absolutely natural and simple in her love of happiness. She disliked artificiality of any kind. The seasons as they came and went were in themselves a constant source of pleasure to her. She loved the Pacific coast with its ever-changing colours, the sea and the deeply gashed mountains. The wind in the great firs and the roaring of the mountain torrents were music in her ears. With the passing of winter passed also the soul of Pauline Johnson to the happy hunting grounds, there to find eternal freedom untrammeled by mortality. To all who knew her she was the “best beloved vagabond.” It was always fine weather and good going on the trail of life when Pauline Johnson blazed the way.

波琳·约翰逊小姐于 1913 年 3 月 7 日在温哥华逝世。遵照她的遗愿，她的骨灰安葬于斯坦利公园，在与斯夸石（Siwash Rock）相望相闻的地方——环园的主车道从英吉利湾入口而来，在那里分为东西两支：西支向巨岩缓缓而下，东支通向大树（the Big Tree）。3 月 8 日《温哥华每日省报》（*Vancouver Daily Province*）的社论写道：

> 她整个性情的基调，是对她所接触到的一切人和事怀有宽厚的仁慈。只要是为了给人以快乐，再大的麻烦她也不辞，再小的细节她也不肯疏忽。她是那种伟大的灵魂之一：在旅途中宁愿自己挨饿，为同伴不知疲倦地劳作，无论顺境逆境始终鼓舞他们前行，并在抵达目标时与他们同欢。她以炽热得近乎令人心酸的挚爱爱着生活。尽管旅途劳顿、经历坎坷，波琳·约翰逊从未丧失从生活中汲取精华的能力。她对快乐的热爱绝对自然、绝对纯真。她厌恶任何矫揉造作。四季的来去本身就是她永不枯竭的愉悦之源。她爱太平洋沿岸变幻无穷的色彩，爱大海和深壑纵横的群山。大冷杉间的风声与山涧的轰鸣，在她耳中皆是音乐。随着冬天的消逝，波琳·约翰逊的灵魂也去往极乐猎场，在那里获得不受尘世羁绊的永恒自由。在所有认识她的人眼里，她是“最受爱戴的流浪者”。在波琳·约翰逊披荆斩棘开路而行的人生旅途上，天气总是晴好，路总是好走。
