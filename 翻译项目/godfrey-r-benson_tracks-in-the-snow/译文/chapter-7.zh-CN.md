## VII

===Original===
After several remands, the proceedings against Trethewy before the magistrates came to a close about the end of February. There was nothing much to note about these proceedings, which ended, as I suppose they must have ended, in his being committed for trial. The reader knows by this time pretty nearly the whole case against him. That Peters had been murdered was certain. The accused had had several altercations with the murdered man. In one of them he had expressed a wish to kill him, and he had repeated this wish to others upon the fatal night. Footprints had been found which, as the reader knows, seemed at first sight plainly indicative of his guilt. Then there was the ladder. It was undoubtedly kept, before the murder, locked up in a place of which only Trethewy had the key. That anyone could have had access to it between the murder and the discovery of the ladder was a view supported only by the uncorroborated statement of the accused that he had left the key of the pump-house that morning, when summoned to speak to the police, and had forgotten to go back for it until the next day. Lastly, the finding of the instrument case, though not very important, at any rate disposed of any improbability that Trethewy would have had such an instrument as the knife that was used.

I daresay this would have been enough to hang a man if this was all; and against this there was nothing to be set, except the immovable persistency of Trethewy and his wife from the first in the tale which they told.

===Chinese===
几度还押之后，特雷瑟维在治安法官面前的那场审理，到二月底前后方才收场。这场审理没有多少值得记下的东西，其结局——我想也只得如此收场——是他被提交巡回法庭受审。到此为止，读者对他的罪状已差不多知道了个全貌。彼得斯死于谋杀，这一点确凿无疑。被告与死者生前曾数度激烈口角。其中一次，他流露过要杀他的念头；而在那致命的一夜，他又把这个念头对旁人重述了一遍。脚印已经找到，而读者知道，那些脚印乍看之下分明指向他的罪行。再有就是那架梯子。案发以前，它无疑锁在一处只有特雷瑟维握有钥匙的地方。至于说有人能在凶案与梯子被发现之间碰过它，此说唯一的凭据，是被告那无人佐证的一面之词：案发那天早晨，他奉召去跟警方问话时，把水泵房的钥匙撂在了原处，直到第二天才想起回去取。最后是那器械匣的查获。此事虽说不要紧，至少也消解了一层疑窦：特雷瑟维手头会有行凶所用的那样一把刀子，这不再是不可能的事了。

倘若事情尽止于此，我想这已足够把一个人送上绞架；而能拿来与之抗衡的，只有一样：特雷瑟维和他的妻子，从最初一刻起，便死死咬定他们那套说法，纹丝不移。

===Original===
Nothing, that is, till after he had been committed for trial. But the very evening after his committal, a slight but almost conclusive circumstance was brought to light, and entirely altered the aspect of the case. That evening I received a visit from Peters’ housemaid, Edith Summers. She had, she said, something on her mind. She had told a falsehood to the police-sergeant on the morning after the murder. She had interrupted the housekeeper to say that the candle by Peters’ bed had been a long candle the night before; she had said this because she had been very severely scolded by the housekeeper for forgetting to put fresh candles in the candlestick; and so she had said what was false, not meaning any harm, but thinking for the moment (as she now tried to explain) that it was true, and that she had done what she had intended. She had confessed to the housekeeper since, but the housekeeper had only said she was an impudent girl to have put in her word then, and had better not put it in again. She had gone to the court expecting to be a witness on some small point and determined to make the matter clear then; but she had not been called. She had spoken to a policeman, and had been told to speak to one of the lawyers. She had tried to get the attention of Trethewy’s lawyer, but he had been too busy to listen to her.

I am ashamed to say that listening to her rather long explanation, I entirely failed to see the significance of what she told me. I said something quite well intentioned about the evil of saying what was not true, and then told the girl kindly, that I did not think there was any harm done. But she had thought about it and was in earnest, and she made me see it in a moment. There were, she explained, other candles in the room, but they were new candles, and they were not lighted that night. From this and what we already knew the conclusion was almost inevitable. Peters was murdered before two inches of ordinary candle, which was burning at 11:30 p.m. on the 28th of January, burnt down.

我是说，直到他被提交巡回法庭之前，再没有别的东西。可就在他被移交候审的当天傍晚，一桩细微却几乎足以定谳的情节浮出了水面，令全案面貌为之改观。那天傍晚，彼得斯家的女仆伊迪丝·萨默斯登门看我。她说，她心里搁着一件事。凶案次日早晨，她对警佐说了假话。她当时打断了女管家的话头，说彼得斯床头那支蜡烛，头天夜里原是一支长蜡烛；她之所以这么说，是因为忘了往烛台里换上新蜡烛，先前被女管家狠狠训斥了一顿；于是她便说了不实的话——并无半点恶意，只是一时间（她如今竭力这样解释）信以为真，以为自己已把分内的事做妥了。事后她已向女管家认了错，可女管家只说她是个放肆的丫头，当时不该多嘴插话，往后还是少插为妙。开庭那天她去了法庭，指望就某桩小事出庭作证，打定主意届时把这事说个明白；却始终没有人传她。她向一个警察说过这事，人家叫她去找律师谈。她想引特雷瑟维那位律师的注意，可那人忙得根本无暇听她说话。

说来惭愧，听她颠来倒去讲了那么长一篇，我竟全然没有领会她这话里的分量。我还十分好心地发了一通议论，说不实之词是桩罪过，随后又和颜悦色地对姑娘说，依我看，这并没有造成什么损害。可她把这事前前后后想过，态度极其认真；一转眼的工夫，她便让我开了窍。她解释说，房间里另有几支蜡烛，但都是新蜡烛，那天夜里一支也没有点。由这一点，再加上我们已掌握的情况，结论几乎无可回避：彼得斯遇害，是在一支普通蜡烛烧完两英寸之前——那支蜡烛在 1 月 28 日晚上 11:30 还燃着。

===Original===
Stupid as it may seem, I had for some time been convinced of Trethewy’s innocence, and yet had never really drawn the necessary inference from it. Of course with the two premises in my mind—Peters was murdered, Trethewy did not murder him—I had been aware, in a sense, of the conclusion, but it had taken no hold of my attention. Now, however, I had evidence of Trethewy’s innocence, which was no longer a private intuition of my own, but was something of which everyone must appreciate the force. Perhaps it was from this, perhaps it was from the sentimental effect of having the time of the crime fixed within such narrow limits; anyhow the thought, “Someone other than Trethewy murdered Peters,” came upon me with a sudden horror which could hardly have been greater if I had only that moment become aware of the original fact of the murder.

I instantly went over in my mind the list of those few who were so placed as to lie within the reach of suspicion. Trethewy could no longer be suspected. Thalberg surely could not. I dismissed the two women servants from my mind immediately. There remained two men—three men—three men, of whom I was one. I knew how easily I could clear myself, for the door had been locked behind me before that candle was lit. But I was the last man known to have seen Peters, and my confused current of thought included me as a man to be suspected. I asked myself of each in turn, is he the guilty man? and in each case I answered no. As I look back now, it seems to me, that the answer “no” did not come to my mind with the same wholehearted conviction in each case. But I did not in the few moments for which I then reflected, I did not till long after do more than go round in this circle: One of us three men murdered Peters. Was it—each of us in turn? No. Could it after all be one of the servants? No! Was there not then in the vast region of possibility some way of accounting for Peters’ death without the guilt of any of us. The plainest reasons bade me answer yes, and yet again I answered no. And so back round the circle.

===Chinese===
这话说出来未免显得愚钝：有一阵子了，我早已确信特雷瑟维是无辜的，却从不曾真正由这份确信推出那个必然的结论。当然，两个前提都摆在我心里——彼得斯被人谋杀了；谋杀他的不是特雷瑟维——在某种意义上，那结论我并非没有意识到，只是它始终不曾攫住我的注意。而如今，我手里有了特雷瑟维无辜的证据；这不再是我一人的私见直觉，而是任谁都必须掂出其分量的事实。这或许是由于前者，或许是由于罪案的时刻被压缩到如此狭窄的界限之内，给人的心头的震动；总之，「谋杀彼得斯的，是特雷瑟维以外的什么人」——这个念头携着一阵骤然的恐怖向我袭来，那恐怖之烈，纵使我直到那一刻才头一回知晓谋杀这桩事实本身，怕也不过如此。

我当即在心中把寥寥几个位置足以落入嫌疑范围的人逐一过了一遍。特雷瑟维已无从怀疑。塔尔贝格想必也不会。两名女仆，我立刻从心里打发了出去。剩下两个男人——三个男人——三个男人，其中一个是我。我知道自己多么容易便可洗刷干净，因为在那支蜡烛点起之前，我身后的门早已锁上。可我是已知最后一个见过彼得斯的人，于是那一团纷乱的思潮，也把我本人卷作了嫌疑的对象。我挨个问自己：他有罪吗？每问一人，我都答：没有。如今回首，我总觉得，那几声「没有」落进心头时，每一回的全然笃定，并不尽同。可在当时思忖的那短短片刻里——乃至此后许久——我所做的，无非是绕着这个圈子打转：我们三个男人中的一个谋杀了彼得斯。是他吗——挨个问过来？不是。到头来难道会是仆人中的一个？不是！那么，在这片广袤的可能性疆域里，难道竟没有一条路，可以解释彼得斯之死，而无须我们中任何一人担罪？最浅白的道理都命我答「有」，可我还是答了「没有」。于是又转回圈子里去。

But the girl was with me and I could not keep her waiting forever. I arrested my mental circle where it began, at the thought: it seems Peters was murdered while two inches of ordinary candle, lighted before 11:30 p.m. on the 28th of January, burnt out. I started up to take the girl at once to see the police, but on a sudden idea I desisted. I wrote a note to the housekeeper, asking that the girl should again come to see me at eight in the evening, and I sent a message to the police-sergeant, asking him to come at the same time. Of course I had often interviewed him on parish matters, and having got him settled into the armchair in my study, in which I could usually put him at his ease, I fired upon him the question, “Sergeant, were those tracks, which we found, really there when you came to Mr. Peters’ house in the morning?” Now Sergeant Speke was a very honest man, but he was (most properly, I am sure) a creature of discipline, and his answer threw, for me, a flood of light on the problem how it is that the very best of the police are so ready to back up one another. He answered immediately and with conviction: “Well, you see, sir, it is not for me to judge.” The answer was on the face of it preposterous. He alone had searched the front of the house that morning, and it was for him alone, of all men, to say whether the tracks were there. He obviously did not see this at all, and I was wise enough to let go an opportunity for moralising to him. I beguiled him, with a glass of wine and other devices of the tempter, into feeling himself off duty for the while, and talking with me as fellow-mortal to fellow-mortal. I very soon discovered, first, that Sergeant Speke had searched carefully enough around the house that morning to have seen the tracks if they had been there, and, secondly, that the man, Speke, as distinct from the Sergeant, knew perfectly well that they were not there.

可姑娘还在我这里等着，我总不能叫她一直等下去。我便把那团兜圈的思绪勒在它的起点上，勒在这个念头上：看来，彼得斯遇害，正落在一支普通蜡烛烧掉两英寸的那段时间里——那支蜡烛在 1 月 28 日晚上 11:30 之前便已点燃。我霍然起身，要立刻带姑娘去见警方，忽生一念，又止住了。我给女管家写了张便条，请她放姑娘晚上八点再来见我；又给警佐捎了口信，请他同一时刻过来。教区的事务上，我自然常同他会面。我把他安顿进书房里那把扶手椅——大凡坐进那把椅子，他多半便能自在下来——随即朝他劈面抛出那个问题：「警佐，我们发现的那些脚印，您那天早晨赶到彼得斯先生宅上时，当真已经在了吗？」斯皮克警佐是个非常诚实的人，但他（我敢说，这再正当不过）是个纪律造出来的生灵；而他这一答，倒替我照亮了一桩悬题：警界里顶顶出色的人物，何以也那般乐于彼此撑腰。他应声而答，斩钉截铁：「呃，您瞧，先生，轮不到我来判断。」这回答摆在明面上是荒唐透顶的。那天早晨，唯独他搜查过宅子的正面；普天之下，也唯独他一个人，说得上脚印在不在那里。他显然全然见不到这一层，而我总算明智，把说教的机会白白放过了。我用一杯酒，外加诱惑者的种种手段，哄得他一时觉得自己脱了差事，同一个凡人对另一个凡人似的同我交谈起来。我很快便查明了两件事：其一，那天早晨，斯皮克警佐在宅子四周搜得够仔细，脚印若真在那里，他不可能看不见；其二，那个作为凡人的斯皮克——有别于作为警佐的斯皮克——心里一清二楚：脚印并不在那里。

Not till then did I summon the girl Edith from the servants’ hall where she was waiting. I made her tell her tale. I saw the Sergeant take a due note of it for transmission to those, to me mysterious, headquarters where I supposed all such matters were digested. I got the assurance that Sergeant Speke was really man enough to see that his own evidence, as to the nonexistence of the tracks that morning, would be noted and digested too. I dismissed the Sergeant and Edith, and went slowly to bed. Did I suspect this person? No! Did I suspect that person? N—no. At last I determined that I would not let my suspicions fasten on any one man, while it might be just as reasonable that his suspicions should fasten on me. But my mind remained full of horror and of the image of a candle-end spluttering out, while the man, who had lighted it to read by, lay dead in those bloody sheets. Very, very glad I was that my wife was at last coming home next day.

I suppose it was from the association of two female names that my dreams, when at last I slept, were of nothing more horrible than the ship *Eleanor*, which, as the reader remembers, probably still sailed the seas.

我想，多半是两个女人的名字在脑中连到了一处；待我终于入睡，梦里再没有比「埃莉诺号」（*Eleanor*）更可怕的物事——读者当还记得，那条船多半仍航行在海上。
