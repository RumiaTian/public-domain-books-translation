## II The Eavesdropper / 二 偷听的人

===Original===
It is one of the canons of correct conduct, scrupulously adhered to (when convenient) by all well-bred persons, that an acquaintance should be initiated by a proper introduction. To this salutary rule, which I have disregarded to the extent of an entire chapter, I now hasten to conform; and the more so inasmuch as nearly two years have passed since my first informal appearance.

===Chinese===
得体行为诸准则之中，有一条是凡有教养之士无不恪守的（在方便的时候）：与人结识，须先经正式介绍。这条有益的规矩，我已整整一个章节置之不理，如今赶紧补行如仪；何况距离我上一回不合规矩的亮相，已经过去将近两年了。

===Original===
Permit me then, to introduce Paul Berkeley, M.B., etc., recently﻿—very recently﻿—qualified, faultlessly attired in the professional frock-coat and tall hat, and, at the moment of introduction, navigating with anxious care a perilous strait between a row of well-filled coal-sacks and a colossal tray piled high with kidney potatoes.

===Chinese===
那么，请允许我介绍：保罗·伯克利（Paul Berkeley），医学学士（M.B.）等等，新近——非常新近——取得行医资格，一身执业医师的礼服大衣与大礼帽穿得无可挑剔；在介绍之际，正小心翼翼地航行于一段险要的海峡之间：一侧是一排塞得满满的煤袋，另一侧则是一大托盘堆得冒尖的肾形土豆。

===Original===
The passage of this strait landed me on the terra firma of Fleur-de-Lys Court, where I halted for a moment to consult my visiting list. There was only one more patient for me to see this morning, and he lived at 49, Nevill’s Court, wherever that might be. I turned for information to the presiding deity of the coal shop.

===Chinese===
驶过这段海峡，我便在百合花院（Fleur-de-Lys Court）的坚实陆地上靠了岸，在此驻足片刻，查阅我的出诊名单。今天上午我只剩一位病人要看了，他住在内维尔巷（Nevill's Court）49 号——管它在什么地方。于是我转身向煤店的主事神明问路。

===Original===
“Can you direct me, Mrs. Jablett, to Nevill’s Court?”

===Chinese===
“劳驾，贾布利特太太（Mrs. Jablett），到内维尔巷去，能给我指个路吗？”

===Original===
She could and she did, grasping me confidentially by the arm (the mark remained on my sleeve for weeks) and pointing a shaking forefinger at the dead wall ahead. “Nevill’s Court,” said Mrs. Jablett, “is a alley, and you goes into it through a archway. It turns out on Fetter Lane on the right ’and as you goes up, oppersight Bream’s Buildings.”

===Chinese===
她能，她也就指了。她一副知交密友的架势一把攥住我的胳膊（那印记在我袖子上留了好几个星期），颤巍巍的食指直指前方那堵死墙。“内维尔巷啊，”贾布利特太太说，“是条小胡同儿，你得打一个门洞儿里钻进去。出了口子就是菲特巷（Fetter Lane），在你往上走的那只右手边儿，正对着布里姆大楼（Bream's Buildings）。”

===Original===
I thanked Mrs. Jablett and went on my way, glad that the morning round was nearly finished, and vaguely conscious of a growing appetite and of a desire to wash in hot water.

===Chinese===
我谢过贾布利特太太，继续赶路，心里高兴上午的巡回出诊眼看就要收尾，又隐隐觉得胃口渐开，还想用热水洗个澡。

===Original===
The practice which I was conducting was not my own. It belonged to poor Dick Barnard, an old St. Margaret’s man of irrepressible spirits and indifferent physique, who had started only the day before for a trip down the Mediterranean on board a tramp engaged in the currant trade; and this, my second morning’s round, was in some sort a voyage of geographical discovery.

===Chinese===
我眼下主持的这份诊务并非我自己的。它属于可怜的迪克·巴纳德（Dick Barnard）——圣玛格丽特医院的老毕业生，兴致旺盛得按都按不住，体格却平平——就在昨天，他登上一艘跑葡萄干买卖的不定期货船，启程游地中海去了；于是这第二个上午的巡回出诊，多少算得上一次地理发现之旅。

===Original===
I walked on briskly up Fetter Lane until a narrow arched opening, bearing the superscription “Nevill’s Court,” arrested my steps, and here I turned to encounter one of those surprises that lie in wait for the traveler in London byways. Expecting to find the gray squalor of the ordinary London court, I looked out from under the shadow of the arch past a row of decent little shops through a vista full of light and color﻿—a vista of ancient, warm-toned roofs and walls relieved by sunlit foliage. In the heart of London a tree is always a delightful surprise; but here were not only trees, but bushes and even flowers. The narrow footway was bordered by little gardens, which, with their wooden palings and well-kept shrubs, gave to the place an air of quaint and sober rusticity; and even as I entered, a bevy of workgirls, with gaily-colored blouses and hair aflame in the sunlight, brightened up the quiet background like the wild flowers that spangle a summer hedgerow.

===Chinese===
我迈开轻快的步子沿菲特巷上行，直到一处窄窄的拱形门洞令我止步，门上题着“内维尔巷”的名目。就在这里，我遭遇了那种专在伦敦僻巷中守候行人的意外之喜。原以为会见到伦敦寻常小院那灰扑扑的寒碜景象，可从拱门的阴影下望出去，掠过一排体面的小铺面，眼前豁然展开一片流光溢彩的景致——一派古旧的、色调温暖的屋顶与墙壁，在阳光照拂的绿荫掩映下错落生辉。在伦敦腹地，一棵树总归是桩令人心喜的意外；而此地非但有树，还有灌木，甚至还有花卉。窄窄的人行道两侧镶着一方方小花园，配上木条栅栏和修剪齐整的灌木，给这地方平添一股古雅而恬淡的村野之气；恰在我进巷的当口，一群女工迎面走来，色彩鲜丽的罩衫、阳光下发如飞焰，把这道安静的背景装点得灿然生色，宛如缀满夏日树篱的点点野花。

===Original===
In one of the gardens I noticed that the little paths were paved with what looked like circular tiles, but which, on inspection, I found to be old-fashioned stone ink-bottles, buried bottom upwards; and I was meditating upon the quaint conceit of the forgotten scrivener who had thus adorned his habitation﻿—a law-writer perhaps or an author, or perchance even a poet﻿—when I perceived the number that I was seeking inscribed on a shabby door in a high wall. There was no bell or knocker, so, lifting the latch, I pushed the door open and entered.

===Chinese===
在其中一座花园里，我注意到园中小径铺着一种看似圆形陶片的东西，细看之下才发现竟是老式的石质墨水瓶，瓶底朝上倒埋土中；我正自玩味那位已被遗忘的文书先生的这番别致巧思——他就这样装点了自己的居所；他也许是位代写讼状的，也许是位作家，说不定还是位诗人——忽见我所寻的门牌号正写在高墙上一扇寒酸的门上。门上既无门铃也无门环，于是我掀起门闩，推门而入。

===Original===
But if the court itself had been a surprise, this was a positive wonder, a dream. Here, within earshot of the rumble of Fleet Street, I was in an old-fashioned garden enclosed by high walls and, now that the gate was shut, cut off from all sight and knowledge of the urban world that seethed without. I stood and gazed in delighted astonishment. Sun-gilded trees and flower beds gay with blossom; lupins, snapdragons, nasturtiums, spiry foxgloves, and mighty hollyhocks formed the foreground; over which a pair of sulphur-tinted butterflies flitted, unmindful of a buxom and miraculously clean white cat which pursued them, dancing across the borders and clapping her snowy paws fruitlessly in midair. And the background was no less wonderful; a grand old house, dark-eaved and venerable, that must have looked down on this garden when ruffled dandies were borne in sedan chairs through the court, and gentle Izaak Walton, stealing forth from his shop in Fleet Street, strolled up Fetter Lane to “go a-angling” at Temple Mills.

===Chinese===
可是，如果说方才那巷子已是一重意外，这里便简直是奇观，是一场梦了。就在舰队街（Fleet Street）车马轰隆声隐约可闻的地方，我竟置身于一座高墙环绕的老式花园之中；此刻园门既已关上，外面那个沸沸扬扬的尘世便被隔绝于视听与心知之外。我伫立原地，惊喜交加地凝望。阳光镀金的树木与繁花争艳的花坛：羽扇豆、金鱼草、旱金莲、尖塔似的毛地黄和高大的蜀葵构成前景；一对硫磺色的蝴蝶在花间翻飞，全不理会一只体态丰腴、干净得不可思议的白猫正追逐着它们，蹦跳着掠过花畦，雪白的爪子在半空一次次徒劳地拍合。而背景也毫不逊色：一座气派的老宅，檐影深黑、古意庄严，当鬈发插翎的纨绔子弟坐着轿子被人抬过这条巷子、当温文的伊萨克·沃尔顿（Izaak Walton）从他在舰队街的铺子里悄悄溜出来、踱上菲特巷到坦普尔磨坊（Temple Mills）“钓一番鱼”的年代，它想必就已俯瞰着这座花园了。

===Original===
So overpowered was I by this unexpected vision that my hand was on the bottom knob of a row of bellpulls before I recollected myself; and it was not until a most infernal jangling from within recalled me to my business that I observed underneath it a small brass plate inscribed “Miss Oman.”

===Chinese===
这意外的景象令我如此神魂颠倒，待回过神来，我的手已经搭在一排门铃拉手中最低的那只上；直到屋内爆出一阵要命至极的刺耳铃响，把我唤回正事，我才看见它下面钉着一块小小的铜牌，上刻“奥曼小姐（Miss Oman）”。

===Original===
The door opened with some suddenness and a short, middle-aged woman surveyed me hungrily.

===Chinese===
房门猛地开了，一个矮小的中年妇人带着一副如饥似渴的神气打量我。

===Original===
“Have I rung the wrong bell?” I asked﻿—foolishly enough, I must admit.

===Chinese===
“我是不是拉错铃了？”我问——我得承认，问得实在够蠢。

===Original===
“How can I tell?” she demanded. “I expect you have. It’s the sort of thing a man would do﻿—ring the wrong bell and then say he’s sorry.”

===Chinese===
“我怎么知道？”她质问道，“我看八成是拉错了。男人干的就是这种事——拉错了铃，然后再说声抱歉。”

===Original===
“I didn’t go as far as that,” I retorted. “It seems to have had the desired effect, and I’ve made your acquaintance into the bargain.”

===Chinese===
“我还没走到那一步呢，”我回敬道，“这铃看样子起了预期的效果，而且顺便还结识了您。”

===Original===
“Whom do you want to see?” she asked.

===Chinese===
“你要见谁？”她问。

===Original===
“Mr. Bellingham.”

===Chinese===
“贝林厄姆先生。”

===Original===
“Are you the doctor?”

===Chinese===
“你就是那位大夫吗？”

===Original===
“I’m *a* doctor.”

===Chinese===
“我是*一个*大夫。”

===Original===
“Follow me upstairs,” said Miss Oman, “and don’t tread on the paint.”

===Chinese===
“跟我上楼来，”奥曼小姐说，“别踩着了油漆。”

===Original===
I crossed the spacious hall, and preceded by my conductress, ascended a noble oak staircase, treading carefully on a ribbon of matting that ran up the middle. On the first-floor landing Miss Oman opened a door and, pointing to the room, said, “Go in there and wait; I’ll tell her you’re here.”

===Chinese===
我穿过宽敞的门厅，由这位引路人前头带路，登上一座气派的橡木楼梯，小心翼翼踩着楼梯正中一条窄长的草垫拾级而上。到了二楼平台，奥曼小姐打开一扇门，朝屋里一指：“进去等着；我去告诉她你来了。”

===Original===
“I said *Mr.* Bellingham﻿—” I began; but the door slammed on me, and Miss Oman’s footsteps retreated rapidly down the stairs.

===Chinese===
“我说的是贝林厄姆*先生*——”我刚开了个头；门已砰地冲我关上，奥曼小姐的脚步声急匆匆地退下楼梯去了。

===Original===
It was at once obvious to me that I was in a very awkward position. The room into which I had been shown communicated with another, and though the door of communication was shut, I was unpleasantly aware of a conversation that was taking place in the adjoining room. At first, indeed, only a vague mutter, with a few disjointed phrases, came through the door, but suddenly an angry voice rang out clear and painfully distinct.

===Chinese===
我当即明白，自己的处境十分尴尬。我被引进的这间屋子与另一间相通；虽然隔门关着，隔壁屋里正在进行的谈话仍隐隐可闻，令我很不受用。起初，透过门传来的确实只有一片含混的低语，夹着几个不相连贯的短语，可突然间，一个怒气冲冲的声音陡然响起，清晰得刺耳扎心。

===Original===
“Yes, I did! And I say it again. Bribery! Collusion! That’s what it amounts to. You want to square me!”

===Chinese===
“不错，我就是说了！我还要再说一遍。贿赂！勾结！说穿了就是这么回事。你是想收买我！”

===Original===
“Nothing of the kind, Godfrey,” was the reply in a lower tone; but at this point I coughed emphatically and moved a chair, and the voices subsided once more into an indistinct murmur.

===Chinese===
“绝无此意，戈弗雷，”回答的声调压低了些；但这时我重重咳了一声，又挪了挪椅子，那两个声音便重新低伏下去，化作一片听不真切的咕哝。

===Original===
To distract my attention from my unseen neighbors I glanced curiously about the room and speculated upon the personalities of its occupants. A very curious room it was, with its pathetic suggestion of decayed splendor and old-world dignity; a room full of interest and character and of contrasts and perplexing contradictions. For the most part it spoke of unmistakable though decent poverty. It was nearly bare of furniture, and what little there was was of the cheapest﻿—a small kitchen table and three Windsor chairs (two of them with arms); a threadbare string carpet on the floor, and a cheap cotton cloth on the table; these, with a set of bookshelves, frankly constructed of grocer’s boxes, formed the entire suite. And yet, despite its poverty, the place exhaled an air of homely if rather ascetic comfort, and the taste was irreproachable. The quiet russet of the tablecloth struck a pleasant harmony with the subdued bluish green of the worn carpet; the Windsor chairs and the legs of the table had been carefully denuded of their glaring varnish and stained a sober brown: and the austerity of the whole was relieved by a ginger jar filled with fresh-cut flowers and set in the middle of the table.

===Chinese===
为了把注意力从那素未谋面的邻人身上引开，我好奇地环顾这间屋子，揣想居于其间的是何等人物。这实在是一间古怪的屋子，隐隐透着繁华销歇、旧日尊荣式微的可怜意味；一间充满趣味与个性、也充满反差与费解之矛盾的屋子。大体而言，它道出的是一种明白无误却尚属体面的清贫。屋里家具几近于无，仅有的几件也最是廉价——一张小厨房桌，三把温莎木椅（其中两把带扶手）；地板上铺一块磨秃了的绳绒地毯，桌上罩一块廉价的棉布台布；这些，再加上一套毫不掩饰用杂货铺木箱钉成的书架，便是全部的家具陈设了。然而，尽管清贫，这地方却散发着一股家常的、甚至可说近乎苦行的舒适气息，而且品味无可挑剔。桌布沉静的红褐色与旧地毯黯淡的青绿色相映成趣，令人悦目；几把温莎椅和桌子腿上那层刺眼的亮漆都被细心磨去，改染成素净的棕色；屋子中央桌上供着一只姜罐，插满新剪的鲜花，为满室的简素平添了一分生气。

===Original===
But the contrasts of which I have spoken were most singular and puzzling. There were the bookshelves, for instance, home made and stained at the cost of a few pence, but filled with recent and costly new works on archeology and ancient art. There were the objects on the mantelpiece: a facsimile in bronze﻿—not bronze plaster﻿—of the beautiful head of Hypnos and a pair of fine Ushabti figures. There were the decorations of the walls, a number of etchings﻿—signed proofs, every one of them﻿—of Oriental subjects, and a splendid facsimile reproduction of an Egyptian papyrus. It was incongruous in the extreme, this mingling of costly refinements with the barest and shabbiest necessaries of life, of fastidious culture with manifest poverty. I could make nothing of it. What manner of man, I wondered, was this new patient of mine? Was he a miser, hiding himself and his wealth in this obscure court? An eccentric savant? A philosopher? Or﻿—more probably﻿—a crank? But at this point my meditations were interrupted by the voice from the adjoining room, once more raised in anger.

===Chinese===
然而，我方才所说的种种反差，实在奇特得令人费解。比如那几架书，自家打造、染色不过花了几便士，架上摆的却尽是考古学与古代艺术方面新近出版的昂贵新作。又如壁炉台上的陈设：一尊许普诺斯（Hypnos）美丽头像的青铜复制品——可不是仿铜石膏的——以及一对精美的巫沙布提俑（Ushabti figures）。再看墙上的装饰：一批取材东方风物的蚀刻版画——张张都是签名试印——和一幅埃及纸草文书（Egyptian papyrus）的精美复制卷。这情形不协调到了极点：昂贵的雅致与最粗陋寒酸的生活必需品混作一处，考究的教养与昭然的贫穷凑成一堆。我全然摸不着头脑。我心想，我这位新病人究竟是个怎样的人物？是个守财奴，连人带钱财躲进这条无名僻巷？是个古怪的学者？一位哲人？还是——多半如此——个偏执的怪人？但这时我的沉思被隔壁的声音打断了，那嗓门又怒气冲冲地拔高了。

===Original===
“But I say that you *are* making an accusation! You are implying that I made away with him.”

===Chinese===
“可我说，你这就*是*在指控！你这是在暗示，是我把他干掉了。”

===Original===
“Not at all,” was the reply; “but I repeat that it is your business to ascertain what has become of him. The responsibility rests upon you.”

===Chinese===
“绝无此意，”对方答道，“但我再说一遍：查明他如今下落，是你分内之事。这责任落在你身上。”

===Original===
“Upon me!” rejoined the first voice. “And what about you? Your position is a pretty fishy one if it comes to that.”

===Chinese===
“落在我身上！”头一个声音驳道，“那你自己呢？真要这么论起来，你的立场才着实可疑呢。”

===Original===
“What!” roared the other. “Do you insinuate that I murdered my own brother?”

===Chinese===
“什么！”另一个吼道，“你是想含沙射影，说我谋害了自己的亲兄弟？”

===Original===
During this amazing colloquy I had stood gaping with sheer astonishment. Suddenly I recollected myself, and dropping into a chair, set my elbows on my knees and clapped my hands over my ears; and thus I must have remained for a full minute when I became aware of the closing of a door behind me.

===Chinese===
这场惊人的对谈进行之际，我一直张着嘴呆立原地。猛然间我回过神来，一屁股坐进椅子里，手肘撑膝，双手捂耳；想必就这副模样足足待了一分钟，才察觉身后有一扇门开了又关。

===Original===
I sprang to my feet and turned in some embarrassment (for I must have looked unspeakably ridiculous) to confront the somber figure of a rather tall and strikingly handsome girl, who, as she stood with her hand on the knob of the door, saluted me with a formal bow. In an instantaneous glance I noted how perfectly she matched her strange surroundings. Black-robed, black-haired, with black-gray eyes and a grave sad face of ivory pallor, she stood, like one of old Terborch’s portraits, a harmony in tones so low as to be but one step removed from monochrome. Obviously a lady in spite of the worn and rusty dress, and something in the poise of the head and the set of the straight brows hinted at a spirit that adversity had hardened rather than broken.

===Chinese===
我一跃而起，颇为窘迫地转过身去（我那副模样必定说不出的滑稽），迎面是一位身量颇高、容貌出众的姑娘一身素黑的身影。她一手按着门钮立在那里，向我端端正正行了个礼。电光石火的一瞥间，我便注意到她与这奇特的环境何其相称。黑衣、黑发，黑灰色的眼睛，一张庄重而忧郁的、象牙般苍白的脸——她伫立在那里，宛如老特博赫（Terborch）笔下的一幅肖像，一部色调低沉的和声，低到只差一步便是单色。尽管衣着陈旧泛乌，她显然是位淑女；头颅的仪态与平直双眉的走势间自有某种东西，隐隐透出一种被逆境淬硬而非折断的精神。

===Original===
“I must ask you to forgive me for keeping you waiting,” she said; and as she spoke a certain softening at the corners of the austere mouth reminded me of the absurd position in which she had found me.

===Chinese===
“让您久等，务请见谅。”她说；说话时，那张严冷的嘴角浮起一丝柔和，倒提醒我想起自己方才被她撞见的可笑处境。

===Original===

“嗯，我本可以送上一句俗套的忠告——保持乐观心境，避免忧烦；不过我想，您听了怕也不会觉得有多大用处。”

===Original===
“No,” she answered bitterly; “it is a counsel of perfection. People in our position are not a very cheerful class, I’m afraid; but still they don’t seek out worries from sheer perverseness. The worries come unsought. But, of course, you can’t enter into that.”

===Chinese===
“是啊，”她苦涩地答道，“那是一条只有圣人做得到的忠告。只怕处在我们这种境地的人，算不得乐观的一类；不过话说回来，他们倒也并非纯粹出于别扭才自寻烦恼。烦恼总是不请自来。当然，这层滋味，您是无从体会的。”

===Original===
“I can’t give any practical help, I fear, though I do sincerely hope that you father’s affairs will straighten themselves out soon.”

===Chinese===
“实际的帮助，我怕是给不上了，”我说，“不过我确实诚心希望，令尊的事务不久便能理顺。”

===Original===
She thanked me for my good wishes and accompanied me down to the street door, where, with a bow and a rather stiff handshake, she gave me my *congé*.

===Chinese===
她谢过我的好意，陪我一路下楼送到临街大门；在那里，她一躬身，与我略嫌生硬地握了握手，便对我下了逐客令（*congé*）。

===Original===
Very ungratefully the noise of Fetter Lane smote on my ears as I came out through the archway, and very squalid and unrestful the little street looked when contrasted with the dignity and monastic quiet of the old garden. As to the surgery, with its oilcloth floor and walls made hideous with gaudy insurance show-cards in sham gilt frames, its aspect was so revolting that I flew to the daybook for distraction, and was still busily entering the morning’s visits when the bottle-boy, Adolphus, entered stealthily to announce lunch.

===Chinese===
从拱门下走出来，菲特巷的喧嚣很不识相地扑击着我的耳膜；与那座老花园的庄重和修道院般的岑寂两相对照，这条小巷显得何等寒碜而不得安宁。至于那间诊室——油布铺的地板，墙上挂满俗艳的保险招贴画，配着冒牌镀金的框子，丑得不堪入目——那副景象实在倒人胃口，我只得逃到日志簿里去寻解脱；正当我还忙着登记上午的出诊记录时，配药小厮阿道弗斯（Adolphus）蹑手蹑脚地走进来，传报午饭已好。
