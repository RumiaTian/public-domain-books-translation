## Appendix / 附录

===Original===
> "Truth is stranger than fiction;" and whoever reads the narrative of Alfrado, will find the assertion verified.
> About eight years ago I became acquainted with the author of this book, and I feel it a privilege to speak a few words in her behalf. Through the instrumentality of an itinerant colored lecturer, she was brought to W﻿﻿—﻿—, Mass. This is an ancient town, where the mothers and daughters seek, not "wool and flax," but *straw*﻿—working willingly with their hands! Here she was introduced to the family of Mrs. Walker, who kindly consented to receive her as an inmate of her household, and immediately succeeded in procuring work for her as a "straw sewer." Being very ingenious, she soon acquired the art of making hats; but on account of former hard treatment, her constitution was greatly impaired, and she was subject to seasons of sickness. On this account Mrs. W. gave her a room joining her own chamber, where she could hear her faintest call. Never shall I forget the expression of her "black, but comely" face, as she came to me one day, exclaiming, "O, aunt J﻿﻿—﻿—, I have at last found a *home*﻿—and not only a home, but a *mother*. My cup runneth over. What shall I render to the Lord for all his benefits?"
> Months passed on, and she was *happy*﻿—truly happy. Her health began to improve under the genial sunshine in which she lived, and she even looked forward with *hope*﻿—joyful hope to the future. But, alas, "it is not in man that walketh to direct his steps." One beautiful morning in the early spring of 1842, as she was taking her usual walk, she chanced to meet her old friend, the "lecturer," who brought her to W﻿﻿—﻿—, and with him was a fugitive slave. Young, well-formed and very handsome, he said he had been a *house*-servant, which seemed to account in some measure for his gentlemanly manners and pleasing address. The meeting was entirely accidental; but it was a sad occurrence for poor Alfrado, as her own sequel tells. Suffice it to say, an acquaintance and attachment was formed, which, in due time, resulted in marriage. In a few days she left W﻿﻿—﻿—, and *all* her home comforts, and took up her abode in New Hampshire. For a while everything went on well, and she dreamed not of danger; but in an evil hour he left his young and trusting wife, and embarked for sea. She knew nothing of all this, and waited for his return. But she waited in vain. Days passed, weeks passed, and he came not; then her heart failed her. She felt herself deserted at a time, when, of all others, she most needed the care and soothing attentions of a devoted husband. For a time she tried to sustain *herself*, but this was impossible. She had friends, but they were mostly of that class who are poor in the things of earth, but "rich in faith." The charity on which she depended failed at last, and there was nothing to save her from the "County House;" *go she must*. But her feelings on her way thither, and after her arrival, can be given better in her own language; and I trust it will be no breach of confidence if I here insert part of a letter she wrote her mother Walker, concerning the matter.
> Two or three letters were received after this by her friends in W﻿﻿—﻿—, and then all was silent. No one of us knew whether she still lived or had gone to her home on high. But it seems she remained in this house until after the birth of her babe; then her faithless husband returned, and took her to some town in New Hampshire, where, for a time, he supported her and his little son decently well. But again he left her as before﻿—suddenly and unexpectedly, and she saw him no more. Her efforts were again successful in a measure in securing a meagre maintenance for a time; but her struggles with poverty and sickness were severe. At length, a door of hope was opened. A kind gentleman and lady took her little boy into their own family, and provided everything necessary for his good; and all this without the hope of remuneration. But let them know, they shall be "recompensed at the resurrection of the just." God is not unmindful of this work﻿—this labor of love. As for the afflicted mother, she too has been remembered. The heart of a stranger was moved with compassion, and bestowed a recipe upon her for restoring gray hair to its former color. She availed herself of this great help, and has been quite successful; but her health is again falling, and she has felt herself obliged to resort to another method of procuring her bread﻿—that of writing an Autobiography.
> I trust she will find a ready sale for her interesting work; and let all the friends who purchase a volume, remember they are doing good to one of the most worthy, and I had almost said most unfortunate, of the human family. I will only add in conclusion, a few lines, calculated to comfort and strengthen this sorrowful, homeless one. "I will help thee, saith the Lord."
> Allida.

> “事实比小说更离奇；”凡读过阿尔弗拉多（Alfrado）这段经历的读者，都会证实这句话不假。
> 大约八年前，我结识了本书的作者，能为她说几句话，我引以为荣。经由一位四处巡回的有色人种演说家引介，她来到了马萨诸塞的 W﻿﻿—﻿—镇。这是一座古镇，这里的母亲和女儿们寻求的不是“羊毛和麻”，而是*草*﻿——甘愿亲手劳作！她被介绍给沃克太太（Mrs. Walker）一家，这位太太慨然应允收她住进自己家中，并且立刻为她谋到一份“缝草帽”的活计。她心灵手巧，很快学会了制帽的手艺；但由于从前受过的苛待，她的身体大受亏损，时常闹病。为此，沃克太太把自己卧房隔壁的一间屋子给她住，她哪怕最轻微的一声呼唤也能听见。我永远不会忘记她那张“黑然而秀美”的脸上的神情：一天她走到我跟前，喊道：“啊，J﻿﻿—﻿—姨妈，我终于找到一个*家*﻿——不只是一个家，还有一位*母亲*。我的福杯满溢。我拿什么报答耶和华的一切厚恩呢？”
> 几个月过去了，她很快活——真正的快活。她生活在和煦的阳光里，健康渐渐好转，甚至能怀着*希望*——欢欣的希望——展望将来。可是，唉，“人的道路不由自己。”1842 年早春一个明媚的早晨，她照例散步，偶然遇见了当年把她带到 W﻿﻿—﻿—镇的老朋友、那位“演说家”，与他同行的是一个逃亡奴隶。此人年轻、体格匀称、十分英俊，自称当过*宅内*仆役，这似乎多少解释了他那绅士般的风度和讨人喜欢的谈吐。这次相逢纯属偶然；但正如她自己的后文所述，这对可怜的阿尔弗拉多是一场伤心事。简而言之，两人结识并生出情愫，到时候便结了婚。几天后她离开了 W﻿﻿—﻿—镇，抛下家中*种种*温暖，到新罕布什尔安了家。有一段日子一切顺遂，她做梦也没想到危险；可是在个不吉利的时辰，他撇下年轻而信任他的妻子，登船出海去了。这一切她一概不知，只等他回来。可她白等了。一天天过去，一星期一星期过去，他杳无音信；她的心便凉了。恰恰在她比任何时候都更需要一位尽职丈夫的照料抚慰之时，她觉出自己被遗弃了。有一阵她试图*自己*撑住，可这办不到。她也有朋友，但多半是那种在地上贫穷、“在信上富足”的人。她倚靠的赈济终于断了，再没有什么能救她免进“县济贫院”；*她非去不可*。但她前去路上的心情和到达之后的情形，用她自己的话来讲更好；我想这不算辜负她的信任，在此摘录她为此事写给沃克妈妈的一封信的一部分。
> 此后她在 W﻿﻿—﻿—镇的朋友们又收到她两三封信，随后便音信全无。我们谁都不知道她是尚在人间，还是已归天家。但看来她在这座济贫院里一直住到生下孩子；随后她那负心的丈夫回来了，把她接到新罕布什尔的某个镇上，一度把她和小儿子供养得还算像样。可是他又像从前那样离开了她——来得突然，去得也突然，她再没见过他。她再度奔走，多少又为自己挣得了一点勉强度日的口粮；但她与贫穷和疾病抗争的日子极其艰难。终于，一扇希望之门开了。一位仁厚的先生和太太把她的小男孩接到自己家中，供给他一切所需；而这一切并不图任何报偿。但愿他们知道，他们必“在义人复活的时候得着报答”。神必不忘记这善工——这出于爱心的劳苦。至于这位忧患的母亲，她也没有被遗忘。一个陌生人心生怜悯，送给她一个能把白发染回原色的方子。她借这桩大有帮助的营生，日子过得颇有起色；可她的健康又在衰败，她觉得不得不另谋一条糊口之路——那就是写一本自传。
> 我盼望她这本饶有趣味的书能够畅销；愿每一位购书的朋友都记得，他们是在造福人类大家庭中最值得敬重、也几乎可以说是最命苦的一员。最后我只想添上几句，好安慰、坚固这忧伤无家的人：“主说，我必帮助你。”
> 阿莉达（Allida）

> Having known the writer of this book for a number of years, and knowing the many privations and mortifications she has had to pass through, I the more willingly add my testimony to the truth of her assertions. She is one of that class, who by some are considered not only as little lower than the angels, but far beneath them; but I have long since learned that we are not to look at the color of the hair, the eyes, or the skin, for the man or woman; their life is the criterion we are to judge by. The writer of this book has seemed to be a child of misfortune.
> Early in life she was deprived of her parents, and all those endearing associations to which childhood clings. Indeed, she may be said not to have had that happy period; for, being taken from home so young, and placed where she had nothing to love or cling to, I often wonder she had not grown up a *monster*; and those very people calling themselves Christians, (the good Lord deliver me from such,) and they likewise ruined her health by hard work, both in the field and house. She was indeed a slave, in every sense of the word; and a lonely one, too.
> But she has found some friends in this degraded world, that were willing to do by others as they would have others do by them; that were willing she should live, and have an existence on the earth with them. She has never enjoyed any degree of comfortable health since she was eighteen years of age, and a great deal of the time has been confined to her room and bed. She is now trying to write a book; and I hope the public will look favorably on it, and patronize the same, for she is a worthy woman.
> Her own health being poor, and having a child to care for, (for, by the way, she has been married,) and she wishes to educate him; in her sickness he has been taken from her, and sent to the county farm, because she could not pay his board every week; but as soon as she was able, she took him from that *place*, and now he has a home where he is contented and happy, and where he is considered as good as those he is with. He is an intelligent, smart boy, and no doubt will make a smart man, if he is rightly managed. He is beloved by his playmates, and by all the friends of the family; for the family do not recognize those as friends who do not include him in their family, or as one of them, and his mother as a daughter﻿—for they treat her as such; and she certainly deserves all the affection and kindness that is bestowed upon her, and they are always happy to have her visit them whenever she will. They are not wealthy, but the latchstring is always out when suffering humanity needs a shelter; the last loaf they are willing to divide with those more needy than themselves, remembering these words, Do good as we have opportunity; and we can always find opportunity, if we have the disposition.
> And now I would say, I hope those who call themselves friends of our dark-skinned brethren, will lend a helping hand, and assist our sister, not in giving, but in buying a book; the expense is trifling, and the reward of doing good is great. Our duty is to our fellow-beings, and when we let an opportunity pass, we know not what we lose. Therefore we should do with all our might what our hands find to do; and remember the words of Him who went about doing good, that inasmuch as ye have done a good deed to one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it to me; and even a cup of water is not forgotten. Therefore, let us work while the day lasts, and we shall in no wise lose our reward.
> Margaretta Thorn.

> 我认识本书作者已有多年，深知她历经了多少穷困和屈辱，因此更乐于为她陈述的真实作证。她属于那样一个阶层：有些人不但认为他们比天使低一等，简直认为他们远在人下；可我早已明白，评判一个男人或女人，不可看头发、眼睛或皮肤的颜色；他们的生活才是我们评判的准绳。本书的作者可说是个苦命的孩子。
> 她自幼失去父母，失去童年所依恋的一切亲爱关系。说实在的，她简直可以说不曾有过那段幸福的时光；因为那么小就离开了家，被安置在一个无物可爱、无物可依的地方，我常奇怪她怎么没有长成一个*心怀怨毒的怪物*；而正是那些自称基督徒的人（愿好上帝救我远离这样的人）——也正是他们让她在田里屋里做苦工，毁坏了她的身体。她确确实实是个奴隶，从一切意义上说都是；而且是个孤零零的奴隶。
> 但在这个沉沦的世上，她也找到了一些朋友，愿意照着愿人待己之道去待人；愿意让她活下去，同他们一起在地上存身。她自十八岁以来从未有过几天舒坦的日子，大部分时间困守房中、缠绵病榻。她如今正尝试写一本书；我希望公众垂青于此书，惠予购读，因为她是个值得敬重的女子。
> 她自己的健康不佳，又要抚养一个孩子（对了，她结过婚），还想让孩子受教育；她病中，孩子被人从她身边带走，送进了县贫民农场，因为她付不起每周的膳宿费；可她身子一撑得住，就把孩子从那个*地方*领了出来，如今孩子有了一个家，住得称心快活，在那里人们待他同自家孩子一般亲。他是个聪明伶俐的男孩，只要教养得法，将来必成大器。玩伴们喜欢他，那家人的一切朋友也都喜欢他；因为那一家人只把待他如自家一员、待他母亲如亲生女儿的人认作朋友——他们正是这样待她的；她受得起人们加给她的一切疼爱和善意，而无论她何时登门，他们都欢欢喜喜接待。他们并不富有，但只要受苦的人需要栖身之所，门上的拉绳永远挂在门外；家里最后一条面包，他们也甘愿与比自己更穷的人分食，因为他们记得这句话：有机会就要行善；只要存着这份心，机会总是找得到的。
> 如今我只想说：凡自称为我们深色皮肤弟兄之友的人，望都伸出援手，帮我们这位姐妹一把——不是白送，而是买一本书；花费微不足道，行善的赏赐却大。我们的本分在于同胞；机会一旦错过，我们不知道失去了什么。所以，凡我们手所当做的事，当尽力去做；并记住那位周流四方行善事者的话：这些事你们既做在我这弟兄中一个最小的身上，就是做在我身上了；即便一杯凉水也不被忘记。所以，趁着白日，让我们做工吧，我们的赏赐断不会失落。
> 玛格丽塔·索恩（Margaretta Thorn）

> Feeling a deep interest in the welfare of the writer of this book, and hoping that its circulation will be extensive, I wish to say a few words in her behalf. I have been acquainted with her for several years, and have always found her worthy the esteem of all friends of humanity; one whose soul is alive to the work to which she puts her hand. Although her complexion is a little darker than my own, I esteem it a privilege to associate with her, and assist her whenever an opportunity presents itself. It is with this motive that I write these few lines, knowing this book must be interesting to all who have any knowledge of the writer's character, or wish to have. I hope no one will refuse to aid her in her work, as she is worthy the sympathy of all Christians, and those who have a spark of humanity in their breasts.
> Thinking it unnecessary for me to write a long epistle, I will close by bidding her God speed.
> C. D. S.

> 我对本书作者的境遇深为关切，盼此书广为流传，愿为她讲几句话。我与她相识多年，一向觉得她当得起一切人类之友的敬重；她是一个一心扑在手上事工之上的人。虽然她的肤色比我的深些，我仍以与她交往为荣，一有机会便相助。正是抱着这个心愿，我写下这几行字；我知道凡了解作者为人、或愿意了解其为人的人，这本书必能引起他们的兴味。我希望没有人拒绝在她的工作上助她一臂之力，因为她当得起一切基督徒、以及胸中尚存一星人性火花之人的同情。
> 想来不必写一封长信，我就此收笔，愿神赐她顺利。
> C. D. S.
