### The Isle of Pines / 松树岛

===Original===
For many years there lived near the town of Gallipolis, Ohio, an old man named Herman Deluse. Very little was known of his history, for he would neither speak of it himself nor suffer others. It was a common belief among his neighbors that he had been a pirate—if upon any better evidence than his collection of boarding pikes, cutlasses, and ancient flintlock pistols, no one knew. He lived entirely alone in a small house of four rooms, falling rapidly into decay and never repaired further than was required by the weather. It stood on a slight elevation in the midst of a large, stony field overgrown with brambles, and cultivated in patches and only in the most primitive way. It was his only visible property, but could hardly have yielded him a living, simple and few as were his wants. He seemed always to have ready money, and paid cash for all his purchases at the village stores roundabout, seldom buying more than two or three times at the same place until after the lapse of a considerable time. He got no commendation, however, for this equitable distribution of his patronage; people were disposed to regard it as an ineffectual attempt to conceal his possession of so much money. That he had great hoards of ill-gotten gold buried somewhere about his tumbledown dwelling was not reasonably to be doubted by any honest soul conversant with the facts of local tradition and gifted with a sense of the fitness of things.

===Chinese===
在俄亥俄州加利波利斯（Gallipolis）镇附近，多年来住着一个名叫赫尔曼·德卢斯（Herman Deluse）的老人。他的来历几乎无人知晓，因为他既不肯自己提起，也不许别人打听。邻居们普遍相信他曾是个海盗——但除了他收藏的一堆登船长矛、弯刀和旧式燧发手枪之外，并没有更好的证据。他独自住在一栋四间小屋里，房屋衰败得很快，除了应付风雨所必需的之外从未修缮过。小屋坐落在一块布满荆棘的大石田中央的微微隆起处，那田地只零星地、用最原始的方式耕种着。这是他唯一可见的财产，但即使他的需求再简单再少，也很难靠它维持生计。他似乎总是有现成的钱，周围村店里的每笔买卖都付现金，很少在同一家店买两次以上，除非隔了相当长一段时间。然而，人们并不因他如此公平地分配惠顾而称赞他；大家倾向于认为这是一种拙劣的掩饰，目的是掩盖他拥有那么多钱的事实。对于熟悉当地传说、又懂得世事常理的诚实人来说，合理地相信他在破败的住所周围某处埋藏着大量不义之财，并非不可理喻。

===Original===
On the 9th of November, 1867, the old man died; at least his dead body was discovered on the 10th, and physicians testified that death had occurred about twenty-four hours previously—precisely how, they were unable to say; for the postmortem examination showed every organ to be absolutely healthy, with no indication of disorder or violence. According to them, death must have taken place about noonday, yet the body was found in bed. The verdict of the coroner's jury was that he "came to his death by a visitation of God." The body was buried and the public administrator took charge of the estate.

===Chinese===
1867年11月9日，老人死了；至少他的尸体是在10日被发现的，医生证明死亡大约发生在二十四小时以前——具体死因他们无法断定；因为尸检显示每个器官都绝对健康，没有任何疾病或暴力的迹象。据他们判断，死亡应发生在正午前后，但尸体是在床上被发现的。验尸陪审团的裁决是"因上帝的召唤而死"。遗体被埋葬，公共管理人接管了遗产。

===Original===
A rigorous search disclosed nothing more than was already known about the dead man, and much patient excavation here and there about the premises by thoughtful and thrifty neighbors went unrewarded. The administrator locked up the house against the time when the property, real and personal, should be sold by law with a view to defraying, partly, the expenses of the sale.

===Chinese===
一次彻底搜查除了已知的情况外，并未发现关于死者的更多信息，而周围那些精明节俭的邻居们在宅基地各处耐心挖掘也一无所获。管理人将房屋锁上，等待不动产和动产依法出售，部分用于支付出售费用。

===Original===
The night of November 20 was boisterous. A furious gale stormed across the country, scourging it with desolating drifts of sleet. Great trees were torn from the earth and hurled across the roads. So wild a night had never been known in all that region, but toward morning the storm had blown itself out of breath and day dawned bright and clear. At about eight o'clock that morning the Rev. Henry Galbraith, a well-known and highly esteemed Lutheran minister, arrived on foot at his house, a mile and a half from the Deluse place. Mr. Galbraith had been for a month in Cincinnati. He had come up the river in a steamboat, and landing at Gallipolis the previous evening had immediately obtained a horse and buggy and set out for home. The violence of the storm had delayed him over night, and in the morning the fallen trees had compelled him to abandon his conveyance and continue his journey afoot.

===Chinese===
11月20日夜间狂风大作。一场猛烈的风暴席卷大地，夹带着凄冷的冻雨肆虐。大树被连根拔起，抛到路面上。整个地区从未经历过如此凶猛的夜，但临近拂晓时风暴已力竭，天亮时一片晴朗。那天早上大约八点钟，亨利·加尔布雷思牧师（Rev. Henry Galbraith）——一位受人敬重的路德宗牧师——步行抵达自家门前，距德卢斯家一英里半。加尔布雷思先生已在辛辛那提待了一个月。他乘汽船沿河而来，前一晚在加利波利斯上岸后，立刻雇了匹马和一辆轻便马车，启程回家。风暴之猛烈使他在途中耽搁了一夜，到了早上，倒下的树木迫使他弃车步行。

===Original===
"But where did you pass the night?" inquired his wife, after he had briefly related his adventure.
"With old Deluse at the 'Isle of Pines,'" was the laughing reply; "and a glum enough time I had of it. He made no objection to my remaining, but not a word could I get out of him."

===Chinese===
"那你昨晚在哪儿过的夜？"妻子听他简要叙述了遭遇后问道。
"在老德卢斯家，'松树岛'那儿，"他笑着回答；"可把我闷坏了。他不反对我在那儿留宿，可我一个字也套不出来。"

===Original===
Fortunately for the interests of truth there was present at this conversation Mr. Robert Mosely Maren, a lawyer and littérateur of Columbus, the same who wrote the delightful "Mellowcraft Papers." Noting, but apparently not sharing, the astonishment caused by Mr. Galbraith's answer this ready-witted person checked by a gesture the exclamations that would naturally have followed, and tranquilly inquired: "How came you to go in there?"

===Chinese===
所幸这场谈话时在场的有罗伯特·莫西利·马伦先生（Robert Mosely Maren），他是哥伦布市的律师兼文人，正是那位写了妙趣横生的《醇酿录》（Mellowcraft Papers）的人。他注意到——但显然并未感受到——加尔布雷思先生的回答引起的震惊，这位机敏的人用手势止住了随之而来的惊呼，平静地问道："你是怎么跑到那儿去的？"

===Original===
This is Mr. Maren's version of Mr. Galbraith's reply:

===Chinese===
以下是马伦先生记录的加尔布雷思先生的答复：

===Original===
"I saw a light moving about the house, and being nearly blinded by the sleet, and half frozen besides, drove in at the gate and put up my horse in the old rail stable, where it is now. I then rapped at the door, and getting no invitation went in without one. The room was dark, but having matches I found a candle and lit it. I tried to enter the adjoining room, but the door was fast, and although I heard the old man's heavy footsteps in there he made no response to my calls. There was no fire on the hearth, so I made one and laying down before it with my overcoat under my head, prepared myself for sleep. Pretty soon the door that I had tried silently opened and the old man came in, carrying a candle. I spoke to him pleasantly, apologizing for my intrusion, but he took no notice of me. He seemed to be searching for something, though his eyes were unmoved in their sockets. I wonder if he ever walks in his sleep. He took a circuit a part of the way round the room, and went out the same way he had come in. Twice more before I sleep he came back into the room, acting precisely the same way, and departing as at first. In the intervals I heard him tramping all over the house, his footsteps distinctly audible in the pauses of the storm. When I woke in the morning he had already gone out."

===Chinese===
"我看见有灯光在房子周围移动，而且冻雨几乎让我睁不开眼，加上又冻得半死，就驾车进了大门，把马拴在旧木棚里，现在还在那儿。然后我敲了门，没人应声，就自己进去了。屋里很暗，但我带着火柴，找到一支蜡烛点上。我试着进隔壁房间，但门锁着，尽管我听见老人在里面沉重的脚步声，他却对我的呼喊毫无反应。壁炉里没有火，于是我自己生了火，把大衣垫在头下，躺在炉前，准备睡觉。不久，我试过的那扇门悄无声息地开了，老人拿着蜡烛走了进来。我友好地跟他说话，为我的闯入道歉，但他对我毫不理睬。他似乎在找什么东西，尽管他的眼珠一动不动。我怀疑他是不是在梦游。他在房间里绕了半圈，然后从进来的路出去了。在我入睡之前他又回来了两次，行为完全一样，走时也和第一次一样。间隙中我听到他在整栋房子里踱来踱去，风暴暂停时他的脚步声清晰可闻。等我早上醒来时，他已经出去了。"

===Original===
Mr. Maren attempted some further questioning, but was unable longer to restrain the family's tongues; the story of Deluse's death and burial came out, greatly to the good minister's astonishment.

===Chinese===
马伦先生试图进一步询问，但已无法再堵住这家人的嘴；德卢斯死亡和下葬的事被和盘托出，善良的牧师大为惊讶。

===Original===
"The explanation of your adventure is very simple," said Mr. Maren. "I don't believe old Deluse walks in his sleep—not in his present one; but you evidently dream in yours."

===Chinese===
"你那番奇遇的解释很简单，"马伦先生说。"我不相信老德卢斯会梦游——至少在他现在这种状态下不会；但你显然在做梦。"

===Original===
And to this view of the matter Mr. Galbraith was compelled reluctantly to assent.

===Chinese===
对此观点，加尔布雷思先生不得不勉强表示同意。

===Original===
Nevertheless, a late hour of the next night found these two gentlemen, accompanied by a son of the minister, in the road in front of the old Deluse house. There was a light inside; it appeared now at one window and now at another. The three men advanced to the door. Just as they reached it there came from the interior a confusion of the most appalling sounds—the clash of weapons, steel against steel, sharp explosions as of firearms, shrieks of women, groans and the curses of men in combat! The investigators stood a moment, irresolute, frightened. Then Mr. Galbraith tried the door. It was fast. But the minister was a man of courage, a man, moreover, of Herculean strength. He retired a pace or two and rushed against the door, striking it with his right shoulder and bursting it from the frame with a loud crash. In a moment the three were inside. Darkness and silence! The only sound was the beating of their hearts.

===Chinese===
然而，第二天深夜，这两位绅士在牧师一个儿子的陪同下，出现在老德卢斯家门前的路上。屋里有灯光；时而从这个窗户透出，时而从那个窗户闪现。三人走到门口。就在他们到达的那一刻，屋内传来一阵最骇人的声响混杂——兵器碰撞，钢铁交击，火器的尖锐爆响，女人的尖叫，男人格斗时的呻吟和咒骂！调查者们站了一会儿，犹豫不决，惊恐万分。然后加尔布雷思先生试了试门。锁着。但牧师是个勇敢的人，而且力大无穷。他退后一两步，猛地撞向大门，用右肩猛撞，随着一声巨响把门从门框上撞开了。转眼间三人进入了屋内。一片漆黑，万籁俱寂！唯一的声音是他们自己的心跳。

===Original===
Mr. Maren had provided himself with matches and a candle. With some difficulty, begotten of his excitement, he made a light, and they proceeded to explore the place, passing from room to room. Everything was in orderly arrangement, as it had been left by the sheriff; nothing had been disturbed. A light coating of dust was everywhere. A back door was partly open, as if by neglect, and their first thought was that the authors of the awful revelry might have escaped. The door was opened, and the light of the candle shone through upon the ground. The expiring effort of the previous night's storm had been a light fall of snow; there were no footprints; the white surface was unbroken. They closed the door and entered the last room of the four that the house contained—that farthest from the road, in an angle of the building. Here the candle in Mr. Maren's hand was suddenly extinguished as by a draught of air. Almost immediately followed the sound of a heavy fall. When the candle had been hastily relighted young Mr. Galbraith was seen prostrate on the floor at a little distance from the others. He was dead. In one hand the body grasped a heavy sack of coins, which later examination showed to be all of old Spanish mintage. Directly over the body as it lay, a board had been torn from its fastenings in the wall, and from the cavity so disclosed it was evident that the bag had been taken.

===Chinese===
马伦先生备有火柴和蜡烛。由于激动，点火时费了些周折，随后他们开始逐室搜查。一切都整齐有序，保持着治安官离开时的样子；没有任何被动过。到处落着薄薄的灰尘。后门半开着，像是疏忽所致，他们首先想到的是那场可怕狂欢的制造者们可能已经逃走了。门被打开，烛光照到外面的地上。前夜风暴的余威带来了一场薄薄的降雪；没有脚印；洁白的地面完好无损。他们关上门，走进房屋四间房中最靠里的那间——离路最远，在建筑的一角。这时马伦先生手中的蜡烛突然被一阵气流吹灭了。紧接着传来一声沉重的倒地声。等蜡烛匆匆重新点亮时，年轻的加尔布雷思先生已被发现趴在离其他人不远的地板上。他死了。一只手紧紧攥着一个沉甸甸的钱袋，后来检查显示里面全是西班牙旧币。尸体正上方，一块木板从墙上的固定处被撕裂下来，从露出的洞穴可以明显看出，那个钱袋就是从那里取出的。

===Original===
Another inquest was held: another postmortem examination failed to reveal a probable cause of death. Another verdict of "the visitation of God" left all at liberty to form their own conclusions. Mr. Maren contended that the young man died of excitement.

===Chinese===
又进行了一次验尸：又一次尸检未能发现可能的死因。又一份"上帝的召唤"的裁决，让所有人自行得出结论。马伦先生坚持认为，年轻人死于过度兴奋。
