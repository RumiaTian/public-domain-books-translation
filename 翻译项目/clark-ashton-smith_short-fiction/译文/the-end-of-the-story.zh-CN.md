## The End of the Story / 故事的结局

===Original===
The following narrative was found among the papers of Christophe Morand, a young law-student of Tours, after his unaccountable disappearance during a visit at his father’s home near Moulins, in November, 1798:

===Chinese===
以下这篇记述，是在克里斯托夫·莫朗（Christophe Morand）的遗稿之中发现的。莫朗是图尔（Tours）一名年轻的法科学生；1798年11月，他在穆兰（Moulins）附近父亲家中省亲期间，不明不白地失踪：

===Original===
A sinister brownish-purple autumn twilight, made premature by the imminence of a sudden thunderstorm, had filled the forest of Averoigne. The trees along my road were already blurred to ebon masses, and the road itself, pale and spectral before me in the thickening gloom, seemed to waver and quiver slightly, as with the tremor of some mysterious earthquake. I spurred my horse, who was woefully tired with a journey begun at dawn, and had fallen hours ago to a protesting and reluctant trot, and we galloped adown the darkening road between enormous oaks that seemed to lean toward us with boughs like clutching fingers as we passed.

With dreadful rapidity, the night was upon us, the blackness became a tangible clinging veil; a nightmare confusion and desperation drove me to spur my mount again with a more cruel rigor; and now, as we went, the first far-off mutter of the storm mingled with the clatter of my horse’s hoofs, and the first lightning flashes illumed our way, which, to my amazement (since I believed myself on the main highway through Averoigne), had inexplicably narrowed to a well-trodden footpath. Feeling sure that I had gone astray, but not caring to retrace my steps in the teeth of darkness and the towering clouds of the tempest, I hurried on, hoping, as seemed reasonable, that a path so plainly worn would lead eventually to some house or château where I could find refuge for the night. My hope was wellfounded, for within a few minutes I descried a glimmering light through the forest-boughs, and came suddenly to an open glade, where, on a gentle eminence, a large building loomed, with several litten windows in the lower story, and a top that was well-nigh indistinguishable against the bulks of driven cloud.

===Chinese===
一场骤然将至的雷暴将暮色提前催落；那不祥的、褐紫色的秋日黄昏，已然弥漫了整座阿维罗涅（Averoigne）的森林。我沿途所经的树木，早已模糊作一团团漆黑的阴影；而道路本身，在愈来愈浓的幽暗之中于我前方显得苍白而鬼气森森，似在微微地摇曳、战栗，宛如受了某场神秘地震的惊扰。我策动坐骑——那牲口因自破晓便启程的跋涉而疲顿已极，数个时辰之前便已委顿下来，满腹怨尤地踽踽小跑——我们沿着那条渐次沉入黑暗的道路飞驰，两旁参天的橡树在我们掠过之际，仿佛以形如攫人五指的枝桠向我们倾身俯压。

黑夜以可怖的迅疾降临到我们头上，那漆黑化作了一重触手可及、紧黏着人的帷幕；一场梦魇般的慌乱与焦灼驱使我以更无情的力道再度策马；此时我们行进之间，风暴最初的、远远的闷响已与我坐骑蹄声的嘚嘚交织在一起，最初的几道闪电照亮了我们前路——令我惊诧的是（因我自以为正走在贯穿阿维罗涅的大道之上），那路竟莫名其妙地收窄成一条被人踩踏得踏实的脚径。我明知自己已经走岔，却不愿在漆黑与那直压而下的风暴乌云之中循原路折返，便继续赶路，心想（这似乎也合乎情理）：一条踩踏得如此明晰的小径，终归会通向某处宅邸或城堡，我可在那里觅得一夜的庇身之所。我这一线希望并非落空——不过数分钟，我便透过林间的枝叶瞥见一点闪烁的灯火，随即骤然来到一片空旷的林间草地上；在一处和缓的高丘之上，一座庞大的建筑影影绰绰地矗立着，下层有几扇透着灯光的窗户，而它的顶端，在那一团团疾驰的乌云的衬托之下，几乎已分辨不清。

===Original===
“Doubtless a monastery,” I thought, as I drew rein, and descending from my exhausted mount, lifted the heavy brazen knocker in the form of a dog’s head and let it fall on the oaken door. The sound was unexpectedly loud and sonorous, with a reverberation almost sepulchral, and I shivered involuntarily, with a sense of startlement, of unwonted dismay. This, a moment later, was wholly dissipated when the door was thrown open and a tall, ruddy-featured monk stood before me in the cheerful glow of the cressets that illumed a capacious hallway.

“I bid you welcome to the abbey of Perigon,” he said, in a suave rumble, and even as he spoke, another robed and hooded figure appeared and took my horse in charge. As I murmured my thanks and acknowledgments, the storm broke and tremendous gusts of rain, accompanied by ever-nearing peals of thunder, drove with demoniac fury on the door that had closed behind me.

“It is fortunate that you found us when you did,” observed my host. “ ’Twere ill for man and beast to be abroad in such a hell-brew.”

===Chinese===
“想必是一座修道院，”我心想，一边勒住马，从那疲惫不堪的坐骑上翻身下来，提起那枚铸成狗头形状的沉重铜门环，让它落在橡木的门扉之上。那声响出乎意料地洪亮低沉，带着一种近乎墓穴般的回响，我不由自主地打了个寒噤，涌起一阵惊愕、一阵异乎寻常的惶遽。然而片刻之后，这一切便荡然无存——门扉洞开，一位身材高大、面色红润的修士，在几盏照亮了宽敞门厅的油灯那欢快的辉光之中，出现在我面前。

“欢迎阁下来到佩里贡修道院（abbey of Perigon），”他用一种温醇低沉的嗓音说道；话音未落，又一位身披长袍、兜帽罩首的身影出现，将我的马牵去照管。当我低声致谢道劳之际，风暴骤然发作，一阵急似一阵的雷鸣伴着倾盆急雨，以恶魔般的狂暴，扑打在我身后那扇已然合拢的门上。

“你能在那个时候寻到我们，实属万幸，”我的主人说道。“这等地狱里熬煮出的风狂雨暴之中，人也好，畜生也好，在外头奔走可实在不是什么好事。”

===Original===
Divining without question that I was hungry as well as tired, he led me to the refectory and set before me a bountiful meal of mutton, brown bread, lentils and a strong excellent red wine.

He sat opposite me at the refectory table while I ate, and, with my hunger a little mollified, I took occasion to scan him more attentively. He was both tall and stoutly built, and his features, where the brow was no less broad than the powerful jaw, betokened intellect as well as a love for good living. A certain delicacy and refinement, an air of scholarship, of good taste and good breeding, emanated from him, and I thought to myself: “This monk is probably a connoisseur of books as well as of wines.” Doubtless my expression betrayed the quickening of my curiosity, for he said, as if in answer:

===Chinese===
他毫不费疑地看出我又累又饿，便领我到食堂，在我面前摆下一席丰盛的饭菜：羊肉、黑面包、扁豆，还有一杯浓烈而极上乘的红葡萄酒。

我在食堂的长案前用餐时，他坐在我的对面；待我的饥火稍稍平息，我便趁机将他端详得更仔细些。他身量既高且壮，面庞上那与有力的下颌同样宽阔的额头，透出学识，亦显出对优裕生活之爱好。一种难以言传的精致与文雅，一种饱学的、品味不凡且教养良好的气度，自他身上散发出来，我心中暗想：“这位修士，恐怕既是美酒的鉴赏家，也是好书的赏鉴家。”想必我的神情泄露了那骤然涌起的好奇，因为他仿佛作答似地说道：

===Original===
“I am Hilaire, the abbot of Perigon. We are a Benedictine order, who live in amity with God and with all men, and we do not hold that the spirit is to be enriched by the mortification or impoverishment of the body. We have in our butteries an abundance of wholesome fare, in our cellars the best and oldest vintages of the district of Averoigne. And, if such things interest you, as mayhap they do, we have a library that is stocked with rare tomes, with precious manuscripts, with the finest works of heathendom and Christendom, even to certain unique writings that survived the holocaust of Alexandria.”

“I appreciate your hospitality,” I said, bowing. “I am Christophe Morand, a law-student, on my way home from Tours to my father’s estate near Moulins. I, too, am a lover of books, and nothing would delight me more than the privilege of inspecting a library so rich and curious as the one whereof you speak.”

Forthwith, while I finished my meal, we fell to discussing the classics, and to quoting and capping passages from Latin, Greek or Christian authors. My host, I soon discovered, was a scholar of uncommon attainments, with an erudition, a ready familiarity with both ancient and modern literature that made my own seem as that of the merest beginner by comparison. He, on his part, was so good as to commend my far from perfect Latin, and by the time I had emptied my bottle of red wine we were chatting familiarly like old friends.

All my fatigue had now flown, to be succeeded by a rare sense of well-being, of physical comfort combined with mental alertness and keenness. So, when the abbot suggested that we pay a visit to the library, I assented with alacrity.

===Chinese===
“我是希莱尔（Hilaire），佩里贡修道院的院长。我们属本笃会（Benedictine），与上帝、与一切世人皆和睦共处；我们并不以为，灵魂的丰盈须借肉身的苦行或匮乏来成就。我们的储膳室里备有丰足的康健之食，地窖之中藏有阿维罗涅全境最上乘、最陈年的佳酿。再者，若这类事物也能令阁下发生兴趣——也许确是如此——我们还有一座藏书楼，里面满是珍本典籍、贵重写卷，汇集了异教与基督教世界最精妙的著作，甚至还有几部劫后仅存的孤本，是从亚历山大城那场浩劫之中幸存下来的。”

“多谢您的盛情款待，”我躬身说道。“我是克里斯托夫·莫朗，一名法科学生，正从图尔赶回穆兰附近父亲的田庄。我亦是个爱书之人，若能有幸一观您所说的那样一座丰富而珍奇的藏书楼，再没有比这更叫我欢喜的事了。”

于是，在我吃完那顿饭的当儿，我们便讨论起古典，引征并互答拉丁、希腊或基督教作家的章句。我很快便发觉，我的主人是一位造诣非凡的学者，其博学、其对古今著述那种信手拈来的熟稔，相形之下使我自家的所学显得不过是个最浅陋的初学者。他那一头，倒也不吝称赞我那远称不上完美的拉丁文；待我将那一瓶红葡萄酒饮尽之时，我们已如老友一般熟络地闲谈了。

我这一路的劳乏此刻已飞散净尽，取而代之的是一种难得的安适之感——身体的舒泰与心智的清明敏锐交织在一起。于是，当院长提议我们去看一看藏书楼时，我便欣然应允。

===Original===
He led me down a long corridor, on each side of which were cells belonging to the brothers of the order, and unlocked, with a huge brazen key that depended from his girdle, the door of a great room with lofty ceiling and several deep-set windows. Truly, he had not exaggerated the resources of the library; for the long shelves were overcrowded with books, and many volumes were piled high on the tables or stacked in corners. There were rolls of papyrus, of parchment, of vellum; there were strange Byzantine or Coptic bibles; there were old Arabic and Persian manuscripts with floriated or jewel-studded covers; there were scores of incunabula from the first printing-presses; there were innumerable monkish copies of antique authors, bound in wood or ivory, with rich illuminations and lettering that was often in itself a work of art.

With a care that was both loving and meticulous, the abbot Hilaire brought out volume after volume for my inspection. Many of them I had never seen before; some were unknown to me even by fame or rumor. My excited interest, my unfeigned enthusiasm, evidently pleased him, for at length he pressed a hidden spring in one of the library tables and drew out a long drawer, in which, he told me, were certain treasures that he did not care to bring forth for the edification or delectation of many, and whose very existence was undreamed of by the monks.

===Chinese===
他领我穿过一条长长的走廊，走廊两侧便是修士们的静室；随后，他以一柄悬于腰带之上的巨大铜钥匙，打开了一间高大宽敞、开着几扇深凹窗户的大屋的房门。他确不曾夸大那座藏书楼的丰厚家底：长长的书架塞得满满当当，许多卷册高高叠在案头，或堆叠于角落。那里有纸莎草、羊皮纸、犊皮纸的卷轴；有奇异的拜占庭或科普特语《圣经》；有古老的阿拉伯与波斯文写卷，封面之上饰以花叶纹或嵌满珠宝；有数十册初代印刷机印行的摇篮本；更有无数修士手抄的古典作家著作，以木质或象牙装帧，彩绘华美，那字迹本身便往往已是一件艺术品。

希莱尔院长以一种既眷爱又审慎的细心，一卷接一卷地取出书来供我观览。其中许多我前所未见；有些甚至连名气与传闻也未曾听闻。我那兴奋的兴致、我那毫不作伪的热忱，显然使他甚为欢悦，因为到了后来，他按动藏书楼一张书案内的一个暗簧，抽出一只长长的抽屉——他告诉我，里面放着几件他不愿轻易示人、以供多数人开蒙或赏玩的珍品，而这些珍品的存在，连修道院里的修士们也做梦都不曾想到。

===Original===
“Here,” he continued, “are three odes by Catullus which you will not find in any published edition of his works. Here, also, is an original manuscript of Sappho—a complete copy of a poem otherwise extant only in brief fragments; here are two of the lost tales of Miletus, a letter of Perides to Aspasia, an unknown dialogue of Plato and an old Arabian work on astronomy, by some anonymous author, in which the theories of Copernicus are anticipated. And, lastly, here is the somewhat infamous Histoire d’Amour, by Bernard de Vaillantcoeur, which was destroyed immediately upon publication, and of which only one other copy is known to exist.”

===Chinese===
“这里，”他接着说，“是卡图卢斯（Catullus）的三首颂歌，你在任何已刊行的他的作品集里都找不到。这里还有萨福（Sappho）的一部原稿——一首诗的完整抄本，除此之外，这首诗仅以零星残篇存世；这里有两篇已佚的米利都（Miletus）故事，一封佩里德斯（Perides）致阿斯帕西娅（Aspasia）的信函，一篇无人知晓的柏拉图（Plato）对话录，还有一部出自某位无名作者之手的古老阿拉伯文天文学著作，其中竟已预示了哥白尼（Copernicus）的学说。最后，这里还有一部名声颇为不堪的《爱史》（Histoire d’Amour），作者贝尔纳·德·瓦扬克尔（Bernard de Vaillantcoeur），此书甫一刊行即被销毁，已知存世者除此之外仅另有一部。”

===Original===
As I gazed with mingled awe and curiosity on the unique, unheard-of treasures he displayed, I saw in one corner of the drawer what appeared to be a thin volume with plain untitled binding of dark leather. I ventured to pick it up, and found that it contained a few sheets of closely written manuscript in old French.

“And this?” I queried, turning to look at Hilaire, whose face, to my amazement, had suddenly assumed a melancholy and troubled expression.

“It were better not to ask, my son.” He crossed himself as he spoke, and his voice was no longer mellow, but harsh, agitated, full of a sorrowful perturbation. “There is a curse on the pages that you hold in your hand: an evil spell, a malign power is attached to them, and he who would venture to peruse them is henceforward in dire peril both of body and soul.” He took the little volume from me as he spoke, and returned it to the drawer, again crossing himself carefully as he did so.

===Chinese===
当我怀着敬畏与好奇交织的心情，凝望他所展示的那一件件举世无双、闻所未闻的珍品时，我瞥见抽屉的一角有一本看似薄薄的、以深色皮革素净装帧、未题书名的卷册。我斗胆将它拿起，发现里面是寥寥数页密密写满的古法文手稿。

“这一本呢？”我问道，转过头去看希莱尔——令我惊愕的是，他的脸上忽然浮起一种忧郁而惶乱的神情。

“最好莫问，我的孩子。”他一边说，一边在胸前画了个十字，他的嗓音不再醇厚，而是变得粗粝、激越，满含一种哀痛的扰攘。“你手中所执的那几页纸上，附着一道诅咒：一个邪咒、一股凶险之力附着其上，凡胆敢披阅之人，自此身心两方皆要陷入可怖的危难。”他一边说着，一边从我手中取过那本小书，放回抽屉，动作之间又郑重地画了一个十字。

===Original===
“But, father,” I dared to expostulate, “how can such things be? How can there be danger in a few written sheets of parchment?”

“Christophe, there are things beyond your understanding, things that it were not well for you to know. The might of Satan is manifestable in devious modes, in diverse manners; there are other temptations than those of the world and the flesh, there are evils no less subtle than irresistible, there are hidden heresies, and necromancies other than those which sorcerers practise.”

“With what, then, are these pages concerned, that such occult peril, such unholy power lurks within them?”

“I forbid you to ask.” His tone was one of great rigor, with a finality that dissuaded me from further questioning.

===Chinese===
“可是，神父，”我斗胆进言，“怎会有这样的事？几页写着字的羊皮纸，其中怎会有凶险？”

“克里斯托夫，世上有超出你理解的事物，有些事你不知道反而为好。撒旦的大能可以千般途径、万种形态显化；除了这尘世与肉身的诱惑之外，尚有别的诱惑；有些邪祟既幽微难测又势不可挡；有隐秘的异端，也有术士所施行之外的其他死灵之术。”

“那么，这几页纸究竟所关何事，其中竟潜伏着这等秘术之险、这等不洁之力？”

“我不许你追问。”他的语气十分严厉，带着一种不容置疑的决绝，打消了我继续追问的念头。

===Original===
“For you, my son,” he went on, “the danger would be doubly great, because you are young, ardent, full of desires and curiosities. Believe me, it is better to forget that you have ever seen this manuscript.” He closed the hidden drawer, and as he did so, the melancholy troubled look was replaced by his former benignity.

“Now,” he said, as he turned to one of the bookshelves, “I will show you the copy of Ovid that was owned by the poet Petrarch.” He was again the mellow scholar, the kindly, jovial host, and it was evident that the mysterious manuscript was not to be referred to again. But his odd perturbation, the dark and awful hints he had let fall, the vague terrific terms of his proscription, had all served to awaken my wildest curiosity, and, though I felt the obsession to be unreasonable, I was quite unable to think of anything else for the rest of the evening. All manner of speculations, fantastic, absurd, outrageous, ludicrous, terrible, defiled through my brain as I duly admired the incunabula which Hilaire took down so tenderly from the shelves for my delectation.

===Chinese===
“至于你，我的孩子，”他接着说，“那凶险将会加倍地大，因为你年轻、炽烈，满怀欲望与好奇。相信我，最好忘了你曾见过这部手稿。”他合上那只暗屉，合上之际，那忧郁惶乱的神情便被先前的和蔼所取代。

“来，”他说着，转身走向一处书架，“我给你看那部奥维德（Ovid），它曾是诗人彼特拉克（Petrarch）的旧藏。”他重又变回了那个醇厚的学者、那位和蔼快活的主人，显然那部神秘的手稿是不许再被提起的了。然而他那古怪的扰攘、他无意间漏出的那些黑暗可怖的暗示、他那道禁令里所用的一切模糊而骇人的字眼，都已足以唤醒我最为狂野的好奇；虽说我也觉出这般执念并不合理，可这一晚余下的时辰里，我再也无法去想别的任何事。种种臆测——荒诞的、悖理的、骇人听闻的、滑稽的、可怖的——如行军列队一般鱼贯穿过我的脑海，而我则一如既往地赞叹着希莱尔那般温存地从架上取下来供我赏玩的那些摇篮本。

At last, toward midnight, he led me to my room—a room especially reserved for visitors, and with more of comfort, of actual luxury in its hangings, carpets and deeply quilted bed than was allowable in the cells of the monks or of the abbot himself. Even when Hilaire had withdrawn, and I had proved for my satisfaction the softness of the bed allotted me, my brain still whirled with questions concerning the forbidden manuscript. Though the storm had now ceased, it was long before I fell asleep; but slumber, when it finally came, was dreamless and profound.

===Chinese===
终于，临近午夜时分，他领我到我的卧房——这是一间专为来客保留的屋子，其帷幔、地毯与厚厚絮棉的床铺所含的舒适、乃至实在的奢华，比之修士乃至院长本人的静室所容许的，都更胜一筹。纵然希莱尔已经退出，纵然我已为了称心而试过那张分派给我的床的柔软，我的脑子仍因那部禁书所引发的种种疑问而旋转不休。风暴此时虽已止息，我却许久方才入睡；然而睡眠一旦真正降临，便是无梦而深沉的。

===Original===
When I awoke, a river of sunshine clear as molten gold was pouring through my window.
