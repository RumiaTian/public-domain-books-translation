## The Mahout / 象夫

===Original===
Arthur Merton, British resident at Jizapur, and his cousin, John Hawley, an Agra newspaper editor, who had run down into Central India for a few weeks’ shooting at Merton’s invitation, reined in their horses just outside the gates of Jizapur. The Maharaja’s elephants, a score of the largest and finest “tuskers” in Central India, were being ridden out for their daily exercise. The procession was led by Raja, the great elephant of State, who towered above the rest like a warship amongst merchantmen. He was a magnificent elephant, over twelve feet from his shoulders to the ground, and of a slightly lighter hue than the others, who were of the usual muddy gray. On the ends of his tusks gleamed golden knobs.

===Chinese===
亚瑟·默顿（Arthur Merton）本是英国驻吉扎普尔（Jizapur）的驻扎官，其堂弟约翰·霍利（John Hawley）则是阿格拉（Agra）一家报馆的编辑，应默顿之邀南下中印度，欲作数周狩猎之游。二人于吉扎普尔城门外勒马驻足。那大君（Maharaja）的象群——整整二十头，皆是中印度境内最为硕壮雄骏的“巨牙象”——正被驱策而出，作每日例行的操练。行列由那头名叫拉贾（Raja）的御象领头，它高踞群象之上，宛若一艘战舰凌驾于商船之间。它是一头气象峥嵘的巨象，自肩至地高逾十二英尺，毛色较其余那些寻常泥灰色的同类略浅一线；两根象牙之端，各顶着一只金灿灿的圆球，熠熠生光。

===Original===
“What a kingly animal!” exclaimed Hawley, as Raja passed.

As he spoke, the mahout, or driver, who had been sitting his charge like a bronze image, turned and met Hawley’s eyes. He was a man to attract attention, this mahout, as distinctive a figure among his brother mahouts as was Raja among the elephants. He was apparently very tall, and of a high-caste type, the eyes proud and fearless, the heavy beard carefully trimmed, and the face cast in a handsome, dignified mold.

===Chinese===
“好一头王者之兽！”拉贾经过时，霍利失声赞叹。

话音未落，那象夫（mahout）——亦即驭象之人——原本端坐象背之上纹然不动，恍若一尊铜铸的造像，此时却回过身来，与霍利四目相接。这象夫是个引人瞩目之人，在同行之间超然特立，一如拉贾之于象群。他看去身量颀长，眉宇间是一派高种姓的气度，双目孤傲而无惧，浓密的髯须修剪得一丝不苟，整张脸轮廓俊朗，气度端方凝重。

===Original===
Hawley gave a second exclamation as he met the mahout’s gaze and stared at the man hard. The Hindu, after an impressive glance, turned his head, and the elephant went on.

“I could swear that I have seen that man before,” said Hawley, at his cousin’s interrogatory expression. “It was near Agra, about six years ago, when I was out riding one afternoon. My horse, a nervous, high-strung Waler, bolted at the sight of an umbrella which someone had left by the roadside. It was impossible to stop him, indeed, I had all I could to keep on. Suddenly, the Hindu we have just passed, or his double, stepped out into the road and grabbed the bridle. He was carried quite a distance, but managed to keep his grip, and the Waler finally condescended to stop. After receiving my thanks with a dignified depredation of the service he had done me, the Hindu disappeared, and I have not seen him since.”

===Chinese===
与那象夫目光一接，霍利又惊呼一声，遂定睛将那人细细打量。那印度人投来一道深沉难测的目光，旋即别转头去，巨象继续前行。

“我可以发誓，那人我从前必定见过。”见堂兄一脸探询之色，霍利说道，“那是大约六年以前、阿格拉近郊的事。某日午后我外出骑马，座下是一匹沃勒马（Waler），生性敏感、神经过敏、极易受惊，因瞧见路边有人遗落了一把阳伞，便受惊狂奔起来。那马根本勒之不住，说实话，我能不摔下马背已是拼尽全力了。忽然，方才我们擦身而过的那个印度人——要么便是一个与他生得一模一样的人——步出路面，一把攥住了马缰。他连拖带拽被带出去好一程，却死死抓住不放，那沃勒马总算肯屈尊停下脚步。听我道谢之时，他神态端重，将这一桩义举视若不足挂齿；其后便飘然而逝，自此我再未见过他。”

===Original===
“It is scarcely probable, though, that this mahout is the same,” Hawhey resumed, after a pause. “My rescuer was dressed as a high-caste, and it is not conceivable that such a one would turn elephant driver.”

“I know nothing of the man,” said Merton, as they rode on into the city. “He has been Raja’s mahout ever since I came here a year ago. Of course, as you say, he cannot be the man who stopped your horse. It is merely a chance resemblance.”

===Chinese===
“不过，这象夫断不可能是同一人。”霍利顿了一顿，又道，“那位救我之人衣着是高种姓的打扮，这般身份的人，断不致沦落到去当一名驭象的象夫。”

“此人来历，我一无所知。”默顿说道，此时二人已策马入城，“自一年前我到任此地起，他便一直是拉贾的象夫。当然，诚如你所言，他决不可能是当年拦马那人。不过是形貌偶然相似罢了。”

===Original===
The next afternoon, Hawley was out riding alone. He had left the main road for a smaller one running into the jungle, intending to visit a ruined temple of which Merton had told him. Suddenly he noticed elephant tracks in the dust, exceedingly large ones, which he concluded could have been made only by Raja. A momentary curiosity as to why the elephant had been ridden off into the jungle, and also concerning the mahout, led Hawley to follow the tracks when the road branched and they took the path opposite to the one that he had intended to follow. In a few minutes he came to a spot of open ground in the thick luxuriant jungle, and reined in quickly at what he saw there.

===Chinese===
翌日下午，霍利独自骑马出游。他舍了大路，转入一条蜿蜒伸入林莽的小径，意欲去寻访一座废寺——那是默顿向他提起过的。忽而，他留意到浮土之上印着象踪，硕大无朋，他断定除拉贾之外，再无别象能留下这般蹄印。一时之间好奇心起：这巨象何以被驱入密林深处？那象夫又所为何事？于是当道路分岔、象踪折向与他原定路径相反的一方时，霍利便循着蹄印跟了下去。不数分钟，他便来到密林深处一片开阔之地，一见眼前景象，赶紧勒马。

===Original===
Raja stood in the clearing, holding something in his trunk which Hawley at first glance took to be a man, dressed in a blue and gold native attire, and with a red turban. Another look told him that it was merely a dummy﻿—some old clothes stuffed with straw. As he watched, the mahout gave a low command, reinforced with a jab behind the ear from his ankus, or goad. Raja gave an upward swing with his trunk, and released his hold on the figure, which flew skyward for at least twenty feet, and then dropped limply to earth. The mahout watched its fall with an expression of what seemed to be malevolence upon his face, though Hawley might have been mistaken as to this at the distance. He gave another command, and a jab at the elephant’s cheek﻿—a peculiar, quick thrust, at which Raja picked the dummy up and placed it on his back behind the mahout in the place usually occupied by the howdah. The Hindu directing, the figure was again seized and hurled into the air.

===Chinese===
只见拉贾伫立于林间空地之上，长鼻卷着一件物事，霍利初一眼瞧去，还当是个人——身着蓝底金线的印度衣冠，头缠一顶红色头巾。再一定睛，才看清不过是个草人——几件旧衣里塞满了稻草罢了。正注视间，那象夫低声发了一句口令，手中象钩（ankus），亦即驱象刺棒，顺势在象耳之后狠狠一戳，以壮声威。拉贾长鼻向上一抡，松开了那草人，只见它直飞上天，足有二十英尺之高，而后软塌塌地坠落在地。那象夫凝视着草人坠落，面上一副似是怨毒之色——不过隔着这般距离，霍利或许看走了眼，也未可知。他又发了一声口令，朝象颊上戳了一下——这一下戳法古怪、疾捷异常——拉贾应声将草人拾起，放于象背之上、象夫身后的位置，正是平日象舆（howdah）所在之处。那印度人指令一下，草人复又被长鼻卷起，再度抛上半空。

===Original===
Much mystified, Hawley watched several repetitions of this strange performance, but was unable to puzzle out what it meant. Finally, the mahout caught sight of him, and rode the elephant hastily away into the jungle on the opposite side of the clearing. Evidently he did not wish to be observed or questioned. Hawley continued his journey to the temple, thinking over the curious incident as he went. He did not see the mahout again that day.

===Chinese===
霍利满腹狐疑，将这诡异一幕看了好几遍反复，仍参不透其中玄机。终于，那象夫瞥见了他，忙不迭驱象急行，从空地另一侧的林莽中遁去。显然，他不愿被人撞见、不愿被人盘问。霍利继续赶往那座废寺，一路上仍在回味这桩离奇之事。那一日，他再没见过那象夫。

===Original===
He spoke of what he had seen to Merton that evening, but his cousin paid little attention to the tale, saying that no one could comprehend anything done by natives, and that it wasn’t worth while to wonder at their actions anyway. Even if one could find the explanation, it wouldn’t be worth knowing.

The scene in the jungle recurred to Hawley many times, probably because of the resemblance of the mahout to the man who had stopped his horse at Agra. But he could think of no plausible explanation of what he had seen. At last he dismissed the matter from his mind altogether.

===Chinese===
当晚，他将目击之事说与默顿听，堂兄却并不怎样上心，只道土著之所为本就无人能懂，又何苦去大惊小怪；纵使能寻出个解释，那解释亦不值一哂。

林中那一幕，在霍利脑海中反复浮现，多半是因为那象夫与阿格拉拦马之人容貌相似之故。但他始终想不出一个说得通的解释。终于，他将这事从心头彻底撇开了。

===Original===
At the time of Hawley’s visit, great preparations were being made for the marriage of the Maharaja of Jizapur, Krishna Singh, to the daughter of the neighboring sovereign. There was to be much feasting, firing of guns, and a gorgeous procession. All the Rajahs, Ranas, and Thakurs, etc., for a radius of at least hundred miles, were to be present. The spectacle, indeed, was one of the inducements that had drawn Hawley down into Central India.

===Chinese===
霍利造访之时，正值吉扎普尔大君克里希纳·辛格（Krishna Singh）迎娶邻邦君主之女，盛大婚礼的筹备正紧锣密鼓地进行。届时将有连日的盛宴、隆隆的礼炮，以及一场穷极奢华的仪仗巡行。方圆百里之内的王公、拉纳、塔库尔诸般贵族，悉数将莅临与会。说起来，这番盛景，正是吸引霍利南下中印度的由头之一。

===Original===
After two weeks of unprecedented activity and excitement in the city of Jizapur, the great day came, with incessant thunder of guns from the Maharajab’s palace during all the forenoon, as the royalty of Central Indian arrived with its hordes of picturesque, tattered, dirty retainers and soldiery. Each king or dignitary was punctiliously saluted according to his rank, which in India is determined by the number of guns that may be fired in his honor.

===Chinese===
吉扎普尔城中历经两周前所未有的喧嚣与躁动，那个大日子终于来临。整整一个上午，大君宫殿那边礼炮之声如雷不息，中印度的王公贵胄携着他们那一群群服饰绚烂、却褴褛肮脏的家臣与兵丁，陆续驾临。每一位君王或显贵莅临，皆循其品阶受礼炮致敬——在印度，人之尊卑，正系于其所能享有的礼炮之数。

===Original===
At noon a great procession, the Maharaja heading it, issued from the palace to ride out and meet the bride and her father and attendants, who were to reach Jizapur at that hour.

Hawley and Merton watched the pageant from the large and many-colored crowd that lined the roadside without the city gates. As Raja, the great State elephant emerged, with Krishna Singh in the gold-embroidered howdah, or canopied seat, on his back, a rising cloud of dust in the distance proclaimed the coming of the bride and her relatives.

===Chinese===
正午时分，一支浩浩荡荡的仪仗从宫门涌出，大君亲领其前，出城去迎接新娘及其父亲与随从——他们恰好将于此时抵达吉扎普尔。

霍利与默顿立于城门之外、夹道而立的万头攒动、五色斑斓的人海之中，观看这一场盛况。那头御象拉贾昂然步出，克里希纳·辛格端坐于其背上那金线绣花的象舆——亦即带篷的宝座——之中。恰在此时，远处腾起的一蓬尘烟，宣告着新娘及其亲眷的车驾正姗姗而来。

===Original===
Behind the Maharaja came a number of elephants, bearing the nobles and dignitaries of Jizapur, and the neighboring princes. Then emerged richly caparisoned horses, with prismatically-attired riders﻿—soldiers and attendants. Over this great glare of color and movement was the almost intolerable light of the midday Eastern sun.

===Chinese===
大君之后，跟随大队象群，载着吉扎普尔的贵胄显要与四邻的土邦王子。继而鱼贯而出的是披挂华美鞍辔的骏马，马上骑士——甲兵与扈从——衣饰如棱镜般七彩流转。这一片炫目流转的斑斓色浪之上，正笼罩着中天印度烈日那几乎令人难以忍受的毒辣光芒。

===Original===
The two Englishmen were some distance from the city gates, so that when the Maharaja’s slow, majestic procession passed them, that of the bridge was drawing near﻿—a similar one, and less gorgeous only because it was smaller.

Perhaps fifty yards separated the two when something happened to bring both processesions to a halt. Hawley, who happened at the moment to be idly watching the elephant Raja, and his driver, saw the mahout reach swiftly forward and stab the animal’s cheek with his goad, precisely as he had done on that day in the jungle when Hawley had come unexpectedly upon him. Probably no one else noticed the action, or, if they did, attached any importance to it in the excitement that followed.

===Chinese===
两名英国人立处距城门已有相当一段路，故而当大君那庄重徐缓的仪仗行经他们身前之时，新娘一方的队伍已正逼近——仪仗相类，唯因规模略小，遂稍减其繁华。

两队相距约莫五十码光景，忽有变故陡生，使两队仪仗齐齐停了下来。其时霍利恰正漫不经心地望着那头巨象拉贾及其驭者，只见那象夫倏然探身向前，以刺棒戳向象颊——手法精准，与他那日在密林中、霍利猝然撞见之时所施展的，分毫不差。大约再无第二人留意到这一举动；纵有留意的，在随后那场骚乱之中，也未曾将它放在心上。

===Original===
As he reached with his trunk for the dummy seated on his back, so Raja reached into the howdah and grasped Krishna Singh about the waist. In an instant the astonished, terror-stricken Maharaja was dangling in midair where the elephant held him poised a moment. Then, in spite of the shouts, commands, and blows of his mahout, Raja began to swing Krishna Singh to and fro, slowly at first, but with a gradually increasing speed. It was like watching a gigantic pendulum. The fascinated crowd gazed in a sudden and tense silence for what seemed to them hours, though they were really only seconds, before the elephant, with a last vicious upward impetus of his helpless victim, reeased his hold. Krishna Singh soared skyward, a blot of gold and red against the intense, stark, blazing azure of the Indian sky. To the horror-stricken onlookers he seemed to hang there for hours, before he began to fall back from the height to which the giant elephant had tossed him as one would toss a tennis-ball. Hawley turned away, unable to look any longer, and in an instant heard the hollow, lifeless thud as the body struck the ground.

===Chinese===
正如它曾以长鼻探向背上的草人那样，拉贾此番将长鼻探入象舆，一把攫住了克里希纳·辛格的腰身。刹那之间，那位惊骇欲绝、魂飞魄散的大君已被悬于半空，巨象将他定格在那里、悬了一瞬。而后，任凭那象夫怎样呼喝、下令、捶击，拉贾全然不理，开始将克里希纳·辛格来来回回地摆荡起来——起初徐缓，继而愈摆愈急。直如看着一具硕大无朋的钟摆。那群如着魔般的看客骤然陷入一种紧绷的死寂，凝目而视——于他们恍若数小时之久，实则不过数秒——直到巨象将他那无力挣扎的牺牲品作最后狠狠一向上一送，松开了长鼻。克里希纳·辛格被抛向九霄，在印度穹苍那浓烈、酷厉、如焚的湛蓝之中，化作一抹金红相间的色斑。在那群骇然失色的观者眼中，他仿佛就那样悬在天上、历历数小时之久，方才从巨象将他抛起的高度——恍若人抛网球一般抛起的高度——朝下跌落。霍利别过头去，再不忍卒睹，旋即便听见那沉闷、了无生气的“砰”然一声——身躯着地。

===Original===
The sound broke the spell of horror and amazement that had held the crowd, and a confused babble arose, interspersed with a few wails and cries. One sharp shriek came from the curtained howdah of the bride. The Maharaja’s body guard at once galloped forward and formed a ring about the body. The crowd, to whom the elephant had gone “musth,” or mad, began to retreat and disperse.

Hawley, in a few words, told his cousin of what he has seen the mahout do, and his belief that the elephant’s action had thus been incited.

The two Englishmen went to the captain of the bodyguard, who was standing by the side of the fallen Maharaja. Krishna Singh lay quite dead, his neck broken by the fall. The captain, upon being informed of what Hawley had seen, directed some of his men to go in search of the mahout, who, in the confusion, had slipped from Raja’s neck, disappearing no one knew where. Their search was unsuccessful, nor did a further one, continued for over a week, reveal any trace of the elephant driver.

===Chinese===
这一声响，打破了那镇慑住人群的恐怖与惊骇的魔咒，一时哗然四起，其间夹杂着几声哀号与惊呼。一声尖利的惨叫，发自新娘那垂着帘幕的象舆之内。大君的近卫旋即飞骑上前，围成一圈，将那具尸身护住。众人只道那象发了“musth”——亦即狂象之症——遂纷纷后退溃散。

霍利三言两语，将他所见那象夫的举动告知堂兄，并说自己深信，巨象之行实乃受其煽诱所致。

二人遂走向那位近卫统领——他正伫立于殒命的大君身侧。克里希纳·辛格已然气绝，颈骨折断于那一摔之下。那统领听罢霍利所述，当即遣手下数人前去搜捕那象夫——方才一片混乱之中，他早已从拉贾颈上滑身而下，不知所踪，无人知其去向。此番搜索一无所获；其后又续搜一周有余，仍寻不见那驭象人的半点踪迹。

But several days afterward Hawley received a letter, bearing the Agra postmark. It was in a hand unfamiliar to him and was written in rather stiff, though perfectly correct English, such as an educated native would write. It was as follows:

To Hawley Sahib:

I am the man who stopped the Sahib’s horse near Agra one day, six years ago. Because I have seen in the Sahib’s eyes that he recognized and remembers me, I am writing this. He will then understand much that has puzzled him.

===Chinese===
然而数日之后，霍利收到一封信，盖着阿格拉的邮戳。字迹于他全然陌生，通篇英文虽略显生硬，却工整无误，一如一位受过教育的土著所书。信中写道：

我便是六年前某日、在阿格拉近郊拦下萨希布坐骑的那个人。因我从萨希布眼中看出，他已将我认出、且记在心里，故而写下此书。读罢，许多令他困惑之事，他便都能了然了。

===Original===

The Maharaja, bearing him little love and being of an intensely suspicious nature, required little proof to believe this, and caused my father and several others of the family to be seized and thrown into the palace dungeons. I, who was but a youth at the time, by some inadvertence was allowed to escape, and made my way to Agra, where I remained several years with distant relatives, learning, in that time, to speak and write English. I was intending to enter the service of the British Raj, when an idea of revenge on Krishna Singh, for my father’s death, suddenly sprang into full conception. I had long plotted, forming many impracticable and futile plans for vengeance, but the one that then occurred to me seemed possible, though extremely difficult. As the Sahib has seen, it proved successful.

===Chinese===
彼时我还不过是个少年，因某种疏漏，竟得以漏网逃脱，辗转逃至阿格拉，在远亲家中寄居数年，于此期间学会了英语的讲读与书写。我本欲投身英属印度政府（British Raj）谋一差事，恰在此时，为家父向克里希纳·辛格复仇之念，忽而在我心中酝酿成形、沛然不可复抑。我早有谋划，曾拟出许多全无用处、徒劳无功的复仇之策，然而彼时浮现于脑际的这一个，似乎可行——纵然难如登天。一如萨希布所亲见，它终告成功。

===Original===
I at once left Agra, disguising myself as a low-caste, and went to Burma, where I learned elephant-driving﻿—a work not easy for one who has not been trained to it from boyhood. In doing this, I sacrificed my caste. In my thirst for revenge, however, it seemed but a little thing.

After four years in the jungle I came to Jizapur and, being a skilled and fully accredited mahout, was given a position in the Maharaja’s stables. Krishna Singh never suspected my identity, for I had changed greatly in the ten years since I had fled from Jizapur, and who would have thought to find Kshatriya in the position of such a low-caste elephant-driver?

===Chinese===
我当即离开阿格拉，乔装成一个低种姓，远赴缅甸（Burma），在那里学起了驭象之术——这门营生，对非自幼习此之人而言，绝非易事。为此，我舍弃了自己的种姓。然则在我复仇的渴念之中，这不过区区小事。

在密林之中历练四年之后，我来到吉扎普尔，以一名身手娴熟、资斧俱全的象夫身份，在大君的象厩中谋得了一个差事。克里希纳·辛格始终未曾疑心我的真实身份——自当年逃离吉扎普尔，十年之间我已面目大改；再者，谁又会想到，一个刹帝利（Kshatriya）种姓之人，竟会沦为一个如此低贱的驭象之夫呢？

===Original===
Gradually, for my skill and trustworthiness, I was advanced in position, and at last was entrusted with the State elephant, Raja. This was what I had long been aiming at, for on my attaining the care of Krishna Singh’s own elephant depended the success or failure of my plan.

This position obtained, my purpose was but half-achieved. It was necessary that the elephant be trained for his part, and this, indeed, was perhaps the most difficult and dangerous pact of my work. It was not easy to avoid observation, and detection was likely to prove fatal to me and to my plan. On that day when the Sahib came upon me in the jungle, I thought my scheme doomed, and prepared to flee. But evidently no idea of the meaning of the performance in the jungle entered the Sahib’s mind.

===Chinese===
渐渐地，因我技艺精熟、行事可靠，位次屡屡升迁，终于，那头御象拉贾被交托到了我的手上。这正是我长久以来苦心谋求的——因为我能否接手照管克里希纳·辛格本人的坐骑，正是我整个谋划成败之所系。

这差事一旦到手，我的目的也不过才达成一半。那巨象尚须为它所当扮演的角色受训，而这，或许正是我整桩营生之中最为艰难凶险的一环。欲避人耳目，殊非易事；一旦败露，于我、于我的谋划，皆将是致命之祸。那一日，萨希布在林中撞见我时，我只当我这计谋已然败露，遂作好了逃遁的准备。然而显然，那林中一幕究竟有何含义，萨希布心中并无所悟。

===Original===
At last came my day of revenge, and after the Maharaja’s death I succeeded in miraculously escaping, though I had fully expected to pay for my vengeance with my own life. I am safe now﻿—not all the police and secret emissaries in India can find me.

The death that my father met has been visited upon his murderer, and the shadow of those dreadful days and of that unavenged crime has at last been lifted from my heart. I go forth content, to face life and fate calmly, and with a mind free and untroubled.

===Chinese===
复仇之日终于到来。大君既殁，我竟奇迹般地脱身得手——尽管我本已打定主意，要以自家性命来为这场复仇付账。如今我已安然无虞——纵使倾尽全印度的巡捕与密探，亦休想将我寻获。

家父所遭的那一死，已原封不动地报应到了凶手身上。那些可怖时日的阴翳、那一桩久未昭雪的罪愆，终于从我心头卸下。我此去心怀安然，将平静地直面人生与命运，了无挂碍、神清气朗。
