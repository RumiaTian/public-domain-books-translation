## XXXI A Parting of Ways / XXXI 分道扬镳

===Original===
“Was the child born dead?”
“Worse than dead!”
Somehow, somewhere, Mary Cresswell had heard these words; long, long, ago, down there in the great pain-swept shadows of utter agony, where Earth seemed slipping its moorings; and now, today, she lay repeating them mechanically, grasping vaguely at their meaning. Long she had wrestled with them as they twisted and turned and knotted themselves, and she worked and toiled so hard as she lay there to make the thing clear﻿—to understand.

===Chinese===
『那孩子，是死产的吗？』
『比死还糟！』
不知怎的、不知在哪里，玛丽·克雷斯韦尔听见过这两句话；很久很久以前，在那被剧痛横扫的、彻底苦楚的阴影里，在那大地仿佛脱了锚的地方；而如今，今天，她躺着，机械地反复念叨着它们，含糊地去抓它们的意思。她跟它们搏斗了很久，它们扭来转去、打成结；她躺在那里那样费力地劳作、苦熬，要把事情弄清楚——要弄明白。

===Original===
“Was the child born dead?”
“Worse than dead!”
Then faint and fainter whisperings: what could be worse than death? She had tried to ask the grey old doctor, but he soothed her like a child each day and left her lying there. Today she was stronger, and for the first time sitting up, looking listlessly out across the world﻿—a queer world. Why had they not let her see the child﻿—just one look at its little dead face? That would have been something. And again, as the doctor cheerily turned to go, she sought to repeat the old question. He looked at her sharply, then interrupted, saying kindly:
“There, now; you’ve been dreaming. You must rest quietly now.” And with a nod he passed into the other room to talk with her husband.

===Chinese===
『那孩子，是死产的吗？』
『比死还糟！』
随后是越来越轻的低语：还有什么能比死更糟？她想问问那位灰白头发的老医生，可他每天都像哄孩子似的哄她，把她留在那里躺着。今天她强了些，头一回坐起来，百无聊赖地望着外面的世界——一个古怪的世界。他们为什么不让她看看那孩子——就看一眼它那死去的小脸？那至少也是一点什么。又一次，医生笑吟吟地转身要走，她想再问那老问题。他锐利地看了她一眼，打断她，和气地说：
『好啦，好啦；你做梦了。现在你必须安静地歇着。』他点点头，进了另一间房，去跟她丈夫说话。

===Original===
She was not satisfied. She had not been dreaming. She would tell Harry to ask him﻿—she did not often see her husband, but she must ask him now and she arose unsteadily and swayed noiselessly across the floor. A moment she leaned against the door, then opened it slightly. From the other side the words came distinctly and clearly:
“﻿—other children, doctor?”
“You must have no other children, Mr. Cresswell.”
“Why?”
“Because the sins of the fathers are visited upon the children unto the third and fourth generation.”

===Chinese===
她不满足。她没做梦。她要叫哈里去问他——她不常见到丈夫，可她现在非问他不可，于是她摇摇晃晃地站起来，悄无声息地晃过地板。她在门边靠了一会儿，把门微微推开。从那一边，字字清晰地传来：
『——还能有别的孩子吗，医生？』
『你不能再有别的孩子了，克雷斯韦尔先生。』
『为什么？』
『因为父亲的罪，要追讨到子孙，三四代之久。』

===Original===
Slowly, softly, she crept away. Her mind seemed very clear. And she began a long journey to reach her window and chair﻿—a long, long journey; but at last she sank into the chair again and sat dry-eyed, wondering who had conceived this world and made it, and why.
A long time afterward she found herself lying in bed, awake, conscious, clear-minded. Yet she thought as little as possible, for that was pain; but she listened gladly, for without she heard the solemn beating of the sea, the mighty rhythmic beating of the sea. Long days she lay, and sat and walked beside those vast and speaking waters, till at last she knew their voice and they spoke to her and the sea-calm soothed her soul.

===Chinese===
她慢慢地、轻轻地爬开。她的头脑似乎很清醒。她开始一段漫长的旅程，去够到她的窗户和椅子——一段长长的、长长的旅程；可她终于又沉进椅子里坐着，眼里没有泪，纳罕这世界究竟是谁构想出来、造出来的，又是为了什么。
很久以后，她发觉自己躺在床上，醒着，清醒，神志清明。然而她尽量少想，因为想就是痛；可她乐意地听着，因为窗外传来大海庄严的搏动，大海那雄浑而有节奏的搏动。长长的日子她躺着、坐着，在那浩瀚而会说话的水边散步，直到她终于听懂了它们的嗓音，它们也对她说话了，于是海的宁静抚慰了她的灵魂。

===Original===
For one brief moment of her life she saw herself clearly: a well-meaning woman, ambitious, but curiously narrow; not willing to work long for the Vision, but leaping at it rashly, blindly, with a deep-seated sense of duty which she made a source of offence by preening and parading it, and forcing it to ill-timed notice. She saw that she had looked on her husband as a means not an end. She had wished to absorb him and his work for her own glory. She had idealized for her own uses a very human man whose life had been full of sin and fault. She must atone.
No sooner, in this brief moment, did she see herself honestly than her old habits swept her on tumultuously. No ordinary atonement would do. The sacrifice must be vast; the world must stand in wonder before this clever woman sinking her soul in another and raising him by sheer will to the highest.

===Chinese===
在她一生中这短短的一刻，她把自己看得清清楚楚：一个出于好意的女人，有抱负，却出奇地狭隘；不肯为那远景长久地劳作，而是冒失地、盲目地向它扑去，带着一种根深蒂固的责任感，而这责任感她又拿来惹人反感——梳饰它、招摇它、不合时宜地强求人注意。她看出，她一向把丈夫当作手段，而非目的。她想吞没他和他的事业，为自己的荣耀所用。她为一个非常凡俗、一生充满罪恶与过咎的男人，按自己的用处涂上了理想的光辉。她必须赎罪。
然而在这一刻，她刚诚实地看清自己，旧日的习气便汹涌地把她卷走了。寻常的赎罪不够。这牺牲必须浩大；这世界必须惊叹地站在一个聪明的女人面前——她把灵魂沉进另一个人，又凭纯粹的意志把他抬到最高处。

===Original===
So after six endless months Mary Cresswell walked into her Washington home again. She knew she had changed in appearance, but she had forgotten to note how much until she saw the stare﻿—almost the recoil﻿—of her husband, the muttered exclamation, the studied, almost overdone welcome. Then she went up to her mirror and looked long, and knew.
She was strong; she felt well; but she was slight, almost scrawny, and her beauty was gone forever. It had been of that blonde white-and-pink type that fades in a flash, and its going left her body flattened and angular, her skin drawn and dead white, her eyes sunken. From the radiant girl whom Cresswell had met three years earlier the change was startling, and yet the contrast seemed even greater than it was, for her glory then had been her abundant and almost golden hair. Now that hair was faded, and falling so fast that at last the doctor advised her to cut it short. This left her ill-shaped head exposed and emphasized the sunken hollows of her face. She knew that she was changed but she did not quite realize how changed, until now as she stood and gazed.

===Chinese===
于是，在六个没完没了的月份之后，玛丽·克雷斯韦尔又走进了她的华盛顿的家。她知道自己容貌变了，可她忘了留意变了多少，直到看见她丈夫的凝视——几乎是退缩——那嘟囔的惊叹、那刻意的、几乎过头的欢迎。于是她走到镜子前看了很久，便知道了。
她身子骨是强的；她觉得挺好；可她瘦削，近乎干瘪，她的美貌一去不返了。她本是那种金发白肤、白里透粉的美，一闪即逝，它的消逝留下她身躯扁塌、棱角分明，皮肤绷紧、白得没有血色，眼睛凹陷。比起三年前克雷斯韦尔初见时那个光彩照人的姑娘，这变化令人惊心；可这反差又似乎比实际的更大，因为当年她的荣光，是她那一头浓密的、近乎金色的头发。如今那头发褪了色，又掉得这样快，医生终于劝她剪短。这便把她那颗头型不佳的脑袋整个露了出来，更衬出她脸上凹陷的坑洼。她知道自己变了，可没完全意识到变了多少——直到此刻她站着端详。

===Original===
Yet she did not hesitate but from that moment set herself to her new life task. Characteristically, she started dramatically and largely. She was to make her life an endless sacrifice; she was to revivify the manhood in Harry Cresswell, and all this for no return, no partnership of soul﻿—all was to be complete sacrifice and sinking soul in soul.
If Mary Cresswell had attempted less she would have accomplished more. As it was, she began well; she went to work tactfully, seeming to note no change in his manner toward her; but his manner had changed. He was studiously, scrupulously polite in private, and in public devoted; but there was no feeling, no passion, no love. The polished shell of his clan reflected conventional light even more carefully than formerly because the shell was cold and empty.

===Chinese===
可她没有犹豫，从那一刻起便投身于她新的人生任务。她性格使然，开局便轰轰烈烈、规模宏大。她要把自己的一生变成无尽的牺牲；她要重新唤回哈里·克雷斯韦尔身上的男子气概，而这一切不求回报、不求灵魂的伴侣——一切都将是彻底的牺牲、是灵魂沉入灵魂。
倘若玛丽·克雷斯韦尔少做一点，她本会做成更多。可她毕竟开了个好头；她婉转地着手，似乎没留意他对她的态度有什么变化；可他的态度确实变了。私下里，他刻意地、谨小慎微地客气；公开场合，则表现得十分殷勤；可这里头没有感情，没有激情，没有爱。他那族人那副光亮的躯壳，比从前更小心地反射着俗套的光，正因为那壳子又冷又空。

===Original===
There were no little flashes of anger now, no poutings nor sweet reconciliations. Life ran very smoothly and courteously; and while she did not try to regain the affection, she strove to enthrall his intellect. She supplied a subcommittee upon which he was serving﻿—not directly, but through him﻿—with figures, with reports, books, and papers, so that he received special commendations; a praise that piqued as well as pleased him, because it implied a certain surprise that he was able to do it.
“The damned Yankees!” he sneered. “They think they’ve got the brains of the nation.”
“Why not make a speech on the subject?” she suggested.

===Chinese===
如今再没有那些小小的发火、赌气，也没有甜蜜的和好了。日子过得十分顺滑、十分客气；她不去试着重获那份情爱，转而努力去征服他的头脑。她替他任职的一个小组委员会——不直接、而是借他之手——提供数据、报告、书籍和文件，于是他屡屡受到特别的嘉许；这种夸奖既叫他高兴，又叫他不快，因为它暗含着一层惊讶：惊讶他竟做得到。
『这帮该死的扬基佬！』他嗤笑。『他们以为全国的脑子都长在他们身上。』
『何不就这个题目做一次演讲呢？』她提议。

===Original===
He laughed. The matter under discussion was the cotton-goods schedule of the new tariff bill, about which really he knew a little; his wife placed every temptation to knowledge before him, even inspiring Senator Smith to ask him to defend that schedule against the low-tariff advocate. Mary Cresswell worked with redoubled energy, and for nearly a week Harry stayed at home nights and studied. Thanks to his wife the speech was unusually informing and well put, and the fact that a prominent free-trader spoke the same afternoon gave it publicity, while Mr. Easterly saw to the press despatches.

Quick reward and promotion were essential to Harry’s success. He was not one to keep up the strain of effort a long time. Unless, then, tangible results came and came quickly, he was liable to relapse into old habits. Therefore he must succeed and succeed at once. She would have preferred a less ornamental position than the ambassadorship, but there were no other openings. Here it was that she made her mistake. It was one thing for her to be a tactful hostess, pleasing her husband and his guests; it was another for her to aim openly at social leadership and political influence. She had at first all the insignia of success. Her dinners became of real political significance and her husband figured more and more as a leading Southerner. The result was twofold. Cresswell, on the one hand, with his usual selfishness, took his rising popularity as a matter of course and as the fruits of his own work; he was rising, he was making valuable speeches, he was becoming a social power, and his only handicap was his plain and overambitious wife. ===Chinese===
