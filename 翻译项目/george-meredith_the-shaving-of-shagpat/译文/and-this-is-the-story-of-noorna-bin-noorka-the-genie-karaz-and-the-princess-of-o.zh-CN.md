## And This Is the Story of Noorna Bin Noorka, the Genie Karaz, and the Princess of Oolb / 努尔卡之子努尔娜、神怪卡拉兹与奥尔布公主的故事
===Original===

Know, that when I was a babe, I lay on my mother’s bosom in the wilderness, and it was the bosom of death. Surely, I slept and smiled, and dreamed the infant’s dream, and knew not the coldness of the thing I touched. So were we even as two dead creatures lying there; but life was in me, and I awoke with hunger at the time of feeding, and turned to my mother, and put up my little mouth to her for nourishment, and sucked her, but nothing came. I cried, and commenced chiding her, and after a while it was as decreed, that certain horsemen of a troop passing through the wilderness beheld me, and seeing my distress and the helpless being I was, their hearts were stirred, and they were mindful of what the poet says concerning succour given to the poor, helpless, and innocent of this world, and took me up, and mixed for me camel’s milk and water from the bags, and comforted me, and bore me with them, after they had paid funeral rites to the body of my mother.
Now, the rosebud showeth if the rose-tree be of the wilds or of the garden, and the chief of that troop seeing me born to the uses of gentleness, carried me in his arms with him to his wife, and persuaded her that was childless to make me the child of their adoption. So I abode with them during the period of infancy and childhood, caressed and cared for, as is said:

> “The flower a stranger’s hand may gather,

Strikes root into the stranger’s breast;

Affection is our mother, father,

Friend, and of cherishers the best.”

且知我尚在襁褓幼婴之时，曾躺卧在荒漠中母亲的怀抱里，而那已是死寂冰凉的怀抱。委实如此，我沉睡着，含笑做着婴孩的美梦，浑然不知自己所依偎之物是何等冰冷。彼时我俩便宛若两具死尸横卧在那处；然而我体内尚有生气，待到喂哺之时，我饥肠辘辘醒转过来，偏过头贴向母亲，凑起稚嫩的小嘴向她索求乳汁，吮吸良久，却一无所获。我号啕大哭，开始啼声埋怨她；过了一阵，命中注定有一队骑兵恰好路经大漠，他们望见了我，见我孤苦伶仃、弱小无助，心中大起恻隐之念；他们深谙诗人关于周济世间贫苦、孤弱与无辜生灵之教诲，便将我抱起，自水囊中取出驼奶与水调匀喂我，抚慰我止哭，并在郑重安葬了我母亲的遗体后，将我一同带走。
花蕾初绽，便可知那玫瑰究竟生于荒野抑或长于华苑；那队骑兵的首领看出我生来便有温文柔淑之质，便一路将我抱在怀中带回家中交给妻子，并劝说膝下无子的妻子将我收为养女。于是我便在婴儿与孩提之年与他们相依为命，备受疼惜呵护，正如诗云：

> “异乡客手采之花，

亦向生人胸膛扎根生华；

慈爱即我父母，

亦为良友与至亲万家。”

And I loved them as their own child, witting not but that I was their child, till on a day while I played among some children of my years, the daughter of the King of Oolb passed by us on a mule, with her slaves and drawn swords, and called to me, “Thou little castaway!” and had me brought to her, and peered upon my face in a manner that frightened me, for I was young. Then she put me down from the neck of her mule where she had seated me, saying, “Child of a dead mother and a runaway father, what need I fear from thy like, and the dreams of a lovesick genie?” So she departed, but I forgot not her words, and dwelt upon them, and grew fevered with them, and drooped. Now, when he saw my bloom of health gone, heaviness on my feet, the light hollowed from my eyes, my benefactor, Ravaloke﻿—he that I had thought my father﻿—took me between his knees, and asked me what it was and the cause of my ailing; and I told him.
Then said he, “This is so: thou art not my child; but I love thee as mine, O my little Desert-flower; and why the Princess should fancy fear of thee I like not to think; but fear thou her, for she is a mask of wiles and a vine trailing over pitfalls; such a sorceress the world knoweth not as Goorelka of Oolb.”
Now, I was penetrated by what he said, and ceased to be a companion to them that loved childish games and romps, and meditated by myself in gardens and closets, feigning sleep when the elder ones discoursed, that I might learn something of this mystery, and all that was spoken perplexed me more, as the sage declareth:

> “Who in a labyrinth wandereth without clue,

More that he wandereth doth himself undo.”

Though I was quick as the quick-eyed falcon, I discovered nought, flying ever at false game﻿—

> “A follower of misleading beams,

A cheated soul, the mock of dreams.”

At times I thought that it was the King of Oolb was my father, and plotted to come in his path; and there were kings and princes of far countries whom I sought to encounter, that they might claim me; but none claimed me. O my betrothed, few gave me love beside Ravaloke, and when the wife that he cherished died, he solely, for I was lost in waywardness and the slave of moody imaginings. ’Tis said:

> “If thou the love of the world for thyself wouldst gain, mould thy breast

Liker the world to become, for its like the world loveth best;”

我亦将他们视作亲生父母般爱戴，浑然不知自己并非他们的骨肉；直到有一日，正当我与几名同龄孩童玩耍之际，奥尔布（Oolb）国王的女儿骑着骡子从我们身旁经过，身旁簇拥着奴隶，明晃晃的宝剑出鞘；她朝我喊道：“你这没人要的小弃儿！”随即命人将我带至身前，死死打量着我的面容，其神态教年幼的我心惊胆战。随后她将放在骡颈上的我放回地面，说道：“亡母弃女，逃父之种，似你这般微末雏儿与那害了相思病的神怪的痴梦，我有何惧哉？”言毕她便扬长而去，可她的话语我却久久无法释怀，日夜翻来覆去思量，乃至忧思成疾，日渐消瘦憔悴。我的恩人拉瓦洛克（Ravaloke）——那位我一直视作生父之人——见我红润尽褪、脚步虚浮、双目神采尽失，便将我揽在双膝之间，询问我因何抱恙；我便将实情如实相告。
他闻言叹道：“实情确是如此：你并非我的亲生骨肉；可我疼你便如己出，我那荒漠中的小花儿啊；至于那公主为何竟会无端忌惮于你，我实不愿深思；但你必须提防于她，因她是一副诡计的面具，是一丛掩覆在陷阱之上的藤蔓；世间再无任何女巫能比得上奥尔布的古雷尔卡（Goorelka of Oolb）。”
我将他的教诲铭记于心，从此不再与喜爱嬉戏玩闹的同伴往来，独自在深苑与密室中沉思冥想；每逢长辈交谈，我便假寐佯睡，只求能从中探听出这团谜雾的些许端倪；然而听到的一切反倒教我愈发困惑，正如智者所言：

> “入迷宫而无引线之人，

行得越深越陷沉沦。”

我虽如锐目猎鹰般聪敏机警，却一无所获，终究只是追逐着虚妄的猎物——

> “误随惑人之微芒，

受骗之魂，任由幻梦戏弄。”

有时我甚至暗想，莫非奥尔布国王才是我生父？便千方百计欲在他途经之处现身；又或者来自异域邦国的列王与王子，我也想方设法前去求见，期盼他们能将我认领相认；然而从未有人将我认领。我的未婚夫啊，除了拉瓦洛克之外，几乎无人给予我疼爱；待到他深爱的妻子过世之后，便唯有他一人待我真情如故，因我深陷任性妄为之中，沦为了阴郁遐想的囚徒。正如古语所云：

> “若欲博取尘世之爱，且将心胸塑改，

变得与这尘世无二，因尘世唯爱同类之貌；”

> “Oh, world diseased! oh, race empirical!

Now, when I was in my chamber, what saw I there but a dress of very costly blue raiment with gold-work broidery and a lovely circlet of gold, and gold bracelets set with stones of turquoise, and a basket of gold woven wire, wherein were toys, wondrous ones﻿—soldiers that cut off each other’s heads and put them on again, springing antelopes, palm-trees that turned to fountains, and others; and lo! a book in red binding, with figures on it and clasps of gold, a great book! So I clapped my hands joyfully, crying, “The old beggar has done it!” and robed myself in the dress, and ran forth to tell Ravaloke. As I ran by a window looking on the inner court, I saw below a crowd of all the slaves of Ravaloke round one that was seeking to escape from them, and ’twas Kadrab with a camel’s hump on his back, and a broad brown plaister over it, the wretch howling, peering across his shoulder, and trying to bolt from his burden, as a horse that would run from his rider. Then I saw that Kadrab also had his wish, his camel’s hump, and thought, “The old beggar, what was he but a genie?” Surely Ravaloke caressed me when he heard of the adventure, and what had befallen Kadrab was the jest of the city; but for me I spared little time away from that book, and studied in it incessantly the ways and windings of magic, till I could hold communication with genii, and wield charms to summon them, and utter spells that subdue them, discovering the haunts of talismans that enthral afreets and are powerful among men. There was that Kadrab coming to me daily to call out in the air for the old beggar-man to rid him of his hump; and he would waste hours looking up into the sky moodily for him, and cursing the five toes of his foot, for he doubted not the two beggars were one, and that he was punished for the kick, and lamented it direly, saying in the thick of his whimperings, “I’d give the foot that did it to be released from my hump, O my fair mistress.” So I pitied him, and made a powder and a spell, and my first experiment in magic was to relieve Kadrab of his hump, and I succeeded in loosening it, and it came away from him, and sank into the ground of the garden where we stood. So I told Kadrab to say nothing of this, but the idle-pated fellow blabbed it over the city, and it came to the ears of Goorelka.

So the genie, when he found himself baffled by me, and Shagpat with the mighty hair in his head, the Identical, he yelled, and fetched Shagpat a slap that sent him into the middle of the street; but Kadza screamed after him, and there was immediately such lamentation in the city about Shagpat, and such tearing of hair about him, that I perceived at once the virtue that was in the Identical. As for Karaz, finding his claim as possessor of the Identical no more valid, he vanished, and has been my rebellious slave since, till thou, O my betrothed, mad’st me spend him in curing thy folly on the horse Garraveen, and he escaped from my circles beyond the dominion of the Ring; yet had he his revenge, for I that was keeper of the Lily, had, I now learned ruefully, a bond of beauty with it, and whatever was a stain to one withered the other. Then that sorceress Goorelka stole my beauty from me by sprinkling a blight on the petals of the fair flower, and I became as thou first saw’st me. But what am I as I now am? Blissful! blissful! Surely I grew humble with the loss of beauty, and by humility wise, so that I assisted Feshnavat to become Vizier by the Ring, and watched for thy coming to shave Shagpat, as a star watcheth; for ’tis written, “A barber alone shall be shearer of the Identical”; and he only, my betrothed, hath power to plant it in Aklis, where it groweth as a pillar, bringing due reverence to Aklis.

我的未婚夫啊，当那神怪惊醒发觉自己头顶失了“独一神发”，那是何等凄厉的尖叫与咆哮啊！整座奥尔布城皆听得真真切切，浩瀚沧海狂暴翻涌泛起如癫狂吐沫般的白浪，神怪如滚滚奔雷般掠海狂飙，在半空中紧追我不放。那是何等惊心动魄的亡命飞遁！此时我发觉自己根本无法握牢那根“独一神发”，皆因它疯狂扭动、蛰刺不休，在我凌空飞遁之际险些滑落脱手。我远远望见大漠一角耸立着一座白色的城郭，便疾坠而下，落在一间布匹铺子旁——铺子里坐着一位安详端坐守着布料匹头的布商，他脑子里想的根本不是命运，而是烤肉串（kabobs）、炖肉与各色罕见的辛香调料。那座城郭如今便冠以他的名号。指安拉起誓（wullahy），若非我当时当机立断将那一根至今仍长在他头上的神发植入他的颅顶，我又怎能从卡拉兹掌心逃脱，又怎能同你相遇相知？天意的裁夺何等奥妙玄奇！为此当归赞安拉！那神怪见自己被我挫败，又见沙格帕特头顶竟然长出了神威浩瀚的“独一神发”，他狂怒咆哮，抡圆了一巴掌将沙格帕特抽翻在大街中央；但卡扎（Kadza）尖叫着追赶出去，全城百姓转瞬间围绕着沙格帕特痛哭流涕，人人为他悲呼扯发，我当下立时领教了“独一神发”之中所蕴含的无上威能。至于卡拉兹，发觉自己拥有“独一神发”的名分已然失效，便凭空隐去，从此沦为了我不服管束的顽劣奴仆，直至你这冤家——我的未婚夫啊，为了化解你骑乘烈马加拉文（Garraveen）惹下的滔天祸事，逼我不得不耗尽神力差遣于他，教他趁机破了法环逃逸出宝戒的管辖之外；然而他终究完成了复仇，因身为百合看护人的我，此时才痛心疾首地得知自身的美貌竟与那株神花紧密相系，凡有一处受损玷污，另一方便随之枯萎凋零。随后妖妇古雷尔卡将枯萎病霜泼洒在娇嫩的花瓣上，夺走了我的绝色美貌，我便沦落为初次与你相见时的那副丑怪模样。然而如今的我又是何等光景？无上极乐！无上极乐！美貌的丧失委实教我学会了谦卑，而谦卑更滋养了智慧，使我得以凭借宝戒襄助费什纳瓦特官拜维齐尔（Vizier），并如星辰守望苍穹般，翘首企盼着你的到来，前来为沙格帕特剃发；因古籍上早有明载：“唯有理发师方能剃落此独一神发”；且普天之下唯有他——我的未婚夫啊，才有神力将它植入阿克利斯（Aklis），教它化作擎天巨柱茁壮成长，为阿克利斯赢得万世敬仰。
