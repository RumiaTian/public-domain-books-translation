## XXI / 二十一
===Original===

Today the familiar handwriting brought no relief. This letter must be the final explanation. She opened it, standing by the hall table. “Dear Miss Henderson﻿—you are very persistent.” She folded the letter up and walked rapidly out into the sunshine. The way down to the Euston Road was very long and sunlit. It was radiant with all the months and weeks and days. She thought of going on with the unread letter and carrying it into the surgery, tearing it up into the waste paper basket and saying I have not read this. It is all right. We will not talk any more. One thing would have gone. But there would be a tremendous cheerfulness and independence and the memory of the things in the other letters. The letter once read two things would have gone, everything. She paused at the corner of the gardens looking down at the pavement. There was in some way that would not come quite clear so much more at stake than personal feelings about the insulting moment. It was something that stuck into everything, made everything intolerable until it was admitted and cancelled. As long as he went on hedging and pretending it was not there there could be no truth anywhere. It was something that must go out of the world, no matter what it cost. It would be smiling and cattish and behaving to drop it. Explained, it would be wiped away, and everything else with it. To accept his assertions would be to admit lack of insight. That would be treachery. The continued spontaneity of manner which it would ensure would be the false spontaneity that sat everywhere﻿ ﻿… all over that woman getting into the bus; brisk cheerful falsity. She glanced through to the end of the letter﻿ ﻿… “foolish gossip which might end by making your position untenable.” *Idiot.* Charming chivalrous gentleman.
---
I want to have it both ways. To keep the consideration and flout the necessity for it. No one shall dare to protect me from gossip. To prove myself independent and truth-demanding I would break up anything. That’s damned folly. Never mind. Why didn’t he admit it at once? He hated being questioned and challenged. He may have thought that manner was “the kindest way.” It is not for him to choose ways of treating me. This cancels the past. But it admits it. Not to admit the past would be to go on forever in a false position. He still hides. But he knows that I know he is hiding.

今天，熟悉的笔迹没有带来宽解。这封信必须是最后的解释。她站在门厅桌边拆开它。“亲爱的亨德森小姐——您真是锲而不舍。”她把信折起，快步走进阳光里。通往尤斯顿路的那段下坡路又长又亮。它被这几个月、几个星期、几天照得光辉灿烂。她想着就这样不读下去，把信带进诊室，撕碎扔进字纸篓，说：这信我没有读。没有事了。我们不再谈了。那样，一样东西就去了。可那将换来天大的快活、独立，加上那几封信里种种事物的记忆。信一旦读了，两样东西就都去了，一切。她在花园的拐角停住，低头望着人行道。不知怎么，这里赌着的，远不止对那受辱一刻的个人感受——虽然那层道理总归说不清。它是一样扎进一切的东西，叫一切都难以为继，直到它被承认、被勾销。只要他继续闪避、继续装作它不在，天下就没有一处有真实。它是一样必须从世界上出去的东西，不管付出什么。把它一笑置之、猫一样地做作，是不行的。解释了，它就会被抹掉，其余一切也一并抹掉。接受他的那套断言，就等于承认自己没有眼光。那是背叛。那会换来的做派上绵绵不绝的自然，将是那种坐在一切地方的假自然…坐在那正在上公共马车的女人浑身的那种；轻快快活的虚假。她把信草草扫到结尾…“愚蠢的闲话，到头来也许弄得您的地位难以维持。”白痴。侠骨柔肠的可爱绅士。
---
我想要两头占。保住那份体恤，又嘲弄对体恤的需要。谁也别想保护我、不叫我挨闲话。为证明我独立、我求真，我什么都肯砸。这是该死的蠢。不管它。他为什么不立刻承认？他恨被质问、被挑战。他也许以为那样做派是“最仁慈的方式”。用什么方式待我，不由他挑。这一下勾销了过去。可它也承认了过去。不承认过去，就永远在一个虚假的位置上走下去。他还在躲。可他知道我知道他在躲。

Where we have been we have been. It may have been through a false estimate of me to begin with. That does not matter. Where we have been we have been. That is not imagination. One day he will know it is not imagination. There is something that is making me very glad. A painful relief. Something forcing me back upon something. There is something that I have smashed, for some reason I do not know. It’s something in my temper, that flares out about things. Life allows no chance of getting at the bottom of things.﻿ ﻿…
---
I have nothing now but my pained self again, having violently rushed at things and torn them to bits. It’s all my fault from the very beginning. But I stand for something. I would dash my head against a wall rather than deny it. I make people hate me by *knowing* them and dashing my head against the wall of their behaviour. I should never make a good chess-player. Is God a chess-player? I shan’t leave until I have proved that no one can put me in a false position. There is something that is untouched by positions.﻿ ﻿…
---
I did not know what I had.﻿ ﻿… Friendship is fine fine porcelain. I have sent a crack right through it.﻿ ﻿…
Mrs. Bailey﻿ ﻿… numbers of people I never think of would like to have me always there.﻿ ﻿…
The sky fitting down on the irregular brown vista bore an untouched life.﻿ ﻿… There were always mornings; at work. I am free to work zealously and generously with and for him.
At *least* I have broken up his confounded complacency.
He will be embarrassed. *I* shan’t.

我们到过的地方，就是到过了。开头也许出于对我的一个错误估量。那不要紧。到过的地方，就是到过了。那不是想象。总有一天他会知道那不是想象。有一样东西正使我很觉快意。一种疼的宽解。一样逼我退回到某样东西上的东西。有一样东西叫我砸碎了，为了一个我自己也不知道的缘故。是我脾气里的什么东西，为一点事就冒出来。生活不给机会让人把事情看到底。…
---
如今我什么也没有了，只剩这个疼着的自己，刚任着性子朝东西扑上去、把它们撕成了碎片。从头到尾全是我的错。可我代表一样东西。我宁可拿脑袋撞墙也不否认它。我因为“识得”人、因为朝他们行为的墙上撞，招得人人恨我。我永远成不了好棋手。上帝是棋手吗？我不走，直到证明了谁也无法把我放进一个虚假的位置。有一样东西，是一切位置碰不着的。…
---
我不知道我曾拥有什么。…友谊是细细的细瓷。我在它上面裂开了一道通缝。…贝利太太…许多我从不想起的人，倒愿意我永远在那儿。…天扣在参差的棕色远景上，载着一段没人动过的生命。…早晨总会有；上工的时候。我可以自由地热忱地、慷慨地同他一起、为他做工了。

至少，我捅破了他那讨厌的自满。

他会尴尬。我不会。
