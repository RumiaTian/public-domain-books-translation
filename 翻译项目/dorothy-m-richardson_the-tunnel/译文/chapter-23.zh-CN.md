## XXIII / 二十三
===Original===

Cool with sound short sleep she rose early, the memory of yesterday giving a Sunday leisure to the usual anxious hurry of breakfast. She was strong with her own possessions. Wimpole Street held nothing but her contract of duties to fulfil. These she could see in a clear vexatious tangle, against the exciting oncoming of everybody’s summer; an excitement that was enough in itself. Patients were pouring out of town﻿—in a fortnight the Orlys would be gone; all Mr. Orly’s accounts must be out by then. In a month Mr. Hancock would go. For a month before her own holiday there would be almost nothing to do. If everyone’s accounts were examined before then, she could get them off at leisure during that month﻿ ﻿… then for this month there was nothing to do but the lessening daily duties and to get everyone to examine accounts; then the house to herself, with only Mr. Leyton there; the cool ease of summer in her room, and her own month ahead.
---
The little lavatory with its long high window sending in the light from across the two sets of back to back tree-shaded Bloomsbury gardens, its little shabby open sink cupboard facing her with its dim unpolished taps and the battered enamel cans on its cracked and blistered wooden top became this morning one of her own rooms, a happy little corner in the growing life that separated her from Wimpole Street. There were no corners such as this in the beautiful clever Hampstead house; no remote shabby happy corners at all; nothing brown and old and at peace. Between him and his house were his housekeeper and servants; between him and his life was his profession﻿ ﻿… and the complex group of people with whom he must perpetually deal, with whom he dealt in alternations of intimacy and formality. He was still at his best in his practice. That was still his life. There was nothing more real as yet in his life than certain times and moments in his room at Wimpole Street.﻿ ﻿… Life had answered no other questions for him.﻿ ﻿… His thought-life and his personal life were troubled and dark and cold﻿ ﻿… in spite of his attachment to some of his family group﻿ ﻿… he could buy beautiful things, and travel freely in his leisure﻿ ﻿… perhaps that, those two glorious things, were sufficient compensation. But there was something wrong about them; they gave a false sense of power﻿ ﻿…

短短一场酣睡过后清清爽爽，她早早起了身，昨日的记忆给往常焦灼勿促的早饭添了星期天般的从容。她靠自己的所有而强壮。温波尔街只拴着她一份待尽的职分。她能把这些职分看成一团清清楚楚的乱麻，衬着人人夏天即将到来的兴奋；那兴奋本身就尽够了。病人正涌出城去——两星期内奥利一家就要走了；到时候奥利先生所有的账都必须发出去。一个月后汉考克先生也要走。她自己的假期之前的那一个月，将几乎无事可做。若是那之前把所有人的账都核完，她那一个月里就能从从容容把它们发出去……那么这一个月里，除了日日递减的日常事务和催人人核账，便无事可做；然后宅子归她一个人，只留着莱顿先生；她房间里夏日的清凉安闲，和她自己的一个月在前头。
---
那间小盥洗室，高高的长窗透进两排背靠背的、绿树荫覆的布卢姆斯伯里花园那头的光，那口寒碜的敞式脸盆柜迎着她，龙头晦暗无光，开裂起泡的木柜面上磕瘪的搪瓷水罐——今晨成了她自己的房间之一，在那把她同温波尔街分开的、日渐生长的生命里一个快活的小角落。汉普斯特德那座美丽聪明的宅子里没有这样的角落；根本没有偏远、寒碜而快活的角落；没有一样棕色的、老旧的、安宁的东西。隔在他和他的宅子之间的是他的管家和仆人；隔在他和他的生活之间的是他的职业……和他必须永无休止地打交道的那一群人，亲疏交替地打着交道。他仍在他的诊所里处于全盛。那仍旧是他的生活。他的生活里，迄今没有比温波尔街他房间里的一些时刻更真实的东西。……生活没有回答过他别的问题。……他的思想生活和个人生活烦恼、幽暗而寒冷……尽管他对他那一族里的几个人有情分……他能买美丽的东西，闲暇时随意旅行……也许那两样堂皇的东西，就是足够的补偿了。可它们有点不对；它们给人权力的错觉……

Supposing I were able to go anywhere on this page﻿ ﻿… Ippington﻿ ﻿… 295 m.; pop. 760﻿ ﻿… trains to Tudworth and thence two or three times daily﻿ ﻿… Spray Bay Hotel.﻿ ﻿… A sparrow cheeped on the window sill and fluttered away. The breath of happiness poured in at the high window; all the places in the railway guide told over their charms; mountains and lakes and rivers, innumerable strips of coast, village streets to walk along for the first time, leading out﻿ ﻿… going, somewhere, in a train. Standing on tiptoe she gazed her thoughts across the two garden spaces towards the grimed backs of the large brown houses. Why was one allowed to be so utterly happy? There it was﻿ ﻿… happily here and happily going away﻿ ﻿… away.
