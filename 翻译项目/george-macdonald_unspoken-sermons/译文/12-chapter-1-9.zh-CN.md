### The Hands of the Father / 父的手
===Original===

> “Father, into thy hand I commend my spirit.”

Neither St. Matthew nor St. Mark tells us of any words uttered by our Lord after the *Eloi*. They both, along with St. Luke, tell us of a cry with a loud voice, and the giving up of the ghost; between which cry and the giving up, St. Luke records the words, “Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit.” St. Luke says nothing of the *Eloi* prayer of desolation. St. John records neither the “*Eloi*,” nor the “Father into thy hands,” nor the loud cry. He tells us only that after Jesus had received the vinegar, he said, “It is finished,” and bowed his head, and gave up the ghost.
Will the Lord ever tell us why he cried so? Was it the cry of relief at the touch of death? Was it the cry of victory? Was it the cry of gladness that he had endured to the end? Or did the Father look out upon him in answer to his “My God,” and the blessedness of it make him cry aloud because he could not smile? Was such his condition now that the greatest gladness of the universe could express itself only in a loud cry? Or was it but the last wrench of pain ere the final repose began? It may have been all in one. But never surely in all books, in all words of thinking men, can there be so much expressed as lay unarticulated in that cry of the Son of God. Now had he made his Father Lord no longer in the might of making and loving alone, but Lord in right of devotion and deed of love. Now should inward sonship and the spirit of glad sacrifice be born in the hearts of men; for the divine obedience was perfected by suffering. He had been amongst his brethren what he would have his brethren be. He had done for them what he would have them do for God and for each other. God was henceforth inside and beneath them, as well as around and above them, suffering with them and for them, giving them all he had, his very life-being, his essence of existence, what best he loved, what best he was. He had been among them, their God-brother. And the mighty story ends with a cry.
Then the cry meant, “It is finished”; the cry meant, “Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit.” Every highest human act is just a giving back to God of that which he first gave to us. “Thou God hast given me: here again is thy gift. I send my spirit home.” Every act of worship is a holding up to God of what God hath made us.

> “父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里。”

圣马太和圣马可都没有告诉我们，主在*以罗伊（Eloi）*之后还说了什么话。他们两位，连同圣路加，都记有一声大声的喊叫和气的断绝；在这喊叫与断气之间，圣路加记下这话：“父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里。”圣路加没有提那荒凉之祷的*以罗伊*。圣约翰既没有记“*以罗伊*”，也没有记“父啊，交在你手里”，也没有记那大声喊叫。他只告诉我们，耶稣受了醋之后，说“成了”，便低下头，将灵交了。
主会有一天告诉我们他为何那样喊叫吗？那是死亡之触时如释重负的一声吗？是得胜的一声吗？是庆幸他忍耐到底的一声吗？还是父应答他的“我的神”而向他观看，那福乐使他只能喊出声来、因为再不能微笑？他此刻的光景是否如此，以至宇宙间最大的欢喜只能以一声大喊来表达？或者那只是最终安息开始前、疼痛最后的一拧？或许这一切都在一声之中。但确实，在一切的书中、在一切思想者的言语中，都从来没有那么多东西，像神之子那一声喊里未及成言地躺着的那么多。如今他已使他的父作主——不再是仅凭创造与爱的大能，而是凭着奉献的权利与爱的作为。如今，内在的儿子名分与甘愿牺牲之灵，要在人的心中生出；因为属神的顺服（obedience）已借受苦得以完全。他在弟兄们中间，已作了他要弟兄们成为的样子。他为他们行了要他们为神、为彼此行的事。神从此在他们里面、在他们底下，也在他们周围、在他们之上，与他们同苦、为他们受苦，把他们所有的一切都给他们——他非常的生命存有、他存在的本质、他最爱的、他最善的。他曾在他们中间，作他们的神弟兄。而这宏伟的故事以一声喊结束。
于是那喊声意味着“成了”；那喊声意味着“父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里”。人每一个最高的行为，都只是把神先赐给我们的归还给神。“神啊，你赐给了我：看哪，又是你的恩赐。我把我的灵送回家去。”每一个敬拜的行为，都是把神所造的我们向神举起。

“Here, Lord, look what I have got: feel with me in what thou hast made me, in this thy own bounty, my being. I am thy child, and know not how to thank thee save by uplifting the heave-offering of the overflowing of thy life, and calling aloud, ‘It is thine: it is mine. I am thine, and therefore I am mine.’ ” The vast operations of the spiritual as of the physical world, are simply a turning again to the source.
The last act of our Lord in thus commending his spirit at the close of his life, was only a summing up of what he had been doing all his life. He had been offering this sacrifice, the sacrifice of himself, all the years, and in thus sacrificing he had lived the divine life. Every morning when he went out ere it was day, every evening when he lingered on the night-lapt mountain after his friends were gone, he was offering himself to his Father in the communion of loving words, of high thoughts, of speechless feelings; and, between, he turned to do the same thing in deed, namely, in loving word, in helping thought, in healing action towards his fellows; for the way to worship God while the daylight lasts is to work; the service of God, the only “divine service,” is the helping of our fellows.
I do not seek to point out this commending of our spirits to the Father as a duty: that is to turn the highest privilege we possess into a burden grievous to be borne. But I want to show that it is the simplest blessedest thing in the human world.
For the Human Being may say thus with himself: “Am I going to sleep﻿—to lose consciousness﻿—to be helpless for a time﻿—thoughtless﻿—dead? Or, more awful consideration, in the dreams that may come may I not be weak of will and scant of conscience?﻿—Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit. I give myself back to thee. Take me, soothe me, refresh me, ‘make me over again.’ Am I going out into the business and turmoil of the day, where so many temptations may come to do less honourably, less faithfully, less kindly, less diligently than the Ideal Man would have me do?﻿—Father, into thy hands. Am I going to do a good deed? Then, of all times﻿—Father, into thy hands; lest the enemy should have me now. Am I going to do a hard duty, from which I would gladly be turned aside﻿—to refuse a friend’s request, to urge a neighbour’s conscience?﻿—Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit. Am I in pain?

“看哪，主啊，我所得着的：请与我一同感受你所造我的、你自己的恩赐——我的存有。我是你的孩子，除了举起你生命满溢的举祭、大声呼说‘这是你的：这是我的。我是你的，所以我是我的’，我不知道该怎样谢你。”属灵世界如物质世界的一切浩大运行，不过是向源头的一次回转。
我们主在生命结束之际这样把灵交托，这最后的行动，只是他一生所作所为的一个总括。这些年他一直在献这祭——他自己的祭；在这样献祭中，他一直活那属神的生命。每天清晨，天未亮他就出去；每天晚上，朋友走后他还流连在被夜色包裹的山上——他都在爱的话语、高的思想、无言的情感之相通中，把自己献给他的父；在两下之间，他转而在行为上做同样的事，就是在爱的话语、助人的思想、医治的行动上，向他的同伴；因为日光之下敬拜神的方式就是作工；对神的事奉——唯一当称“神圣事奉”的——就是帮助我们的同伴。
我并不想把这样把我们自己的灵交托给父指点为本分：那是把我们所有的最高特权变成难以担负的重担。但我想表明，这是人世间最单纯、最蒙福的事。
因为人可以这样对自己说：“我要去睡了——要失去意识——一时无助——无思——死了一般吗？或者，更可畏的考虑：在可能来的梦里，我岂不会意志软弱、良心稀薄？——父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里。我把自己归还给你。接住我，抚慰我，更新我，‘把我重新造一遍。’我要出去进入这日的营营与扰攘，那里会有这么多试探，要我做那比理想之人要我做的更不光荣、更不忠信、更不仁慈、更不殷勤的事吗？——父啊，交在你手里。我要行一件善事吗？那么，胜过任何时候——父啊，交在你手里；惟恐仇敌这时得着我。我要行一件艰苦的本分、一件我巴不得被推掉的本分吗——拒绝朋友的请求、促动邻人的良心？——父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里。我在痛苦中吗？

Is illness coming upon me to shut out the glad visions of a healthy brain, and bring me such as are troubled and untrue?﻿—Take my spirit, Lord, and see, as thou art wont, that it has no more to bear than it can bear. Am I going to die? Thou knowest, if only from the cry of thy Son, how terrible that is; and if it comes not to me in so terrible a shape as that in which it came to him, think how poor to bear I am beside him. I do not know what the struggle means; for, of the thousands who pass through it every day, not one enlightens his neighbour left behind; but shall I not long with agony for one breath of thy air, and not receive it? shall I not be torn asunder with dying?﻿—I will question no more: Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit. For it is thy business, not mine. Thou wilt know every shade of my suffering; thou wilt care for me with thy perfect fatherhood; for that makes my sonship, and inwraps and infolds it. As a child I could bear great pain when my father was leaning over me, or had his arm about me: how much nearer my soul cannot thy hands come!﻿—yea, with a comfort, father of me, that I have never yet even imagined; for how shall my imagination overtake thy swift heart? I care not for the pain, so long as my spirit is strong, and into thy hands I commend that spirit. If thy love, which is better than life, receive it, then surely thy tenderness will make it great.”
Thus may the Human Being say with himself.
Think, brothers, think, sisters, we walk in the air of an eternal fatherhood. Every uplifting of the heart is a looking up to The Father. Graciousness and truth are around, above, beneath us, yea, *in* us. When we are least worthy, then, most tempted, hardest, unkindest, let us yet commend our spirits into his hands. Whither else dare we send them? How the earthly father would love a child who would creep into his room with angry, troubled face, and sit down at his feet, saying when asked what he wanted: “I feel so naughty, papa, and I want to get good”! Would he say to his child: “How dare you! Go away, and be good, and then come to me?” And shall we dare to think God would send us away if we came thus, and would not be pleased that we came, even if we were angry as Jonah? Would we not let all the tenderness of our nature flow forth upon such a child?

疾病要临到我，把健康头脑的欢喜异象关在外面，把烦乱不真的景象带来吗？——主啊，接住我的灵，并照你的惯例看顾它，叫它受的不多于它所能受的。我要死了吗？你知道那是何等可怕——哪怕只凭你儿子那一声喊；它若不以临到他的那样可怕的形状临到我，就想想比起来我是多么不配承受。我不知道那争扎意味着什么；因为每天经历它的千百人中，没有一个能启迪他留在身后的邻人；但我会不会切慕你的一口气而不得？我会不会被死撕开？——我不再问了：父啊，我将我的灵交在你手里。因为那是你的事，不是我的。我受苦的每一层深浅你都必知道；你必以你完全的父爱看顾我；因为是那父爱造作我的儿子名分、包裹它、怀抱它。我作孩子时，只要父亲俯身在我上面、或用臂膀搂着我，就能忍受大痛：你的手更能挨近我的灵魂到何等地步！——是的，以一种我从未想象过的安慰，我的父；因为我的想象怎能追上你迅捷的心？只要我的灵刚强，我就不在乎那痛，而我把这灵交在你手里。你的爱——比生命更好——若接住它，你的温柔必使它尊大。”
人可以这样对自己说。
弟兄们，想想，姊妹们，想想：我们行走在永恒父性的空气里。心每一次的抬起，都是向父（the Father）仰望。恩慈与真理在我们四围、在我们上面、在我们底下，是的，*在*我们里面。当我们最不配、最受试探、最硬心、最无亲情的时候，让我们仍把我们的灵交在他手里。此外我们还敢把它们送到哪里去呢？地上的父亲会怎样爱这样一个孩子：他带着愤怒烦乱的脸爬进父亲的房间，坐在他脚边，被问到要什么时说：“我觉得自己很坏，爸爸，我想要变好”！他会对孩子说：“你怎敢来！走开，先变好，再来见我”吗？我们岂敢想，我们这样去时神会把我们送走、会不喜悦我们来——即使我们像约拿那样发怒？我们岂不让我们天性全部的温柔都流泻到这样一个孩子身上呢？

And shall we dare to think that if we being evil know how to give good gifts to our children, God will not give us his own spirit when we come to ask him? Will not some heavenly dew descend cool upon the hot anger? some genial raindrop on the dry selfishness? some glance of sunlight on the cloudy hopelessness? Bread, at least, will be given, and not a stone; water, at least, will be sure, and not vinegar mingled with gall.
Nor is there anything we can ask for ourselves that we may not ask for another. We may commend any brother, any sister, to the common fatherhood. And there will be moments when, filled with that spirit which is the Lord, nothing will ease our hearts of their love but the commending of all men, all our brothers, all our sisters, to the one Father. Nor shall we ever know that repose in the Father’s hands, that rest of the Holy Sepulchre, which the Lord knew when the agony of death was over, when the storm of the world died away behind his retiring spirit, and he entered the regions where there is only life, and therefore all that is not music is silence, (for all noise comes of the conflict of Life and Death)﻿—we shall never be able, I say, to rest in the bosom of the Father, till the fatherhood is fully revealed to us in the love of the brothers. For he cannot be our father save as he is their father; and if we do not see him and feel him as their father, we cannot know him as ours. Never shall we know him aright until we rejoice and exult for our race that he is *the* Father. He that loveth not his brother whom he hath seen, how can he love God whom he hath not seen? To rest, I say, at last, even in those hands into which the Lord commended his spirit, we must have learned already *to love our neighbour as ourselves*.

我们既邪恶尚且知道把好东西给儿女，我们岂敢想，我们去求时神不会把他自己的灵给我们？会不会有属天的甘露凉凉地降在火热的怒气上？会不会有亲切的雨点落在干渴的自私上？会不会有一瞥阳光照在阴郁的绝望上？饼，至少必被给，不是石头；水，至少必可靠，不是掺着苦胆的醋。
我们能为自己求的，没有一样不能为别人求。我们可以把任何弟兄、任何姊妹交托给那共同的父性。也必有这样的时刻：被那主之灵充满时，唯有把所有的人、我们所有的弟兄、所有的姊妹都交托给那一位父，才能卸下我们心中爱的重担。我们也永远不能知道那在父手中的安息——那圣墓的安息，就是主在死亡的痛苦过去之后、在世界的风暴在他退去的灵后面平息之后、在他进入那只有生命的境界（因此凡不是音乐的尽是寂静，因为一切噪声都来自生死之争）时所知道的——我说，我们永远不能安息在父的怀中，直到那父性在弟兄的爱中向我们完全启示。因为唯有作他们的父，他才能作我们的父；我们若看不见、感受不到他是他们的父，就不能认识他是我们的。直到我们为我们的族类喜乐夸胜、因为他是*那*父，我们才能正当地认识他。不爱所看见的弟兄的，怎能爱没有看见的神呢？要最终安息——我说——在主曾把他灵交托的那双手里，我们必须已经学会*爱人如己*。
