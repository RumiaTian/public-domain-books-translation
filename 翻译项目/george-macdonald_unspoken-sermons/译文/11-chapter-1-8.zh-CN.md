### The *Eloi* / 以罗伊（Eloi）
===Original===

> “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”

I do not know that I should dare to approach this, of all utterances into which human breath has ever been moulded, most awful in import, did I not feel that, containing both germ and blossom of the final devotion, it contains therefore the deepest practical lesson the human heart has to learn. The Lord, the Revealer, hides nothing that can be revealed, and will not warn away the foot that treads in naked humility even upon the ground of that terrible conflict between him and Evil, when the smoke of the battle that was fought not only with garments rolled in blood but with burning and fuel of fire, rose up between him and his Father, and for the one terrible moment ere he broke the bonds of life, and walked weary and triumphant into his arms, hid God from the eyes of his Son. He will give us even to meditate the one thought that slew him at last, when he could bear no more, and fled to the Father to know that he loved him, and was well-pleased with him. For Satan had come at length yet again, to urge him with his last temptation; to tell him that although he had done his part, God had forgotten his; that although he had lived by the word of his mouth, that mouth had no word more to speak to him; that although he had refused to tempt him, God had left him to be tempted more than he could bear; that although he had worshipped none other, for that worship God did not care. The Lord hides not his sacred sufferings, for truth is light, and would be light in the minds of men. The Holy Child, the Son of the Father, has nothing to conceal, but all the Godhead to reveal. Let us then put off our shoes, and draw near, and bow the head, and kiss those feet that bear forever the scars of our victory. In those feet we clasp the safety of our suffering, our sinning brotherhood.
It is with the holiest fear that we should approach the terrible fact of the sufferings of our Lord. Let no one think that those were less because he was more. The more delicate the nature, the more alive to all that is lovely and true, lawful and right, the more does it feel the antagonism of pain, the inroad of death upon life; the more dreadful is that breach of the harmony of things whose sound is torture. He felt more than man could feel, because he had a larger feeling. He was even therefore worn out sooner than another man would have been.

> “我的神，我的神，为什么离弃我？”

我不知道自己是否敢就近这句话——人的一切气息所曾凝成的言辞中，含义最可畏的一句——若非感到它既含着那至终奉献的胚芽与花朵，也就因此含着人心所必学之最深的实践教训。主，那启示者，不隐藏任何可启示的事，也不驱逐那以赤裸谦卑踏进他与恶之间那可怕争战之地的脚——那场争战，不仅以滚血的衣服、更以烈火与燃料争战，其硝烟升在他与他父（the Father）之间，在那断开生命之缆、疲惫而得胜地走进父怀之前的那个可畏时刻，把神藏在他儿子的眼外。他甚至容我们默想那最后杀死他的一个念头——当他再不能承受、逃到父那里去知道父爱他、喜悦他之时。因为撒但终于又来了，要以最后的试探催逼他；告诉他：他虽尽了他的分，神却忘了神的分；他虽靠神口中的道活着，那口却再没有道对他说；他虽拒绝试探神，神却任他被试探过于所能受；他虽不敬拜别个，神却不在乎那敬拜。主不隐藏他圣洁的受难，因为真理就是光，且愿作人心里的光。那圣洁的孩子、父的儿子，没有任何要隐藏的，只有整个的神格要启示。那么，让我们脱下鞋，就近，垂首，亲那双永远带着我们胜利之疤的脚。在那双脚里，我们抱住我们受苦的、犯罪的弟兄们得安全。
我们当以最圣洁的敬畏，就近我们主受难这可怕的事实。谁也不要以为因为他更是神，那些苦难就更少。天性越精致，对一切可爱真实、合法公义之事越敏感，就越感受到痛苦的敌对、死亡对生命的侵入；那事物和谐之破裂——其声就是折磨——就越可怕。他比人感受得更多，因为他有更大的感受力。他甚至因此比别人更快地耗竭。

These sufferings were awful indeed when they began to invade the region about the will; when the struggle to keep consciously trusting in God began to sink in darkness; when the Will of The Man put forth its last determined effort in that cry after the vanishing vision of the Father: “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” Never had it been so with him before. Never before had he been unable to see God beside him. Yet never was God nearer him than now. For never was Jesus more divine. He could not see, could not feel him near; and yet it is “*My* God” that he cries.
Thus the Will of Jesus, in the very moment when his faith seems about to yield, is finally triumphant. It has no *feeling* now to support it, no beatific vision to absorb it. It stands naked in his soul and tortured, as he stood naked and scourged before Pilate. Pure and simple and surrounded by fire, it declares for God. The sacrifice ascends in the cry, “My God.” The cry comes not out of happiness, out of peace, out of hope. Not even out of suffering comes that cry. It was a cry *in* desolation, but it came out of Faith. It is the last voice of Truth, speaking when it can but cry. The divine horror of that moment is unfathomable by human soul. It was blackness of darkness. And yet he would believe. Yet he would hold fast. God was his God yet. *My God*﻿—and in the cry came forth the Victory, and all was over soon. Of the peace that followed that cry, the peace of a perfect soul, large as the universe, pure as light, ardent as life, victorious for God and his brethren, he himself alone can ever know the breadth and length, and depth and height.
Without this last trial of all, the temptations of our Master had not been so full as the human cup could hold; there would have been one region through which we had to pass wherein we might call aloud upon our Captain-Brother, and there would be no voice or hearing: he had avoided the fatal spot! The temptations of the desert came to the young, strong man with his road before him and the presence of his God around him; nay, gathered their very force from the exuberance of his conscious faith. “Dare and do, for God is with thee,” said the devil. “I know it, and therefore I will wait,” returned the king of his brothers.

当苦难开始侵入意志四周的领域，当竭力保持有意识地信靠神开始在黑暗中下沉，当*那人*的意志，在父的形象隐没之后，以那呼喊发出它最后决定性的一搏：“我的神，我的神，为什么离弃我？”——这些苦难确实可畏。他从前从未如此。他从前从没有看不见神在身旁。然而神从未像此刻这样亲近他。因为耶稣从未像此刻这样属神。他看不见、感觉不到他在近旁；然而他喊的正是“*我的*神”。
这样，耶稣的意志，在他的信心（faith）似乎就要屈服的那一刻，得了最终的胜利。此刻它没有*感觉*支撑，没有荣福异象吸引。它赤裸在他的灵魂里、受着折磨，如他赤裸受鞭打站在彼拉多（Pilate）面前一样。纯一而简单，四围是火，它宣告站在神一边。祭物在那呼喊中上升：“我的神。”这呼喊不是出于幸福，不是出于平安，不是出于盼望。甚至不是出于受苦。那是在荒凉*之中*的呼喊，却出于信心。那是真理的最后声音，在只能喊叫时说话。那一刻属神的恐怖，人心无法测度。那是黑暗之黑暗。然而他仍愿信。然而他仍愿持守。神仍是他的神。*我的神*——胜利随那呼喊而出，不久一切就都完结了。至于那呼喊之后的平安——一个完全灵魂的平安，大如宇宙，纯如光，炽如生命，为神并为弟兄们得胜——其阔、其长、其深、其高，唯有他自己永远知道。
若没有这最后的一试，我们夫子的试探之杯就没有满到人所能容的程度；就会有一个区域，我们行经其中时大声呼求我们的长兄——主帅，却无声音无应允：他避开了那致命之地！旷野的试探临到那年轻力壮、前路正长、神的同在环围的人；不，试探的力量正取自他自觉之信心的充沛。“放胆去行吧，因为神与你同在，”魔鬼说。“我知道，所以我等候，”他弟兄们的王回答。

And now, after three years of divine action, when his course is run, when the old age of finished work is come, when the whole frame is tortured until the regnant brain falls whirling down the blue gulf of fainting, and the giving up of the ghost is at hand, when the friends have forsaken him and fled, comes the voice of the enemy again at his ear: “Despair and die, for God is not with thee. All is in vain. Death, not Life, is thy refuge. Make haste to Hades, where thy torture will be over. Thou hast deceived thyself. He never was with thee. He was the God of Abraham. Abraham is dead. Whom makest thou thyself?” “My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?” the Master cries. For God was his God still, although he had forsaken him﻿—forsaken *his vision* that his faith might glow out triumphant; forsaken *himself*? no; come nearer to him than ever; come nearer, even as﻿—but with a yet deeper, more awful pregnancy of import﻿—even as the Lord himself withdrew from the bodily eyes of his friends, that he might dwell in their profoundest being.
I do not think it was our Lord’s deepest trial when in the garden he prayed that the cup might pass from him, and prayed yet again that the will of the Father might be done. For that will was then present with him. He was living and acting in that will. But now the foreseen horror has come. He is drinking the dread cup, and the Will has vanished from his eyes. Were that Will visible in his suffering, his will could bow with tearful gladness under the shelter of its grandeur. But now his will is left alone to drink the cup of The Will in torture. In the sickness of this agony, the Will of Jesus arises perfect at last; and of itself, unsupported now, declares﻿—a naked consciousness of misery hung in the waste darkness of the universe﻿—declares for God, in defiance of pain, of death, of apathy, of self, of negation, of the blackness within and around it; calls aloud upon the vanished God.
This is the Faith of the Son of God. God withdrew, as it were, that the perfect Will of the Son might arise and go forth to find the Will of the Father.

而如今，经过三年属神的行动，他的路程已尽，完工的暮年已到，整个身躯受折磨，直到作王的大脑晕旋着坠入昏厥的蓝色深渊，交出灵的时候近了，朋友们都离弃他逃走了，仇敌的声音又在他耳畔响起：“绝望而死吧，因为神不与你同在。一切尽是徒然。死，不是生，才是你的避难所。赶紧下到阴间吧，那里你的折磨就结束了。你欺骗了自己。他从未与你同在。他是亚伯拉罕的神。亚伯拉罕死了。你将自己当作谁呢？”“我的神，我的神，为什么离弃我？”主呼喊。因为神仍是他的神，尽管他离弃了他——离弃了*他的异象*，好叫他的信心灿然得胜；离弃了*他自己*吗？不；比以往更亲近他；更亲近，正如——却带着更深、更可畏的意蕴——正如主自己从朋友们肉身的眼前退去，好住进他们最深的存有里。
我不认为我们主最深的试炼是在园中祷告叫杯离开他、又再祷告愿父的旨意成全的时候。因为那时那旨意与他同在。他正活在那旨意中、行在那旨意中。但如今那预见的恐怖已经来到。他正喝那可畏的杯，而那旨意已从他的眼中消失。若那旨意在受苦中可见，他的意志就能含泪欢欣地俯伏在它宏伟的荫庇之下。但如今他的意志被单独留下，在折磨中喝下那旨意之杯。在这剧痛的昏病中，耶稣的意志至终完全挺立；孤身无倚，宣告——一个赤裸的苦难意识悬在宇宙荒废的黑暗中——宣告站在神一边，蔑视痛苦、死亡、麻木、自我、否定、以及它里面和四围的黑；大声呼召那隐没的神。
这是神儿子的信心。神退去了，仿佛为叫子的完全意志得以挺立、出发，去寻见父的旨意。

Is it possible that even then he thought of the lost sheep who could not believe that God was their Father; and for them, too, in all their loss and blindness and unlove, cried, saying the word they might say, knowing for them that “God” means “Father” and more, and knowing now, as he had never known till now, what a fearful thing it is to be without God and without hope? I dare not answer the question I put.
But wherein or what can this Alpine apex of faith have to do with the creatures who call themselves Christians, creeping about in the valleys, hardly knowing that there are mountains above them, save that they take offence at and stumble over the pebbles washed across their path by the glacier streams? I will tell you. We are and remain such creeping Christians, because we look at ourselves and not at Christ; because we gaze at the marks of our own soiled feet, and the trail of our own defiled garments, instead of up at the snows of purity, whither the soul of Christ clomb. Each, putting his foot in the footprint of the Master, and so defacing it, turns to examine how far his neighbour’s footprint corresponds with that which he still calls the Master’s, although it is but his own. Or, having committed a petty fault, I mean a fault such as only a petty creature could commit, we mourn over the defilement to ourselves, and the shame of it before our friends, children, or servants, instead of hastening to make the due confession and amends to our fellow, and then, forgetting our paltry self with its well-earned disgrace, lift up our eyes to the glory which alone will quicken the true man in us, and kill the peddling creature we so wrongly call our *self*. The true self is that which can look Jesus in the face, and say *My Lord*.
When the inward sun is shining, and the wind of thought, blowing where it lists amid the flowers and leaves of fancy and imagination, rouses glad forms and feelings, it is easy to look upwards, and say “My God.” It is easy when the frosts of external failure have braced the mental nerves to healthy endurance and fresh effort after labour, it is easy then to turn to God and trust in him, in whom all honest exertion gives an ability as well as a right to trust. It is easy in pain, so long as it does not pass certain undefinable bounds, to hope in God for deliverance, or pray for strength to endure.

是否可能，即使在那时，他还想着那些不信神是他们父的迷羊（the lost sheep）；也为着他们——在他们一切的失丧、失明、无爱之中——呼喊，说出他们所能说的话，替他们知道“神”意味着“父”且不止于父，并且如今——他从未像此刻这样知道——没有神、没有盼望是何等可怕的事？我不敢回答我提出的问题。
但这信心的阿尔卑斯之巅，与那些自称基督徒、在谷底爬行、几乎不知头顶有山的受造之物有什么相干——除了他们被冰川溪水冲过路面的卵石绊倒跌跤？我告诉你们。我们之所以是、且始终是这等爬行的基督徒，是因为我们看自己而不看基督；因为我们盯着自己污足的印子和脏衣的拖痕，而不仰望那纯净的雪——基督的灵魂所攀向之处。每个人把脚踩进主的脚印、把它踩污，随即转身去察看邻人的脚印与那他仍称为主的脚印相符到什么程度——尽管那不过是自己的。或者，犯了一个小过失——我是说只有一个小受造之物才犯得出的过失——我们哀叹自己的沾污、哀叹它在朋友、儿女、仆人面前的羞耻，却不赶紧向同伴作当有的认罪与补偿，然后忘掉那带着应得之辱的鄙陋自我，举目仰望那独能激励我们里面真实之人、杀死我们错称之为*自我*的那个贩卖小我的荣耀。真实的自我，是能仰望耶稣的脸说*我主*的那个。
当内里的太阳照耀，思想之风任意吹拂于幻想与想象的花叶之间，唤起欢喜的形象与情感时，向上看、说“我的神”是容易的。当外面失败的霜寒把心神的神经锻炼成健康的忍耐与劳作后的新努力时，转向神信靠他也是容易的——在他里面，一切诚实的奋发既给信靠的权利、也给信靠的能力。在痛苦中——只要它不越过某些无法界定的界限——盼望神的解救、或祷告（prayer）求力量忍受，也是容易的。

But what is to be done when all feeling is gone? when a man does not know whether he believes or not, whether he loves or not? when art, poetry, religion are nothing to him, so swallowed up is he in pain, or mental depression, or disappointment, or temptation, or he knows not what? It seems to him then that God does not care for him, and certainly he does not care for God. If he is still humble, he thinks that he is so bad that God cannot care for him. And he then believes for the time that God loves us only because and when and while we love him; instead of believing that God loves us always because he is our God, and that we live only by his love. Or he does not believe in a God at all, which is better.
So long as we have nothing to say to God, nothing to do with him, save in the sunshine of the mind when we feel him near us, we are poor creatures, willed upon, not willing; reeds, flowering reeds, it may be, and pleasant to behold, but only reeds blown about of the wind; not bad, but poor creatures.
And how in such a condition do we generally act? Do we not sit mourning over the loss of our feelings? or worse, make frantic efforts to rouse them? or, ten times worse, relapse into a state of temporary atheism, and yield to the pressing temptation? or, being heartless, consent to remain careless, conscious of evil thoughts and low feelings alone, but too lazy, too content to rouse ourselves against them? We know we must get rid of them some day, but meantime﻿—never mind; we do not *feel* them bad, we do not feel anything else good; we are asleep and we know it, and we cannot be troubled to wake. No impulse comes to arouse us, and so we remain as we are.
God does not, by the instant gift of his Spirit, make us always feel right, desire good, love purity, aspire after him and his will. Therefore either he will not, or he cannot. If he will not, it must be because it would not be well to do so. If he cannot, then he would not if he could; else a better condition than God’s is conceivable to the mind of God﻿—a condition in which he could save the creatures whom he has made, better than he can save them. The truth is this: He wants to make us in his own image, *choosing* the good, *refusing* the evil. How should he effect this if he were *always* moving us from within, as he does at divine intervals, towards the beauty of holiness?

但当一切感觉都消失时，该怎么办？当一个人不知道自己是信还是不信、是爱还是不爱？当艺术、诗、宗教对他都成虚空，他已如此被痛苦、或心灵的抑郁、或失望、或试探、或他不知道的什么吞没？那时在他看来，神不顾念他，而他也确实不顾念神。他若仍然谦卑，他想自己坏到神无法顾念。于是他一时相信：神爱我们，只是因为、只是当、只是当我们爱他时；而不是相信神爱我们始终如一，因为他是我们的神，我们只靠他的爱活着。或者他根本不信有神——那倒更好。
只要我们唯有在心思的晴日里、感觉他亲近时，才与神有话说、与他有事关，我们就是贫乏的受造之物——被意志所加、而非有意志的；芦苇，开花的芦苇，也许好看，却只是随风摇摆的芦苇；不坏，但是贫乏的受造之物。
在这样的光景里，我们通常怎样行呢？我们岂不是坐着哀悼感觉的丧失？或者更糟，狂乱地努力唤起它们？或者，糟糕十倍，退回到一时的无神论，屈服于紧逼的试探？或者，既然心里冰冷，就甘于随波逐流，只意识到恶念和低下的情感，却懒得、也太安逸得不肯奋起抵挡它们？我们知道总有一天必须除掉它们，但眼下——不要紧；我们不*觉得*它们坏，也觉不到别的什么好；我们睡着了且知道自己睡着，却嫌麻烦不愿醒。没有冲动来唤醒我们，于是我们依然故我。
神并不以他灵即刻的恩赐，使我们总感觉对、总渴望善、总爱纯净、总渴慕他和他的旨意。所以，他或者不愿，或者不能。他若不愿，必是因为那样并不好。他若不能，那么他即使能也不愿；否则一个比神的更好的光景在神的心意中竟可设想——一个他能比现在更好地拯救他所造之物的光景。真相乃是：他要照他自己的形象造我们——*拣选*善，*拒绝*恶。他若*总*从里面推动我们——像他在属神的间歇所做的那样——朝向圣洁之美，他怎能成全这一点呢？

God gives us room *to be*; does not oppress us with his will; “stands away from us,” that we may act from ourselves, that we may exercise the pure will for good. Do not, therefore, imagine me to mean that we can do anything of ourselves without God. If we choose the right at last, it is all God’s doing, and only the more his that it is ours, only in a far more marvellous way his than if he had kept us filled with all holy impulses precluding the need of choice. For up to this very point, for this very point, he has been educating us, leading us, pushing us, driving us, enticing us, that we may choose him and his will, and so be tenfold more his children, of his own best making, in the freedom of the will found our own first in its loving sacrifice to him, for which in his grand fatherhood he has been thus working from the foundations of the earth, than we could be in the most ecstatic worship flowing from the divinest impulse, without this *willing* sacrifice. For God made our individuality as well as, and a greater marvel than, our dependence; made our *apartness* from himself, that freedom should bind us divinely dearer to himself, with a new and inscrutable marvel of love; for the Godhead is still at the root, is the making root of our individuality, and the freer the man, the stronger the bond that binds him to him who made his freedom. He made our wills, and is striving to make them free; for only in the perfection of our individuality and the freedom of our wills can we be altogether his children. This is full of mystery, but can we not see enough in it to make us very glad and very peaceful?
Not in any other act than one which, in spite of impulse or of weakness, declares for the Truth, for God, does the will spring into absolute freedom, into true life.
See, then, what lies within our reach every time that we are thus lapt in the folds of night. The highest condition of the human will is in sight, is attainable. I say not the highest condition of the Human Being; that surely lies in the Beatific Vision, in the sight of God. But the highest condition of the Human Will, as distinct, not as separated from God, is when, not seeing God, not seeming to itself to grasp him at all, it yet holds him fast.
