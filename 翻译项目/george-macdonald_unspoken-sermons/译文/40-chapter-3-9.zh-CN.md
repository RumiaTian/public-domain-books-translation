### The Displeasure of Jesus / 耶稣的不悦
===Original===

> When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping which came with her, he groaned in the spirit, and was troubled.

Grimm, in his lexicon to the New Testament, after giving as the equivalent of the word *ἐμβριμάομαι* in pagan use, “I am moved with anger,” “I roar or growl,” “I snort at,” “I am vehemently angry or indignant with someone,” tells us that in Mark 1:43, and Matthew 9:30, it has a meaning different from that of the pagans, namely, “I command with severe admonishment.” That he has any authority for saying so, I do not imagine, and believe the statement a blunder. The Translators and Revisers, however, have in those passages used the word similarly, and in one place, the passage before us, where a true version is of yet more consequence, have taken another liberty and rendered the word “groaned.” The Revisers, at the same time, place in the margin what I cannot but believe its true meaning﻿—“was moved with indignation.”
Let us look at all the passages in which the word is used of the Lord, and so, if we may, learn something concerning him. The only place in the gospel where it is used of any but the Lord is Mark 14:5. Here both versions say of the disciples that they “murmured at” the waste of the ointment by one of the women who anointed the Lord. With regard to this rendering I need only remark that surely “murmured at” can hardly be strong enough, especially seeing “they had indignation among themselves” at the action.
It is indeed right and necessary to insist that many a word must differ in moral weight and colour as used of or by persons of different character. The anger of a good man is a very different thing from the anger of a bad man; the displeasure of Jesus must be a very different thing from the displeasure of a tyrant. But they are both anger, both displeasure, nevertheless. We have no right to change a root-meaning, and say in one case that a word means “he was indignant,” in another that it means “he straitly or strictly charged,” and in a third that it means “he groaned.” Surely not thus shall we arrive at the truth! If any statement is made, any word employed, that we feel unworthy of the Lord, let us refuse it; let us say, “I do not believe that”; or, “There must be something there that I cannot see into: I must wait; it cannot be what it looks to me, and be true of the Lord!”

格里姆在他那部新约词典里，先给出 *ἐμβριμάομαι* 一词在异教用法中的对应义——「我动了怒气」「我吼叫或咆哮」「我对……喷鼻息」「我对某人激烈地发怒或愤慨」——然后告诉我们，在马可福音1:43和马太福音9:30，它有一个不同于异教徒的意思，即「我带着严厉的告诫吩咐」。我不认为他这样说有什么根据，并且相信这陈述是一个错误。然而，译经者与修订者在那些经文里都同样用了那样的译法，而在另一处——就是摆在我们眼前的这段，一个真实译法更关系重大的地方——又取了另一重自由，把这个词译作「悲叹」。修订者同时把我不信不是其真义的——「动了义怒」——放在页边。

让我们看看所有用这个词说到主的经文，这样，倘若可以，我们就能学到一点关于他的事。福音书里唯一一处把这词用在主以外之人身上的，是马可福音14:5。在那里，两个译本都说，对于一位膏主之女人枉费香膏的事，门徒们「向她发怨言」。就这译法我只需指出：「向她发怨言」恐怕不够强，尤其看到他们「彼此动了怒气」。

坚持许多词在用于或出自不同品格之人时，必须在道德分量与色彩上不同，这确实是对的、也是必需的。好人的怒与坏人的怒是很不同的东西；耶稣的不悦必与暴君的不悦很不同。但它们两者都是怒，都是不悦。我们无权更改一个根义，说这处这词的意思是「他动了义怒」，那处是「他严严地嘱咐」，第三处是「他悲叹」。我们必不能因此达到真理！若有任何陈述、任何用词，我们觉得配不上主，就让我们拒绝它；让我们说：「我不信这个」；或说：「那里必有我看不透的东西：我必须等候；它不可能既是我看起来的样子，又是属于主的真实！」

But to accept the word as used of the Lord, and say it means something quite different from what it means when used by the same writer of someone else, appears to me untruthful.
We shall take first the passage, Mark 1:43﻿—in the authorized version, “And he straitly charged him”; in the revised, “And he strictly charged him,” with “sternly” in the margin. Literally, as it seems to me, it reads, and ought to be read, “And being angry” or “displeased” or “vexed” “with him, he immediately dismissed him.” There is even some dissatisfaction implied, I think, in the word I have translated “dismissed.” The word in John 9:34, “they cast him out,” is the same, only a little intensified.
This adds something to the story, and raises the question, Why should Jesus have been angry? If we can find no reason for his anger, we must leave the thing as altogether obscure; for I do not know where to find another meaning for the word, except in the despair of a would-be interpreter.
Jesus had cured the leper﻿—not with his word only, which would have been enough for the mere cure, but was not enough without the touch of his hand﻿—the Sinaitic version says “his hands”﻿—to satisfy the heart of Jesus﻿—a touch defiling him, in the notion of the Jews, but how cleansing to the sense of the leper! The man, however, seems to have been unworthy of this delicacy of divine tenderness. The Lord, who could read his heart, saw that he made him no true response﻿—that there was not awaked in him the faith he desired to rouse: he had not drawn the soul of the man to his. The leper was jubilant in the removal of his pain and isolating uncleanness, in his deliverance from suffering and scorn; he was probably elated with the pride of having had a miracle wrought for *him*. In a word, he was so full of himself that he did not think truly of his deliverer.
The Lord, I say, saw this, or something of this kind, and was not satisfied. He had wanted to give the man something so much better than a pure skin, and had only roused in him an unseemly delight in his own cleanness﻿—*unseemly*, for it was such that he paid no heed to the Lord, but immediately disobeyed his positive command. The moral position the man took was that which displeased the Lord, made him angry. He saw in him positive and rampant self-will and disobedience, an impertinent assurance and self-satisfaction.

但接受这个词用在主身上，又说它在此的意思与同一位作者用在别人身上时的意思大不相同，在我看来是不诚实的。

我们先看马可福音1:43——钦定本作「耶稣严严地嘱咐他」；修订本作「耶稣切切地嘱咐他」，页边注作「严厉地」。照我看，按字面它读作、也应当读作：「耶稣*恼怒*他」——或「不悦」他，或「对他动气」——「就立刻打发他走」。我想，连那个我译作「打发」的词里，也含着几分不满。约翰福音9:34「他们把他赶出去」用的正是这个词，只是稍加强化。

这给故事添了一点东西，也引出了问题：耶稣为什么会动怒？我们若找不出他动怒的理由，就只能把这事当作全然隐晦的；因为除了在自命解经者的绝望里，我不知道还能到哪里为这词另找一个意思。

耶稣治好了那长大麻风的——不只用他的话，单单一句话对治病本身原已足够，却不足以满足耶稣的心，还必须有他手的触摸——西乃抄本作「他的双手」——那触摸在犹太人的观念里叫他染了污秽，对麻风病人的感觉却是何等的洁净！然而这人似乎配不上这神圣温柔的体贴。那能读人心的主，看出他没有给他真实的回应——他里面没有唤醒他所要唤醒的信心：他没有把这人的灵魂吸引到自己的灵魂这里来。那麻风病人为他疼痛与隔绝之不洁的除去而欢腾，为他脱离苦难与轻蔑而欢腾；他多半还因居然有神迹为*他*行出来而傲然自得。总而言之，他满了自己，以致对他的拯救者没有真实的想念。

我说，主看出了这个，或这一类的事，并不满足。他本想给这人一样远比洁净皮肤更好的东西，却只在他里面激起了一种对自己的洁净不合宜的欢喜——*不合宜*，因为它叫他全不理会主，立刻违背了主明确的命令。这人所取的道德立场，就是那使主不悦、使他动怒的。主在他身上看见猖獗而公然的任性与悖逆，看见无礼的自恃与自满。

Filled, not with pure delight, or the childlike merriment that might well burst forth, mingled with tears, at such deliverance; filled, not with gratitude, but gratification, the keener that he had been so long an object of loathing to his people; filled with arrogance because of the favour shown to him, of all men, by the great prophet, and swelling with boast of the same, he left the presence of the healer to thwart his will, and, commanded to tell no man, at once “began”﻿—the frothy, volatile, talking soul﻿—“to publish it much, and to blaze abroad the matter, insomuch that Jesus could no more openly enter into a city, but was without in desert places.”
Let us next look at the account of the healing of the two blind men, given in the ninth chapter of Matthew’s gospel. In both the versions the same phrases are used in translation of the word in question, as in the story of the leper in Mark’s gospel﻿—“straitly,” “strictly,” “sternly charged them.” I read the passage thus: “And Jesus was displeased”﻿—or, perhaps, “much displeased”﻿—“with them, saying, See that no man know it.”
“But they went forth, and spread abroad his fame in all that land.” Surely here we have light on the cause of Jesus’ displeasure with the blind men! it was the same with them as with the leper: they showed themselves bent on their own way, and did not care for his. Doubtless they were, in part, all of them moved by the desire to spread abroad his fame; that may even have seemed to them the best acknowledgment they could render their deliverer. They never suspected that a great man might desire to avoid fame, laying no value upon it, knowing it for a foolish thing. They did not understand that a man desirous of helping his fellows might yet avoid a crowd as obstructive to his object. “What is a prophet without honour?” such virtually ask, nor understand the answer, “A man the more likely to prove a prophet.” These men would repay their healer with trumpeting, not obedience. By them he should have his right﻿—but as they not be judged fit! In his modesty he objected, but they would take care he should not go without his reward! Through them he should reap the praises of men! “Not tell!” they exclaim. “Indeed, we will tell!” They were too grateful not to rumour him, not grateful enough to obey him.
We cannot surely be amazed at their self-sufficiency.

其次让我们看马太福音第九章中医好两个瞎子的记载。两个译本在这词的翻译上，都与马可福音麻风病人的故事用了同样的短语——「严严地」「切切地」「严厉地嘱咐他们」。我这样读这段经文：「耶稣就*不悦*他们」——或作「大大不悦他们」——「说，你们要小心，不可叫人知道。」「但他们出去，竟把他的名声传遍了那地方。」无疑，这里我们就看见了耶稣对两个瞎子不悦的原因之光！他们的情形与那麻风病人一样：他们显出一心走自己的路，不理会他的路。无疑，他们——三人都是——也部分出于传扬他名声的愿望；这愿望在他们看，甚至可能是他们能给拯救者的最好报答。他们从未想到，一个大人物可能渴望避开名声，不把它放在眼里，知道那是件愚蠢的东西。他们不明白，一个渴望帮助同伴的人，可能仍然避开人群，因为人群阻碍他的目标。「没有尊荣的先知算什么先知？」他们实际上这样问，也不懂得那回答：「一个更有可能证明是先知的人。」这些人要用吹喇叭来偿还他们的医治者，而不是用顺服（obedience）。他们要叫他得着他的权利——却要照他们判定合适的方式！他以谦卑推辞，他们却要负责叫他不至没有报酬！他们要叫他借他们收割人的称赞！「不告诉！」他们喊道，「我们偏要告诉！」他们太感谢他了，不能不去传扬他；又感谢得不够，不肯顺服他。

我们对他们的自满，当然不会惊奇。

How many are there not who seem capable of anything for the sake of the church or Christianity, except the one thing its Lord cares about﻿—that they should do what he tells them! He would deliver them from themselves into the liberty of the sons of God, make them his brothers; they leave him to vaunt their church. His commandments are not grievous; they invent commandments for him, and lay them, burdens grievous to be borne, upon the necks of their brethren. God would have us sharers in his bliss﻿—in the very truth of existence; they worship from afar, and will not draw nigh. It was not, I think, the obstruction to his work, not the personal inconvenience it would cause him, that made the Lord angry, but that they would not be his friends, would not do what he told them, would not be the children of his father, and help him to save their brethren. When Peter in his way next﻿—much the same way as theirs﻿—opposed the will of the Father, saying, “That be far from thee, Lord!” he called him Satan, and ordered him behind him.
Does it affect anyone to the lowering of his idea of the Master that he should ever be angry? If so, I would ask him whether his whole conscious experience of anger be such, that he knows but one kind of anger. There is a good anger and a bad anger. There is a wrath of God, and there is a wrath of man that worketh not the righteousness of God. Anger may be as varied as the colour of the rainbow. God’s anger can be nothing but Godlike, therefore divinely beautiful, at one with his love, helpful, healing, restoring; yet is it verily and truly what we call anger. How different is the anger of one who loves, from that of one who hates! yet is anger anger. There is the degraded human anger, and the grand, noble, eternal anger. Our anger is in general degrading, because it is in general impure.
It is to me an especially glad thought that the Lord came so near us as to be angry with us. The more we think of Jesus being angry with us, the more we feel that we must get nearer and nearer to him﻿—get within the circle of his wrath, out of the sin that makes him angry, and near to him where sin cannot come. There is no quenching of his love in the anger of Jesus. The anger of Jesus is his recognition that we are to blame; if we were not to blame, Jesus could never be angry with us; we should not be of his kind, therefore not subject to his blame.

耶稣竟然会动怒，这会不会叫谁降低他对主的想法？若会，我要问他：他全部有意识的动怒经验，是不是只有一种怒？怒有好的怒，有坏的怒。有神的忿怒，也有那不成就神之义（righteousness）的人的忿怒。怒可以像彩虹的颜色那样多样。神的怒只能是神样的，因此是神圣地美的，与他的爱为一，有帮助、医治、复原；然而它确确实实是我们所称的怒。爱者的怒与恨者的怒何等不同！然而怒就是怒。有堕落的人怒，也有宏大、高贵、永恒的怒。我们的怒通常是堕落的，因为它通常是不洁的。

对我来说，这是一个特别可喜的想法：主来到这么近的地方，近到会向我们动怒。我们越多想到耶稣向我们动怒，就越觉得我们必须越来越靠近他——进入他怒气的圈子里，脱离那使他动怒的罪，靠近那罪不能到的地方。耶稣的怒里没有他的爱熄灭。耶稣的怒，是他承认我们当受责备；我们若不当受责备，耶稣就永不能向我们动怒；我们就不是他的同类，因此不属他责备的范围。

拉撒路必须来帮他应付这两位他无法叫她们相信的姊妹！拉撒路尝过死亡的滋味，知道它是什么：他必须来，作他的见证！「她们看不见你了，拉撒路，还以为你去了她们不信的那个乌有之乡。出来吧；从那看不见的里面出来。我们要叫她们安心。」我再说，这对拉撒路是苛刻的；他在那边更好；但他必须再来陪主一段时候，然后再被留给他的姊妹们，叫她们和千百万像她们的人可以知道：神是活人的神（the God of the living），不是死人的神。

犹太人说：「你看他爱这人是何等恳切！」但哪个基督徒能相信，耶稣哭是出于对拉撒路的爱？那是出于对神的爱、对马大和马利亚的爱。他没有失去拉撒路；马大和马利亚却是迷失了、离开了她们在天上的父。「来，我的弟兄；作见证！」他喊；拉撒路就出来了，手脚裹着布。「解开，叫他走，」他说——一个活着的真理在世上行走：他从未死过，如今出来了；他从未失落，如今复原了！他回来归自己人所经过的是一道奇异的门——一道少用的门，只有一位认识——但他就在那里！哦，马大和马利亚的心！主见她们的喜乐，他的烦难想必也得了报偿！

凡读到这里的基督徒女子，我现在请她想一想我要摆在她面前的话。

拉撒路还得再死一次；我们可以确信，他为那可喜的事实感谢过神。
