## III / 第三章

===Original===
In these days such a verdict and such an ending to a tragedy would be out of the question; but there were no police in those times to take up a case if it chanced to slip by the Coroner. Once past the Coroner, and the criminal was practically safe. The county officers were never in a hurry for such prosecutions, for a gallows cost at least £300. They wanted a public prosecutor then ten times more than we do now. Sibbold was shunned by the very men who had acquitted him; but there is no reason to suppose he ever felt remorse. He was made of that kind of stuff of which the men in armour, his ancestors, were composed, who thought little or nothing of human life. But one day he met Arthur, his eldest son, face to face upon the stairs. It was the first time they had met since the inquest﻿—Arthur had avoided the place, and wandered about a good deal by himself, till some simple folk began to think that it was he who had committed the deed, and that his conscience was troubling him.

This meeting on the stairs took place by accident one morning﻿—Sibbold was going to pass, but Arthur put his hand on his shoulder, “I saw you do it,” he said. He had just entered the rick yard when the shot was fired. He had held his peace, but his mind could not rest. “I cannot stay here,” he said, “I am going. I shall never see you again.”

Old Sibbold stood like a stone; but presently put his hand in his pocket and held out his purse.

“No,” said Arthur; “not a penny of that, it would be blood money.”

He went, and evil report went after him. Perhaps it was James who fanned the flame, but for years afterwards it was always believed that Arthur had shot the basket-maker. Only the Swamp people combated the notion. Arthur was one of them, and understood their language﻿—it was impossible. Not to have to return to these times, it will be, perhaps, best to at once finish with old Sibbold; though the event did not really happen till some time after Arthur’s departure.

===Chinese===
换作当今，这样一份裁决、这样一场悲剧的收尾，自是断无可能；但在那个年月，倘若案子侥幸从验尸官（Coroner）眼皮底下漏过，并无警察会重新立案追查。罪犯只要过了验尸官这一关，便算得上平安无虞。郡里的司法长官向来对这类公诉毫无急切之意，因为单是造一座绞刑架就得花费至少 300 英镑（£300）。彼时需要一位公诉人之迫切，比之当今更甚十倍。当初裁定西博尔德无罪的那些人，转头便将他视若瘟神避之不及；然而毫无理由以为他曾生出过半点悔意。他本就是用他那些身披铁甲的先祖同样的质料铸成的——在那些老祖宗眼里，人命根本如草芥般微不足道。然而有一天，他与长子阿瑟在楼梯上狭路相逢。那是死因听证会以来的头一遭碰面——阿瑟一直避着宅子，常常独自在外面四处游荡，以至有些头脑简单的乡民竟开始疑心凶案是他所为，是良心不安在折磨着他。

那一日清晨，二人在楼梯上的相遇纯属偶然——西博尔德正欲擦身而过，阿瑟却一把按住了他的肩膀。“我亲眼看着你动的手，”阿瑟说道。枪声响起的那一刻，他恰好踏入柴草场。他虽守口如瓶，内心却片刻不得安宁。“我不能再留在这儿了，”他说，“我要走了。我再也不会见你。”

老西博尔德僵立如石雕；但片刻后，他把手探入口袋，掏出钱包递了过去。

“不，”阿瑟说，“那里面的一文一厘我都不要，那是沾血的人命钱。”

他一走了之，流言蜚语却如影随形。或许正是詹姆斯在暗中煽风点火，但在此后许多年里，人们始终坚信是阿瑟开枪打死了那个编筐匠人。唯有泽地人奋力驳斥这等谰言。阿瑟是他们当中的一员，通晓他们的言语——绝不可能是他。为了不再折返赘述那些前尘往事，不妨在此一并将老西博尔德的结局交代清楚；尽管那桩变故实际上是在阿瑟离去好一段时日后才发生的。

===Original===
Sibbold went to a fair at some twenty miles distance﻿—a yearly custom of his; and returning home in the evening, he was met by highwaymen, it is supposed, and refusing to give up his money bag, was shot. At all events his horse came home riderless, and the body of the old man was found on the heath divested of every article of value. Suspicion at once fell on his known enemies, the Swamp people. Their cottages were searched and nothing found. Their men were interrogated, but had all been either at home or in another direction. Calm reason put down Sibbold’s death to misadventure with highwaymen, common enough in those times; but there were those who always held that it was done in revenge, as it was believed that the gypsies retained the old vendetta creed.

As Arthur did not return, James took possession, and went on as usual; but he did not disturb the Swamp settlement. He avoided them, and they avoided him.

When Will Baskette was shot he left a widow and two sons, one of them was strong and hardy, the other, about sixteen, was delicate and unfit for rough outdoor life. This fact was well-known to the clergyman at Wolf’s Glow, the Rev. Ralph Boteler, who was really a benevolently-minded man.

===Chinese===
西博尔德前往大约二十英里外的一个集市——那是他经年不改的例行旧习；据推测，在傍晚归家途中他遭遇了拦路劫匪，因执意不肯交出钱袋而遭枪击身亡。无论如何，他的坐骑独自空鞍归来，而老人的尸首被人在荒原上寻获时，身上值钱之物已被洗劫一空。嫌疑顿时落在他积怨甚深的仇家——泽地人身上。官差搜查了他们的茅舍，却一无所获；盘问了他们的男丁，但众人当时要么安居家中，要么身处他方。冷静理智之人将西博尔德之死归咎于拦路强盗的意外劫杀——在彼时那原是司空见惯之事；然而始终有人坚信此乃寻仇索命，因人们认定吉普赛人骨子里依然笃守着血亲复仇的古老信条。

鉴于阿瑟一去不返，詹姆斯便顺理成章接管了家业，照常过活；但他并未去侵扰泽地定居点。他对他们敬而远之，他们对他亦视若路人。

当年威尔·巴斯克特遭枪击毙命时，遗下一个寡妇和两个儿子；其中一个生得结实强壮，另一个年方十六岁上下，体质孱弱，不堪忍受粗重的户外风霜劳作。狼辉地（Wolf’s Glow）的本堂牧师拉尔夫·博特勒牧师（Rev. Ralph Boteler）对这一实情洞若观火，而他委实是一位心存仁慈的善人。

===Original===
The widow and her eldest son joined the gypsy tribe and abandoned the Swamp. The Rev. Ralph Boteler took the delicate Romy Baskette into his service as man of all work, meaning to help in the garden and clean the parson’s nag. Romy could not read, and the parson taught him﻿—also to write. Being quiet and good-looking, the lad won on the vicar, who after a time found himself taking a deep interest in the friendless orphan. It ended in Romy leaving the garden and the stable, and being domiciled in the studio, where the parson filled his head with learning, not forgetting Latin and Greek.

The vicar was a single man, middle-aged, with very little thought beyond his own personal comfort, except that he liked to see the hounds throw off, being too stout to follow them. He had, however, one hobby; and, like other men who are moderate enough upon other topics, he was violence itself upon this. Of all the hobbies in the world, this parson’s fancy was geology﻿—then just beginning to emerge as a real science.

The neighbours thought the vicar was as mad as a March hare on this one point. He grubbed up the earth in forty places with a small mattock he had made on purpose at the village blacksmith’s. He broke every stone in the district with a hammer which the same artisan made for him.

His craze was that the neighbourhood of Wolf’s Glow was rich in the two great stores of nature which make countries powerful﻿—i.e. in Coal and Iron. He proved it in twenty ways. First, the very taste of the water, and the colour of the earth in the streams; by the nodules of dark, heavy stone which abounded; by the oily substance often found floating on the surface of ponds﻿—rock oil; by the strata and the character of the fossils; by actual analysis of materials picked up by himself; lastly, by archaeology.

===Chinese===
遗孀与其长子归入了吉普赛部族，弃离了那片泽地。拉尔夫·博特勒牧师收留了体弱的罗米·巴斯克特（Romy Baskette）当杂工，原本只是打算让他打理菜园、照料刷洗牧师的小马。罗米目不识丁，牧师便悉心教他识字认句——还教他习字书写。少年生性沉静，相貌堂堂，渐渐深得牧师欢心，不久牧师便发觉自己对这个无依无靠的孤儿倾注了莫大的心血。到头来，罗米彻底告别了庭院和马厩，住进了牧师的书斋画室，牧师将满腹学识悉数灌输进他的脑海，更未漏下拉丁语与希腊语。

这位本堂牧师是个独身汉，人届中年，除了因身躯过于富态骑不得烈马，只爱在一旁瞧瞧猎犬开跑纵跃之外，平日里除却自身的安逸享乐几乎不操心旁事。然而，他却有一项癖好；与那些在其他事体上平和中庸、独独在某件事上狂热入骨的人一样，他对此嗜好执迷得近乎发狂。世间万千癖好之中，这位牧师痴迷的偏偏是地质学——彼时这门学问才刚开始作为一门真正的科学崭露头角。

邻里乡亲皆以为牧师在这一节上疯癫得宛如三月的野兔。他特意让村里的铁匠打制了一把小鹤嘴锄，拿着它在足足四十处地方掘土刨地。他又用同一位铁匠为他打造的铁锤，将方圆地界的每一块石头都砸了个遍。

他的痴念在于：认定狼辉地一带富含大自然赐予的两大造化宝藏——即令国势强盛的煤与铁。他以二十种方式予以佐证：首先，从水质的滋味与溪流泥土的色泽探知；其次，凭借此地俯拾皆是的暗黑沉重结核石；再次，依凭池塘水面上屡屡浮泛的油质物——即岩石沥青油；复次，根据地层构造与化石的性状特征；还有他亲自采集材料进行的切实化验分析；最后，则是借助于考古之据。

===Original===
Wolf’s Glow! What was the meaning of that singular name? The only Glow in the county. Wolf was, perhaps, a man’s name in the centuries since. But Glow? Glow was, without a doubt, the ancient British for *coal*.

The people who argued against him﻿—and they were all he met﻿—ridiculed the idea of the ancient Britons knowing anything of coal. Boteler produced his authorities to show that they, and their conquerors the Romans, were perfectly familiar with that mineral. Wolf’s Glow was, in fact, Wolf’s Coal Pit. “Very well,” said the Objectors, “show us the coal pit, and we’ll believe.” This the vicar could not do, and was held to be mad accordingly.

But all this talking, and searching, and analysing made a deep impression upon the mind of young Romy Baskette, who was now hard upon twenty years of age. Boteler, really desirous of pushing the lad on, sent him to London, whither Arthur Sibbold had preceded him, and placed him, at a high premium, in the care of a friend of his, who was in the iron trade. Romy grew and prospered, and being of a serious disposition saved all the money he could lay hands on. Presently old Boteler died, and left him, not all, but a great share of his worldly wealth. With this he bought a share in the iron business, and became a partner. Wealth rolled in upon him, and at an early period of life he retired from active labour, married, and bought an estate a few miles from Wolf’s Glow.

In his leisure hours the memory of the old days with the vicar returned. He resolved to test the vicar’s theory. He purchased a small piece of land in Wolf’s Glow parish, sank a shaft, and sure enough came upon *coal*.

===Chinese===
狼辉地（Wolf’s Glow）！这古怪地名究竟寓意何在？全郡独此一家唤作“辉地”（Glow）。“狼”（Wolf）一字，或许是数百年前某位先人的姓名。然而“辉”（Glow）又作何解？那“Glow”毫无疑问正是古不列颠语里指称“煤”（*coal*）的字眼！

那些同他争辩的人——凡他撞见者无一例外——皆嗤笑古不列颠人哪懂什么煤炭。博特勒搬出权威典籍以资佐证，力陈古不列颠人及其征服者罗马人对这等矿物早已了如指掌。所谓“狼辉地”，追根溯源实则是“沃尔夫的煤坑”（Wolf’s Coal Pit）。“那好哇，”反对者们讥讽道，“且指给我们瞧瞧煤坑在哪儿，我们便信你。”这却是牧师无能为力的，众人因而越发认定他是个不折不扣的疯子。

然而，这一切滔滔雄辩、遍地勘测与精细化验，却在年轻的罗米·巴斯克特心头留下了不可磨灭的深印，彼时他已快满二十岁了。博特勒真心巴望提携这个青年立身成才，便将他送往伦敦——阿瑟·西博尔德先他一步去了那里；牧师还支付了一大笔丰厚的拜师学徒费，将他托付给自己一位经营铁器生意的故友照应。罗米日渐精明能干、事业有成，因生性持重内敛，凡过手的银钱他都竭力积攒留存。不久老博特勒与世长辞，将自己的现世财产分出大半遗赠给他，虽非全数，亦颇为可观。罗米凭此在铁器商行购入股权，晋升为合伙人。财富如滚雪球般纷至沓来，在人生尚属早年之际，他便退隐脱身、不再躬亲劳作，成了家，并在距狼辉地数英里之外购置了一座庄园田产。

闲暇岁月里，昔日与牧师朝夕相处的时光历历重现。他决心亲手检验牧师当年的学说。他在狼辉地教区买下一小块地皮，开凿了一口竖井，果不其然，赫然挖出了*煤炭*。

===Original===
This discovery revivified the whole man. He cast off sloth, forgot all about retirement, and plunged into business again. Another search, conducted by practical hands, proved the existence of iron.

There was a furore. Collieries were started; iron furnaces set going. It was just at the dawn of the great iron and coal trade. The railways had been started, and the demand was greater than the supply. Romy Baskette and Company soon employed two thousand hands coal-digging and iron-smelting. The man, in fact, wore himself out at the trade of moneymaking. He could not rest. Night and day his brain was at work: An accidental conversation with one of his workmen suggested to him a new idea. The smiths of the time could not make nails fast enough for all the building that was going on. This workman had been a sailor in his day, and had seen nails abroad which were made in batches by machinery, instead of slowly and laboriously, one by one, by hand.

Baskette caught at the idea. He studied and learnt what he could. He made a voyage himself abroad, and soon mastered the secret. He erected machinery, and *cut* nails were first made. The consumption was enormous. The business of this Baskette and Company became so large that it almost passed out of control. Meantime other firms had come and settled, bought land, dug up coal, and set up smelting furnaces. In ten years the population from being absolutely nil rose to thirty-five thousand people. By this time Romy had killed himself. But that mattered little, for he had left a son, and a son who inherited all his genius, and was﻿—if anything still “harder in the mouth.” He was named, from his mother’s family, Sternhold Baskette.

===Chinese===
这一发现令他整个人脱胎换骨、精神大振。他摒弃了闲散怠惰，将悠然隐退之事抛诸脑后，重新全力杀入商海。由行家里手主持的另一轮勘探，又证实了铁矿的存在。

一时间轰动全境，举世沸腾。煤矿纷纷破土开采，炼铁熔炉昼夜不熄。正值铁与煤大工业狂飙初起之晨曦。铁道初兴，天下对煤铁之需求远超供给。罗米·巴斯克特公司（Romy Baskette and Company）麾下不多时便雇佣了两千名工手从事掘煤与炼铁。实则，这个人在逐利聚财的苦役中生生耗尽了自身心血。他无法停歇。夜以继日，他的脑子都在飞速运转：与手下一名工匠的偶然交谈，又为他撞出一道崭新的灵感。彼时的铁匠即便日夜赶工，打制铁钉的速度也远远赶不上四方浩大营建工地的急需。这名工匠早年曾出海当过水手，曾在海外见识过借助机械成批冲压切割制成的铁钉，无须再靠手工慢条斯理、费力吃劲地一颗颗锻打。

巴斯克特如获至宝紧抓不放。他穷尽一切渠道钻研探求。他甚至亲自扬帆渡海远赴异邦，很快便勘破了其中的关窍秘诀。他运回并架设起机器设备，开创了有史以来最早的*机制切削铁钉*（*cut* nails）。市场消耗吞吐之巨令人咋舌。这间巴斯克特公司的产业版图扩张得如此庞大，乃至几乎到了难以驾驭的境地。在此期间，其他商号也接踵而至在此扎根，置买土地，采掘煤炭，筑炉冶炼。短短十年间，当地人口便从原本的荒芜死寂、空无一人，暴增至三万五千人。至此，罗米已然将自己活活累死。然而这并无大碍，因他膝下留有一子；这个儿子不仅承袭了他全部的绝世天赋，而且若论脾性心志，甚至还要更加“铁齿铜牙、执拗刚愎”。他随母系家族之姓氏，定名为斯特恩霍尔德·巴斯克特（Sternhold Baskette）。

Sternhold picked up the plough-handle which had dropped from his father’s grasp, and continued the good work, never once looking back. But although equally clever, the bent of his genius was different from that of old Romy. Romy was at heart a speculator, and believed in personal property. Sternhold was a Conservative, and put his faith in real property, houses, and land. He kept up the old forges and collieries, but he started no new ones. He invested the money in land and houses, particularly the latter. His life may be summed up in two strokes of genius﻿—the first was bringing the iron horse to Stirmingham, as the new town was called; the second was the building lease investment.

It is hard to give the preeminence to either. They were both profound schemes﻿—neither would have been complete without the other. He did not originate the idea of the railway﻿—that was done for him﻿—but he put it on its legs, and he brought it to the centre of the town.

The original scheme almost omitted Stirmingham. Railways were not then fully understood; their projectors had such vast ideas in their heads, they aimed at long trunk lines, and so this railroad was to connect London, the sea, and a certain large town﻿—larger than Stirmingham then, but now nothing beside the modern city.

Sternhold, as the largest shareholder, and as finding the capital to get through Parliament, prevailed to have the course altered so as to sweep by Stirmingham. He knew that this would improve his property there at least fifty percent. But he had other ideas in his head. The line could not be finished under three years, and in those three years it was his intention to become possessed of the whole ground upon which the town of Stirmingham stood. He foresaw that it would become a mighty centre. He braced up his nerves, and prepared to spend his darling hoards like water.

斯特恩霍尔德接过了从亡父手中滑落的犁柄，继续奋力耕耘这番宏图伟业，矢志不渝，绝不回顾。然而，尽管父子二人同样精明过人，其才智禀赋的向背却大相径庭。老罗米骨子里是个投机客，笃信动产与流动资金；斯特恩霍尔德则是个保守派，将全部信条寄托于不动产——房屋与土地之上。他维持着旧有的锻铁炉与煤矿的运转，却不再另辟新灶。他将赚得的真金白银倾注于土地与房产投资，尤以购置房产为最。他的一生功业可归纳为两手神来之笔：其一，是将呼啸的“铁马”（iron horse）引向了这座初具雏形、被称为斯脱明翰（Stirmingham）的新兴市镇；其二，则是开展建筑土地租赁投资（building lease investment）。
