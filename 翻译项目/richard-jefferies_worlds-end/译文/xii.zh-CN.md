## XII / 第十二章

===Original===
There was once a very wise man who invented what was considered a saying almost inspired, which was afterwards inculcated as a most important lesson by mighty princes upon their sons, and continues to be constantly quoted in our day with approval﻿—it is, that “Unity is strength.” But the strength of the claimants to the Baskette estate consisted in the number of their scattered forces.

All that Marese, the heir, could have desired was that by some means they could be condensed into one person, and thus destroyed at a blow. They had increased as the years went by in a geometrical ratio, till the total formed a small battalion.

When the idea of making claims upon the estate first arose in America, there were already quite fifty families who in one way or another thought they had “rights.” About a dozen visited Stirmingham, and all but drove poor old Sternhold frantic. That was a generation ago, and the tribe had nearly trebled. Mothers of children who had the remotest possible chance of a share in the prize took care to have them christened Baskette or Sternhold. There were Sternhold Baskette Browns, Baskette Johnsons, Baskette Stirmingham Slicks, English Baskette Williamsons, and every possible combination of Baskette.

Those who, either by the male or female side, really could show some species of proof that they were descended from the seventeen squatters who were transhipped to the States now numbered no less than one hundred and forty-three individuals﻿—men, women, and children. To distinguish themselves from other claimants, they called themselves “The True Swampers.”

But in addition to these, there was a host of other Baskettes, who in one way or another foisted in their names. There were Baskets, Bascots, Buscots, Biscuits, Buschcotts, Bosquettes﻿—every conceivable variation of spelling from every State and territory, who declared that they were related to the parent stem of Will Baskette, the squatter, who was shot by old Sibbold. These might be called for distinction the pseudo-Baskettes.

从前有位绝顶聪明的大智者，曾道出过一句被奉为神启般的警世格言；此后，列国赫赫显赫的诸侯君王无不以此奉为至理名言教诲其子嗣，时至今日，这句箴言在世间依然屡屡被人们赞许称颂并奉为圭臬——那便是：“团结即力量”（Unity is strength）。然而，那些向巴斯克特庄园（Baskette estate）产业提出索赔之人，其力量之所在，却恰恰在于他们那散落各方、散漫繁多的庞杂人马。

身为合法继承人的马雷斯（Marese），唯一朝思暮想的奢望，便是能通过某种手段将这伙人凝缩成区区一人，好教他一击即中、一举将其彻底歼灭。然而随着岁月流转，这帮人竟以几何级数（geometrical ratio）疯狂繁衍暴增，直至总人数集结成了一支不折不扣的小型营队（small battalion）。

当向这片庞大产业声索索赔的念头最初在美洲（America）萌生发端之时，便已然有足足五十个（50个）家族各执一词，自以为握有某种“合法权益”。彼时有十数人（约十二三人）远渡重洋造访了斯脱明翰（Stirmingham），险些将可怜的老斯特恩霍尔德（Sternhold）逼得彻底发疯。那已是一代人之前（约三十年前）的旧事了，而如今这一支庞大宗族的人丁已然几乎翻了整整两倍（增殖至近三倍）。只要膝下子女哪怕有极其渺茫的一丝机会分润这块诱人肥肉，当母亲的便务必处心积虑、煞费苦心地教孩子在受洗时取名为“巴斯克特”或“斯特恩霍尔德”。于是乎，坊间冒出了斯特恩霍尔德·巴斯克特·布朗（Sternhold Baskette Brown）、巴斯克特·约翰逊（Baskette Johnson）、巴斯克特·斯脱明翰·斯里克（Baskette Stirmingham Slick）、英格兰·巴斯克特·威廉森（English Baskette Williamson），以及凡是能想得到的与“巴斯克特”的各种五花八门的拼凑组合。

至于那些无论通过父系还是母系血统，确实能够拿出某种证据证明自己系当年被海船转运发配至美洲合众国（the States）的那十七名（17名）原初棚户流民后裔之人，如今男男女女连同老幼孩童在内，已然不下整整一百四十三人（143人）。为了同其余冒领索赔之徒划清界限，他们自封为“真泽地派”（The True Swampers）。

然而除却这批人之外，另有一大群形形色色的“巴斯克特”，想方设法、百般钻营着将自己的姓氏硬生生塞了进来。其中有叫巴斯克茨（Baskets）、巴斯科茨（Bascots）、布斯科茨（Buscots）、比斯基茨（Biscuits，甚至拼同“饼干”）、布施科茨（Buschcotts）、博斯克茨（Bosquettes）——来自各个州与准州的每一种凡能想象得出的拼写变体应有尽有，全都言之凿凿宣称自己与当年惨遭老西博尔德开枪射杀的流民老祖威尔·巴斯克特（Will Baskette）的主干血脉同气连枝。为了便于区分，大可将这一干人等统称为“伪巴斯克特派”（pseudo-Baskettes）。

Then among the True Swampers there was an inner circle, who professed to have prominent “rights” on account of their progenitors having been more nearly related to the original Will Baskette. They argued that the others were not true Baskettes, and had only adopted that name from the chief, while they were real blood Baskettes.

In addition, there was another host of people who made a virtue of proclaiming that they were not named Baskette. They did not profess to be named Baskette﻿—they did not take a name which was not theirs! They were Washingtons, Curries, Bolters, Gregorys, Jamesons, and so on. But they had claims because their father’s wives were of the Baskette blood.

Finally, there was another subdivision who loudly maintained that half of the original cotters who landed in New York were not Baskettes, but Gibbs, Webbes, Colborns, and so on, and that they were the descendants of these people. And there were some who went the length of declaring that they were descended from two alleged illegitimate sons of old Romy Baskette!

The Baskette Battalion was therefore made up of﻿—1st. The Pure Blood Baskettes; 2nd. The True Swampers; 3rd. Demi-Baskettes, who had that name added to another; 4th. Nominal Baskettes, whose names had an accidental resemblance; 5th. The Feminine Baskettes, descended from women of Baskette strain; 6th. Independent Squatters, not Baskettes, but companions; 7th. Illegitimate Baskettes!

Then there were the Sibbolds﻿—such a catalogue! These had been slower to wake up to their “rights” than the Baskettes, but when they did discover them they came in crowds. First, there were the descendants, in a straight line, of the eight sons of James Sibbold, shipped (six with families) to New York. They had multiplied exceedingly, and there was no end to them. The simply Sibbolds, as we may call them, numbered no less than two hundred and eighteen, all told﻿—men, women, and children. Every one of these had some register, some old book﻿—many of these books were worm-eaten copies of Tom Paine’s *Rights of Man*﻿—some piece of paper or other to prove that they had the blood of James Sibbold in their veins.

Then there were all the ramifications, pretty much like the Baskette branches; innumerable cadets distantly related, innumerable people whose wife’s uncle’s mother or cousin’s name was Sibbold; and all the various Sibbolde, Sibboldes, Sibald, Sigbeld, Sybels, Sibils, Sibelus, Sibilsons, *ad libitum*. Illegitimate Sibbolds were as plentiful as blackberries, and all ready to argue the merits of the case with revolver and bowie. If the Baskettes made up a battalion, the Sibbolds formed an army!

在“真泽地派”内部，尚且存在着一个核心圈子（inner circle），他们坚称因自己的直系先祖同原初的威尔·巴斯克特血缘更为亲近，因而享有高人一等的首要“权利”。他们极力辩称，其余人等根本不是真正的巴斯克特，只不过是当年因袭了首领的名号而已，唯独他们自己才是真正流淌着巴斯克特骨血的嫡系。

除此之外，另有浩浩荡荡的一伙人，反倒以高调宣称自己并不姓巴斯克特为美德。他们既不自诩姓巴斯克特——也绝不冒领不属于自己的姓号！他们姓华盛顿（Washington）、柯里（Currie）、博尔特（Bolter）、格雷戈里（Gregory）、詹姆森（Jameson）等等，不一而足。然而他们之所以提出索赔权利，盖因他们父亲的元配或继室流着巴斯克特的血统。

最后，尚有一个分支派别大声疾呼地坚称，当年在纽约（New York）登岸的那批原初棚农流民中，足足有一半（1/2）根本不姓巴斯克特，而是姓吉布斯（Gibbs）、韦布（Webbes）、科尔伯恩（Colborns）等等，而他们正是这批人的血脉传人。更有甚者，竟公然声称自己是老罗米·巴斯克特（Romy Baskette）传闻中两个（2个）私生子的后裔！

于是乎，这支巴斯克特大营（Baskette Battalion）便由如下各色人马拼凑而成：——第一（1st.），纯血巴斯克特派（The Pure Blood Baskettes）；第二（2nd.），真泽地派（The True Swampers）；第三（3rd.），半巴斯克特派（Demi-Baskettes），即在原有姓氏上缀加巴斯克特名号者；第四（4th.），名义巴斯克特派（Nominal Baskettes），即姓氏拼写偶有相似者；第五（5th.），母系巴斯克特派（The Feminine Baskettes），即出自巴斯克特血统女性门下者；第六（6th.），独立棚户派（Independent Squatters），虽非巴斯克特族人，却是当年同甘共苦的流民同伴；第七（7th.），私生巴斯克特派（Illegitimate Baskettes）！

紧接着便是西博尔德家族（Sibbolds）——那才叫一本洋洋大观的庞杂名册！这帮人虽说比巴斯克特族人较迟才觉察出自身的“权利”，可一旦他们如梦初醒，便如潮水般蜂拥而至。首先，是詹姆斯·西博尔德当年被押送至纽约的八个儿子（8子，其中6人携家带眷）的直系后裔。他们生息繁衍得极为旺盛，人数漫无止境。单单这些纯粹西博尔德人（simply Sibbolds），若我们可这般称呼的话，男女老幼总计便不下整整二百一十八人（218人）。他们人人手中都揣着某种登记簿、某种发黄古籍——其中许多是虫蛀残破的托马斯·潘恩（Tom Paine）所著《人的权利》（*Rights of Man*）——或是随身携带某张破纸残页，以此证明自己体内奔淌着詹姆斯·西博尔德的血脉。

随后便是盘根错节的旁系分支，同巴斯克特一族的门系如出一辙；数不胜数的远亲支系、数不清的那些舅母的姑妈或表亲姓西博尔德的人；以及五花八门的西博尔德拼写变体：Sibbolde、Sibboldes、Sibald、Sigbeld、Sybels、Sibils、Sibelus、Sibilsons等等，随心所欲、漫无节制（ad libitum）。私生西博尔德人更是多如过江之鲫、俯拾皆是（如黑莓般多），且个个随时准备拔出左轮手枪（revolver）和鲍伊猎刀（bowie knife）来辩争这桩产权的是非曲直。倘若说巴斯克特族人凑成了一个营队（battalion），那么西博尔德族人简直组建起了一整支大军（army）！

Between these two great divisions there was the bitterest enmity. The Baskettes derided the claims of the Sibbolds; the Sibbolds derided those of the Baskettes. The Sibbolds told the Baskettes that they were an ill-conditioned lot; if they had been respectable people, and really his relations, old Sternhold would never have shipped them to America out of his sight. The Baskettes retorted that the Sibbolds were ashamed to stay in England, for they were the sons of a murderer; they were the descendants of a dastardly coward, who shot a man through a window. The Sibbolds snarled, and pointed out that the great chief of the Baskettes was nothing but a thief, caught in the act and deservedly punished; a lot of semi-gypsies, rogues, and vagabonds. Their very name showed that they were but basket-makers; they were not even pure gypsy blood﻿—miserable squatters on another man’s property.

Blows were not unfrequently exchanged in the saloons and drinking-stores over these quarrels. The result was the formation of two distinct societies, each determined to prosecute its own claim and to oust the other at all hazards. The Baskette battalion relied upon the admitted nonpayment of rent by their forefathers to upset all subsequent agreements, and they agreed also that this agreement which their forefathers had signed was not binding on the remote descendants. The document was obtained by trickery, and the land was not put to the use the vendors had understood it was to be put, as the representatives now alleged, to simple agricultural purposes. Further, each of those who signed the document only gave up his cottage and the small plot of garden round it; they did not sell the waste land between the islands.

The Sibbolds principal argument was that their forefathers could not sign away an entailed estate without previously cutting off the entail, and it was acknowledged that this had not been done. But, said the Baskettes, there was a question if the land ever was entailed; let the Sibbolds produce the deed, and if it was not entailed, where was their claim?

Each of these divisions formed itself into a society, with a regular committee and place of meeting, a minute-book to record accumulated evidence, legal gentlemen to advise, corresponding secretaries, and Heaven knows what. They actually issued gazettes﻿—printed sheets of intelligence. There was the *Baskette Gazette* and the *Sibbold Gazette*, which papers carefully recorded all deaths, marriages, and new claims. There was a complete organisation, and a﻿—fine thing it was for the lawyers and some few sharp young men.

Of late these societies had received more or less cordial overtures from the eight building societies at Stirmingham who held the leases. The first four societies encouraged the Baskette battalion, the second held out hopes to the Sibbolds. The cunning building societies, without committing themselves, desired nothing better than protracted litigation between these claimants and the heir, in the certainty that meantime they should reap the benefit.

因了这些无休无止的争执骂阵，在各处酒馆与饮酒铺舍内大打出手、拳脚相向乃是家常便饭。这般水火不容的仇视，最终导致了两个截然对立的社团组织的建立，双方皆横下一条心要全力推进自身索赔主张，并不惜一切代价将对方排挤出局。巴斯克特大营倚仗着其先祖向来拒缴地租这一公认事实，企图推翻其后达成的一切协议，同时他们一致认定，当年先祖签署的那份文契对远代子孙根本不具约束力。况且那份文书纯属欺诈诱骗所得，土地亦未曾按照出让人当初所理解的用途进行开发——正如其如今的法权代表所声称的那样，当初说定仅用于单纯的农业用途。更有甚者，当年在文书上画押的每一个人，仅仅是出让了自己的窝棚茅舍及其周围那一小块菜园苗圃；他们决未出让各座沙洲小岛之间的那大片荒滩荒地。

西博尔德一派的核心抗辩则是：其先祖在预先解除限定继承（cutting off the entail）之前，根本无权通过签字放弃一项限定继承产业（entailed estate），而众所周知，当年此节程序从未履行。然而巴斯克特人反驳道，这片土地究竟是否曾被确立为限定继承产业尚存疑问；且教西博尔德人出示那份地契契据来，倘若原本就未曾限定继承，他们的索赔主张又立足于何处？

这两大阵营各自分化组成了一个规制齐备的社团协会，设有正规委员会与固定会所，置备了专门记录累积呈堂证供的会议纪要簿（minute-book），聘请了指点迷津的律师绅士，配备了通联文书秘书，以及天晓得还有多少五花八门的头衔差使。他们甚至俨然印行起了公报——刊印各种情报讯息的报纸散页。坊间应运而生了《巴斯克特公报》（*Baskette Gazette*）与《西博尔德公报》（*Sibbold Gazette*），这两份报纸详尽无遗地刊载着所有的讣告、婚讯以及全新涌现的索赔主张。整套组织架构可谓完备严密，而这——对于律师们以及少数几个精明透顶的年轻小伙来说，不啻为一桩大发横财的美事。

近来，这两个协会均从斯脱明翰掌握着营建租约的八家营造协会（eight building societies，8家营造协会）那里，收到了或多或少显得殷勤备至的接触示好。前四家营造协会暗中给巴斯克特大营鼓劲撑腰，后四家（第二组）则向西博尔德一派抛出希望的橄榄枝。这帮老奸巨猾的营造协会绝不轻易给自身招揽半点法律干系，它们求之不得的，莫过于看到这些索赔之徒与合法继承人之间陷入旷日持久的泥潭诉讼，因它们深信在此期间自身必能渔翁得利、坐收渔利。

Among the American corps of claimants there were men of all classes﻿—from common labourers, saloon-keepers, etc., up to judges, editors, financiers, merchants; and many of them were clever, farseeing persons, who, without putting any weight upon the somewhat strained “rights” they professed to believe in, still thought that there was “something in it, you know,” and money might be got by persistent agitation, if it was only hush-money.

Throughout many turbulent States there was at one time quite a feeling aroused against England (which added its venom to the unfortunate Alabama business), as having unjustly kept what was due to American citizens. These societies had their regular agents in Stirmingham and London, whose duty it was to report every change that took place, every variation of the case, and to accumulate evidence and transmit it. These bulletins were received by the “caucuses,” and sometimes printed in the *Gazettes*.

Besides these regular organisations, who had money at disposal and were really formidable, there were several free lances careering over the country, representing themselves as the sons of the elder brother of Romy Baskette, the brother who had disappeared with the gypsies. These were downright impostors, and yet got a living out of the case. Several lecturers also promenaded the States, who made a good thing of it by giving a popular version of the story, illustrated by a diorama of incidents in the lives of the principal actors, from the shooting of Will Baskette to the appearance of Lucia Marese as Lady Godiva. It was singular that no one presented himself as a descendant of Arthur Sibbold; he seemed to have been quite forgotten. So much for America.

From Australia there came, time after time, the most startling reports, as is usual when any cause célèbre is proceeding in the Old World. Now, it was a miner at the diggings who had made extraordinary disclosures; now, some shepherd on a sheep-run, after a fit of illness, found his memory returned, and recollected where important deeds were deposited.

Nothing, however, came of it. The principal seats of disturbance were America and England; for England produced a crop of what we may call Provisional, or Partial Claimants. Here and there, scattered all over the country﻿—from Kent to Cornwall, from Hampshire to Northumberland﻿—were people of the name of Baskette, which is a very ancient English cognomen, and to be found in every collection of surnames.
