## IV / 第四章

===Original===
The owner of three parts of Stirmingham﻿—now a monstrous overgrown city, just building a cathedral﻿—actually had nothing but a little bread and cheese for supper. There were people who condoled with him, and offered to lend him sums of money﻿—not large, but very useful to a starving man, one would have thought. He shrugged his shoulders and said, “Thank you; I’ll wait.”

Certain keen speculators tried to come round him in twenty different ways. They represented that all this mass of bricks and mortar﻿—this unfinished Belgravia﻿—really was not worth owning; no one could ever find the coin to finish the plans, and house property had depreciated ninety percent.

“Very true,” said Sternhold. “Good morning, gentlemen.”

He held on like grim death. Men of genius always do﻿—mark Caesar, and all of them. ’Tis the bulldog that wins.

===Chinese===
斯脱明翰（Stirmingham）绝大部分产业的主人——此时该市已是一座庞大臃肿、野蛮扩张的巨邑，眼下正筹建一座主教座堂——晚饭居然除了一点干面包和奶酪之外别无他物。有些人对他表示同情慰问，并主动提出借给他几笔钱财——数额虽不大，但人们本以为这对于一个忍饥挨饿的人来说，自是大有裨益。他耸了耸肩，只道：“多谢，我等得起。”

某些精明的投机客使尽二十种（20种）百般花样，企图将他笼络劝服。他们向他力陈，眼前这一大片砖瓦灰浆——这片半途而废的贝尔格拉维亚（Belgravia）——着实毫无保有之价值；绝不可能有人筹措得出足以竣工这些规划的真金白银，况且房产早已贬值了百分之九十（90%）。

“所言极是，”斯特恩霍尔德说，“诸位先生，早安，请便罢。”

他像死神索命般死死硬撑到底。天才之辈莫不如此——瞧瞧凯撒（Caesar），以及古往今来的所有英豪便知。唯有死咬不放的牛头犬才能最终制胜。

===Original===
By-and-by things began to take a turn. The markets looked up. Iron and coal got brisker. The first locomotive was put on the line, then another, and another; London could be reached in two hours, goods could be transmitted in six, instead of thirty by the old canal or turnpike.

The Stock Exchange got busy again. You could hear the masons and bricklayers﻿—chink, tink, tinkle, as their trowels chipped off the edges﻿—singing away in chorus. The whistle of the engines was never silent. Vast clouds of smoke hung over the country from the factories and furnaces. Two or three new trades were introduced﻿—among others, the placed goods, cheap jewellery, and idol-making businesses, and trade-guns for Africa. Rents began to rise; in two years they went up forty percent. The place got a name throughout the length and breadth of the kingdom, and a Name is everything to a town as well as to an individual.

But by a curious contradiction, just as his property began to rise in value, and his investment looked promising, Sternhold grew melancholy and walked about more wretched than ever. The truth soon leaked out﻿—he had no money to complete his half-finished streets and blocks of houses. Nothing could induce him to borrow; not a halfpenny would he take from any man.

There the streets and houses, the theatres, clubhouses, magnificent mansions, huge hotels, languished, half-finished, some a story, some two stories high, exposed to wind and weather. In the midst of a great city there was all this desolation, as if an enemy had wreaked his vengeance on this quarter only.

===Chinese===
渐渐地，时局开始迎来了转机。行市行情日渐抬头。生铁与煤炭交易重新活跃起来。第一台蒸汽机车驶上了铁轨，随后是第二台、第三台；如今前往伦敦仅需两个小时（2小时），货物送达只需六个小时（6小时），而在以往循着老运河或收费公路，则需要整整三十个小时（30小时）。

证券交易所再度喧嚣繁忙起来。你可以听见石匠和瓦工们——瓦刀敲削石棱发出铿锵丁当的清脆声响——齐声放开嗓子引吭高歌。火车机车的汽笛声终日不绝于耳。工厂与熔炉喷吐出的滚滚浓烟笼罩在乡野上空。两三门新兴行当被引入城中——其中便有镀层器皿、廉价首饰、神像制造，以及销往非洲的贸易火枪。地租与房租开始飙升；短短两年（2年）间就上涨了百分之四十（40%）。这座城邑的大名传遍了王国的每一个角落，而名声对于一座城镇、正如对于一个凡人一样，便是立身的一切。

然而出于一种奇特的矛盾，正当其地产价值水涨船高、投资前景一片大好之际，斯特恩霍尔德却愈发郁郁寡欢，形销骨立地四处踯躅，比以往任何时候都更显得落魄凄惶。实情很快便泄露出来——他根本没有现钱来竣工那些修筑了一半的街道与连排楼宇。没有任何缘由能劝诱他去借贷分文；无论何人的哪怕半个便士，他也断不肯借取。

街道与房舍、剧院、会所、宏伟府邸、巍峨饭店，全都半途而废地荒弃在那里，有的仅有一层，有的高两层，任由凄风苦雨摧残剥蚀。在这座巍峨巨邑的腹心，竟弥漫着这般满目荒凉，宛如某个死敌单单挑选了这一街区肆意倾泄报复之怒火。

===Original===
Large as were the sums derived from his other properties﻿—houses and shops and land, which were occupied﻿—it was all eaten up in the attempt to finish this *marble* Rome in the middle of a brick Babylon. Heavy amounts too had to be disbursed to keep the railway going, for it did not pay a fraction of dividend yet. Men of business pressed on Sternhold. “Let us complete the place,” they said. “Sell it to us on building leases; no one man can do the whole. Then we will form three or four companies or syndicates, lease it of you, complete the buildings, and after seventy years the whole will revert to you or your heirs.”

Still Sternhold hesitated. At last he did lease a street or two in this way to a company, who went to work like mad, paid the masons and bricklayers double wages, kept them at it day and night, and speedily were paying twenty percent, dividends on their shares out of the rents of the completed buildings. This caused a rush. Company after company was formed. They gave Sternhold heavy premiums for the privilege to buy of him; even then it was difficult to get him to grant the leases. When he did accept the terms and the ready cash, every halfpenny of it went to complete streets on his own account; and so he lived, as it were, from hand to mouth.

After all this excitement and rush, after some thousands of workmen were put at it, they did not seem to make much impression upon the huge desolation of brick and mortar. Streets and squares rose up, and still there were acres upon acres of wilderness, foundations half-dug out and full of dirty water, walls three feet high, cellars extending heaven only knew where.

===Chinese===
尽管他从其他已入驻租用的产业——房产、商铺与土地——中获取的收益数额颇丰，但全都被投入到在这座砖造巴比伦正中修筑一座*大理石*罗马的企图中，吞噬得干干净净。为了维持铁路运转，他还必须支付巨额开销，因为铁路迄今尚未派发分文红利。精明商人纷纷向斯特恩霍尔德进言施压：“交由我们来竣工这片地方罢，”他们说，“以营造租约（building leases）的形式将其租售给我们；单凭一己之力，绝不可能独揽全局。届时我们将组建三四家公司或辛迪加联合体，向您承租土地，完成全部建筑营造，待七十年（70年）租期届满，整座产业便将悉数归还给您或您的后嗣。”

斯特恩霍尔德依旧迟疑不决。最终，他确乎以此种方式将一两条街租给了一家公司，该公司如痴如狂地投入营建，给石匠与瓦工开出双倍工钱，驱使他们日夜连轴赶工，不久便从已竣工建筑的地租收入中，为其股份派发高达百分之二十（20%）的股息分红。这引发了一阵疯狂的抢购狂潮。一家又一家公司如雨后春笋般接连成立。他们为了争夺向他承租购买的特权，向斯特恩霍尔德奉上巨额权利金（premiums）；然而即便是这样，想要说动他批出批租契约仍是千难万难。而每当他应允了条件、收下了现银，每一文半便士又全都被他投入到由自己名下继续营造街道的无底洞里；如此一来，他本人的生活可以说依旧过得朝不保夕、捉襟见肘。

历经这番狂热轰烈与喧嚣竞逐，在数以千计的工匠劳力投入营建之后，对于那片由砖石灰浆构筑的浩瀚荒芜废墟，似乎并未造成多么显著的改观。一条条街道与广场拔地而起，然而四周依然横亘着连绵数英亩的荒蛮旷野，地基只挖了一半便积满了肮脏污浊的污水，残垣断壁不过三英尺（3英尺）高，纵横交错的地窖深不可测，唯有老天爷才晓得通向何方。

===Original===
People came for miles to see it, and called it “Baskette’s Folly.” After a while, however, they carefully avoided it, and called it something worse﻿—i.e. “The Rookery;” for all the scum and ruffianism of an exceptionally scummy and ruffianly residuum chose it as their stronghold. Thieves and worse﻿—ill-conditioned women﻿—crowds of lads, gypsies, pedlars﻿—the catalogue would be as long as Homer’s﻿—took up their residence in these foundations and cellars. They seized on the planks which were lying about in enormous piles, and roofed over the low walls; and where planks would not do they got canvas.

Now, it is well-known that this class of people do not do much harm when they are scattered about and separated here, there, and everywhere over a city; but as soon as they are concentrated in one spot, then it becomes serious. Gangs are formed, they increase in boldness; the police are defied, and not a house is safe.

This place became a crying evil. The papers raved about it, the police (there were police now) complained and reported it to headquarters. There was a universal clamour. By this time Stirmingham had got a corporation, aldermen, and mayor, who met in a gorgeous Guildhall, and were all sharp men of business. Now the corporation began to move in this matter of “Baskette’s Folly.” Outside people gave them the credit of being good citizens, animated by patriotic motives, anxious for the honour of their town, and desirous of repressing crime. Keen thinkers knew better﻿—the Corporation was not above a good stroke of business. However, what they did was this: After a great deal of talk and palaver, and passing resolutions, and consulting attorneys, and goodness knows what, one morning a deputation waited upon Sternhold Baskette, Esq., at his hotel (he always lived at an hotel), and laid before him a handsome proposition. It was to the effect that he should lease them the said “Folly,” or incomplete embryo city, for a term of years, in consideration whereof they would pay down a certain sum, and contract to erect buildings according to plans and specifications agreed upon, the whole to revert in seventy years to Sternhold or his heirs.

===Chinese===
人们从数英里外纷至沓来一睹奇观，将其戏称为“巴斯克特的蠢事”（Baskette’s Folly）。然而过了一阵子，人们便小心翼翼地绕道而行，改以更恶劣的字眼唤它——亦即“群鸦巢”（The Rookery）；盖因在一群格外下流凶顽的社会渣滓之中，一切败类恶棍皆选定此地作为盘踞的大本营。盗贼以及较盗贼犹有过之之辈——品行恶劣的娼妇、成群结伙的市井浪子、吉普赛人、行商小贩——若要一一罗列，名册恐怕能同荷马史诗里的战船表一般漫长——统统在这片残存地基与深邃地窖里安营扎寨、栖身定居。他们肆意强占堆积如山的散落木板，搭在矮墙上充作屋顶；若是木板不够用，他们便扯来帆布遮风挡雨。

众所周知，此等市井莠民若散居在城市各处、东一撮西一簇地零落分流，倒也造不成多大危害；然而他们一旦扎堆盘踞在一处，局势便立时变得不可收拾。帮派由此纠集成势，行径愈发猖狂胆大；他们公然蔑视警宪，致使全城没有哪一户人家能够安枕无忧。

此地演变成了一处万民痛斥的罪恶毒瘤。各家报纸口诛笔伐、大张挞伐，警察（如今城里已有警队设立）怨声载道，屡屡向总署呈递告急报告。全城舆论一片哗然。此时斯脱明翰已然组建了市政法团（Corporation），设立了市府参事（aldermen）与市长（mayor），他们齐聚在金碧辉煌的市政厅（Guildhall）共商政务，人人皆是精明过人的生意好手。如今市政当局开始在“巴斯克特的蠢事”这一事端上采取动作了。外界民众盛赞他们是恪尽职守的优良市民，出于热忱的爱乡爱城动机，既关切本镇的声誉，又一心除恶禁暴、压制罪行。然而洞若观火之辈心如明镜——市政法团绝不介意顺手捞上一笔绝妙的商业巨利。无论如何，他们的行事手腕如下：历经连篇累牍的高谈阔论与空洞套话，通过了一道道决议，咨询了事务律师，以及经历了只有天晓得的繁文缛节之后，一天清晨，一支代表团前往斯特恩霍尔德·巴斯克特老爷（Sternhold Baskette, Esq.）下榻的旅店拜会（他素来在旅店寓居），并向他呈递了一份优厚体面的方案。方案的大致内容是：由他将所谓的“狂想楼”或曰未竣工的雏形新城，批租给当局若干年期；作为对价，当局将当场支付一笔定金款项，并签约承诺按照双方议定的规划与工程规程建造楼宇，整座新城在七十年（70年）后悉数归还给斯特恩霍尔德或其法定继承人。

Sternhold fought hard﻿—he asked for extravagant terms, and had to be brought to reason by a threat of an appeal by the Corporation to Parliament for a private Act.

This sobered him, for he was never quite happy in his secret mind about his title. Terms were agreed upon, the earnest money paid, and the masons began to work. Then suddenly there was an uproar. The companies or syndicates who had leased portions of the estate grew alarmed lest this enormous undertaking should, when finished, depreciate their property. They cast about for means of opposing it. It is said﻿—but I cannot believe it﻿—that they gave secret pay to the thieves and ruffians in the cellars to fight the masons and bricklayers, and drive them off.

At all events serious collisions occurred. But the Corporation was too strong. They telegraphed to London and got reinforcements, and carried the entrenchments by storm.

Then, so goes the discreditable rumour, the companies bribed the masons and bricklayers, who built so badly that every now and then houses fell in, and there was a fine loss! Finally they got up an agitation, cried down the Corporation for wasting public funds, and, what was far more serious, brought high legal authority to prove that *as a Corporation* they had no power to pledge themselves to such terms as they had, or indeed to enter into such a contract without polling the whole city.

===Chinese===
斯特恩霍尔德寸步不让、锱铢必争——他狮子大开口索要离谱的苛刻条件，直到市政法团威胁要向议会提请颁布特别私法案（private Act）予以强制征用，才迫使他恢复了理智。

这一招让他彻底清醒下来，因为在内心深处，他对自己对这片土地的产权权属始终耿耿于怀、心怀不安。双方最终达成了协议条款，定金付讫，石匠们随即破土动工。然而刹那间平地起风波。早前承租了该地产若干街区的各家公司或辛迪加联合体登时惊恐万分，唯恐这一庞然工程一旦大功告成，反倒会使他们手中的产业大幅贬值。他们开始四处钻营罗织阻挠对抗之策。坊间传闻——尽管我委实难以置信——声称他们暗中塞钱给地窖里的群贼恶棍，雇佣他们聚众围攻石匠与泥瓦工，并将其暴力驱逐。

无论如何，现场确乎爆发了极其严重的流血冲突。然而市政法团的势力太过雄厚。他们向伦敦拍发加急电报调集增援警力，最终以雷霆万钧之势强攻攻克了暴徒盘踞的堡垒工事。

随后，据那条极不光彩的传闻所述，各大公司又暗中收买行贿石匠与瓦工，教他们偷工减料粗制滥造，致使房屋时不时发生坍塌，造成了惨重的损失！最终，他们掀起了一场舆论风暴，大肆抨击痛斥市政法团挥霍公帑，更有甚者，他们还搬出了最高级别的法律权威人士，以证明市政当局*以法团身份*，根本无权许下如此这般的承诺条款，甚至在未获全城市民公投表决之前，压根儿无权缔结此类合同契约。

This alarmed the Corporation. There were secret meetings and long faces. But if one lawyer discovers a difficulty, another can always suggest a way round the corner. The Corporation went to Parliament, and got a private Act; but they did not go as a body. They went through Sternhold, who was persuaded; and indeed it looked plausible, that by so doing, and by getting the sanction of the House of Commons, he improved his own title.

Then the Corporation smiled, and built away faster than ever. In the course of an almost incredibly short time the vast plans of Sternhold were completed by the various companies, by the Corporation, and by himself; for every penny he got as premium, every penny of ground-rent, every penny from his collieries, iron furnaces, and cut-nail factories, went in bricks and mortar. It was the most magnificent scheme, perhaps, ever started by a single man. The city was proud of it. Like Augustus, he had found it brick, and left it marble.

Yet, in reality, he was no richer. The largest owner, probably, of house property in the world, he could but just pay his way at his hotel. Although he had a fine country house (which old Romy had purchased) in the suburbs, he never used it﻿—it was let. He preferred a hotel as a single man because there was no trouble to look after servants, etc. He lived in the most economical manner﻿—being obliged to, in fact.

Yet this very economy increased the popular belief in his riches. He was a miser. Give a man that name, let it once stick to him, and there is no limit to the fables that will be eagerly received as truth. Give a dog a bad name and hang him. Call a man a miser, and, if he is so inclined, he can roll in borrowed money, dine every day on presents of game and fish, and marry anyone he chooses. I only wish I had the reputation.

这着实让市政法团惊慌失措。密谋会议接连召开，人人面如土色、愁眉不展。然而倘若一个律师寻出一处刁难症结，另一个律师便总能出谋划策绕过弯子。市政法团向议会请愿，成功获准了一道特别私法案；但他们并非以法团集体名义出面，而是假借斯特恩霍尔德之名提请。后者被说服并深信不疑；而且听上去确也合情合理——通过借此路径获取下议院（House of Commons）的法定背书，他自身对那片地产的产权凭证亦将得以巩固与确权。

于是市政法团喜笑颜开，以前所未有的速度加紧营建。在一段短得近乎令人难以置信的岁月里，斯特恩霍尔德那宏伟壮阔的宏图大略，经由各家公司、市政法团以及他本人的合力推展，终于大告功成；盖因他所斩获的每一文权利金、每一文地皮租金，以及来自煤矿、炼铁高炉和机制切钉作坊的每一枚便士，统统全数倾注进了砖瓦灰浆之中。这堪称有史以来由区区一介凡人独自肇启的最为宏伟浩瀚的规划。全城皆引以为傲。正如奥古斯都大帝（Augustus）当年所言，他接手的是一座砖造的城池，留下的却是一座大理石的巨都。

然而在骨感现实中，他并未因此变得更加富有。他很可能是这世间坐拥房产规模至巨的地主，但他手中的进项却仅仅勉强够支付他在旅店里的起居食宿账单。尽管他在城郊拥有一处典雅优美的乡间府邸（当年由老罗米·巴斯克特所购置），但他却从未踏足安居——那座宅子早已招租外赁。作为一个单身汉，他更乐意寄居旅馆，因为这样便省去了料理仆役雇工等繁琐杂务。他过着极尽节俭的生活——实际上，乃是迫于生计不得不尔。
