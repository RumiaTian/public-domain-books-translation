Why should I not? Such had been the question which Sir Peregrine Orme had asked himself over and over again, in these latter days, since Lady Mason had been staying at his house; and the purport of the question was this:﻿—Why should he not make Lady Mason his wife?
I and my readers can probably see very many reasons why he should not do so; but then we are not in love with Lady Mason. Her charms and her sorrows﻿—her soft, sad smile and her more lovely tears have not operated upon us. We are not chivalrous old gentlemen, past seventy years of age, but still alive, keenly alive, to a strong feeling of romance. That visit will perhaps be remembered which Mr. Furnival made at The Cleeve, and the subsequent interview between Lady Mason and the baronet. On that day he merely asked himself the question, and took no further step. On the subsequent day and the day after, it was the same. He still asked himself the question, sitting alone in his library; but he did not ask it as yet of anyone else. When he met Lady Mason in these days his manner to her was full of the deference due to a lady and of the affection due to a dear friend; but that was all. Mrs. Orme, seeing this, and cordially concurring in this love for her guest, followed the lead which her father-in-law gave, and threw herself into Lady Mason’s arms. They two were fast and bosom friends.
And what did Lady Mason think of all this? In truth there was much in it that was sweet to her, but there was something also that increased that idea of danger which now seemed to envelop her whole existence. Why had Sir Peregrine so treated her in the library, behaving towards her with such tokens of close affection? He had put his arm round her waist and kissed her lips and pressed her to his old bosom. Why had this been so? He had assured her that he would be to her as a father, but her woman’s instinct had told her that the pressure of his hand had been warmer than that which a father accords to his adopted daughter. No idea of anger had come upon her for a moment; but she had thought about it much, and had thought about it almost in dismay. What if the old man did mean more than a father’s love? It seemed to her as though it must be a dream that he should do so; but what if he did? How should she answer him? In such circumstances what should she do or say?

毫无疑问：得了佩里格林爵士给她的名分，来日这一场争斗里她要占大便宜。她当真觉得，多克拉斯同约瑟夫·梅森未必敢把威胁的那项指控，加到佩里格林·奥姆爵士的妻子头上。再者，论品性的证据，还有什么比这样一桩婚姻更扎实的？可弗尼瓦尔先生将怎样受用它？他若见怪，这一仗离了他打得成么？不；打不成。律师的学识、历练、手段，同从男爵的地位、名望一样，于她缺一不可。可弗尼瓦尔先生为什么竟要见怪？"我不明白。"她对自己说，"我看不出有什么可怪之处。"可她良心深处有个低语告诉她：会见怪的。弗尼瓦尔先生必须被告知么？必须立刻告知么？还有卢修斯会说什么、会怎么想？儿子将冲她说出的那些硬话，她拿什么去接？她知道他话会硬，会大大不喜欢母亲这第二回婚事。哪个成年的儿子，听说母亲将嫁，心里痛快过？克利夫从此便是她的家——就是说，她若做下这件事。克利夫便是她的家；她便须事事同奥利农场分开。想到这里，她的心退回去，退回那些久已逝去的日子——那些她焦灼欲裂、只求奥利农场落到她脚边襁褓里的婴孩名下的日子。她记得自己怎样向那位父亲恳求，陈说儿子的权利——声言，而且声言得有理：为她自己，她一无所求；可为他——岂止可求，岂不是可讨？另一个儿子不是早有安置，那几个成年女儿不都嫁了阔人么？"他不也是您的孩子么？"她曾恳求。

===Original===

“Is he not your own, and as well worthy of your love?” She had succeeded in getting the inheritance for the baby at her feet;﻿—but had his having it made her happy, or him? Then her child had been all in all to her; but now she felt that that child was half estranged from her about this very property, and would become wholly estranged by the method she was taking to secure it! “I have toiled for him,” she said to herself, “rising up early, and going to bed late; but the thief cometh in the night and despoileth it.” Who can guess the bitterness of her thoughts as she said this?
But her last thoughts, as she sat there thinking, were of him﻿—Sir Peregrine. Would it be well for him that he should do this? And in thus considering she did not turn her mind chiefly to the usual view in which such a marriage would be regarded. Men might call Sir Peregrine an old fool and laugh at him; but for that she would, with God’s help, make him amends. In those matters, he could judge for himself; and should he judge it right thus to link his life to hers, she would be true and leal to him in all things.
But then, about this trial. If there came disgrace and ruin, and an utter overthrow? If﻿—? Would it not be well at any rate that no marriage should take place till that had been decided? She could not find it in her heart to bring down his old gray hairs with utter sorrow to the grave.

"他不也是您亲生的，同样配您的爱么？"她到底为她脚边的婴孩争到了那份承业；——可产业到了手，叫她幸福了么，叫他幸福了么？那时她的孩子便是她的一切；如今她觉得，恰恰为了这份产业，这孩子已同她生分了一半；照她保全产业的这路走法，还要整个儿生分！"我为他操碎了心，"她对自己说，"起早贪黑；贼却在夜里进来，把它偷走了。"她说这话时心里的苦，谁猜得着？
可她坐在那里想下去，最后的念头落在他身上——佩里格林爵士。此事于他可好？这样掂量时，她并不把心主要放在世人对这样婚姻的老看法上。男人们尽可以骂佩里格林爵士老糊涂、笑话他；这个，靠着上帝，她自会补偿他。这些事上，他自会裁断；他若裁断得把此生同她的连在一起是对的，她便事事对他真、对他忠。
可是，还有那场官司。倘若来的竟是耻辱与覆灭，竟是彻底的倾覆？倘若——？那么，至少等它见分晓再行礼，岂不好些？她横不下心，叫他年老的白发含着大悲进坟。
