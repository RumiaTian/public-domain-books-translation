### II / 二

===Original===
What, then, is implied in a government's resting on consent?

===Chinese===
那么，政府立足于同意，究竟意谓着什么？

===Original===
If it be said that the consent of the *strongest party* in a nation, is all that is necessary to justify the establishment of a government that shall have authority over the weaker party, it may be answered that the most despotic governments in the world rest upon that very principle, viz.: the consent of the strongest party. These governments are formed simply by the consent or agreement of the strongest party, that they will act in concert in subjecting the weaker party to their dominion. And the despotism, and tyranny, and injustice of these governments consist in that very fact. Or at least that is the first step in their tyranny; a necessary preliminary to all the oppressions that are to follow.

===Chinese===
倘若有人说，一国之中*最强党派*（the strongest party）的同意，便足以证成一个支配较弱党派的政府的建立，那么可以回答：世界上最专制的政府，恰恰立足于这一原则，即：最强党派的同意。这些政府的成立，不过是由于最强党派彼此同意、相约协同行动，把较弱党派置于其支配之下。而这些政府的专制、暴虐与不义，恰恰就在于此。或者至少，这是其暴政的第一步，是随后一切压迫的必要前奏。

===Original===
If it be said that the consent of the *most numerous party*, in a nation, is sufficient to justify the establishment of their power over the less numerous party, it may be answered:

===Chinese===
倘若有人说，一国之中*人数最多的党派*（the most numerous party）的同意，足以证成将其权力加于人数较少的党派之上，那么可以回答：

===Original===
First. That two men have no more natural right to exercise any kind of authority over one, than one has to exercise the same authority over two. A man's natural rights are his own, against the whole world; and any infringement of them is equally a crime, whether committed by one man, or by millions; whether committed by one man, calling himself a robber, (or by any other name indicating his true character,) or by millions, calling themselves a government.

===Chinese===
第一，两人对一人行使任何一种权力，并不比一人对两人行使同样的权力更具自然权利（natural rights）。一个人的自然权利是他自己的，足以对抗全世界；对它的任何侵犯都同等地构成罪行，无论其行于一人，还是行于数百万人；无论犯罪者是自称强盗的一人（或以其他任何表明其真实本性的名号自称），还是自称政府的数百万人。

===Original===
Second. It would be absurd for the most numerous party to talk of establishing a government over the less numerous party, unless the former were also the strongest, as well as the most numerous: for it is not to be supposed that the strongest party would ever submit to the rule of the weaker party, merely because the latter were the most numerous. And as matter of fact, it is perhaps never that governments are established by the most numerous party. They are usually, if not always, established by the less numerous party; their superior strength consisting in their superior wealth, intelligence, and ability to act in concert.

===Chinese===
第二，人数最多的党派侈谈在人数较少的党派之上建立政府，实属荒谬，除非前者既人数最多、又力量最强：因为不能设想最强党派会仅仅因为后者人数最多，就屈从于较弱党派的统治。而事实上，政府或许从来不是由人数最多的党派建立的。即便并非一贯如此，它们通常也是由人数较少的党派建立的；其力量优势在于更雄厚的财富、更高的智识，以及协同行动的能力。

===Original===
Third. Our Constitution does not profess to have been established simply by the majority; but by "the people;" the minority, as much as the majority.

===Chinese===
第三，我们的宪法（the Constitution）并不自称仅由多数（majority）确立，而是由「人民」确立；少数（minority）与多数，同在其内。

===Original===
Fourth. If our fathers, in 1776, had acknowledged the principle that a majority had the right to rule the minority, we should never have become a nation; for they were in a small minority, as compared with those who claimed the right to rule over them.

===Chinese===
第四，倘若我们的先辈在 1776 年便承认多数有权统治少数这一原则，我们便永远不会成为一个国家；因为与那些自称有权统治他们的人相比，他们只居于少数。

===Original===
Fifth. Majorities, *as such*, afford no guarantees for justice. They are men of the same nature as minorities. They have the same passions for fame, power, and money, as minorities; and are liable and likely to be equally—perhaps more than equally, because more boldly—rapacious, tyrannical and unprincipled, if entrusted with power. There is no more reason, then, why a man should either sustain, or submit to, the rule of a majority, than of a minority. Majorities and minorities cannot rightfully be taken at all into account in deciding questions of justice. And all talk about them, in matters of government, is mere absurdity. Men are dunces for uniting to sustain any government, or any laws, *except those in which they are all agreed*. And nothing but force and fraud compel men to sustain any other. To say that majorities, as such, have a right to rule minorities, is equivalent to saying that minorities have, and ought to have, no rights, except such as majorities please to allow them.

===Chinese===
第五，多数派*就其为多数派而言*（as such），并不能为正义提供任何保证。他们与少数派同为人类，本性无二。他们与少数派一样怀有对名誉、权力与金钱的欲望；一旦被托付权力，也同样可能——或许更甚于同等，因为更加肆无忌惮——变得贪婪、暴虐、寡廉鲜耻。因此，一个人并没有更多的理由去支撑或屈从多数的统治，胜过去支撑或屈从少数的统治。在裁断正义问题时，多数与少数根本不应被正当地纳入考量。凡在政府事务上侈谈多数少数，纯属无稽之谈。人们若联合起来支撑任何政府或任何法律，*唯独*一致同意者*除外*，那便是愚不可及；而迫使人支撑除此之外的任何东西的，唯有暴力与欺诈。说多数派*就其为多数派而言*有权统治少数派，就等于说少数派没有、也不应当有任何权利，除非多数派乐意许给他们。

===Original===
Sixth. It is not improbable that many or most of the worst of governments—although established by force, and by a few, in the first place—come, in time, to be supported by a majority. Such majorities, very likely, could be found in half, perhaps in nine-tenths, of all the countries on the globe. What do they prove? Nothing but the tyranny and corruption of the very governments that have reduced so large portions of the people to their present ignorance, servility, degradation, and corruption; an ignorance, servility, degradation, and corruption that are best illustrated in the simple fact that they *do* sustain the governments that have so oppressed, degraded, and corrupted them. They do nothing towards proving that the governments themselves are legitimate; or that they ought to be sustained, or even endured, by those who understand their true character. The mere fact, therefore, that a government chances to be supported by a majority, of itself proves nothing that is necessary to be proved, in order to know whether such government should be sustained, or not.

===Chinese===
第六，许多乃至大多数最坏的政府——虽然起初由少数人以暴力建立——后来未必不会获得多数的支撑。但即便如此，这多数大部分是由人民中最愚昧、最迷信、最怯懦、最依附、最奴性、最腐化的部分构成的：其中有人被真正构成政府的少数人的权势、智识、财富与傲慢所震慑，有人被其诡诈所欺骗，有人被其利诱所腐化。这样的多数，地球上恐怕有一半、甚至十分之九的国家都能找得出来。它们能证明什么？除了证明这些政府本身的暴虐与腐化——正是这些政府把如此广大的民众沦落到今日的愚昧、奴性、堕落与腐化——之外，什么也证明不了；而这种愚昧、奴性、堕落与腐化，最鲜明地体现在一个简单的事实之中：他们*竟然*支撑着如此压迫他们、贬抑他们、腐化他们的政府。这些多数丝毫不能证明政府本身的正当（legitimate）；丝毫不能证明政府应当被支撑，乃至应当被那些明了其真实本性者所忍受。因此，一个政府碰巧获得多数支撑这一单纯事实，本身并不能证明任何为判断该政府应否被支撑所必须证明的东西。

===Original===
Seventh. The principle that the majority have a right to rule the minority, practically resolves all government into a mere contest between two bodies of men, as to which of them shall be masters, and which of them slaves; a contest, that—however bloody—can, in the nature of things, never be finally closed, so long as man refuses to be a slave.

===Chinese===
第七，多数有权统治少数这一原则，实际上把一切政府化为两群人之间的一场竞逐——看谁为主人、谁为奴隶；这场竞逐，无论多么血腥，就事物的本性而言，只要人尚不肯为奴，便永远不会最终了结。

### III / 三

===Original===
But to say that the consent of either the strongest party, or the most numerous party, *in a nation*, is a sufficient justification for the establishment or maintenance of a government that shall control the whole nation, does not obviate the difficulty. The question still remains, how comes such a thing as "a nation" to exist? How do many millions of men, scattered over an extensive territory—each gifted by nature with individual freedom; required by the law of nature to call no man, or body of men, his masters; authorized by that law to seek his own happiness in his own way, to do what he will with himself and his property, so long as he does not trespass upon the equal liberty of others; authorized also, by that law, to defend his own rights, and redress his own wrongs; and to go to the assistance and defence of any of his fellow men who may be suffering any kind of injustice—how do many millions of such men *come to be a nation*, in the first place? How is it that each of them comes to be stripped of all his natural, God-given rights, and to be incorporated, compressed, compacted, and consolidated into a mass with other men, whom he never saw; with whom he has no contract; and towards many of whom he has no sentiments but fear, hatred, or contempt? How does he become subjected to the control of men like himself, who, by nature, had no authority over him; but who command him to do this, and forbid him to do that, as if they were his sovereigns, and he their subject; and as if their wills and their interests were the only standards of his duties and his rights; and who compel him to submission under peril of confiscation, imprisonment, and death?

===Chinese===
但是，说一国之中最强党派或人数最多党派的同意，便足以证成一个支配全国的政府的建立或维持，并未消除困难。问题依然存在：「一个国家」（a nation）这种东西究竟从何而来？散布于广袤领土上的数百万人——每个人都蒙自然赋予个体自由；依自然法（law of nature）的要求，不得认何人或任何团体为主；依该法得自行其道地追求自身幸福，得任意处置自己及自己的财产，只要不侵犯他人同等的自由；依该法还得以保卫自身权利、伸雪自身冤屈，并得去援助与卫护任何正遭受不义的同胞——这数百万这样的人，究竟是如何*成为一个国家的*？他们中的每一个人究竟是如何被剥去其全部天赋的、上帝所赐的权利，被编并、压缩、紧束、凝合为一团，混同于其他他素未谋面、与之毫无契约、且对其中的许多人只怀恐惧、憎恨或轻蔑之人？他又是如何落到受与自己同类之人的支配——这些人按其本性对他并无任何权力——他们命令他做这个、禁止他做那个，仿佛他们是他的君上、他是他们的臣仆；仿佛他们的意志与利益，乃是其义务与权利的唯一准绳；并以没收、监禁与死刑之危，强迫他俯首就范？

===Original===
Clearly all this is the work of force, or fraud, or both.

===Chinese===
显然，这一切皆是暴力、欺诈、或二者兼施的产物。

===Original===
By what right, then, did *we* become "a nation?" By what right do we continue to be "a nation?" And by what right do either the strongest, or the most numerous, party, now existing within the territorial limits, called "The United States," claim that there really is such "a nation" as the United States? Certainly they are bound to show the rightful existence of "a nation," before they can claim, *on that ground*, that they themselves have a right to control it; to seize, for their purposes, so much of every man's property within it, as they may choose; and, at their discretion, to compel any man to risk his own life, or take the lives of other men, for the maintenance of their power.

===Chinese===
那么，*我们*究竟是凭什么权利成为「一个国家」的？我们凭什么权利继续作为「一个国家」存在？而如今居于所谓「美利坚合众国」疆域之内的最强党派或人数最多党派，又是凭什么权利宣称：确乎存在美利坚合众国这样一个「国家」？无疑，他们必须先证明「一个国家」的存在具有正当性，然后才有资格*以此为据*，宣称自己有权支配它；有权为其目的、随其所欲地攫取国内每个人的财产；并有权恣意强令任何人为维持其权力而冒自己生命之险，或夺他人之性命。

===Original===
To speak of either their numbers, or their strength, is not to the purpose. The question is by what *right* does the nation exist? And by what *right* are so many atrocities committed by its authority? or for its preservation?

===Chinese===
谈他们的数目也好，谈他们的力量也罢，都不得要领。问题是：这个国家凭何*权利*存在？凭何*权利*，才有这许多暴行假其权威而行，或为其存续而施？

===Original===
The answer to this question must certainly be, that at least *such a nation* exists by no right whatever.

===Chinese===
对这个问题的回答必然是：至少*这样一个国家*的存在，毫无权利可言。

===Original===
We are, therefore, driven to the acknowledgment that nations and governments, if they can rightfully exist at all, can exist only by consent.

===Chinese===
因此，我们不得不承认：国家与政府，倘若竟可正当存在，也只能凭同意而存在。

### IV / 四

===Original===
The question, then, returns, What is implied in a government's resting on consent?

===Chinese===
那么，问题又回来了：政府立足于同意，究竟意谓着什么？

===Original===
Manifestly this one thing (to say nothing of others) is necessarily implied in the idea of a government's resting on consent, viz.: *the separate, individual consent of every man who is required to contribute, either by taxation or personal service, to the support of the government.* All this, or nothing, is necessarily implied, because one man's consent is just as necessary as any other man's. If, for example, A claims that his consent is necessary to the establishment or maintenance of government, he thereby necessarily admits that B's and every other man's are equally necessary; because B's and every other man's rights are just as good as his own. On the other hand, if he denies that B's or any other particular man's consent is necessary, he thereby necessarily admits that neither his own, nor any other man's is necessary; and that government need not be founded on consent at all.

===Chinese===
显然，至少有这样一件事（姑且不论其他）必然隐含于「政府立足于同意」的观念之中，即：*每一个被要求以纳税（taxation）或人身劳役来供养政府的人，其分别的、个别的同意。*必然是全部如此，否则便是全然不如此；因为一个人的同意与任何其他人的同意，同样是必要的。举例来说，倘若甲主张他的同意为政府的建立或维持所必需，那他由此便必然承认：乙以及一切其他人的同意也同样必要；因为乙以及一切其他人的权利，与他的权利同样正当。反过来，倘若他否认乙或任何特定之人的同意是必要的，那他由此便必然承认：他自己的同意、以及任何其他人的同意，都不是必要的；政府根本无须立足于同意。

===Original===
There is, therefore, no alternative but to say, either that the separate, individual consent of every man, *who is required to aid, in any way, in supporting the government*, is necessary, or that the consent of no one is necessary.

===Chinese===
因此别无选择：要么，*以任何方式被要求资助维持政府*的每一个人，其分别的、个别的同意是必要的；要么，任何人的同意都不是必要的。

===Original===
Clearly this individual consent is indispensable to the idea of treason; for if a man has never consented or agreed to support a government, he breaks no faith in refusing to support it. And if he makes war upon it, he does so as an open enemy, and not as a traitor—that is, as a betrayer, or treacherous friend.

===Chinese===
显然，这种个别的同意，对于叛国（treason）的观念乃不可或缺；因为一个人倘若从未同意或约定支撑一个政府，那么他拒绝支撑它，并未背弃任何信义。而倘若他向它开战，他是以公开敌人的身份这样做，而不是以叛徒（traitor）——即出卖者、背信的伪友——的身份。

===Original===
All this, or nothing, was necessarily implied in the Declaration made in 1776. If the necessity for consent, then announced, was a sound principle in favor of three millions of men, it was an equally sound one in favor of three men, or of one man. If the principle was a sound one in behalf of men living on a separate continent, it was an equally sound one in behalf of a man living on a separate farm, or in a separate house.

===Chinese===
这全部，或者全然没有，正是 1776 年发表的宣言（the Declaration）所必然隐含的。倘若当时宣告的「同意之为必要」，作为一项原则有利于三百万人，那么它同样有利于三个人，乃至一个人。倘若这项原则有利于居于一个单独大陆上的人们，那么它同样有利于居于一个单独农场上、一座单独房屋里的人。

===Original===
Moreover, it was only as separate individuals, each acting for himself, and not as members of organized governments, that the three millions declared their consent to be necessary to their support of a government; and, at the same time, declared their dissent to the support of the British Crown. The governments, then existing in the Colonies, had no constitutional power, *as governments*, to declare the separation between England and America. On the contrary, those governments, *as governments*, were organized under charters from, and acknowledged allegiance to, the British Crown. Of course the British king never made it one of the chartered or constitutional powers of those governments, *as governments* to absolve the people from their allegiance to himself. So far, therefore, as the Colonial Legislatures acted as revolutionists, they acted only as so many individual revolutionists, and not as constitutional legislatures. And their representatives at Philadelphia, who first declared Independence, were, in the eye of the constitutional law of that day, simply a committee of Revolutionists, and in no sense constitutional authorities, or the representatives of constitutional authorities.

===Chinese===
再者，那三百万人宣告：他们的同意是支撑政府所必需；同时宣告：他们不支持英国王权（the British Crown）。这一宣告与这一异议，都只是以彼此分离的个人、各自为自身行动的身份作出的，而不是以有组织的政府成员的身份。当时存在于各殖民地的政府，*作为政府*并无宣告英、美分离的宪法权力。相反，那些政府*作为政府*，是依据英国王室的特许状（charters）组织起来的，并承认对英国王室效忠（allegiance）。英国国王当然从未把「解除人民对他本人的效忠」列为那些政府*作为政府*的特许权力或宪法权力之一。因此，就殖民地议会以革命者的身份行动而论，他们也只是作为许许多多个别的革命者行动，而非作为拥有宪法地位的议会。而他们在费城率先宣告独立的代表们，按当时的宪法法律来看，不过是一个革命者委员会，绝无宪法机关的资格，也不是宪法机关的代表。

===Original===
It was also, in the eye of the law, only as separate individuals, each acting for himself, and exercising simply his natural rights as an individual, that the people at large *assented to, and ratified the Declaration*.

===Chinese===
同样，在法律眼中，全体人民*赞同并批准这份宣言*，也只是以彼此分离的个人、各自为自身行动、单纯行使个人自然权利的身份。

===Original===
It was also only as so many individuals, each acting for himself, and exercising simply his natural rights, that they revolutionized the *constitutional character* of their local governments, (so as to exclude the idea of allegiance to Great Britain); changing their forms only as and when their convenience dictated.

===Chinese===
同样，他们革新其地方政府的*宪法性质*（从而排除对大不列颠的效忠观念），也只是作为许许多多个别的个人、各自为自身行动、单纯行使自然权利；而且只是在他们认为便利之时、并按其便利所指示的方式，才改变其形式。

===Original===
The whole Revolution, therefore, as a Revolution, was declared and accomplished by the people, acting separately as individuals, and exercising each his natural rights, and not by their governments in the exercise of their constitutional powers.

===Chinese===
因此，整场革命，作为一场革命，是由人民以个人身份分别行动、各自行使自己的自然权利而宣告并完成的，而不是由他们的政府在行使宪法权力时完成的。

===Original===
It was, therefore, as individuals, and only as individuals, each acting for himself alone, that they declared that their consent—that is, their individual consent, for each one could consent only for himself—was necessary to the creation or perpetuity of any government that they could rightfully be called on to support.

===Chinese===
因此，他们宣告：对任何他们可被正当地要求支撑的政府而言，其创立或延续都必须以他们的同意为必要——亦即他们各自的同意，因为每个人都只能为自己表示同意；而作出这一宣告的，正是个人，且仅仅是个人，每个人都单为自己行动。

===Original===
In the same way each declared, for himself, that his own will, pleasure, and discretion were the only authorities he had any occasion to consult, in determining whether he would any longer support the government under which he had always lived. And if this action of each individual were valid and rightful when he had so many other individuals to keep him company, it would have been, in the view of natural justice and right, equally valid and rightful, if he had taken the same step alone. He had the same natural right to take up arms alone to defend his own property against a single tax-gatherer, that he had to take up arms in company with three millions of others, to defend the property of all against an army of tax-gatherers.

===Chinese===
同样，每个人都为自己宣告：在决定是否继续支撑他一向生活于其下的政府时，他自己的意志、意愿与裁量，乃是他需要征询的唯一权威。而倘若每个人的这一行动，在有如此众多他人同行之时是有效而正当的，那么，依自然正义与自然权利的观点，他独自采取同样的步骤，也同样有效而正当。他独自拿起武器、保卫自己的财产、对抗单个的收税官，与他和三百万人一起拿起武器、保卫所有人的财产、对抗收税官的大军，具有同等的自然权利。

===Original===
Thus the whole Revolution turned upon, asserted, and, in theory, established, the right of each and every man, at his discretion, to release himself from the support of the government under which he had lived. And this principle was asserted, not as a right peculiar to themselves, or to that time, or as applicable only to the government then existing; but as a universal right of all men, at all times, and under all circumstances.

===Chinese===
由此可见，整场革命所环绕、所主张、并在理论上所确立的，是每一个人都可凭自己的裁量，解除自己对一向生活于其下的政府的支撑这一权利。而这一原则之被主张，并非作为他们所特有的权利，或那个时代所特有的权利，或仅适用于当时政府的权利，而是作为一切人在一切时代、一切境况下普遍享有的权利。

===Original===
George the Third called our ancestors traitors for what they did at that time. But they were not traitors *in fact*, whatever he or his laws may have called them. They were not traitors in fact, because they betrayed nobody, and broke faith with nobody. They were his equals, owing him no allegiance, obedience, nor any other duty, except such as they owed to mankind at large. Their political relations with him had been purely voluntary. They had never pledged their faith to him that they would continue these relations any longer than it should please them to do so; and therefore they broke no faith in parting with him. They simply exercised their natural right of saying to him, and to the English people, that they were under no obligation to continue their political connection with them, and that, for reasons of their own, they chose to dissolve it.

===Chinese===
乔治三世（George the Third）称我们的先辈因当时的所作所为而为叛徒。但无论他或他的法律怎样称呼他们，他们*事实上*并不是叛徒。说他们事实上不是叛徒，是因为他们没有出卖任何人，没有对任何人背信。他们与他是平等的，不欠他任何效忠、服从或任何其他义务，只欠他们对全人类所负的义务。他们与他的政治关系原是纯粹自愿的。他们从未向他立信担保，说这些关系要延续到他们乐意之时以后；因此他们与他决裂，并未背弃任何信义。他们只是行使了自己的自然权利，向他、也向英国人民声明：他们没有义务继续与他们的政治联系，而且出于自己的理由，他们选择解除这种联系。

===Original===
What was true of our ancestors, is true of revolutionists in general. The monarchs and governments, from whom they choose to separate, attempt to stigmatize them as traitors. But they are not traitors in fact; inasmuch as they betray, and break faith with, no one. Having pledged no faith, they break none. They are simply men, who, for reasons of their own—whether good or bad, wise or unwise, is immaterial—choose to exercise their natural right of dissolving their connection with the governments under which they have lived. In doing this, they no more commit the crime of treason—which necessarily implies treachery, deceit, breach of faith—than a man commits treason when he chooses to leave a church, or any other voluntary association, with which he has been connected.

===Chinese===
我们的先辈如此，一般的革命者亦莫不如此。他们选择与之分离的君主和政府，企图把叛徒的污名加于其身。但他们事实上并不是叛徒，因为他们没有出卖任何人，没有对任何人背信。未曾立信，便无所背弃。他们只是一些人，出于自己的理由——理由是好是坏、是智是愚，无关宏旨——选择行使自己的自然权利，解除自己与一向生活于其下的政府的联系。这样做时，他们并没有犯下叛国罪——此罪必然意谓着背信、欺诈、毁约——正如一个人选择脱离他一向所属的教会或任何其他自愿团体时，并没有犯下叛国罪一样。

===Original===
This principle was a true one in 1776. It is a true one now. It is the only one on which any rightful government can rest. It is the one on which the Constitution itself professes to rest. If it does not really rest on that basis, it has no right to exist; and it is the duty of every man to raise his hand against it.

===Chinese===
这一原则在 1776 年是真理，如今仍是真理。它是任何正当政府得以立足的唯一基础。宪法自己也自称立足于这一基础。倘若它果真不是立足于这一基础，它便无权存在；每个人都有责任起而反抗它。

===Original===
If the men of the Revolution designed to incorporate in the Constitution the absurd ideas of allegiance and treason, which they had once repudiated, against which they had fought, and by which the world had been enslaved, they thereby established for themselves an indisputable claim to the disgust and detestation of all mankind.

===Chinese===
倘若革命志士们有意把效忠与叛国这些荒谬观念——那些他们曾经拒斥、曾经为之奋战、并曾使全世界为之受奴役的观念——写进宪法，那他们便为自己确立了一项无可争辩的名分：招致全人类的憎恶与唾弃。

===Original===
---

In subsequent numbers, the author hopes to show that, under the principle of individual consent, the little government that mankind need, is not only practicable, but natural and easy; and that the Constitution of the United States authorizes no government, except one depending wholly on voluntary support.

===Chinese===
在后续各分册中，作者希望表明：在个人同意的原则之下，人类所需要的那一点小小的政府，不仅切实可行，而且自然而简便；并且，美利坚合众国宪法所授权的政府，唯有全然依赖自愿支撑的那一种。

## Introductory / 导言

===Original===
The question of treason is distinct from that of slavery; and is the same that it would have been, if free States, instead of slave States, had seceded.

===Chinese===
叛国（treason）问题不同于奴隶制问题；而且，即便当初脱离联邦的不是蓄奴州，而是自由州，这个问题也仍然是同一个问题。

===Original===
On the part of the North, the war was carried on, not to liberate the slaves, but by a government that had always perverted and violated the Constitution, to keep the slaves in bondage; and was still willing to do so, if the slaveholders could be thereby induced to stay in the Union.

===Chinese===
就北方而言，这场战争得以进行，并非为了解放奴隶，而是由一个一贯曲解并践踏宪法（the Constitution）的政府发动的，其目的是使奴隶继续处于奴役之中；倘若借此能够诱使蓄奴者留在联邦（Union）之内，这个政府仍然愿意如此行事。

===Original===
The principle, on which the war was waged by the North, was simply this: That men may rightfully be compelled to submit to, and support, a government that they do not want; and that resistance, on their part, makes them traitors and criminals.

===Chinese===
北方进行这场战争所依据的原则，简而言之就是：人们可以被正当地强迫服从并供养一个他们并不想要的政府；而他们的反抗，则使他们成为叛国者（traitors）与罪犯。

===Original===
No principle, that is possible to be named, can be more self-evidently false than this; or more self-evidently fatal to all political freedom. Yet it triumphed in the field, and is now assumed to be established. If it be really established, the number of slaves, instead of having been diminished by the war, has been greatly increased; for a man, thus subjected to a government that he does not want, is a slave. And there is no difference, in principle—but only in degree—between political and chattel slavery. The former, no less than the latter, denies a man’s ownership of himself and the products of his labor; and asserts that other men may own him, and dispose of him and his property, for their uses, and at their pleasure.

===Chinese===
凡是举得出来的原则，没有一条比这条更自明地虚假，也没有一条对一切政治自由更自明地具有毁灭性。然而它已在战场上获胜，如今并被径直假定为已然确立。倘若它果真确立，那么奴隶的数目非但没有因这场战争而减少，反而大大增加了；因为一个如此屈从于他所不想要的政府的人，就是一个奴隶。而在政治奴隶制（political slavery）与动产奴隶制（chattel slavery）之间，并无原则上的差别——只有程度上的差别。前者与后者一样，否认一个人对自身及其劳动产品的所有权；并宣称他人可以占有他，为自己的用途、随心所欲地处置他及其财产。

===Original===
Previous to the war, there were some grounds for saying that—in theory, at least, if not in practice—our government was a free one; that it rested on consent. But nothing of that kind can be said now, if the principle on which the war was carried on by the North, is irrevocably established.

===Chinese===
战前，尚有某种理由可以说——至少在理论上，即便不在实践上——我们的政府是一个自由的政府；它建立在同意（consent）之上。但倘若北方进行战争所依据的那条原则被不可撤销地确立下来，如今就再也不能作此说了。

===Original===
If that principle be *not* the principle of the Constitution, the fact should be known. If it *be* the principle of the Constitution, the Constitution itself should be at once overthrown.

===Chinese===
倘若那条原则*并非*宪法的原则，这一事实就应当为人所知。倘若它*确系*宪法的原则，那么宪法本身就应当立即被推翻。

## VI The Constitution of No Authority / 第六分册　毫无权威的宪法

===Original===
The first and second numbers of this series were published in 1867. For reasons not necessary to be explained, the sixth is now published in advance of the third, fourth and fifth.

===Chinese===
本系列的第一、二两册已于 1867 年出版。由于若干无须说明的原因，第六册现先于第三、四、五册出版。

### I / 一

===Original===
The Constitution has no inherent authority or obligation. It has no authority or obligation at all, unless as a contract between man and man. And it does not so much as even purport to be a contract between persons now existing. It purports, at most, to be only a contract between persons living eighty years ago. And it can be supposed to have been a contract then only between persons who had already come to years of discretion, so as to be competent to make reasonable and obligatory contracts. Furthermore, we know, historically, that only a small portion even of the people then existing were consulted on the subject, or asked, or permitted to express either their consent or dissent in any formal manner. Those persons, if any, who did give their consent formally, are all dead now. Most of them have been dead forty, fifty, sixty, or seventy years. *And the Constitution, so far as it was their contract, died with them.* They had no natural power or right to make it obligatory upon their children. It is not only plainly impossible, in the nature of things, that they *could* bind their posterity, but they did not even attempt to bind them. That is to say, the instrument does not purport to be an agreement between anybody but “the people” *then* existing; nor does it, either expressly or impliedly, assert any right, power, or disposition, on their part, to bind anybody but themselves. Let us see. Its language is:

===Chinese===
宪法（the Constitution）并不具有任何固有的权威或约束力（obligation）。除了作为人与人之间订立的一份契约（contract），它根本毫无权威或约束力。而它甚至压根不曾自称是现今在世者之间的一份契约；它至多自称是八十年前在世者之间的一份契约。而且，即便就当时而言，也只能设想它是在那些已达知情明理之年、从而有能力订立合理的、具有约束力之契约的人之间订立的。况且，我们从历史上知道，即便在当时的全部人口中，也只有一小部分人曾就这个问题被征询、被请求，或被允许以任何正式的方式表达他们的同意（consent）或异议。那些曾以正式方式表示同意的人——如果确有其人——如今已全部故去，其中大多数已故去四十年、五十年、六十年或七十年。*而宪法，就其曾是他们的契约而言，已随他们一同死去。*他们没有任何自然的力量或权利，能使宪法对其子女具有约束力。他们*能够*约束自己的后代，这不仅按事物的本性显然不可能，而且他们甚至根本没有作过这种尝试。这就是说，这份文书（instrument）只是自称存在于*当时*的“人民”之间的一项协议（agreement），此外并不自称存在于任何人之间；而且，无论明示还是默示，它都没有宣称这些人拥有任何权利、权力或意愿，去约束自身以外的任何人。让我们看看。它的措辞是：

===Original===
> “We, the people of the United States [that is, the people *then existing* in the United States], in order to form a more perfect union, insure domestic tranquillity, provide for the common defence, promote the general welfare, and secure the blessings of liberty to ourselves *and our posterity*, do ordain and establish this Constitution for the United States of America.”

===Chinese===
> “我们，美利坚合众国的人民［即*当时*存在于美利坚合众国的人民］，为组建一个更完善的联邦、确保国内安宁、筹办共同防务、增进普遍福利，并使我们自己*和我们的后代*得享自由的幸福，特此为美利坚合众国制定并确立本宪法。”

===Original===
It is plain, in the first place, that this language, *as an agreement*, purports to be only what it at most really was, viz.: a contract between the people *then existing*; and, of necessity, binding, as a contract, only upon those *then existing*. In the second place, the language neither expresses nor implies that they had any intention or desire, nor that they imagined they had any right or power, to *bind* their “posterity” to live under it. It does not say that their “posterity” will, shall, or must live under it. It only says, in effect, that their hopes and motives in adopting it were that it might prove useful to their posterity, as well as to themselves, by promoting their union, safety, tranquillity, liberty, etc.

===Chinese===
首先，显而易见，这段话*作为一项协议*，只是自称它实际上至多曾是的东西，即：*当时*在世的人民之间的一份契约；而作为契约，它必然只约束*当时*在世的人。其次，这段话既未表达、也未暗示：他们有任何约束其“后代”生活于其下的意图或愿望，或者他们自认为拥有任何这样做的权利或权力。它并没有说他们的“后代”将会、应当或必须生活于其下。它实际上只是说，他们采纳宪法的希望与动机在于：通过增进联合、安全、安宁、自由等等，宪法或许能证明于他们的后代有益，也同样于他们自己有益。

===Original===
Suppose an agreement were entered into, in this form:

===Chinese===
试想一份以下列形式订立的协议：

===Original===
> “We, the people of Boston, agree to maintain a fort on Governor’s Island, to protect ourselves *and our posterity* against invasion.”

===Chinese===
> “我们，波士顿的人民，同意在总督岛（Governor’s Island）上维持一座堡垒，以保护我们自己*和我们的后代*免遭入侵。”

===Original===
This agreement, *as an agreement*, would clearly bind nobody but the people then existing. Secondly, it would assert no right, power, or disposition, on their part, to *compel* their “posterity” to maintain such a fort. It would only indicate that the supposed welfare of their posterity was one of the motives that induced the original parties to enter into the agreement.

===Chinese===
这份协议*作为一项协议*，显然只约束当时在世的人民，此外不约束任何人。其次，它并不主张他们拥有任何权利、权力或意愿，去*强迫*他们的“后代”维持这样一座堡垒。它只是表明，其后代设想中的福祉，是促使原缔约方订立该协议的动机之一。

===Original===
When a man says he is building a house for himself *and his posterity*, he does not mean to be understood as saying that he has any thought of *binding* them, nor is it to be inferred that he is so foolish as to imagine that he has any right or power to *bind* them, to live in it. So far as they are concerned, he only means to be understood as saying that his hopes and motives, in building it, are that they, or at least some of them, may find it for their happiness to live in it.

===Chinese===
当一个人说，他为自己*和他的后代*建造一座房屋时，不能把他理解为：他有过*约束*后代居住其中的念头；也不能由此推想他愚蠢到自认为拥有任何*约束*他们居住其中的权利或权力。就他的后代而言，他只是想让人这样理解：他建造房屋的希望与动机是，他们——或至少他们当中的某些人——或许会觉得居住其中是幸福的。

===Original===
So when a man says he is planting a tree for himself *and his posterity*, he does not mean to be understood as saying that he has any thought of *compelling* them, nor is it to be inferred that he is such a simpleton as to imagine that he has any right or power to compel them, to eat the fruit. So far as they are concerned, he only means to say that his hopes and motives, in planting the tree, are that its fruit may be agreeable to them.

===Chinese===
同样，当一个人说，他为自己*和他的后代*栽种一棵树时，不能把他理解为：他有过*强迫*后代吃果实的念头；也不能由此推想他傻到自认为拥有任何强迫他们吃果实的权利或权力。就他的后代而言，他只是想说，他栽种这棵树的希望与动机是，果实或能合他们的心意。

===Original===
So it was with those who originally adopted the Constitution. Whatever may have been their personal intentions, the legal meaning of their language, so far as their “posterity” was concerned, simply was, that their hopes and motives, in entering into the agreement, were that it might prove useful and acceptable to their posterity; that it might promote their union, safety, tranquillity, and welfare; and that it might tend “to secure to them the blessings of liberty.” The language does not assert nor at all imply, any right, power, or disposition, on the part of the original parties to the agreement, to *compel* their “posterity” to live under it. If they had intended to *bind* their posterity to live under it, they should have said that their object was, not “to secure to them the blessings of liberty,” but to make slaves of them; for if their “posterity” are bound to live under it, they are nothing less than the slaves of their foolish, tyrannical, and dead grandfathers.

===Chinese===
最初采纳宪法的人，情形也是如此。无论他们个人的意图如何，就其“后代”而言，他们语言的含义在法律上仅仅是：他们订立这项协议的希望与动机，是它或许能证明对后代有用并为他们所接受；是它或许能促进他们的联合、安全、安宁与福祉；是它或许有助于“保障他们得享自由的幸福”。这段话并没有主张，也全然没有暗示：协议的原缔约方拥有任何权利、权力或意愿，去*强迫*他们的“后代”生活于其下。倘若他们有意*约束*后代生活于其下，那他们就应当说，他们的目标不是“保障后代得享自由的幸福”，而是使后代沦为奴隶；因为倘若他们的“后代”受约束而必须生活于其下，那这些后代就纯粹是他们那些愚蠢、专横而早已死去的祖父辈的奴隶。

===Original===
It cannot be said that the Constitution formed “the people of the United States,” for all time, into a corporation. It does not speak of “the people” as a corporation, but as individuals. A corporation does not describe itself as “we,” nor as “people,” nor as “ourselves.” Nor does a corporation, in legal language, have any “posterity.” It supposes itself to have, and speaks of itself as having, perpetual existence, as a single individuality.

===Chinese===
不能说宪法把“美利坚合众国人民”永久地组成了一个法人团体（corporation）。宪法说到“人民”时，不是把他们当作法人团体，而是当作个人。法人团体不会自称为“我们”，不会自称为“人民”，也不会自称为“我们自己”。而且在法律语言中，法人团体也没有什么“后代”。它以单一的个体身份，设想自己拥有、并且自称拥有永续的存在。

===Original===
Moreover, no body of men, existing at any one time, have the power to create a perpetual corporation. A corporation can become practically perpetual only by the voluntary accession of new members, as the old ones die off. But for this voluntary accession of new members, the corporation necessarily dies with the death of those who originally composed it.

===Chinese===
再者，任何一个时代在世的人群，都无权创立一个永续的法人团体。法人团体唯有靠新成员的自愿加入——旧成员凋零之时新人接续——才能在实际上臻于永续。若没有新成员的这种自愿加入，法人团体必然随其原初组成者的死亡而消亡。

===Original===
Legally speaking, therefore, there is, in the Constitution, nothing that professes or attempts to bind the “posterity” of those who established it.

===Chinese===
因此，从法律上说，宪法中没有任何内容自称或试图约束其制定者的“后代”。

===Original===
If, then, those who established the Constitution, had no power to bind, and did not attempt to bind, their posterity, the question arises, whether their posterity have bound themselves? If they have done so, they can have done so in only one or both of these two ways, viz. by voting, and paying taxes.

===Chinese===
既然宪法的制定者无权约束、也未曾试图约束他们的后代，那么问题便是：他们的后代是否已约束了自己？倘若确已如此，那也只能通过以下两种方式之一或兼而有之做到，即：投票，与纳税。

### II / 二

===Original===
Let us consider these two matters, voting and tax paying, separately. And first of voting.

===Chinese===
让我们把投票与纳税这两件事分开来考察。先说投票。

===Original===
All the voting that has ever taken place under the Constitution, has been of such a kind that it not only did not pledge the whole people to support the Constitution, but it did not even pledge any one of them to do so, as the following considerations show.

===Chinese===
迄今在宪法之下发生的一切投票，都具有这样的性质：它不仅没有使全体人民作出支持宪法的承诺（pledge），甚至没有使他们当中的任何一个人作出这种承诺，下面的考量可以表明这一点。

===Original===
1. In the very nature of things, the act of voting could bind nobody but the actual voters. But owing to the property qualifications required, it is probable that, during the first twenty or thirty years under the Constitution, not more than one tenth, fifteenth, or perhaps twentieth of the whole population (black and white, men, women, and minors) were permitted to vote. Consequently, so far as voting was concerned, not more than one tenth, fifteenth, or twentieth of those then existing, could have incurred any obligation to support the Constitution.

===Chinese===
1. 按事物的本性，投票行为只能约束实际投票者本人。但由于财产资格的限制，在宪法之下头二三十年间，全部人口（黑人白人、男男女女及未成年人）中获准投票者，恐怕不超过十分之一、十五分之一，甚或二十分之一。因此，就投票而言，当时在世的人中，至多只有十分之一、十五分之一或二十分之一，可能负有支持宪法的任何义务。

===Original===
At the present time, it is probable that not more than one sixth of the whole population are *permitted* to vote. Consequently, so far as voting is concerned, the other five-sixths can have given no pledge that they will support the Constitution.

===Chinese===
时至今日，全部人口中获*准*投票者，恐怕不超过六分之一。因此，就投票而言，其余六分之五的人不可能作出过支持宪法的任何承诺。

===Original===
2. Of the one-sixth that are *permitted* to vote, probably not more than two-thirds (about one-ninth of the whole population) have *usually* voted. Many never vote at all. Many vote only once in two, three, five, or ten years, in periods of great excitement.

===Chinese===
2. 在获*准*投票的六分之一当中，*通常*实际投票的，恐怕不超过三分之二（约为全部人口的九分之一）。许多人从不投票。许多人只在群情激奋的时期，每隔两年、三年、五年或十年才投一次票。

===Original===
No one, by voting, can be said to pledge himself for any longer period than that for which he votes. If, for example, I vote for an officer who is to hold his office for only a year, I cannot be said to have thereby pledged myself to support the government beyond that term. Therefore, on the ground of actual voting, it probably cannot be said that more than one-ninth, or one-eighth, of the whole population are *usually* under any pledge to support the Constitution.

===Chinese===
没有人能够说，通过投票，他作出的承诺超出了他所投票的那个任期。举例来说，如果我投票选出一名任期仅一年的官员，就不能说我因此承诺在该任期之外继续支持政府。因此，就实际投票而论，恐怕不能说全部人口中有超过九分之一或八分之一的人*通常*处于支持宪法的任何承诺之下。

===Original===
3. It cannot be said that, by voting, a man pledges himself to support the Constitution, unless the act of voting be a perfectly voluntary one on his part. Yet the act of voting cannot properly be called a voluntary one on the part of any very large number of those who do vote. It is rather a measure of necessity imposed upon them by others, than one of their own choice. On this point I repeat what was said in a former number,1 viz.:

===Chinese===
3. 除非投票是一个人完全自愿的行为，否则不能说一个人通过投票承诺支持宪法。然而，在确实投票的那些人当中，相当多的人的投票行为，说不上是自愿的。它毋宁是他人强加给他们的一种迫不得已之举，而非他们自己的选择。关于这一点，我重申本系列前一册1中说过的话，即：

===Original===
> “In truth, in the case of individuals, their actual voting is not to be taken as proof of consent, *even for the time being*. On the contrary, it is to be considered that, without his consent having even been asked, a man finds himself environed by a government that he cannot resist; a government that forces him to pay money, render service, and forego the exercise of many of his natural rights, under peril of weighty punishments. He sees, too, that other men practise this tyranny over him by the use of the ballot. He sees further, that, if he will but use the ballot himself, he has some chance of relieving himself from this tyranny of others, by subjecting them to his own. In short, he finds himself, without his consent, so situated that, if he use the ballot, he may become a master; if he does not use it, he must become a slave. And he has no other alternative than these two. In self-defence, he attempts the former. His case is analogous to that of a man who has been forced into battle, where he must either kill others, or be killed himself. Because, to save his own life in battle, a man attempts to take the lives of his opponents, it is not to be inferred that the battle is one of his own choosing. Neither in contests with the ballot﻿—which is a mere substitute for a bullet﻿—because, as his only chance of self-preservation, a man uses a ballot, is it to be inferred that the contest is one into which he voluntarily entered; that he voluntarily set up all his own natural rights, as a stake against those of others, to be lost or won by the mere power of numbers. On the contrary, it is to be considered that, in an exigency into which he had been forced by others, and in which no other means of self-defence offered, he, as a matter of necessity, used the only one that was left to him.

===Chinese===
> “实际上，就个人而论，其实际投票不能被当作同意的证据，*哪怕是暂时的同意*。恰恰相反，应当看到：一个人甚至从未被征求过同意，就发现自己已为一个他无法抗拒的政府所包围；这个政府逼迫他出钱、服役、放弃行使自己的许多自然权利，违者有重罚之虞。他还看到，别人正是利用选票对他实行这种暴政。他进而看到，只要他自己也使用选票，他就有几分机会使别人屈从于自己的暴政，从而把自己从别人的暴政之下解救出来。总之，他发现自己未经同意便身处这样的境地：使用选票，他可能成为主人；不使用选票，他必定沦为奴隶。而除此之外，他别无选择。出于自卫，他尝试前者。他的处境，类似于一个被迫上阵的人，在那里他要么杀人，要么被杀。一个人在战场上为保全自己的性命而试图取对手的性命，由此不能推论说，这场战斗是他自己选择的。同样，在与选票的较量中——选票不过是子弹的替代品——一个人把使用选票当作自我保存的唯一机会，由此也不能推论说，这场较量是他自愿投入的；不能说他自愿把自己的一切自然权利都押作赌注，与他人的权利对赌，任凭人数多寡的力量来决定输赢。恰恰相反，应当看到：在一个被他人逼入、且别无其他自卫手段可用的危急境地中，他迫不得已，使用了唯一留给他的手段。

===Original===
> “Doubtless the most miserable of men, under the most oppressive government in the world, if allowed the ballot, would use it, if they could see any chance of thereby meliorating their condition. But it would not, therefore, be a legitimate inference that the government itself, that crushes them, was one which they had voluntarily set up, or ever consented to.

===Chinese===
> “无疑，世上最受压迫的政府之下的最悲惨的人们，倘若获准持有选票，只要能由此看到改善自身处境的一线机会，也会使用它。但由此并不能正当地推论：那个把他们碾碎的政府本身，就是他们自愿建立的，或是他们曾经同意过的。

===Original===
> “Therefore, a man’s voting under the Constitution of the United States, is not to be taken as evidence that he ever freely assented to the Constitution, *even for the time being*. Consequently we have no proof that any very large portion, even of the actual voters of the United States, ever really and voluntarily consented to the Constitution, *even for the time being*. Nor can we ever have such proof, until every man is left perfectly free to consent, or not, without thereby subjecting himself or his property to be disturbed or injured by others.”

===Chinese===
> “因此，一个人在美利坚合众国宪法之下的投票，不能被当作他曾自由赞同宪法的证据，*哪怕是暂时的赞同*。因此，我们没有任何证据证明：即便是美利坚合众国的实际选民当中，有任何很大一部分人曾经真正地、自愿地同意过宪法，*哪怕是暂时地同意*。我们也永远不可能有这样的证据，除非每个人都全然自由地决定同意与否，而不致因此使自己或自己的财产遭到他人的侵扰或损害。”

===Original===
As we can have no legal knowledge as to who votes from choice, and who from the necessity thus forced upon him, we can have no legal knowledge, *as to any particular individual*, that he voted from choice; or, consequently, that by voting, he consented, or pledged himself, to support the government. Legally speaking, therefore, the act of voting utterly fails to pledge anyone to support the government. It utterly fails to prove that the government rests upon the voluntary support of anybody. On general principles of law and reason, it cannot be said that the government has any voluntary supporters at all, until it can be distinctly shown *who* its voluntary supporters are.

===Chinese===
由于我们无法在法律上知道，谁是出于选择而投票，谁是出于这样被强加的不得已而投票，我们便无法在法律上知道*任何一个特定的人*是出于选择而投票；从而也无法知道，他通过投票同意了政府，或承诺支持政府。因此，从法律上说，投票行为完全不能使任何人承担支持政府的承诺。它完全不能证明政府建立在任何人的自愿支持之上。按照法律与理性的一般原则，在能够明确指明*谁*是政府的自愿支持者之前，不能说政府有任何自愿的支持者。

===Original===
4. As taxation is made compulsory on all, whether they vote or not, a large proportion of those who vote, no doubt do so to prevent their own money being used against themselves; when, in fact, they would have gladly abstained from voting, if they could thereby have saved themselves from taxation alone, to say nothing of being saved from all the other usurpations and tyrannies of the government. To take a man’s property without his consent, and then to infer his consent because he attempts, by voting, to prevent that property from being used to his injury, is a very insufficient proof of his consent to support the Constitution. It is, in fact, no proof at all. And as we can have no legal knowledge as to *who* the particular individuals are, if there are any, who are willing to be taxed for the sake of voting, or who would prefer freedom from taxation to the privilege of voting, we can have no legal knowledge that any particular individual consents to be taxed for the sake of voting; or, consequently, consents to support the Constitution.

===Chinese===
4. 既然征税对一切人都是强制的，不论其投票与否，那么投票者当中无疑有相当大一部分人之所以投票，只是为了防止自己的钱被用来对付自己；而事实上，倘若仅仅不投票就能使他们免于纳税——更不用说免于政府的其他一切篡夺（usurpation）与暴政——他们会乐于放弃投票。不经一个人的同意就夺走他的财产，然后仅仅因为他试图通过投票防止那份财产被用来损害自己，就推定他同意——这作为他同意支持宪法的证据，是极不充分的。事实上，这根本不是证据。而且，既然我们无法在法律上知道，究竟*谁*——如果确有其人的话——是为了投票而甘愿纳税，或者宁愿免于纳税也不要投票的特权，我们便无法在法律上知道任何一个特定的人为了投票而同意纳税；从而也无法知道他同意支持宪法。

===Original===
5. At nearly all elections, votes are given for various candidates for the same office. Those who vote for the unsuccessful candidates cannot properly be said to have voted to sustain the Constitution. They may, with more reason, be supposed to have voted, not to support the Constitution, but specially to prevent the tyranny which they anticipate the successful candidate intends to practise upon them under color of the Constitution; and therefore may reasonably be supposed to have voted against the Constitution itself. This supposition is the more reasonable, inasmuch as such voting is the only mode allowed to them of expressing their dissent to the Constitution.

===Chinese===
5. 几乎在一切选举中，人们都为同一职位的各个不同候选人投票。把票投给落选者的人，说不上是投票支持宪法。更有理由设想，他们投票不是支持宪法，而恰恰是为了防止他们所预料的、当选者打算假宪法之名施加于他们的暴政；因此，可以合理地设想他们是投票反对宪法本身的。这一设想之所以愈加合理，还因为这样的投票是容许他们表达对宪法之异议的唯一方式。

===Original===
6. Many votes are usually given for candidates who have no prospect of success. Those who give such votes may reasonably be supposed to have voted as they did, with a special intention, not to support, but to obstruct the execution of, the Constitution; and, therefore, against the Constitution itself.

===Chinese===
6. 通常也有许多票投给毫无当选希望的候选人。可以合理地设想，投这种票的人正是有意为之：不是要支持宪法的实施，而是要阻挠宪法的实施；因而也就是反对宪法本身。

===Original===
7. As all the different votes are given secretly (by secret ballot), there is no legal means of knowing, from the votes themselves, *who* votes for, and *who* against, the Constitution. Therefore voting affords no legal evidence that any *particular individual* supports the Constitution. And where there can be no legal evidence that *any particular individual* supports the Constitution, it cannot legally be said that anybody supports it. It is clearly impossible to have any legal proof of the intentions of large numbers of men, where there can be no legal proof of the intentions of any particular one of them.

===Chinese===
7. 既然所有这些不同的选票都是秘密投出的（即以秘密投票〔secret ballot〕方式），那么从选票本身，就无法以法律上的手段知道*谁*投了赞成宪法之票，*谁*投了反对宪法之票。因此，投票不能提供任何法律证据，证明任何一个*特定的人*支持宪法。而在无法有法律证据证明*任何一个特定的人*支持宪法的地方，在法律上便不能说有任何人在支持它。显然，倘若连任何一个特定的人的意图都无法有法律上的证明，那么众人的意图就更不可能有法律上的证明。

===Original===
8. There being no legal proof of any man’s intentions, in voting, we can only conjecture them. As a conjecture, it is probable that a very large proportion of those who vote, do so on this principle, viz., that if, by voting, they could but get the government into their own hands (or that of their friends), and use its powers against their opponents, they would then willingly support the Constitution; but if their opponents are to have the power, and use it against them, then they would *not* willingly support the Constitution.

===Chinese===
8. 既然任何人的投票意图都没有法律上的证明，我们便只能加以猜测。作为猜测，恐怕投票者中有很大一部分人是本着这样一个原则行事的，即：倘若通过投票，他们能把政府抓到自己（或自己朋友）手中，并利用政府权力对付其对手，他们那时将乐于支持宪法；但倘若权力将归于其对手，并被用来对付他们，那么他们就*不*乐于支持宪法。

===Original===
In short, men’s voluntary support of the Constitution is doubtless, in most cases, wholly contingent upon the question whether, by means of the Constitution, they can make themselves masters, or are to be made slaves.

===Chinese===
简言之，人们对宪法的自愿支持，无疑在多数情况下全然取决于这样一个问题：凭借宪法，他们能使自己成为主人，还是将沦为他人的奴隶。

===Original===
Such contingent consent as that is, in law and reason, no consent at all.

===Chinese===
如此视情形而定的同意，在法律与理性上，全然不是同意。

===Original===
9. As everybody who supports the Constitution by voting (if there are any such) does so secretly (by secret ballot), and in a way to avoid all personal responsibility for the acts of his agents or representatives, it cannot legally or reasonably be said that anybody at all supports the Constitution by voting. No man can reasonably or legally be said to do such a thing as to assent to, or support, the Constitution, *unless he does it openly, and in a way to make himself personally responsible for the acts of his agents, so long as they act within the limits of the power he delegates to them*.

===Chinese===
9. 既然凡通过投票支持宪法的人（如果确有其人）都是秘密行事的（以秘密投票方式），并且其行事方式旨在规避对其代理人或代表的行为的一切个人责任，那么在法律上、在道理上便不能说有任何人在通过投票支持宪法。一个人赞同宪法或支持宪法，*除非他公开行事，并且以其行事方式使自己对代理人的行为承担个人责任——只要代理人是在他所授予权力的范围之内行事*，否则无论在道理上还是法律上，都不能说他做了这样的事。

===Original===
10. As all voting is secret, (by secret ballot,) and as all secret governments are necessarily only secret bands of robbers, tyrants, and murderers, the general fact that our government is practically carried on by means of such voting, only proves that there is among us a secret band of robbers, tyrants and murderers, whose purpose is to rob, enslave, and, so far as necessary to accomplish their purposes, murder, the rest of the people. The simple fact of the existence of such a band does nothing towards proving that “the people of the United States,” or any one of them, voluntarily supports the Constitution.

===Chinese===
10. 既然一切投票都是秘密的（以秘密投票方式），而一切秘密的政府都必然只是秘密的强盗、暴君和杀人犯团伙，那么我们的政府实际上靠这种投票来运转这一笼统事实，便只能证明：我们当中存在一个秘密的强盗、暴君和杀人犯团伙，其目的在于抢劫、奴役其余的人民，并在为达成其目的所必需的限度内杀害他们。这样一个团伙的存在，这一单纯的事实，丝毫不能证明“美利坚合众国人民”——或他们当中的任何一个人——在自愿支持宪法。

===Original===
For all the reasons that have now been given, voting furnishes no legal evidence as to who the particular individuals are (if there are any), who *voluntarily* support the Constitution. It therefore furnishes no legal evidence that anybody supports it *voluntarily*.

===Chinese===
基于现已提出的全部理由，投票不能就*自愿*支持宪法的特定个人是谁（如果确有其人）提供任何法律证据。因此，它也不能提供任何法律证据，证明有任何人在*自愿*支持宪法。

===Original===
So far, therefore, as voting is concerned, the Constitution, legally speaking, has no supporters at all.

===Chinese===
因此，就投票而论，从法律上说，宪法根本没有任何支持者。

===Original===
And, as matter of fact, there is not the slightest probability that the Constitution has a single bona fide supporter in the country. That is to say, there is not the slightest probability that there is a single man in the country, who both understands what the Constitution really is, *and sincerely supports it for what it really is*.

===Chinese===
而事实上，宪法在这个国家哪怕有一个真诚的（bona fide）支持者，其可能性也微乎其微。也就是说，这个国家哪怕存在这样一个人——他既明白宪法实际上是什么，*又为宪法实际上的样子而真诚支持它*——其可能性也微乎其微。

===Original===
The ostensible supporters of the Constitution, like the ostensible supporters of most other governments, are made up of three classes, viz.: 1. Knaves, a numerous and active class, who see in the government an instrument which they can use for their own aggrandizement or wealth. 2. Dupes﻿—a large class, no doubt﻿—each of whom, because he is allowed one voice out of millions in deciding what he may do with his own person and his own property, and because he is permitted to have the same voice in robbing, enslaving, and murdering others, that others have in robbing, enslaving, and murdering himself, is stupid enough to imagine that he is a “free man,” a “sovereign”; that this is “a free government”; “a government of equal rights,” “the best government on earth,”2 and suchlike absurdities. 3. A class who have some appreciation of the evils of government, but either do not see how to get rid of them, or do not choose to so far sacrifice their private interests as to give themselves seriously and earnestly to the work of making a change.

===Chinese===
宪法表面上的支持者，如同大多数其他政府表面上的支持者一样，由三类人构成，即：1. 无赖，人数众多而活跃的一类，他们在政府身上看到一种可以用来为自己谋取权势或财富的工具。2. 受骗者——无疑人数庞大的一类——他们当中的每一个人，因为在决定如何处置自己的人身和自己的财产时，被允许在数百万人中拥有一票；因为别人在抢劫、奴役、谋杀他时有一票，他也被允许在抢劫、奴役、谋杀别人时拥有同样的一票，便愚蠢到想象自己是“自由人”，是“主权者”（sovereign）；想象这是“一个自由的政府”、“一个权利平等的政府”、“世界上最好的政府”2，以及诸如此类的胡话。3. 还有一类人对政府的祸害有所认识，但要么看不出如何铲除这些祸害，要么不愿把自己的私人利益牺牲到那种程度，去严肃而切实地献身于促成变革的工作。

### III / 三

===Original===
The payment of taxes, being compulsory, of course furnishes no evidence that anyone voluntarily supports the Constitution.

===Chinese===
纳税既是强制的，当然不能提供任何证据，证明有人在自愿支持宪法。

===Original===
It is true that the *theory* of our Constitution is, that all taxes are paid voluntarily; that our government is a mutual insurance company, voluntarily entered into by the people with each other; that each man makes a free and purely voluntary contract with all others who are parties to the Constitution, to pay so much money for so much protection, the same as he does with any other insurance company; and that he is just as free not to be protected, and not to pay any tax, as he is to pay a tax, and be protected.

===Chinese===
诚然，我们宪法的*理论*是说：一切税收都是自愿缴纳的；我们的政府是一个相互保险公司（mutual insurance company），由人民彼此自愿结成；每个人都与所有其他宪法当事人订立一项自由的、纯粹自愿的契约，交若干钱，买若干保护，一如他与其他任何保险公司打交道；而且，他不受保护、不缴任何税的自由，与他纳税受保护的自由，恰好相当。

===Original===
But this theory of our government is wholly different from the practical fact. The fact is that the government, like a highwayman, says to a man: *Your money, or your life.* And many, if not most, taxes are paid under the compulsion of that threat.

===Chinese===
但我们政府的这一理论与实际情形全然不同。实际情形是：政府像拦路劫匪（highwayman）一样，对一个人说：*要钱，还是要命。*许多税收——即便不是大多数——正是在这一威胁的胁迫下缴纳的。

===Original===
The government does not, indeed, waylay a man in a lonely place, spring upon him from the road side, and, holding a pistol to his head, proceed to rifle his pockets. But the robbery is none the less a robbery on that account; and it is far more dastardly and shameful.

===Chinese===
诚然，政府不会在僻静之处拦路，不会从路旁跃出，不会用手枪抵住一个人的脑袋然后搜掠他的衣袋。但抢劫并不因此就不再是抢劫；它反而更加卑怯、更加可耻。

===Original===
The highwayman takes solely upon himself the responsibility, danger, and crime of his own act. He does not pretend that he has any rightful claim to your money, or that he intends to use it for your own benefit. He does not pretend to be anything but a robber. He has not acquired impudence enough to profess to be merely a “protector,” and that he takes men’s money against their will, merely to enable him to “protect” those infatuated travellers, who feel perfectly able to protect themselves, or do not appreciate his peculiar system of protection. He is too sensible a man to make such professions as these. Furthermore, having taken your money, he leaves you, as you wish him to do. He does not persist in following you on the road, against your will; assuming to be your rightful “sovereign,” on account of the “protection” he affords you. He does not keep “protecting” you, by commanding you to bow down and serve him; by requiring you to do this, and forbidding you to do that; by robbing you of more money as often as he finds it for his interest or pleasure to do so; and by branding you as a rebel, a traitor, and an enemy to your country, and shooting you down without mercy, if you dispute his authority, or resist his demands. He is too much of a gentleman to be guilty of such impostures, and insults, and villainies as these. In short, he does not, in addition to robbing you, attempt to make you either his dupe or his slave.

===Chinese===
拦路劫匪把他行为的责任、危险和罪责，全都只揽在自己一人身上。他并不假装他对你的钱有什么正当的权利主张，也不假装他打算把这笔钱用于你自身的利益。除了强盗，他并不假装自己还是别的什么。他还没有无耻到这般地步，竟自称不过是个“保护者”，自称他违背人们的意愿拿走他们的钱，只是为了能够“保护”那些痴迷的旅人——这些人自认为完全有能力保护自己，要不就是不领他那一套独特保护体系的情。他是个明白人，说不出这样的表白。再者，拿了你的钱之后，他便如你所愿，离你而去。他不会违背你的意愿，执意在路上尾随你；不会凭借他提供给你的“保护”，僭称自己是你的正当“主权者”。他不会不停地“保护”你：命令你俯首帖耳、为他效劳；要求你做这个，禁止你做那个；一觉得于己有利或有趣，便又抢走你更多的钱；你若质疑他的权威、抗拒他的要求，便给你打上叛逆、叛国者、国家之敌的烙印，毫无怜悯地将你击倒。他是十足的绅士，干不出诸如此类的欺瞒、侮辱和恶行。总之，他在抢劫你之外，并不试图使你或是做他的受骗者，或是做他的奴隶。

===Original===
The proceedings of those robbers and murderers, who call themselves “the government,” are directly the opposite of these of the single highwayman.

===Chinese===
那些自称“政府”的强盗和杀人犯的所作所为，与这个孤身劫匪的行径恰成直接的反面。

===Original===
In the first place, they do not, like him, make themselves individually known; or, consequently, take upon themselves personally the responsibility of their acts. On the contrary, they secretly (by secret ballot) designate some one of their number to commit the robbery in their behalf, while they keep themselves practically concealed. They say to the person thus designated:

===Chinese===
首先，他们不像他那样公开自己的个人身份，从而也不像他那样亲自承担其行为的责任。恰恰相反，他们秘密地（以秘密投票方式）指定其当中的一个成员，代表他们去实施抢劫，而他们自己实际上隐匿不露。他们对这个被指定的人说：

===Original===
Go to A﻿—﻿— B﻿—﻿—, and say to him that “the government” has need of money to meet the expenses of protecting him and his property. If he presumes to say that he has never contracted with us to protect him, and that he wants none of our protection, say to him that that is our business, and not his; that we *choose* to protect him, whether he desires us to do so or not; and that we demand pay, too, for protecting him. If he dares to inquire who the individuals are, who have thus taken upon themselves the title of “the government,” and who assume to protect him, and demand payment of him, without his having ever made any contract with them, say to him that that, too, is our business, and not his; that we do not *choose* to make ourselves *individually* known to him; that we have secretly (by secret ballot) appointed you our agent to give him notice of our demands, and, if he complies with them, to give him, in our name, a receipt that will protect him against any similar demand *for the present year*. If he refuses to comply, seize and sell enough of his property to pay not only our demands, but all your own expenses and trouble beside. If he resists the seizure of his property, call upon the bystanders to help you (doubtless some of them will prove to be members of our band). If, in defending his property, he should kill any of our band who are assisting you, capture him at all hazards; charge him (in one of our courts) with murder; convict him, and hang him. If he should call upon his neighbors, or any others who, like him, may be disposed to resist our demands, and they should come in large numbers to his assistance, cry out that they are all rebels and traitors; that “our country” is in danger; call upon the commander of our hired murderers; tell him to quell the rebellion and “save the country,” cost what it may. Tell him to kill all who resist, though they should be hundreds of thousands; and thus strike terror into all others similarly disposed. See that the work of murder is thoroughly done, that we may have no further trouble of this kind hereafter. When these traitors shall have thus been taught our strength and our determination, they will be good loyal citizens for many years, and pay their taxes without a why or a wherefore.

===Chinese===
去找甲——乙——，对他说，“政府”需要钱，以支付保护他和他的财产的开销。倘若他竟敢说，他从未与我们订立过保护他的契约，他根本不要我们的保护，那就对他说，这是我们的事，不是他的事；我们*选择*保护他，不管他愿不愿意让我们保护；而且我们要求他为这种保护付费。倘若他胆敢追问，那些如此僭取“政府”名号的人、那些自命保护他并要他付钱的人究竟是些什么人——尽管他从未与他们订过任何契约——就对他说，这也是我们的事，不是他的事；我们*不*愿向他公开我们*个人的*身份；我们已秘密地（以秘密投票方式）任命你为我们的代理人，去把我们的要求通知他；倘若他照办，就以我们的名义给他一张收据，使他免于*本年度*内的任何同类索取。倘若他拒不照办，就查封并变卖他的足量财产，不仅要偿付我们的索求，还要偿付你自己的全部开销和辛劳。倘若他抗拒对其财产的查封，就呼喝旁观者助你一臂之力（他们当中无疑会有人被证明是我们团伙的成员）。倘若他在保卫自己的财产时杀死了我们团伙中助你之人，就不计任何风险将他擒获；在我们的一个法庭上以谋杀罪控告他；给他定罪，把他绞死。倘若他召唤他的邻人，或其他任何像他一样倾向于抗拒我们要求的人，而他们成群结队前来相助，就高呼他们全是叛逆和叛国者；“我们的国家”危在旦夕；召唤我们雇用的杀人犯统领，叫他平定叛乱，“拯救国家”，不惜任何代价。叫他杀尽一切抵抗者，哪怕他们数以十万计；借此杀一儆百，震慑所有心怀同类意向之人。要确保杀戮之事做得干净彻底，好叫我们日后不再有这等麻烦。等这些叛国者被如此教训过、见识了我们的力量与决心之后，他们就会在往后许多年里做恭顺的良民，不问缘由、不问所以地缴纳税款。

===Original===
It is under such compulsion as this that taxes, so called, are paid. And how much proof the payment of taxes affords, that the people *consent* to support “the government,” it needs no further argument to show.

===Chinese===
所谓税款，正是在这样的胁迫之下缴纳的。至于这种纳税究竟为民众*同意*支持“政府”提供了多少证明，已无须再费笔墨论证。

===Original===
2. Still another reason why the payment of taxes implies no consent, or pledge, to support the government, is that the tax payer does not know, and has no means of knowing, who the particular individuals are who compose “the government.” To him “the government” is a myth, an abstraction, an incorporeality, with which he can make no contract, and to which he can give no consent, and make no pledge. He knows it only through its pretended agents. “The government” itself he never sees. He knows indeed, by common report, that certain persons, of a certain age, are *permitted* to vote; and thus to *make* themselves parts of, or (if they choose) opponents of, the government, for the time being. But who of them do thus vote, and especially *how* each one votes (whether so as to aid or oppose the government), he does not know; the voting being all done secretly (by secret ballot). Who, therefore, practically compose “the government,” for the time being, he has no means of knowing. Of course he can make no contract with them, give them no consent, and make them no pledge. Of necessity, therefore, his paying taxes to them implies, on his part, no contract, consent, or pledge to support them﻿—that is, to support “the government,” or the Constitution.

===Chinese===
2. 纳税之所以不意味着支持政府的任何同意或承诺，还有一层理由：纳税人不知道、也无从知道，组成“政府”的究竟是哪些特定的人。对他来说，“政府”是一个神话，一种抽象，一个无形无体之物；他无法与它订立契约，无法对它表示同意，也无法向它作出承诺。他只是通过那些冒牌代理人认识它。“政府”本身，他从未见过。诚然，他凭传闻知道，某些达到一定年龄的人*获准*投票，从而*使*自己暂时成为政府的一分子，或者（如果他们愿意的话）成为政府的反对者。但他们当中谁这样投了票，尤其每一个人*如何*投票（是助益政府还是反对政府），他并不知道；因为投票全部是秘密进行的（以秘密投票方式）。那么，实际上暂时组成“政府”的究竟是哪些人，他便无从知道。他当然无法与之订立契约，无法表示同意，也无法作出承诺。因此，必然地，他向他们纳税，并不意味着他一方作出任何支持他们的契约、同意或承诺——也就是说，不意味着支持“政府”或宪法。

===Original===
3. Not knowing who the particular individuals are, who call themselves “the government,” the tax payer does not know whom he pays his taxes to. All he knows is that a man comes to him, representing himself to be the agent of “the government”﻿—that is, the agent of a secret band of robbers and murderers, who have taken to themselves the title of “the government,” and have determined to kill everybody who refuses to give them whatever money they demand. To save his life, he gives up his money to this agent. But as this agent does not make his principals individually known to the tax payer, the latter, after he has given up his money, knows no more who are “the government”﻿—that is, who were the robbers﻿—than he did before. To say, therefore, that by giving up his money to their agent, he entered into a voluntary contract with them, that he pledges himself to obey them, to support them, and to give them whatever money they should demand of him in the future, is simply ridiculous.

===Chinese===
3. 由于不知道自称“政府”的究竟是哪些特定的人，纳税人不知道自己是把税缴给了谁。他只知道有一个人来到他面前，自称是“政府”的代理人——也就是说，是一个秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙的代理人；这个团伙僭取了“政府”的名号，并决意杀死一切拒绝交出其所索金钱的人。为了活命，他把自己的钱交给了这个代理人。但由于这个代理人并不使其委托人以个人身份为纳税者所知，后者交出钱财之后，对于谁是“政府”——也就是说，谁是劫匪——仍与从前一样一无所知。因此，说什么他由于把钱交给了他们的代理人，就与他们缔结了一项自愿的契约，说他承诺服从他们、支持他们，并答应今后他们索要多少钱就给他们多少钱——这简直是荒谬绝伦。

===Original===
4. All political power, as it is called, rests practically upon this matter of money. Any number of scoundrels, having money enough to start with, can establish themselves as a “government;” because, with money, they can hire soldiers, and with soldiers extort more money; and also compel general obedience to their will. It is with government, as Caesar said it was in war, that money and soldiers mutually supported each other; that with money he could hire soldiers, and with soldiers extort money. So these villains, who call themselves governments, well understand that their power rests primarily upon money. With money they can hire soldiers, and with soldiers extort money. And, when their authority is denied, the first use they always make of money, is to hire soldiers to kill or subdue all who refuse them more money.

===Chinese===
4. 所谓的政治权力，实际上全落在这个钱的问题上。任何数目的恶棍，只要有足够的本钱起家，就可以把自己立为“政府”；因为有了钱，他们就能雇到士兵；有了士兵，就能勒索到更多的钱；而且还能强迫人们普遍服从其意志。政府之事，正如恺撒论战争所说：钱与士兵互相支撑；有了钱，他就能雇士兵；有了士兵，他就能勒索钱。这些自称为政府的恶棍，十分明白他们的权力首先立足于金钱。有了钱，他们就能雇士兵；有了士兵，他们就能勒索钱。而当他们的权威遭到否认时，他们对金钱的头一项用途，总是雇兵去杀死或制服一切拒绝再给他们钱的人。

===Original===
For this reason, whoever desires liberty, should understand these vital facts, viz.: 1. That every man who puts money into the hands of a “government” (so called), puts into its hands a sword which will be used against himself, to extort more money from him, and also to keep him in subjection to its arbitrary will. 2. That those who will take his money, without his consent, in the first place, will use it for his further robbery and enslavement, if he presumes to resist their demands in the future. 3. That it is a perfect absurdity to suppose that any body of men would ever take a man’s money without his consent, for any such object as they profess to take it for, viz., that of protecting him; for why should they wish to protect him, if he does not wish them to do so? To suppose that they would do so, is just as absurd as it would be to suppose that they would take his money without his consent, for the purpose of buying food or clothing for him, when he did not want it. 4. If a man wants “protection,” he is competent to make his own bargains for it; and nobody has any occasion to rob him, in order to “protect” him against his will. 5. That the only security men can have for their political liberty, consists in their keeping their money in their own pockets, until they have assurances, perfectly satisfactory to themselves, that it will be used as they wish it to be used, for their benefit, and not for their injury. 6. That no government, so called, can reasonably be trusted for a moment, or reasonably be supposed to have honest purposes in view, any longer than it depends wholly upon voluntary support.

===Chinese===
因此，凡渴望自由的人，都应当明白这些性命攸关的事实，即：1. 每一个把钱交到所谓“政府”手中的人，都是把一柄剑交到它手中，这柄剑将被用来对付他自己，向他勒索更多的钱，并且使他屈从于它的专断意志。2. 那些头一步不经他同意就夺走他的钱的人，倘若他日后胆敢抗拒他们的要求，就会用这笔钱来进一步抢劫他、奴役他。3. 设想有任何一群人会不经一个人同意就夺走他的钱，却是为了他们自称的那种目的——即保护他——那是十足的荒谬；因为倘若他并不希望他们保护，他们何以要保护他？设想他们会这样做，就如同设想他们会不经他同意就夺走他的钱，去为他买他并不想要的衣食，一样荒谬。4. 一个人倘若想要“保护”，他自有能力为自己的保护讨价还价；谁也没有必要为了“保护”他而去抢劫他，违背他的意愿。5. 人们政治自由的唯一保障，就在于把钱留在自己口袋里，直到他们得到令自己完全满意的担保：这笔钱将按他们所愿的方式使用，用于造福他们，而非加害他们。6. 任何一个所谓政府，只要不是全然依赖自愿的支持，便一刻也不能正当地加以信任，也不能正当地设想它怀有诚实的目的。

===Original===
These facts are all so vital and so self-evident, that it cannot reasonably be supposed that anyone will *voluntarily* pay money to a “government,” for the purpose of securing its protection, unless he first makes an explicit and purely voluntary contract with it for that purpose.

===Chinese===
这些事实全都如此紧要、如此不言自明，以致不能合理地设想：会有任何人为了换取政府的保护而*自愿*向一个“政府”付钱，除非他先为此与它订立一项明确的、纯粹自愿的契约。

===Original===
It is perfectly evident, therefore, that neither such voting, nor such payment of taxes, as actually takes place, proves anybody’s consent, or obligation, to support the Constitution. Consequently we have no evidence at all that the Constitution is binding upon anybody, or that anybody is under any contract or obligation whatever to support it. And nobody *is* under any obligation to support it.

===Chinese===
因此，再明显不过：无论是实际发生的这种投票，还是这种纳税，都不能证明任何人有支持宪法的同意或义务。因而我们完全没有证据表明，宪法对任何人具有约束力，或任何人负有支持它的任何契约或义务。而且事实上，也*的确*没有人负有任何支持它的义务。

### IV / 四

===Original===
*The Constitution not only binds nobody now, but it never did bind anybody.* It never bound anybody, because it was never agreed to by anybody in such a manner as to make it, on general principles of law and reason, binding upon him.

===Chinese===
*宪法不仅现在不约束任何人，而且从来不曾约束过任何人。*它从来不曾约束任何人，因为从来没有任何人以这样一种方式赞同过它，以致按照法律与理性的一般原则，它能对他具有约束力。

===Original===
It is a general principle of law and reason, that a *written* instrument binds no one until he has signed it. This principle is so inflexible a one, that even though a man is unable to write his name, he must still “make his mark,” before he is bound by a written contract. This custom was established ages ago, when few men could write their names; when a clerk﻿—that is, a man who could write﻿—was so rare and valuable a person, that even if he were guilty of high crimes, he was entitled to pardon, on the ground that the public could not afford to lose his services. Even at that time, a written contract must be signed; and men who could not write, either “made their mark,” or signed their contracts by stamping their seals upon wax affixed to the parchment on which their contracts were written. Hence the custom of affixing seals, that has continued to this time.

===Chinese===
法律与理性有一条一般原则：一份*书面*文书，在签署之前不约束任何人。这条原则铁定不移，以致一个人即便不会书写自己的姓名，也必须先“画押”（make his mark），方能受书面契约的约束。这一习俗确立于远古，那时很少有人会写自己的名字；那时，一个能书写的人——即文士（clerk）——是如此稀罕而宝贵的角色，以致即便身犯重罪，也有资格获得赦免，理由是公众承担不起失去其效劳的损失。就在那个时代，书面契约也必须签署；不会书写的人，或者“画押”，或者把印章钤于蜡上——蜡黏附在书写契约的羊皮纸上——以此签署契约。盖印的习俗由此相沿至今。

===Original===
The law holds, and reason declares, that if a written instrument is not signed, the presumption must be that the party to be bound by it, did not choose to sign it, or to bind himself by it. And law and reason both give him until the last moment, in which to decide whether he will sign it, or not. Neither law nor reason requires or expects a man to agree to an instrument, *until it is written*; for until it is written, he cannot know its precise legal meaning. And when it is written, and he has had the opportunity to satisfy himself of its precise legal meaning, he is then expected to decide, and not before, whether he will agree to it or not. And if he do not *then* sign it, his reason is supposed to be, that he does not choose to enter into such a contract. The fact that the instrument was written *for him to sign*, or with the hope that he would sign it, goes for nothing.

===Chinese===
法律认定，理性宣告：一份书面文书若未经签署，就必须推定（presumption）：受它约束的当事人不愿签署它，不愿以它约束自己。法律与理性都给他留到最后一刻，让他决定签署与否。*在文书写成之前*，法律与理性都不要求、也不期待一个人赞同它；因为文书未写成之时，他无从知道其确切的法律含义。而当文书已经写成，他也有机会弄清其确切的法律含义之后，人们才期待他作出决定——而非在此之前——赞同与否。倘若他*那时*不签署，就应当认为他的理由是：他不愿订立这样的契约。文书是为*让他签署*而写成，或怀着他会签署的希望而写成——这一事实无关紧要。

===Original===
Where would be the end of fraud and litigation, if one party could bring into court a written instrument, *without any signature*, and claim to have it enforced, upon the ground that it was written for another man to sign? that this other man had promised to sign it? that he ought to have signed it? that he had had the opportunity to sign it, if he would? but that he had refused or neglected to do so? yet that is the most that could ever be said of the Constitution.3 The very judges, who profess to derive all their authority from the Constitution﻿—from an instrument that nobody ever signed﻿—would spurn any other instrument, not signed, that should be brought before them for adjudication.

===Chinese===
倘若一方当事人可以把一份*没有任何签名*的书面文书提交法庭，并主张予以强制执行，理由是它是写给另一个人签署的？是那个人答应过签署？是他本应当签署？是他本来有机会签署，只要他愿意？只是他拒绝或怠于签署？——那么，欺诈与争讼何日是了？然而，关于宪法，人们所能说出的至多也就是这些。3那些自称其全部权威皆源于宪法的法官们——源于一份从未有人签署的文书——却会把任何其他未经签署而呈请裁断的文书弃如敝屣。

===Original===
Moreover, a written instrument must, in law and reason, not only be signed, but must also be delivered to the party (or to someone for him), in whose favor it is made, before it can bind the party making it. The signing is of no effect, unless the instrument be also delivered. And a party is at perfect liberty to refuse to deliver a written instrument, after he has signed it. He is as free to refuse to deliver it, as he is to refuse to sign it. The constitution was not only never signed by anybody, but it was never delivered by anybody to anybody, or to anybody’s agent or attorney. It can therefore be of no more validity as a contract, than can any other instrument, that was never signed or delivered.

===Chinese===
再者，按照法律与理性，一份书面文书不仅要经签署，还必须交付（deliver）给文书所惠及的一方（或代其收受的人），方能约束制作文书的当事人。签署并不生效力，除非文书同时已经交付。当事人在签署之后，仍然全然可以自由地拒绝交付。他拒绝交付的自由，与他拒绝签署的自由一样。宪法不仅从未经任何人签署，也从未经任何人交付给任何人，或任何人的代理人或受托人。因此，作为契约，它的效力不会比任何其他从未签署、从未交付的文书更多一分。

### V / 五

===Original===
As further evidence of the general sense of mankind, as to the practical necessity there is that all men’s *important* contracts, especially those of a permanent nature, should be both written and signed, the following facts are pertinent.

===Chinese===
人类的普遍见识认为：人们的一切*重要*契约，尤其是那些具有长久性质的契约，都必须既写成书面又经签署，这在实践上实属必要。下列事实可为这种普遍见识提供进一步的证据。

===Original===
For nearly two hundred years﻿—that is, since 1677﻿—there has been on the statute book of England, and the same, in substance, if not precisely in letter, has been reenacted, and is now in force, in nearly or quite all the States of this Union, a statute, the general object of which is to declare that no action shall be brought to enforce contracts of the more important class, *unless they are put in writing, and signed by the parties to be held chargeable upon them*.4

===Chinese===
近两百年来——亦即自 1677 年以来——英格兰的制定法汇编（statute book）上一直载有一项制定法（statute）；本联邦的各州几乎——乃至全然——都以其要旨相同（纵使字句未必尽同）的形式重新颁布了这部法律，至今施行不辍。这部法律的总的宗旨是宣告：较重要一类的契约，*除非已写成书面，并经应受其约束的当事人签署*，不得提起诉讼请求强制执行。4

===Original===
The principle of the statute, be it observed, is, not merely that written contracts shall be signed, but also that all contracts, except those specially exempted﻿—generally those that are for small amounts, and are to remain in force but for a short time﻿—*shall be both written and signed*.

===Chinese===
应当看到，这部法律的原则，不只是说书面契约须经签署，而且是说一切契约——专门豁免者除外，通常即那些数额甚小、只须短时存续的契约——*都必须既写成书面又经签署*。

===Original===
The reason of the statute, on this point, is, that it is now so easy a thing for men to put their contracts in writing, and sign them, and their failure to do so opens the door to so much doubt, fraud, and litigation, that men who neglect to have their contracts﻿—of any considerable importance﻿—written and signed, ought not to have the benefit of courts of justice to enforce them. And this reason is a wise one; and that experience has confirmed its wisdom and necessity, is demonstrated by the fact that it has been acted upon in England for nearly two hundred years, and has been so nearly universally adopted in this country, and that nobody thinks of repealing it.

===Chinese===
这部法律就此所持的理由是：如今人们把契约写成书面并加签署，已属易事；而不这样做，则会为疑义、欺诈与争讼敞开大门，以致凡怠于把自己稍具重要性的契约写成书面并加签署的人，便不应再享有法庭为其强制执行的便利。这个理由是明智的；经验已证实了它的明智与必要，其明证便是：英格兰奉行它近两百年，这个国家也几乎普遍采纳了它，而且无人想到要废除它。

===Original===
We all know, too, how careful most men are to have their contracts written and signed, even when this statute does not require it. For example, most men, if they have money due them, of no larger amount than five or ten dollars, are careful to take a note for it. If they buy even a small bill of goods, paying for it at the time of delivery, they take a receipted bill for it. If they pay a small balance of a book account, or any other small debt previously contracted, they take a written receipt for it.

===Chinese===
我们也都知道，即使这部法律并未作此要求，大多数人对契约之写成书面并经签署，也是何等小心。例如，大多数人即使别人只欠他们五元十元，也谨慎地索取一张借据（note）。即使购买一纸小小的货单、在交货时当场付清，也要取得一张经签收的账单。即使偿付账簿账目上的小额结欠，或任何其他先前结下的小额债务，也要取得一纸书面收据。

===Original===
Furthermore, the law everywhere (probably) in our country, as well as in England, requires that a large class of contracts, such as wills, deeds, etc., shall not only be written and signed, but also sealed, witnessed, and acknowledged. And in the case of married women conveying their rights in real estate, the law, in many States, requires that the women shall be examined separate and apart from their husbands, and declare that they sign their contracts free of any fear or compulsion of their husbands.

===Chinese===
此外，在我国各地——大概一如在英格兰——法律要求一大类契约，如遗嘱、契据（deeds）等等，不仅要写成书面并经签署，还须盖印、见证并认证（acknowledged）。而已婚妇女让与其不动产上的权利时，许多州的法律要求把妇女与其丈夫分开、单独加以讯问，并令其声明：她们签署契约，未受丈夫的任何恐吓或强制。

===Original===
Such are some of the precautions which the laws require, and which individuals﻿—from motives of common prudence, even in cases not required by law﻿—take, to put their contracts in writing, and have them signed, etc., to guard against all uncertainties and controversies in regard to their meaning and validity. And yet we have what purports, or professes, or is claimed, to be a contract﻿—the Constitution﻿—made eighty years ago, by men who are now all dead, and who never had any power to bind *us*, but which (it is claimed) has nevertheless bound three generations of men, consisting of many millions, and which (it is claimed) will be binding upon all the millions that are to come; but which nobody ever signed, sealed, delivered, witnessed, or acknowledged; and which few persons, compared with the whole number that are claimed to be bound by it, have ever read, or even seen, or ever will read, or see. And of those who ever have read it, or ever will read it, scarcely any two, perhaps no two, have ever agreed, or ever will agree, as to what it means.

===Chinese===
法律要求种种防范，个人出于寻常审慎的动机——即便在法律并不要求的场合——也采取种种防范，把契约写成书面、加以签署等等，以防在契约的含义与效力上发生一切不确定与争执。然而我们却有一份自称、自命或被说成是契约的东西——宪法——它订立于八十年前，出自一批如今已全部作古的人之手，这些人从来没有任何约束*我们*的权力；而它（据说）却已约束了数以百万计的三代人，并且（据说）还将约束行将出现的千百万后人；可是从来没有人签署过它、盖印过它、交付过它、见证过它或认证过它；而且，与据说受它约束的总人数相比，只有寥寥无几的人读过它，甚至见过它；将来会读它、见它的人也为数寥寥。而在读过或将要读它的人当中，几乎没有哪两个人——或许根本没有哪两个人——曾经、或将会就它的含义达成一致。

===Original===
Moreover, this supposed contract, which would not be received in any court of justice sitting under its authority, if offered to prove a debt of five dollars, owing by one man to another, is one by which﻿—*as it is generally interpreted by those who pretend to administer it*﻿—all men, women and children throughout the country, and through all time, surrender not only all their property, but also their liberties, and even lives, into the hands of men who by this supposed contract, are expressly made wholly irresponsible for their disposal of them. And we are so insane, or so wicked, as to destroy property and lives without limit, in fighting to compel men to fulfil a supposed contract, which, inasmuch as it has never been signed by anybody, is, on general principles of law and reason﻿—such principles as we are all governed by in regard to other contracts﻿—the merest waste paper, binding upon nobody, fit only to be thrown into the fire; or, if preserved, preserved only to serve as a witness and a warning of the folly and wickedness of mankind.

===Chinese===
再者，这份所谓的契约，倘若被拿出来证明一人欠另一人五元钱的债务，在任何一座依其权威开庭的法庭上都不会被接受；然而，*按照那些僭居施政之职者的通行解释*，全国的男女老少，世世代代，都凭它把自己全部的财产，还有自己的自由乃至生命，交到一些人手中；而这份所谓的契约又明明白白地规定，这些人无论怎样处置它们，都全然不负责任。而我们竟疯狂到、或邪恶到这种地步：为了强迫人们履行这份所谓的契约而战，不惜无限度地毁灭财产与生命；这份契约，由于从未经任何人签署，按照法律与理性的一般原则——也就是我们在其他一切契约上人人受其支配的那些原则——不过是彻头彻尾的废纸，不约束任何人，只配扔进火里；即便保存下来，也只是让它充当一份见证与警示，见证并警醒人类的愚蠢与邪恶。

### VI / 六

===Original===
It is no exaggeration, but a literal truth, to say that, by the Constitution﻿—*not as I interpret it, but as it is interpreted by those who pretend to administer it*﻿—the properties, liberties, and lives of the entire people of the United States are surrendered unreservedly into the hands of men who, it is provided by the Constitution itself, shall never be “questioned” as to any disposal they make of them.

===Chinese===
这样说绝非夸张，而是字字属实：按照宪法——*不是按我的解释，而是按那些僭居施政之职者的解释*——美利坚合众国全体人民的财产、自由与生命，都被毫无保留地交到一些人手中；而宪法本身明文规定，这些人无论怎样处置它们，都永远不得受到“质询”（questioned）。

===Original===
Thus the Constitution (Art. 1, Sec. 6) provides that, “for any speech or debate [or vote,] in either house, they [the senators and representatives] shall not be questioned in any other place.”

===Chinese===
例如，宪法（第1条第6款）规定：“对于在任一院内所作的任何演讲或辩论［或所投的票，］他们［参议员和众议员］不得在任何其他地方受到质询。”

===Original===
The whole lawmaking power is given to these senators and representatives, when acting by a two-thirds vote;5 and this provision protects them from all responsibility for the laws they make.

===Chinese===
全部立法权都授予了这些参议员和众议员，只要他们以三分之二的票数共同行事即可；5而这一条款保护他们，使他们对自己制定的法律不负任何责任。

===Original===
The Constitution also enables them to secure the execution of all their laws, by giving them power to withhold the salaries of, and to impeach and remove, all judicial and executive officers, who refuse to execute them.

===Chinese===
宪法还使他们能够确保自己的一切法律获得执行，其办法是授给他们权力：对一切拒不执行其法律的司法与行政官员，扣发薪俸，并加以弹劾、罢免。

===Original===
Thus the whole power of the government is in their hands, and they are made utterly irresponsible for the use they make of it. What is this but absolute, irresponsible power?

===Chinese===
如此，政府的全部权力便落入他们手中，而他们对自己如何行使这种权力，全然不负责任。这不是绝对的、不负责任的权力，又是什么？

===Original===
It is no answer to this view of the case to say that these men are under oath to use their power only within certain limits; for what care they, or what should they care, for oaths or limits, when it is expressly provided, by the Constitution itself, that they shall never be “questioned,” or held to any responsibility whatever, for violating their oaths, or transgressing those limits?

===Chinese===
对这个看法，回答说这些人已宣誓只在一定限度之内行使其权力——这是不解决问题的；因为宪法本身明文规定：他们纵然违反誓言、逾越那些限度，也永远不得受到“质询”，永远不负任何责任；既然如此，他们对誓言或限度会在意什么？又应当在意什么？

===Original===
Neither is it any answer to this view of the case to say that the particular individuals holding this power can be changed once in two or six years; for the power of each set of men is absolute during the term for which they hold it; and when they can hold it no longer, they are succeeded only by men whose power will be equally absolute and irresponsible.

===Chinese===
对这个看法，回答说掌握这种权力的那些特定的人每两年或六年可以更换一次——这同样不解决问题；因为每一批人在其任职期间，权力都是绝对的；而当他们再也无法掌握权力时，接替他们的只是另一些人，其权力将同样绝对、同样不负责任。

===Original===
Neither is it any answer to this view of the case to say that the men holding this absolute, irresponsible power, must be men chosen by the people (or portions of them) to hold it. A man is none the less a slave because he is allowed to choose a new master once in a term of years. Neither are a people any the less slaves because permitted periodically to choose new masters. What makes them slaves is the fact that they now are, and are always hereafter to be, in the hands of men whose power over them is, and always is to be, absolute and irresponsible.6

===Chinese===
对这个看法，回答说掌握这种绝对而不负责任的权力的人，必定是由人民（或人民中的一部分）选出的人——这同样不解决问题。一个人即便每隔若干年被允许挑选一次新主人，也丝毫不会因此少一分奴隶的身份。人民即便被允许定期挑选新主人，也丝毫不会因此少一分奴隶的身份。使他们成为奴隶的，是这样一个事实：他们如今、并且将来永远，都落在一些人手中，而这些人对他们的权力现在是、并且将来永远是绝对而不负责任的。6

===Original===
The right of absolute and irresponsible dominion is the right of property, and the right of property is the right of absolute, irresponsible dominion. The two are identical; the one necessarily implying the other. Neither can exist without the other. If, therefore, Congress have that absolute and irresponsible lawmaking power, which the Constitution﻿—according to their interpretation of it﻿—gives them, it can only be because they own us as property. If they own us as property, they are our masters, and their will is our law. If they do not own us as property, they are not our masters, and their will, *as such*, is of no authority over us.

===Chinese===
绝对而不负责任的支配权就是财产权（the right of property），而财产权就是绝对而不负责任的支配权。二者同一，其一必然蕴含其二，无此则彼亦不存。因此，如果国会拥有宪法——按照他们对宪法的解释——所授予的那种绝对而不负责任的立法权，那就只能是因为他们把我们当作财产而据为己有。倘若他们把我们当作财产据为己有，他们就是我们的主人，他们的意志就是我们的法律。倘若他们并未把我们当作财产据为己有，他们就不是我们的主人，他们的意志*本身*对我们便没有任何权威。

===Original===
But these men who claim and exercise this absolute and irresponsible dominion over us, dare not be consistent, and claim either to be our masters, or to own us as property. They say they are only our servants, agents, attorneys, and representatives. But this declaration involves an absurdity, a contradiction. No man can be my servant, agent, attorney, or representative, and be, at the same time, uncontrollable by me, and irresponsible to me for his acts. It is of no importance that I appointed him, and put all power in his hands. If I made him uncontrollable by me, and irresponsible to me, he is no longer my servant, agent, attorney, or representative. If I gave him absolute, irresponsible power over my property, I gave him the property. If I gave him absolute, irresponsible power over myself, I made him my master, and gave myself to him as a slave. And it is of no importance whether I *called him* master or servant, agent or owner. The only question is, what power did I put into his hands? Was it an absolute and irresponsible one? or a limited and responsible one?

===Chinese===
然而，这些对我们宣称并行使着这种绝对而不负责任的支配权的人，却不敢贯彻始终，不敢宣称自己要么是我们的主人，要么把我们当作财产据为己有。他们说，他们不过是我们的仆人、代理人、受托人和代表。但这种声明本身就包含一个荒谬、一个矛盾。没有人能够既是我的仆人、代理人、受托人或代表，同时又不受我的控制、且对其行为不向我负责。我任命了他，把一切权力都放到他手中——这无关紧要。倘若我使他不受我的控制、不向我负责，他就不再是我的仆人、代理人、受托人或代表。倘若我给了他支配我财产的绝对而不负责任的权力，我就是把财产给了他。倘若我给了他支配我自身的绝对而不负责任的权力，我就是立他为我的主人，把我自己当作奴隶交给了他。至于我*称他*为主人还是仆人、代理人还是财产所有人，无关紧要。唯一的问题是：我放进他手中的是什么权力？是绝对的、不负责任的权力？还是有限的、负责任的权力？

===Original===
For still another reason they are neither our servants, agents, attorneys, nor representatives. And that reason is, that we do not make ourselves responsible for their acts. If a man is my servant, agent, or attorney, I necessarily make myself responsible for all his acts done within the limits of the power I have entrusted to him. If I have entrusted him, as my agent, with either absolute power, or any power at all, over the persons or properties of other men than myself, I thereby necessarily make myself responsible to those other persons for any injuries he may do them, so long as he acts within the limits of the power I have granted him. But no individual who may be injured in his person or property, by acts of Congress, can come to the individual electors, and hold them responsible for these acts of their so-called agents or representatives. This fact proves that these pretended agents of the people, of everybody, are really the agents of nobody.

===Chinese===
他们不是我们的仆人、代理人、受托人或代表，还有一层理由。这层理由就是：我们并没有让自己为他们的行为承担责任。倘若一个人是我的仆人、代理人或受托人，那么凡他在我所托付权力的范围之内作出的一切行为，我必然要为之负责。倘若我以代理人身份把权力——无论是绝对的权力，还是任何权力——托付给他，去支配我自身以外其他他人的人身或财产，那么只要他在我所授予权力的范围之内行事，我就必然要向那些其他人负责，赔偿他可能加给他们的任何损害。然而，任何因国会的行为而在人身或财产上受到损害的个人，都无法找到具体的选民个体，让他们为其所谓代理人或代表的这些行为承担责任。这一事实证明：这些以人民、以全体人人自居的冒牌代理人，实际上谁的代理人也不是。

===Original===
If, then, nobody is individually responsible for the acts of Congress, the members of Congress are nobody’s agents. And if they are nobody’s agents, they are themselves individually responsible for their own acts, and for the acts of all whom they employ. And the authority they are exercising is simply their own individual authority; and, by the law of nature﻿—the highest of all laws﻿—anybody injured by their acts, anybody who is deprived by them of his property or his liberty, has the same right to hold them individually responsible, that he has to hold any other trespasser individually responsible. He has the same right to resist them, and their agents, that he has to resist any other trespassers.

===Chinese===
因此，既然没有人为国会的行为承担个人责任，国会议员便不是任何人的代理人。而既然他们不是任何人的代理人，他们就必须以个人身份对自己的行为、并对他们所雇用的一切人的行为负责。他们正在行使的权威，纯粹是他们自己的个人权威；而且，按照自然法（the law of nature）——一切法律中的最高法律——凡因其行为受到损害的人，凡被他们夺去财产或自由的人，都有同样的权利让他们以个人身份承担责任，一如他有权让任何其他侵害者（trespasser）以个人身份承担责任。他有同样的权利抵抗他们及其代理人，一如他有权利抵抗任何其他侵害者。

### VII / 七

===Original===
It is plain, then, that on general principles of law and reason﻿—such principles as we all act upon in courts of justice and in common life﻿—the Constitution is no contract; that it binds nobody, and never did bind anybody; and that all those who pretend to act by its authority, are really acting without any legitimate authority at all; that, on general principles of law and reason, they are mere usurpers, and that everybody not only has the right, but is morally bound, to treat them as such.

===Chinese===
因此，显而易见：按照法律与理性的一般原则——也就是我们在法庭上、在日常生活中人人遵行的那类原则——宪法根本不是契约；它不约束任何人，也从来不曾约束过任何人；一切僭称凭其权威行事的人，实际上都在全无任何正当权威地行事；按照法律与理性的一般原则，他们不过是篡夺者（usurpers）；而且，人人不仅有权利、而且在道德上有义务，把他们当作篡夺者对待。

===Original===
If the people of this country wish to maintain such a government as the Constitution describes, there is no reason in the world why they should not sign the instrument itself, and thus make known their wishes in an open, authentic manner; in such manner as the common sense and experience of mankind have shown to be reasonable and necessary in such cases; *and in such manner as to make themselves (as they ought to do) individually responsible for the acts of the government*. But the people have never been asked to sign it. And the only reason why they have never been asked to sign it, has been that it has been known that they never would sign it; that they were neither such fools nor knaves as they must needs have been to be willing to sign it; that (at least as it has been practically interpreted) it is not what any sensible and honest man wants for himself; nor such as he has any right to impose upon others. It is, to all moral intents and purposes, as destitute of obligation as the compacts which robbers and thieves and pirates enter into with each other, but never sign.

===Chinese===
倘若这个国家的人民愿意维持宪法所描绘的这样一种政府，那么世上没有任何理由可以阻止他们签署这份文书本身，从而以公开、真实可靠的方式表明他们的意愿；以人类的常识与经验所表明的、在此类情形下合理而必要的方式；*并且以这样一种方式，使他们自己（正如他们理应做的那样）为政府的行为承担个人责任*。然而，人民从未被要求签署它。而人民之所以从未被要求签署它，唯一的原因就是：人们早已知道他们绝不会签署它；知道他们若愿意签署，就必须既是傻瓜又是无赖，而他们并非如此；（至少按其实际解释来看）它不是任何一个明理而诚实的人为自己想要的东西，也不是他有权强加给别人的东西。就一切道德意图与目的而言，它同强盗、窃贼和海盗彼此缔结却从不签署的那些约定（compact）一样，毫无约束力。

===Original===
If any considerable number of the people believe the Constitution to be good, why do they not sign it themselves, and make laws for, and administer them upon, each other; leaving all other persons (who do not interfere with them) in peace? Until they have tried the experiment for themselves, how can they have the face to impose the Constitution upon, or even to recommend it to, others? Plainly the reason for such absurd and inconsistent conduct is that they want the Constitution, not solely for any honest or legitimate use it can be of to themselves or others, but for the dishonest and illegitimate power it gives them over the persons and properties of others. But for this latter reason, all their eulogiums on the Constitution, all their exhortations, and all their expenditures of money and blood to sustain it, would be wanting.

===Chinese===
倘若人民中有相当数目的人相信宪法是好的，他们为什么不自己去签署它，彼此之间为它立法、依它治理，而让所有其他人（只要不干涉他们）安然自处？在他们亲自试验过之前，他们怎么有脸把宪法强加给别人，甚至把它推荐给别人？这种荒谬而自相矛盾的行为，原因显而易见：他们要宪法，不只是为了它对他们自己或别人可能有的任何诚实而正当的用途，更是为了它给予他们对他人人身和财产的那种不诚实、不正当的权力。倘若没有这后一层原因，他们对宪法的一切颂词、一切劝谕、一切为支撑它而付出的金钱与血，就统统不会有了。

### VIII / 八

===Original===
The Constitution itself, then, being of no authority, on what authority does our government practically rest? On what ground can those who pretend to administer it, claim the right to seize men’s property, to restrain them of their natural liberty of action, industry, and trade, and to kill all who deny their authority to dispose of men’s properties, liberties, and lives at their pleasure or discretion?

===Chinese===
那么，宪法本身既毫无权威，我们的政府实际上又是立足于什么权威之上？那些僭居施政之职的人，又能凭什么根据主张自己有权夺取人们的财产，限制人们行动、经营产业与贸易的自然自由，并且杀戮一切否认其有权随意、任意处置人们的财产、自由与生命的人？

===Original===
The most they can say, in answer to this question, is, that some half, two-thirds, or three-fourths of the male adults of the country have a *tacit understanding* that they will maintain a government under the Constitution; that they will select, by ballot, the persons to administer it; and that those persons who may receive a majority, or a plurality, of their ballots, shall act as their representatives, and administer the Constitution in their name, and by their authority.

===Chinese===
对于这个问题，他们至多只能回答说：这个国家的成年男性当中，约有二分之一、三分之二或四分之三的人怀有一种*默契*（tacit understanding），即他们将维持一个宪法之下的政府；他们将以投票选出施政人员；而获得他们过半数票或相对多数票的那些人，则充当他们的代表，以他们的名义、凭他们的权威施行政务。

===Original===
But this tacit understanding (admitting it to exist) cannot at all justify the conclusion drawn from it. A tacit understanding between A, B, and C, that they will, by ballot, depute D as their agent, to deprive me of my property, liberty, or life, cannot at all authorize D to do so. He is none the less a robber, tyrant, and murderer, because he claims to act as their agent, than he would be if he avowedly acted on his own responsibility alone.
===Chinese===
但这种默契（即便承认它存在）根本不能为由此得出的结论提供正当性。甲、乙、丙之间达成默契，约定以投票方式委托丁充当他们的代理人，来剥夺我的财产、自由或生命，这丝毫不能授权丁这样做。丁并不会因为自称作为他们的代理人行事，就比他公然宣称单凭自身责任行事时，更少一分强盗、暴君和杀人犯的属性。
===Original===
Neither am I bound to recognize him as their agent, nor can he legitimately claim to be their agent, when he brings no *written* authority from them accrediting him as such. I am under no obligation to take his word as to who his principals may be, or whether he has any. Bringing no credentials, I have a right to say he has no such authority even as he claims to have: and that he is therefore intending to rob, enslave, or murder me on his own account.
===Chinese===
我也没有义务承认他是他们的代理人；当他拿不出他们出具的*书面*授权来证明其这一身份时，他也无权正当地自称是他们的代理人。至于他的委托人是谁、他究竟有没有委托人，我没有任何义务听信他的一面之词。他既拿不出任何凭证，我就有权说：他连自己自称拥有的那种授权也并不存在；因此，他图谋抢劫、奴役或谋杀我，完全是为他自己打算。
===Original===
This tacit understanding, therefore, among the voters of the country, amounts to nothing as an authority to their agents. Neither do the ballots by which they select their agents, avail any more than does their tacit understanding; for their ballots are given in secret, and therefore in a way to avoid any personal responsibility for the acts of their agents.
===Chinese===
因此，这个国家选民之间的这种默契，作为对其代理人的授权，一文不值。他们挑选代理人所投的选票，也并不比这种默契更有分量；因为他们的选票是在秘密状态下投出的，也就是说，是以规避对其代理人行为的任何个人责任的方式投出的。
===Original===
No body of men can be said to authorize a man to act as their agent, to the injury of a third person, unless they do it in so open and authentic a manner as to make themselves personally responsible for his acts. None of the voters in this country appoint their political agents in any open authentic manner, or in any manner to make themselves responsible for their acts. Therefore these pretended agents cannot legitimately claim to be really agents. Somebody must be responsible for the acts of these pretended agents; and if they cannot show any open and authentic credentials from their principals, they cannot, in law or reason, be said to have any principals. The maxim applies here, that what does not appear, does not exist. If they can show no principals, they have none.
===Chinese===
任何一群人，除非以足够公开、真实可靠的方式进行授权，使自己为其行为承担个人责任，就谈不上授权某人为其代理人去损害第三人。这个国家的选民，没有一个以任何公开、真实可靠的方式任命其政治代理人，也没有以任何使自己为其行为承担责任的方式任命。因此，这些冒牌代理人无权正当地声称自己是真正的代理人。这些冒牌代理人的行为，总得有人来负责；而如果他们拿不出来自委托人的任何公开、真实可靠的凭证，那么无论在法律上还是理性上，都不能说他们有什么委托人。这里适用这样一条格言：凡未显现者，即不存在。拿不出委托人，他们就没有委托人。
===Original===
But even these pretended agents do not themselves know who their pretended principals are. These latter act in secret; for acting by secret ballot is acting in secret as much as if they were to meet in secret conclave in the darkness of the night. And they are personally as much unknown to the agents they select, as they are to others. No pretended agent therefore can ever know by whose ballots he is selected, or consequently who his real principals are. Not knowing who his principals are, he has no right to say that he has any. He can, at most, say only that he is the agent of a secret band of robbers and murderers, who are bound by that faith which prevails among confederates in crime, to stand by him, if his acts, done in their name, shall be resisted.
===Chinese===
然而，就连这些冒牌代理人自己，也不知道他们那些冒牌委托人是谁。后者行事秘而不宣；因为以秘密投票（secret ballot）方式行事，就如同在夜色笼罩下于密室中聚会行事一样，完全是暗中行事。对于他们所选定的代理人，这些委托人像对其他人一样，个人身份全然不为人知。因此，任何冒牌代理人都不可能知道自己是被谁的选票选出来的，从而也不可能知道他真正的委托人是谁。既然不知道自己的委托人是谁，他就无权宣称自己有委托人。他至多只能说，自己是秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙的代理人；这个团伙靠着犯罪同伙之间通行的那种信义彼此约束：倘若他以他们的名义所作的行为遭到抵抗，他们必与他站在一起。
===Original===
Men honestly engaged in attempting to establish justice in the world, have no occasion thus to act in secret; or to appoint agents to do acts for which they (the principals) are not willing to be responsible.
===Chinese===
诚心致力于在世界上建立正义的人，没有必要这样暗中行事，也没有必要去任命代理人，去干他们（委托人）自己不愿承担责任的行为。
===Original===
The secret ballot makes a secret government; and a secret government is a secret band of robbers and murderers. Open despotism is better than this. The single despot stands out in the face of all men, and says: “I am the State: My will is law: I am your master: I take the responsibility of my acts: The only arbiter I acknowledge is the sword: If anyone denies my right, let him try conclusions with me.”
===Chinese===
秘密投票造就秘密的政府；而秘密的政府就是秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙。公开的专制也比这要好。独夫暴君立于众人面前，说：“朕即国家：我的意志就是法律：我是你们的主人：我为自己的行为承担责任：我承认的唯一仲裁者是刀剑：谁若否认我的权力，就让他与我决一雌雄。”
===Original===
But a secret government is little less than a government of assassins. Under it, a man knows not who his tyrants are, until they have struck, and perhaps not then. He may *guess*, beforehand, as to some of his immediate neighbors. But he really *knows* nothing. The man to whom he would most naturally fly for protection, may prove an enemy, when the time of trial comes.
===Chinese===
而秘密的政府几乎就是一个刺客的政府。在这种政府之下，一个人不知道自己的暴君是谁，除非暴君已经出手——甚至到那时也未必知道。事先，对于他身边最切近的某些邻人，他或许可以*猜测*。但他其实一无所*知*。到了考验来临时，他最自然会逃去寻求保护的那个人，也许竟是个敌人。
===Original===

我们那些冒牌大使、国务卿、总统和参议员号称与之缔约的所谓“国家”，同我们自己的“国家”一样是虚构。按照法律与理性的一般原则，不存在这样的“国家”。也就是说，比如说，全体英格兰人民、或任何以这个名字自称的公开的、公开申明的、负得起责任的人群，从来不曾通过彼此之间任何公开、书面或其他真实可靠的契约，把自己组成任何真诚的（bona fide）、正当的社团或组织，也从来不曾授权任何国王、女王或其他代表，以他们的名义缔结条约，或以这类条约束缚他们——无论是束缚其个人，还是束缚其作为社团。
===Original===
Our pretended treaties, then, being made with no legitimate or bona fide nations, or representatives of nations, and being made, on our part, by persons who have no legitimate authority to act for us, have intrinsically no more validity than a pretended treaty made by the Man in the Moon with the king of the Pleiades.
===Chinese===
因此，我们那些冒牌条约，既然既非与正当的、真诚的国家或国家的代表所缔结，就我们这一方而言，又是由根本无正当权限代表我们行事的人所缔结，那么其内在的有效性，同月中人与昴星团国王订立的一纸假条约相比，并不多出一分。
### XVII / 十七

===Original===
On general principles of law and reason, debts contracted in the name of “the United States,” or of “the people of the United States,” are of no validity. It is utterly absurd to pretend that debts to the amount of twenty-five hundred millions of dollars are binding upon thirty-five or forty millions of people, when there is not a particle of legitimate evidence—such as would be required to prove a private debt—that can be produced against any one of them, that either he, or his properly authorized attorney, ever contracted to pay one cent.
===Chinese===
按照法律与理性的一般原则，以“美利坚合众国”或“美利坚合众国人民”的名义订立的债务，是无效的。说什么数额高达二十五亿美元的债务约束着三千五百万或四千万人民，而在能对其中任何一个人出示的证据中，找不到一丝一毫正当的证据——像证明私人债务所要求的那种证据——证明他本人或其正式授权的代理人曾订约偿付一分钱，这种说法荒谬绝伦。
===Original===
Certainly, neither the whole people of the United States, nor any number of them, ever separately or individually contracted to pay a cent of these debts.
===Chinese===
可以肯定，美利坚合众国人民，无论是全体还是其中任何一部分人，从来不曾共同地或个别地订约偿还这些债务的一分钱。
===Original===
Certainly, also, neither the whole people of the United States, nor any number of them, ever, by any open, written, or other authentic and voluntary contract, united themselves as a firm, corporation, or association, by the name of “the United States,” or “the people of the United States,” and authorized their agents to contract debts in their name.
===Chinese===
同样可以肯定，美利坚合众国人民，无论是全体还是其中任何一部分人，从来不曾通过任何公开、书面或其他真实可靠的自愿契约，以“美利坚合众国”或“美利坚合众国人民”之名联合为一个商号、法人或社团，并授权其代理人以他们的名义订立债务。
===Original===
Certainly, too, there is in existence no such firm, corporation, or association as “the United States,” or “the people of the United States,” formed by any open, written, or other authentic and voluntary contract, and having corporate property with which to pay these debts.
===Chinese===
同样可以肯定，世上也并不存在这样一个商号、法人或社团——名为“美利坚合众国”或“美利坚合众国人民”，由任何公开、书面或其他真实可靠的自愿契约组成，并且拥有可用以偿还这些债务的法人财产。
===Original===
How, then, is it possible, on any general principle of law or reason, that debts that are binding upon nobody individually, can be binding upon forty millions of people collectively, when, on general and legitimate principles of law and reason, these forty millions of people neither have, nor ever had, any corporate property? never made any corporate or individual contract? and neither have, nor ever had, any corporate existence?
===Chinese===
那么，按照法律或理性的任何一般原则，怎么可能有一些对任何个人都没有约束力的债务，却对四千万人集体地有约束力——须知，按照正当的法律与理性的一般原则，这四千万人现在没有、也从来不曾有过任何法人财产；从未订立过任何法人契约或个人契约；现在没有、也从来不曾有过任何法人存在？
===Original===
Who, then, created these debts, in the name of “the United States?” Why, at most, only a few persons, calling themselves “members of Congress,” etc. who pretended to represent “the people of the United States,” but who really represented only a secret band of robbers and murderers, who wanted money to carry on the robberies and murders in which they were then engaged; and who intended to extort from the future people of the United States, by robbery and threats of murder (and real murder, if that should prove necessary), the means to pay these debts.
===Chinese===
那么，这些以“美利坚合众国”名义设立的债务，究竟是谁搞出来的呢？咳，至多不过是几个自称“国会议员”等等的人物；他们号称代表“美利坚合众国人民”，实际上只代表一个秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙；这伙人要钱去进行他们当时正在从事的抢劫和谋杀，并打算靠抢劫和以谋杀相威胁（如果证明有必要，就真刀真枪地杀），从未来的美利坚合众国人民身上勒索偿还这些债务的手段。
===Original===
This band of robbers and murderers, who were the real principals in contracting these debts, is a secret one, because its members have never entered into any open, written, avowed, or authentic contract, by which they may be individually known to the world, or even to each other. Their real or pretended representatives, who contracted these debts in their name, were selected (if selected at all) for that purpose secretly (by secret ballot), and in a way to furnish evidence against none of the principals *individually*; and these principals were really known *individually* neither to their pretended representatives who contracted these debts in their behalf, nor to those who lent the money. The money, therefore, was all borrowed and lent in the dark; that is, by men who did not see each other’s faces, or know each other’s names; who could not then, and cannot now, identify each other as principals in the transactions; and who consequently can prove no contract with each other.
===Chinese===
这个作为订约真正委托人的强盗和杀人犯团伙是个秘密团伙，因为它的成员从未缔结过任何公开的、书面的、公开申明的或真实可靠的契约，使他们得以向世人、甚至向彼此显明个人身份。以他们的名义订立这些债务的、他们真实的或冒牌的代表，是为这一目的（如果毕竟被选过的话）以秘密方式（通过秘密投票）选出的，其选法使人无法据此对任何一位委托人*个人*提出证据；而这些委托人的*个人*身份，无论对替他们订立这些债务的冒牌代表，还是对出借钱款的人，实际上都无从知晓。因此，这笔钱全是在黑暗中借入和贷出的；也就是说，借贷双方是一些彼此没见过面、不知道对方姓名的人；他们当时不能、如今也不能在交易中确认彼此的委托人身份；因此他们无法证明彼此之间存在任何契约。
===Original===
Furthermore, the money was all lent and borrowed for criminal purposes; that is, for purposes of robbery and murder; and for this reason the contracts were all intrinsically void; and would have been so, even though the real parties, borrowers and lenders, had come face to face, and made their contracts openly, in their own proper names.
===Chinese===
况且，这笔钱的贷与借全是为犯罪目的——也就是为抢劫和谋杀的目的；因此这些契约全都在本质上无效；即使真实的当事人——借方和贷方——曾经面对面、以自己的真实名义公开订立契约，这些契约也照样无效。
===Original===
Furthermore, this secret band of robbers and murderers, who were the real borrowers of this money, having no legitimate corporate existence, have no corporate property with which to pay these debts. They do indeed pretend to own large tracts of wild lands, lying between the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, and between the Gulf of Mexico and the North Pole. But, on general principles of law and reason, they might as well pretend to own the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans themselves; or the atmosphere and the sunlight; and to hold them, and dispose of them, for the payment of these debts.
===Chinese===
再者，这个作为这笔钱真实借入方的秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙，没有任何正当的法人存在，也就没有任何可用来偿还这些债务的法人财产。他们的确自称拥有横亘于大西洋与太平洋之间、墨西哥湾与北极之间的大片荒地。但是，按照法律与理性的一般原则，他们这样自称，同自称拥有大西洋和太平洋本身、或者拥有大气和阳光，并为了偿还这些债务而持有和处分它们，没有什么两样。
===Original===
Having no corporate property with which to pay what purports to be their corporate debts, this secret band of robbers and murderers are really bankrupt. They have nothing to pay with. In fact, they do not propose to pay their debts otherwise than from the proceeds of their future robberies and murders. These are confessedly their sole reliance; and were known to be such by the lenders of the money, at the time the money was lent. And it was, therefore, virtually a part of the contract, that the money should be repaid only from the proceeds of these future robberies and murders. For this reason, if for no other, the contracts were void from the beginning.
===Chinese===
这个秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙没有任何可用来偿付其号称的法人债务的法人财产，实际上已经破产。他们一无所有，无从偿付。事实上，他们打算偿债的唯一来源，就是他们未来抢劫和谋杀的所得。他们自己供认，这是他们唯一的指望；而放贷者在放贷之时也知道这一点。因此，这笔钱只从这些未来的抢劫和谋杀所得中偿还，实际上就是契约的一部分。单凭这一条理由——纵使没有别的理由——这些契约从一开始就是无效的。
===Original===
In fact, these apparently two classes, borrowers and lenders, were really one and the same class. They borrowed and lent money from and to themselves. They themselves were not only part and parcel, but the very life and soul, of this secret band of robbers and murderers, who borrowed and spent the money. Individually they furnished money for a common enterprise; taking, in return, what purported to be corporate promises for individual loans. The only excuse they had for taking these so-called corporate promises of, for individual loans by, the same parties, was that they might have some apparent excuse for the future robberies of the band (that is, to pay the debts of the corporation), and that they might also know what shares they were to be respectively entitled to out of the proceeds of their future robberies.
===Chinese===
事实上，这表面上分为两类的借方和贷方，实际上是同一类人。他们是自己向自己借钱、自己把钱贷给自己。对于这个借入和花费这笔钱的秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙来说，他们不只是其中的一部分，而正是它的生命与灵魂。他们以个人身份为一件共同事业出资，换回来的则是号称法人承诺的凭据，以抵偿个人的放款。他们之所以收下这些同一伙人打的所谓法人借据、以抵个人的放款，唯一的借口是：这样他们就可以为该团伙未来的抢劫预备某种表面上的名目（也就是偿还法人的债务），而且他们也可以借此知道，在未来抢劫的所得中，他们各自有权分得多少份额。
===Original===
Finally, if these debts had been created for the most innocent and honest purposes, and in the most open and honest manner, by the real parties to the contracts, these parties could thereby have bound nobody but themselves, and no property but their own. They could have bound nobody that should have come after them, and no property subsequently created by, or belonging to, other persons.
===Chinese===
最后，即使这些债务是契约的真实当事人为最无辜、最正当的目的，以最公开、最诚实的方式设立的，这些当事人也借此只能约束他们自己，只能支配他们自己的财产。他们约束不了任何后来者，也支配不了其后由他人创设的或属于他人的任何财产。
### XVIII / 十八

===Original===
The Constitution having never been signed by anybody; and there being no other open, written, or authentic contract between any parties whatever, by virtue of which the United States government, so called, is maintained; and it being well known that none but male persons, of twenty-one years of age and upwards, are allowed any voice in the government; and it being also well known that a large number of these adult persons seldom or never vote at all; and that *all* those who do vote, do so secretly (by secret ballot), and in a way to prevent their individual votes being known, either to the world, or even to each other; and consequently in a way to make no one openly responsible for the acts of their agents, or representatives—all these things being known, the questions arise: *Who* compose the real governing power in the country? Who are the men, *the responsible men*, who rob us of our property? Restrain us of our liberty? Subject us to their arbitrary dominion? And devastate our homes, and shoot us down by the hundreds of thousands, if we resist? How shall we find these men? How shall we know them from others? How shall we defend ourselves and our property against them? Who, of our neighbors, are members of this secret band of robbers and murderers? How can we know which are *their* houses, that we may burn or demolish them? Which *their* property, that we may destroy it? Which *their* persons, that we may kill them, and rid the world and ourselves of such tyrants and monsters?
===Chinese===
宪法从未经任何人签署；在任何当事方之间也不存在任何其他公开、书面或真实可靠的契约，凭以维系所谓的合众国政府；众所周知，在政府中享有任何发言权的，只有年满二十一岁的男性；同样众所周知，这些成年男子当中为数众多的人很少投票或从不投票；而*所有*投票的人，都是秘密地投（通过秘密投票），其投法使人无从知道他们的个人选票——既不让世人知道，甚至也不让彼此知道；从而其投法使任何人都无须公开对其代理人或代表的行为负责——凡此种种既已明了，问题便随之而来：*谁*是这个国家真正的统治力量？那些抢劫我们财产、限制我们自由、使我们屈从其专横统治的人，那些一旦我们抵抗就蹂躏我们的家园、以数十万计地枪杀我们的人——这些*负得起责任的人*究竟是谁？我们要到哪里去找这些人？我们如何把他们同其他人区别开来？我们如何保卫自己和自己的财产不受他们侵害？我们的邻人当中，谁是这个秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙的成员？我们怎样才能知道哪些是*他们的*房子，好去放火焚烧或夷为平地？哪些是*他们的*财产，好去加以毁坏？哪些是*他们的*人身，好去杀掉他们，为世界也为我们自己除掉这样的暴君和怪物？
===Original===
These are questions that must be answered, before men can be free; before they can protect themselves against this secret band of robbers and murderers, who now plunder, enslave, and destroy them.
===Chinese===
在人们能够获得自由之前，在他们能够保护自己不受这个如今正在掠夺、奴役和毁灭他们的秘密的强盗和杀人犯团伙之害之前，这些问题必须得到回答。
===Original===
The answer to these questions is, that only those who have the will and the power to shoot down their fellow men, are the real rulers in this, as in all other (so called) civilized countries; for by no others will civilized men be robbed, or enslaved.
===Chinese===
这些问题的答案是：无论在这个国家，还是在所有其他所谓文明国家，真正的统治者只能是那些既有意志又有权力枪杀自己同类的人；因为文明人受抢劫、受奴役，决不会出自其他任何人之手。
===Original===
Among savages, mere physical strength, on the part of one man, may enable him to rob, enslave, or kill another man. Among barbarians, mere physical strength, on the part of a body of men, disciplined, and acting in concert, though with very little money or other wealth, may, under some circumstances, enable them to rob, enslave, or kill another body of men, as numerous, or perhaps even more numerous, than themselves. And among both savages and barbarians, mere want may sometimes compel one man to sell himself as a slave to another. But with (so called) civilized peoples, among whom knowledge, wealth, and the means of acting in concert, have become diffused; and who have invented such weapons and other means of defence as to render mere physical strength of less importance; and by whom soldiers in any requisite number, and other instrumentalities of war in any requisite amount, can always be had for money, the question of war, and consequently the question of power, is little else than a mere question of money. As a necessary consequence, those who stand ready to furnish this money, are the real rulers. It is so in Europe, and it is so in this country.
===Chinese===
在蒙昧人（savages）当中，单凭一个人的体力，就足以使他抢劫、奴役或杀死另一个人。在野蛮人（barbarians）当中，单凭一伙受过训练、协同行事之人的体力——尽管他们几乎没有钱财或其他财富——在某些情况下，也足以使他们抢劫、奴役或杀死另一伙人数与他们相当、甚至可能更多的人。无论在蒙昧人还是野蛮人当中，单是穷困，有时也会迫使一个人把自己卖身为奴。然而对于所谓文明的民族来说——知识的财富、物质的财富以及协同行动的手段已经普及；他们发明的武器和其他防御手段已使单纯的体力变得不那么重要；对这些人来说，只要有钱，任何所需数量的士兵和其他战争工具总可以弄到手——战争问题，从而权力问题，几乎不过是一个金钱问题。其必然结果是：随时准备提供这笔钱的人，才是真正的统治者。欧洲如此，这个国家也如此。
===Original===
In Europe, the nominal rulers, the emperors and kings and parliaments, are anything but the real rulers of their respective countries. They are little or nothing else than mere tools, employed by the wealthy to rob, enslave, and (if need be) murder those who have less wealth, or none at all.
===Chinese===
在欧洲，名义上的统治者——皇帝、国王和议会——全然不是其各自国家的真正统治者。他们几乎不外乎是富人们雇用的纯粹工具，用来抢劫、奴役，以及（在必要时）谋杀那些财富较少或一无所有的人。
===Original===
The Rothschilds, and that class of moneylenders of whom they are the representatives and agents—men who never think of lending a shilling to their next-door neighbors, for purposes of honest industry, unless upon the most ample security, and at the highest rate of interest—stand ready, at all times, to lend money in unlimited amounts to those robbers and murderers, who call themselves governments, to be expended in shooting down those who do not submit quietly to being robbed and enslaved.
===Chinese===
罗斯柴尔德家族，以及以他们为代表和代理人的那类放贷者——这些人给左邻右舍放一个先令去经营正当产业，非要有最十足的担保、收最高的利息不可——却随时准备把数额不限的钱贷给那些自称政府的强盗和杀人犯，供其用来枪杀那些不肯安安静静被抢劫、被奴役的人。
===Original===
They lend their money in this manner, knowing that it is to be expended in murdering their fellow men, for simply seeking their liberty and their rights; knowing also that neither the interest nor the principal will ever be paid, except as it will be extorted under terror of the repetition of such murders as those for which the money lent is to be expended.
===Chinese===
他们这样放贷，明知这笔钱将用来谋杀他们的同类，而后者不过是寻求自己的自由和权利而已；也明知利息和本钱都永远不会得到偿付，除非靠恐怖来勒索——靠再干一遍那种谋杀的恐怖来勒索，而放出去的这笔钱正是为那种谋杀花用的。
===Original===
These moneylenders, the Rothschilds, for example, say to themselves: If we lend a hundred millions sterling to the Queen and Parliament of England, it will enable them to murder twenty, fifty, or a hundred thousand people in England, Ireland, or India; and the terror inspired by such wholesale murder, will enable them to keep the whole people of those countries in subjection for twenty, or perhaps fifty, years to come; to control all their trade and industry; and to extort from them large amounts of money, under the name of taxes; and from the wealth thus extorted from them, they (the Queen and Parliament) can afford to pay us a higher rate of interest for our money than we can get in any other way. Or, if we lend this sum to the Emperor of Austria, it will enable him to murder so many of his people as to strike terror into the rest, and thus enable him to keep them in subjection, and extort money from them, for twenty or fifty years to come. And they say the same in regard to the Emperor of Russia, the King of Prussia, the Emperor of France, or any other ruler, so called, who, in their judgment, will be able, by murdering a reasonable portion of his people, to keep the rest in subjection, and extort money from them, for a long time to come, to pay the interest and principal of the money lent him.
===Chinese===
这些放贷者——例如罗斯柴尔德家族——心里盘算的是：如果我们把一亿英镑贷给英格兰女王和议会，就足以使他们能够在英格兰、爱尔兰或印度谋杀两万、五万或十万人；这种大屠杀所引起的恐怖，将使他们能够在此后二十年，也许五十年内，使那些国家的全体人民俯首帖耳，控制其一切工商行业，并以赋税之名从他们身上勒索大笔钱财；而从这样勒索来的财富当中，他们（女王和议会）也就付得起我们的钱比任何其他放贷门路都高的利息。或者，如果我们把这笔钱贷给奥地利皇帝，就足以使他谋杀其人民中的相当一部分，把恐怖打进其余人的心里，从而使他能够在此后二十年或五十年内使他们俯首帖耳，并从他们身上勒索钱财。对于俄国皇帝、普鲁士国王、法兰西皇帝，或任何其他所谓的统治者，只要照他们判断，此人能靠谋杀其人民中适当的一部分，使其余的人在此后长时期内俯首帖耳、不断被勒索钱财，以支付所贷钱款的利息和本钱，他们都抱着同样的盘算。
===Original===
And why are these men so ready to lend money for murdering their fellow men? Solely for this reason, viz., that such loans are considered better investments than loans for purposes of honest industry. In short, the industrial and commercial slavery of the great body of the people, North and South, black and white, is the price which these lenders of blood money demand, and insist upon, and are determined to secure, in return for the money lent for the war.
===Chinese===
如今，这些血钱放贷者来讨还他们的钱；于是所谓的政府变成他们的工具——一个奴颜婢膝、卑劣万分的工具——去从北方和南方被奴役人民的劳动中把这笔钱勒索出来。勒索要通过直接、间接和不平等课税的各种形式来进行。不仅要足额偿付名义上的债务和利息——后者本来就大得惊人；而且这些债主还要得到进一步的偿付——也许是双倍、三倍乃至四倍的偿付——办法是：对进口品征收这样的关税，使我们的本土制造商能从其商品上实现高得惊人的价格；再就是建立这样的银行业垄断，使他们能控制北方人民大众自己的工业和商业，从而奴役和掠夺之。总而言之，使北方和南方、黑人和白人的广大人民沦于工业上和商业上的被奴役地位，就是这些血钱放贷者索要、坚持并决心到手的代价，作为其为战争所放钱款的回报。
===Original===
This programme having been fully arranged and systematized, they put their sword into the hands of the chief murderer of the war, and charge him to carry their scheme into effect. And now he, speaking as their organ, says: “*Let us have peace.*”
===Chinese===
这套方案既经周密布置、系统化之后，他们便把剑交到这场战争的头号杀人犯手中，责令他把他们的计划付诸实施。如今，他以他们的喉舌的身份发言说：“*让我们享有和平。*”
===Original===
The meaning of this is: Submit quietly to all the robbery and slavery we have arranged for you, and you can have “peace.” But in case you resist, the same lenders of blood-money, who furnished the means to subdue the South, will furnish the means again to subdue you.
===Chinese===
这话的意思是：乖乖服从我们为你们安排好的一切抢劫和奴役，你们就可以有“和平”。但倘若你们抵抗，那帮血钱放贷者——正是他们提供了征服南方的手段——还会再一次提供征服你们的手段。
===Original===
These are the terms on which alone this government, or, with few exceptions, any other, ever gives “peace” to its people.
===Chinese===
这个政府——以及除了极少数例外之外的任何其他政府——能给其人民的“和平”，从来只有这样的条件。
===Original===
The whole affair, on the part of those who furnished the money, has been, and now is, a deliberate scheme of robbery and murder; not merely to monopolize the markets of the South, but also to monopolize the currency, and thus control the industry and trade, and thus plunder and enslave the laborers, of both North and South. And Congress and the president are today the merest tools for these purposes. They are obliged to be, for they know that their own power, as rulers, so called, is at an end, the moment their credit with the blood-money loan-mongers fails. They are like a bankrupt in the hands of an extortioner. They dare not say nay to any demand made upon them. And to hide at once, if possible, both their servility and their crimes, they attempt to divert public attention, by crying out that they have “Abolished Slavery!” That they have “Saved the Country!” That they have “Preserved our Glorious Union!” and that, in now paying the “National Debt,” as they call it (as if the people themselves, *all of them who are to be taxed for its payment*, had really and voluntarily joined in contracting it), they are simply “Maintaining the National Honor!”
===Chinese===
就出钱的一方而言，这整件事过去是、如今仍是一个蓄意的抢劫与谋杀方案：不仅为了垄断南方的市场，还为了垄断通货，从而控制北南双方的工业和商业，从而掠夺和奴役北南双方的劳动者。而国会和总统，今天正是为这些目的服务的十足工具。他们不能不是，因为他们心里清楚：一旦他们在血钱放贷贩子那里的信用失效，他们自己作为所谓统治者的权力也就到了头。他们就像落在勒索者手里的破产者，对向他们提出的任何要求都不敢说半个不字。而为了尽可能把他们的奴颜和罪行一并掩盖起来，他们试图转移公众的视线，大声嚷嚷说他们“废除了奴隶制！”，“拯救了国家！”，“保卫了我们光荣的联邦！”，还说什么如今偿付他们所谓的“国债”（仿佛人民自己——*所有将要为此缴税的人民*——真的自愿参与订立了这笔债务），就纯粹是“维护国家荣誉！”
===Original===
By “maintaining the national honor,” they mean simply that they themselves, open robbers and murderers, assume to be the nation, and will keep faith with those who lend them the money necessary to enable them to crush the great body of the people under their feet; and will faithfully appropriate, from the proceeds of their future robberies and murders, enough to pay all their loans, principal and interest.
===Chinese===
所谓“维护国家荣誉”，他们的意思无非是：他们自己——一伙公开的强盗和杀人犯——僭称自己就是国家；对那些放钱给他们、使他们得以把广大人民踩在脚下的人，他们将信守不渝；并将忠实地从他们未来抢劫和谋杀的所得中，拨出足够的份额，以偿付他们全部贷款的本息。
===Original===
The pretence that the “abolition of slavery” was either a motive or justification for the war, is a fraud of the same character with that of “maintaining the national honor.” Who, but such usurpers, robbers, and murderers as they, ever established slavery? Or what government, except one resting upon the sword, like the one we now have, was ever capable of maintaining slavery? And why did these men abolish slavery? Not from any love of liberty in general—not as an act of justice to the black man himself, but only “as a war measure,” and because they wanted his assistance, and that of his friends, in carrying on the war they had undertaken for maintaining and intensifying that political, commercial, and industrial slavery, to which they have subjected the great body of the people, both white and black. And yet these impostors now cry out that they have abolished the chattel slavery of the black man—although that was not the motive of the war—as if they thought they could thereby conceal, atone for, or justify that other slavery which they were fighting to perpetuate, and to render more rigorous and inexorable than it ever was before. There was no difference of principle—but only of degree—between the slavery they boast they have abolished, and the slavery they were fighting to preserve; for all restraints upon men’s natural liberty, not necessary for the simple maintenance of justice, are of the nature of slavery, and differ from each other only in degree.
===Chinese===
说什么“废除奴隶制”或是战争的动机、或是战争的辩护理由，这种托词同“维护国家荣誉”的托词是同一路货色的骗局。除了他们这样的篡夺者、强盗和杀人犯，还有谁建立过奴隶制？除了像我们如今这个政府这样以刀剑为凭的政府，还有什么政府有能力维持奴隶制？而这些人为什么废除奴隶制？不是出于对一般自由的任何热爱——也不是出于对黑人本身的一项正义之举——而只是“作为一项战争措施”，因为他们要在自己承担的那场战争中获得黑人和他的朋友们的援助；那场战争，正是为了维持并加紧那种政治的、商业的和工业的奴役——他们已把广大人民置于这种奴役之下，不分白人黑人。可如今这些骗子却大嚷大叫，说他们废除了黑人的动产奴隶制（chattel slavery）——尽管那根本不是战争的动机——仿佛他们以为借此就能掩饰、抵赎或辩护那另一种奴役——他们奋力使之永久化、并使之比以往任何时候都更严酷更无情的那一种。他们吹嘘废除了的那种奴隶制，同他们奋力保全的那种奴役之间，没有原则上的差别——只有程度上的差别；因为一切对人们自然自由的限制，凡非单纯维持正义所必需者，都属奴役的性质，彼此仅在程度上有所不同。
===Original===
If their object had really been to abolish slavery, or maintain liberty or justice generally, they had only to say: All, whether white or black, who want the protection of this government, shall have it; and all who do not want it, will be left in peace, so long as they leave us in peace. Had they said this, slavery would necessarily have been abolished at once; the war would have been saved; and a thousand times nobler union than we have ever had would have been the result. It would have been a voluntary union of free men; such a union as will one day exist among all men, the world over, if the several nations, so called, shall ever get rid of the usurpers, robbers, and murderers, called governments, that now plunder, enslave, and destroy them.
===Chinese===
倘若他们的目的真是废除奴隶制、或普遍维护自由和正义，他们原本只需说：凡是想要这个政府保护的人，无论白人黑人，都可得其保护；凡是不想要的人，只要不打扰我们，就任其相安无事。倘若他们说了这话，奴隶制必然立刻废除；战争也可以免掉；其结果将是一种比我们曾有过的任何联合高贵一千倍的联合。那将是自由人的自愿联合；这样一种联合，有朝一日将在全世界一切人中间实现——只要那些所谓的各个国家，终究能摆脱那些被称作政府的篡夺者、强盗和杀人犯——正是这些人如今在掠夺、奴役和毁灭他们。
===Original===
Still another of the frauds of these men is, that they are now establishing, and that the war was designed to establish, “a government of consent.” The only idea they have ever manifested as to what is a government of consent, is this—that it is one to which everybody must consent, or be shot. This idea was the dominant one on which the war was carried on; and it is the dominant one, now that we have got what is called “peace.”
===Chinese===
这些人的另一个骗局是：说什么他们如今正在建立的、战争就是要建立的，是一个“同意的政府”。至于什么是同意的政府，他们所表现出来的一贯只有这么一个观念——那就是：人人都必须同意的政府，否则枪毙。这个观念是进行战争的主导观念；而在我们得到了所谓“和平”的今天，它依然是主导观念。
===Original===
Their pretences that they have “Saved the Country,” and “Preserved our Glorious Union,” are frauds like all the rest of their pretences. By them they mean simply that they have subjugated, and maintained their power over, an unwilling people. This they call “Saving the Country;” as if an enslaved and subjugated people—or as if any people kept in subjection by the sword (as it is intended that all of us shall be hereafter)—could be said to have any country. This, too, they call “Preserving our Glorious Union;” as if there could be said to be any Union, glorious or inglorious, that was not voluntary. Or as if there could be said to be any union between masters and slaves; between those who conquer, and those who are subjugated.
===Chinese===
他们那些“拯救了国家”、“保卫了我们光荣的联邦”的托词，同其余一切托词一样是骗局。托这些话，他们的意思无非是：他们已经征服了一个不情愿的人民，并维持着对其权力的支配。他们把这叫做“拯救国家”，仿佛一个被奴役、被征服的人民——仿佛任何被刀剑慑服的人民（正像打算让我们大家日后都被这样慑服那样）——还能说拥有什么国家似的。他们把这又叫做“保卫我们光荣的联邦”，仿佛还有什么不是出于自愿的联邦——光荣的也罢，不光荣的也罢——可以说成联邦似的；仿佛主人和奴隶之间、征服者和被征服者之间，还可以说什么联合似的。
===Original===
All these cries of having “abolished slavery,” of having “saved the country,” of having “preserved the union,” of establishing “a government of consent,” and of “maintaining the national honor,” are all gross, shameless, transparent cheats—so transparent that they ought to decieve no one—when uttered as justifications for the war, or for the government that has succeeded the war, or for now compelling the people to pay the cost of the war, or for compelling anybody to support a government that he does not want.
===Chinese===
所有这些叫嚷——什么“废除了奴隶制”，什么“拯救了国家”，什么“保卫了联邦”，什么建立了“同意的政府”，什么“维护了国家荣誉”——当它们被拿来为战争辩护、为战后接续的那个政府辩护、为如今强迫人民支付战争费用辩护、为强迫任何人支持一个他所不想要的政府辩护的时候，全都是粗鄙、无耻、一目了然的欺骗——一目了然到本不该骗过任何人。
===Original===
The lesson taught by all these facts is this: As long as mankind continue to pay “National Debts,” so-called—that is, so long as they are such dupes and cowards as to pay for being cheated, plundered, enslaved, and murdered—so long there will be enough to lend the money for those purposes; and with that money a plenty of tools, called soldiers, can be hired to keep them in subjection. But when they refuse any longer to pay for being thus cheated, plundered, enslaved, and murdered, they will cease to have cheats, and usurpers, and robbers, and murderers and blood-money loan-mongers for masters.
===Chinese===
所有这些事实教给人们的教训就是：只要人类还继续偿付所谓“国债”，也就是说，只要他们还愚蠢怯懦到付钱请人来欺骗、抢劫、奴役和谋杀自己，就会有人有的是钱可放，供这些用途；而有了这笔钱，就雇得起大批被称作士兵的工具来使他们屈服。而一旦他们拒绝再为被人这样欺骗、抢劫、奴役和谋杀而付钱，欺骗者、篡夺者、强盗、杀人犯和血钱放贷贩子，也就再当不成他们的主人了。

## II The Constitution / 第二分册　宪法

### I / 一

===Original===
The Constitution says:

===Chinese===
宪法（the Constitution）说：

===Original===
> “We, the people of the United States, in order to form a more perfect union, establish justice, insure domestic tranquility, provide for the common defence, promote the general welfare, and secure the blessings of liberty to ourselves and our posterity, do ordain and establish this Constitution for the United States of America.”

===Chinese===
> 「我们合众国人民，为建设更完善的联邦、树立正义、保障国内安宁、筹划共同防务、增进公共福利，并使我们自己和我们的后代得享自由的幸福，特此为美利坚合众国制定并确立本宪法。」

===Original===
The meaning of this is simply: We, the people of the United States, *acting freely and voluntarily as individuals, consent and agree* that we will cooperate with each other in sustaining such a government as is provided for in this Constitution.

===Chinese===
这段话的意思不过是说：我们合众国人民，*作为个人，自由而自愿地同意并约定*：我们将彼此合作，共同维持本宪法所规定的那种政府。

===Original===
The necessity for the consent of “the people” is implied in this declaration. *The whole authority of the Constitution rests upon it. If they did not consent, it was of no validity. Of course it had no validity, except as between those who actually consented.* No one’s consent could be presumed against him, without his actual consent being given, any more than in the case of any other contract to pay money, or render service. And to make it binding upon anyone, his signature, or other positive evidence of consent, was as necessary as in the case of any other contract. If the instrument meant to say that any of “the people of the United States” would be bound by it, who did not consent, it was a usurpation and a lie. The most that can be inferred from the form, “*We, the people,*” is, that the instrument *offered* membership to *all* “the people of the United States;” leaving it for them to accept or refuse it, at their pleasure.

===Chinese===
这一宣言本身便已隐含：「人民」的同意（consent）实属必要。*宪法的全部权威皆系于此。倘若人民并未同意，宪法便属无效；当然，它也唯有在实际表示同意的人之间才有效力。*除非本人实际表示同意，任何人的同意都不得被推定（presume）加之于他，这与任何其他约定付钱或提供服务的契约并无二致。而要使宪法对任何人具有拘束力，也同任何其他契约一样，必须有他的签名或其他积极的同意证据。倘若这份文件的意思是，「合众国人民」之中凡未表示同意者也要受它拘束，那它便是一场篡夺（usurpation），一句谎言。从「*我们合众国人民*」这一措辞中，至多只能推出：这份文件向*全体*「合众国人民」*发出了*成员资格的邀约，听凭他们自行决定接受与否。

===Original===
The agreement is a simple one, like any other agreement. It is the same as one that should say: “We, the people of the town of A——, agree to sustain a church, a school, a hospital, or a theatre, for ourselves and our children.”

===Chinese===
这一约定同其他任何约定一样简单，就等于是说：「我们A镇的人民，约定为我们自己和我们的子女维持一座教堂、一所学校、一座医院或一家剧院。」

===Original===
Such an agreement clearly could have no validity, except as between those who actually consented to it. If a portion only of “the people of the town of A——,” should assent to this contract, and should then proceed to *compel* contributions of money or service from those who had not consented, they would be mere robbers; and would deserve to be treated as such.

===Chinese===
这样的约定显然唯有在实际同意者之间才能有效力。倘若只是「A镇人民」的一部分赞同这纸契约，接着便去*强索*未曾同意之人的金钱或劳役，那他们就纯属强盗，也理应被当作强盗对待。

===Original===
Neither the conduct nor the rights of these signers would be improved at all by their saying to the dissenters: “We offer you equal rights with ourselves, in the benefits of the church, school, hospital, or theatre, which we propose to establish, and equal voice in the control of it. It would be a sufficient answer for the others to say: ‘We want no share in the benefits, and no voice in the control of your institution; and will do nothing to support it.’ ”

===Chinese===
这些签署者纵然对异议者说：「在我们拟议设立的教堂、学校、医院或剧院的利益上，我们给你们与我们同等的权利，在它的管理上也给你们同等的发言权」，他们的行径和他们的权利也断不会因此有丝毫改观。对方只需这样回答便足够了：「你们那机构的好处，我们不要分享；它的管理，我们不要发言；维持它的事，我们一概不做。」

===Original===
The number who actually consented to the Constitution of the United States, at the first, was very small. Considered as the act of the whole people, the adoption of the Constitution was the merest farce and imposture, binding upon nobody.

===Chinese===
当初实际同意美国宪法的人，为数极少。若把它当作全体人民的行为，宪法的通过便不过是一出最浅薄的闹剧、一场欺世盗名的骗局，拘束不了任何人。

===Original===
The women, children, and blacks, of course, were not asked to give their consent. In addition to this, there were, in nearly or quite all the States, property qualifications that excluded probably one half, two thirds, or perhaps even three fourths, of the white male adults from the right of suffrage. And of those who were allowed that right, we know not how many exercised it.

===Chinese===
妇女、儿童和黑人，自然是无人去征求他们的同意的。除此之外，在几乎乃至全部各州，都设有财产资格限制，白人成年男子大概有一半、三分之二，甚或四分之三，被排除在选举权（suffrage）之外。而在享有这项权利的人里面，有多少人实际行使过它，我们就不得而知了。

===Original===
Furthermore, those who originally agreed to the Constitution, could thereby bind nobody that should come after them. They could contract for nobody but themselves. They had no more natural right or power to make political contracts, binding upon succeeding generations, than they had to make marriage or business contracts binding upon them.

===Chinese===
再者，最初同意宪法的人，并不能由此拘束后世的任何人。他们所能代为缔约的，唯有他们自己。要说有权缔结拘束子孙后代的政治契约，他们并不比有权缔结拘束后代的婚姻契约或商业契约多出半分自然权利或能力。

===Original===
Still further. Even those who actually voted for the adoption of the Constitution, did not pledge their faith *for any specific time*; since no specific time was named, in the Constitution, during which the association should continue. It was, therefore, merely an association during pleasure; even as between the original parties to it. Still less, if possible, has it been anything more than a merely voluntary association, during pleasure, between the succeeding generations, who have never gone through, as their fathers did, with so much even as any outward formality of adopting it, or of pledging their faith to support it. Such portions of them as pleased, and as the States permitted to vote, have only done enough, by voting and paying taxes, (and unlawfully and tyrannically extorting taxes from others,) to keep the government in operation for the time being. And this, in the view of the Constitution, they have done voluntarily, and because it was for their interest, or pleasure, and not because they were under any pledge or obligation to do it. Any one man, or any number of men, have had a perfect right, at any time, to refuse his or their further support; and nobody could rightfully object to his or their withdrawal.

===Chinese===
更进一步。就连当初实际投票赞成通过宪法的人，也并未承诺自己的信义*及于任何特定的年限*；因为宪法并没有指明这个联合（association）应当延续多久。因此，即便在其原初缔约各方之间，它也不过是一个愿意维持多久就维持多久的联合。至于相继的世世代代——他们连父辈所经历过的那种通过宪法、宣誓拥护宪法的外在形式都从未走过——若说还有分别，就更加不过是一个全凭意愿、随时可散的自愿联合了。他们之中凡属愿意、而且各州也准许投票的那部分人，靠投票、纳税（外加非法而且专横地向别人勒索税款），做到的不过是让政府一时维持运转而已。而照宪法的观念，他们这样做是出于自愿，是因为这样合乎自己的利益或心意，并不是因为受了什么誓言或义务的约束。任何一个人，或任何一些人，任何时候都有完完全全的权利拒绝继续支持；任何人也无权正当地反对他或他们退出。

===Original===
There is no escape from these conclusions, if we say that the adoption of the Constitution was the act of the people, as individuals, and not of the States, as States. On the other hand, if we say that the adoption was the act of the States, as States, it necessarily follows that they had the right to secede at pleasure, inasmuch as they engaged for no specific time.

===Chinese===
倘若我们说，通过宪法是人民*作为个人*的行为，而不是各州*作为州*的行为，那么上述结论便无从回避。反过来，倘若我们说通过宪法是各州*作为州*的行为，那就必然随之得出：各州有权随意退出（secede），因为它们并未约定任何特定的期限。

===Original===
The consent, therefore, that has been given, whether by individuals, or by the States, has been, at most, only a consent for the time being; not an engagement for the future. In truth, in the case of individuals, their actual voting is not to be taken as proof of consent, even *for the time being*. On the contrary, it is to be considered that, without his consent having ever been asked, a man finds himself environed by a government that he cannot resist; a government that forces him to pay money, render service, and forego the exercise of many of his natural rights, under peril of weighty punishments. He sees, too, that other men practise this tyranny over him by the use of the ballot. He sees further that, if he will but use the ballot himself, he has some chance of relieving himself from this tyranny of others, by subjecting them to his own. In short, he finds himself, without his consent, so situated that, if he use the ballot, he may become a master; if he does not use it, he must become a slave. And he has no other alternative than these two. In self-defence, he attempts the former. His case is analogous to that of a man who has been forced into battle, where he must either kill others, or be killed himself. Because, to save his own life in battle, a man attempts to take the lives of his opponents, it is not to be inferred that the battle is one of his own choosing. Neither in contests with the ballot—which is a mere substitute for a bullet—because, as his only chance of self-preservation, a man uses a ballot, is it to be inferred that the contest is one into which he voluntarily entered; that he voluntarily set up all his own natural rights, as a stake against those of others, to be lost or won by the mere power of numbers. On the contrary, it is to be considered that, in an exigency, into which he had been forced by others, and in which no other means of self-defence offered, he, as a matter of necessity, used the only one that was left to him.

===Chinese===
因此，迄今为止所给出的同意，无论出自个人还是出自各州，至多只是就一时而言的同意，并不是对未来的承诺。说实在的，就个人而论，其实际投票甚至不能当作*就一时而言*同意的证明。相反，应当这样来看：一个人从未被征求过同意，却发现自己已被一个他无力抗拒的政府团团围住；这个政府逼他出钱、服役，逼他放弃行使自己的许多自然权利（natural rights），否则重罚便要临头。他还看到，别人正是凭着一纸选票（ballot）对他实行这种暴政。他进一步看到，只要他自己也来投票，他就有一线机会，把别人置于他自己的支配之下，从而解脱别人加于他的暴政。总之，他发现自己未经同意便被摆在这般境地：投票，他可以做主人；不投票，他就得做奴隶。除这两条之外，他别无选择。出于自卫，他试着走前一条。他的处境，好比一个被强拽上战场的人：在那里，他要么杀死别人，要么被别人杀死。一个人在战场上为保自己的性命而设法取对手的性命，绝不能由此推断这场仗是他自己挑的。同样，在同选票的角力之中——选票不过是子弹（bullet）的代用品——一个人把投票当作自保的唯一机会而去投票，也绝不能由此推断这场角力是他自愿加入的；不能说他自愿把自己的一切自然权利统统押上赌台，去对赌别人的权利，输赢全凭人数的多寡。恰恰相反，应当看到：他是在被别人逼入的绝境之中，在没有别的自卫办法可想的关头，出于不得已，才动用了留给他的仅有的那一个办法。

===Original===
Doubtless the most miserable of men, under the most oppressive government in the world, if allowed the ballot, would use it, if they could see any chance of thereby ameliorating their condition. But it would not therefore be a legitimate inference that the government itself, that crushes them, was one which they had voluntarily set up, or ever consented to.

===Chinese===
毫无疑问，世间压迫最重的政府之下那最悲惨的人们，倘若获准投票，只要看得出有借此改善处境的一线可能，也一定会去投。但绝不能由此正当地推出：那个把他们碾得粉碎的政府，便是他们自己自愿建立的，或者他们曾经同意过的。

===Original===
Therefore a man’s voting under the Constitution of the United States, is not to be taken as evidence that he ever freely assented to the Constitution, *even for the time being*. Consequently we have no proof that any very large portion, even of the actual voters of the United States, ever really and voluntarily consented to the Constitution, *even for the time being*. Nor can we ever have such proof, until every man is left perfectly free to consent, or not, without thereby subjecting himself or his property to injury or trespass from others.

===Chinese===
所以，一个人在美国宪法之下投票，不能当作他曾经自由同意宪法的证据，*哪怕只是就一时而言*。因此，我们拿不出证据证明：即便是美国的实际投票人当中的很大一部分，曾经真正地、自愿地同意过宪法，*哪怕只是就一时而言*。而且，除非每一个人都享有完完全全的自由来决定同意与否，并且不致因此让自己的人身或财产遭到他人的侵害与侵夺，否则我们永远也拿不出这样的证据。

### II / 二

===Original===
The Constitution says:

===Chinese===
宪法说：

===Original===
> “Treason against the United States shall consist only in levying war against them, or in adhering to their enemies, giving them aid and comfort.”

===Chinese===
> 「对合众国的叛国罪（treason），仅限于对合众国发动战争，或依附其敌人、给予其敌人援助与慰藉（aid and comfort）。」

===Original===
This is the only definition of treason given by the Constitution, and it is to be interpreted, like all other criminal laws, in the sense most favorable to liberty and justice. Consequently the treason here spoken of, must be held to be treason *in fact*, and not merely something that may have been falsely called by that name.

===Chinese===
这是宪法给出的唯一的叛国定义；而且，同一切刑事法律一样，必须按最有利于自由与正义的意义去解释它。因此，这里所说的叛国，必须认定为*事实上的*（in fact）叛国，而不仅仅是某种也许被人妄加了这名字的东西。

===Original===
To determine, then, what is treason *in fact*, we are not to look to the codes of Kings, and Czars, and Kaisers, who maintain their power by force and fraud; who contemptuously call mankind their “subjects;” who claim to have a special license from Heaven to rule on earth; who teach that it is a religious duty of mankind to obey them; who bribe a servile and corrupt priesthood to impress these ideas upon the ignorant and superstitious; who spurn the idea that their authority is derived from, or dependent at all upon, the consent of their people; and who attempt to defame, by the false epithet of traitors, all who assert their own rights, and the rights of their fellow men, against such usurpations.

===Chinese===
那么，要判定什么才是*事实上的*叛国，我们不要去看那些国王、沙皇和德皇的法典：他们靠武力和欺诈维持权势；轻蔑地把人类唤作自己的「臣民」（subjects）；自称领有上天特赐的在世统治特许状；教导世人说，服从他们乃人类的宗教义务；收买奴颜婢膝、腐化堕落的教士，把这套观念灌进愚昧迷信的人心里；唾弃那种主张他们的权威源自人民同意、或无论如何须以人民同意为凭的观念；并且拿「叛徒」这顶诬妄的帽子，加给一切主张自身权利与同胞权利、起来反抗这等篡夺的人。

===Original===
Instead of regarding this false and calumnious meaning of the word treason, we are to look at its true and legitimate meaning in our mother tongue; at its use in common life; and at what would necessarily be its true meaning in any other contracts, or articles of association, which men might voluntarily enter into with each other.

===Chinese===
我们不要理会叛国一词这种虚假而诽谤的含义，而要看它在我们的母语里真实而正当的含义；看它在日常生活中的用法；看它在人与人自愿缔结的任何其他契约或社团章程里必然应有的真实含义。

===Original===
The true and legitimate meaning of the word “treason,” then, necessarily implies treachery, deceit, breach of faith. Without these, there can be no treason. A traitor is a betrayer—one who practices injury, *while professing friendship*. Benedict Arnold was a traitor, solely because, *while professing friendship for the American cause*, he attempted to injure it. An open enemy, however criminal in other respects, is no traitor.

===Chinese===
这样看来，「叛国」（treason）一词真实而正当的含义，必然蕴含着背弃（treachery）、欺诈、背信（breach of faith）。没有这些，就谈不上叛国。叛国者（traitor）是个出卖者——他*一面标榜交情*，一面下毒手。本尼迪克特·阿诺德（Benedict Arnold）之所以是叛国者，唯一的原因就是他*一面标榜同情美国的事业*，一面却图谋损害它。一个公开的敌人，无论别的方面多么罪恶，都不是叛国者。

===Original===
Neither does a man, who has once been my friend, become a traitor by becoming an enemy, if before doing me an injury, he gives me fair warning that he has become an enemy; and if he makes no unfair use of any advantage which my confidence, in the time of our friendship, had placed in his power.

===Chinese===
一个曾经是我的朋友的人，也不会单因为成了敌人就成了叛国者——只要他在下手加害之前，先明明白白地警告我：他已经与我反目；只要他没有不正当地利用我们亲善之时我因信任而放到他手里的任何便利。

===Original===
For example, our fathers—even if we were to admit them to have been wrong in other respects—certainly were not traitors *in fact, after* the fourth of July, 1776; since on that day they gave notice to the King of Great Britain that they repudiated his authority, and should wage war against him. And they made no unfair use of any advantages which his confidence had previously placed in their power.

===Chinese===
比方说，我们的先辈——就算我们承认他们在别的方面做错了——在 1776 年 7 月 4 日*之后*，断然不是*事实上的*叛国者；因为那一天他们知会英王：他们否认他的权威，将要同他兵戎相见。而他们也没有不正当地利用他此前出于信任交到他们手里的任何便利。

===Original===
It cannot be denied that, in the late war, the Southern people proved themselves to be open and avowed enemies, and not treacherous friends. It cannot be denied that they gave us fair warning that they would no longer be our political associates, but would, if need were, fight for a separation. It cannot be alleged that they made any unfair use of advantages which our confidence, in the time of our friendship, had placed in their power. Therefore they were not traitors in fact: and consequently not traitors within the meaning of the Constitution.

===Chinese===
无可否认，在最近这场战争中，南方人民证明了自己是公开的、公然声明的敌人，而非奸诈的朋友。无可否认，他们事先向我们打过明白的招呼：他们不再做我们的政治伙伴，必要的话，就要为分离而战。也不能指摘他们不正当地利用了亲善时期我们因信任而放到他们手里的任何便利。因此，他们不是事实上的叛国者；从而也不是宪法意义上的叛国者。

===Original===
Furthermore, men are not traitors *in fact*, who take up arms against the government, *without having disavowed allegiance to it*, provided they do it, either to resist the usurpations of the government, *or to resist what they sincerely believe to be such usurpations*.

===Chinese===
再者，起兵反抗政府而*未曾声明放弃对它的效忠（allegiance）*的人，也不是*事实上的*叛国者——只要他们这样做，是为了抵抗政府的篡夺，*或是抵抗他们真诚相信属于此类篡夺的行为*。

===Original===
It is a maxim of law that there can be no crime without a criminal intent. And this maxim is as applicable to treason as to any other crime. For example, our fathers were not traitors in fact, for resisting the British Crown, *before* the fourth of July, 1776—that is, before they had thrown off allegiance to him—provided they *honestly believed* that they were simply defending their rights against his usurpations. Even if they were mistaken in their law, that mistake, if an innocent one, could not make them traitors in fact.

===Chinese===
法律格言有云：无犯意则无罪行。这条格言适用于叛国罪，一如适用于任何其他罪行。比方说，我们的先辈在 1776 年 7 月 4 日*之前*——即在他们脱离对英王的效忠之前——因抵抗英国王室而并不是事实上的叛国者，只要他们*真诚地相信*自己不过是在捍卫权利、抵抗英王的篡夺。即便他们在法律上认识有误，只要这种错误出于无心，也不能使他们成为事实上的叛国者。

===Original===
For the same reason, the Southern people, if they sincerely believed—as it has been extensively, if not generally, conceded, at the North, that they did—in the so-called constitutional theory of “State Rights,” did not become traitors *in fact*, by acting upon it; and consequently not traitors within the meaning of the Constitution.

===Chinese===
出于同样的理由，南方人民倘若真诚信奉所谓「州权」（State Rights）的宪法理论——对此，北方也广泛地、纵非普遍地承认他们确曾如此信奉——那么他们据之以行动，并不因此成为*事实上的*叛国者，从而也不是宪法意义上的叛国者。

### III / 三

===Original===
The Constitution does not say *who* will become traitors, by “levying war against the United States, or adhering to their enemies, giving them aid and comfort.”

===Chinese===
宪法并未说明，*谁*会因「对合众国发动战争，或依附其敌人、给予其敌人援助与慰藉」而成为叛国者。

===Original===
It is, therefore, only by inference, or reasoning, that we can know *who* will become traitors by these acts.

===Chinese===
因此，*谁*会因这些行为而成为叛国者，我们只能靠推论或说理去获知。

===Original===
Certainly if Englishmen, Frenchmen, Austrians, or Italians, making no professions of support or friendship to the United States, levy war against them, or adhere to their enemies, giving them aid and comfort, they do not thereby make themselves traitors, within the meaning of the Constitution; and why? Solely because they would not be traitors *in fact*. Making no professions of support or friendship, they would practice no treachery, deceit, or breach of faith. But if they should voluntarily enter either the civil or military service of the United States, and pledge fidelity to them, (*without* being naturalized,) and should then betray the trusts reposed in them, either by turning their guns against the United States, or by giving aid and comfort to their enemies, they would be traitors *in fact*; and therefore traitors within the meaning of the Constitution; and could be lawfully punished as such.

===Chinese===
当然，如果英国人、法国人、奥地利人或意大利人，从未对美国表示过支持或友好，却对美国发动战争，或依附其敌人、给予援助与慰藉，他们并不因此就成为宪法意义上的叛国者。为什么？唯一的理由是：他们不会是*事实上的*叛国者。既未表示过支持或友好，也就不会有什么背弃、欺诈或背信。但是，如果他们自愿加入美国的文职或军职，宣誓效忠美国（*即便并未*归化入籍），随后却背弃寄托于他们的信任——或调转枪口对准美国，或给予美国的敌人援助与慰藉——那他们就是*事实上的*叛国者，因而也是宪法意义上的叛国者，可以依法受到惩处。

===Original===
There is not, in the Constitution, a syllable that implies that persons, born within the territorial limits of the United States, have allegiance imposed upon them on account of their birth in the country, or that they will be judged by any different rule, on the subject of treason, than persons of foreign birth. And there is no power, in Congress, to add to, or alter, the language of the Constitution, on this point, so as to make it more comprehensive than it now is. Therefore treason in fact—that is, actual treachery, deceit, or breach of faith—must be shown in the case of a native of the United States, equally as in the case of a foreigner, before he can be said to be a traitor.

===Chinese===
宪法中没有一个音节暗示：凡出生于美国疆域之内的人，仅因其出生于这个国家，效忠义务便加诸其身；也没有一个音节暗示：在叛国问题上，裁判他们所适用的规则可与外国出生者有所不同。国会（Congress）也无权在这一点上对宪法的文字增删改动，使其适用范围比现在更广。因此，土生的美国人同外国人一样，必须先证明存在事实上的叛国——即实际的背弃、欺诈或背信——然后才能说他是叛国者。

===Original===
Congress have seen that the language of the Constitution was insufficient, *of itself*, to make a man a traitor—on the ground of birth in this country—who levies war against the United States, but practices no treachery, deceit, or breach of faith. They have, therefore—although they had no constitutional power to do so—apparently attempted to enlarge the language of the Constitution on this point. And they have enacted:

===Chinese===
国会已经看出，单凭生于这个国家的理由，宪法文字*本身*不足以使一个对美国发动战争、却没有背弃、欺诈或背信之行的人成为叛国者。于是他们——尽管并无此等宪法权力——显然试图在这一方面扩张宪法的文字。他们制定了这样的法令：

===Original===
> “That if any person or persons, *owing allegiance to the United States of America*, shall levy war against them, or shall adhere to their enemies, giving them aid and comfort, [ … ] such person or persons shall be adjudged guilty of treason against the United States, and shall suffer death.”
> — —Statute, April 30, 1790, Section 1.

===Chinese===
> 「凡*对美利坚合众国负有效忠义务*之任何个人或众人，如对合众国发动战争，或依附其敌人、给予其援助与慰藉，［……］应判定犯有对合众国之叛国罪，处以死刑。」
> ——1790 年 4 月 30 日法令（Statute），第 1 条。

===Original===
It would be a sufficient answer to this enactment to say that it is utterly unconstitutional, if its effect would be to make any man a traitor, who would not have been one under the language of the Constitution alone.

===Chinese===
对这项法令，回答一句便已足够：倘若其效果竟是使任何依据宪法文字本身并不成为叛国者的人成了叛国者，那它便是彻头彻尾违宪的。

===Original===
The whole pith of the act lies in the words, “persons owing allegiance to the United States.” But this language really leaves the question where it was before, for it does not attempt to show or declare who *does* “owe allegiance to the United States;” although those who passed the act, no doubt thought, or wished others to think, that allegiance was to be presumed (as is done under other governments) against all born in this country, (unless possibly slaves).

===Chinese===
该法令的全部要害，全在「对合众国负有效忠义务的人」这一措辞之中。但这番措辞其实把问题依然留在原处，因为它并未试图表明或宣告*谁*「对合众国负有效忠义务」；尽管通过该法令的人，无疑指望着——或者希望别人以为——效忠可以（像其他政府治下那样）对凡生于本国之人一体推定（或许奴隶除外）。

===Original===
The Constitution itself, uses no such word as “allegiance,” “sovereignty,” “loyalty,” “subject,” or any other term, such as is used by other governments, to signify the services, fidelity, obedience, or other duty, which the people are assumed to owe to their government, regardless of their own will in the matter. As the Constitution professes to rest wholly on consent, no one can owe allegiance, service, obedience, or any other duty to it, or to the government created by it, except with his own consent.

===Chinese===
宪法本身并不使用「效忠」（allegiance）、「主权」（sovereignty）、「忠诚」（loyalty）、「臣民」（subject）这类字眼，也不使用其他政府所用的任何术语，去指称人民被认定欠其政府的服役、忠诚、服从或其他义务，而不问人民自己在此事上的意愿。既然宪法自称完全立足于同意，那么除非本人同意，谁也不欠宪法或依宪法而设的政府任何效忠、服役、服从或其他义务。

===Original===
The word “allegiance” comes from the Latin words *ad* and *ligo*, signifying “to bind to.” Thus a man under allegiance to a government, is a man *bound to it*; or bound to yield it support and fidelity. And governments, *founded otherwise than on consent*, hold that all persons born under them, are under allegiance to them: that is, are bound to render them support, fidelity, and obedience; and are traitors if they resist them.

===Chinese===
「效忠」一词源自拉丁语的 *ad* 与 *ligo*，意为「绑缚于」。因此，效忠于一个政府的人，就是*被绑缚于该政府*的人，即有义务给它支持和忠诚。而那些*并非建立在同意之上*的政府则宣称：凡生于其治下者，皆对其负有效忠义务；也就是说，有义务给予它们支持、忠诚和服从，抵抗者即为叛国者。

===Original===
But it is obvious that, *in truth and in fact*, no one but himself can bind anyone to support any government. And our Constitution admits this fact when it concedes that it derives its authority wholly from the consent of the people. And the word treason is to be understood in accordance with that idea.

===Chinese===
但显而易见，*实实在在地*说，除了本人，谁也不能绑缚任何人去支持任何政府。我们的宪法也承认这一事实——它自认其权威完全来自人民的同意。「叛国」一词，也应按这一观念来理解。

===Original===
It is conceded that a person of foreign birth comes under allegiance to our government only by special voluntary contract. If a native has allegiance imposed upon him, against his will, he is in a worse condition than the foreigner; for the latter can do as he pleases about assuming that obligation. The accepted interpretation of the Constitution, therefore, makes the foreigner a free person, on this point, while it makes the native a slave.

===Chinese===
人们承认，外国出生者唯有通过一项特别的自愿契约，才受我们政府效忠义务的约束。倘若土生者的效忠是违背其意愿而被强加的，那他的处境就比外国人更糟；因为外国人是否承担那一义务，悉听尊便。于是，流行的宪法解释在这一点上使外国人成为自由人，却使土生者成为奴隶。

===Original===
The only difference—if there be any—between natives and foreigners, in respect of allegiance, is, that a native has a *right*—offered to him by the Constitution—to come under allegiance to the government, if he so please; and thus entitle himself to membership in the body politic. His allegiance cannot be refused. Whereas a foreigner’s allegiance can be refused, if the government so please.

===Chinese===
就效忠而言，土生者与外国人之间*如果说有什么*差别，那便在于：土生者享有一项*权利*——由宪法提供给他的一项权利——他若愿意，即可归入对该政府的效忠之下，从而取得政治体（body politic）成员的资格。他的效忠不能被拒绝；而外国人的效忠，政府若不愿意，则可以拒绝。

### IV / 四

===Original===
The Constitution certainly supposes that the crime of treason can be committed only by man, as an individual. It would be very curious to see a man indicted, convicted, or hanged, otherwise than as an individual; or accused of having committed his treason otherwise than as an individual. And yet it is clearly impossible that anyone can be personally guilty of treason, can be a traitor *in fact*, unless he, as an individual, has in some way voluntarily pledged his faith and fidelity to the government. Certainly no man, or body of men, could pledge it for him, without his consent; and no man, or body of men, have any right to presume it against him, when he has not pledged it himself.

===Chinese===
宪法无疑设定：叛国罪只能由个人以其个人身份犯下。若看到一个人不是以个人身份遭到起诉、定罪或绞刑，或不是以个人身份被控犯下叛国罪行，那可就怪哉了。然而，同样清楚的是：除非他作为个人，曾以某种方式自愿向政府许诺自己的信义与忠诚，否则任何人都不可能亲身负叛国之罪、成为*事实上的*叛国者。诚然，任何人或任何团体，都不能未经他同意而替他作此许诺；而在他未曾亲自许诺之时，任何人或任何团体也无权对他作此推定。

### V / 五

===Original===
It is plain, therefore, that if, when the Constitution says treason, it means treason—treason in fact, and nothing else—there is no ground at all for pretending that the Southern people have committed that crime. But if, on the other hand, when the Constitution says treason, it means what the Czar and the Kaiser mean by treason, then our government is, in principle, no better than theirs; and has no claim whatever to be considered a free government.

===Chinese===
因此，道理很明白：如果宪法说叛国时指的就是叛国——*事实上的*叛国，别无其他——那么，宣称南方人民犯了这种罪，便全无根据。但反过来，如果宪法说叛国时，指的是沙皇和德皇所谓的那种叛国，那么我们的政府在原则上并不比他们高明，也就根本没有资格被看作自由政府。

### VI / 六

===Original===
One essential of a free government is that it rest wholly on voluntary support. And one certain proof that a government is not free, is that it coerces more or less persons to support it, against their will. All governments, the worst on earth, and the most tyrannical on earth, are free governments to that portion of the people who voluntarily support them. And all governments—though the best on earth in other respects—are nevertheless tyrannies to that portion of the people—whether few or many—who are compelled to support them against their will. A government is like a church, or any other institution, in these respects. There is no other criterion whatever, by which to determine whether a government is a free one, or not, than the single one of its depending, or not depending, solely on voluntary support.

===Chinese===
自由政府的一个本质特征，是它完全依靠自愿的支持。而政府不自由的一个确凿证明，是它强使或多或少的人违心支持它。一切政府，哪怕是世上最坏、最专制的政府，对于自愿支持它的那部分人来说，都是自由政府。而一切政府——纵在其他方面是世上最好的政府——对于被迫违心支持它的那部分人来说，无论其人数或多或少，都仍然是暴政。在这些方面，政府与教会或任何其他机构并无不同。判定一个政府是否自由，除了它是否仅仅依靠自愿的支持这一条标准之外，再没有任何别的标准。

### VII / 七

===Original===
No middle ground is possible on this subject. Either “taxation without consent is robbery,” or it is not. If it is *not*, then any number of men, who choose, may at any time associate; call themselves a government; assume absolute authority over all weaker than themselves; plunder them at will; and kill them if they resist. If, on the other hand, “taxation without consent *is* robbery,” it necessarily follows that every man who has not consented to be taxed, has the same natural right to defend his property against a tax-gatherer, that he has to defend it against a highwayman.

===Chinese===
在这个问题上没有中间道路可走。「未经同意的征税即抢劫」，要么成立，要么不成立。如果*不*成立，那么无论多少人，只要愿意，随时都可以结伙，自命为政府，对一切弱于己者攫取绝对权威，随意劫掠，谁若抵抗便加以杀戮。反过来，如果「未经同意的征税*就是*抢劫」，那就必然随之得出：凡未同意纳税的人，都有同样的自然权利，抵御收税吏就像抵御拦路劫匪（highwayman）一样，保卫自己的财产。

### VIII / 八

===Original===
It is perhaps unnecessary to say that the principles of this argument are as applicable to the State governments, as to the national one.

===Chinese===
这一论证的原理既适用于联邦政府，也同样适用于各州政府——这一点或许无须多说。

===Original===
The opinions of the South, on the subjects of allegiance and treason, have been equally erroneous with those of the North. The only difference between them, has been, that the South has held that a man was (primarily) under involuntary allegiance to the *State* government; while the North held that he was (primarily) under a similar allegiance to the United States government; whereas, in truth, he was under no involuntary allegiance to either.

===Chinese===
在效忠与叛国问题上，南方的见解与北方犯了同样的错误。双方唯一的分歧在于：南方认为，人（首先）对*州*政府负有非自愿的效忠；北方则认为，人（首先）对美国政府负有同样的效忠；而实际上，人对两者都不负有任何非自愿的效忠。

### IX / 九

===Original===
Obviously there can be no law of treason more stringent than has now been stated, consistently with political liberty. In the very nature of things there can never be any liberty for the weaker party, on any other principle; and political liberty always means liberty for the weaker party. It is only the weaker party that is ever oppressed. The strong are always free by virtue of their superior strength. So long as government is a mere contest as to which of two parties shall rule the other, the weaker must always succumb. And whether the contest be carried on with ballots or bullets, the principle is the same; for under the theory of government now prevailing, the ballot either signifies a bullet, or it signifies nothing. And no one can consistently use a ballot, unless he intends to use a bullet, if the latter should be needed to insure submission to the former.

===Chinese===
显而易见，凡与政治自由相容，叛国法就不可能比上文所述的更为严苛。按事物的本性，除这一原则之外，弱者永远得不到任何自由；而政治自由从来就意味着弱者的自由。受压迫的从来只是弱者；强者凭其优势的实力永远是自由的。只要政府仍不过是两派之中谁统治谁的角逐，弱者就必然屈服。而这场角逐无论用选票进行还是用子弹进行，道理都一样；因为在如今盛行的政府理论之下，选票要么意味着子弹，要么就什么也不是。任何人若不打算在必要时用子弹来确保别人服从那张选票，他使用选票就不能自圆其说。

### X / 十

===Original===
The practical difficulty with our government has been, that most of those who have administered it, have taken it for granted that the Constitution, *as it is written* was a thing of no importance; that it neither said what it meant, nor meant what it said; that it was gotten up by swindlers, (as many of its authors doubtless were,) who said a great many good things, which they did not mean, and meant a great many bad things, which they dared not say; that these men, under the false pretence of a government resting on the consent of the whole people, designed to entrap them into a government of a part, who should be powerful and fraudulent enough to cheat the weaker portion out of all the good things that were said, but not meant, and subject them to all the bad things that were meant, but not said. And most of those who have administered the government, have assumed that all these swindling intentions were to be carried into effect, in the place of the written Constitution. Of all these swindles, the treason swindle is the most flagitious. It is the most flagitious, because it is equally flagitious, in principle, with any; and it includes all the others. It is the instrumentality by which all the others are made effective. A government that can at pleasure accuse, shoot, and hang men, as traitors, for the one general offence of refusing to surrender themselves and their property unreservedly to its arbitrary will, can practice any and all special and particular oppressions it pleases.

===Chinese===
我们政府在实践上的症结一向是：历来临理政府的大多数人，都把这样的事当作理所当然：宪法*白纸黑字所写的*内容无足轻重；它说的不是它所指的，它指的也不是它所说的；它是一伙骗子（其起草者中无疑颇多此辈）炮制出来的东西：这些人说了许多动听的话却并不作此想，存了许多坏的念头却不敢说出口；这些人打着政府立足于全体人民之同意的虚伪旗号，蓄意以一个部分人的政府诱全体人民入彀——这一部分人只须足够强大、足够奸诈，便可把说过而并未作数的一切美事从较弱的那部分人手中骗光，再把想做而不敢明言的一切恶事尽数加于他们身上。而历来临理政府的大多数人都认定：应当让这一切欺诈意图付诸实施，以取代那部成文宪法。在这一切骗局之中，叛国骗局最为罪大恶极。说它最罪大恶极，是因为它在原则上同任何其他骗局一样恶毒，并且把其余一切骗局囊括在内；正是凭借这个工具，其余一切骗局才得以奏效。一个政府，倘能随意把人当作叛国者加以控告、枪杀、绞死，罪名只有一个总名：拒绝把自己和自己的财产毫无保留地交给它的专断意志摆布——那么，任何特殊的、具体的压迫，它都做得出来，且随心所欲。

===Original===
The result—and a natural one—has been that we have had governments, State and national, devoted to nearly every grade and species of crime that governments have ever practised upon their victims; and these crimes have culminated in a war that has cost a million of lives; a war carried on, upon one side, for chattel slavery, and on the other for political slavery; upon neither for liberty, justice, or truth. And these crimes have been committed, and this war waged, by men, and the descendants of men, who, less than a hundred years ago, said that all men were equal, and could owe neither service to individuals, nor allegiance to governments, except with their own consent.

===Chinese===
结果——也是顺理成章的结果——便是：我们的各州政府与联邦政府，几乎专力于政府曾经加诸其受害者的每一等第、每一品类的罪行；这些罪行最终达于顶点，酿成一场夺去百万生命的大战：一方为动产奴隶制（chattel slavery）而战，另一方为政治奴役而战；没有一方是为自由、正义或真理而战。而犯下这些罪行、进行这场战争的，正是那些人以及那些人的后裔——不到一百年前，他们曾经说，人人生而平等，非经本人同意，对个人不负服役之责，对政府不负效忠之义。

### XI / 十一

===Original===
No attempt or pretence, that was ever carried into practical operation amongst civilized men—unless possibly the pretence of a “Divine Right,” on the part of some, to govern and enslave others—embodied so much of shameless absurdity, falsehood, impudence, robbery, usurpation, tyranny, and villany of every kind, as the attempt or pretence of establishing a government *by consent*, and getting the actual consent of only so many as may be necessary to keep the rest in subjection by force. Such a government is a mere conspiracy of the strong against the weak. It no more rests on consent than does the worst government on earth.

===Chinese===
在文明人当中曾经付诸实行的种种图谋或借口之中——或许唯有某些人自称「君权神授」（Divine Right）、统治并奴役他人的那套借口可以除外——没有哪一种像那所谓*经同意*建立政府的图谋或借口那样，包藏着这样多的无耻谬妄、虚假、无赖、劫夺、篡窃、暴虐与形形色色的卑劣：这种政府所取得的实际同意，仅仅来自那么一些人——其数目恰以能靠武力把其余的人压制于臣服为限。这样的政府，不过是强者对付弱者的一个阴谋。它并不比世上最坏的政府更多地立足于同意。

===Original===
What substitute for their consent is offered to the weaker party, whose rights are thus annihilated, struck out of existence, by the stronger? Only this: *Their consent is presumed!* That is, these usurpers condescendingly and graciously *presume* that those whom they enslave, *consent* to surrender their all of life, liberty, and property into the hands of those who thus usurp dominion over them! And it is pretended that this presumption of their consent—when no actual consent has been given—is sufficient to save the rights of the victims, and to justify the usurpers! As well might the highwayman pretend to justify himself by presuming that the traveller *consents* to part with his money. As well might the assassin justify himself by simply *presuming* that his victim consents to part with his life. As well might the holder of chattel slaves attempt to justify himself by presuming that they consent to his authority, and to the whips and the robbery which he practises upon them. The presumption is simply a presumption that the weaker party consent to be slaves.

===Chinese===
权利就这样被强者取消、抹杀、归于乌有的弱者，人家拿什么来顶替他们的同意呢？只有这一句：*推定他们的同意！*也就是说，这些篡夺者屈尊俯就、宽宏大量地*推定*：被他们奴役的人*同意*把自己的生命、自由和财产的一切，统统交进那班如此篡夺统治之权的人手里！而且还佯称：这种对他们同意的推定——在实际的同意根本未曾给出的时候——足以保全受害者的权利，足以为篡夺者正名！这就好比拦路劫匪也可以自认有理，说他*推定*旅客*同意*交出自己的钱财；也好比刺客可以自我辩白，说他径直*推定*受害者同意交出自己的性命；又好比动产奴隶主可以试图自证正当，说他推定奴隶们同意他的权威，同意他加诸他们的鞭笞与劫夺。这种推定，说穿了，无非是推定弱者甘愿为奴。

===Original===
Such is the presumption on which alone our government relies to justify the power it maintains over its unwilling subjects. And it was to establish that presumption as the inexorable and perpetual law of this country, that so much money and blood have been expended.

===Chinese===
我们的政府要为它加于不顺服的「臣民」的权力作辩护，所倚仗的唯独是这种推定。而耗费了那么多的金钱与鲜血，正是为了把这种推定确立为这个国家不容抗拒、永世不改的法律。
