## XII / 十二

===Original===
They drove home in silence, Mrs. Lee disturbed with anxieties and doubts, partly caused by her sister, partly by Mr. Ratcliffe; Sybil divided between amusement at Victoria’s conquest, and alarm at her own boldness in meddling with her sister’s affairs. Desperation, however, was stronger than fear. She made up her mind that further suspense was not to be endured; she would fight her battle now before another hour was lost; surely no time could be better. A few moments brought them to their door. Mrs. Lee had told her maid not to wait for them, and they were alone. The fire was still alive on Madeleine’s hearth, and she threw more wood upon it. Then she insisted that Sybil must go to bed at once. But Sybil refused; she felt quite well, she said, and not in the least sleepy; she had a great deal to talk about, and wanted to get it off her mind. Nevertheless, her feminine regard for the “Dawn in June” led her to postpone what she had to say until with Madeleine’s help she had laid the triumph of the ball carefully aside; then, putting on her dressing-gown, and hastily plunging Carrington’s letter into her breast, like a concealed weapon, she hurried back to Madeleine’s room and established herself in a chair before the fire. There, after a moment’s pause, the two women began their long-deferred trial of strength, in which the match was so nearly equal as to make the result doubtful; for, if Madeleine were much the cleverer, Sybil in this case knew much better what she wanted, and had a clear idea how she meant to gain it, while Madeleine, unsuspicious of attack, had no plan of defence at all.

===Chinese===
马车默默地把她们送回家。李夫人心乱如麻，满是焦虑疑惧，一半因妹妹而起，一半因拉特克利夫先生而起；西比尔则又是好笑又是心惊——好笑的是维多利亚旗开得胜，心惊的是自己斗胆插手了姐姐的事。不过，绝望终究压过了恐惧。她打定了主意：这悬而不决的日子再也熬不下去了，这一仗要现在就打，一个钟头也不能再丢；的确，再没有比这更好的时机了。不多时，她们到了自家门口。李夫人早吩咐侍女不必等门，家里只有她们两个。马德琳壁炉里的火还燃着，她又添了几块柴。随后她坚持西比尔必须立刻去睡。西比尔却不肯；她说自己好好的，一点也不困；有一大篇话要讲，不吐出来心里放不下。话虽如此，她那一份对“六月的黎明”的女性式顾惜，还是让她把要讲的话暂且按下，先在马德琳的帮助下，把舞会上这件大获全胜的杰作仔仔细细安放妥当；然后披上晨衣，把卡林顿的信匆匆往怀里一插，像藏一件暗器，赶回马德琳的房间，在炉火前一张椅子里坐定。顿了一顿，两个女人便开始了这场一拖再拖的角力。这一局双方几乎旗鼓相当，胜负难卜；因为马德琳固然聪明得多，西比尔这一次却远比她更明白要的是什么、又打算怎样到手；而马德琳全不料会遭袭，压根没有半点守御的布置。

===Original===
“Madeleine,” began Sybil, solemnly, and with a violent palpitation of the heart, “I want you to tell me something.”

===Chinese===
“马德琳，”西比尔开了口，神色郑重，心口怦怦狂跳，“我要你告诉我一件事。”

===Original===
“What is it, my child?” said Mrs. Lee, puzzled, and yet half ready to see that there must be some connection between her sister’s coming question and the sudden illness at the ball, which had disappeared as suddenly as it came.

===Chinese===
“什么事，我的孩子？”李夫人问，心里困惑，却又隐隐觉得，妹妹即将出口的问话，同舞会上那场来得突然、去得也突然的急病，必定有几分牵连。

===Original===
“Do you mean to marry Mr. Ratcliffe?”

===Chinese===
“你是不是打算嫁给拉特克利夫先生？”

===Original===
Poor Mrs. Lee was quite disconcerted by the directness of the attack. This fatal question met her at every turn. Hardly had she succeeded in escaping from it at the ball scarcely an hour ago, by a stroke of good fortune for which she now began to see she was indebted to Sybil, and here it was again presented to her face like a pistol. The whole town, then, was asking it. Ratcliffe’s offer must have been seen by half Washington, and her reply was awaited by an immense audience, as though she were a political returning-board. Her disgust was intense, and her first answer to Sybil was a quick inquiry:

===Chinese===
可怜的李夫人被这一击的直截打得方寸大乱。这个致命的问题走到哪儿撞到哪儿。还不到一个钟头之前，在舞会上，她多亏一桩侥幸才从这问题底下脱了身——如今她才开始明白，这桩侥幸还得谢西比尔——谁知它转眼又像一支手枪，直挺挺递到她脸前。这么说来，全城都在问它了。拉特克利夫的求婚，必定已被半个华盛顿看在眼里；无数看客正等着她的裁决，仿佛她是一个政治选票申报委员会。她心里的厌恶无以复加，对西比尔的第一个回答，是急急的反问：

===Original===
“Why do you ask such a question? have you heard anything﻿—has anyone talked about it to you?”

===Chinese===
“你怎么问起这样的问题？你听见了什么——可有人同你谈起过？”

===Original===
“No!” replied Sybil; “but I must know; I can see for myself without being told, that Mr. Racliffe is trying to make you marry him. I don’t ask out of curiosity; this is something that concerns me nearly as much as it does you yourself. Please tell me! don’t treat me like a child any longer! let me know what you are thinking about! I am so tired of being left in the dark! You have no idea how much this thing weighs on me. Oh, Maude, I shall never be happy again until you trust me about this.”

===Chinese===
“没有！”西比尔回答，“可我必须知道；不用人说，我自己也看得出来，拉特克利夫先生在设法让你嫁给他。我不是出于好奇；这件事同我切身相关，几乎不下于同你自己。求你告诉我！别再拿我当孩子！让我知道你在想什么！被蒙在鼓里，我受够了！你想不出这件事压在我心上有多沉。啊，莫德，这件事上你不信我，我再也不会快活了。”

===Original===
Mrs. Lee felt a little pang of conscience, and seemed suddenly to become conscious of a new coil, tightening about her, in this wretched complication. Unable to see her way, ignorant of her sister’s motives, urged on by the idea that Sybil’s happiness was involved, she was now charged with want of feeling, and called upon for a direct answer to a plain question. How could she aver that she did not mean to marry Mr. Ratcliffe? to say this would be to shut the door on all the objects she had at heart. If a direct answer must be given, it was better to say “Yes!” and have it over; better to leap blindly and see what came of it. Mrs. Lee, therefore, with an internal gasp, but with no visible sign of excitement, said, as though she were in a dream:

===Chinese===
李夫人良心上微微刺了一下，又仿佛猛然觉出，在这团可悲的乱麻里，又有一道绳圈收紧了她的周身。她认不清路数，摸不透妹妹的用意，又被“西比尔的幸福系于此举”的念头催逼着，如今倒落了个没有心肝的罪名，还被逼着对一个明白问题作直截了当的回答。她怎么能一口咬定自己不打算嫁拉特克利夫先生呢？这么说，就等于把她心心念念的目标一律关在门外。既然非直接回答不可，倒不如说“是”，一了百了；不如闭着眼纵身一跳，看看落个什么结果。于是李夫人心里倒抽一口气，面上不动声色，像说梦话似的开了口：

===Original===
“Well, Sybil, I *will* tell you. I would have told you long ago if I had known myself. Yes! I have made up my mind to marry Mr. Ratcliffe!”

===Chinese===
“好吧，西比尔，我*一定*告诉你。要是我早明白了自己的心，早就告诉你了。是的！我拿定了主意，要嫁给拉特克利夫先生！”

===Original===
Sybil sprang to her feet with a cry: “And have you told him so?” she asked.

===Chinese===
西比尔喊了一声，跳了起来：“那你也这么告诉他了？”她问。

===Original===
“No! you came and interrupted us just as we were speaking. I was glad you did come, for it gives me a little time to think. But I am decided now. I shall tell him tomorrow.”

===Chinese===
“还没有！我们正说着话，你就来打断了。你来得也好，让我有工夫想一想。可如今我定了。明天我就告诉他。”

===Original===
This was not said with the air or one whose heart beat warmly at the thought of confessing her love. Mrs. Lee spoke mechanically, and almost with an effort. Sybil flung herself with all her energy upon her sister; violently excited, and eager to make herself heard, without waiting for arguments, she broke out into a torrent of entreaties:

===Chinese===
这几句话说出口时，全不像一个心事倾吐在即、心口热乎乎直跳的人该有的气色。李夫人说得很机械，几乎还带着几分吃力。西比尔使出全身的劲儿扑向姐姐；她激愤已极，急着要人听她说话，等不及摆什么论证，便迸出一连串的哀恳：

===Original===
“Oh, don’t, don’t, don’t! Oh, please, please, don’t, my dearest, dearest Maude! unless you want to break my heart, don’t marry that man! You can’t love him! You can never be happy with him! he will take you away to Peonia, and you will die there! I shall never see you again! He will make you unhappy; he will beat you, I know he will! Oh, if you care for me at all, don’t marry him! Send him away! don’t see him again! let us go ourselves, now, in the morning train, before he comes back. I’m all ready; I’ll pack everything for you; we’ll go to Newport; to Europe﻿—anywhere, to be out of his reach!”

===Chinese===
“啊，别，别，别！啊，求你，求你，别这样，我最亲最亲的莫德！除非你想把我的心踩碎，千万别嫁给那个人！你不可能爱他！你同他永远不会幸福！他会把你带到皮奥尼亚去，你会死在那儿的！我就再也见不着你了！他会叫你受苦；他会打你，我知道他一定会！啊，你若还有一点疼我，就别嫁给他！把他打发走！别再见他！咱们这就自己走，搭早晨的火车，趁他还没回来。我全预备好了；你的东西我都替你收拾；咱们去纽波特，去欧洲——去哪儿都行，只要够不着他的手！”

===Original===
With this passionate appeal, Sybil threw herself on her knees by her sister’s side, and, clasping her arms around Madeleine’s waist, sobbed as though her heart were already broken. Had Carrington seen her then he must have admitted that she had carried out his instructions to the letter. She was quite honest, too, in it all. She meant what she said, and her tears were real tears that had been pent up for weeks. Unluckily, her logic was feeble. Her idea of Mr. Ratcliffe’s character was vague, and biased by mere theories of what a Prairie Giant of Peonia should be in his domestic relations. Her idea of Peonia, too, was indistinct. She was haunted by a vision of her sister, sitting on a horsehair sofa before an airtight iron stove in a small room with high, bare white walls, a chromolithograph on each, and at her side a marble-topped table surmounted by a glass vase containing funereal dried grasses; the only literature, Frank Leslie’s periodical and the *New York Ledger*, with a strong smell of cooking everywhere prevalent. Here she saw Madeleine receiving visitors, the wives of neighbours and constituents, who told her the Peonia news.

===Chinese===
西比尔这样声泪俱下地一求，便扑跪到姐姐身边，双臂搂住马德琳的腰，抽噎痛哭，仿佛心已经碎了。卡林顿若是看见她这时的模样，也只得承认，他的嘱咐她一字不差地照办了。这一切她也全是真心实意。她说的话句句当真，眼泪也是憋了几个星期的真眼泪。只可惜她的逻辑虚弱无力。她对拉特克利夫先生品格的想像是模糊的，还偏着一层纯理论的成见：一位皮奥尼亚的草原巨人，治起家来该是什么做派。她对皮奥尼亚的想像也是影影绰绰。她心头老驱不散一幅幻景：姐姐坐在一间小屋里，四壁雪白，又高又光，一面墙上一张石印彩画，身下一只马鬃沙发，面前一只能封得严严实实的铁炉子，手边一张大理石面小桌，桌上一只玻璃瓶，插着一蓬丧气沉沉的干草；全部读物，只有弗兰克·莱斯利（Frank Leslie）的画刊和《纽约账簿报》（New York Ledger），满屋子弥漫着一股浓重的油烟味。在这幅图景里，她看见马德琳坐在那儿会客，客人是街坊和选民家的太太们，给她带来皮奥尼亚的新闻。

===Original===
Notwithstanding her ignorant and unreasonable prejudice against western men and women, western towns and prairies, and, in short, everything western, down to western politics and western politicians, whom she perversely asserted to be the lowest of all western products, there was still some common sense in Sybil’s idea. When that inevitable hour struck for Mr. Ratcliffe, which strikes sooner or later for all politicians, and an ungrateful country permitted him to pine among his friends in Illinois, what did he propose to do with his wife? Did he seriously suppose that she, who was bored to death by New York, and had been able to find no permanent pleasure in Europe, would live quietly in the romantic village of Peonia? If not, did Mr. Ratcliffe imagine that they could find happiness in the enjoyment of each other’s society, and of Mrs. Lee’s income, in the excitements of Washington? In the ardour of his pursuit, Mr. Ratcliffe had accepted in advance any conditions which Mrs. Lee might impose, but if he really imagined that happiness and content lay on the purple rim of this sunset, he had more confidence in women and in money than a wider experience was ever likely to justify.

===Chinese===
尽管西比尔对西部的男男女女、西部的城镇和草原——一句话，对西部的一切，连西部的政治和西部的政客也算在内——怀着无知而不通情理的偏见，还执拗地断言西部政客是西部一切物产中最低等的货色，她的想法里到底还有几分常识。当那不可避免的一刻为拉特克利夫先生敲响时——这一刻或迟或早，一切政客都会等来——当忘恩负义的国家听任他在伊利诺伊的朋友堆里憔悴时，他打算把妻子怎么办？他当真以为，这位被纽约腻烦得要死、在欧洲也没能找到长久乐趣的女子，会安安静静地住进皮奥尼亚那座浪漫的村庄？若不然，拉特克利夫先生可是指望，凭着两个人彼此做伴、凭着李夫人的进项，在华盛顿的热闹里就能寻得幸福？在炽热的追求里，拉特克利夫先生预先一口应承了李夫人可能提出的一切条件；可他若当真以为幸福和知足就伏在这轮落日的紫边上，那他对女人和金钱的信心，可就远非更广的阅历所能证明的了。

===Original===
Whatever might be Mr. Ratcliffe’s schemes for dealing with these obstacles they could hardly be such as would satisfy Sybil, who, if inaccurate in her theories about Prairie Giants, yet understood women, and especially her sister, much better than Mr. Ratcliffe ever could do. Here she was safe, and it would have been better had she said no more, for Mrs. Lee, though staggered for a moment by her sister’s vehemence, was reassured by what seemed the absurdity of her fears. Madeleine rebelled against this hysterical violence of opposition, and became more fixed in her decision. She scolded her sister in good, set terms﻿—

===Chinese===
无论拉特克利夫先生有什么锦囊妙计来对付这些障碍，总难以是能叫西比尔满意的计较；她对草原巨人的理论纵然靠不住，对女人、尤其对自己的姐姐，却比拉特克利夫先生这辈子所能领会的还透亮得多。在这一点上她立得稳；而她也本该到此为止，因为李夫人虽然被妹妹的激烈撼了一撼，随即又被她那份显得荒唐的恐惧宽了心。马德琳对这种歇斯底里的暴力反对起了逆反，主意反而拿得更死。她拿出一套板正的话，把妹妹好生训了一顿——

===Original===
“Sybil, Sybil! you must not be so violent. Behave like a woman, and not like a spoiled child!”

===Chinese===
“西比尔，西比尔！不许这样撒泼。要拿出女人的样子，别像个惯坏了的孩子！”

===Original===
Mrs. Lee, like most persons who have to deal with spoiled or unspoiled children, resorted to severity, not so much because it was the proper way of dealing with them, as because she knew not what else to do. She was thoroughly uncomfortable and weary. She was not satisfied with herself or with her own motives. Doubt encompassed her on all sides, and her worst opponent was that sister whose happiness had turned the scale against her own judgment.

===Chinese===
李夫人同大多数不得不对付孩子的人一样——不管孩子惯坏没惯坏——都祭起了严厉；倒不全是严厉乃是对付孩子的正道，多半因为她不知道还有什么别的法子。她浑身不自在，乏透了。她对自己不满意，对自己的动机也不满意。疑虑四面围困，而她最狠的对头，正是那个拿幸福二字把她自己的判断压了秤的妹妹。

===Original===
Nevertheless her tactics answered their object of checking Sybil’s vehemence. Her sobs came to an end, and she presently rose with a quieter air.

===Chinese===
不过她的战术达到了目的，压住了西比尔的汹汹之势。抽噎止了，不多时她便起身，神色安静了些。

===Original===
“Madeleine,” said she, “do you really want to marry Mr. Ratcliffe?”

===Chinese===
“马德琳，”她说，“你当真想嫁拉特克利夫先生吗？”

===Original===
“What else can I do, my dear Sybil? I want to do whatever is for the best. I thought you might be pleased.”

===Chinese===
“不这样我还能怎样呢，亲爱的西比尔？我只想拣最好的去做。我还当你听了会高兴呢。”

===Original===
“You thought I might be pleased?” cried Sybil in astonishment. “What a strange idea! If you had ever spoken to me about it I should have told you that I hate him, and can’t understand how you can abide him. But I would rather marry him myself than see you marry him. I know that you will kill yourself with unhappiness when you have done it. Oh, Maude, please tell me that you won’t!” And Sybil began gently sobbing again, while she caressed her sister.

===Chinese===
“你当我听了会高兴？”西比尔惊得嚷起来，“多怪的念头！你若同我提起过一句，我早告诉你了：我恨他，不明白你怎么容得了他。可我宁肯自己嫁给他，也不肯看你嫁给他。我知道，这事你一旦做了，准把自己活活愁死。啊，莫德，求你说一声你不嫁！”说着，西比尔又轻轻抽泣起来，一面爱抚着姐姐。

Mrs. Lee was infinitely distressed. ===Original===
Thus it came about that at last Mrs. Lee spoke, with an appearance of decision far from representing her internal tremor.

===Chinese===
于是，李夫人终于开了口，那决断的口吻，同她心里的战栗全不相称。

===Original===
“Sybil, dear, I have made up my mind to marry Mr. Ratcliffe because there is no other way of making everyone happy. You need not be afraid of him. He is kind and generous. Besides, I can take care of myself; and I will take care of you too. Now let us not discuss it anymore. It is broad daylight, and we are both tired out.”

===Chinese===
“西比尔，亲爱的，我拿定主意嫁拉特克利夫先生，因为再没有别的法子叫人人都得幸福。你不必怕他。他和善，慷慨。再说，我会照顾自己；也会照顾你。这事咱们不要再谈了。天已大亮，咱们俩都乏透了。”

===Original===

===Chinese===
西比尔霎时完全平静下来，站到姐姐面前，仿佛两人的身份从今要倒个个儿似的，说道：

===Original===

===Chinese===

===Original===
Mrs. Lee, looking at her with more surprise than ever, could not force herself to speak; but she shook her head slowly and decidedly.

===Chinese===
李夫人望着她，比先前更惊，一句话也说不出，只缓缓地、决然地摇了摇头。

===Original===
