## III The First Round / 三 第一回合

===Original===
“After this, as it was now midday, and I had killed enough meat, we marched back triumphantly to camp, where I proceeded to concoct a stew of buffalo beef and compressed vegetables. When this was ready we ate the stew, and then I took a nap. About four o’clock, however, Gobo woke me up, and told me that the head man of one of Wambe’s kraals had arrived to see me. I ordered him to be brought up, and presently he came, a little, wizened, talkative old man, with a waistcloth round his middle, and a greasy, frayed kaross made of the skins of rock rabbits over his shoulders.

“I told him to sit down, and then abused him roundly. ‘What did he mean,’ I asked, ‘by disturbing me in this rude way? How did he dare to cause a person of my quality and evident importance to be awakened in order to interview his entirely contemptible self?’

“I spoke thus because I knew that it would produce an impression on him. Nobody, except a really great man, he would argue, would dare to speak to him in that fashion. Most savages are desperate bullies at heart, and look on insolence as a sign of power.

===Chinese===
“这事过后，时已正午，肉也打得够了，我们凯旋般开回营地，我便着手用牛肉和压缩蔬菜炖一锅菜。炖好之后我们吃了，随后我打了个盹。可是约莫四点钟，戈博把我叫醒，说万贝的一处克拉尔（kraal，班图人的村寨）的头人求见。我吩咐带他上来，不多时他就来了：一个小小的、干瘪的、话多的老头，腰间围一块腰布，肩上披一件油腻破烂的卡罗斯（kaross，兽皮披肩）——岩兔皮缝的。

“我叫他坐下，接着便痛痛快快把他骂了一顿。‘你是什么意思，’我问，‘这般无礼地搅扰我？你怎么敢为了会你这么个不值一提的货色，叫醒我这等身份、这等显而易见的重要人物？’

“我这样讲，因为我知道这会在他身上产生印象。他自会盘算：除非是真正的大人物，谁敢用这种口气同他说话。大多数野人骨子里都是十足的恶霸，把无礼看作权势的标记。

===Original===
“The old man instantly collapsed. He was utterly overcome, he said; his heart was split in two, and well realized the extent of his misbehaviour. But the occasion was very urgent. He heard that a mighty hunter was in the neighbourhood, a beautiful white man, how beautiful he could not have imagined had he not seen (this to me!), and he came to beg his assistance. The truth was, that three bull elephants such as no man ever saw had for years been the terror of their kraal, which was but a small place—a cattle kraal of the great chief Wambe’s, where they lived to keep the cattle. And now of late these elephants had done them much damage; but last night they had destroyed a whole patch of mealie land, and he feared that if they came back they would all starve next season for want of food. Would the mighty white man then be pleased to come and kill the elephants? It would be easy for him to do—oh, most easy! It was only necessary that he should hide himself in a tree, for there was a full moon, and then when the elephants appeared he would speak to them with the gun, and they would fall down dead, and there would be an end of their troubling.

“Of course I hummed and hawed, and made a great favour of consenting to his proposal, though really I was delighted to have such a chance. One of the conditions that I made was that a messenger should at once be despatched to Wambe, whose kraal was two days’ journey from where I was, telling him that I proposed to come and pay my respects to him in a few days, and to ask his formal permission to shoot in his country. Also I intimated that I was prepared to present him with *hongo*, that is, blackmail, and that I hoped to do a little trade with him in ivory, of which I heard he had a great quantity.

===Chinese===
“老头当即垮了下来。他说他完全折服了；他的心裂成了两半，深深明白自己罪过的分量。但事情实在紧急。他听说左近来了一位了不起的猎手，一位俊美的白人——不亲眼看见，简直无法想象有那么俊美（这话是对我说的！）——他是来恳求援手的。实情是：三头谁也没见过的公象，多年来一直是他们那座村寨的祸根。那不过是个小地方——大酋长万贝的一处牧牛寨，他们住在那里替他看牛。近来这些象屡屡糟害他们；昨夜里更毁掉整片玉米地，他生怕它们再来，来年青黄不接，全寨都要挨饿。那么，尊贵的白人大爷可肯赏光去把象打死？这对他是轻而易举的——啊，再容易不过！只消他藏到一棵树上，因为月圆，等象来了，他拿枪同它们说句话，它们便会应声倒毙，从此祸患永绝。

“我自然哼哼哈哈，摆足了架子才肯俯允所请，其实心里为有这等机会欢喜不迭。我提的条件之一，是即刻派一名信使去见万贝——他的克拉尔离我这里两天路程——告诉他过几天我打算登门拜望，并正式请求在他境内打猎的许可。我还示意，我准备奉上*洪戈*（hongo），也就是买路钱；又说希望能同他做一点象牙生意，听说他手里存货不少。

===Original===
“This message the old gentleman promised to despatch at once, though there was something about his manner which showed me that he was doubtful as to how it would be received. After that we struck our camp and moved on to the kraal, which we reached about an hour before sunset. This kraal was a collection of huts surrounded by a slight thorn-fence, perhaps there were ten of them in all. It was situated in a kloof of the mountain down which a rivulet flowed. The kloof was densely wooded, but for some distance above the kraal it was free from bush, and here on the rich deep ground brought down by the rivulet were the cultivated lands, in extent somewhere about twenty or twenty-five acres. On the kraal side of these lands stood a single hut, that served for a mealie store, which at the moment was used as a dwelling-place by an old woman, the first wife of our friend the head man.

“It appears that this lady, having had some difference of opinion with her husband about the extent of authority allowed to a younger and more amiable wife, had refused to dwell in the kraal any more, and, by way of marking her displeasure, had taken up her abode among the mealies. As the issue will show, she was, it happened, cutting off her nose to spite her face.

“Close by this hut grew a large baobab tree. A glance at the mealie grounds showed me that the old head man had not exaggerated the mischief done by the elephants to his crops, which were now getting ripe. Nearly half of the entire patch was destroyed. The great brutes had eaten all they could, and the rest they had trampled down. I went up to their spoor and started back in amazement—never had I seen such a spoor before. It was simply enormous, more especially that of one old bull, that carried, so said the natives, but a single tusk. One might have used any of the footprints for a hip-bath.

===Chinese===
“老先生答应即刻把口信发出去，只是他那做派里有点什么，让我看出发信的人对它会被如何接下心存疑虑。此后我们拔营，开到那座村寨去，日落前约一小时到了。这村寨是一群茅屋，围着一道单薄的荆棘篱，统共约有十座。它坐落在山间一道峡谷（kloof）里，一条小溪顺谷而下。峡谷里林木茂密，但在村寨上方一段却无灌木，就在这溪水冲积出的深厚沃土上，是他们的耕地，面积约二十到二十五英亩。耕地靠寨子的一侧立着一座孤零零的茅屋，原是存放玉米的仓房，眼下却住着一位老妇人——我们那位头人朋友的大老婆。

“原来这位夫人，为着丈夫许给一位更年轻也更和善的老婆多大的权柄，同他生了一场意见，从此不肯再住在寨里，为表不悦，索性搬到玉米中间住了下来。而后来的结果将表明，她这一着，恰是割了鼻子跟自己的脸过不去。

“茅屋近旁长着一棵高大的猴面包树。朝玉米地一望，我便知道老头人并没有夸大象群对他庄稼——庄稼正待成熟——的祸害：整片地毁掉了将近一半。那些巨兽吃饱了能吃的，余下的全踩倒了。我走到它们脚印前，惊得倒退——从未见过这样的蹄印：简直大得吓人，尤其是一头老公象的，土人说它只长一根牙。随便哪个脚印，都够拿来当坐浴盆使。

===Original===
“Having taken stock of the position, my next step was to make arrangements for the fray. The three bulls, according to the natives, had been spoored into the dense patch of bush above the kloof. Now it seemed to me very probable that they would return tonight to feed on the remainder of the ripening mealies. If so, there was a bright moon, and it struck me that by the exercise of a little ingenuity I might bag one or more of them without exposing myself to any risk, which, having the highest respect for the aggressive powers of bull elephants, was a great consideration to me.

“This then was my plan. To the right of the huts as you look up the kloof, and commanding the mealie lands, stands the baobab tree that I have mentioned. Into that baobab tree I made up my mind to go. Then if the elephants appeared I should get a shot at them. I announced my intentions to the head man of the kraal, who was delighted. ‘Now,’ he said, ‘his people might sleep in peace, for while the mighty white hunter sat aloft like a spirit watching over the welfare of his kraal what was there to fear?’

“I told him that he was an ungrateful brute to think of sleeping in peace while, perched like a wounded vulture on a tree, I watched for his welfare in wakeful sorrow; and once more he collapsed, and owned that my words were ‘sharp but just.’

===Chinese===
“把形势掂量过后，下一步便是布置这一场较量。照土人的说法，三头公象的踪迹已进了峡谷上方那片密灌木。我看它们今夜多半还要回来，吃那剩下的将熟的玉米。倘若如此，月色正好，我盘算着只消略施小计，便可收拾它们一头或几头，而不必自己冒什么风险——我对公象的攻击本领素来怀有最高的敬意，这一点对我可不是小事。

“我的计划是这样的：顺峡谷往上看，在茅屋右方、俯临玉米地的地方，立着我方才说的那棵猴面包树。我拿定主意就上那棵树去。这样象若来到，我便有枪可开。我把打算对寨里头人一说，他喜出望外。‘这下，’他说，‘他的人民可以安安稳稳睡觉了，因为尊贵的白人猎手高高在上，像神灵一样守护着他的村寨的福祉，还有什么可怕的呢？’

“我对他说，他真是个没良心的畜生：我在树上像一只负伤的秃鹫那样栖着，为他的福祉忧醒不寐，他倒想着安睡；于是他又一次垮了下去，承认我的话‘厉害，然而公道’。

“However, as I have said, confidence was completely restored; and that evening everybody in the kraal, including the superannuated victim of jealousy in the little hut where the mealie cobs were stored, went to bed with a sense of sweet security from elephants and all other animals that prowl by night.

“For my part, I pitched my camp below the kraal; and then, having procured a beam of wood from the head man—rather a rotten one, by the way—I set it across two boughs that ran out laterally from the baobab tree, at a height of about twenty-five feet from the ground, in such fashion that I and another man could sit upon it with our legs hanging down, and rest our backs against the bole of the tree. This done I went back to the camp and ate my supper. About nine o’clock, half-an-hour before the moonrise, I summoned Gobo, who, thinking that he had seen about enough of the delights of big game hunting for that day, did not altogether relish the job; and, despite his remonstrances, gave him my eight-bore to carry, I having the .570-express. Then we set out for the tree. It was very dark, but we found it without difficulty, though climbing it was a more complicated matter. However, at last we got up and sat down, like two little boys on a form that is too high for them, and waited. I did not dare to smoke, because I remembered the rhinoceros, and feared that the elephants might wind the tobacco if they should come my way, and this made the business more wearisome, so I fell to thinking and wondering at the completeness of the silence.

“At last the moon came up, and with it a moaning wind, at the breath of which the silence began to whisper mysteriously. Lonely enough in the newborn light looked the wide expanse of mountain, plain, and forest, more like some vision of a dream, some reflection from a fair world of peace beyond our ken, than the mere face of garish earth made soft with sleep. Indeed, had it not been for the fact that I was beginning to find the log on which I sat very hard, I should have grown quite sentimental over the beautiful sight; but I will defy anybody to become sentimental when seated in the damp, on a very rough beam of wood, and halfway up a tree. So I merely made a mental note that it was a particularly lovely night, and turned my attention to the prospect of elephants. But no elephants came, and after waiting for another hour or so, I think that what between weariness and disgust, I must have dropped into a gentle doze. Presently I awoke with a start. Gobo, who was perched close to me, but as far off as the beam would allow—for neither white man nor black like the aroma which each vows is the peculiar and disagreeable property of the other—was faintly, very faintly clicking his forefinger against his thumb. I knew by this signal, a very favourite one among native hunters and gun-bearers, that he must have seen or heard something. I looked at his face, and saw that he was staring excitedly towards the dim edge of the bush beyond the deep green line of mealies. I stared too, and listened. Presently I heard a soft large sound as though a giant were gently stretching out his hands and pressing back the ears of standing corn. Then came a pause, and then, out into the open majestically stalked the largest elephant I ever saw or ever shall see. Heavens! what a monster he was; and how the moonlight gleamed upon his one splendid tusk—for the other was missing—as he stood among the mealies gently moving his enormous ears to and fro, and testing the wind with his trunk. While I was still marvelling at his girth, and speculating upon the weight of that huge tusk, which I swore should be my tusk before very long, out stepped a second bull and stood beside him. He was not quite so tall, but he seemed to me to be almost thicker-set than the first; and even in that light I could see that both his tusks were perfect. Another pause, and the third emerged. He was shorter than either of the others, but higher in the shoulder than No. 2; and when I tell you, as I afterwards learnt from actual measurement, that the smallest of these mighty bulls measured twelve feet one and a half inches at the shoulder, it will give you some idea of their size. The three formed into line and stood still for a minute, the one-tusked bull gently caressing the elephant on the left with his trunk.

“Then they began to feed, walking forward and slightly to the right as they gathered great bunches of the sweet mealies and thrust them into their mouths. All this time they were more than a hundred and twenty yards away from me (this I knew, because I had paced the distances from the tree to various points), much too far to allow of my attempting a shot at them in that uncertain light. They fed in a semicircle, gradually drawing round towards the hut near my tree, in which the corn was stored and the old woman slept.

“然而，如我所说，人心是完完全全定下来了；那天晚上，寨里人人——连存放玉米棒子的小屋里那位妒火中烧的老牌受害者也算上——都怀着一种甜美的安全感上了床，觉得从此大象也好，夜里出没的其他走兽也好，都害不着他们了。

“至于我，我把营扎在寨子下方；然后向头人讨了一根木梁——顺带说一句，相当糟朽的一根——把它架在猴面包树横生的两根枝上，离地约二十五英尺高，架得我同另一个人可以坐在上面垂着腿，背靠树干。架好之后我回营吃了晚饭。约莫九点钟，月亮升起前半小时，我唤来戈博——他认定当天大猎的那份妙处已经见识得够多了，对这差事全无兴致；我不顾他的抗议，把八号枪交给他背，自己拿 .570 特快步枪。随后我们便向那棵树进发。天很黑，但我们没费事就找到了树，爬上去却费事些。不过我们终于上去了，坐下来，像两个坐在高得够不着地的条凳上的小男孩，等着。我不敢抽烟，因为记着犀牛的教训，生怕象若朝我来会闻见烟味，这使得这桩差事愈发无趣，我便胡思乱想起来，暗自诧异这寂静竟如此彻底。

“终于月亮上来了，随之而来一阵呜咽似的风，风息一吹，寂静开始神秘地窃窃私语。在新生的月光下，山峦、平原与森林的辽阔景象够多冷清的了，与其说是叫睡意软化了的、白晃晃的人间面目，不如说更像梦中的幻象，像我们视野之外某个美好的和平世界投来的倒影。说实话，若不是坐下的这根木梁渐渐硌得慌，这场美景真要叫我伤感起来；可是我倒要看看，谁能在潮湿里、在一根糙木头上、在半树高的地方伤感得起来。于是我仅在心里记下一笔：今夜月色格外可爱；便把心思转向大象上。可象没有来，又等了一个钟头上下，我想是一半困乏一半厌烦，我竟迷迷糊糊打起盹来。忽然我一惊而醒。戈博栖在我近旁——横梁容许的最远处，因为白人黑人彼此都嫌对方的气味，各自咬定那是对方独有而又讨嫌的物事——正极轻极轻地用食指叩着拇指。我凭这个信号——土人猎手和背枪人最爱用的一种——知道他必定看见了或听见了什么。我看他的脸，见他正兴奋地瞪着玉米那道深绿线外灌木的模糊边缘。我也瞪过去，也听。不久我听见一阵又软又大的声响，仿佛一个巨人轻轻伸出双手，把立着的玉米穗压向后方。然后一停；然后，踱进开阔地里来的，是我平生所见、也必是此生将见的最最大的一头象，步态庄严。天哪！好一个庞然大物；它立在玉米间，巨大的耳朵轻轻前后扇动，鼻子试探着风，月光在那根独牙上流转生辉——另一根没有了。我还在惊叹它的腰身，还在掂量那根巨牙的分量——我发誓不久它就得是我的牙——第二头公象迈出来，立在它旁边。它不及第一头高，但在我看来几乎比第一头还粗壮；即便在那样的光线下我也看得出，它两根牙齐全完好。再一顿，第三头也出来了。它比前两头都矮，肩膀却比二号还高；我告诉你们——这是我后来实地量得的——这三头巨象中最小的一头，肩高十二英尺一英寸半，你们由此可以想见它们的个头。三头排成一行，静静立了约一分钟，独牙象用鼻子轻轻爱抚着左边那头。

“随后它们开始进食，一边向前、稍稍偏右地移动，一边卷起一大把一大把甜玉米塞进嘴里。这期间它们始终离我一百二十多码（这我知道，因为我从树下步测过到各点的距离），在那样的微光里，太远了，开枪是没有把握的。它们沿着一条半圆弧吃过去，渐渐兜向我树旁那座茅屋——屋里存着玉米，睡着那位老妇人。

“This went on for between an hour and an hour and a half, till, what between excitement and hope, that maketh the heart sick, I grew so weary that I was actually contemplating a descent from the tree and a moonlight stalk. Such an act in ground so open would have been that of a stark staring lunatic, and that I should even have been contemplating it will show you the condition of my mind. But everything comes to him who knows how to wait, and sometimes too to him who doesn’t, and so at last those elephants, or rather one of them, came to me.

“After they had fed their fill, which was a very large one, the noble three stood once more in line some seventy yards to the left of the hut, and on the edge of the cultivated lands, or in all about eighty-five yards from where I was perched. Then at last the one with a single tusk made a peculiar rattling noise in his trunk, just as though he were blowing his nose, and without more ado began to walk deliberately toward the hut where the old woman slept. I made my rifle ready and glanced up at the moon, only to discover that a new complication was looming in the immediate future. I have said that a wind rose with the moon. Well, the wind brought rain-clouds along its track. Several light ones had already lessened the light for a little while, though without obscuring it, and now two more were coming up rapidly, both of them very black and dense. The first cloud was small and long, and the one behind big and broad. I remember noticing that the pair of them bore a most comical resemblance to a dray drawn by a very long rawboned horse. As luck would have it, just as the elephant arrived within twenty-five yards or so of me, the head of the horse-cloud floated over the face of the moon, rendering it impossible for me to fire. In the faint twilight which remained, however, I could just make out the gray mass of the great brute still advancing towards the hut. Then the light went altogether and I had to trust to my ears. I heard him fumbling with his trunk, apparently at the roof of the hut; next came a sound as of straw being drawn out, and then for a little while there was complete silence.
