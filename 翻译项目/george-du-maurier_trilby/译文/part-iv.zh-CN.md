===Original===

Midday had struck. The expected hamper had not turned up in the Place St. Anatole des Arts.
All Madame Vinard’s kitchen battery was in readiness; Trilby and Madame Angèle Boisse were in the studio, their sleeves turned up, and ready to begin.
At twelve the trois Angliches and the two fair *blanchisseuses* sat down to lunch in a very anxious frame of mind, and finished a pâté de foie gras and two bottles of Burgundy between them, such was their disquietude.
The guests had been invited for six o’clock.
Most elaborately they laid the cloth on the table they had borrowed from the Hôtel de Seine, and settled who was to sit next to whom, and then unsettled it, and quarrelled over it﻿—Trilby, as was her wont in such matters, assuming an authority that did not rightly belong to her, and of course getting her own way in the end.
And that, as the Laird remarked, was her confounded Trilbyness.
Two o’clock﻿—three﻿—four﻿—but no hamper! Darkness had almost set in. It was simply maddening. They knelt on the divan, with their elbows on the windowsill, and watched the street lamps popping into life along the quays﻿—and looked out through the gathering dusk for the van from the Chemin de Fer du Nord﻿—and gloomily thought of the Morgue, which they could still make out across the river.
At length the Laird and Trilby went off in a cab to the station﻿—a long drive﻿—and, lo! before they came back the long-expected hamper arrived, at six o’clock.
And with it Durien, Vincent, Antony, Lorrimer, Carnegie, Petrolicoconose, Dodor, and l’Zouzou﻿—the last two in uniform, as usual.
And suddenly the studio, which had been so silent, dark, and dull, with Taffy and Little Billee sitting hopeless and despondent round the stove, became a scene of the noisiest, busiest, and cheerfulest animation. The three big lamps were lit, and all the Chinese lanterns. The pieces of resistance and the pudding were whisked off by Trilby, Angèle, and Madame Vinard to other regions﻿—the porter’s lodge and Durien’s studio (which had been lent for the purpose); and everyone was pressed into the preparations for the banquet. There was plenty for idle hands to do. Sausages to be fried for the turkey, stuffing made, and sauces, salads mixed, and punch﻿—holly hung in festoons all round and about﻿—a thousand things.

正午钟声已经敲响。圣阿纳托尔艺术广场期盼中的大礼品柳条筐却迟迟不见踪影。

到了12点（12），三位英国佬和两位美丽的精洗女工怀着惶惶不安的心情坐下来吃午饭；出于焦虑，他们竟不知不觉吃光了一整块鹅肝酱排，喝光了2瓶勃艮第葡萄酒。
客人们受邀赴宴的时间定在傍晚6点（6）。
他们极其讲究地在从塞纳河旅馆借来的大餐桌上铺好台布，安排谁挨着谁坐，接着又推翻重来，为此争论不休——在诸如此类的事情上，特里尔比一如既往地行使着并不真正属于她的威权，当然最终也总是由着她的性子定了下来。
而正如领主所言，这正是她身上那股改不掉的特里尔比作派。
2点、3点、4点——大礼品筐依然不见影儿！夜幕几乎已经降临。这简直教人抓狂。他们双膝跪在长榻上，胳膊肘支在窗台上，眼看着码头沿岸的街灯一盏盏亮起——在渐浓的暮色中向外张望，寻找着从法国北部铁路（Chemin de Fer du Nord）开来的货运马车——并郁闷地遥望着河对岸隐约可见的停尸房。
终于，领主和特里尔比乘上一辆马车赶往火车站——好长一段路程——然而，瞧啊！还没等他们赶回来，傍晚6点（6），那只期盼已久的大礼品筐终于送到了。
随之而来的还有迪里安、文森特、安东尼、洛里默、卡耐基、彼得罗利科科诺斯、多多尔和祖祖——最后两位一如既往地身穿军装。
画室方才还一片寂静、昏暗而沉闷，塔菲和小比利曾绝望沮丧地围坐在火炉旁，此刻却刹那间化作一幕最喧闹、最繁忙、最欢腾热闹的景象。3盏大灯被点亮了，所有的中国灯笼也全都点亮了。主菜的大块肉食和李子布丁被特里尔比、昂热勒和维纳尔太太手脚麻利地转移到了别处——门房小屋和迪里安为此特意借出的雕塑画室；所有人都被动员起来投入到盛宴的筹备之中。闲着的手多得是活计可干。要为烤火鸡煎香肠，要调拌填料，调制酱汁，拌沙拉，调制潘趣热酒——四下里挂起冬青花环彩带——千头万绪的忙碌事。

===Original===

Everybody was so clever and good-humored that nobody got in anybody’s way﻿—not even Carnegie, who was in evening dress (to the Laird’s delight). So they made him do the scullion’s work﻿—cleaning, rinsing, peeling, etc.
The cooking of the dinner was almost better fun than the eating of it. And though there were so many cooks, not even the broth was spoiled (cockaleekie, from a receipt of the Laird’s).
It was ten o’clock before they sat down to that most memorable repast.
Zouzou and Dodor, who had been the most useful and energetic of all its cooks, apparently quite forgot they were due at their respective barracks at that very moment: they had only been able to obtain *la permission de dix heures*. If they remembered it, the certainty that next day Zouzou would be reduced to the ranks for the fifth time, and Dodor confined to his barracks for a month, did not trouble them in the least.
The waiting was as good as the cooking. The handsome, quick, authoritative Madame Vinard was in a dozen places at once, and openly prompted, rebuked, and ballyragged her husband into a proper smartness. The pretty little Madame Angèle moved about as deftly and as quietly as a mouse; which of course did not prevent them both from genially joining in the general conversation whenever it wandered into French.
Trilby, tall, graceful, and stately, and also swift of action, though more like Juno or Diana than Hebe, devoted herself more especially to her own particular favorites﻿—Durien, Taffy, the Laird, Little Billee﻿—and Dodor and Zouzou, whom she loved, and *tutoyé’d en bonne camarade* as she served them with all there was of the choicest.
The two little Vinards did their little best﻿—they scrupulously respected the mince-pies, and only broke two bottles of oil and one of Harvey sauce, which made their mother furious. To console them, the Laird took one of them on each knee and gave them of his share of plum-pudding and many other unaccustomed good things, so bad for their little French tumtums.
The genteel Carnegie had never been at such a queer scene in his life. It opened his mind﻿—and Dodor and Zouzou, between whom he sat (the Laird thought it would do him good to sit between a private soldier and a humble corporal), taught him more French than he had learned during the three months he had spent in Paris. It was a specialty of theirs.

每个人都手脚麻利、和气融融，谁也没有碍谁的事——甚至连身穿晚礼服的卡耐基也不例外（这让领主大为开怀）。于是大家派他去干帮厨打杂的粗活——清洗、冲刷、削皮打皮等等。
烹饪这顿大餐的乐趣，几乎比吃大餐本身还要痛快。尽管下厨的厨子有这么多人，汤却丝毫没有煮坏（那是苏格兰韭葱鸡汤[cockaleekie]，依照领主祖传的秘方烹制）。
直到夜里10点（10），大家才围坐下来享用这顿极难忘怀的盛宴。
祖祖和多多尔原本是所有帮厨中最顶用、最卖力的两位，此刻显然彻底忘掉了他们这个钟点本该准时返回各自的兵营销假：他们当初只请到了夜间10点（10）为止的准假条（*la permission de dix heures*）。即便他们想起来了，次日祖祖铁定要第5次（5th）被贬为列兵、多多尔要被关1个月（1 month）禁闭的下场，也丝毫没有扰乱他们的兴致。
端盘伺候的水平丝毫不输烹饪手艺。漂亮、麻利又威严的维纳尔太太一人兼顾十几处角落，公开指点、训斥、数落她的丈夫，逼他动作麻利机灵起来。娇小俏丽的昂热勒太太像小老鼠般轻巧无声地来回穿梭；这自然丝毫不妨碍她们两人在大家偶尔扯到法语话题时和蔼地插上几句闲聊。
特里尔比身材高挑、优美庄重，动作又极其迅捷敏捷，与其说像赫柏，倒不如说更像朱诺或狄安娜；她格外悉心照料自己最偏爱的那几位——迪里安、塔菲、领主、小比利——以及她疼爱的多多尔和祖祖，在给他们奉上最上乘的佳肴时，她像个好同伴一样亲切地直呼“你”（*tutoyé’d en bonne camarade*）。
维纳尔家的两个小家伙也尽了他们小小的微薄之力——他们规规矩矩地不敢偷碰肉馅饼，只打碎了2瓶（2）食油和1瓶（1）哈维肉汁酱，这让他们的母亲火冒三丈。为了安抚他们，领主把两个孩子一边膝盖上抱一个，把自己那份李子布丁和许多其他平日难得一尝的好东西分给他们吃，尽管这对法国小娃娃稚嫩的小肚子可没半点好处。
文质彬彬的卡耐基一生中从未置身过如此奇特的场面。这令他大开眼界——坐在多多尔和祖祖中间（领主觉得让他夹在一个列兵和一个下士之间坐坐对他大有裨益），他听到了一些关于拿破仑三世私人品行最骇人听闻的军中轶闻，以及法军在克里米亚前线种种最精彩绝伦的下流营房段子。

It was more colloquial than what is generally used in diplomatic circles, and stuck longer in the memory; but it hasn’t interfered with his preferment in the Church.
He quite unbent. He was the first to volunteer a song (without being asked) when the pipes and cigars were lit, and after the usual toasts had been drunk﻿—her Majesty’s health, Tennyson, Thackeray, and Dickens; and John Leech.
He sang, with a very cracked and rather hiccupy voice, his only song (it seems)﻿—an English one, of which the burden, he explained, was French:

Veeverler companyee!”

And Zouzou and Dodor complimented him so profusely on his French accent that he was with difficulty prevented from singing it all over again.

It was his favorite song, and one of the beautiful songs of the world, and he sang it very well﻿—and it became popular in the quartier latin ever after.

It is a quite superexcellent ditty, and haunts my memory to this day; and one felt sure that Nancy was a dear and a sweet, wherever she lived, and when. So Taffy was encored twice﻿—once for her sake, once for his own.
And finally, to the surprise of all, the bold dragoon sang (in English) “My Sister Dear,” out of *Masaniello*, with such pathos, and in a voice so sweet and high and well in tune, that his audience felt almost weepy in the midst of their jollification, and grew quite sentimental, as Englishmen abroad are apt to do when they are rather tipsy and hear pretty music, and think of their dear sisters across the sea, or their friends’ dear sisters.
Madame Vinard interrupted her Christmas dinner on the model-throne to listen, and wept and wiped her eyes quite openly, and remarked to Madame Boisse, who stood modestly close by: “*Il est gentil tout plein, ce dragon! Mon Dieu! comme il chante bien! Il est Angliche aussi, il paraît. Ils sont joliment bien élevés, tous ces Angliches﻿—tous plus gentils les uns que les autres! et quant à Monsieur Litrebili, on lui donnerait le bon Dieu sans confession!*”
And Madame Boisse agreed.
Then Svengali and Gecko came, and the table had to be laid and decorated anew, for it was suppertime.
Supper was even jollier than dinner, which had taken off the keen edge of the appetites, so that everyone talked at once﻿—the true test of a successful supper﻿—except when Antony told some of his experiences of bohemia; for instance, how, after staying at home all day for a month to avoid his creditors, he became reckless one Sunday morning, and went to the Bains Deligny, and jumped into a deep part by mistake, and was saved from a watery grave by a bold swimmer, who turned out to be his boot-maker, Satory, to whom he owed sixty francs﻿—of all his duns the one he dreaded the most﻿—and who didn’t let him go in a hurry.
Whereupon Svengali remarked that he also owed sixty francs to Satory﻿—“*Mais comme che ne me baigne chamais, che n’ai rien à craindre!*”
Whereupon there was such a laugh that Svengali felt he had scored off Antony at last and had a prettier wit. He flattered himself that he’d got the laugh of Antony *this* time.

这真是一首极其上乘的小曲，直到今天依然回响在我的记忆中；大家深信无论南希生活在何处、何时，她定然是个可爱温存的人儿。于是塔菲被喝彩再唱了两次（twice）——一次是看在南希的面子上，一次是冲着他本人。
最后，出乎所有人的意料，那位英勇的龙骑兵用英语演唱了歌剧《波尔蒂契的哑女》（*Masaniello*）中的《我亲爱的妹妹》（“My Sister Dear”），歌声如此凄美，嗓音如此甜美清亮且音调精准，以至于在狂欢的气氛中听众们几乎感动得眼眶湿润，泛起了异国游子特有的伤感柔情——正如英国人在海外略带醉意、听到优美乐音、思念起大洋彼岸亲爱的姐妹或朋友的姐妹时所惯常流露的那样。

宵夜比正餐还要欢闹，正餐已经消磨掉了大家饥肠辘辘的锐气，于是大家全都争相发言——这正是一顿宵夜成功与否的真正标志——唯有在安东尼讲述他在波希米亚的奇遇时大家才安静下来；比如，为了躲避债主，他曾整整1个月（1 month）白天大门不出二门不迈，直到某个周日早晨他索性破罐子破摔去了德里尼浴场，误跳进深水区，险些溺毙，多亏一位勇猛的游泳者将他救了上来，结果那人竟然是他的鞋匠萨托里（Satory）——在他所有债主中这是他最害怕的一个，他欠了萨托里60法郎（60 francs）——鞋匠救起他后可没轻易放他脱身。
斯文加利随即插话打趣说，他也欠了萨托里60法郎（60 francs）——“但我从不洗澡（*Mais comme che ne me baigne chamais*），所以我压根儿用不着担心（*che n’ai rien à craindre!*）！”
这番话引得全场哄堂大笑，斯文加利觉得自己总算压过了安东尼一头，幽默感更胜一筹。他自鸣得意地以为自己*这一回*让安东尼沦为了笑柄。

And after supper Svengali and Gecko made such lovely music that everybody was sobered and athirst again, and the punch-bowl, wreathed with holly and mistletoe, was placed in the middle of the table, and clean glasses set all round it.
Then Dodor and l’Zouzou stood up to dance with Trilby and Madame Angèle, and executed a series of cancan steps, which, though they were so inimitably droll that they had each and all to be encored, were such that not one of them need have brought the blush of shame to the cheek of modesty.
Then the Laird danced a sword-dance over two T squares and broke them both. And Taffy, baring his mighty arms to the admiring gaze of all, did dumbbell exercises, with Little Billee for a dumbbell, and all but dropped him into the punch-bowl; and tried to cut a pewter ladle in two with Dodor’s sabre, and sent it through the window; and this made him cross, so that he abused French sabres, and said they were made of worse pewter than even French ladles; and the Laird sententiously opined that they managed these things better in England, and winked at Little Billee.
Then they played at “cockfighting,” with their wrists tied across their shins, and a broomstick thrust in between; thus manacled, you are placed opposite your antagonist, and try to upset him with your feet, and he you. It is a very good game. The cuirassier and the Zouave playing at this got so angry, and were so irresistibly funny a sight, that the shouts of laughter could be heard on the other side of the river, so that a *sergent de ville* came in and civilly requested them not to make so much noise. They were disturbing the whole quartier, he said, and there was quite a *rassemblement* outside. So they made him tipsy, and also another policeman, who came to look after his comrade, and yet another; and these guardians of the peace of Paris were trussed and made to play at cockfighting, and were still funnier than the two soldiers, and laughed louder and made more noise than anyone else, so that Madame Vinard had to remonstrate with them; till they got too tipsy to speak, and fell fast asleep, and were laid next to each other behind the stove.

Also, his vanity about himself had become as nothing, and he missed it almost as much as his affection.
Yet he told himself over and over again that he was a great artist, and that he would spare no pains to make himself a greater. But that was no merit of his own.
2 + 2 = 4, also 2 × 2 = 4; that peculiarity was no reason why 4 should be conceited; for what was 4 but a result, either way?
Well, he was like 4﻿—just an inevitable result of circumstances over which he had no control﻿—a mere product or sum; and though he meant to make himself as big a 4 as he could (to cultivate his peculiar *fourness*), he could no longer feel the old conceit and self-complacency; and they had been a joy, and it was hard to do without them.
At the bottom of it all was a vague, disquieting unhappiness, a constant fidget.
And it seemed to him, and much to his distress, that such mild unhappiness would be the greatest he could ever feel henceforward﻿—but that, such as it was, it would never leave him, and that his moral existence would be forevermore one long, gray, gloomy blank﻿—the glimmer of twilight﻿—never glad, confident morning again!
So much for Little Billee’s convalescence.

在这一切的心底深处，潜藏着一种莫名的、令人心烦意乱的隐隐不快，一种无休止的烦躁不安。
令他深感痛苦的是，在他看来，这种淡淡的不快恐怕已是他今后所能体会到的极限情感——然而即便只是这种淡淡的阴霾，也将永远纠缠着他，他的精神世界将永远沦为一片漫长、灰暗、凄凉的虚无死寂——一抹微弱的暮光薄暮——再也不会迎来欢欣雀跃、自信满怀的破晓黎明！
小比利的康复大致如此。
