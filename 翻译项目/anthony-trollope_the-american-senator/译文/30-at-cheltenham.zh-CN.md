## XXX At Cheltenham / 三十 在切尔滕纳姆
===Original===

The month at Cheltenham was passed very quietly and would have been a very happy month with Mary Masters but that there grew upon her from day to day increasing fears of what she would have to undergo when she returned to Dillsborough. At the moment when she was hesitating with Larry Twentyman, when she begged him to wait six months and then at last promised to give him an answer at the end of two, she had worked herself up to think that it might possibly be her duty to accept her lover for the sake of her family. At any rate she had at that moment thought that the question of duty ought to be further considered, and therefore she had vacillated. When the two months’ delay was accorded to her, and within that period the privilege of a long absence from Dillsborough, she put the trouble aside for a while with the common feeling that the chapter of accidents might do something for her. Before she had reached Cheltenham the chapter of accidents had done much. When Reginald Morton told her that he could not have congratulated her on such prospects, and had explained to her why in truth he had been angry at the bridge﻿—how he had been anxious to be alone with her that he might learn whether she were really engaged to this man﻿—then she had known that her answer to Larry Twentyman at the end of the two months must be a positive refusal.
But as she became aware of this a new trouble arose and harassed her very soul. When she had asked for the six months she had not at the moment been aware, she had not then felt, that a girl who asks for time is supposed to have already surrendered. But since she had made that unhappy request the conviction had grown upon her. She had read it in every word her stepmother said to her and in her father’s manner. The very winks and hints and little jokes which fell from her younger sisters told her that it was so. She could see around her the satisfaction which had come from the settlement of that difficult question﻿—a satisfaction which was perhaps more apparent with her father than even with the others. Then she knew what she had done, and remembered to have heard that a girl who expresses a doubt is supposed to have gone beyond doubting. While she was still at Dillsborough there was a feeling that no evil would arise from this if she could at last make up her mind to be Mrs.

厄尚夫人自称这一趟做客，满意之极，又盼来年再续。
「我本想留你安家在此——我这厢还有个家可待你——只是你在这儿要比在迪尔斯伯勒埋得更深。况且那边有你的本分，或许不该掠下。可只要你父亲肯放，再来。」
归程上她实在说不上快活。离她该给拉里·特温特曼答复的日子还差三个礼拜；可答复既已备好，何苦再吊他三个礼拜？她知道，继母很快会逼她吐口；父亲也急着想知她冥想的成果。她真正的缓刑期，便是切尔滕纳姆这一趟；如今这期限到了头。每过一站，她便想起雷金纳德·莫顿对她说过的话，想起两人的交谈怎样被打断——间或，怕还被帮衬——全亏那鸟的荒唐。挨着他、听他低语的声音，曾何等甜！他同那另一位年轻人差别何其大——那一位衣着的鲜亮，如今叫她看着格外生厌！倘若命里注定要做特温特曼太太，那切尔滕纳姆原不该来。这一点她如今拿得死死的。
她独自乘「灌木」客栈的公共马车，从迪尔斯伯勒车站回来。她没指望谁接。谁又该来接她？搬运夫掠好箱子；马车把她掠在家门口。

===Original===

But she remembered well how she had gone down with Reginald Morton, and how delightful had been every little incident of the journey. Even to walk with him up and down the platform while waiting for the train had been a privilege. She thought of it as she got out of the carriage and remembered that she had felt that the train had come too soon.
At her own door her father met her and took her into the parlour where the tea-things were spread, and where her sisters were already seated. Her stepmother soon came in and kissed her kindly. She was asked how she had enjoyed herself, and no disagreeable questions were put to her that night. No questions, at least, were asked which she felt herself bound to answer. After she was in bed Kate came to her and did say a word. “Well, Mary, do tell me. I won’t tell anyone.” But Mary refused to speak a word.

===Chinese===

可她清清楚楚记得，去时是雷金纳德·莫顿同行；一路上桩桩小事，何等叫人欢喜。连候车时同他在月台上走个来回，都是一份殊荣。下车时她想着这个，记起自己当时只觉得：火车来得太早。
到家门口，父亲迎她进客厅；茶具已经摆开，妹妹们也已落座。继母随即进来，亲亲热热吻了她。人人问她过得可好；那晚没有一句叫她难堪的话。至少，没有问她一句自觉非答不可的。她上床后，凯特溜来说了一句。
「好玛丽，告诉我吧。我谁也不告诉你。」
可玛丽一个字也不肯说。
