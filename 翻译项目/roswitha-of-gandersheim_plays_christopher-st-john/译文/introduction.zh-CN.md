## Introduction / 导言

===Original===
By His Eminence Cardinal Gasquet

===Chinese===
加斯奎特枢机主教（His Eminence Cardinal Gasquet）撰

===Original===
Whatever may be thought of the precise merits of these six short dramas, now translated into English for the first time,1 it will be conceded that a collection of plays bearing the date of the 10th century, authenticated as the work of a woman, and a nun, is a remarkable phenomenon, interesting to students of monasticism and of the drama alike.

At one time, it is interesting to note, it was suggested that the author of these dramas was an Englishwoman. In fact, the English scholar, Laurence Humfrey, who first introduced them to notice in this country, endeavoured to prove that Roswitha was no other than St. Hilda of Northumbria. His theory cannot, of course, be maintained; but the very anxiety shown to identify this talented poetess and dramatist as a native of this country is evidence of the high estimation in which her compositions were held in the 16th century, the time when Laurence Humfrey, an exile from England for his religion, learnt to know them in Germany. It is now an established fact that the plays are the work of a Benedictine nun of Gandersheim, in Saxony, and their merits certainly justify her biographer’s exclamation: “Rara avis in Saxonia visa est.”

It used to be assumed that between the 6th and the 12th century all dramatic representations ceased, but each of these centuries when patiently searched has yielded some dramatic texts. The feudal period, reckoned the most barbarous, and Germania, set down then, as later in history, as the least civilized of countries, have produced the most considerable and least imperfect of these texts in the plays of Hrotsuitha, or Roswitha, a nun of the Order of St. Benedict, who spent her religious life in the Convent of Gandersheim.

===Chinese===
无论人们对这六部短剧的确切价值作何评判——它们此刻是首次被译成英文1——有一点当为世人所公认：一批署着 10 世纪年月的剧作，经确证出自一位女性、而且是一位修女之手，实乃一种非凡的现象，无论研究隐修制度的研究者还是研究戏剧的研究者，都会对之深感兴趣。

说来有趣，曾有一度，有人提出这些剧作的作者是一位英国女子。事实上，最早使它们在英国受到注意的英国学者劳伦斯·汉弗莱，就曾力图证明罗斯维塔不是别人，正是诺森布里亚的圣希尔达。他的说法当然站不住脚；然而，人们如此急切地想把这位才华横溢的女诗人、女剧作家认作本国之人，这件事本身就证明：在 16 世纪，她的作品备受推崇——正是在那个世纪，劳伦斯·汉弗莱因宗教信仰流亡异国，在德意志得识这些作品。如今已是确定无疑的事实：这些剧本出自萨克森甘德斯海姆的一位本笃会修女之手，而它们的优点也确实当得起她的传记作者那声赞叹：「Rara avis in Saxonia visa est」（萨克森大地上惊现一只珍禽）。

人们过去一向认定，从 6 世纪到 12 世纪，一切戏剧演出俱已断绝；然而只要耐心搜求，这些世纪中的每一个都交出过若干戏剧文本。封建时代被目为最野蛮的时代，日耳曼尼亚在当时——一如后世史册所载——被贬为最不开化的国度；然而恰恰是它们，产出了这批文本中最可观、最少残缺的作品，即罗斯维塔（Hrotsuitha，或作 Roswitha）的剧作——她是圣本笃会的一位修女，在甘德斯海姆修女院度过了她的修道生涯。

===Original===
There is a marked difference between her plays and such dramas as *The Mystery of the Wise and Foolish Virgins*, which is little more than an amplification of the sequence of the liturgy. We find here an author familiar not only with the Scriptures, the works of the Fathers of the Church, of the hagiographers, and of the Christian philosophers, but with Plautus, Terence, Horace, Virgil, and Ovid﻿—an author who, on her own confession, took the theatre of Terence as her model.

The Abbey of Gandersheim, where these plays were written, was founded about the year 850 by Ludolph, Duke of Saxony, at the request of his wife Oda, a Frankish princess. Although these were what men call “the dark ages,” the darkness was comparative. The Saxon court at this time was enlightened, and the Abbeys of Saxony, notably that of Corbei, were centres of learning and civilization. Gandersheim was one of the “free abbeys,” that is to say its Abbess held it direct from the King. Her rights of overlordship extended for many miles; she had her own law courts, and sent her men-at-arms into the field. In fact, she enjoyed the usual privileges and undertook the usual responsibilities of a feudal baron, and as such had the right to a seat in the Imperial Diet. Coins are extant, struck by the Abbesses of Gandersheim, whose portraits they bear.

During the 10th and 11th centuries these Abbesses were drawn chiefly from the royal house of Saxony, which had been raised to the dignity of the Imperial throne of Germania. Leuckfeld, in his voluminous history of Gandersheim, quotes a contemporary chronicler who praises the royal nuns for keeping all luxury and state out of the life of the community, and for observing the Rule of St. Benedict strictly. “They were forbidden,” says the chronicler, “to eat away from the common table at the appointed times, except in case of sickness. They slept together, and came together to celebrate the canonical hours. And they set to work together whenever work had to be done.” The Abbess who ruled the community in Roswitha’s time was Gerberg, or Gerberga, a niece of the Emperor Otho I. Gerberg was a good classical scholar, and Roswitha tells us, in one of the introductory prefaces with which, fortunately for posterity, her works are freely sprinkled, how much she owed to the tuition of this Abbess, “younger in years than I, but far older in learning.”

她的剧本与《聪明童女与愚拙童女神秘剧》（The Mystery of the Wise and Foolish Virgins）一类戏剧判然有别——后者不过是礼拜仪式序列的铺陈扩充罢了。我们在她这里看到的是这样一位作者：不仅谙熟圣经、教父著作、圣徒传记作家与基督教哲学家们的作品，而且熟稔普劳图斯、泰伦提乌斯、贺拉斯、维吉尔与奥维德——而且，照她自己的招认，她正是以泰伦提乌斯的戏剧为自己的范本。

这些剧本的诞生地甘德斯海姆修道院，约于 850 年由萨克森公爵卢道夫应其妻、法兰克公主奥达之请而创建。虽然那时正值世人所谓的「黑暗时代」，但这黑暗只是相对而言。当时的萨克森宫廷开明有识，萨克森诸修道院——尤其是科尔维修道院——皆是学问与文明的中心。甘德斯海姆是「自由修道院」之一，也就是说，其院长修女直辖于国王。她的领主权力延绵数里；她有自己的法庭，也遣自己的武装扈从上阵。事实上，她享有封建男爵惯常享有的种种特权，也承担封建男爵惯常承担的种种义务，并且凭此身份享有帝国议会的席位。甘德斯海姆历代院长修女打铸的钱币至今犹存，币上还带着她们的肖像。

在 10 与 11 世纪，这些院长修女主要出自萨克森王族——彼时这一王族已登上日耳曼尼亚帝位的尊荣。洛伊克费尔德在其卷帙浩繁的甘德斯海姆史中，引述了一位同时代编年史家的话，赞扬这些王室修女将一切奢华与排场摈于修会生活之外，并严格遵守圣本笃会规。编年史家写道：「除患病外，她们不得在规定时刻离开公共餐桌另行进食。她们同寝共息，同聚诵念时辰日课；凡有工役，便一同劳作。」罗斯维塔时代治理这座修女院的院长，是格贝格，或作格贝加（Gerberga），皇帝奥托一世之侄女。格贝格是一位造诣颇深的古典学者。罗斯维塔的作品里点缀着大量序引文字——这是后世之大幸——她在其中一篇里告诉我们，她从这位院长的教诲中受益何其之多：她「年纪比我轻，学问却远比我长」。

It is from such sentences as this that we are able to gain a little information about Roswitha’s life. Her mention of certain historical events and personages proves that she was born after the year 912 and before the year 940 (the known date of Gerberg’s birth). She seems to have entered the religious life at Gandersheim when she was about twenty-three years old. She tells us nothing about her antecedents, but as Gandersheim was an exclusive house we may assume that she was of gentle birth. What education or experience of the world she had had before she became a nun is a matter of guesswork.

Roswitha wrote in Latin, the only language used in the 10th century in the West for literary composition. Conrad Celtes, the well-known humanist, discovered the manuscript, the writing of which cannot be earlier than the 9th, or later than the 10th century, in the library of the Benedictine monastery of St. Emmeran, Ratisbon, in the last days of the 15th century. In the year 1501 it was printed. This first edition has an interesting frontispiece representing the nun poet and dramatist presenting her works to the Emperor Otho II, in the presence of her Abbess Gerberg, who wears the crown of a “Fürstabtin.” This and the other plates illustrating incidents in the plays have been attributed to both Dürer and Cranach, but they are not signed. Another edition, that of Schurzfleisch, in nearly all respects a reprint of the first, was issued in 1707, augmented with biographical and philological notes. The text given in the Latin Patrology (Migne, Tomus 137) is taken from the Schurzfleisch edition. More valuable to the student is Magnin’s edition. The French commentator collated the Celtes and Schurzfleisch texts with the original manuscript, which in 1803 had been moved from St. Emmeran to the Munich library, and found one or two readings preferable to those of Celtes. Magnin also restored some stage directions omitted by Celtes, one of which, in the eighth scene of *Callimachus*, affords, as the English translator notes, valuable evidence that the play was acted, or at least intended for representation.

The original manuscript is divided into three parts. The first contains eight poems or metrical legends of the Saints in which reliable authorities are carefully followed, much skill being shown, however, in the arrangement of the material and in the handling of the “leonine hexameter.” The second part consists of the six plays here given in English; the third, of a long unfinished poem called “Panegyric of the Othos.” Celtes changed the order, which is to be regretted, as it is obviously chronological. Roswitha’s preface to Part III shows more confidence than the preface to the plays, and very much more than the diffident preface to the poems. One of these poems, “Passio Sancti Pelagii,” once enjoyed a very high reputation, and is often quoted by Spanish and Portuguese hagiographers. The Bollandists print it entire in the *Acta Sanctorum*. It has another interest in that Roswitha tells us that she obtained her facts from a witness of the saint’s martyrdom.

正是从这样的语句里，我们才得以获知一点关于罗斯维塔生平的讯息。她提及的某些史事与人物证明，她生于 912 年之后、940 年（格贝格已知的生年）之前。她似乎是在二十三岁上下进入甘德斯海姆开始修道生活的。对于自己的家世，她只字未提；不过，既然甘德斯海姆是一所门第森严的修女院，我们不妨推断她出身于高贵门第。至于她在成为修女之前受过什么教育、有过怎样的阅历，那就只能凭人猜度了。

罗斯维塔以拉丁文写作——在 10 世纪的西方，这是文学创作所用的唯一语言。15 世纪末，知名人文主义者康拉德·策尔蒂斯在雷根斯堡圣埃默兰本笃会修道院的藏书室里发现了这部手稿；其抄写年代不会早于 9 世纪，也不会晚于 10 世纪。1501 年，它付梓刊行。这第一版有一帧饶有趣味的卷首插画，画中这位修女诗人兼剧作家正将作品呈献给皇帝奥托二世，她的院长格贝格侍立一旁，头戴「帝国女院长」（Fürstabtin）之冠。这一幅以及描绘剧中情事的其余诸图，曾被归于丢勒与克拉纳赫两人名下，但均无签名。另一种版本，即舒尔茨弗莱施版，于 1707 年问世，几乎在所有方面都是第一版的重印，只是增补了传记与语文学的注释。《拉丁教父文集》（米涅，第 137 卷）所载文本即取自舒尔茨弗莱施版。对研究者更有价值的是马尼南的版本。这位法国评注家将策尔蒂斯本与舒尔茨弗莱施本同原始手稿进行了校勘——那部手稿已于 1803 年从圣埃默兰移藏慕尼黑图书馆——并发现一两处读法优于策尔蒂斯本。马尼南还恢复了若干被策尔蒂斯略去的舞台提示，其中《卡利马科斯》第八场的一条，恰如英译者所指出的，提供了宝贵的证据，证明该剧曾上演过，或至少是打算演出的。

原始手稿分为三个部分。第一部分收诗八首，即圣徒的韵体传奇，其中小心翼翼地依循可靠的权威史料，但在材料的布局与「列奥尼体六音步」（leonine hexameter）的驾驭上，颇见功力。第二部分即此处译为英文的六部剧作；第三部分是一首未完成的长诗，题为《奥托诸帝颂》（Panegyric of the Othos）。策尔蒂斯变更了原有的顺序，这是很可惋惜的，因为那顺序显然是按年代先后编排的。罗斯维塔为第三部分所写的序言，比剧作的序言显出更多的自信，更远远胜过那篇怯生生的小诗序言。这些诗中有一首《圣佩拉吉乌斯受难记》（Passio Sancti Pelagii），一度享有极高的声誉，西班牙与葡萄牙的圣徒传记作家常加征引。博兰德学会将全文收入《圣徒行传》（Acta Sanctorum）刊行。它还有另一重意味：罗斯维塔告诉我们，她的事实材料得自这位圣徒殉道的目击者。

Although Roswitha claims Terence as her master in the art of playwriting, it cannot be said that she imitates him closely. When *Paphnutius* was acted in London in 1914 the dramatic critic of *The Times* was justified from one point of view in asserting that Roswitha’s style is “not in the least Terentian.” For one thing she is quite indifferent to the “unities,” and transports us from place to place with bewildering abruptness. Her relation to Terence, as she herself insists, is one of moral contrasts rather than of literary parallels. The “situation” in Terence’s comedies almost invariably turns on the frailty of women; in Roswitha’s plays as invariably on their heroic adherence to chastity. Although considerable variety is shown in the treatment of each story, the motive is always the same﻿—to glorify uncompromising fidelity to the vow of virginity. This nun dramatist deals courageously, but, it must be added, delicately, when it is remembered that she lived in an age when even the best educated were neither fastidious nor restrained in manners or conversation, with the temptations which her characters overcome. The preface to her plays shows that it was not without some qualms of conscience that she wrote of things “which should not even be named among us.” But the purity of her intentions, which was obviously recognized by her religious superiors, should induce the most prudish reader to refrain from charges of impropriety. With all their shortcomings, Roswitha’s works have a claim to an eminent place in medieval literature, and do honour to her sex, to the age in which she lived, and to the vocation which she followed.

罗斯维塔虽奉泰伦提乌斯为编剧艺术之师，却不能说她亦步亦趋地模仿他。1914 年《帕夫努提乌斯》在伦敦上演时，《泰晤士报》的戏剧评论家断言罗斯维塔的风格「丝毫没有泰伦提乌斯的气息」，从一个角度看，这话并非无据。其一，她对「三一律」全然无所顾惜，带我们辗转于一个个地点之间，其仓促突兀令人目眩。她与泰伦提乌斯的关系，正如她自己坚称的，是道德上的对照，而非文学上的因袭。泰伦提乌斯喜剧中的「关目」几乎无一不以女子的脆弱为枢轴；罗斯维塔剧中的关目则无一不以女子对贞洁的英勇坚守为枢轴。各剧的情节处理虽变化多端，动机却始终如一——颂扬对守贞誓言毫不妥协的忠诚。这位修女剧作家勇敢地处理她的角色所战胜的种种诱惑，但必须补充一句：她处理得极有分寸——要记得，她生活的那个时代，连教养最好的人士在举止谈吐上也谈不上讲究与克制。她的剧作序言表明，她提笔写那些「在我们中间连提都不该提」的事情时，并非全无良知的忐忑。然而她动机的纯洁——这一点显然得到了她修道长上的认可——当能令最为拘谨的读者也不再指其失当。纵有种种缺憾，罗斯维塔的作品在中世纪文学中理应占有一个显赫的位置，并为她的性别、为她所处的时代、为她所奉的圣召增光。
