## A Reminiscence of Dr. Samuel Johnson / 塞缪尔·约翰逊博士的回忆
===Original===

Humphry Littlewit, Esq.

The Privilege of Reminiscence, however rambling or tiresome, is one generally allow’d to the very aged; indeed, ’tis frequently by means of such Recollections that the obscure occurrences of History, and the lesser Anecdotes of the Great, are transmitted to Posterity.

Though many of my readers have at times observ’d and remark’d a Sort of antique Flow in my Stile of Writing, it hath pleased me to pass amongst the Members of this Generation as a young Man, giving out the Fiction that I was born in 1890, in **America**. I am now, however, resolv’d to unburden myself of a secret which I have hitherto kept thro’ Dread of Incredulity; and to impart to the Publick a true knowledge of my long years, in order to gratifie their taste for authentick Information of an Age with whose famous Personages I was on familiar Terms. Be it then known that I was born on the family Estate in **Devonshire**, of the 10th day of August, 1690, (or in the new **Gregorian** Stile of Reckoning, the 20th of August) being therefore now in my 228th year. Coming early to **London**, I saw as a Child many of the celebrated Men of King **William’s** Reign, including the lamented Mr. **Dryden**, who sat much at the Tables of **Will’s** Coffee-House. With Mr. **Addison** and Dr. **Swift** I later became very well acquainted, and was an even more familiar Friend to Mr. **Pope**, whom I knew and respected till the Day of his Death. But since it is of my more recent Associate, the late Dr. **Johnson**, that I am at this time desir’d to write; I will pass over my Youth for the present.

I had first Knowledge of the Doctor in May of the year 1738, though I did not at that Time meet him. Mr. **Pope** had just compleated his Epilogue to his Satires, (the Piece beginning: “Not twice a Twelvemonth you appear in Print.”) and had arrang’d for its Publication. On the very Day it appear’d, there was also publish’d a Satire in Imitation of **Juvenal**, entituled “**London**,” by the then unknown **Johnson**; and this so struck the Town, that many Gentlemen of Taste declared, it was the Work of a greater Poet than Mr. **Pope**. Notwithstanding what some Detractors have said of Mr. **Pope’s** petty Jealousy, he gave the Verses of his new Rival no small Praise; and having learnt thro’ Mr. **Richardson** who the Poet was, told me, “that Mr. **Johnson** wou’d soon be **deterre**.”

回忆之特权，无论怎样散漫、怎样惹人厌烦，世人通常总肯赐予高龄之人；确实，历史上那些晦暗的事迹、伟人们的细枝逸闻，往往正是靠这类追忆才得以传于后世。

尽管我的读者中不少人在我的文字风格里时而察见并论及一种古旧的流风，我却乐于以一个年轻人的身份行走于这一代人中间，散布虚构，说自己生于一八九〇年的**美洲**。然而我如今决意卸下一桩秘密——我至今讳它，只因怕人不信——并向公众透露我漫长岁月的真数，好满足他们对一个时代的可靠见闻的胃口，那个时代的名人，我都过从甚密。那么请知晓：我生于**德文郡**的祖传田宅，时在一六九〇年八月十日（依**格列高利**新历，则为八月二十日），如此算来，今年已是我的第二百二十八岁。我早年到了**伦敦**，孩提时便见过**威廉**王朝许多名人，包括那位令人悼念的**德莱顿**先生，他常坐在**威尔**咖啡馆的桌旁。后来我同**艾迪生**先生与**斯威夫特**博士十分相熟，同**蒲柏**先生更是莫逆，直到他去世那日，我始终敬重他。只是我此刻想写的，是我较晚的伙伴、已故的**约翰逊**博士，故少年时事，姑且从略。

我初知博士，是在一七三八年五月，虽则当时并未谋面。**蒲柏**先生刚写完他的《讽刺诗集》跋（即以“你一年上不了两回印刷所”起句的那篇），并已安排刊行。刊出的当天，又有一篇仿**尤文纳尔**的讽刺诗问世，题作《**伦敦**》，作者便是当时尚无声名的**约翰逊**；此诗轰动京城，许多有鉴赏力的绅士宣称，其作者比**蒲柏**先生更伟大。尽管有些诋毁者说**蒲柏**先生如何小气嫉妒，他对这位新对手的诗句却嘉许不少；又经**理查森**先生打听到诗人是谁之后，对我说：“**约翰逊**先生不久便会**名世**。”

I had no personal Acquaintances with the Doctor till 1763, when I was presented to him at the **Mitre** Tavern by Mr. **James Boswell**, a young **Scotchman** of excellent Family and great Learning, but small Wit, whose metrical Effusions I had sometimes revis’d.

Dr. **Johnson**, as I beheld him, was a full, pursy Man, very ill drest, and of slovenly Aspect. I recall him to have worn a bushy Bob-Wig, untyed and without Powder, and much too small for his Head. His Cloaths were of rusty brown, much wrinkled, and with more than one Button missing. His Face, too full to be handsom, was likewise marred by the Effects of some scrofulous Disorder; and his Head was continually rolling about in a sort of convulsive way. Of this Infirmity, indeed, I had known before; having heard of it from Mr. **Pope**, who took the Trouble to make particular Inquiries.

Being nearly seventy-three, full nineteen Years older than Dr. **Johnson**, (I say Doctor, though his Degree came not till two Years afterward) I naturally expected him to have some Regard for my Age; and was therefore not in that Fear of him, which others confess’d. On my asking him what he thought of my favourable Notice of his Dictionary in **The Londoner**, my periodical Paper, he said: “Sir, I possess no Recollection of having perus’d your Paper, and have not a great Interest in the Opinions of the less thoughtful Part of Mankind.” Being more than a little piqued at the Incivility of one whose Celebrity made me solicitous of his Approbation, I ventur’d to retaliate in kind, and told him, I was surpris’d that a Man of Sense shou’d judge the Thoughtfulness of one whose Productions he admitted never having read. “Why, Sir,” reply’d **Johnson**, “I do not require to become familiar with a Man’s Writings in order to estimate the Superficiality of his Attainments, when he plainly shows it by his Eagerness to mention his own Productions in the first Question he puts to me.” Having thus become Friends, we convers’d on many Matters. When, to agree with him, I said I was distrustful of the Authenticity of **Ossian’s** Poems, Mr. **Johnson** said: “That, Sir, does not do your Understanding particular Credit; for what all the Town is sensible of, is no great Discovery for a **Grub-Street** Critick to make. You might as well say, you have a strong Suspicion that **Milton** wrote *Paradise Lost*!”

直到一七六三年，我才与博士本人相识，是**詹姆斯·鲍斯韦尔**先生在**米特**酒馆引见的。鲍斯韦尔是个出身上等门第、学问渊博的**苏格兰**青年，只是机锋不足，他的诗稿我时有代为斟酌。

**约翰逊**博士，在我眼中是个肥硕臃肿的人，衣冠不整，仪容邋遢。我记得他戴一顶浓密的短假发，不系带，不敷粉，而且比他的头小了许多。他的衣裳是锈棕色，皱皱巴巴，不止一粒纽扣失落。他的脸因过于肥满而谈不上俊雅，又因瘰疬之症的后遗症而更受损；他的头还不住地以一种抽搐的样子左右滚动。这毛病我原有所闻，是**蒲柏**先生费心特意打听过告诉我的。

我时年将近七十三，比**约翰逊**博士足足年长十九岁（我称他博士，虽说他的学位还是两年之后的事），自然指望他对我这年纪存几分敬意，因此也不像旁人自认的那样怕他。我问他，对我在自己的期刊《伦敦人》上对他的字典的嘉评有何高见，他说：“先生，我不记得读过您的刊物，对人类中较少深思的那部分人的意见，也不大有兴味。”此人名望正盛，他的赞许为我所渴仰，这般无礼不免叫我着恼，我便斗气以其人之道还治，对他说，一个明白人，既承认从未读过人家的文章，却去断断人家的深浅，实在出我意外。“哎呀，先生，”**约翰逊**答道，“用不着熟悉一个人的著作，也能估量他学问的浅薄——他见我第一问，便急着自陈其大作，这浅薄已自招供了。”我们就这样成了朋友，纵谈了许多事。有一回为附和他，我说我不信**莪相**那些诗是真品，**约翰逊**博士说：“先生，这话并不为您的见识添什么光彩；全城人都明白的事，**格拉布街**（Grub-Street）的批评家再发现一回，算不得高明。您何不说，您强烈疑心**弥尔顿**的《失乐园》是**弥尔顿**写的！”

I thereafter saw **Johnson** very frequently, most often at Meetings of **The Literary Club**, which was founded the next Year by the Doctor, together with Mr. **Burke**, the parliamentary Orator, Mr. **Beauclerk**, a Gentleman of Fashion, Mr. **Langton**, a pious Man and Captain of Militia, Sir **J. Reynolds**, the widely known Painter, Dr. **Goldsmith**, the Prose and poetick Writer, Dr. **Nugent**, father-in-law to Mr. **Burke**, Sir **John Hawkins**, Mr. **Anthony Chamier**, and my self. We assembled generally at seven o’clock of an Evening, once a Week, at the **Turk’s-Head**, in **Gerrard-Street**, **Soho**, till that Tavern was sold and made into a private Dwelling; after which Event we mov’d our Gatherings successively to **Prince’s** in **Sackville-Street**, **Le Tellier’s** in **Dover-Street**, and **Parsloe’s** and the **Thatched House** in **St. James’s-Street**. In these Meetings we preserv’d a remarkable Degree of Amity and Tranquillity, which contrasts very favourably with some of the Dissensions and Disruptions I observe in the literary and amateur Press Associations of today. This Tranquillity was the more remarkable, because we had amongst us Gentlemen of very opposed Opinions. Dr. **Johnson** and I, as well as many others, were high Tories; whilst Mr. **Burke** was a Whig, and against the **American** War, many of his Speeches on that Subject having been widely publish’d. The least congenial Member was one of the Founders, Sir **John Hawkins**, who hath since written many misrepresentations of our Society. Sir **John**, an eccentrick Fellow, once declin’d to pay his part of the Reckoning for Supper, because ’twas his Custom at Home to eat no Supper. Later he insulted Mr. **Burke** in so intolerable a Manner, that we all took Pains to show our Disapproval; after which Incident he came no more to our Meetings. However, he never openly fell out with the Doctor, and was the Executor of his Will; though Mr. **Boswell** and others have Reason to question the genuineness of his Attachment. 此后我常常见到**约翰逊**，多半是在**文学俱乐部**的聚会上。那是次年博士一手创立的，同创者有议会雄辩家**伯克**先生、时髦绅士**博克莱尔**先生、虔信之人兼民团上尉**兰顿**先生、声名远播的画家**雷诺兹**爵士、散文与韵文兼工的**哥尔德斯密斯**博士、**伯克**先生的岳丈**纽金特**博士、**约翰·霍金斯**爵士、**安东尼·尚米耶**先生，以及在下。我们通常每星期一晚，晚七点，在**索霍**区**杰拉德街**的**土耳其人头**酒馆聚会，直到那酒馆易主改作私宅；此后我们的聚会相继移到**萨克维尔街**的**普林斯**、**多佛街**的**勒泰利耶**，以及**圣詹姆斯街**的**帕斯洛**和**茅屋**。在那些聚会上，我们保持着一种可观的和睦与安谧，比起今日文学界和业余报界那些内讧与破裂，实在体面得多。这安谧尤为难得，因为我们中间有意见极相左的君子。**约翰逊**博士和我，以及许多同人，是坚决的托利党；**伯克**先生却是辉格党，又反对**美洲**战争，他在那个题目上的许多演说流传甚广。最不投机的一位会员是创会人之一的**约翰·霍金斯**爵士，他后来写了许多歪曲本会的话。**霍金斯**爵士是个乖僻的人，有一回不肯出他那份晚饭的账，理由是在他自己家里向来不进晚饭。后来他又以不可容忍的方式侮辱**伯克**先生，我们全体特意表示了反感；此事之后他再不来赴会了。然而他同博士从未公开闹翻，还是博士遗嘱的执行人；虽说**鲍斯韦尔**先生等人有理由怀疑这份情谊的真伪。俱乐部较晚的会员有演员、**约翰逊**博士的早年挚友**大卫·加里克**先生，**托·**与**约·沃顿**二位先生，**亚当·斯密**博士，《英诗拾遗》的作者**珀西**博士，史家**爱德华·吉本**先生，音乐家**伯尼**博士，批评家**马隆**先生，还有**鲍斯韦尔**先生。至於

**Garrick** obtain’d Admittance only with Difficulty; for the Doctor, notwithstanding his great Friendship, was for ever affecting to decry the Stage and all Things connected with it. **Johnson**, indeed, had a most singular Habit of speaking for **Davy** when others were against him, and of arguing against him, when others were for him. I have no Doubt but that he sincerely lov’d Mr. **Garrick**, for he never alluded to him as he did to **Foote**, who was a very coarse Fellow despite his comick Genius. Mr. **Gibbon** was none too well lik’d, for he had an odious sneering Way which offended even those of us who most admir’d his historical Productions. Mr. **Goldsmith**, a little Man very vain of his Dress and very deficient in Brilliancy of Conversation, was my particular Favourite; since I was equally unable to shine in the Discourse. He was vastly jealous of Dr. **Johnson**, though none the less liking and respecting him. I remember that once a Foreigner, a **German**, I think, was in our Company; and that whilst **Goldsmith** was speaking, he observ’d the Doctor preparing to utter something. Unconsciously looking upon **Goldsmith** as a meer Encumbrance when compar’d to the greater Man, the Foreigner bluntly interrupted him and incurr’d his lasting Hostility by crying, “Hush, Toctor **Shonson** iss going to speak!”

In this luminous Company I was tolerated more because of my Years than for my Wit or Learning; being no Match at all for the rest. My Friendship for the celebrated Monsieur **Voltaire** was ever a Cause of Annoyance to the Doctor; who was deeply orthodox, and who us’d to say of the **French** Philosopher: “*Vir est acerrimi Ingenii et paucarum Literarum.*”

**加里克**先生获准入会，颇费了一番周折；因为博士虽与他交谊极笃，却总爱作践戏台和同它沾边的一切。**约翰逊**确实有个极古怪的习惯：别人贬**戴维**时他偏替他说话，别人捧**戴维**时他偏同他抬杠。我毫不怀疑他真心爱**加里克**先生，因为他提到他从不像提到**富特**那样——富特虽有滑稽天才，为人却极粗俗。**吉本**先生不怎么讨人喜欢，他那种可憎的讥诮腔，连我们中间最佩服他史学的人也要被冒犯。**哥尔德斯密斯**先生，一个小个子，极自负于衣著，谈吐又殊欠光彩，却是我的心头至好；因为谈锋之上我也一样发不出亮。他对**约翰逊**博士怀有强烈的妒意，却仍旧喜欢他、敬重他。我记得有一回，一位外国人——我想是个**德国**人——在我们中间；**哥尔德斯密斯**正说话时，他瞧见博士要开言了。那外国人下意识把**哥尔德斯密斯**同那位大人物一比，只当他是个赘物，便鲁莽地打断了他，喊道：“嘘，**琼森**博士要说话了！”从此结下了他永久的仇恨。

在这群煌煌的人物中间，我所以被容忍，多半是为着我的年纪，而非我的机锋或学问；同其余诸公相比，我全然不济。我对著名的**伏尔泰**先生的友谊，历来是博士的一桩烦恼；他信教极诚，常这样论那位**法兰西**哲学家：“*Vir est acerrimi Ingenii et paucarum Literarum*。”（其人锐敏绝伦，而学殖甚浅。）

Mr. **Boswell**, a little teazing Fellow whom I had known for some Time previously, us’d to make Sport of my aukward Manners and old-fashion’d Wig and Cloaths. Once coming in a little the worse for Wine (to which he was addicted) he endeavour’d to lampoon me by means of an Impromptu in verse, writ on the Surface of the Table; but lacking the Aid he usually had in his Composition, he made a bad grammatical Blunder. I told him, he shou’d not try to pasquinade the Source of his Poesy. At another Time **Bozzy** (as we us’d to call him) complain’d of my Harshness toward new Writers in the Articles I prepar’d for *The Monthly Review*. He said, I push’d every Aspirant off the Slopes of Parnassus. “Sir,” I reply’d, “you are mistaken. They who lose their Hold do so from their own Want of Strength; but desiring to conceal their Weakness, they attribute the absence of Success to the first Critick that mentions them.” I am glad to recall that Dr. **Johnson** upheld me in this Matter.

Dr. **Johnson** was second to no Man in the Pains he took to revise the bad Verses of others; indeed, ’tis said that in the book of poor blind old Mrs. **Williams**, there are scarce two lines which are not the Doctor’s. At one Time **Johnson** recited to me some lines by a Servant to the Duke of **Leeds**, which had so amus’d him, that he had got them by Heart. They are on the Duke’s Wedding, and so much resemble in Quality the Work of other and more recent poetick Dunces, that I cannot forbear copying them:

“When the Duke of **Leeds** shall marry’d be

To a fine young Lady of high Quality

How happy will that Gentlewoman be

In his Grace of **Leeds’** good Company.”

I ask’d the Doctor, if he had ever try’d making Sense of this Piece; and upon his saying he had not, I amus’d myself with the following Amendment of it:

When Gallant **Leeds** auspiciously shall wed

The virtuous Fair, of antient Lineage bred,

How must the Maid rejoice with conscious Pride

To win so great an Husband to her Side!

On showing this to Dr. **Johnson**, he said, “Sir, you have straightened out the Feet, but you have put neither Wit nor Poetry into the Lines.”

**鲍斯韦尔**先生，一个小小的促狭鬼，我认识他有些时日了，惯爱拿我笨拙的举止和过时的假发衣著取笑。有一回他带着几分酒意（他嗜酒）进来，想在桌面上一首即兴打油诗里挖苦我；但少了平日起草捉刀的帮手，他犯了个糟糕的语法错。我对他说，他不该去讥诮他那些诗的源头。又有一回，**博齐**（我们常这样叫他）抱怨我在《每月评论》上写的文章里对新起作家太苛刻，说我把每一个攀爬者都从帕纳索斯山坡上推了下去。“先生，”我答道，“您错了。抓不住的人，是自家力气不济；他们想遮掩这软弱，便把不成名归咎于第一个提到他们的批评家。”我很欣慰地记得，在这件事上**约翰逊**博士是站在我这一边的。

**约翰逊**博士肯费心替人改劣诗，天下无人能出其右；据说可怜的瞎眼**威廉斯**老太太那本诗集里，几乎没有两行不是博士的手笔。有一回**约翰逊**背给我听**利兹**公爵一个仆人的几句诗，那诗叫他乐不可支，都记熟了。诗咏公爵的婚事，其成色同近来别的一些诗中蠢才的作品如此相像，我忍不住抄在这里：

“当**利兹**公爵娶亲成家

娶一位高贵门第的娇娃

那位淑女该多么快活

有**利兹**大人陪她过活。”

我问博士可曾设法把这首诗读通；他说不曾，我便自娱，替它改成了下面这样：

当豪侠的**利兹**喜结良缘，

娶淑女出自名门世系，

那闺秀该怎样自豪欣然，

赢得如此夫婿相守不离！

拿给**约翰逊**博士看，他说：“先生，您把音步理顺了，可诗行里仍旧没有机智，也没有诗。”

It wou’d afford me Gratification to tell more of my Experiences with Dr. **Johnson** and his circle of Wits; but I am an old Man, and easily fatigued. I seem to ramble along without much Logick or Continuity when I endeavour to recall the Past; and fear I light upon but few Incidents which others have not before discuss’d. Shou’d my present Recollections meet with Favour, I might later set down some further Anecdotes of old Times of which I am the only Survivor. I recall many Things of **Sam Johnson** and his Club, having kept up my Membership in the Latter long after the Doctor’s Death, at which I sincerely mourn’d. I remember how **John Burgoyne**, Esq., the General, whose Dramatick and Poetical Works were printed after his Death, was blackballed by three Votes; probably because of his unfortunate Defeat in the **American** War, at **Saratoga**. Poor **John**! His Son fared better, I think, and was made a Baronet. But I am very tired. I am old, very old; it is Time for my Afternoon Nap.

我原愿再讲讲我同**约翰逊**博士及其智士圈子交往的经历；无奈我已老朽，极易困倦。我一回忆往昔，便像信步乱走，谈不上多少条理和连贯；而且恐怕撞见的逸事，多是别人早已谈过的。倘若这一番回忆竟蒙青睐，我日后或可再写下几则旧日掌故——那些事的亲历者，如今只剩我一人了。我记得**塞姆·约翰逊**和他的俱乐部的许多事，博士身后，我在后者的会籍还维持了许多年，他的死，我是真心哀恸过的。我记得**约翰·伯戈因**将军——他的戏剧和诗作都在身后印行——以三票之差被黑球拒于门外；多半是因为他在**美洲**战争的**萨拉托加**不幸败绩。可怜的**约翰**！他的儿子倒走运些，我记得，得了从男爵封号。可是我乏了。我老了，老得很；该睡午觉了。
