## XXIV A Shelf of Books / 二十四 一书架的书

===Original===
That spring, shortly after Sandy went to stay with Tempy, there was an epidemic of mumps among the schoolchildren in Stanton, and, old as he was, he was among its early victims. With jaws swollen to twice their normal size and a red sign, **mumps**, on the house, he was forced to remain at home for three weeks. It was then that the boy began to read books other than the ones he had had to study for his lessons. At Aunt Hager’s house there had been no books, anyway, except the Bible and the few fairy tales that he had been given at Christmas; but Tempy had a case full of dusty volumes that were used to give dignity to her sitting-room: a row of English classics bound in red, an *Encyclopedia of World Knowledge* in twelve volumes, a book on household medicine full of queer drawings, and some modern novels﻿—*The Rosary*, *The Little Shepherd of Kingdom Come*, the newest Harold Bell Wright, and all that had ever been written by Gene Stratton Porter, Tempy’s favorite author. The Negro was represented by Chesnutt’s *House Behind the Cedars*, and the *Complete Poems* of Paul Lawrence Dunbar, whom Tempy tolerated on account of his fame, but condemned because he had written so much in dialect and so often of the lower classes of colored people. Tempy subscribed to *Harper’s Magazine*, too, because Mrs. Barr-Grant had taken it. And in her sewing-room closet there was also a pile of *The Crisis*, the thin Negro monthly that she had been taking from the beginning of its publication.

Sandy had heard of that magazine, but he had never seen a copy; so he went through them all, looking at the pictures of prominent Negroes and reading about racial activities all over the country, and about racial wrongs in the South. In every issue he found, too, stirring and beautifully written editorials about the frustrated longings of the black race, and the hidden beauties in the Negro soul. A man named Du Bois wrote them.

“Dr. William Edward Burghardt Du Bois,” said Tempy, “and he is a great man.”

那年春天，桑迪住到坦皮家不久，斯坦顿的学童中间流行起腮腺炎（mumps），他虽然年纪已经不小，却还是最早染上的一批。两腮肿得比平常大了一倍，家门上挂着块写着**腮腺炎**的红牌子，他只好在家困了三个星期。就是打那三个星期起，这孩子开始读起功课以外的书来。黑格外婆家里本来就没什么书，除了圣经，就是他圣诞节得的那几本童话；坦皮倒有一柜子落满灰尘的书，摆在那儿专给客厅添体面：一排红皮精装的英国古典名著，一部十二卷的《世界知识百科全书》（Encyclopedia of World Knowledge），一本满是稀奇古怪插图的家庭医药书，还有几本现代小说——《玫瑰经》（The Rosary）、《天国降临的小牧人》（The Little Shepherd of Kingdom Come）、哈罗德·贝尔·赖特（Harold Bell Wright）新出的一本，外加坦皮最心爱的作家吉恩·斯特拉顿·波特（Gene Stratton Porter）写过的全部作品。替黑人撑场面的，只有切斯纳特（Chesnutt）的《雪松后面的房子》（House Behind the Cedars），和保罗·劳伦斯·邓巴的《诗歌全集》——邓巴这位，坦皮看在他名气大的份上勉强容他，心里却又贬他，嫌他方言写得太多，又尽写有色人种里的下层。坦皮还订了《哈泼斯杂志》（Harper's Magazine），因为巴尔-格兰特太太订过这份刊物。她针线间的壁橱里，还堆着一摞《危机》（The Crisis）——那份薄薄的黑人月刊，她打创刊号起就一直订着。

这份杂志桑迪听说过，可从来没见过一本，于是他把那一摞全翻了个遍，看那些知名黑人的照片，读全国各地黑人活动的消息，读南方种族压迫下的桩桩冤屈。每一期里，他还找得到几篇激动人心、文笔漂亮的社论，写黑人种族被压抑的渴望，写黑人灵魂深处藏着的美。写这些社论的，是一个名叫杜波依斯（Du Bois）的人。

「威廉·爱德华·伯格哈特·杜波依斯博士（Dr. William Edward Burghardt Du Bois），」坦皮说，「他可是个伟人。」

===Original===
“Great like Booker T. Washington?” asked Sandy.

“Teaching Negroes to be servants, that’s all Washington did!” Tempy snorted in so acid a tone that Sandy was silent. “Du Bois wants our rights. He wants us to be real men and women. He believes in social equality. But Washington﻿—huh!” The fact that he had established an industrial school damned Washington in Tempy’s eyes, for there were enough colored workers already. But Du Bois was a doctor of philosophy and had studied in Europe!﻿ ﻿… That’s what Negroes needed to do, get smart, study books, go to Europe! “Don’t talk to me about Washington,” Tempy fumed. “Take Du Bois for your model, not some white folks’ nigger.”

“Well, Aunt Hager said﻿—” then Sandy stopped. His grandmother had thought that Booker T. was the greatest of men, but maybe she had been wrong. Anyway, this Du Bois could write! Gee, it made you burn all over to read what he said about a lynching. But Sandy did not mention Booker Washington again to Tempy, although, months later, at the library he read his book called *Up from Slavery*, and he was sure that Aunt Hager hadn’t been wrong. “I guess they are both great men,” he thought.

「跟布克·T·华盛顿一样伟大吗？」桑迪问。

「教黑人当仆役，华盛顿干的就这点事儿！」坦皮嗤了一声，那口气酸得桑迪当下没了言语。「杜波依斯要的是咱们的权利。他要咱们做堂堂正正的男人和女人。他信奉社会平等。可华盛顿——哼！」华盛顿办过一所工业学校，光凭这一条，他在坦皮眼里就算毁了，因为有色的干活人手已经够多了。可杜波依斯是位哲学博士，还在欧洲留过学！……黑人要办的正是这个：学精明，念书，上欧洲去！「别跟我提华盛顿，」坦皮火气直冒。「要学就学杜波依斯，别学那种白人跟前的老黑。」

「可是，黑格外婆说过——」桑迪话到嘴边又咽了回去。外婆生前认为布克·T是天底下顶了不起的人物，可兴许她错了呢。再说了，这位杜波依斯文章写得真好！嚯，读他写私刑的那几句话，能叫你浑身上下烧起来。不过桑迪再没在坦皮跟前提起布克·华盛顿，虽然几个月之后，他在图书馆里读了华盛顿写的那本《超越奴役》（Up from Slavery），而且他确信外婆并没有错。「我琢磨，他们俩都是伟人。」他想。

Sandy’s range of reading increased, too, when his aunt found a job for him that winter in Mr. Prentiss’s gift-card and printing-shop, where he kept the place clean and acted as delivery boy. This shop kept a shelf of current novels and some volumes of the new poetry﻿—Sandburg, Lindsay, Masters﻿—which the Young Women’s Club of Stanton was then studying, to the shocked horror of the older white ladies of the town. Sandy knew of this because Mr. Prentiss’s daughter, a student at Goucher College, used to keep shop and she pointed out volumes for the boy to read and told him who their authors were and what the books meant. She said that none of the colored boys they had employed before had ever been interested in reading; so she often lent him, by way of encouragement, shopworn copies to be taken home at night and returned the next day. Thus Sandy spent much of his first year with Tempy deep in novels too mature for a fourteen-year-old boy. But Tempy was very proud of her studious young nephew. She began to decide that she had made no mistake in keeping him with her, and when he entered the high school, she bought him his first long-trouser suit as a spur towards further application.

Sandy became taller week by week, and it seemed to Tempy as if his shirtsleeves became too short for him overnight. His voice was changing, too, and he had acquired a liking for football, but his after-school job at Prentiss’s kept him from playing much. At night he read, or sometimes went to the movies with Buster﻿—but Tempy kept him home as much as she could. Occasionally he saw Willie-Mae, who was keeping company with the second cook at Wright’s Hotel. And sometimes he saw Jimmy Lane, who was a bellhop now and hung out with a sporty crowd in the rear room of Cudge Windsor’s pool hall. But whenever Sandy went into his old neighborhood, he felt sad, remembering Aunt Hager and his mother, and Jimboy, and Harriett﻿—for his young aunt had gone away from Stanton, too, and the last he heard about her rumored that she was on the stage in Kansas City. Now the little house where Sandy had lived with his grandmother belonged to Tempy, who kept it rented to a family of strangers.

In high school Sandy was taking, at his aunt’s request, the classical course, which included Latin, ancient history, and English, and which required a great deal of reading. His teacher of English was a large, masculine woman named Martha Fry, who had once been to Europe and who loved to talk about the splendors of old England and to read aloud in a deep, mannish kind of voice, dramatizing the printed words. It was from her that Sandy received an introduction to Shakespeare, for in the spring term they studied *The Merchant of Venice*. In the spring also, under Miss Fry’s direction, the first-year students were required to write an essay for the freshman essay prizes of both money and medals. And in this contest Sandy won the second prize. It was the first time in the history of the school that a colored pupil had ever done anything of the sort, and Tempy was greatly elated. There was a note in the papers about it, and Sandy brought his five dollars home for his aunt to put away. But he gave his bronze medal to a girl named Pansetta Young, who was his classmate and a newfound friend.

那年冬天，大姨又给桑迪寻了个差事，在普伦蒂斯先生（Mr. Prentiss）的贺卡印刷铺里做事——扫地抹灰、当送货的孩子——他的阅读天地也随之大开。铺子里存着一架子时新小说和几卷新诗——桑德堡（Sandburg）、林赛（Lindsay）、马斯特斯（Masters）——那阵子斯坦顿青年妇女俱乐部（Young Women's Club）正拿它们研读，看得城里上了年纪的白人太太们又惊又骇。这事桑迪知道，因为普伦蒂斯先生的女儿——高彻学院（Goucher College）的学生——常来看店，她会给这孩子挑出该读的书，告诉他作者是谁，书里讲些什么。她说，他们先前雇过的有色男孩，没一个对念书有兴趣；所以为着鼓励他，她常把店里翻旧了的书借给他，晚上带回家去，第二天还回来。就这样，桑迪跟着坦皮过的头一年，大半年工夫都泡在那些对一个十四岁男孩来说过于老成的小说里。可坦皮为她这个好学的小外甥得意极了。她开始认定，当初把桑迪留在身边没有留错；到他进中学的时候，她给他买了平生头一套长裤西装，好鞭策他越发用功。

桑迪一礼拜一礼拜地往上蹿个儿，在坦皮看来，他的衬衫袖子就像一夜之间短了一截似的。他的嗓音也在变，还喜欢上了橄榄球，可放学后要在普伦蒂斯铺子里上工，害他难得打上几场。晚上他看书，有时也跟巴斯特去看电影——不过坦皮总尽量把他圈在家里。他偶尔见着薇莉-梅，她正跟赖特旅馆（Wright's Hotel）的二厨谈着朋友。有时也见着吉米·莱恩，他如今当上了茶房，跟一帮讲究穿戴、花哨浪荡的小伙泡在卡奇·温莎台球厅的后屋里。可不管什么时候回老街坊去，桑迪心里都发酸，想起黑格外婆，想起妈妈，想起吉姆博伊，想起哈丽雅特——因为他这位小姨也离开了斯坦顿，他听到的最后一桩传闻说，她在堪萨斯城登了台。如今桑迪和外婆住过的那所小房子归了坦皮名下，一直租给一户不相识的人家住着。

在中学里，桑迪应大姨的要求选了古典课程，有拉丁文、古代史，还有英文，要读大量的书。他的英文老师是个身材高大、男子气十足的女人，名叫玛莎·弗赖（Martha Fry），去过一回欧洲，最爱谈论旧日英格兰的荣华，朗读起来嗓音低沉、带着男人腔，把印在纸上的字句念得跟做戏一样。桑迪的莎士比亚启蒙正是打她这儿来的，因为春季学期里他们学了《威尼斯商人》（The Merchant of Venice）。也是在那年春天，一年级学生遵照弗赖小姐的指点，人人要写一篇作文，去争那新生作文奖——又发奖金、又发奖牌的。就在这场角逐里，桑迪得了二等奖。建校以来，有色学生干出这等名堂还是破天荒头一遭，坦皮欢喜坏了。报上还为这事登了条简讯，桑迪把那五美元带回家交给大姨收着。可他把那枚铜质奖章送给了一个名叫潘塞塔·杨（Pansetta Young）的女孩子——她是他同学，也是新交的朋友。

From the first moment in school that he saw Pansetta, he knew that he liked her, and he would sit looking at her for hours in every class that they had together﻿—for she was a little baby-doll kind of girl, with big black eyes and a smooth pinkish-brown skin, and her hair was curly on top of her head. Her widowed mother was a cook at the Goucher College dining-hall; and she was an all-alone little girl, for Pansetta had no brothers or sisters. After Thanksgiving Sandy began to walk part of the way home with her every day. He could not accompany her all the way because he had to go to work at Mr. Prentiss’s shop. But on Christmas he bought her a box of candy﻿—and sent it to her by mail. And at Easter-time she gave him a chocolate egg.

“Unh-huh! You got a girl now, ain’t you?” teased Buster one April afternoon when he caught Sandy standing in front of the high school waiting for Pansetta to come out.

“Aw, go chase yourself!” said Sandy, for Buster had a way of talking dirty about girls, and Sandy was afraid he would begin that with Pansetta; but today his friend changed the subject instead.

“Say come on round to the pool hall tonight and I’ll teach you to play billiards.”

在学校里，桑迪打第一眼看见潘塞塔，就知道自己喜欢她。两人一起上的课，他堂堂都能坐在那儿瞅她几个钟头——因为她是个洋娃娃似的小姑娘，一双黑黑的大眼睛，皮肤细滑、棕里透粉，头顶上的头发打着卷儿。她寡居的母亲在高彻学院食堂掌厨；她还是个孤零零的小姑娘，因为潘塞塔没有兄弟姐妹。感恩节过后，桑迪开始每天陪她走一段路回家。他没法一路送到底，因为还得上普伦蒂斯先生的铺子里做工。圣诞节他给她买了一盒糖——还是邮寄给她的。到了复活节，她送了他一枚巧克力蛋。
