## Tug of War / 拔河

===Original===
For the last two or three days the weather had been terrific; but on the third day it so far cleared up that one of the men who belonged to the fishing station thought that they might manage to drag the nets a bit that day. The others, however, were not inclined to venture out. Now it is the custom for the various crews to lend each other a hand in pushing off the boats, and so it happened now. When, however, they came to the Fembøring, which was drawn up a good distance ashore, they found the oars and the thwarts turned upside down in the boat, and, more than that, despite all their exertions, it was impossible to move the boat from the spot. They tried once, twice, thrice; but it was of no use. But then one of them, who was known to have second sight, said that, from what he saw, it would be best not to touch the boat at all that day; it was too heavy for the might of man to move. One of the crew, however, who belonged to the fishing-station (he was a smart lad of fourteen), was amusing them all the time with all manner of pranks and tomfoolery. He now caught up a heavy stone, and pitched it with all his might right into the stern of the boat. Then, suddenly and plainly visible to them all, out of the boat rushed a Draug in seaman’s clothes, but with a heavy crop of seaweed instead of a head. It had been weighing down the boat by sitting in the stern, and now dashed into the sea, so that the foam spirted all over them. After that the Fembøring glided quite smoothly into the water. Then the man with second sight looked at the boy, and said that he should not have done so. But the lad went on laughing as before, and said he didn’t believe in such stuff. When they had come home in the evening, and the folks lay sleeping in the fishing-station, they heard, about twelve o’clock at night, the lad yelling for help; it even seemed to one of them, by the light of the train-oil lamp, as if a heavy hand were stretching forward from the door right up to the bench on which the lad lay. The lad, yelling and struggling, had already been dragged as far as the door before the others had so far come to their senses as to think of grasping him round the body to prevent him from being dragged right out. And now, in mid doorway, a hard fight began, the Draug dragging him by the legs, while the whole crew tugged against him with the boy’s arms and upper limbs. Thus, amidst yelling and groaning, they swayed to and fro all through the midnight hour, backwards and forwards, in the half-open door; and now the Draug, and now the men, had the most of the boy on their side of the doorway. All at once the Draug let go, so that the whole crew fell higgledy-piggledy backwards on to the floor. Then they found that the boy was dead; it was only then that the Draug had let him go.

===Chinese===
前两三天，天气一直狂暴得吓人；到第三天总算放晴了些，渔场上有个汉子琢磨着，这天或许还能勉强下海收一收网。可其余的人都不愿冒险出海。按老规矩，各船的人下水时总要彼此搭把手推船，这回也不例外。谁知等他们来到那条拖上岸搁得离水老远的芬伯艇（Fembøring）跟前，却发现桨和座板在船里翻了个底朝天；更邪门的是，任凭众人使尽气力，那船竟纹丝不动。他们试了一回，两回，三回；全不顶事。这时人群里一个出了名生有阴阳眼（second sight）的汉子开了腔：依他看出来的光景，这天最好压根儿别去碰那条船；它坠得太沉，不是人力挪得动的。偏偏船上有个渔场来的小子（十四岁，机灵得很），一直胡闹耍宝、插科打诨，逗得大伙儿直乐。这会儿他拎起一块大石头，使足了劲，直朝船尾砸去。说时迟那时快，只见一个德劳格（Draug）猛地从船里窜了出来，众人都看得清清楚楚：它穿着水手的衣裳，顶着的脑袋却是一蓬沉甸甸的海草。方才正是它坐在船尾把船坠住，此刻一头扎进海里，激起的水花溅了众人满身。打那以后，那条芬伯艇便顺顺当当滑进了水里。阴阳眼汉子瞧着少年，说他不该这么干。可那小子照旧嘻嘻哈哈，说这种玩意儿他才不信。傍晚他们回到家，渔场里的人们躺下睡熟了，到半夜十二点上下，众人忽然听见那小子声声喊救命；借着鱼油灯的光，其中一人甚至瞧见，一只沉甸甸的手仿佛正从门口一直伸到那小子躺卧的长凳上。那小子又叫又挣，已经一路被拖到门口，其余的人才算醒过神来，想起拦腰抱住他，别让他被整个拖出门去。于是在门道正中，一场硬仗开了场：德劳格拉住他的双腿，全船的人攥着他的双臂和上身，跟他往回对拔。就这样，在喊叫与呻吟声里，他们在半开的门中前前后后地拉锯摇摆，熬过了整个午夜时辰；一会儿是德劳格，一会儿是众人，各自把少年身子的大半截拽到自己那一边。冷不防德劳格撒了手，全船人登时仰面朝天，跌作一堆。这时他们才发现，少年已经死了；原来直到那时，德劳格才松开了他。
