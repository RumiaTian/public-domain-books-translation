===Original===

Vernon’s

The Banking House of the Vernons was known through all the Home Counties as only second to the Bank of England in stability and strength. That is to say, the people who knew about such matters, the business people, the professional classes, and those who considered themselves to be acquainted with the world, allowed that it ought to be considered second: but this opinion was not shared by the greater proportion of its clients, the shopkeepers in Redborough and the adjacent towns, the farmers of a wide district, and all the smaller people whose many united littles make up so much wealth. To them Vernon’s bank was the emblem of stability, the impersonation of solid and substantial wealth. It had risen to its height of fame under John Vernon, the grandfather of the present head of the firm, though it had existed for two or three generations before him. But John Vernon was one of those men in whose hands everything turns to gold. What the special gift is which determines this it is difficult to tell, but there can be little doubt that it is a special gift, just as it is a particular genius which produces a fine picture or a fine poem. There were wiser men than he, and there were men as steady to their work and as constantly in their place, ready for all the claims of business, but not one other in whose hands everything prospered in the same superlative way. His investments always answered, his ships always came home, and under his influence the very cellars of the banking-house, according to the popular imagination, filled with gold. At one period of his career a panic seized the entire district, and there was a run upon the bank, by which it was evident anybody else must, nay, ought, to have been ruined; but John Vernon was not ruined. It was understood afterwards that he himself allowed that he did not understand how he had escaped, and nobody else could understand it: but he did escape, and as a natural consequence became stronger and richer, and more universally credited than ever. His son after him had not the same genius for money, but at least he had the genius for keeping what he had got, which is next best.

弗农银行（Vernon's）

弗农家的这家银行（The Banking House of the Vernons）在整个伦敦周边诸郡无人不晓，其稳固与实力公认仅次于英格兰银行（Bank of England）。也就是说，懂行的人——生意人、专业人士，以及那些自认为见过世面的人——承认它理应名列第二；但它的绝大多数主顾却不这么看。对雷德伯勒（Redborough）及邻近城镇的店主们、对一片广阔地区的农户们，以及所有那些以其涓滴之力汇聚成巨富的小民百姓来说，弗农银行是稳固的象征，是殷实财富的化身。它是在本行现任掌门的祖父约翰·弗农（John Vernon）手中臻于极盛的，尽管在他之前这家银行已经营了两三代人。但约翰·弗农是那种万事到了他手里都点石成金的人。这种禀赋究竟是什么，很难说清；但毋庸置疑，它确是一种特殊的天赋，正如产出一张好画或一首好诗靠的是一种独特的天才一样。世上有比他更明智的人，也有像他一样勤于职守、时刻坐镇行中、随时应付认领业务的人，但再没有第二个人，能让万事在他手中同样出类拔萃地兴旺发达。他的投资总是获利，他的船队总能满载而归；在他的治下，照民间的想象，连银行的地下室都堆满了黄金。在他生涯的某个时期，恐慌席卷了整个地区，银行遭到挤兑，换了别人——不但必然、而且理应——早已倾家荡产；但约翰·弗农没有倒。事后据他自己承认，他也不明白自己是如何逃过这一劫的，旁人更无从理解：但他确实逃过去了，并且自然而然地变得比以往更强大、更富有、更受信赖。他之后的儿子没有他那样生财的天才，但至少有守成的天才，这已是次佳的了。

Edward Vernon, however, was not so fortunate in his family as in his affairs. He had two sons, one of whom died young, leaving a little daughter to be brought up by her grandfather; the other “went wrong.” Oh, never-ending family tragedy, never ending, still beginning, the darkest anguish that exists in the world! The younger son went wrong, and died also in his father’s lifetime, leaving a helpless little family of children, and a poor wife stupefied with trouble. She did her best, poor soul, to bring up her boy to ways the very opposite of those in which his father had stumbled and fallen, and it was supposed that he would marry his cousin Catherine Vernon, and thus unite once more all the money and prestige of the house. He too was John Vernon, and resembled the golden great-grandfather, and great things were hoped of him. He entered the bank in old Mr. Vernon’s time, and gave every promise of being a worthy successor as long as the senior partner, the head of the house, lived. But when the old gentleman died and John Vernon became in his turn the head of the house, there very soon appeared signs of change. In the first place the marriage with his cousin never came to pass; things had seemed to promise fairly so long as the grandfather with whom she lived was alive. But after, there was an immediate cooling of sentiment. Whose fault this was nobody knew. She said nothing on the subject even to her dearest friends; nor did he say anything; but he laughed and waved aside all questions as a man who “could an if he would”﻿—. His mother, for her part, said a great deal. She ran between them like an excited hen, shaking her tail-feathers and cackling violently. What did they mean by it? What was it for? She asked her son how he could forget that if Catherine’s money went out of the business it would make the most extraordinary difference? and she bade Catherine remember that it would be almost dishonest to enrich another family with money which the Vernons had toiled for. Catherine, who was not by any means an ordinary girl, smiled upon her, perhaps a little sadly, and entered into no explanations. But her son, as was natural, scoffed at his mother. “What should you know about the business?” he said. Poor Mrs. Vernon thought she had heard enough of it to understand it, or at least to understand the intentions of those who understood it.

然而爱德华·弗农（Edward Vernon）在家庭事务上却不似在生意上那般幸运。他有两个儿子：一个早亡，留下一个由祖父抚养的小女儿；另一个则“走了歪路”。啊，这永无休止的家庭悲剧，永不终结，又不断重演，这是人世间最深沉的痛苦！小儿子走了歪路，也在父亲生前死去，撇下一群无依无靠的孩子，和一个被苦难折磨得麻木的贫妻。可怜的人，她竭尽全力，要把儿子培养成人，走的恰是他父亲失足跌倒的那条路的反面；人们原指望他会娶表妹凯瑟琳·弗农（Catherine Vernon），从而把全家的钱财与声望重新合为一体。他也叫约翰·弗农，酷似那位点石成金的曾祖父，人们对他寄予厚望。他在老弗农先生在世时就进了银行，只要这位资深合伙人、这一家银行的掌门人还活着，他处处显示出配得上继承人的样子。但等老先生去世、约翰·弗农轮到自己成为一家之主后，很快就显出了变化的迹象。首先，与表妹的婚事终究没有成。只要与她同住的祖父在世，事情看起来还挺有指望。但祖父一死，情分立刻冷了下来。这该怪谁，谁也不知道。对这个话题，她连最亲密的朋友也不吐露半句；他也不置一词，只是笑着把一切询问挥到一边，像个“能而不为”的人。他的母亲则不然，话说了个没完。她像一只激动的母鸡在他们两人之间跑来跑去，抖着尾巴上的羽毛，咯咯地叫个不停。他们这是什么意思？图的是什么？她问儿子，怎么能忘记：凯瑟琳的钱一旦从生意中抽走，就会造成天大的差别？她叮嘱凯瑟琳记住，把弗农家辛苦挣来的钱去养肥别家，那几乎是不诚实的。凯瑟琳绝非寻常女子，对着她微笑，也许还带着几分伤感，却不作任何解释。而她的儿子，正如自然之理，对母亲嗤之以鼻。“你懂什么生意？”他说。可怜的弗农太太自以为听得多到足以懂了，或至少懂了那些懂行的人的意图。

But what is the use of a mother’s remonstrances? The new generation will please itself and take its way. She scolded and wept for years after, poor soul, in vain, and yet could never learn that it was in vain, but began anew day after day weeping, entreating, remonstrating, falling into nervous crises of passion a hundred and a hundred times over. How much better for her to have held her tongue! but how could she help it? She was not of that placid and patient nature which can be wise. And gradually things began to go badly with John. He married a young lady belonging to a county family, but with no money to keep up her pretensions. He had his stables full of horses and his house full of company. “What is it all to come to?” cried his poor, anxious, angry, disappointed, despairing mother, seeking opportunities to have a few words with him, to speak to him seriously, to remind him of his duty. To be sure she did a great deal more harm than good. She drew many a blow upon herself which she might have escaped had she been content to allow that his life had passed far beyond her guidance; but the poor lady would not be taught. And it was quite true what John Vernon said. It would take a long time, he told her, before a few horses and pleasant company would affect Vernon’s bank. As the head of that establishment he was expected to be hospitable, and keep almost open house; the country which trusted in him knew he could afford it. The Redborough people went further, and liked to see the confidence with which he spent his money. What could that do to Vernon’s? He had never lived up to his income yet, he believed. So he told his mother, who was never satisfied, and went on till the day of her death always seeking a few words with him﻿—an opportunity of speaking seriously to her son. Poor mother! nothing went very well with her; perhaps she was not clever either at managing her children or her money. The partisans of the Vernons said so at least; they said so of all the wives that were not Vernons, but interlopers, always working harm. They said so also of Mrs. John, and there his mother thought they were not far wrong. But none of her children turned out very satisfactorily; the girls married badly; Edward, her younger son, went into the Church, and never was more than a vicar, and their money matters would not go right. Certainly she was not a fortunate woman.

可是一位母亲的规劝又有什么用呢？新一代自有主张，要走自己的路。此后多年，她徒劳地又骂又哭，可怜的人，却永远学不会这是徒劳，日复一日地重新开始哭泣、恳求、规劝，成百上千次地陷入狂乱的情绪发作。她要是闭住嘴该多好！可她怎么忍得住呢？她天生不是那种沉得住气、有耐心、因而能明智处事的性子。渐渐地，约翰的景况开始不妙了。他娶了一位郡中世家的年轻小姐，但她没有钱财支撑她的排场。他的马厩里养满马匹，家里宾客盈门。“这一切到头来会怎样？”他那可怜、焦急、愤怒、失望、绝望的母亲喊道，寻找机会同他说上几句话，同他严肃地谈谈，提醒他尽自己的本分。的确，她做的坏事比好事多得多。她给自己招来了许多打击，倘若她甘心承认儿子的生活早已远远脱离了她的管教，这些打击本是可以躲开的；但这可怜的太太不肯受教。而约翰·弗农说的话倒是千真万确。他告诉她，要等区区几匹马和几场欢宴影响到弗农银行，还早着呢。作为这家银行的掌门，他本就该好客，几乎就该门庭大开；信任他的乡野知道他供得起。雷德伯勒的人更进一步，乐于看到他花钱时那份自信。那对弗农银行能有什么损害？他相信，他还从未入不敷出过呢。他这样告诉永不满足的母亲；而她直到去世的那一天，还在寻求与儿子说几句话的机会——一次同儿子严肃谈谈的机会。可怜的母亲！她的诸事没有一件顺遂；也许她既不擅长管教儿女，也不擅长打理钱财。弗农家的拥趸们至少是这么说的；他们对所有不是弗农家出身、而是外来的、总在惹祸的媳妇都是这么说的。他们对约翰太太也这么说，而在这一点上，连他母亲都觉得他们说得不算离谱。但她的儿女没有一个很有出息：女儿们婚事不如意；小儿子爱德华进了教会，终身不过是个牧师；他们的钱财事务也总不顺畅。的确，她不是个有福的女人。

But she died, happily for her, before anything material happened to realise her alarms in respect to John.

It is astonishing how money grows when it is in the way of growing﻿—when it has got the genuine impulse and rolls every kindred atom near it, according to some occult law of attraction, into itself. But just as wonderfully as money grows does it melt away when the other﻿—the contrary process﻿—has begun. John Vernon was quite right in saying that the bank justified, nay, almost demanded, a certain amount of expenditure from its chief partner. And he was more, much more, than its chief partner. Catherine, though she was as deeply interested in it as himself, took no responsibility whatever﻿—how should she, a girl who knew as much about money as her pony did? She took less interest, indeed, than in ordinary circumstances she would have done, for there was certainly something, whatever it might be, which had interrupted the natural intercourse between the two cousins. They were not at ease with each other like brother and sister, as everything suggested they ought to have been﻿—not sufficiently at ease to consider their mutual interests together, as partners ought to have done. But this did not satisfy the respectable firm of solicitors who advised the banker.

“Miss Vernon is not a girl any longer,” said Mr. Pounce, who was its head; upon which John Vernon laughed, one of those offensive laughs with which a coarse-minded man waves the banner of his sex over an unmarried woman.

“No,” he said, “Catherine’s growing an old maid. She must look alive if she means to get a husband.”

Mr. Pounce was not a sentimentalist, and no doubt laughed sometimes too at the unfortunate women who had thus failed in the object of their life; but he respected Miss Vernon, and he was very doubtful of her cousin.

“Husband or no husband, I think she ought to be consulted,” he said.

“Oh, I will take Catherine in my own hands,” was the cousin’s reply.

不过她死得倒算幸运，赶在任何实质变故发生、坐实她对约翰的种种忧惧之前。

钱这东西，处在滋长的路上时长得惊人——一旦有了真正的势头，便按照某种神秘的引力法则，把近旁每一个同类原子都滚进自己怀里。可一旦那相反的过程开始，钱的消融也同样神奇。约翰·弗农说银行养得起、甚至几乎需要它的首席合伙人有一定的开销，这话说得不错。而且他远不止是首席合伙人。凯瑟琳虽然同他一样与银行利害攸关，却不承担任何责任——她能承担什么呢？一个对钱的了解同她的小马驹一样的姑娘？她对银行的兴趣，实际上比在正常情况下本该有的还要少，因为这两位表兄妹之间的自然往来，确确实实被某种东西——管它是什么——中断了。他们相处不像兄妹那样自在，尽管种种迹象都表明他们本该如此——他们自在不到能够像合伙人本该的那样，一起商量共同利益的程度。这一点，至少其中一人认为，无论如何都是荒谬的。当他的律师们问起凯瑟琳对这样或那样的事情怎么想时，他当面笑话他们。

“她会怎么想？她能知道什么？她当然把这一切都交给我，”他说。“一个姑娘家怎么会懂银行业务？”

“嫁人不嫁人的，我认为应当同她商量，”他说。

“哦，凯瑟琳的事我自己来管，”那位表弟答道。

And thus life went on, very gay, fast, amusing, and expensive on one side; very quiet and uneventful on the other. John Vernon built himself a grand new house, in which there were all the latest improvements and scientific luxuries, which the most expensive upholsterers filled with the most costly furniture, and for which the skilfullest gardeners all but created ready-made trees and shrubberies. He filled it with fine company﻿—names which the clerks at the bank felt were a credit to the establishment, and which the townsfolk looked upon with admiring awe: and there was nothing in the county to equal Mrs. John Vernon’s dresses and diamonds. What is all that to a great bank, gathering money every hour?﻿—nothing! Even Mr. Pounce acknowledged this. Personal extravagance, as long as it is merely hospitality and show, must go a very long way indeed before it touches the great revenue of such a business. It was not the diamonds nor the feasts that they were afraid of. But to be lavish with money is a dangerous fault with a man who is a business man. It is a very common sin, but there is nothing more perilous. In Manchester or Liverpool, where they turn over a fortune every day, perhaps this large habit of sowing money about does not matter. People there are accustomed to going up and down. Bankruptcy, even, does not mean the end of the world in these regions. But a banker in a country town, who has all the money of a district in his hands, should not get into this reckless way. His clients are pleased﻿—up to a certain limit. But when once the first whisper of suspicion has been roused it flies fast, and the panic with which rural depositors rush upon a bank which has awakened the ghost of an apprehension, is even more cruel and unreflecting than other panics. It went on a long time, and where it was that the first suggestion came from, nobody ever knew. Probably it did not come from anyone﻿—it was in the air, it struck two people, all at once, talking to each other, and the electricity of the contact found a single syllable of utterance. When that was done, all was done. Everybody had been waiting for this involuntary signal; and when it came, it flew like lightning through all Redborough, and out into the roads and lanes﻿—to distant farmhouses, into the rectories and vicarages, even to the labourer’s cottage.

于是日子就这样过下去：一边非常快活、放纵、有趣而奢侈；另一边则非常平静、无事发生。约翰·弗农给自己盖了一座堂皇的新宅，屋里有一切最新的改良和科学的奢华，最昂贵的家具商用最贵的家具将它填满，最巧的园艺师几乎为它造出了现成的树木和灌木丛。他以上宾满座——那些名字连银行的职员们都觉得为本行增光，镇民们怀着叹服的敬畏仰望：全县没有一样东西比得上约翰·弗农太太的衣裙和钻石。这对一家每小时都在聚敛金钱的大银行算得了什么？——算不了什么！连庞斯先生也承认这一点。只要个人挥霍仅仅是好客与排场，要走很远很远才碰得到这种生意庞大收益的边。他们害怕的不是钻石，也不是盛宴。但对一个生意人来说，挥霍成性是一种危险的毛病。这是极常见的罪过，但没有比它更凶险的了。在曼彻斯特或利物浦，人们每天倒手一笔家财，也许这种撒钱的大手大脚无关紧要。那里的人习惯了起起落落。甚至在那些地方，破产也不意味着世界末日。但一个小镇上的银行家，一区之财尽握于手，就不该沾染这种无所顾忌的做派。他的主顾们高兴——直到某个限度。可是一旦疑虑的第一声耳语被唤起，它便飞快传开；乡间的存户们冲向一家唤起一丝疑影的银行时的那种恐慌，比别的恐慌更冷酷、更盲目。这样过了很久；最初的暗示究竟来自何处，谁也说不清。也许它并非来自任何人——它在空气中，同时击中两个正在交谈的人，接触的电火花找到了一个音节吐露出来。这一吐露，一切便都定了。人人都在等着这个不自觉的信号；信号一来，它便如闪电一般传遍整个雷德伯勒，传到大路小径上——传到遥远的农舍，传进教区长住宅和牧师寓所，甚至传到雇工的小屋。

“It’s said as Vernon’s bank’s a-going to break,” the ploughmen in the fields said to each other. It did not matter much to them; and perhaps they were not sorry that the farmer, who grew fat (they thought) on their toil, should feel that he was also human. The farmers had something of the same feeling in respect to their landlords, but could not indulge it for the furious terror that took possession of themselves. Vernon’s bank! Safer than the Bank of England, was what they had all said exultingly. Very few of them had sufficient command of themselves to wait now and inquire into it and see how far the panic was well founded. To wait would have been to leave the chance of salvation to other men.

Mrs. John Vernon was considered very refined and elegant according to the language of the day, a young lady with many accomplishments. But it was the fashion of the time to be unpractical just as it is the fashion of our time that women should understand business and be ready for any emergency. To wear your hair in a high loose knot on the top of your head, with ringlets straying down your cheek, and across the always uncovered whiteness of your shoulders, and to sing the songs of Mr. Haynes Bayley, “Oh No, We Never Mention Her,” or “The Soldier’s Tear”﻿—could anything be more entirely inconsistent with business habits? Mrs. John would have considered it a slight to the delicacy of her mind to have been supposed to know anything about the bank; and when the head clerk demanded an audience at an unseasonable hour one summer evening she was entirely taken aback.
