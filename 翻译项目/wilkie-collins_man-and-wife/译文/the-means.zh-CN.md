## LVI The Means / 第五十六 手段（The Means）
===Original===

The new day dawned; the sun rose; the household was astir again. Inside the spare room, and outside the spare room, nothing had happened.
At the hour appointed for leaving the cottage to pay the promised visit to Holchester House, Hester Dethridge and Geoffrey were alone together in the bedroom in which Anne had passed the night.
“She’s dressed, and waiting for me in the front garden,” said Geoffrey. “You wanted to see me here alone. What is it?”
Hester pointed to the bed.
“You want it moved from the wall?”
Hester nodded her head.
They moved the bed some feet away from the partition wall. After a momentary pause, Geoffrey spoke again.
“It must be done tonight,” he said. “Her friends may interfere; the girl may come back. It must be done tonight.”
Hester bowed her head slowly.
“How long do you want to be left by yourself in the house?”
She held up three of her fingers.
“Does that mean three hours?”
She nodded her head.
“Will it be done in that time?”
She made the affirmative sign once more.
Thus far, she had never lifted her eyes to his. In her manner of listening to him when he spoke, in the slightest movement that she made when necessity required it, the same lifeless submission to him, the same mute horror of him, was expressed. He had, thus far, silently resented this, on his side. On the point of leaving the room the restraint which he had laid on himself gave way. For the first time, he resented it in words.
“Why the devil can’t you look at me?” he asked.
She let the question pass, without a sign to show that she had heard him. He angrily repeated it. She wrote on her slate, and held it out to him﻿—still without raising her eyes to his face.
“You know you can speak,” he said. “You know I have found you out. What’s the use of playing the fool with *me*?”
She persisted in holding the slate before him. He read these words:
“I am dumb to you, and blind to you. Let me be.”
“Let you be!” he repeated. “It’s a little late in the day to be scrupulous, after what you have done. Do you want your Confession back, or not?”
As the reference to the Confession passed his lips, she raised her head. A faint tinge of color showed itself on her livid cheeks; a momentary spasm of pain stirred her deathlike face. The one last interest left in the woman’s life was the interest of recovering the manuscript which had been taken from her.

新的一天破晓；日头升起；宅里又醒动了。备用的客房里、客房外，一夜无事。
到了该离小屋、践霍尔彻斯特府之约的钟点，赫丝特·德思里奇同杰弗里两个，单独在安妮过了夜的那间卧室里。
「她穿戴好了，在前头花园里等我，」杰弗里说。「你有话要单独在这儿见我。什么事？」
赫丝特指了指床。
「你想把床从墙边挪开？」
赫丝特点了点头。
他们把床从隔墙挪开几英尺。顿了片刻，杰弗里又开口。
「就在今夜动手，」他说。「她的亲友会来插手；丫头也可能回来。就在今夜动手。」
赫丝特缓缓点头。
「你要这宅子独自归你多久？」
她竖起三根手指。
「是说三个钟头？」
她点头。
「这个工夫办得完？」
她又做了肯定的表示。
到这时为止，她的眼睛从没有抬起来望过他。听他说话的神态里，在情势所需时最细小的一个动作里，表达的都是同一样的死气沉沉的顺从，同一样的无言的惊怖。他这边，到此为止一直默默地记恨着这个。临出屋那一刻，他强加给自家的克制垮了。他头一回用言语发作出来。
「你他妈为什么不能看我一眼？」他问。
她由这问题滑过去，不作一点听见了的表示。他怒冲冲地又问一遍。她在石板上写字，举给他看——仍不抬眼望他的脸。
「你知道你是会说话的，」他说。「你知道我识破了。跟我装傻顶什么用？」
她只管把石板举在他面前。他读到了这些字：
「俺对你哑，对你瞎。由俺去。」
「由你去！」他重复道。「干完了那些事，这时候来讲良心，未免太晚了。你的《忏悔书》到底要不要回去？」
一提《忏悔书》，她抬起了头。青灰的脸颊上透出一丝淡淡的颜色；死一样的脸上，掠过一阵短短的、痛楚的痉挛。这女人生命里剩下的最后一点牵挂，就是要回那被夺走的手稿。

To *that* appeal the stunned intelligence still faintly answered﻿—and to no other.
“Remember the bargain on your side,” Geoffrey went on, “and I’ll remember the bargain on mine. This is how it stands, you know. I have read your Confession; and I find one thing wanting. You don’t tell how it was done. I know you smothered him﻿—but I don’t know how. I want to know. You’re dumb; and you can’t tell me. You must do to the wall here what you did in the other house. You run no risks. There isn’t a soul to see you. You have got the place to yourself. When I come back let me find this wall like the other wall﻿—at that small hour of the morning you know, when you were waiting, with the towel in your hand, for the first stroke of the clock. Let me find that; and tomorrow you shall have your Confession back again.”
As the reference to the Confession passed his lips for the second time, the sinking energy in the woman leaped up in her once more. She snatched her slate from her side; and, writing on it rapidly, held it, with both hands, close under his eyes. He read these words:
“I won’t wait. I must have it tonight.”
“Do you think I keep your Confession about me?” said Geoffrey. “I haven’t even got it in the house.”
She staggered back; and looked up for the first time.
“Don’t alarm yourself,” he went on. “It’s sealed up with my seal; and it’s safe in my bankers’ keeping. I posted it to them myself. You don’t stick at a trifle, Mrs. Dethridge. If I had kept it locked up in the house, you might have forced the lock when my back was turned. If I had kept it about me﻿—I might have had that towel over my face, in the small hours of the morning! The bankers will give you back your Confession﻿—just as they have received it from me﻿—on receipt of an order in my handwriting. Do what I have told you; and you shall have the order tonight.”
She passed her apron over her face, and drew a long breath of relief. Geoffrey turned to the door.
“I will be back at six this evening,” he said. “Shall I find it done?”
She bowed her head.
His first condition accepted, he proceeded to the second.
“When the opportunity offers,” he resumed, “I shall go up to my room. I shall ring the dining room bell first. You will go up before me when you hear that﻿—and you will show me how you did it in the empty house?”
She made the affirmative sign once more.

对*那*一声呼唤，她那昏沉的神志还微弱地应着——对别的，一概不应。
「记着你的那半边交易，」杰弗里接着说，「我也记着我这半边。眼下的情形你是知道的。你的《忏悔书》我读了；我发觉缺了一样。你没写那是怎么干的。我知道你捂死了他——可我不知道怎么捂的。我要知道。你哑；说不得给我听。这面墙，你得照另一所宅子里的那面办一回。你冒不着风险。没有一个活人看得见你。宅子归你一个人。我回来时，让我见到这面墙同那面墙一个样——就在你晓得的那个凌晨小小几点钟，你手拿毛巾、等第一声钟响的时辰。让我见着了，明天你的《忏悔书》就回到你手里。」
第二次提到《忏悔书》，这女人体内沉落的气力又蹿了起来。她从身侧一把抢过石板；飞快写了一行，双手捧着，直举到他眼皮底下。他读到了这些字：
「俺不等。俺今夜就要。」
「你当我把你的《忏悔书》揣在身上？」杰弗里说。「它连这宅子都不在。」
她踉跄着退了一步；头一回抬眼朝上望。
「别慌，」他接着说。「它拿我的印封了口；稳稳存在我银行里。我亲手寄给他们的。你于小事是不迟疑的，德思里奇太太。我若把它锁在宅里，你兴许趁我转身就把锁撬了。我若把它揣在身上——那凌晨小小几点钟，我脸上兴许就盖上那条毛巾了！银行会把你的《忏悔书》还你——原封、同我交给他们的一样——凭我一纸亲笔的手令。把我吩咐的办好；今夜手令就到你手里。」
她拿围裙抹了一把脸，长长吐出一口宽慰的气。杰弗里转向门口。
「今晚六点我回来，」他说。「我能见着办妥了么？」
她点头。
第一个条件得了一应，他进而提第二个。
「等机会来了，」他接着说，「我就上楼进我屋里。我先摇饭厅的铃。你听见铃声，先我一步上去——把你在那所空宅里的手段，做给我看？」
她又一次做了肯定的表示。

At the same moment the door in the passage below was opened and closed again. Geoffrey instantly went downstairs. It was possible that Anne might have forgotten something; and it was necessary to prevent her from returning to her own room.
They met in the passage.
“Tired of waiting in the garden?” he asked, abruptly.
She pointed to the dining-room.
“The postman has just given me a letter for you, through the grating in the gate,” she answered. “I have put it on the table in there.”
He went in. The handwriting on the address of the letter was the handwriting of Mrs. Glenarm. He put it unread into his pocket, and went back to Anne.
“Step out!” he said. “We shall lose the train.”
They started for their visit to Holchester House.

也就在这一霎，楼下过道的门开了又关上。杰弗里立刻下了楼。安妮兴许忘了什么东西；不能不拦她回自家的屋。
他们在过道里相遇。
「在花园里等腻了？」他突兀地问。
她朝饭厅一指。
「邮差刚从门上的栅格里递给我一封你的信，」她答。「我搁在那边的桌上了。」
他进了饭厅。信封上的笔迹是格伦纳姆太太的。他没有拆，揣进口袋，回到安妮身边。
「脚下加紧！」他说。「要误车了。」
他们动身赴霍尔彻斯特府之访。
