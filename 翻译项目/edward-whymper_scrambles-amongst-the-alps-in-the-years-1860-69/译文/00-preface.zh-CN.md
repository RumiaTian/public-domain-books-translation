## Preface / 序
===Original===

In the year 1860, shortly before leaving England for a long continental tour, a certain eminent London publisher requested me to make for him some sketches of the great Alpine peaks. At that time I had only a literary acquaintance with mountain-climbing, and had not even seen﻿—much less set foot upon﻿—a mountain. Amongst the peaks which were upon the list was Mont Pelvoux, in Dauphiné. The sketches that were required of it were to celebrate the triumph of some Englishmen who intended to make its ascent. They came﻿—they saw﻿—but they did not conquer. By a mere chance I fell in with a very agreeable Frenchman who accompanied this party, and was pressed by him to return to the assault. In 1861 we did so, with my friend Macdonald﻿—and we conquered. This was the origin of my Scrambles amongst the Alps.
The ascent of Mont Pelvoux (including the disagreeables) was a very delightful scramble. The mountain air did *not* act as an emetic; the sky did *not* look black, instead of blue; nor did I feel tempted to throw myself over precipices. I hastened to enlarge my experience, and went to the Matterhorn. I was urged towards Mont Pelvoux by those mysterious impulses which cause men to peer into the unknown. This mountain was reputed to be the highest in France, and on that account was worthy of attention; and it was believed to be the culminating point of a picturesque district of great interest, which was then almost unexplored! The Matterhorn attracted me simply by its grandeur. It was considered to be the most completely inaccessible of all mountains, even by those who ought to have known better. Stimulated to make fresh exertions by one repulse after another, I returned, year after year, as I had opportunity, more and more determined to find a way up it, or to *prove* it to be really inaccessible.
A considerable portion of this volume is occupied by the history of these attacks on the Matterhorn, and the other excursions that are described have generally some connection, more or less remote, with that mountain or with Mont Pelvoux. All are new excursions (that is, excursions made for the first time), unless the contrary is pointed out. Some have been passed over very briefly, and entire ascents or descents have been disposed of in a single line. If they had been worked out at full length, three volumes, instead of one, would have been required.

1860 年，我即将离开英国作一次长期的欧陆旅行之际，伦敦一位著名出版商请我为他绘制几幅阿尔卑斯巨峰的素描。那时我对登山仅有过书本上的认识，甚至从未见过山——更谈不上登临。清单所列群峰之中，有多菲内的佩尔武峰（Mont Pelvoux）。所需的那几幅素描，本是为了颂扬几位打算登上此峰的英国人的胜利。他们来了——他们看见了——却没有征服。出于纯粹的偶然，我结识了随这支队伍同行的一位非常和蔼的法国人，他力劝我再度发起冲锋。1861 年，我与朋友麦克唐纳一道付诸行动——我们征服了它。这便是我这些「阿尔卑斯攀行」的由来。
攀登佩尔武峰（连同种种不快之处）是一次极为愉快的攀行。山间的空气*并未*让我恶心欲呕；天空*并未*显得发黑而非湛蓝；我也毫不觉得想纵身跳下悬崖。我赶紧扩展见识，前往马特洪峰（Matterhorn）。当初驱使我走向佩尔武峰的，是那种促使人类窥探未知世界的神秘冲动。此峰号称法兰西第一高峰，仅此一点便值得注意；人们还相信它是一大片引人入胜的风景区的最高点，而那片区域在当时几乎无人涉足！马特洪峰吸引我的，仅仅是它的雄伟。它被公认为群山之中最无从企及的一座，连那些本该更有见识的人也作如是观。一次次的失利反而激励我发起新的努力，我年复一年、一有机会便重返此地，决心越来越坚定：或是找到一条登顶之路，或是*证明*它确实无法企及。
本书相当一部分篇幅记述的正是这些向马特洪峰发起冲击的历史，所描写的其他攀行也大多与该峰或佩尔武峰有着或远或近的联系。这些行程皆为开创之举（即破天荒的头一遭），除非另有说明。有些行程只一笔带过，整段的登顶或下撤仅以一行文字交代。倘若一一铺陈开来，需要的便不是一卷而是三卷了。

Generally speaking, the salient points alone have been dwelt upon, and the rest has been left to the imagination. This treatment spares the reader from much useless repetition.
In endeavouring to make the book of some use to those who may wish to go mountain-scrambling, whether in the Alps or elsewhere, undue prominence, perhaps, has been given to our mistakes and failures; and it will doubtless be pointed out that our practice must have been bad if the principles which are laid down are sound, or that the principles must be unsound if the practice was good. We were not immaculate. Our blunders are not held up to be admired, or to be imitated, but to be avoided.
These Scrambles amongst the Alps were holiday excursions, and as such they should be judged. They are spoken of as sport, and nothing more. The pleasure that they gave me cannot be transferred to others. The ablest pens have failed, and I think must always fail, to give a true idea of the grandeur of the Alps. The most minute descriptions of the greatest writers do nothing more than convey impressions that are entirely erroneous﻿—the reader conjures up visions, it may be magnificent ones, but they are infinitely inferior to the reality. I have dealt sparingly in descriptions, and have employed illustrations freely, in the hope that the pencil may perhaps succeed where the pen must inevitably have failed.
About fifty of the subjects were drawn on the wood by the late Mr. James Mahoney, and I am much indebted to that artist for the care and fidelity with which he followed my slight memoranda, and for the spirit he put into his admirable designs. Most of his drawings will be identified by his monogram. Twenty of the remainder are the work of Mr. Cyrus Johnson.

一般而言，书中只详述关键之处，其余则留待读者想象。这种处理可使读者免于许多无谓的重复。
为使本书对那些有意去山地攀行的人——无论在阿尔卑斯还是别处——多少有所助益，我们或许过分突出了自己的失误与挫败；无疑会有人指出：倘若书中确立的原则可靠，我们的实践必定糟糕透顶；倘若实践尚可，那些原则必定靠不住。我们并非无可指摘。我们的过失被摆出来，不是供人赞叹或仿效，而是供人引以为戒。
这些阿尔卑斯攀行本是假日出游，也应当以假日出游来看待。它们只被称为运动，仅此而已。它们带给我的乐趣无法转授他人。再能干的笔杆子也未能、而且我认为永远不可能传达出阿尔卑斯之雄伟的真实概念。最伟大的作家笔下最细致入微的描写，也无非传递出全然错误的印象——读者凭空构想出种种景象，或许气象万千，却远逊于现实万万。我在描写上着墨甚少，而大量使用插图，寄望铅笔能在笔墨注定失败之处或许有所成。
