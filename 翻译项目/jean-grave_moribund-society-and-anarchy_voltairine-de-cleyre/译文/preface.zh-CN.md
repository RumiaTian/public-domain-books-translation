## Preface / 序

===Original===

*Moribund Society and Anarchy* first appeared in France about a decade since, published by P. V. Stock, printer of numerous works pertaining to Anarchy. The conscience (?) of the French army, which the Dreyfus affair has since revealed in all its delicate scrupulosity, was immediately incensed by the chapter entitled “Militarism,” and the author was speedily arrested, tried, and sentenced to two years imprisonment. The book was suppressed, and the French army, presumably, breathed more freely.

A mistake! When persecution begins the gospel spreads. Men were anxious to know what it was that had so frightened the “free government” of France as to call forth such severe punishment of a poor shoemaker. The work was circulated, translated in German, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Jewish; only in English it remained untranslated. Several times announcements that we were about to have an English version appeared; still it was not forthcoming.

In 1897 the writer met Jean Grave at the residence of an exiled French comrade in London, and there engaged to undertake the work, the author concurring. Although originally prompted by the English comrades and their promise of a publisher, later developments have made it more expedient to get out an American edition. Among these the only one which really concerns the public is the gigantic stride toward militarism which this country has taken during the past year. Previous to that I was exceedingly dubious as to the effect of the famous prosecuted chapter, which was likely to fall flat on the unmilitary American public. But now that we have entered upon the “manifest destiny” of “civilized nations;” now that our government has resorted to the same tactics of colonization, protection, subjugation, and conquest; now that our standing army has been increased fourfold, and military place-hunting is the ambition of the hour; now that our workingmen are seizing the opportunity to barter their “free citizenship in the greatest country on earth” for the abject service of man-killing on foreign soils at the rate of $15.60 per month and keep, this proscribed Chapter XIII comes with its own note﻿—a most discordant one indeed﻿—into the war-chorus at present holding the public ear. And the translator devoutly prays that as in France the great sin was its distribution among the soldiery, the like offense may be repeated here, where the army is still in a nascent condition and the man not yet buried under the uniform. Look in the glass and see how you like the reflection, soldiers!

The P. V. Stock edition having been suppressed, E. Pouget, the daring publisher of *Père Peinard*, brought out another, ostensibly published in London. Though inelegant in appearance it contains an additional chapter; and it is from this Pouget edition that the present translation has been made. I have adhered as strictly as possible to the text, being unwilling to make either additions or subtractions, though it has sometimes seemed to me that Mr. Grave is unnecessarily diffuse.

《垂死的社会与无政府主义》（*Moribund Society and Anarchy*）约于十年前初现于法国，由 P. V. 斯托克（P. V. Stock）出版——此人印行过大量关涉无政府主义的著作。法国军队的那份良知（？）——德雷福斯事件（Dreyfus affair）后来把它那套纤毫毕现的精微审慎展露无遗——当即被题为“军国主义”（“Militarism”）的一章所激怒，作者旋即被捕、受审，并被判处两年监禁。此书遭到查禁，而法国军队想必呼吸得自在些了。

打错了算盘！迫害一起，福音便传扬开来。人们急欲知道，究竟是什么东西把法国的“自由政府”吓成这般，竟至对一个穷苦鞋匠施以如此严酷的惩罚。于是此书流传开来，被译成德文、西班牙文、葡萄牙文、意大利文、犹太文；唯独英文迟迟无人移译。英译本即将问世的预告登过好几次；可它始终不见出来。

1897 年，笔者在伦敦一位流亡的法国同志的寓所会见了让·格拉弗（Jean Grave），并当场应承承担这项工作，作者对此亦表赞同。这件事最初虽由英国同志们推动、并得他们许诺代为接洽出版者，但后来的种种变故使刊行一个美国版本更为相宜。这些变故之中，真正与公众相干的只有一桩：过去一年间，这个国家朝军国主义跨出了巨大的一步。在那以前，我对那篇出了名的遭公诉的一章之效果疑虑殊深，因为它多半会在并无尚武之气的美国公众面前落得悄无声息。可是如今，我们既已踏上“文明国家”的“昭昭天命”；既已使出殖民、保护、压服、征服的那同一套手腕；既已把常备军扩充为原先的四倍，而钻营军职成了时下竞逐的志业；既已见我们的工人抓紧机会，把“世上最伟大国家里的自由公民身份”拿去换取那到外国土地上去杀人的卑贱差役——月饷 15.60 美元，外加膳宿——这篇遭查禁的第十三章便携着它自己的音符——的的确确是一枚极不谐和的音符——加入了此刻灌满公众耳朵的战争大合唱。而译者虔诚祈祷：既然在法国，此书的大罪在于把它散发到士兵中间，但愿同样的罪过也在这里重演；在这里，军队尚在初生状态，人还没有被埋没在军装底下。士兵们，往镜子里照一照，看看你们喜不喜欢镜中映出的那副模样！

P. V. 斯托克版既已遭禁，《佩纳尔老爹》（*Père Peinard*）那位胆气十足的出版人埃·普热（E. Pouget）便推出了另一个版本，表面上号称伦敦所出。这一版本外观虽欠雅致，内容却多出增补的一章；本书的译文正是据这个普热版移译的。我尽可能地严格依循原文，不愿作任何增删，尽管在我看来，格拉弗先生的行文有时不必要地失之冗蔓。

As to the principal object of the work, that of furnishing an inclusive criticism of the institutions of our moribund society and the necessity of its speedy dissolution, I think any fair-minded reader will be convinced that it has been pretty thoroughly done. As to the “What next?” it is far less certain.

With this, however, Jean Grave﻿—sturdy, patient, indomitable Jean Grave, sitting today in his fifth-floor Parisian garret, untouched by his imprisonment, convinced as ever, steadily writing, writing to the workers of the world, casting forth images of the “Future Society,”﻿—would not agree. He is sure of his remedy﻿—Communism; I, of his criticism, Anarchy.

Voltairine de Cleyre.

June, 1899.

===Chinese===

至于本书的主要目标——对我们这个垂死社会的种种制度、以及此一社会必须速速解体的道理，作一番包罗宏富的批判——我想任何心存公正的读者都会信服：这一点已做得相当彻底。至于“然后呢？”（“What next?”），那就远没有这般有把握了。

然而，对这后一点，让·格拉弗——坚韧、耐心、不屈不挠的让·格拉弗，今天坐在他那巴黎五层楼的阁楼里，系狱之灾未能损他分毫，信念一如往昔，笔耕不辍，写给全世界的工人，抛掷出一幅幅“未来社会”（“Future Society”）的图景——是不会同意的。他确信自己的药方——共产主义；至于我，确信的是他的批判——无政府主义。

伏尔泰琳·德·克莱尔（Voltairine de Cleyre）

1899 年 6 月。
