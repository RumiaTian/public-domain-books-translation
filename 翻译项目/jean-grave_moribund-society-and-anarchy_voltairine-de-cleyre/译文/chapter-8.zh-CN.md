## VIII The Magistracy / VIII 司法当局
===Original===
The Magistracy

Authority, as we have seen, springs from that right which arrogates force to itself. But man having widened the field of his thought it became necessary for this authority to justify its existence. Combining with religious sentiment and the support of the priests, it claimed to be of divine origin, assumed the form of an exclusive caste, and eventually succeeded in resisting the brutal power of the king and the nobles: thus the magistracy was founded. And when the bourgeoisie seized the power, in 1789, they took care not to destroy this pillar of social order. (Moreover, did not the nobility of gowns belong much more properly to the bourgeoisie than to the nobility of the sword?) They were thus relieved of the task of searching for a mode of recruit more in accord with the new aspirations.

Divine right having gotten a powerful shock in the decapitation of Louis XVI, the magistracy could not continue to lean upon the said right without the risk of likewise passing under this equalizing leveler. Hence they invented, or rather deified, the “law.” The magistracy was constituted its guardian and incorruptible administrator, so-called. The trick was done; the most redoubtable and necessary institution for the defense of privilege succeeded in preserving itself, and becoming the priestess of this new entity, the law, created by the new masters. The submission of France to the regime of the “law” is, in fact, one of the conquests of ’89 whose benefits the bourgeois historians are exceedingly fond of setting forth. The codification of authority, according to these, its censer-bearers, had the immediate effect of legitimizing the most shameless arbitrariness. From then on Frenchmen were all to be equal; the people no longer had anything to demand. Thereafter there was to be but one master, before whom, it is true, all had to bow, which had the effect of equalizing their situations. This master was the “law.” But we who are not satisfied with words, when we try to find out what the workers have gained by this transformation, see that they have got just one more duping. In fact, in the time of the absolute monarchy, when the king and the nobles constrained the peasant to serve them, there was no way of deceiving oneself about it; the formula “for such is our good pleasure” showed whence they derived their rights: they claimed them by the right of the sword only, counting much more upon that than the divine will; consequently it was upon force that their claim was based. Their orders were obeyed, their claims were submitted to; but because the people were in no condition to resist them. There were at least no imbeciles to come and say to us﻿—repeating the phrases of the interested﻿—that we must obey because it is “the law,” and it is the duty of everyone to conform thereto until it be changed.

司法当局

权威（authority），如我们所见，发源于那种把强力僭据为己有的权利。但人类既已拓宽了思想的疆域，这种权威便不得不为自己的存在寻找辩护。它与宗教情感、与教士的支持相结合，自称源出神意，取得了排他等级的形式，终于成功地顶住了国王与贵族的残暴权力：司法当局（the magistracy）便是这样建立起来的。而当资产阶级（bourgeoisie）于 1789 年夺得权力时，他们小心翼翼，不去摧毁这根社会秩序的支柱。（何况，长袍贵族（nobility of gowns）难道不是本来就更多属于资产阶级，而较少属于佩剑贵族（nobility of the sword）吗？）如此一来，他们便省去了另觅一种更合乎新愿望的补员方式的麻烦。

君权神授（divine right）之说既已在路易十六（Louis XVI）身首异处之时遭到沉重一击，司法当局若还要倚傍上述权利，便难免同样被送到这把把众人拉平的铡刀之下。于是他们发明了“法律”，或者不如说，把“法律”奉为神明。司法当局于是被确立为这“法律”的守护人，立为它那所谓的不可收买的管理者。把戏就此得逞；这件为捍卫特权所最可畏、最不可少的机构，成功地保全了自己，并当上了这个新实体的女祭司——这“法律”，乃新主子们所造。法国归顺于“法律”之治，实际上是“‘89 年”诸项征服之一，而这些征服的好处，正是资产阶级史家极其乐于铺陈的。依这些替权威捧香炉的人之见，权威的法典化立即收到的效果，便是把最无耻的专断合法化。从此，法国人全都平等了；人民再没有什么可要求的了。此后只应有一个主子；诚然，在这主子面前人人皆须躬身，而这恰好收到了使众人处境平等的效果。这主子便是“法律”。但我们这些不以辞句为满足的人，一旦设法查明工人由这场变革究竟得到了什么，便看出他们不过是又受了一场愚弄。事实上，在绝对君主制时代，当国王与贵族强制农民为他们服役时，谁也无法在这件事上自欺；“朕意如此”（for such is our good pleasure）这一套语，表明了他们的权利从何而来：他们只是凭刀剑之权主张这些权利，而且对刀剑的倚仗远过于对神意的倚仗；因此，其权利要求所依托的正是强力。他们的命令得到服从，他们的索取得到顺从；但那是因为人民全然无力抵抗。至少那时还没有白痴跑来对我们说——重复着利益相关者的套话——我们必须服从，因为这是“法律”，而且在法律更改之前，人人都有遵从的义务。

If it be admitted that the law may change it is thereby presumed that the law may become retrogressive; and to acknowledge that, is to admit that from its very nature it may injure someone, for there are always individuals in advance of their generation. The law, then, is not just; it has not that respectable character with which men have sought to invest it. If this law injures my interests or violates my liberty why should I be compelled to obey it, and what is the unalterable compact which can justify these abuses? In scientific matters when the savants after great research and labor at length formulate what is called a natural law, it is not because a majority or “chamber,” composed of persons believing themselves superior to the rest of mortals, has decided, by virtue of its members’ will, that natural forces were ordered to conform to such or such a mode of evolution. We should laugh in the face of the imbecile who would make such a pretense. When a natural law is proclaimed, it is because it has been discovered that if a certain phenomenon be produced, if a certain chemical combination had been effected, it is by virtue of such and such a force, or the existence of such and such affinities; the environment in which the phenomenon took place being given, it was impossible for it to be otherwise. Given forces set in motion under given conditions produce given results; this is mathematical. Therefore the newly-discovered law does not come upon the scene to govern the phenomenon, but to explain its causes. These laws may be discovered, doubted, and even denied; the divers substances which compose our earth will none the less continue to combine according to their properties or affinities, the earth will turn, without any force being needed to protect the evolution thereof, or punish, those who might want to “violate the laws.”

In our society it is otherwise. These laws seem to be made to be violated; because those who made them consulted only their personal preferences, the interests of those whom they represented, and the average degree of moral evolution in their epoch, without taking into account the character, tendencies, and affinities of those who were to submit to them﻿—which, moreover, would be impossible, the diversity of individual character and tendencies being given. Each estate has its laws; nor can there be any single and universal law in sociology, as there is in physics, under penalty of its becoming arbitrary and inapplicable. In fact there is not, in our society, a single law which does not injure some of its members, either in their material interests or their ideas; not a single law which each triumphant party has not been able to turn against its adversaries. Power once obtained, every illegal party becomes legal, for it is that party which, through its creatures, administers the “law.” We may then conclude that the law being nothing but the will of the strongest, one is obliged to obey it only when too weak to resist it; that nothing really legitimizes it, and that this famous “legality” is only a question of more or less force. So when these rogues oppose the workers with their supreme argument, “legality,” the latter may laugh in their faces and ask if anyone ever came to consult the toilers about the making of those laws. And even if the people should have adhered to these laws for a time, the latter could have no effectiveness except so long as those who accepted them continued to believe them useful, and were willing to conform to them. It would be funny if, under the pretext that at a given moment of our life we had agreed to a certain line of conduct, we were forced to adopt it for the rest of our existence, without being able to modify it, because to do so would be to displease a certain number of persons who, for one cause or another finding profit for themselves in the existing order, would like to crystallize the present condition. But what is more ridiculous still, is to desire to subject us to the laws of past generations, the pretense that we should believe we owe respect and obedience to fancies which it pleased certain nincompoops to codify and set up as laws fifty years ago! The presumption of wanting to enslave the present to the conceptions of the past!

At this point we hear the recriminations of all the makers of laws and those that get their living out of them; they naively fall into line and cry out with the others that society could not exist if there were no longer any laws; that people would be cutting each other’s throats if they had no tutelary authority to keep them in fear and respect of acquired rank and condition. Later we shall see that, in spite of law and coercion, crimes continue to be committed; that the laws are powerless to repress or prevent them, since they are the result of the vicious organization which governs us; and that, consequently, we must not seek to maintain or to modify the laws, but to change the social system.

倘若承认法律可以更改，那便等于认定法律也可能变得倒退；而承认了这一点，也就是承认它就其本性而言可能伤害某些人，因为总有走在自己时代前面的个人。可见法律并不公正；它并不具有人们力图赋予它的那种可敬品性。倘若这部法律损害我的利益或侵犯我的自由，为什么我必须被迫服从它？又是哪一纸永不可易的契约，能为这些滥用辩护？在科学领域，当学者们经过大量研究和辛劳，终于表述出一条所谓的自然规律（natural law）时，那并不是因为一个由自以为高于其余凡人的成员组成的多数或“议院”，曾凭其成员的意志裁定：自然力应遵照这样或那样的演化方式。谁若发此狂言，我们定会当面耻笑这个白痴。一条自然规律之所以被宣布，是因为人们已经发现：某一现象若经产生、某一化合若经实现，乃是凭借某某之力，或某某亲和力（affinity）的存在；既定产生该现象的环境，它便不可能另成样子。既定的力在既定条件下发生作用，产生既定的结果；这是数学般确定的。因此，新发现的规律并不是登台来统辖现象，而是来解释现象的成因。这些规律尽可以被发现、被怀疑、甚至被否认；构成我们地球的种种物质，仍将照样按其性质或亲和力继续化合，地球仍将运转，无需任何强力来保护这一演化过程，也无需惩罚那些想“违犯规律”的人。

在我们这个社会里，情形便不同了。这些法律仿佛生来就是供人违犯的；因为立法者所咨询的，只是自己的个人偏好、他们所代表者的利益，以及他们那个时代道德演化的平均程度，全不顾及将受其支配的人们的性情、趋向与亲和力——何况这也根本不可能，因为个人性情与趋向本来就千差万别。每个等级各有其法律；社会学里也不可能有物理学里那种单一而普适的规律，否则它就难免专断而不适用。事实上，在我们这个社会里，没有一部法律不损害其某些成员——或损其物质利益，或损其观念；没有一部法律不曾被每一个得胜的政党掉转头来打击自己的对手。权力一旦到手，一切非法的政党便都成了合法的，因为正是这个政党在通过它的爪牙执掌“法律”。于是可以断言：法律无非是最强者的意志，人只在弱得无力抵抗时才不得不服从它；没有任何东西真正使它合法化，这著名的“合法性”（legality）不过是强力多寡的问题。所以，当这帮无赖祭出他们的至高论据——“合法性”——来对付工人时，工人尽可当面耻笑他们，并且问一句：可曾有人来征询过劳动者，问他们同不同意制定这些法律？退一步说，即便人民曾一度归附这些法律，它们也只在接受者继续信其有益、并愿意遵从的期间内才有效力。倘若借口我们一生中某个时刻曾赞成过某种行为准则，便强迫我们此后终身奉行、不得更改，只因为更改便要开罪于某些人——这些人出于这样那样的缘故，从现存秩序里找到了自己的好处，巴不得把现状凝固起来——那才滑稽呢。但更荒唐的，还是想把我们置于往代人的法律之下，妄想我们应当相信自己负有敬意与服从的义务——对五十年前某些蠢货一时高兴编纂起来、奉为法律的那些奇想！竟想把现在奴役于过去的种种观念之下，这是何等的狂妄！

话说到这里，我们便听见全体立法者以及靠法律吃饭的人们的一片责难；他们天真地随声附和，跟着旁人一起叫嚷：一旦不再有法律，社会就无法存在；人们若没有一种监护性的权威（tutelary authority）令其畏惧、令其敬重既得的等级与地位，便会互相割断喉咙。往后我们将看到：尽管有法律与强制，罪行仍在不断发生；法律既镇压不了、也防止不了罪行，因为罪行正是统治着我们的那个邪恶组织的产物；因此，我们不该谋求维持或修改法律，而该谋求改变社会制度。

But what makes us still more indignant is that certain persons are audacious enough to set themselves up as judges of others. So long as authority leaned upon its divine source, so long as justice passed for an emanation from God, we can understand that those invested with authority should have believed themselves peculiar beings, endowed by the divine will with a portion of its omnipotence and infallibility, and should have imagined themselves fit to distribute rewards and punishments to the herd of vulgar mortals. But in our century of science and free criticism, when it is recognized that all men are kneaded out of the same dough, subject to the same passions, the same caprices, the same mistakes, today when an agonizing divinity no longer comes to animate with its breath the ever fallible reason of mortals, we ask ourselves how it comes that there are men ignorant, enough, or presumptuous enough, to dare to assume in cold blood and with deliberate intent the terrible responsibility of taking away another man’s life or any portion of his liberty. When, in the most ordinary affairs of daily life we are most of the time unable to succeed in analyzing not only the causes which prompt our immediate neighbors to act but very often the true motives of our own acts, how can anybody have the self-sufficiency to believe himself capable of disentangling the truth in an affair of which he knows neither the beginning, nor the actors, nor the motives which prompted their actions, and which comes before the tribunal only after being magnified, commented upon, distorted by the misrepresentations of those who participated in it in any way whatsoever or, more frequently, have heard of it only through the repetitions of others?

You, who pose as severe and infallible judges of this man who has killed or robbed, do you know the motives which prompted him? Do you know the circumstances of environment, heredity, or even chance, which influenced his mind and led him to commit the act with which you reproach him? You, the implacable men that hurl your anathema against the accused whom public force has brought before your bar, have you ever asked yourselves whether, if placed in the same circumstances and surroundings under which this man acted, you would not have done worse? If, even, you were the impeccable, austere, and stainless men you are supposed to be, you, who with a word pitilessly cut off human life and liberty, you would not dare to utter your decisions if you had thoroughly reflected on human frailty; were you conscious of what you are doing, you would recoil appalled before your task! How could you help being troubled with nightmares! How could your dreams help being peopled with specters of the victims which your pretended justice creates every day! Were it not for that official unconsciousness which stupidity and habit give, you would end by succumbing to the weight of remorse and the haunting of phantoms evoked by your judgments. Our epoch of criticism and positive science no longer admits the principle of distributive justice, nor recognizes the legitimacy of a superior authority rewarding the good and chastising the wicked. Against this ancient doctrine, which the conceptions of the age during one period of humanity’s evolution rendered logical, we promulgate the opposite idea. We no longer see actions as good or bad, except as they are agreeable or disagreeable to us, and in consequence act accordingly. We approve or become enthusiastic, defend or attack, according to the benefit or injury received by our interests, our passions, and our conceptions of the ideal. The common need of solidarity which leads people subjected to the same attacks to unite for their defense is to us the guarantee of a future social order less troubled than our own. We do not judge, but work and struggle; and we believe that universal harmony will result from the free action of all men, when once the suppression of private property no longer permits a handful of persons to enslave their fellows. Hence we cannot admit that, six weeks or six years after an act has been committed, a group of persons supported by armed force should assemble to judge, in the name of some entity or other, and reward or punish the author of the act. That is hypocrisy and cowardice. You reproach a man with having killed, and to teach him that he was wrong you have him killed by the executioner, society’s hired assassin! The executioner and you have not even the excuse of having risked your own necks, since you proceed under cover of an armed force which protects you. We are at war with the ruling caste: recognize, gentlemen of the magistracy, that you are its retainers, and let us alone with your big words and fine phrases. Maintain the privileges whose care is confided to you, use the force which ignorance concedes to you, but leave justice in peace; she has nothing to do with you!

That you might be able to judge appreciatively of the ignominy of your role in beating down others, we would like, O judges, that it might happen to you that, being innocent, you should fall into the clutches of your fellows, to be judged in your turn. In such a situation you might learn what anguish and terror they have had to pass through who have filed before your bar, and whom you have tortured, you, magistrates, as the cat tortures the mouse. With the floods of eloquence from the prosecuting attorney pleading against you rolling about your ears, you might see passing before your eyes the specters of those unfortunates that, during your career, you have immolated upon the altar of social vengeance; you might ask yourselves then, with terror, if they also were not innocent. O yes, we would heartily wish that there might be one among you falsely accused, who should go through the terrors of those that come before your bar. For if, his innocence being one day admitted, he were reinstated in his functions, it is strongly to be presumed that he would reenter his place in the tribunal only to tear his robe and apologize for his criminal life as magistrate, judging haphazard and trafficking in human lives.

但更令我们愤慨的是，竟有人狂妄到自封为他人的裁判官。当权威尚倚傍其神圣来源、当正义尚被视作神的流溢之时，我们尚能理解：那些身任权威之人自以为是特殊的生灵，蒙神意分得了一份全能与无谬，并自命配向庸众凡人分发赏与罚。可是在我们这个科学与自由批评的世纪，在人人公认所有人都由同一块面团揉成、同受同样的情欲、任性、谬误支配的今天，在一位垂死的神明不再以气息吹活凡人那永远易谬的理性的今天——我们要问：世上怎么还会有愚昧到那种地步、或狂妄到那种地步的人，竟敢冷着血、蓄着意，承担起剥夺他人生命、或剥夺其自由之任何一部分的可怖责任？在日常生活最寻常的事务里，我们多半连促动近在身旁之人行动的原因都分析不清，甚至常常连自己行为的真实动机也分析不清；这时候，一个人怎么还能自负到相信自己有能力从一桩案子里理出真相——这案子，他既不知其发端，又不识其当事人，更不明促使当事人行动的种种动机，而且这案子还是被各色参与其事者的夸大、评点与歪曲——更常见的，是被只从旁人转述中听说此事者的层层歪曲——涂抹一番之后，才被搬到法庭上来的？

你们这些摆出严厉而无谬架子的裁判官，来审这个杀了人或劫了财的人——你们可知道促使他动手的动机？你们可知道那些影响了他的头脑、引他犯下你们此刻正责难于他的行为的种种境况——环境的境况、遗传（heredity）的境况、乃至偶然的境况？你们这些毫不容情的人，对着被公共武力押到你们法官席前的被告掷出你们的诅咒，可曾扪心自问：倘若置于此人作案时所处的同样境遇与环境之中，你们未必不会干得更坏？退一步说，即便你们真是人们设想的那种完美无瑕、严峻冷峻、清白无垢的人——你们，这些凭一句话就无情斩断人的生命与自由的人——倘若你们彻底思量过人性的脆弱，也绝不敢宣出你们的裁决；倘若你们意识到自己干的是什么，你们定会惊骇得在这份职守面前却步！你们怎么可能不被噩梦纠缠！你们的梦乡怎么可能不挤满冤魂——你们那冒牌的正义每天都在制造新的冤魂！若不是愚蠢与习惯养出的那种官式浑噩，你们终究会被悔恨的重压、被你们一声声判决召唤来的鬼影的纠缠所压垮。我们这个批评与实证科学的时代，已不再承认分配正义（distributive justice）的原则，也不再承认某种更高权威奖善惩恶的合法性。针对这条古老的学说——在人类演化的某一时期，时代的种种观念曾使它显得合乎逻辑——我们宣示相反的观念。我们不再把行为看作善的或恶的，而只看它们于我们是可喜还是可厌，并据此行事。我们赞许或为之热烈喝彩，捍卫或攻击，全看我们的利益、我们的情欲、我们的理想观念所受的是益还是损。共同的团结（solidarity）需要，促使遭受同样侵害的人们联合起来自卫——在我们看来，这正是一种比我们的社会较少纷扰的未来社会秩序的保证。我们不下裁判，我们劳动、斗争；我们相信，一旦废除私有制（private property），一小撮人再不能奴役其同类，普遍和谐（universal harmony）便会由一切人的自由行动中产生。因此我们不能承认：一桩行为实施了六周或六年之后，一伙有武装力量撑腰的人应当集合起来，以这个或那个实体的名义对它作出裁判，奖赏或惩罚行为人。这是伪善，是怯懦。你们责难一个人杀了人，而为了教他明白他错了，你们却叫刽子手——社会雇来的刺客——把他杀掉！刽子手和你们甚至没有押上自家脖颈作借口，因为你们是在一支保护你们的武装力量掩护下行事的。我们正同统治等级交战：司法当局的先生们，承认吧，你们是它的仆从；收起你们的大话与漂亮辞句，别来纠缠我们。守住那托付给你们照管的特权，动用无知让给你们的强力，但请让正义安宁；她与你们毫无干系！

为了教你们能够真切掂量你们在打压他人时所扮角色的奇耻，哦，裁判官们，我们但愿你们遭逢这样的事：身为无辜，却落入同僚的魔爪，轮到你们受审。处在这种境地，你们或许才能体会：那些曾在你们席前鱼贯而过的人，那些被你们——你们这些司法官——像猫折腾老鼠那样折腾过的人，经历了怎样的痛苦与恐怖。当控诉你们的检察官那滔滔雄辩在你们耳际翻滚时，你们或许会看见那些不幸者的鬼影从眼前掠过——在你们的生涯里，你们把他们宰献在社会复仇的祭坛上；那时，你们或许会心怀恐怖地自问：他们是否也是无辜的。哦，是的，我们由衷希望你们中间有一个蒙冤受诬的人，去亲历站上你们席前的人所经历的种种恐怖。因为，倘若他的无辜终有一日得到承认，他得以官复原职，那么极有可能，他重登法庭席位，将只是为了撕碎自己的法袍，只为向他身为司法官的那段罪恶生涯——凭臆断下判、以人命做交易的生涯——谢罪。
