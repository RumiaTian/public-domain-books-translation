## XXII Anarchism and Its Practicability / XXII 无政府主义及其可实现性

Anarchism and Its Practicability

“Your ideas are all right in theory, but they are not practicable; men need some tangible power to govern them and force them to respect the social contract.” Such is the objection urged against us as a last resort by advocates of the present social order, when after thorough discussion we have answered their arguments and demonstrated that the worker can hope for no sensible improvement of his lot while the machinery of the present social system is preserved. “Your ideas are all right, but they are not practicable; man is not yet sufficiently developed to live in such an ideal state. In order to put them into practice human beings must first have become perfect,” is added by many other persons, undoubtedly sincere, but who misled by education and habit, see only the difficulties and are not yet sufficiently convinced of the principles to work for their realization. And in addition to these avowed adversaries and these indifferentists who may become friends, there rises up a third category of persons more dangerous than declared opponents. These latter pretend to be animated with enthusiasm for our ideas; they loudly assert that nothing can be greater, that the present organization is worthless and must vanish before the new idea, that it is the goal towards which humanity is tending, etc. “But,” they add, “it is not immediately practicable; humanity must be prepared for it, brought to understand this happy condition;” and under this pretext of being practical they seek to revive those reform projects which we have just shown to be illusory. They perpetuate existing prejudices by flattering those to whom they speak, and seek personally to profit as much as possible from the present situation; before long their ideal vanishes to make room for the instinct towards the preservation of the existing order of things. Unfortunately it is but too true that those ideas which are the end and aim of our aspirations are not immediately realizable. The number of persons who have understood them is yet too small a minority to exercise any immediate influence upon events or the course of our social organization. But is that any reason why we should not work for their realization? If one is convinced of the justice of his principles why not try to put them in practice? If everybody were to say, “It is not possible,” and passively accept the yoke of the present society, it is plain that the capitalistic order of things would still have many centuries to run.

If the first thinkers who fought the Church and the monarchy on behalf of natural ideas and independence; who faced the executioner and the scaffold in order to proclaim these, had said “it is not possible,” while dreaming of their ideal, we should, today, still be bound by mystical conceptions and seignorial rights. It is because there have always been people who were not “practical,” but singularly convinced of a truth and seeking to disseminate it, wherever they could, with all their might, that man, today, begins to be familiar with his own origin, and to get rid of his superstitions concerning divine and human authority. In one of the chapters of his really valuable book, *Outlines of a Morality Without Authority or Duty*, M. Guyau develops this admirable idea: “He who does not act as he thinks, thinks incompletely.” Nothing can be truer. When one is thoroughly convinced of an idea, it is impossible for him, feeling it, not to seek to spread it and endeavor to realize it. How often do disputes arise between friends over trivial matters, in which each maintains his own view without any other motive than the conviction that he is in the right of the matter. Yet to please one’s friend, or even to avoid wounding him, it would cost nothing to let him speak his mind without either approving or disapproving; since the thing he maintains is of no real importance to our convictions, why not let him have his way? And this we often do in a conversation concerning things about which we have no fixed opinion; but directly something about which we have an opinion comes up, presto! we take sides and dispute with our best friend in defense of our own opinion. Now, if people act this way about trifles, how much stronger must be the impulse received when it is a question of opinions which have to do with the future of all humanity, the enfranchisement of our class, our posterity, and ourselves!

无政府主义及其可实现性

“你们的思想在理论上都对，可是行不通；人们需要有某种有形的权力来统治他们，强迫他们尊重社会契约。”当我们经过充分讨论、驳倒了对方的论据、并证明只要现存社会制度的整套机器照旧维持，工人便休想对自身境遇有任何切实的改善之后，现存社会秩序的辩护者们便把这样的反对意见当作最后的法宝抛了出来。“你们的思想很好，可是行不通；人类还没有充分发展到能生活在这样一种理想国度之中。要把它们付诸实践，人类必须先变得完美无缺。”许多别的人又这样补充说；这些人无疑是真诚的，只是被教育和习惯引入歧途，只看得见困难，还不足以信服这些原则，因而不肯为其实现效力。除了这些公然的论敌和这些尚可成为朋友的冷漠者之外，还崛起着第三类人，他们比公开的反对者更危险。这后一种人佯装对我们的思想满怀热忱；他们大声宣称没有什么比这更伟大，宣称现今的组织一无是处、必将在新思想面前消亡，宣称它正是人类正在趋赴的目标，等等。“可是，”他们补充道，“它不能立时实行；人类必须先为此做好准备，先被引导着理解这种幸福的境况；”于是，借着这种讲求实际的名义，他们力图复活我们方才证明为虚妄的那些改良方案。他们借奉承听众来使既存的偏见绵延不绝，又极力从当今的局势中为自己捞取尽可能多的好处；不久，他们的理想便烟消云散，让位于保存现存事物秩序的本能。遗憾的是，我们热望所归的这些思想确实不能立时实现，这是千真万确的。理解了这些思想的人，人数还只是太小的少数，不足以对事态或我们社会组织的进程施加任何直接的影响。但这难道就成了我们不肯为其实现效力的理由吗？一个人若确信自己的原则是正义的，为什么不去设法把它们付诸实践？倘若人人都说“这是不可能的”，从而逆来顺受地接受现今社会的枷锁，那么显而易见，资本主义秩序还会再延续许多个世纪。

倘若当年那些为了自然的思想与独立而对抗教会与君主制、面对刽子手与断头台也要把这些思想宣示于人的最初的思想家们，在梦想自己的理想时说过一声“不可能”，那么我们今天想必仍被神秘主义的观念和领主权利束缚着。正因为始终有过这样一些人——他们不讲“实际”，却对一项真理抱有异乎寻常的信念，并随时随地竭尽全力传播它——人类今天才开始熟悉自身的起源，才开始摆脱关于神的权威与人的权威的种种迷信。居友（Guyau）在他那部真正有价值的著作《无权威与无义务的道德概论》（*Outlines of a Morality Without Authority or Duty*）的一章里，阐发了这条可敬佩的箴言：“凡不依其所思而行者，其思必不完全。”再没有比这更真实的了。一个人若彻底信服了一种思想，他既感受到它，便不可能不去传播它、不去力求实现它。朋友之间为鸡毛蒜皮的小事而起争执，何其常见：各执己见，别无他故，只因确信自己在这件事上有理。其实，为讨朋友的欢心，甚至只为不刺伤他，任他畅抒己见而不置可否，本不费分毫；既然他所坚持的事于我们的信念并无真正的干系，何妨听之任之？在谈及我们本无定见的种种事情时，我们也常常正是这样做的；可是话题一转到我们持有见解的事情上，说时迟那时快！我们立刻选边站队，为捍卫自己的见解同挚友争辩起来。人们尚且为琐事如此行事，那么，当问题关乎全人类的未来、关乎我们这个阶级、我们的子孙后代以及我们自身的解放时，所受的推动又该何等强烈！

Truly we understand that not everyone can bring the same amount of resistance to bear in the struggle, the same degree of energy in combating existing institutions. Temperaments and characters are not all moulded alike. The difficulties are so great, poverty so severe, persecutions so multiplied, that we comprehend how there must be degrees in efforts towards the propaganda of what is admitted to be true and just. But acts are always in proportion to the impulse received and the intensity of one’s faith in his beliefs. Very often one may be deterred by considerations of one’s family, one’s relations, or the necessities of earning one’s daily bread; but whatever be the force of these considerations, if one is really a man they will never go so far as to make him swallow all the infamies that spread out before his eyes. There comes a time when one sends considerations to the devil, remembering that he is a man and that he had dreamed of something better than what he has been compelled to submit to. —He who is incapable of making any sacrifice for the principles he claims to profess, does not really believe in them at all; he decorates himself with the label merely for show, because at some time it looked well, or because he pretends to justify certain vices, by the help of these principles: beware of taking him into confidence﻿—he will deceive you.

As to those who seek to profit by existing institutions, ostensibly for the purpose of aiding the propaganda of new ideas, they are ambitious knaves who flatter the future in order to enjoy the present in peace.

It is, then, quite plain that our ideas are not immediately realizable; we do not hesitate to admit it. But they will become so through the energy exerted by those who will understand them. The greater the intensity of the propaganda the nearer the hour of realization. It is not by yielding to existing institutions that we shall do battle with them, nor yet by hiding our light under a bushel. To fight these institutions, to work for the advancement of new ideas we must have energy; this energy can come from nothing but conviction. Those, then, who already have the conviction must find their men and labor to impart it to them.

我们确实明白，并非人人都能在斗争中拿出同等的抵抗力，都能在对抗现存制度时拿出同等的干劲。气质与性格并非千人一面。困难如此之大，贫困如此之深，迫害如此之频，所以我们懂得：在宣传那被公认为真实与正义的事业的努力上，必然有程度之别。但行动总是与所受的推动和信奉的强度成正比。人常常会因顾念家庭、亲属，或因挣得每日面包的需要而却步；但无论这些顾虑力量多大，一个人倘若真是个男子汉，它们决不至于迫使他咽下眼前铺陈开来的种种丑恶。总会有那么一天，他把种种顾虑抛到九霄云外，记起自己是个男子汉，记起自己曾梦想过比被迫屈从的境况更好的东西。——凡不肯为自己宣称信奉的原则作半点牺牲的人，根本就不真信这些原则；他不过是拿这块招牌装点门面，或因它一度看着光鲜，或因他想借这些原则为某些恶习开脱：当心，别对他推心置腹——他必将欺骗你。

至于那些借现存制度营私、表面上却说是为了助益新思想宣传的人，他们是些野心勃勃的无赖，奉承未来，为的是安享当下。

可见，我们的思想确乎不能立时实现；这一点我们毫不迟疑地承认。但它们将随着理解这些思想的人们所施展的干劲而成为现实。宣传愈是热烈，实现的时辰便愈是临近。向现存制度屈从，不能同它们作战；把我们的光藏在斗底下，同样不能。要同这些制度作战，要推动新思想的进展，我们必须有力量；这力量除信念外别无来源。那么，已经怀有信念的人，就必须去寻访自己的同道，并着力把信念传授给他们。

Reforms being inapplicable, as we think we have shown, it would hence be conscious deception to recommend them to the workers. Furthermore we know that the force of circumstances will infallibly drive the workers to a revolution: crises, enforced idleness, the development of machinery, political complications, all conspire to throw the workers upon the street, and compel them to revolt in order to affirm their right to existence. Now, since the revolution is inevitable and all reforms illusory, nothing remains but to prepare for the struggle; that is what we are doing by moving directly towards our object, leaving to the ambitious the business of carving out positions and sinecures for themselves from the misery they pretend they would assuage.

Just here, however, we anticipate an objection: “If you recognize that your ideas are not yet ready to be put in practice,” it will be said, “are you not preaching abnegation to the present generation for the sake of future generations, in asking them to strive for an idea whose immediate realization you cannot guarantee to them?” In nowise do we preach abnegation; we merely refuse to delude ourselves as to the facts, nor are we willing to encourage enthusiasts in deceiving themselves. We take the facts as they are, analyze and set them forth thus:﻿—A class which owns all and is unwilling to give up anything on the one side; on the other side a class which produces all, possesses nothing, and has no other alternative than a cowardly cringing to its exploiters, slavishly waiting for them to throw it a bone to gnaw, having no longer dignity, pride, or any quality which uplifts human character, or else to revolt and imperatively demand what is refused to all its genuflections. For those who think only of their own personality, those who want to enjoy themselves at any price and no matter how, there is nothing pleasant in the alternative. We would advise all such to yield to the exactions of present society, to try to chip out their own little niche, not to look where they plant their feet, not to be afraid of crushing those who hinder them; such people have nothing in common with us. But to those who think they can be really free only when their liberty ceases to trammel the liberty of the weakest of their fellows; to those who cannot be happy until they know that the pleasures in which they delight have not cost some disinherited one his tears, to them we say that there is no abnegation on the part of anyone who recognizes that one must struggle to be free.

We proclaim this material fact, that there can be no enfranchisement of humanity save through the application of our principles; it rests with humanity to decide whether it will free itself completely, at one stroke, or whether there must forever be a privileged minority which will profit by all its progress at the expense of those who are dying of want while producing for others. Shall we be the ones to see the morning shine? Will it be the present generation, or that which follows it or a still later one? We do not know, we do not care; it will be those who will have enough energy and courage in their breasts to want to be free, who will find the way to obtain freedom.

我们自认已经证明改良行不通，既然如此，再向工人们推荐改良，便是有意的欺骗。再者，我们知道情势的力量必将把工人驱向革命：危机、被迫赋闲、机器的发展、政治纠葛，这一切都在合谋把工人抛上街头，迫使他们起而反抗，以申明自己的生存权。既然革命不可避免而一切改良尽属虚妄，那么除了备战这场斗争之外别无他途；我们正是这样做的：径直朝我们的目标进发，而把从他们佯称要纾解的苦难中为自己谋取职位与肥缺的营生，留给那些野心家。

然而就在这里，我们预想到一个反对意见。人们会说：“你们既然承认自己的思想尚未准备好付诸实践，那么，你们要求这一代人为后代诸代去追求一个你们无法向他们保证其立时实现的理想，岂不是在向这一代人宣讲自我克弃吗？”我们绝非宣讲自我克弃；我们只是拒绝在事实面前自欺，也不愿怂恿热心者们自欺。我们把事实如其所是地拿来，加以分析，如此陈述：——一边是一个占有了一切而不肯放弃任何东西的阶级；另一边是一个生产了一切、却一无所有的阶级，它除了下列二者别无出路：要么怯懦地匍匐于剥削者脚下，奴性地等待他们丢一根骨头来啃，把尊严、自豪以及一切足以提振人格的品质丧失净尽；要么起而反抗，斩钉截铁地索要它千跪万拜都讨不到的东西。对于那些只想着一己人格的人、那些不惜任何代价不择手段也要行乐的人，这二者择一并无惬意可言。我们奉劝这些人去屈从现今社会的勒索，去为自己凿出一小块壁龛，走路不必看脚下，压碎挡道的人也不必心惊；这种人与我们毫无共通之处。但对另一些人——他们认为，只有当自己的自由不再妨及最弱小的同伴的自由时，自己才算真正自由；他们要知道自己所沉醉的欢愉并未让某个被剥夺者付出眼泪，才能够感到幸福——我们要对他们说：凡认识到人必须斗争才能获得自由的人，就没有谁是在自我克弃。

我们宣告这一确凿的事实：人类的解放，唯有通过运用我们的诸原则方能实现；究竟是由人类一鼓作气彻底解放自己，还是永远存在一个特权少数，靠牺牲那些为他人生产而死于匮乏者的利益、坐收一切进步之利，这要由人类自己来决定。见到曙光乍现的，会是我们吗？会是这一代人，是紧随其后的一代，还是更晚的一代？我们不知道，也不计较；那必将是胸中怀有足够的精力与勇气、一心想要自由的人——他们自会找到获得自由的道路。
