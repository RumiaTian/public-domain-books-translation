## Preface to the Second Edition / 第二版序
===Original===

The rapid sale of the first edition of this translation has encouraged the Association at whose request it was made to endeavour to make it more widely known to the various English-speaking populations, by printing a larger edition at a lower price. It is hoped, also, that the enlarged circulation of a work so graphic, and written by one who has so thoroughly studied the real aspects of war, as seen by those on the spot, may lead not so much to sentimental emotions and vague protests, as to a businesslike discussion of the means by which the resort to war may be at any rate rendered more and more infrequent. The English Government has lately given repeated and practical proofs of its sincere desire to substitute the peaceful and rational method of arbitration for the rough, cruel, and uncertain decision of force; and the conspicuous success of that method hitherto﻿—though tried under circumstances not altogether favourable﻿—must have prepared thinking men for the question: “Why cannot some scheme for the formation of an International Tribunal of Arbitration be formed and debated among the Powers who, by taking part in the Congress at Paris after the Crimean War, formally admitted the principle, and who have already seen it successfully applied in practice”? To this question, which has been frequently asked, no satisfactory answer has yet been given, nor to the further question why our Government should not introduce the subject to the great Powers, after showing so unmistakably its adherence to the principle. People differ, and, probably, will always differ, as to the light in which they regard war. A very small and rapidly diminishing minority regard it as a good thing in itself﻿—most as an evil which in our present stage of civilisation cannot always be avoided; some as a crime formally prohibited by the moral law and the Christian religion. All of the two latter classes ought to join in any practical steps for diminishing the occasions of war; and of these the one which is most within the scope of politicians is the promotion of International Arbitration. The Association to which I belong has published this work in the confident hope that its circulation will aid in hastening this much-needed reform.
The Translator

===Chinese===

本译本第一版销售迅速，这鼓舞了当初请我翻译的协会，促使其以较低的价格印行更大的一版，努力使它在英语世界的广大人群中更为人知。人们还希望，一部如此生动的作品——出自一位透彻研究过战争真实面貌（那是亲历者眼中的面貌）的作者之手——的推广，与其说引人发出感伤的情绪和空泛的抗议，不如说引出一场务实的讨论：讨论用怎样的办法，才能至少使诉诸战争变得一次比一次稀少。英国政府近来已屡次以实际行动证明，它真诚地希望用和平而理智的仲裁办法，取代武力那粗粝、残忍而无定裁断；这一办法迄今——尽管是在并非全然有利的情势下试行——的显著成功，必定已使思考的人们准备好迎接这样一个问题：「既然各大国在克里米亚战争之后的巴黎和会上已正式承认了仲裁原则，又已见过它在实践中成功运用，为什么不能拟定某种设立国际仲裁法庭的方案，在它们中间进行商讨？」对这个常被问起的问题，至今没有令人满意的回答；对更进一步的问题——为什么我国政府在如此明白地表明拥护这一原则之后，还不把它向各大国提出——也同样没有。人们对战争的看法各不相同，而且多半永远会各不相同：极少数且正在迅速减少的人视战争本身为好事；大多数人视之为在我们当前的文明阶段无法全然避免的恶；有些人则视之为道德律和基督教所明文禁止的罪。后两类人都应当联合起来，采取一切切实的步骤，减少战争的因由；而在政治家力所能及的范围之内，其中一步便是促进国际仲裁。我所属的协会怀着坚定的信心印行这部作品，相信它的流传将有助于加速这项亟需的改革。
译者
