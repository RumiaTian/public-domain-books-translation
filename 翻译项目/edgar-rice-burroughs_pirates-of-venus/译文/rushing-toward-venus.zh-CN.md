## III Rushing Toward Venus / III 飞向金星

===Original===
The psychological effects of an experience such as that through which I had been passing must be considerable, and even though they could be neither weighed nor measured, I was yet conscious of changes that had taken place in me because of them. For thirty days I had been racing alone through space toward absolute annihilation, toward an end that would probably not leave a single nucleus of the atoms that compose me an electron to carry on with, I had experienced the ultimate in solitude, and the result had been to deaden my sensibilities; doubtless a wise provision of nature.
Even the realization that the splendid crescent, looming enormously off the starboard bow of the torpedo, was Venus failed to excite me greatly. What if I were to approach Venus more closely than any other human being of all time! It meant nothing. Were I to see God, himself, even that would mean nothing. It became apparent that the value of what we see is measurable only by the size of our prospective audience. Whatever I saw, who might never have an audience, was without value.

===Chinese===
我所经历的这番体验，其心理影响想必相当可观；纵使无法称量、无从测度，我仍觉察到它在我身上引发的种种变化。整整三十天，我独自一人在太空中疾驰，奔向彻底的湮灭——奔向一个很可能连构成我躯体的原子核都不剩一个电子去延续的结局。我尝到了孤独的极致，其结果便是我的感官变得迟钝；这无疑是大自然一项明智的安排。
就连那轮壮丽的新月——它巨大无比地悬在鱼雷右舷前方——我意识到那是金星，也未能令我有多大兴奋。纵使我即将比有史以来任何人都更贴近金星，又当如何！那毫无意义。就算我亲眼见到上帝他老人家，那也毫无意义。事情变得很明显：我们所见之物的价值，唯有以我们预期听众的规模来衡量。无论我看见什么，而我这个人也许永远不会有一个听众，那都是没有价值的。

===Original===
Nevertheless, more to pass away the time than because I was particularly interested in the subject, I began to make some rough calculations. These indicated that I was about eight hundred sixty-five thousand miles from the orbit of Venus and that I should cross it in about twenty-four hours. I could not, however, compute my present distance from the planet accurately. I only knew that it appeared very close. When I say close, I mean relatively. The earth was some twenty-five million miles away, the Sun about sixty-eight million, so that an object as large as Venus, at a distance of one or two million miles, appeared close.
As Venus travels in her orbit at the rate of nearly twenty-two miles per second, or over one million six hundred thousand miles in a terrestrial day, it appeared evident to me that she would cross my path some time within the next twenty-four hours.
It occurred to me that, passing closely, as was unavoidable, she might deflect the course of the torpedo and save me from the Sun; but I knew this to be a vain hope. Undoubtedly, the path of the torpedo would be bent, but the Sun would not relinquish his prey. With these thoughts, my apathy returned, and I lost interest in Venus.

===Chinese===
尽管如此，与其说是对这题目特别上心，不如说是为了打发时间，我动手做了些粗略的计算。结果表明：我距金星轨道约八十六万五千英里，大约二十四小时后便会越过它。然而，我无法精确算出此刻与行星本体的距离。我只知道它看上去很近。我说近，是相对而言。地球远在大约两千五百万英里之外，太阳约六千八百万英里，因而在一两百万英里之外一个金星那般大小的天体，便显得近了。
金星沿其轨道运行的速度将近每秒二十二英里，也就是地球一昼夜要走过一百六十多万英里；由此看来，她显然会在未来二十四小时之内的某一刻横越我的航线。
我转念一想：既然不可避免地要从她近旁掠过，她或许能使鱼雷偏转航向，把我从太阳手里救下；可我明白这只是徒然之望。鱼雷的路径无疑会被拽弯，但太阳绝不会松开他的猎物。想到这里，我的漠然重又涌上心头，对金星也失了兴致。

===Original===
Selecting a book, I lay down on my bed to read. The interior of the cabin was brightly illuminated. I am extravagant with electricity. I have the means of generating it for eleven more months; but I shall not need it after a few weeks, so why should I be parsimonious?
I read for a few hours, but as reading in bed always makes me sleepy, I eventually succumbed. When I awoke, I lay for a few minutes in luxurious ease. I might be racing toward extinction at the rate of thirty-six thousand miles an hour, but I, myself, was unhurried. I recalled the beautiful spectacle that Venus had presented when I had last observed her and decided to have another look at her. Stretching languorously, I arose and stepped to one of the starboard portholes.

===Chinese===
我挑了一本书，躺到床上翻看。舱内灯火通明。我在用电上头是挥霍无度的。我还有办法继续发电十一个月；可再过几个星期我就用不着它了，又何必小气？
我读了几小时，可卧床读书总让我犯困，我终究还是败给了睡意。醒来时，我慵懒惬意地躺了几分钟。我也许正以每小时三万六千英里的速度奔向灭亡，可我本人却从容不迫。我回想起上一次观测时金星呈现的那番美景，决意再去看她一眼。我懒洋洋地伸了个懒腰，起身走到右舷的一扇舷窗前。

===Original===
The picture framed by the casing of that circular opening was gorgeous beyond description. Apparently less than half as far away as before, and twice as large, loomed the mass of Venus outlined by an aureole of light where the Sun, behind her, illuminated her cloudy envelope and lighted to burning brilliance a thin crescent along the edge nearest me.
I looked at my watch. Twelve hours had passed since I first discovered the planet, and now, at last, I became excited. Venus was apparently half as far away as it had been twelve hours ago, and I knew that the torpedo had covered half the distance that had separated us from her orbit at that time. A collision was possible, it even seemed within the range of probability that I should be dashed to the surface of this inhospitable, lifeless world.
Well, what of it? Am I not already doomed? What difference can it make to me if the end comes a few weeks sooner than I had anticipated? Yet I was excited. I cannot say that I felt fear. I have no fear of death﻿—that left me when my mother died; but now that the great adventure loomed so close I was overwhelmed by contemplation of it and the great wonder that it induced. What would follow?

===Chinese===
那圆形窗框所框住的一幅图景，华美得难以言表。金星那巨大的星体赫然可见——显然不到原先一半的距离，个头却大了一倍——其轮廓镶着一圈光环，那是身后的太阳照亮了她云雾似的外衣，又把她朝我这一侧边缘上一弯薄薄的新月点燃成灼目的辉煌。
我看了看表。自我初次发现这颗行星起，已过去了十二个小时，如今，我终于兴奋起来。金星显然只及十二小时前一半的距离，我由此可知鱼雷已驶完那时我与她轨道之间相隔路程的一半。碰撞是可能的，甚至看来很可能——我将被摔上这个不宜居、无生机的世界的表面。
那么，又当如何？我岂非早已在劫难逃？结局比我所预期的早来几个星期，于我又有何分别？然而我确实兴奋。我不能说自己感到了恐惧。我不怕死——那份恐惧在母亲去世时便离我而去了；可如今这伟大的冒险已迫在眉睫，我竟被对它的冥想、被它所唤起的巨大惊奇压倒了。接下来会发生什么？

===Original===
The long hours dragged on. It seemed incredible to me, accustomed though I am to thinking in units of terrific speed, that the torpedo and Venus were racing toward the same point in her orbit at such inconceivable velocities, the one at the rate of thirty-six thousand miles per hour, the other at over sixty-seven thousand.
It was now becoming difficult to view the planet through the side port, as she moved steadily closer and closer to our path. I went to the periscope﻿—she was gliding majestically within its range. I knew that at that moment the torpedo was less than thirty-six thousand miles, less than an hour, from the path of the planet’s orbit, and there could be no doubt now but that she had already seized us in her grasp. We were destined to make a clean hit. Even under the circumstances I could not restrain a smile at the thought of the marksmanship that this fact revealed. I had aimed at Mars and was about to hit Venus; unquestionably the all-time cosmic record for poor shots.

===Chinese===
漫长的时辰缓缓流逝。尽管我惯于以惊人的速度为单位来思考，鱼雷与金星正以如此不可思议的速度朝着她轨道上的同一点竞速飞驰——前者时速三万六千英里，后者逾六万七千英里——这于我仍像是难以置信的事。
如今要从侧舷窗观察这颗行星已越来越难，因为她正稳步地越来越贴近我们的航线。我走向潜望镜——她正庄严地滑入镜中视野。我明白此刻鱼雷距行星轨道已不足三万六千英里，不足一小时之程，且毫无疑问，她已把我们攥在了掌中。我们注定要正中目标。即便在此情形下，一想到这桩事实所揭示的枪法，我仍不禁莞尔。我瞄准的是火星，却即将命中金星；这无疑是亘古宇宙以来最糟糕的一枪。

===Original===
Even though I did not shrink from death, even though the world’s best astronomers have assured us that Venus must be unfitted to support human life, that where her surface is not unutterably hot it is unutterably cold, even though she be oxygenless, as they aver, yet the urge to live that is born with each of us compelled me to make the same preparations to land that I should have had I successfully reached my original goal, Mars.
Slipping into a fleece-lined suit of coveralls, I donned goggles and a fleece-lined helmet; then I adjusted the oxygen tank that was designed to hang in front of me, lest it foul the parachute, and which can be automatically jettisoned in the event that I reach an atmosphere that will support life, for it would be an awkward and dangerous appendage to be cumbered with while landing. Finally, I adjusted my chute.
I glanced at my watch. If my calculations have been correct, we should strike in about fifteen minutes. Once more I returned to the periscope.

===Chinese===
纵使我不畏惧死亡，纵使世上最优秀的天文学家们都断言金星必不适宜人类生存，她的表面非极度炎热即极度苦寒，纵使如他们所言她全无氧气，可那种与生俱来的求生本能仍驱使我做出与当初顺利抵达原定目标火星时同样的着陆准备。
我套上一身内有绒衬的连体工装，戴上护目镜和绒衬头盔；接着调好那只氧气罐——它设计成挂在我身前的样式，免得缠住降落伞，一旦我进入可供呼吸的大气层，它便能被自动抛掉，因为带着这么个累赘着陆既笨拙又危险。最后，我整理好降落伞。
我瞥了一眼表。倘若我的计算无误，我们大约十五分钟后撞击。我再度回到潜望镜前。

===Original===
The sight that met my eyes was awe inspiring. We were plunging toward a billowing mass of black clouds. It was like chaos on the dawn of creation. The gravitation of the planet had seized us. The floor of the cabin was no longer beneath me﻿—I was standing on the forward bulkhead now; but this condition I had anticipated when I designed the torpedo. We were diving nose on toward the planet. In space there had been neither up nor down, but now there was a very definite down.
From where I stood I could reach the controls, and beside me was the door in the side of the torpedo. I released three batteries of parachutes and opened the door in the wall of the inner torpedo. There was a noticeable jar, as though the parachutes had opened and temporarily checked the speed of the torpedo. This must mean that I had entered an atmosphere of some description and that there was not a second to waste.

===Chinese===
映入眼帘的景象令人敬畏。我们正朝着一团翻涌的黑色云海俯冲而下。那宛如创世之初的混沌。行星的引力已攫住了我们。舱底不再在我脚下——此刻我站在前舱壁上；但这种情形，我在设计鱼雷时便已预料到了。我们正箭头朝下向行星俯冲。在太空中本无上下之分，可如今却有了非常明确的「下」。
从我站着的位置能够得着操纵装置，身旁便是鱼雷侧面的舱门。我放开三组降落伞，又打开了内层鱼雷壁上的门。传来一阵明显的震动，仿佛降落伞已经张开，暂时拽住了鱼雷的速度。这必然意味着我已进入某种大气层，一刻也耽搁不得。

===Original===
With a single movement of a lever I loosed the remaining parachutes; then I turned to the outer door. Its bolts were controlled by a large wheel set in the center of the door and were geared to open quickly and with ease. I adjusted the mouthpiece of the oxygen line across my lips and quickly spun the wheel.
Simultaneously the door flew open and the air pressure within the torpedo shot me out into space. My right hand grasped the rip cord of my chute; but I waited. I looked about for the torpedo. It was racing almost parallel with me, all its parachutes distended above it. Just an instant’s glimpse I had of it, and then it dove into the cloud mass and was lost to view; but what a weirdly magnificent spectacle it had presented in that brief instant!

===Chinese===
我把操纵杆一推，放出了剩余的降落伞；随即转向外层舱门。门上的插销由门中央一个大轮盘控制，传动机构使其能迅速而省力地开启。我把输氧管衔在唇间，迅速旋动轮盘。
门应声弹开，鱼雷内的气压把我喷射到了太空之中。我右手紧攥着降落伞的开伞索；可我等着。我四下张望寻找鱼雷。它几乎与我平行疾驰，所有的降落伞在它上方撑得满满的。我只瞥见了它一瞬，它便一头扎进云团，消失不见；可就在那一刹那，它呈现了一幅何等奇诡壮丽的景象！

===Original===
Safe now from any danger of fouling with the torpedo, I jerked the rip cord of my parachute just as the clouds swallowed me. Through my fleece-lined suit I felt the bitter cold; like a dash of ice water the cold clouds slapped me in the face; then, to my relief, the chute opened, and I fell more slowly.
Down, down, down I dropped. I could not even guess the duration, nor the distance. It was very dark and very wet, like sinking into the depths of the ocean without feeling the pressure of the water. My thoughts during those long moments were such as to baffle description. Perhaps the oxygen made me a little drunk; I do not know. I felt exhilarated and intensely eager to solve the great mystery beneath me. The thought that I was about to die did not concern me so much as what I might see before I died. I was about to land on Venus﻿—the first human being in all the world to see the face of the veiled planet.

===Chinese===
如今已无与鱼雷缠绕之虞，我赶在被云团吞没的一瞬猛拉开伞索。隔着绒衬的工装，我感到刺骨的严寒；冰冷的云雾像一泼冰水般抽在我脸上；继而，令我如释重负的是，降落伞张开了，我下落得慢了些。
我坠落、坠落、坠落。我连时间多久、距离多远都无从揣测。四周漆黑而潮湿，宛如沉入海洋深处却感受不到水的压力。在那漫长的时刻里，我心头的思绪难以言表。也许是氧气让我有些微醺；我不晓得。我感到一阵振奋，急切地想揭开身下那个巨大奥秘。我即将死去这一念头，倒不如我临死前可能看见的景象更叫我挂心。我即将踏上金星——全世界有史以来第一个得见这颗蒙面行星真容的人。

===Original===
Suddenly I emerged into a cloudless space; but far below me were what appeared in the darkness to be more clouds, recalling to my mind the often advanced theory of the two cloud envelopes of Venus. As I descended, the temperature rose gradually, but it was still cold.
As I entered the second cloud bank, there was a very noticeable rise in temperature the farther I fell. I shut off the oxygen supply and tried breathing through my nose. By inhaling deeply I discovered that I could take in sufficient oxygen to support life, and an astronomical theory was shattered. Hope flared within me like a beacon on a fog-hid landing field.

===Chinese===
忽然我钻入一片无云的空间；可在我身下远处，黑暗中显出的仿佛又是云层，这让我想起常被人提起的「金星双云层」理论。我继续下降，气温缓缓上升，可仍带着寒意。
进入第二层云带之后，我落得越深，气温的上升就越发明显。我关掉氧气，试着用鼻子呼吸。我发觉只要深深吸气，便能吸入足以维持生命的氧气——一项天文学理论就此被打破。希望在我心中燃起，宛如浓雾笼罩的机场上一盏灯塔。

===Original===
As I floated gently downward, I presently became aware of a faint luminosity far below. What could it be? There were many obvious reasons why it could not be sunlight; sunlight would not come from below, and, furthermore, it was night on this hemisphere of the planet. Naturally many weird conjectures raced through my mind. I wondered if this could be the light from an incandescent world, but immediately discarded that explanation as erroneous, knowing that the heat from an incandescent world would long since have consumed me. Then it occurred to me that it might be refracted light from that portion of the cloud envelope illuminated by the Sun, yet if such were the case, it seemed obvious that the clouds about me should be luminous, which they were not.

===Chinese===
我悠悠地往下飘，不多时便察觉身下远处有一抹微光。那会是什么？有许多明显的理由说明它不可能是阳光：阳光不会来自下方；况且在这颗行星的此半球此刻正是黑夜。自然，种种离奇的猜测掠过我心头。我猜想这会不会是一个白热世界所发的光，可旋即否定了这个解释——因为一个白热世界的热度早就把我烤成灰了。继而我想到，它或许是云层外衣中被太阳照亮的那部分折射下来的光；可倘若如此，我身边的云团理应也是明亮的，然而它们并不。

===Original===
There seemed only one practical solution. It was the solution that an earth man would naturally arrive at. Being what I am, a highly civilized creature from a world already far advanced by science and invention, I attributed the source of this light to these twin forces of superior intelligence. I could only account for that faint glow by attributing it to the reflection upon the under side of the cloud mass of artificial light produced by intelligent creatures upon the surface of this world toward which I was slowly settling.
I wondered what these beings would be like, and if my excitement grew as I anticipated the wonders that were soon to be revealed to my eyes, I believe that it was a pardonable excitement, under the circumstances. Upon the threshold of such an adventure who would not have been moved to excitement by contemplation of the experiences awaiting him?

===Chinese===
看来只有一个说得通的解释。那是一个地球人自然而然会得出的解释。身为我自己这样一个人——一个来自已被科学与发明推向高度文明的世界的、高度文明的生物——我把这光源归于优越智力的这两股孪生之力。那抹微光，我只能这样来解释：在我缓缓降落下去的这个世界表面上，有智慧的生灵制造了人造光，那光反射到云团的底面。
我暗自揣测这些生灵会是何模样，而当我预期着即将展现在眼前的种种奇景时，我的兴奋若是有增无减，我相信在此情形下那是情有可原的。站在这样一场冒险的门槛上，谁不会因思量着等着自己的种种经历而心潮澎湃呢？

===Original===
Now I removed the mouthpiece of the oxygen tube entirely and found that I could breathe easily. The light beneath me was increasing gradually. About me I thought I saw vague, dark shapes among the cloud masses. Shadows, perhaps, but of what? I detached the oxygen tank and let it fall. I distinctly heard it strike something an instant after I had released it. Then a shadow loomed darkly beneath me, and an instant later my feet struck something that gave beneath them.
I dropped into a mass of foliage and grasped wildly for support. A moment later I began to fall more rapidly and guessed what had happened; the parachute had been up-tilted by contact with the foliage. I clutched at leaves and branches, fruitlessly, and then I was brought to a sudden stop; evidently the chute had fouled something. I hoped that it would hold until I found a secure resting place.

===Chinese===
这时我索性把输氧管衔嘴整个拿开，发觉自己能够自如呼吸。身下的光一点一点亮起来。我似乎看见云团之间有模糊的黑色形体。大概是影子吧，可是什么的影子？我解下氧气罐，任它坠落。我清楚地听见它在脱手后一瞬撞上了什么东西。接着一团黑影在我身下赫然现出，转眼之间我的双脚便踩上了某个在脚下让了一让的东西。
我一头扎进一团枝叶之中，拼命伸手抓握支撑。片刻之后我开始坠落得更快，猜到了是怎么回事：降落伞被枝叶顶得翘了起来。我胡乱抓扯叶子和枝桠，徒劳无功，接着我骤然被拉住；显然是伞缠住了什么东西。我盼望它能撑住，直到我寻得一个安稳的落脚处。

===Original===
As I groped about in the dark, my hand finally located a sturdy branch, and a moment later I was astride it, my back to the bole of a large tree﻿—another theory gone the ignoble path of countless predecessors; it was evident that there was vegetation on Venus. At least there was one tree; I could vouch for that, as I was sitting in it, and doubtless the black shadows I had passed were other, taller trees.
Having found secure lodgment, I divested myself of my parachute after salvaging some of its ropes and the straps from the harness, which I thought I might find helpful in descending the tree. Starting at the top of a tree, in darkness and among clouds, one may not be positive what the tree is like nearer the ground. I also removed my goggles. Then I commenced to descend. The girth of the tree was enormous, but the branches grew sufficiently close together to permit me to find safe footing.

===Chinese===
我在黑暗中摸索，手终于摸到一根粗壮的枝桠，转眼我便跨坐其上，背靠着大树的树干——又一项理论步上了无数前驱者的不体面之路：金星上显然有植被。至少有一棵树；这一点我可以担保，因为我正坐在它身上，而方才我掠过的那些黑影，无疑是一棵棵更高的树。
