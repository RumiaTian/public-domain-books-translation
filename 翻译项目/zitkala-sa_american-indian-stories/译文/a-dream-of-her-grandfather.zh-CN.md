## A Dream of Her Grandfather / 梦见祖父

===Original===
Her grandfather was a Dakota "medicine man." Among the Indians of his day he was widely known for his successful healing work. He was one of the leading men of the tribe and came to Washington, DC, with one of the first delegations relative to affairs concerning the Indian people and the United States government.

His was the first band of the Great Sioux Nation to make treaties with the government in the hope of bringing about an amicable arrangement between the red and white Americans. The journey to the nation's capital was made almost entirely on pony-back, there being no railroads, and the Sioux delegation was beset with many hardships on the trail. His visit to Washington, in behalf of peace among men, proved to be his last earthly mission. From a sudden illness, he died and was buried here.

===Chinese===
她的祖父是一位达科他（Dakota）「巫医」（medicine man）。在他那个年代的印第安人中间，他因治病屡屡见效而名闻遐迩。他是部落里的头面人物之一，曾随最早的一批代表团前往华盛顿（Washington, DC），商谈印第安民众与美国政府之间的事务。

他的部落是大苏族（Great Sioux Nation）中最早与政府缔结条约的一支，盼着在美国的红种人与白种人之间达成一项和睦的安排。当时还没有铁路，去国都的这一路几乎全程骑着小马，苏族代表团沿途饱尝艰辛。他这趟为人类和平奔走的华盛顿之行，竟成了他在人世间的最后一项使命。他因突患急病去世，就安葬在那里。

===Original===
When his small granddaughter grew up she learned the white man's tongue, and followed in the footsteps of her grandfather to the very seat of government to carry on his humanitarian work. Though her days were filled with problems for welfare work among her people, she had a strange dream one night during her stay in Washington. The dream was this: Returning from an afternoon out, she found a large cedar chest had been delivered to her home in her absence. She sniffed the sweet perfume of the red wood, which reminded her of the breath of the forest﻿—and admired the box so neatly made, without trimmings. It looked so clean, strong and durable in its native genuineness. With elation, she took the tag in her hand and read her name aloud. "Who sent me this cedar chest?" she asked, and was told it came from her grandfather.

Wondering what gift it could be her grandfather wished now to confer upon her, wholly disregarding his death years ago, she was all eagerness to open the mystery chest.

She remembered her childhood days and the stories she loved to hear about the unusual powers of her grandfather﻿—recalled how she, the wee girl, had coveted the medicine bags, beaded and embroidered in porcupine quills, in symbols designed by the great "medicine man," her grandfather. Well did she remember her merited rebuke that such things were never made for relics. Treasures came in due time to those ready to receive them.

===Chinese===
待他的小孙女长大成人，她学会了白人的语言，追随祖父的足迹来到政府所在地，继续他的人道事业。她在华盛顿逗留期间，白天尽管满是为族民福利事业操劳的种种难题，一天夜里却做了一个奇怪的梦。梦是这样的：午后外出归来，她发现一只大雪松木箱趁她不在家时送到了门上。红雪松木的甜香扑鼻，让她想起森林的气息——箱子做工齐整、不施雕饰，令人赞叹，朴素本真之中显得那么洁净、坚固、耐久。她兴冲冲拿起标签，大声念出自己的名字。「是谁给我送来这只雪松木箱？」她问道，得到的回答是：它来自她的祖父。

祖父多年前已经去世，如今却仿佛全然不顾这一层，还要赐给她一件礼物——她一面猜想那会是什么，一面迫不及待地要打开这只神秘的木箱。

她想起自己的童年，想起那些她最爱听的、关于祖父非凡神力的故事——想起自己这个小小的女娃，当年如何眼馋那些药袋（medicine bag）：缀着珠串、用豪猪刺（porcupine quill）绣出纹样，图样出自伟大的「巫医」——她的祖父——之手。她也分明记得自己挨的那顿应得的训斥：这样的物件，从来就不是做来当遗物收藏的。宝物自会在恰当的时机，降临到有备而来的人手中。

===Original===
In great expectancy, she lifted the heavy lid of the cedar chest. "Oh!" she exclaimed, with a note of disappointment, seeing no beaded Indian regalia or trinkets. "Why does my grandfather send such a light gift in a heavy, large box?" She was mystified and much perplexed.

The gift was a fantastic thing, of texture far more delicate than a spider's filmy web. It was a vision! A picture of an Indian camp, not painted on canvas nor yet written. It was dream-stuff, suspended in the thin air, filling the enclosure of the cedar wood container. As she looked upon it, the picture grew more and more real, exceeding the proportions of the chest. It was all so illusive a breath might have blown it away; yet there it was, real as life﻿—a circular camp of white cone-shaped teepees, astir with Indian people. The village crier, with flowing headdress of eagle plumes, mounted on a prancing white pony, rode within the arena. Indian men, women and children stopped in groups and clusters, while bright painted faces peered out of teepee doors, to listen to the chieftain's crier.

===Chinese===
她满怀期待地掀开雪松木箱沉重的箱盖。「哦！」她惊叹一声，带着几分失望——里面既没有缀珠的印第安盛装，也没有什么小饰品。「祖父为什么用一只又大又沉的箱子，装这么轻的一份礼物？」她大惑不解，满心困惑。

这件礼物是个奇妙的东西，质地比蜘蛛的薄纱网还要纤巧得多。那是一个幻象！一幅印第安营地的图景，不是画在画布上，也不是用文字写成。那是梦的材料，悬在稀薄的空气里，满满充盈着雪松木箱内的整个空间。她凝望之下，那画面越来越真切，渐渐超出木箱的轮廓。它是那样虚幻，一口气就能吹散；可它就在那里，栩栩如生——一座环形营地，白色的圆锥形梯皮帐篷（teepee）环列四周，印第安人来来往往，一片喧腾。传令人（village crier）头戴飘垂的鹰羽冠，骑着一匹腾跃的白马，在营地当中驰骋。印第安男人、女人和孩子们三五成群地停下脚步，涂着鲜亮彩绘的面孔从梯皮帐篷门口探出来，聆听首领的传令人宣讲。

===Original===
At this point, she, too, heard the full melodious voice. She heard distinctly the Dakota words he proclaimed to the people. "Be glad! Rejoice! Look up, and see the new day dawning! Help is near! Hear me, everyone."

She caught the glad tidings and was thrilled with new hope for her people.

===Chinese===
这时，她也听见了那浑厚悦耳的嗓音。她清楚地听见他向众人宣讲的达科他语：「高兴起来吧！欢欣吧！抬起头，看新的一天正在破晓！援手已近！大家听我说。」

她接住这喜讯，为自己的族民涌起新的希望，心潮激荡。
