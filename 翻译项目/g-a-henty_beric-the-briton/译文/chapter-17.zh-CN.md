## XVII / 十七
===Original===

Betrothal

Nero had, within a short time of Beric’s establishment in the palace, spoken to him of his apprehension of the increasing power of the party who, having reverted to the opinions of the Stoic philosophers, were ever denouncing the luxury and extravagance of modern ways, and endeavouring, both by example and precept, to reintroduce the simplicity and severity of former times.

“All this,” Nero said angrily, “is of course but a cloak under which to attack me. Piso and Plautus, Seneca and Lucan, do but assume this severity of manners. They have plotted and intrigued against me. I shall never be safe while they live.”

“Caesar,” Beric said gravely, “I am but a soldier, but born a free Briton and a chief. I cannot sell my service, but must give it loyally and heartily. You honour me with your favour and confidence; I believe that I am worthy of it. I do not serve you for money. Already I have begged you not to heap presents upon me. Wealth would be useless to me did I desire it. Not only have you offered to bestow estates upon me, but I have learned already that there are many others who, seeing that I am favoured by you, would purchase my friendship or my advocacy by large sums. I should despise myself if I cared for money. You would, I know honour me not only with your trust that I can be relied upon to do my duty as your guard, but by treating me as one in your confidence in other matters. At the risk, then, of exciting your displeasure and forfeiting your favour, I must again pray you not to burden me with state matters. Of these I know nothing, and wish to know nothing. Save that of Seneca, I scarce know the names of the others of whom you have spoken. I am wholly ignorant of the intrigues of court life, and I seek to know nothing of them, and am therefore in no position to give any opinion on these matters; and did I speak from only partial knowledge I should do these men great wrong.

“In the next place, Caesar, I am not one who has a double face, and if you ask my opinion of a matter in which I thought that others had ill advised you, I should frankly say that I thought you were wrong; and the truth is never palatable to the great. I try, therefore, to shut my ears to everything that is going on around me, for did I take note of rumours my loyalty to you might be shaken.”

订婚

贝里克进宫不久，尼禄便向他吐露过心病：如今有一党人重新尊奉斯多噶哲人的道理，日日指斥时下的奢靡，想以身教言教重振古昔的简朴克己，其势日张。

“这一切，”尼禄怒道，“当然不过是攻讦我的幌子。皮索、普劳图斯、塞内加、卢卡努斯，那一脸的道学全是装的。他们阴谋算计我。这班人不死，我一日不得安生。”

“恺撒，”贝里克正色说，“我一介武夫，却是生而自由的不列颠人、是个首领。我的效命卖不得钱，只能诚心诚意地给。您以恩宠信义待我，我自信当得起。我侍奉您，不为钱财；早求过您，赏赐不要再堆给我。财富于我无用，我纵想要也无用。您不但许过我田产；我还知道，许多人见我得您青眼，要出重金买我的交情、买我的进言。我在乎钱，就自己看轻自己。我知道，您敬我的，不止是我护卫尽职这一层信义，还有把我当心腹之意。所以，哪怕触怒失宠，我仍要求您：国务，莫再相累。那些我一无所知，也不想知。方才那几位，除了塞内加，我连名字都叫不全。宫廷的阴谋我全然不懂，也不想懂，因此没有置喙的资格；凭一知半解说长道短，反倒冤屈了人家。

“再者，恺撒，我不是两面人。您若问到我以为旁人进言进错了的事，我会直说您错了；而真话，从来不入贵人之耳。所以我尽力塞住耳朵不听四下的事——风声听多了，我对您的忠心怕要动摇。”

===Original===

“Perhaps you are right,” Nero said, after a long pause. “But tell me, once and for all, what you do think on general matters. It is good to have the opinion of one whom I know to be honest.”

“On one subject only are my convictions strong, Caesar. I think that the terrible persecution of the Christians is in itself horrible, and contrary to all the traditions of Rome. These are harmless people. They make no disturbances; they do injury to no one; they are guilty of no act that would justify in any way the tortures inflicted upon them. I am not a Christian, I know nothing of their doctrines; but I am unable to understand how one naturally clement and kind hearted as you are can give way to the clamour of the populace against these people. As to those of whom you speak, and others, I have no opinions; but were I Caesar, strong in the support of the Praetorian guards, and in the affection of the people at large, I would simply despise plotters. The people may vaguely admire the doctrines of the Stoics, but they themselves love pleasure and amusements and spectacles, and live upon your bounty and generosity. There can then be nothing to fear from open force. Should there be conspirators who would attempt to compass their ends by assassination, you have your guards to protect you. You have myself and my little band of countrymen ready to watch over you unceasingly.”

“No care and caution will avail against the knife of the assassin,” Nero said gloomily. “It is only by striking down conspirators and assassins that one can guard one’s self against their weapons. Julius Caesar was killed when surrounded by men whom he deemed his friends.”

===Chinese===

“也许你说得对，”半晌，尼禄说。“可告诉我——就这一回，总论大事，你到底是什么见识。听听一个我明知诚实的人的见解，有好处。”

“只有一件事，恺撒，我的主意拿得死。我以为，那样酷烈地迫害基督徒，本身就是骇人的，且违了罗马全部的传统。这些人无害：不滋事，不伤人，没有一行当得起加给他们的那些酷刑。我不是基督徒，他们的教义我一无所知；可我不明白，您这样天生宽仁心软的人，怎会顺从民众的叫嚣来对付这些人。至于方才说的那几位等等，我没有见解；可我若做了恺撒——有禁卫军撑腰，有万民爱戴——阴谋家，一笑置之罢了。斯多噶的道理，民众不过远远慕其名；他们爱的是行乐、是热闹、是场面，靠您的慷慨活着。明枪，无可惧。真有图谋行刺的，自有护卫；还有我，同我那一小队族人，昼夜不眨眼地守着您。”

“防得再严，防不住刺客的刀，”尼禄阴郁地说。“只有先发制人、砍倒阴谋家，才防得住他们的凶器。尤利乌斯·恺撒，就是死在他当作朋友的那些人围拢之中。”

===Original===

Beric could not deny the truth of Nero’s words. “That is true, Caesar, and therefore I do not presume to criticise or even to have an opinion upon acts of state policy. These are matters utterly beyond me. I know nothing of the history of the families of Rome. I know not who may, with or without reason, deem that they have cause of complaint against you, or who may be hostile to you either from private grievances or personal ambitions, and knowing nothing I wish to know nothing. I desire, as I said when you first spoke to me, to be regarded as a watchdog, to be attached to you by personal kindness, and to guard you night and day against conspirators and assassins. I beseech you not to expect more from me, or to deem it possible that a Briton can be qualified to give any opinion whatever as to a matter so alien to him as the intrigues and conspiracies of an imperial city. Did I agree with you, you would soon doubt my honesty; did I differ from you, I should incur your displeasure.”

Nero looked up at the frank countenance of the young Briton.

“Enough,” he said smiling, “you shall be my watchdog and nothing more.”

As time went on Nero’s confidence in his British guard steadily increased. He had his spies, and knew how entirely Beric kept himself aloof from intimate acquaintanceship with any save the family of Norbanus, and learned, too, that he had refused many large bribes from suitors. For a time, although he knew it not, Beric was constantly watched. His footsteps were followed when he went abroad, his conversations with others in the baths, which formed the great centres of meeting, and stood to the Romans in the place of modern clubs, were listened to and noted. It was observed that he seldom went to convivial gatherings, and that at any place when the conversation turned on public affairs he speedily withdrew; that he avoided all display of wealth, dressed as quietly as it was possible for one in the court circle to do, and bore himself as simply as when he had been training in the ludus of Scopus. There he still went very frequently, practising constantly in arms with his former companions, preferring this to the more formal exercises of the gymnasium. Thus, after a time, Nero became confirmed in his opinion of Beric’s straightforward honesty, and felt that there was no fear of his being tampered with by his enemies.

===Chinese===

尼禄这话，贝里克驳不得。“是实情，恺撒；所以我不敢妄议国政，连见解也不敢有。那些事全然在我之外。罗马各家的历史恩怨我一概不知：谁有真委屈、谁无理取闹，谁怀私怨、谁存野心，我一概不知；既不知，便不想知。初蒙垂询时我便求过：把我当一条看家犬，以私恩系于您身，昼夜替您防阴谋、防刺客。求您莫再指望别的，也莫指望一个不列颠人配对帝都的阴谋诡计置一词——那于他隔如天壤。我顺着您说，您早晚疑我不诚；我逆着您说，又开罪于您。”

尼禄抬眼看着这年轻不列颠人坦诚的脸。

“够了，”他微笑道，“你就做我的看家犬，别的什么都不做。”

日子越久，尼禄对这不列颠护卫的信义越深。他有他的暗探，深知贝里克除了诺巴努斯一家，不同任何人深交；又知他谢绝过请托人的多笔重贿。有一阵子——贝里克自己并不知道——他的一举一动都有人盯：出门有人跟；在浴场——罗马人聚会的中心，抵得后世的俱乐部——同人交谈，有人听、有人记。众人报上去的是：他极少赴宴；席间一谈到公务他便抽身；不摆阔，衣着素净到宫廷圈子里能容的极限；起居做派，同在斯科帕斯学堂受训时一样简朴。学堂他仍常去，同旧日同伴天天过手，宁舍公共操练场那份正经操练。这样过了些时日，尼禄对贝里克的坦直诚实再无怀疑，深信敌人的手伸不进他。

One result of this increased confidence was that Beric’s hours of leisure became much restricted, for Nero came to require his attendance whenever he appeared in public. With Beric and Boduoc among the group of courtiers that followed him, the emperor felt assured there was no occasion to fear the knife of the assassin; and it was only when he was at the baths, where only his most chosen friends were admitted, or during the long carousals that followed the suppers, that Beric was at liberty, and in the latter case Boduoc was always near at hand in case of need.

Nero’s precautions were redoubled after the detection of the conspiracy of Piso. That this plot was a real one, and not a mere invention of Nero to justify his designs upon those he hated and feared, is undoubted. The hour for the attempt at assassination had been fixed, the chief actor was prepared and the knife sharpened. But the executions that followed embraced many who had no knowledge whatever of the plot. Seneca was among the victims against whom there was no shadow of proof.

After the discovery of this plot Beric found his position more and more irksome in spite of the favour Nero showed him. Do what he would he could not close his ears to what was public talk in Rome. The fabulous extravagances of Nero, the public and unbounded profligacy of himself and his court, the open defiance of decency, the stupendous waste of public money on the new and most sumptuous palace into which he had now removed, were matters that scandalized even the population of Rome. Senators, patricians, grave councillors, noble matrons were alike willingly or unwillingly obliged to join in the saturnalia that prevailed. The provinces were ruined to minister to the luxury of Rome. The wealth of the noblest families was sequestrated to the state. All law, order, and decency were set at defiance.

===Chinese===

信任加深的一个结果，是贝里克的闲暇大减：尼禄凡公开露面，都要他在侧。随行的廷臣堆里有贝里克同波杜克两条大汉，皇帝便觉刺客的刀近不了身；只有入浴时——只准入至交——和晚宴后的长夜纵饮，贝里克才得脱身；纵饮的场合，波杜克也总在近处备着。

皮索的阴谋败露后，尼禄的戒备加倍。那桩阴谋是真有其事，不是尼禄为除眼中钉编造的：行刺的时辰已定，下手的人已备，刀已磨快。可随后的大狱，牵连了许多同阴谋毫不相干的人；塞内加也在被杀之列，对他其实连一点影子的罪证也无。

这桩阴谋之后，纵然恩宠日隆，贝里克的处境却越发煎熬。罗马街谈巷议的事，他再掩耳也掩不住。尼禄穷奢极欲到神话般的地步；他同他宫廷的荒淫，公开而不加节制；廉耻公然被践踏；公帑像水一样泼进那座新落成的穷奢极侈的宫殿——他如今已搬了进去——连罗马百姓也为之咋舌。元老、贵族、肃穆的顾问、贵妇，或甘或不愿，都得加入这场狂欢。外省刮穷了，供罗马的挥霍；名门的家产抄没入官；法律、秩序、廉耻，一概被踩在脚下。

To the Britons, simple in their tastes and habits, this profusion of luxury, this universal profligacy seemed absolutely monstrous. When they met together and talked of their former life in their rude huts, it seemed that the vengeance of the gods must surely fall upon a people who seemed to have lost all sense of virtue, all respect for things human and divine. To Beric the only bearable portions of his existence were the mornings he spent in reading, and in the study of Greek with Chiton, and in the house of Norbanus. Of Lesbia he saw little. She spent her life in a whirl of dissipation and gaiety, accompanying members of her family to all the fêtes in defiance of the wishes of Norbanus, whose authority in this matter she absolutely set at naught.

“The emperor’s invitations override the authority of one who makes himself absurd by his presumption of philosophy. I live as do other Roman ladies of good family. Divorce me if you like; I have the fortune I brought you, and should prefer vastly to go my own way.”

This step Norbanus would have taken but for the sake of Aemilia. By his orders the latter never went abroad with her mother or attended any of the public entertainments, but lived in the quiet society of the personal friends of Norbanus. Lesbia had yielded the point, for she did not care to be accompanied by a daughter of marriageable age, as by dint of cosmetics and paint she posed as still a young woman. Aemilia had long since recovered her spirits, and was again the merry girl Beric had known at Massilia.

One day when Beric called he saw that Norbanus, who was seldom put out by any passing circumstance, was disturbed in mind.

“I am troubled indeed,” he said, in answer to Beric’s inquiry. “Lesbia has been proposing to me the marriage of Rufinus Sulla, a connection of hers, and, as you know, one of Nero’s intimates, with Aemilia.”

Beric uttered an exclamation of anger.

“He is one of the worst of profligates,” he exclaimed. “I would slay him with my own hand rather than that Aemilia should be sacrificed to him.”

===Chinese===

在不列颠人看来——他们口味习性两样简朴——这样的穷奢极欲、这样普遍的荒淫，简直是怪物。同伴相聚，谈起从前粗屋陋舍的日子，都觉得：一个人人丧尽德性、不知敬神人为何物的族类，神的刑罚必定要落下来。在贝里克，日子里熬得下去的，只有读书的早晨、同基顿习希腊文的早晨，和在诺巴努斯家的时光。莱斯比亚他见得少了：她泡在纸醉金迷的旋涡里，拖着娘家的人赴遍大小宴会，全不把诺巴努斯的意愿放在眼里——他在这一头的主权，被她踩得一钱不值。

“皇帝的召请，压倒一个拿哲学装门面、自命不凡得可笑的丈夫的权威。我照罗马世家出身的太太们那样过日子。要休我便休；我的陪嫁我自己带走，正巴不得各行其路。”

若不为埃米利娅，诺巴努斯早走了这一步。他下令：埃米利娅不得随母出门，不得赴一切公开宴乐，只在他至交的安静交际里过活。莱斯比亚竟也让了这一条——她本就不愿带着一个待嫁之年的女儿在身边：靠脂粉铅华，她还充着年轻夫人呢。埃米利娅早已恢复生气，又是马西利亚时贝里克识得的那位快活姑娘了。

一天贝里克登门，见素来泰山崩于前不改色的诺巴努斯，满面愁容。

“实在心烦，”贝里克动问，他答道。“莱斯比亚向我提亲：把埃米利娅许给鲁菲努斯·苏拉——她的亲戚，你知道，也是尼禄的狎客之一。”

贝里克怒喝一声。

“那是头一等的无耻之徒！”他喊。“叫我亲手杀了他，也强似把埃米利娅牺牲给他！”

===Original===

“And I would slay her first,” Norbanus said calmly; “but, as Lesbia threatened when I indignantly refused the proposal, Rufinus has but to ask Nero’s approval, and before his orders my authority as a father goes for nothing. I see but one way. It has seemed to me for a long time, Beric, that you yourself felt more warmly towards Aemilia than a mere friend. Putting aside our obligations to you for having risked your life in defence of Ennia, there is no one to whom I would more willingly give her. Have I been mistaken in your thoughts of her?”

“By no means,” Beric said. “I love your daughter Aemilia, but I have never spoken of it to you for two reasons. In the first place I shall not be for some years of the age at which we Britons marry, and in the second I am but a captive. At present I stand high in the favour of Nero, but that favour may fail me at any day, and my life at the palace is becoming unbearable; but besides, it is impossible that this orgy of crime and debauchery can continue. The vengeance of heaven cannot be much longer delayed. The legions in the provinces are utterly discontented and well nigh mutinous, and even if Rome continues to support Nero the time cannot be far off when the legions proclaim either Galba, or Vespasian, or some other general, as emperor, and then the downfall of Nero must come. How then could I ask you for the hand of Aemilia, a maiden of noble family, when the future is all so dark and troubled and my own lot so uncertain?

“那我就先杀了埃米利娅，”诺巴努斯平静地说。“可莱斯比亚放话了——我愤然回绝时——鲁菲努斯只消求尼禄一纸允准，他的谕令面前，我这点父权一钱不值。我只有一条路。贝里克，许久以来我看在眼里：你对埃米利娅，情分早不止于朋友。且不说你为救恩尼娅舍命的恩，天底下我也最愿把她许给你。你的心思，我可看错了？”

“没看错，”贝里克说。“我爱你的女儿埃米利娅；一直没提，有两层缘故。一，照我们不列颠人的规矩，我离娶亲的年纪还差着几年；二，我终究是个俘虏。如今我在尼禄跟前得宠，可这份宠随时会塌，宫里的日子已熬不住了；再说，这样一场罪恶荒淫的狂欢，断没有长不了的理。天罚拖不了多久了。各行省的军团怨气冲天，几乎要哗变；就算罗马城还撑尼禄，军团宣布加尔巴、韦帕芗或哪位将军为帝的日子也不远了——那时尼禄必倒。前路这样黑、这样乱，我自己的命数这样悬，怎么开得了口，向你要一位名门的闺女？”

“I cannot raise my sword against Caesar, for, however foul his crimes, he has treated me well. Had it not been for that I would have made for Praeneste, when the gladiators rose there the other day, and for the same reason I can do nothing to prepare the way for a rising here. I know the ludus of Scopus would join to a man. There is great discontent among the other schools, for the people have become so accustomed to bloodshed that they seem steeled to all pity, and invariably give the signal for the despatch of the conquered. As to your offer, Norbanus, I thank you with all my heart; but were it not for this danger that threatens from Rufinus, I would say that at the present time I dare not link her lot to mine. The danger is too great, the future too dark. It seems to me that the city and all in it are seized with madness, and above all, at the present time, I would not for worlds take her to the palace of Nero. But if Aemilia will consent to a betrothal to me, putting off the period of marriage until the times are changed, I will, with delight, accept the offer of her hand, if she too is willing, for in Briton, as in Gaul, our maidens have a voice in their own disposal.”

Norbanus smiled. “Methinks, Beric, you need not fear on that score. Since the day when you fought the lion in the arena you have been her hero and the lord of her heart. Even I, although but short sighted as to matters unconnected with my work, could mark that, and I believe it is because her mother sees and fears it that she has determined to marry her to Rufinus. I will call her down to find out whether she is ready to obey my wishes.”

In a minute or two Aemilia came down from the women’s apartments above.

“My child,” Norbanus said, “I have offered you in marriage to Beric. He has accepted, saving only that you must come to him not in obedience to my orders but of your own free will, since it is the custom of his country that both parties should be equally free of choice. What do you say, my child?”

Aemilia had flushed with a sudden glow of colour as her father began, and stood with downcast eyes until he had finished.

“One moment before you decide, Aemilia,” Beric said. “You know how I am situated, and that at any moment I may be involved in peril or death; that life with me can scarcely be one of ease or luxury, and that even at the best you may be an exile forever from Rome.”

“我不能举剑反恺撒——他罪再大，待我厚。不为这个，前几天普雷内斯特角斗士起事，我就投奔去了；也是为这个，我不能为罗马这里的起事铺一砖一瓦。我知道斯科帕斯学堂会倾馆相从；别家学堂怨气也大——如今看客见血见惯了，心肠硬得没了怜悯，败者几乎一律处死。至于你的美意，诺巴努斯，我全心感谢；可若不是鲁菲努斯这头逼来，眼下的时世，我不敢把她的命数拴上我的。险太大，前路太黑。我看这座城、城里的人全疯了；尤其眼下，万万不能带她进尼禄的宫。可倘若埃米利娅肯同我订下婚约，把成婚的日子推到时局翻覆之后——她若也情愿，我便欢欢喜喜接受她——在我们不列颠，同高卢一样，闺女自己的婚事，她自己有一票。”
