## III / 第三章

===Original===
Mr. Glowry returned from London with the loss of his lawsuit. Justice was with him, but the law was against him. He found Scythrop in a mood most sympathetically tragic; and they vied with each other in enlivening their cups by lamenting the depravity of this degenerate age, and occasionally interspersing divers grim jokes about graves, worms, and epitaphs. Mr. Glowry’s friends, whom we have mentioned in the first chapter, availed themselves of his return to pay him a simultaneous visit. At the same time arrived Scythrop’s friend and fellow-collegian, the Honourable Mr. Listless. Mr. Glowry had discovered this fashionable young gentleman in London, “stretched on the rack of a too easy chair,” and devoured with a gloomy and misanthropical *nil curo*, and had pressed him so earnestly to take the benefit of the pure country air, at Nightmare Abbey, that Mr. Listless, finding it would give him more trouble to refuse than to comply, summoned his French valet, Fatout, and told him he was going to Lincolnshire. On this simple hint, Fatout went to work, and the imperials were packed, and the post-chariot was at the door, without the Honourable Mr. Listless having said or thought another syllable on the subject.

===Chinese===
格洛里先生从伦敦回来，官司输了个精光。正义在他这一边，法律却在对面。他见斯克罗普正处在一腔极具同病相怜之感的悲剧情绪里；父子俩便你追我赶，借着哀叹这堕落时代的堕落来给杯中之物助兴，间或还点缀几个关于坟墓、蛆虫和墓志铭的冷峭笑话。我们在第一章提过的格洛里先生那几位朋友，趁他回来，一齐登门拜访。与此同时，斯克罗普的大学同窗好友、尊贵的李斯特里斯先生（the Honourable Mr. Listless）也到了。格洛里先生在伦敦发现了这位时髦青年——「直挺挺摊在一张过于柔软的安乐椅刑架上」，被一种阴郁而厌世的 *nil curo*（我全不在乎）啃噬着——便恳切相劝，请他来噩梦隐修院受用受用纯净的乡间空气；李斯特里斯先生盘算下来，拒绝比从命还要费事，便唤来他的法国贴身男仆法图（Fatout），告诉他要动身去林肯郡。得了这简简单单一句话的由头，法图便着手操办一切：车顶行李箱收拾停当，邮车式四轮马车停在了门口，而尊贵的李斯特里斯先生在这件事上，再没说过、也没想过另一个音节。

===Original===
Mr. and Mrs. Hilary brought with them an orphan niece, a daughter of Mr. Glowry’s youngest sister, who had made a runaway love-match with an Irish officer. The lady’s fortune disappeared in the first year: love, by a natural consequence, disappeared in the second: the Irishman himself, by a still more natural consequence, disappeared in the third. Mr. Glowry had allowed his sister an annuity, and she had lived in retirement with her only daughter, whom, at her death, which had recently happened, she commended to the care of Mrs. Hilary.

Miss Marionetta Celestina O’Carroll was a very blooming and accomplished young lady. Being a compound of the *allegro vivace* of the O’Carrolls, and of the *andante doloroso* of the Glowries, she exhibited in her own character all the diversities of an April sky. Her hair was light-brown; her eyes hazel, and sparkling with a mild but fluctuating light; her features regular; her lips full, and of equal size; and her person surpassingly graceful. She was a proficient in music. Her conversation was sprightly, but always on subjects light in their nature and limited in their interest: for moral sympathies, in any general sense, had no place in her mind. She had some coquetry, and more caprice, liking and disliking almost in the same moment; pursuing an object with earnestness while it seemed unattainable, and rejecting it when in her power as not worth the trouble of possession.

===Chinese===
希拉里先生伉俪随身带来一位孤女，是格洛里先生最小的妹妹的女儿；那位妹妹当年跟一个爱尔兰军官私奔成了亲。那位太太的财产，头一年里就消失了：爱情，顺理成章，第二年里也消失了：那位爱尔兰人本人，照更加顺理成章的推论，第三年里也消失了。格洛里先生给妹妹立了一份年金，她便带着独女深居简出；前些时日她去世了，临终把女儿托付给希拉里太太照管。

玛丽奥内塔·塞莱斯蒂娜·奥卡罗尔小姐（Marionetta Celestina O’Carroll）是位容光焕发、多才多艺的少女。她是奥卡罗尔家的 *allegro vivace*（活泼的快板）与格洛里家的 *andante doloroso*（哀伤的行板）的化合物，因而在自己这一副性情里，展览出四月晴空的一切阴晴不定。她的头发是浅褐色；眼睛是榛色的，闪着柔和而游移的光；五官端正；双唇丰润，大小匀停；身段袅娜，超逸绝尘。她精于音律。她的谈吐轻快活泼，但话题永远天生轻浅、兴味有限：因为道德同情——就其任何普遍的意义而言——在她心里没有位置。她有几分卖弄风情，更有几分反复无常，喜爱与厌憎几乎发生在同一瞬；一件东西似乎得不到时，她孜孜以求；一旦到了手，又嫌占有它不值得费那个事，弃之如敝屣。

===Original===
Whether she was touched with a *penchant* for her cousin Scythrop, or was merely curious to see what effect the tender passion would have on so outré a person, she had not been three days in the Abbey before she threw out all the lures of her beauty and accomplishments to make a prize of his heart. Scythrop proved an easy conquest. The image of Miss Emily Girouette was already sufficiently dimmed by the power of philosophy and the exercise of reason: for to these influences, or to any influence but the true one, are usually ascribed the mental cures performed by the great physician Time. Scythrop’s romantic dreams had indeed given him many *pure anticipated cognitions* of combinations of beauty and intelligence, which, he had some misgivings, were not exactly realised in his cousin Marionetta; but, in spite of these misgivings, he soon became distractedly in love; which, when the young lady clearly perceived, she altered her tactics, and assumed as much coldness and reserve as she had before shown ardent and ingenuous attachment. Scythrop was confounded at the sudden change; but, instead of falling at her feet and requesting an explanation, he retreated to his tower, muffled himself in his nightcap, seated himself in the president’s chair of his imaginary secret tribunal, summoned Marionetta with all terrible formalities, frightened her out of her wits, disclosed himself, and clasped the beautiful penitent to his bosom.

===Chinese===
她究竟是对表哥斯克罗普动了 *penchant*（倾心），还是纯粹好奇，想看看温柔的情热在这么一个怪诞人物身上会烧出什么景象——总之，她进宅不满三日，便把美貌与才艺的全部钓饵一齐撒出，要把他那颗心当战利品擒获。斯克罗普果然不堪一攻。埃米莉·吉鲁埃特小姐的倩影，在哲学的威力与理性的操练之下，早已淡得够可以了：因为大医师时间手到病除的种种心疾之愈，人们照例归功于这类影响，或除了真正原因之外的任何影响。斯克罗普的浪漫迷梦，确实曾给予他许多*纯粹的先期认知*（pure anticipated cognitions），关乎美与智的种种组合；他隐约有些疑心，这些组合在表妹玛丽奥内塔身上并未严丝合缝地兑现；但尽管有这些疑心，他很快便神魂颠倒地堕入了情网；小姐把这一切看得分明，随即变了一副战术，摆出十足的高冷与矜持，恰与她先前表露的热烈而天真的依恋等量齐观。斯克罗普被这骤变惊得目瞪口呆；可他没有跪倒在她脚下讨一个解释，反倒退回自己的塔楼，把睡帽往下一裹，坐进他幻想中秘密法庭的庭长高椅，以种种骇人的仪节传唤玛丽奥内塔，把她吓得魂飞魄散，然后亮明身份，把这位美丽的悔罪者一把搂进胸怀。

===Original===
While he was acting this reverie—in the moment in which the awful president of the secret tribunal was throwing back his cowl and his mantle, and discovering himself to the lovely culprit as her adoring and magnanimous lover, the door of the study opened, and the real Marionetta appeared.

The motives which had led her to the tower were a little penitence, a little concern, a little affection, and a little fear as to what the sudden secession of Scythrop, occasioned by her sudden change of manner, might portend. She had tapped several times unheard, and of course unanswered; and at length, timidly and cautiously opening the door, she discovered him standing up before a black velvet chair, which was mounted on an old oak table, in the act of throwing open his striped calico dressing-gown, and flinging away his nightcap—which is what the French call an imposing attitude.

===Chinese===
正当他把这场白日梦演到酣处——恰在秘密法庭那位威严的庭长掀开兜帽和大氅、向可爱的罪人揭示自己原是她倾心爱慕、宽宏大量的情郎的一刻——书房的门开了，货真价实的玛丽奥内塔出现了。

引她上塔来的动机，是一点点悔意、一点点关切、一点点情意，再加上一点点担忧——斯克罗普因她态度突变而突然绝迹，这兆头究竟作何解。她敲了几回门，无人听见，自然也无人应答；末了她怯生生、轻轻地推开门，只见他正站在一把黑丝绒椅子前——那椅子架在一张老橡木桌上——恰在张开条纹印花布晨衣、甩掉睡帽的那个动作之中——此情此景，法国人称之为气势凌人的姿势。

===Original===
Each stood a few moments fixed in their respective places—the lady in astonishment, and the gentleman in confusion. Marionetta was the first to break silence. “For heaven’s sake,” said she, “my dear Scythrop, what is the matter?”

“For heaven’s sake, indeed!” said Scythrop, springing from the table; “for your sake, Marionetta, and you are my heaven—distraction is the matter. I adore you, Marionetta, and your cruelty drives me mad.” He threw himself at her knees, devoured her hand with kisses, and breathed a thousand vows in the most passionate language of romance.

===Chinese===
两人各在各的位置怔了片刻——小姐惊讶，绅士狼狈。玛丽奥内塔先开的口。「看在老天爷份上，」她说，「亲爱的斯克罗普，这是怎么了？」

「可不是看在老天爷份上！」斯克罗普喊道，一跃离桌；「是看在你的份上，玛丽奥内塔，你就是我的老天爷——我怎么的？我疯了。我爱你，玛丽奥内塔，你的残忍把我逼疯了。」他扑倒在她膝前，把她的手吻了个饱，用传奇里最热烈的语言，喘出一千个誓愿。

===Original===
Marionetta listened a long time in silence, till her lover had exhausted his eloquence and paused for a reply. She then said, with a very arch look, “I prithee deliver thyself like a man of this world.” The levity of this quotation, and of the manner in which it was delivered, jarred so discordantly on the high-wrought enthusiasm of the romantic inamorato, that he sprang upon his feet, and beat his forehead with his clenched fist. The young lady was terrified; and, deeming it expedient to soothe him, took one of his hands in hers, placed the other hand on his shoulder, looked up in his face with a winning seriousness, and said, in the tenderest possible tone, “What would you have, Scythrop?”

Scythrop was in heaven again. “What would I have? What but you, Marionetta? You, for the companion of my studies, the partner of my thoughts, the auxiliary of my great designs for the emancipation of mankind.”

===Chinese===
玛丽奥内塔默然听了半晌，直到情郎的雄辩告罄，停下来等回音。她这才摆出一副十分俏皮的神情，说道：「妾请君且像个尘世中人那样说话。」这句引语，连同说它的那副腔调，在这位高烧不退的浪漫情郎昂扬的热忱上刺耳地一撞，他一蹦而起，攥拳直捶自己的额头。小姐吓坏了，觉得还是安抚为上策，便一手握住他一只手，另一只手搭上他的肩，仰起脸望着他，带着动人的郑重，用再温柔不过的声调说：「你要什么，斯克罗普？」

斯克罗普又上了天堂。「我要什么？除你之外还要什么，玛丽奥内塔？要你，做我学问的同伴、思想的合伙人、我解放人类宏图大业的辅弼。」

===Original===
“I am afraid I should be but a poor auxiliary, Scythrop. What would you have me do?”

“Do as Rosalia does with Carlos, divine Marionetta. Let us each open a vein in the other’s arm, mix our blood in a bowl, and drink it as a sacrament of love. Then we shall see visions of transcendental illumination, and soar on the wings of ideas into the space of pure intelligence.”

===Chinese===
「我怕我当不好这个辅弼，斯克罗普。你要我做什么呢？」

「像罗莎莉娅（Rosalia）待卡洛斯（Carlos）那样去做，神圣的玛丽奥内塔。咱们各自在对方臂上割开一条血管，把血混进一只碗里，喝下去，当爱情的圣餐。然后我们便会看见先验启明的种种异象，乘着理念的翅膀，翱翔进纯粹智性的太空。」

===Original===
Marionetta could not reply; she had not so strong a stomach as Rosalia, and turned sick at the proposition. She disengaged herself suddenly from Scythrop, sprang through the door of the tower, and fled with precipitation along the corridors. Scythrop pursued her, crying, “Stop, stop, Marionetta—my life, my love!” and was gaining rapidly on her flight, when, at an ill-omened corner, where two corridors ended in an angle, at the head of a staircase, he came into sudden and violent contact with Mr. Toobad, and they both plunged together to the foot of the stairs, like two billiard-balls into one pocket. This gave the young lady time to escape, and enclose herself in her chamber; while Mr. Toobad, rising slowly, and rubbing his knees and shoulders, said, “You see, my dear Scythrop, in this little incident, one of the innumerable proofs of the temporary supremacy of the devil; for what but a systematic design and concurrent contrivance of evil could have made the angles of time and place coincide in our unfortunate persons at the head of this accursed staircase?”

===Chinese===
玛丽奥内塔答不上来；她的胃口没有罗莎莉娅那般坚强，这提议叫她恶心欲呕。她猛地从斯克罗普怀里挣脱，闪身出了塔门，顺着走廊仓皇飞奔。斯克罗普在后紧追，一路喊：「站住，站住，玛丽奥内塔——我的性命，我的爱人！」眼看看就要得手，偏偏在一个不祥的拐角——两条走廊在那里交成一个死角，正当楼梯口——他一头撞上图巴德先生，两人一齐滚落楼梯底，像两颗台球滚进同一个袋。这一撞给小姐赢得了脱身的时间，把自己关进了卧房；图巴德先生慢慢爬起来，揉着膝盖和肩膀，说道：「亲爱的斯克罗普，你在这个小小的意外里，看见了魔鬼暂时称尊的无数明证之一；若非邪恶有一套成体系的设计、彼此协同的机谋，又怎能让时间与地点的死角，恰好在咱们两个倒霉人身上、在这该死的楼梯口撞个正着？」

===Original===
“Nothing else, certainly,” said Scythrop: “you are perfectly in the right, Mr. Toobad. Evil, and mischief, and misery, and confusion, and vanity, and vexation of spirit, and death, and disease, and assassination, and war, and poverty, and pestilence, and famine, and avarice, and selfishness, and rancour, and jealousy, and spleen, and malevolence, and the disappointments of philanthropy, and the faithlessness of friendship, and the crosses of love—all prove the accuracy of your views, and the truth of your system; and it is not impossible that the infernal interruption of this fall downstairs may throw a colour of evil on the whole of my future existence.”

===Chinese===
「除了这个，再没别的了，」斯克罗普说，「您完全正确，图巴德先生。邪恶，与祸殃，与苦难，与混乱，与虚空，与心灵的愁烦，与死亡，与疾病，与暗杀，与战争，与贫困，与瘟疫，与饥馑，与贪婪，与自私，与怨毒，与嫉妒，与郁怒，与恶意，与博爱的落空，与友谊的背信，与爱情的乖舛——桩桩件件，都证明您见解的精确、您体系的真实；何况这一跤跌下楼来的地狱式打岔，也未必不会给我下半辈子整个儿抹上一层邪恶的颜色。」

===Original===
“My dear boy,” said Mr. Toobad, “you have a fine eye for consequences.”

So saying, he embraced Scythrop, who retired, with a disconsolate step, to dress for dinner; while Mr. Toobad stalked across the hall, repeating, “Woe to the inhabiters of the earth, and of the sea, for the devil is come among you, having great wrath.”

===Chinese===
「好孩子，」图巴德先生说，「你对后果有好眼力。」

说着，他拥抱了斯克罗普；斯克罗普迈着凄惶的脚步退下去更衣用饭；图巴德先生则大步穿过门厅，口中念念有词：「地与海的居民有祸了，因为魔鬼已来到你们中间，怒气冲冲。」
