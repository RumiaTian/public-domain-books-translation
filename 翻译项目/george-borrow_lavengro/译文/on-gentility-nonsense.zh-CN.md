## IV On Gentility Nonsense / 四 论体面的胡话
===Original===

What is gentility? People in different stations in England entertain different ideas of what is genteel,368 but it must be something gorgeous, glittering or tawdry, to be considered genteel by any of them. The beau-ideal of the English aristocracy, of course with some exceptions, is some young fellow with an imperial title, a military personage of course, for what is military is so particularly genteel, with flaming epaulets, a cocked hat and plume, a prancing charger, and a band of fellows called generals and colonels, with flaming epaulets, cocked hats and plumes, and prancing chargers vapouring behind him. It was but lately that the daughter of an English marquis was heard to say, that the sole remaining wish of her heart﻿—she had known misfortunes, and was not far from fifty﻿—was to be introduced to﻿—whom? The Emperor of Austria! The sole remaining wish of the heart of one who ought to have been thinking of the grave and judgment, was to be introduced to the miscreant who had caused the blood of noble Hungarian females to be whipped out of their shoulders, for no other crime than devotion to their country, and its tall and heroic sons. The middle classes﻿—of course there are some exceptions﻿—admire the aristocracy, and consider them pinks, the aristocracy who admire the Emperor of Austria, and adored the Emperor of Russia, till he became old, ugly and unfortunate, when their adoration instantly terminated; for what is more ungenteel than age, ugliness and misfortune! The beau-ideal with those of the lower classes, with peasants and mechanics, is some flourishing railroad contractor: look, for example, how they worship Mr. Flamson.369 This person makes his grand début in the year ’thirty-nine, at a public meeting in the principal room of a country inn. He has come into the neighbourhood with the character of a man worth a million pounds, who is to make everybody’s fortune; at this time, however, he is not worth a shilling of his own, though he flashes about dexterously three or four thousand pounds, part of which sum he has obtained by specious pretences, and part from certain individuals who are his confederates. But in the year ’forty-nine, he is really in possession of the fortune which he and his agents pretended he was worth ten years before﻿—he is worth a million pounds. By what means has he come by them?

什么是体面？英格兰不同阶层的人对何为体面各有各的想法368，可在他们谁看来，要算体面，总得是些绚烂的、闪亮的、或花哨的东西。英格兰贵族的理想典型——自然有几个例外——是某个带帝国头衔的年轻人，一个军界人物——军界的末就是特别体面——着火焰般的肩章，戴卷边插羽的三角帽，骑一匹腾跳的战马，身后跟着一帮叫做将军、上校的家伙，同样火焰般的肩章、卷边插羽的三角帽、腾跳的战马，跟在后面噴云吐雾。就在不久前，还听得一位英格兰侯爵的女儿说，她心底仅剩的愿望——她经受过不幸，也年近五十了——就是被引见给——谁？奥地利皇帝！一个本该想着坟墓与审判的人，心底仅剩的愿望，竟是被引见给那个恶棍——他叫高贵的匈牙利女性的血从肩头被鞭抽出来，罪名无他，只因她们热爱自己的国家和它高大英勇的儿郎。中产阶级——自然也有例外——崇拜贵族，把贵族当皎皎者；而贵族崇拜奥地利皇帝，也崇拜过俄国皇帝——直到他老了、丑了、倒霉了，他们的崇拜便立刻终止；因为还有什么比年老、丑陋与不幸更不体面的呢！下层人们、庄稼人和匠人们的理想典型，是哪个发了迹的铁路承包商：譬如说，瞧瞧他们怎样崇拜弗拉姆森先生369。此君盛大的登台是在三十九年，在一家乡村客栈的大厅里的一次公众集会上。他带着百万英镑身价的名声来到那一带，说要叫人人发财；其实那时节他自己一个先令也不值，尽管他灵巧地挥攞着三四千镑——这钱一部分凭貌似有理的名目弄来，一部分出自他的几个同伙。可到四十九年，他真握有了他和他的代理人们十年前假称的那份家产——他值一百万镑了。这钱他是怎么来的？

By railroad contracts, for which he takes care to be paid in hard cash before he attempts to perform them, and to carry out which he makes use of the sweat and blood of wretches who, since their organisation, have introduced crimes and language into England to which it was previously almost a stranger﻿—by purchasing, with paper, shares by hundreds in the schemes to execute which he contracts, and which are of his own devising; which shares he sells as soon as they are at a high premium, to which they are speedily forced by means of paragraphs, inserted by himself and agents, in newspapers devoted to his interest, utterly reckless of the terrible depreciation to which they are almost instantly subjected. But he is worth a million pounds, there can be no doubt of the fact﻿—he has not made people’s fortunes, at least those whose fortunes it was said he would make; he has made them away; but his own he has made, emphatically made it; he is worth a million pounds. Hurrah for the millionaire! The clown who views the pandemonium of red brick which he has built on the estate which he has purchased in the neighbourhood of the place of his grand début in which every species of architecture, Greek, Indian and Chinese, is employed in caricature﻿—who hears of the grand entertainment he gives at Christmas in the principal dining-room, the hundred wax-candles, the wagon-load of plate, and the oceans of wine which form parts of it, and above all the two ostrich poults, one at the head, and the other at the foot of the table, exclaims: “Well! if he a’n’t bang up, I don’t know who be; why he beats my lord hollow!” The mechanic of the borough town, who sees him dashing through the streets in an open landau, drawn by four milk-white horses, amidst its attendant outriders; his wife, a monster of a woman, by his side, stout as the wife of Tamerlane, who weighed twenty stone, and bedizened out like her whose person shone with the jewels of plundered Persia, stares with silent wonder, and at last exclaims: “That’s the man for my vote!” You tell the clown that the man of the mansion has contributed enormously to corrupt the rural innocence of England; you point to an incipient branch railroad, from around which the accents of Gomorrah are sounding, and beg him to listen for a moment, and then close his ears.

靠铁路合同——合同他务必先收现钱、后开工；靠榨取可怜虫们的汗与血来完工——这班人自从成帮结伙进了英格兰，带来了英格兰先前几乎闻所未闻的罪行和言语；靠用纸头成百地购买他自己设计的那些工程的股票——合同正是他签来实施这些工程的；股价一升水他便立刻抛出，而升水是靠他自己和代理人塞进那些效忠于他的报纸里的短文，硬拉上去的——股票几乎转眼惨跌，他全然不管。可他值一百万镑，这事实毫无可疑——他没有叫人人的梦想成真，至少没有叫那些据说他要叫他们发财的人成真；他把他们的财“成”没了；可他自己的财他是成全了的，千真万确地成全了；他值一百万镑。百万富翁万岁！那庄稼汉望着他在地产上盖起的那座红砖的魔窟——地产就买在他盛大登台那个地方的左近，里头希腊式、印度式、中国式，各式建筑无一不在受着漫画式的糟蹋——听着人说他圣诞在大餐厅里摆下的盛大筵席：一百支蜡烛，一整车银器，成海的酒，尤其是那两只鸵鸟雏，一只在桌首、一只在桌尾——不禁喝彩：「嗐！他还不算阔到顶，那谁算？他把咱们老爷都盖过去了！」那选镇上的匠人，见他坐着敞篷四轮马车轰然穿过街道，四匹乳白的马拉着，前呼后拥的骑手随行；他身边那位夫人，一座山似的女人，壮得像帖木儿的王后——那位重二十英石——又打扮得珠光宝气，像那个满身闪着掠来的波斯珠宝的女人——看得目瞪口呆，末了喊出：「这人夺我一票！」你对庄稼汉说，那座宅子的主人对败坏英格兰乡村的清白出了大力；你指给他看一条新开的铁路支线，蛾摩拉的口音正从那周围传来，请他听上一听，然后捂上耳朵。

Hodge scratches his head and says: “Well, I have nothing to say to that; all I known is that he is bang up, and I wish I were he;” perhaps he will add﻿—a Hodge has been known to add﻿—“He has been kind enough to put my son on that very railroad; ’tis true the company is somewhat queer, and the work rather killing, but he gets there half a crown a day, whereas from the farmers he would only get eighteenpence.” You remind the mechanic that the man in the landau has been the ruin of thousands, and you mention people whom he himself knows, people in various grades of life, widows and orphans amongst them, whose little all he has dissipated, and whom he has reduced to beggary by inducing them to become sharers in his delusive schemes. But the mechanic says: “Well, the more fools they to let themselves be robbed. But I don’t call that kind of thing robbery, I merely call it outwitting; and everybody in this free country has a right to outwit others if he can. What a turnout he has!” One was once heard to add: “I never saw a more genteel-looking man in all my life except one, and that was a gentleman’s walley, who was much like him. It is true he is rather undersized, but then, madam, you know, makes up for all.”

霍奇挠着头说：「嗐，这个我设话说；我只知道他是阔到了顶，我恨不得自己就是他；」兴许他还会添一句——霍奇是添得出这种话的——「他还够意思，把我儿子安插在那条铁路上；不假，同伴是有点不大齐整，活计也真要命，可他在那儿一天挣半个克朗，跟着庄稼人只能挣十八便士。」你提醒那匠人：四轮马车里那个人是千百户人家倾家荡产的祸根；你举出他自己也认得的人，各行各业都有，寡妇孤儿也在内，把微薄的全部家当拋在他那虛妄的股份里，被他诱到血本无归、沦为乞丐。可那匠人说：「嗐，谁让他们傻，甘愿叫人抢。再说我不把那号事叫抢，我只叫它斗心眼；在这个自由的国度，谁都有权斗心眼，斗得过就斗。瞧他那一副排场！」有人还听过一位添上一句：「我一辈子没见过比他更体面的男人，只除了一个，是位老爷的贴身仆人，跟他像得很。不假，他个子是矮了点，可您知道的，太太，排场抵得了一切。」
