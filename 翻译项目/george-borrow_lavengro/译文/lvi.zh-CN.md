===Original===

Rather late in the morning I awoke; for a few minutes I lay still, perfectly still; my imagination was considerably sobered; the scenes and situations which had pleased me so much over night appeared to me in a far less captivating guise that morning. I felt languid and almost hopeless﻿—the thought, however, of my situation soon roused me﻿—I must make an effort to improve the posture of my affairs; there was no time to be lost; so I sprang out of bed, breakfasted on bread and water, and then sat down doggedly to write the life of Joseph Sell.
It was a great thing to have formed my plan, and to have arranged the scenes in my head, as I had done on the preceding night. The chief thing requisite at present was the mere mechanical act of committing them to paper. This I did not find at first so easy as I could wish﻿—I wanted mechanical skill; but I persevered, and before evening I had written ten pages. I partook of some bread and water; and, before I went to bed that night, I had completed fifteen pages of my life of Joseph Sell.
The next day I resumed my task﻿—I found my power of writing considerably increased; my pen hurried rapidly over the paper﻿—my brain was in a wonderfully teeming state; many scenes and visions which I had not thought of before were evolved, and, as fast as evolved, written down; they seemed to be more pat to my purpose, and more natural to my history, than many others which I had imagined before, and which I made now give place to these newer creations: by about midnight I had added thirty fresh pages to my *Life and Adventures of Joseph Sell*.
The third day arose﻿—it was dark and dreary out of doors, and I passed it drearily enough within; my brain appeared to have lost much of its former glow, and my pen much of its power; I, however, toiled on, but at midnight had only added seven pages to my history of Joseph Sell.
On the fourth day the sun shone brightly﻿—I arose, and, having breakfasted as usual, I fell to work. My brain was this day wonderfully prolific, and my pen never before or since glided so rapidly over the paper; towards night I began to feel strangely about the back part of my head, and my whole system was extraordinarily affected. I likewise occasionally saw double﻿—a tempter now seemed to be at work within me.

早上醒得相当晚；有好几分钟我一动不动地躺着，纹丝不动；我的想象力已大大冷静下来；昨夜令我如此心醉的那些场景和境况，这天早晨在我眼里显得远不如先前动人了。我浑身乏力，几乎绝望——然而一想到自身的处境，我立刻振作起来——必须设法改善眼下的局面；一刻也耽搁不得；于是我翻身下床，就着水吃了些面包权当早饭，随即执拗地坐下，动笔写约瑟夫·塞尔的生平。
计划已定，场景也已在头脑中安排停当，就像前夜所做的那样，这是一大便利。眼下最要紧的，不过是把它们落到纸上的纯机械功夫。起初这事并不如我指望的那般顺手——我欠缺下笔的熟练；但我咬牙坚持，到傍晚已写满十页。我又吃了些面包，喝了些水；那晚上床之前，约瑟夫·塞尔的生平已完成十五页。
第二天我继续开工——发觉自己的笔力大增；笔尖在纸上飞驰——头脑处于一种奇异的丰饶状态；许多先前未曾想及的场景与幻象源源涌现，随涌现随落笔；比起我先前构思的许多段落，它们似乎更切题，也更贴合我的故事，于是我便让旧稿让位于这些新生的创造：到午夜前后，我的*《约瑟夫·塞尔的生平与奇遇》*又添了整整三十页。
第三天到来——屋外天昏地暗，我在屋里也过得够凄惶；我的头脑似乎失去了先前的许多热力，笔也失去了许多力量；但我仍旧苦撑，到午夜只给约瑟夫·塞尔的生平添了七页。
第四天阳光明媚——我起了床，照常用过早餐，便伏案工作。这一天我的头脑丰产得出奇，笔尖在纸上滑行之快，之前不曾有过，之后也再没有过；将近入夜，我的后脑开始有种异样的感觉，周身也大受影响。我还时常看东西重影——此刻我体内似乎有个诱惑者在作祟。

“You had better leave off now for a short space,” said the tempter, “and go out and drink a pint of beer; you have still one shilling left﻿—if you go on at this rate, you will go mad﻿—go out and spend sixpence, you can afford it, more than half your work is done.” I was about to obey the suggestion of the tempter, when the idea struck me that, if I did not complete the work whilst the fit was on me, I should never complete it; so I held on. I am almost afraid to state how many pages I wrote that day of the life of Joseph Sell.
From this time I proceeded in a somewhat more leisurely manner; but, as I drew nearer and nearer to the completion of my task, dreadful fears and despondencies came over me. It will be too late, thought I; by the time I have finished the work, the bookseller will have been supplied with a tale or a novel. Is it probable that, in a town like this, where talent is so abundant﻿—hungry talent too﻿—a bookseller can advertise for a tale or a novel, without being supplied with half a dozen in twenty-four hours? I may as well fling down my pen﻿—I am writing to no purpose. And these thoughts came over my mind so often, that at last, in utter despair, I flung down the pen. Whereupon the tempter within me said: “And, now you have flung down the pen, you may as well fling yourself out of the window; what remains for you to do?” Why, to take it up again, thought I to myself, for I did not like the latter suggestion at all﻿—and then forthwith I resumed the pen, and wrote with greater vigour than before, from about six o’clock in the evening until I could hardly see, when I rested for awhile, when the tempter within me again said, or appeared to say: “All you have been writing is stuff, it will never do﻿—a drug﻿—a mere drug;” and methought these last words were uttered in the gruff tones of the big publisher. “A thing merely to be sneezed at,” a voice like that of Taggart added; and then I seemed to hear a sternutation﻿—as I probably did, for, recovering from a kind of swoon, I found myself shivering with cold. The next day I brought my work to a conclusion.
But the task of revision still remained; for an hour or two I shrank from it, and remained gazing stupidly at the pile of paper which I had written over.

「你最好暂且停一停，」那诱惑者说，「出去喝一品脱啤酒；你还剩一先令——照这样熬下去，你会发疯的——出去花上六便士吧，你花得起，活儿已完成一大半了。」我正要听从诱惑者的提议，忽然想到：若不趁这股劲头在身时把书稿完成，就永远完不成了；于是我坚持了下来。那天我给约瑟夫·塞尔的生平写下了多少页，我几乎不敢说出口。
从这时起，我的进度略微从容了些；可是随着任务渐近完成，可怕的恐惧与沮丧却阵阵袭来。太迟了，我想；等我写完，书商那里只怕早已有了一部小说或故事。在这座城里，人才济济——还都是饿着肚子的人才——一个书商登广告征求小说或故事，二十四小时里不收到半打来稿，天下有这等事吗？我不如掠下笔——写了也是白写。这类念头缠着我，缠了一回又一回，最后我在彻底的绝望中掠下了笔。于是我体内的诱惑者发话了：「既然笔已经掠了，索性把自己从窗口扔出去得了；你还有什么可做的？」做的是把笔重新捡起来，我心想，因为后一条建议我半点也不喜欢——于是立刻重新执笔，写得比先前更猛，从傍晚六点上下一直写到眼睛几乎看不清字，才歇了片刻；这时我体内的诱惑者又发话了，或者说仿佛发话了：「你写的这一切全是废物，根本不行——滞销货——地道的滞销货；」而且我觉得最后这几个字，是用那位大出版商的粗嗓子说出来的。「不过是叫人打个喷嚏的东西，」一个像塔格特的声音补了一句；接着我便仿佛听见一声喷嚏——多半是真听见了，因为我从一阵晕厥似的恍惚中缓过来时，发现自己正冷得发抖。第二天，我把全书收了尾。
可还有通读修改这道工序；有一两个钟头我畏畏缩缩不敢动手，只管呆呆望着那摞写满字迹的纸。

===Original===

I was all but exhausted, and I dreaded, on inspecting the sheets, to find them full of absurdities which I had paid no regard to in the furor of composition. But the task, however trying to my nerves, must be got over; at last, in a kind of desperation, I entered upon it. It was far from an easy one; there were, however, fewer errors and absurdities than I had anticipated. About twelve o’clock at night I had got over the task of revision. “Tomorrow, for the bookseller,” said I, as my head sank on the pillow. “Oh me!”
