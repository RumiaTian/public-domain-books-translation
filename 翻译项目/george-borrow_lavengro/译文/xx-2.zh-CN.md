### XX / 二十
===Original===

On reaching the plain above, I found my Romany friends breakfasting, and on being asked by Mr. Petulengro to join them, I accepted the invitation. No sooner was breakfast over than I informed Ursula and her husband that they would find the property, which I had promised them, below in the dingle, commending the little pony Ambrol to their best care. I took leave of the whole company, which was itself about to break up camp and to depart in the direction of London, and made the best of my way to the public-house. I had a small bundle in my hand, and was dressed in the same manner as when I departed from London, having left my wagoner’s slop with the other effects in the dingle. On arriving at the public-house, I informed the landlord that I was come for my horse, inquiring, at the same time, whether he could not accommodate me with a bridle and saddle. He told me that the bridle and saddle, with which I had ridden the horse on the preceding day, were at my service for a trifle; that he had received them some time since in payment for a debt, and that he had himself no use for them. The leathers of the bridle were rather shabby, and the bit rusty, and the saddle was old-fashioned; but I was happy to purchase them for seven shillings, more especially as the landlord added a small valise, which he said could be strapped to the saddle, and which I should find very convenient for carrying my things in. I then proceeded to the stable, told the horse we were bound on an expedition, and giving him a feed of corn, left him to discuss it, and returned to the barroom to have a little farewell chat with the landlord, and at the same time to drink with him a farewell glass of ale. Whilst we were talking and drinking, the niece came and joined us: she was a decent, sensible young woman, who appeared to take a great interest in her uncle, whom she regarded with a singular mixture of pride and disapprobation﻿—pride for the renown which he had acquired by his feats of old, and disapprobation for his late imprudences. She said that she hoped that his misfortunes would be a warning to him to turn more to his God than he had hitherto done, and to give up cockfighting and other lowlife practices.

到了上头的平地，我见我的罗曼尼朋友们正在吃早饭；彼得伦格罗先生邀我同吃，我便领了情。早饭一毕，我便告诉厄休拉和她丈夫：我许给他们的那些东西就在谷地底下，又把小马驹安布罗（Ambrol）托付给他们，嘱他们好生照看。我向全伙人告了别——他们自己也正要拔营，往伦敦方向去——随后便径直赶往那家酒馆。我手里提着个小包袱，衣着同离开伦敦时一般无二——赶车人的粗布褂子同其他家当，都留在谷地里了。到了酒馆，我告诉掌柜我是来取马的，顺带问他能否给配一副笼头和一副鞍子。掌柜说，昨日我骑马用的那副笼头鞍子，凑一点钱就归我；那是前些日子人家抵账给他的，他自己用不上。笼头的皮子颇旧，衔铁生了锈，鞍子也是老样式；可我乐意出七先令买下——尤其因为掌柜还搭上一只小皮挂包，说可以捆在鞍后，装东西再方便不过。随后我到马房去，告诉马儿我们就要出门远行，喂了它一料豆麦，让它自去享用，自己回到酒柜间，同掌柜闲话几句告别的话，顺便同他喝一杯临行的麦酒。我们正说着喝着，侄女过来入了伙：她是个正经懂事的年轻女子，看来很把叔父放在心上；她看叔父的眼神里，骄傲与非难古怪地掺在一处——为他当年扬名的事迹骄傲，为他近来的胡为非难。她说，但愿这番灾祸能给他一个警醒，叫他比先前多多亲近上帝，把斗鸡和种种下流的营生一并丢了。

To which the landlord replied, that with respect to cockfighting he intended to give it up entirely, being determined no longer to risk his capital upon birds, and with respect to his religious duties, he should attend the church of which he was churchwarden at least once a quarter, adding, however, that he did not intend to become either canter or driveller, neither of which characters would befit a publican surrounded by such customers as he was, and that to the last day of his life he hoped to be able to make use of his fists. After a stay of about two hours I settled accounts, and having bridled and saddled my horse, and strapped on the valise, I mounted, shook hands with the landlord and his niece, and departed, notwithstanding that they both entreated me to tarry until the evening, it being then the heat of the day.

===Chinese===

掌柜答道：斗鸡么，他打算彻底戒了，再不拿本钱往鸡身上冒险；至于教里的本分，他好歹是教堂的司事，至少每季度去坐上一回；不过他又说，他不打算装成个满嘴仁义的假善人，也不打算做个淌口水的痴汉——这两副做派，都配不上一个周遭围着这般主顾的酒馆掌柜；他还指望到咽气那天，两只拳头都使得动。待了约莫两个钟头，我结了账，给马上了笼头和鞍子，捆好挂包，翻身上马，同掌柜和他的侄女握了手，便动身了——虽说两人都再三挽留我捱到傍晚再走，其时正是全天最热的时候。
