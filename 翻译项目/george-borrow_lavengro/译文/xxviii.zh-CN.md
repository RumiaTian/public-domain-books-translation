### XXVIII / 二十八
===Original===

At last my brother arrived; he looked pale and unwell; I met him at the door. “You have been long absent!” said I.
“Yes,” said he, “perhaps too long; but how is my father?”
“Very poorly,” said I, “he has had a fresh attack; but where have you been of late?”
“Far and wide,” said my brother; “but I can’t tell you anything now, I must go to my father. It was only by chance that I heard of his illness.”
“Stay a moment,” said I. “Is the world such a fine place as you supposed it to be before you went away?”
“Not quite,” said my brother, “not quite; indeed I wish﻿—but ask me no questions now, I must hasten to my father.”
There was another question on my tongue, but I forebore; for the eyes of the young man were full of tears. I pointed with my finger, and the young man hastened past me to the arms of his father.
I forebore to ask my brother whether he had been to old Rome.
What passed between my father and brother I do not know; the interview, no doubt, was tender enough, for they tenderly loved each other; but my brother’s arrival did not produce the beneficial effect upon my father which I at first hoped it would; it did not even appear to have raised his spirits. He was composed enough, however. “I ought to be grateful,” said he; “I wished to see my son, and God has granted me my wish; what more have I to do now than to bless my little family and go?”
My father’s end was evidently at hand.
And did I shed no tears? did I breathe no sighs? did I never wring my hands at this period? the reader will perhaps be asking. Whatever I did and thought is best known to God and myself; but it will be as well to observe, that it is possible to feel deeply and yet make no outward sign.
And now for the closing scene.
At the dead hour of night, it might be about two, I was awakened from sleep by a cry which sounded from the room immediately below that in which I slept. I knew the cry, it was the cry of my mother, and I also knew its import; yet I made no effort to rise, for I was for the moment paralysed. Again the cry sounded, yet still I lay motionless﻿—the stupidity of horror was upon me. A third time, and it was then that, by a violent effort bursting the spell which appeared to bind me, I sprang from the bed and rushed downstairs. My mother was running wildly about the room; she had awoke and found my father senseless in the bed by her side.

哥哥终于到了；他脸色苍白，带着病容；我在门口迎着他。「你这回去了好久！」我说。
「是，」他说，「兴许太久了；父亲怎么样？」
「很不好，」我说，「又犯了一场；可你晚近在哪儿？」
「四处奔波，」哥哥说；「现在什么也讲不成，我得先去父亲那儿。他的病我也是碰巧听说的。」
「且慢，」我说。「世界真有你走之前想的那么好么？」
「不尽然，」哥哥说，「不尽然；说实在的我倒愿——可现在什么都别问我；我得赶紧去父亲那儿。」
我舌尖上还顶着另一个问题，可我忍住了：那年轻人的眼睛满含着泪。我一指，他赶忙从我身边过去，投进父亲的怀抱。
哥哥到没到过老罗马，我终究没问。
父亲同哥哥之间说了什么，我不知道；那一场相见无疑够温存，他们彼此深爱；可哥哥的归来并没有像我起初指望的那样，在父亲身上生出起色；连他的心绪也不见抬高。可他倒安详。「我该知足了，」他说；「我想见我儿子，上帝准了我的愿；如今我还有什么要办，不外祝福我这一小家，然后去么？」
父亲的终点分明已在眼前。
那么我没有流泪么？没有叹气么？这一阵我就从没绞过手么？读者许要这样问。我做了什么、想了什么，上帝同我自知；不过有一句不妨说说：感情极深的人，也可以一点不露在外面。
如今说那收梢的一幕。
夜的死寂时辰，约莫两点，一阵哭喊把我从睡里惊醒——声音出自我睡的屋子正底下那间。我认得那哭喊：是母亲的；我也认得它的含意；可我一时起不来：我瘫住了。哭喊又起，我还是一动不动——恐怖的痴愚压着我。第三声响起，这才猛一使劲，挣脱了仿佛捆住我的魔咒，跳下床冲下楼去。母亲在屋里乱转；她一觉醒来，发现父亲在身边床上，已没了知觉。

I essayed to raise him, and after a few efforts supported him in the bed in a sitting posture. My brother now rushed in, and snatching up a light that was burning, he held it to my father’s face. “The surgeon, the surgeon!” he cried; then dropping the light, he ran out of the room followed by my mother; I remained alone, supporting the senseless form of my father; the light had been extinguished by the fall, and an almost total darkness reigned in the room. The form pressed heavily against my bosom﻿—at last methought it moved. Yes, I was right, there was a heaving of the breast, and then a gasping. Were those words which I heard? Yes, they were words, low and indistinct at first, and then audible. The mind of the dying man was reverting to former scenes. I heard him mention names which I had often heard him mention before. It was an awful moment; I felt stupefied, but I still contrived to support my dying father. There was a pause, again my father spoke: I heard him speak of Minden, and of Meredith, the old Minden sergeant, and then he uttered another name, which at one period of his life was much on his lips, the name of﻿—but this is a solemn moment! There was a deep gasp: I shook, and thought all was over; but I was mistaken﻿—my father moved and revived for a moment; he supported himself in bed without my assistance. I make no doubt that for a moment he was perfectly sensible, and it was then that, clasping his hands, he uttered another name clearly, distinctly﻿—it was the name of Christ. With that name upon his lips, the brave old soldier sank back upon my bosom, and, with his hands still clasped, yielded up his soul.

我试着扶起他，几番努力之后，把他半靠着撑坐在床上。哥哥这时冲进来，抓起一支点着的蜡烛，凑到父亲脸前。「外科医生！快请外科医生！」他喊；随即丢下蜡烛，跑出屋去，母亲跟着；只剩我一人，撑着父亲那无知觉的身躯；蜡烛跌灭了，屋里几乎全黑。那身躯沉沉压着我的胸口——末了，我仿佛觉出它动了。是，没错：胸口一起一伏，跟着是一阵喘息。我听见的是话么？是，是话：先低而含混，随后听得清了。垂死者的心神回到旧日光景里去了。我听见他提起从前常提的名字。那是怕人的时辰；我觉得痴住了，可还是撑住了我垂死的父亲。停了一停，父亲又开口：我听见他说起明登，说起梅瑞迪斯——那位明登老中士；随后他吐出另一个名字——他一生有一个时期挂在嘴边的——那名字是——可这是庄严的时刻！深深一声喘息：我一抖，以为一切完了；可我错了——父亲动了动，回光返照了一瞬：不用我扶，他自己撑在床上。我毫不怀疑：有那么一刻他完全清醒；就在那一刻，他双手合十，清清楚楚地念出另一个名字——是基督的名字。唇上带着那个名字，这位勇敢的老兵向后靠在我胸口，双手仍合着，交出了他的灵魂。
