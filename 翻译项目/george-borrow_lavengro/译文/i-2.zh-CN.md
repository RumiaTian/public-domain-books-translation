### I / 一
===Original===

I awoke at the first break of day, and, leaving the postillion fast asleep, stepped out of the tent. The dingle was dank and dripping. I lighted a fire of coals, and got my forge in readiness. I then ascended to the field, where the chaise was standing as we had left it on the previous evening. After looking at the cloud-stone near it, now cold, and split into three pieces, I set about prying narrowly into the condition of the wheel and axletree. The latter had sustained no damage of any consequence, and the wheel, as far as I was able to judge, was sound, being only slightly injured in the box. The only thing requisite to set the chaise in a travelling condition appeared to be a linchpin, which I determined to make. Going to the companion wheel, I took out the linchpin, which I carried down with me to the dingle, to serve me as a model.
I found Belle by this time dressed, and seated near the forge. With a slight nod to her like that which a person gives who happens to see an acquaintance when his mind is occupied with important business, I forthwith set about my work. Selecting a piece of iron which I thought would serve my purpose, I placed it in the fire, and plying the bellows in a furious manner, soon made it hot; then seizing it with the tongs, I laid it on the anvil, and began to beat it with my hammer, according to the rules of my art. The dingle resounded with my strokes. Belle sat still, and occasionally smiled, but suddenly started up, and retreated towards her encampment, on a spark which I purposely sent in her direction alighting on her knee. I found the making of a linchpin no easy matter; it was, however, less difficult than the fabrication of a pony-shoe; my work, indeed, was much facilitated by my having another pin to look at. In about three-quarters of an hour I had succeeded tolerably well, and had produced a linchpin which I thought would serve. During all this time, notwithstanding the noise which I was making, the postillion never showed his face. His nonappearance at first alarmed me: I was afraid he might be dead, but, on looking into the tent, I found him still buried in the soundest sleep. “He must surely be descended from one of the seven sleepers,” said I, as I turned away, and resumed my work.

天刚破晓我便醒了，丢下还酣睡的驿车夫，跨出帐篷。谷地里潮气沉沉，露水滴沥。我生了炭火，把锻炉收拾停当，随后上到地里——那辆轻便马车还照昨晚的样子停在那儿。先看了看车旁那块雷石，如今已经冷了，裂成三片；我便细细检查车轮和轮轴。轮轴没有伤着要紧处，轮子据我看来也还结实，只是轴壳微有损伤。要叫马车重新上路，看来只缺一枚轴销，我便决定自己打一枚。走到另一边那只轮子跟前，我取出那枚轴销，带下谷地来，权当样儿。
这时贝尔已经穿戴齐整，坐在锻炉旁边。我朝她略一点头——就像一个心里装着要紧事的人，碰见熟人时给的那种点头——随即动手干活。我拣了一块看得合用的铁，放进火里，狠狠拉动风箱，很快把它烧红；再用钳子夹出，搁上铁砧，按着我这门手艺的规矩抡锤敲打。谷地间回荡着我的锤声。贝尔安坐不动，时时微笑；忽有一颗火星——是我故意朝她那边扇去的——落在她膝上，她爳地跳起，退回她的营帐去了。我发觉打一枚轴销委实不易；不过总比打小马蹄铁容易些；何况手边有一枚现成的比着，活计省力多了。约莫三刻钟，我干得总算不坏，打成了一枚看来使得的轴销。这半天工夫，任凭我闹出多大动静，驿车夫始终没露面。起初我不免着慌：只怕他死了；往帐篷里一看，他还埋在最沉的睡里。「他准是七个睡眠者的后代，」我转身继续干活时说。

My work finished, I took a little oil, leather and sand, and polished the pin as well as I could; then, summoning Belle, we both went to the chaise, where, with her assistance, I put on the wheel. The linchpin which I had made fitted its place very well, and having replaced the other, I gazed at the chaise for some time with my heart full of that satisfaction which results from the consciousness of having achieved a great action; then, after looking at Belle in the hope of obtaining a compliment from her lips, which did not come, I returned to the dingle, without saying a word, followed by her. Belle set about making preparations for breakfast; and I taking the kettle went and filled it at the spring. Having hung it over the fire, I went to the tent in which the postillion was still sleeping, and called upon him to arise. He awoke with a start, and stared around him at first with the utmost surprise, not unmixed, I could observe, with a certain degree of fear. At last, looking in my face, he appeared to recollect himself. “I had quite forgot,” said he, as he got up, “where I was, and all that happened yesterday. However, I remember now the whole affair, thunderstorm, thunderbolt, frightened horses, and all your kindness. Come, I must see after my coach and horses; I hope we shall be able to repair the damage.” “The damage is already quite repaired,” said I, “as you will see, if you come to the field above.” “You don’t say so,” said the postillion, coming out of the tent; “well, I am mightily beholden to you. Good morning, young gentlewoman,” said he, addressing Belle, who, having finished her preparations was seated near the fire. “Good morning, young man,” said Belle, “I suppose you would be glad of some breakfast; however, you must wait a little, the kettle does not boil.” “Come and look at your chaise,” said I; “but tell me how it happened that the noise which I have been making did not awake you; for three-quarters of an hour at least I was hammering close at your ear.” “I heard you all the time,” said the postillion, “but your hammering made me sleep all the sounder; I am used to hear hammering in my morning sleep.

活计完毕，我取了点油、皮革和砂子，把轴销尽量打磨光亮；然后唤上贝尔，两人一同到马车跟前，由她搭手，我把轮子装了上去。我打的那枚轴销安在位子上正合适；把另一枚也照旧装好，我对着马车端详了好一阵，满心都是干成一桩大事之后的那种惬意；随后指望贝尔嘴里赏我一句好话，看了看她——好话没有来——我便一言不发回到谷地，她跟在后头。贝尔动手预备早饭；我提了水壶，去泉边灌水。水壶挂上炉火，我走进驿车夫还在酣睡的帐篷，唤他起身。他惊醒过来，先朝四下里直瞪，一脸的诧异，我瞧得分明，诧异里还掺着几分惧怕。末了他望着我的脸，像是记起了自己。「我全忘了，」他起身说，「忘了这是在哪儿，昨天出的事也全忘了。不过如今全想起来了——雷雨、霹雷、惊马，还有你的一番好意。来，我得去看看我的车和马；但愿损处还修得好。」「损处早已修好了，」我说，「你到上头地里一看便知。」「不会吧，」驿车夫说着走出帐篷；「哎呀，太叫我不安了。早安，年轻的太太，」他对贝尔说——她已备好早饭，坐在火边。「早安，年轻人，」贝尔说，「我料你想吃早饭了；不过还得稍等，水还没开呢。」「来看看你的车吧，」我说；「只是先告诉我，我闹出那么大动静，怎么竟没吵醒你？我至少有整整三刻钟，在你耳边抡锤子。」「你敲你的，我全听见了，」驿车夫说，「可你越敲我睡得越沉；我早晨睡觉，听惯了打铁声。

There’s a forge close by the room where I sleep when I’m at home, at my inn; for we have all kinds of conveniences at my inn﻿—forge, carpenter’s shop, and wheelwright’s﻿—so that when I heard you hammering I thought, no doubt, that it was the old noise, and that I was comfortable in my bed at my own inn.” We now ascended to the field, where I showed the postillion his chaise. He looked at the pin attentively, rubbed his hands, and gave a loud laugh. “Is it not well done?” said I. “It will do till I get home,” he replied. “And that is all you have to say?” I demanded. “And that’s a good deal,” said he, “considering who made it. But don’t be offended,” he added, “I shall prize it all the more for its being made by a gentleman, and no blacksmith; and so will my governor, when I show it to him. I shan’t let it remain where it is, but will keep it, as a remembrance of you, as long as I live.” He then again rubbed his hands with great glee, and said: “I will now go and see after my horses, and then to breakfast, partner, if you please.” Suddenly, however, looking at his hands, he said, “Before sitting down to breakfast I am in the habit of washing my hands and face; I suppose you could not furnish me with a little soap and water.” “As much water as you please,” said I, “but if you want soap, I must go and trouble the young gentlewoman for some.” “By no means,” said the postillion, “water will do at a pinch.” “Follow me,” said I, and leading him to the pond of the frogs and newts, I said, “this is my ewer; you are welcome to part of it﻿—the water is so soft that it is scarcely necessary to add soap to it;” then lying down on the bank, I plunged my head into the water, then scrubbed my hands and face, and afterwards wiped them with some long grass which grew on the margin of the pond. “Bravo,” said the postillion, “I see you know how to make a shift:” he then followed my example, declared he never felt more refreshed in his life, and, giving a bound, said, “he would go and look after his horses.”
We then went to look after the horses, which we found not much the worse for having spent the night in the open air. My companion again inserted their heads in the corn-bags, and, leaving the animals to discuss their corn, returned with me to the dingle, where we found the kettle boiling. We sat down, and Belle made tea, and did the honours of the meal.

我家客栈里，我睡觉那间屋子旁边就有一座锻炉；我们客栈里各样家伙齐全——锻炉、木匠铺、修车铺都有；所以听见你打铁，我只当还是那老一套，只当自家舒舒服服躺在客栈自己床上呢。」我们于是一同上到地里，我把马车指给驿车夫看。他对着那枚轴销细细端详，搓了搓手，放声大笑。「打得不好么？」我说。「撑到我到家总够了，」他答。「你就说这一句？」我追问。「这一句也就不少了，」他说，「想想打它的人是谁。不过别见怪，」他添了一句，「它是位绅士打的，不是铁匠打的，我反倒越发要宝贝它；等我拿给我们掌柜的看，掌柜的也要宝贝。我不打算让它留在原处，要收着它，当个纪念——记着你——一辈子。」于是他又欢欢喜喜搓着手说：「我这就去看马，看完回来吃早饭，伙计，要是你赏脸。」可他忽然看了看自己的手，说：「坐下吃早饭之前，我照例要洗一洗手脸；你这儿兴许能给我弄点肥皂和水吧。」「水要多少有多少，」我说，「肥皂么，就得上那边麻烦那位年轻的太太了。」「那倒不必，」驿车夫说，「凑合着水也行。」「跟我来，」我说着，把他领到那口青蛙蝾螈的池子边，「这就是我的水盂；欢迎你用一部分——这水软得很，简直用不着加肥皂。」说着我趴在岸上，一头扎进水里，然后搓洗手脸，再拿池边长着的长草擦干。「好样儿的，」驿车夫说，「我看你是将就的行家。」他跟着照做了一遍，声称平生没这么爽快过，纵身一跳，说「这就去看马」。
我们便去看马；两匹牲口露天过了一夜，并没有大伤。我的同伴又把它们的头塞进谷袋，撇下它们去嚼谷子，随我回到谷地，水壶正开。我们坐下，贝尔沏了茶，尽了主人之谊。

The postillion was in high spirits, ate heartily, and, to Belle’s evident satisfaction, declared that he had never drank better tea in his life, or indeed any half so good. Breakfast over, he said that he must now go and harness his horses, as it was high time for him to return to his inn. Belle gave him her hand and wished him farewell. The postillion shook her hand warmly, and was advancing close up to her﻿—for what purpose I cannot say﻿—whereupon Belle, withdrawing her hand, drew herself up with an air which caused the postillion to retreat a step or two with an exceedingly sheepish look. Recovering himself, however, he made a low bow, and proceeded up the path. I attended him, and helped to harness his horses and put them to the vehicle; he then shook me by the hand, and taking the reins and whip mounted to his seat; ere he drove away he thus addressed me: “If ever I forget your kindness and that of the young woman below, dash my buttons. If ever either of you should enter my inn you may depend upon a warm welcome, the best that can be set before you, and no expense to either, for I will give both of you the best of characters to the governor, who is the very best fellow upon all the road. As for your linchpin, I trust it will serve till I get home, when I will take it out and keep it in remembrance of you all the days of my life:” then giving the horses a jerk with his reins, he cracked his whip and drove off.
I returned to the dingle, Belle had removed the breakfast things, and was busy in her own encampment. Nothing occurred, worthy of being related, for two hours, at the end of which time Belle departed on a short expedition, and I again found myself alone in the dingle.

驿车夫兴头极高，吃得香甜，还声称平生没喝过这么好的茶——连一半这么好的也没喝过，这话叫贝尔听着分明受用。早饭吃罢，他说该去套马了，回客栈的时辰早到了。贝尔伸手同他道别。驿车夫热乎乎握住她的手，还朝她凑近前去——为着什么目的我说不上——贝尔随即抽回手，把身子一挺，那神气叫驿车夫倒退了一两步，一脸的讪讪。不过他随即定住神，深深一躬，顺小径上去了。我陪着他，帮着套马、把车扣上；他同我握手，接过缰绳和鞭子，登上了座；临行前他对我这样说道：「我要是忘了你的恩、忘了底下这位年轻女人的恩，叫我的纽扣全崩掉。你们俩谁要是进我那家客栈，管保热热闹闹迎接你，摆上头等的好菜，一个钱也不用花——我自会在掌柜的面前替你们两个说顶好的口碑，掌柜的是这一路顶好的家伙。至于你那枚轴销，我信它撑得到家；到家我就把它卸下来，收着纪念你，一辈子收着。」说罢缰绳一抖，鞭子一甩，赶车去了。
我回到谷地。贝尔已收拾了早饭的杯盘，在自己的营帐里忙。足足两个钟头，没有一件事值得记载；之后贝尔出门办一趟短差，谷地里又只剩我一个人了。
