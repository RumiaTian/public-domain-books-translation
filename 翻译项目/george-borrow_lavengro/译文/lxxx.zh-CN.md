### LXXX / 第八十章
===Original===

We were now drawing very near the hills, and Peter said, “If you are to go into Wales, you must presently decide, for we are close upon the border.”
“Which is the border?” said I.
“Yon small brook,” said Peter, “into which the man on horseback, who is coming towards us, is now entering.”
“I see it,” said I, “and the man; he stops in the middle of it, as if to water his steed.”
We proceeded till we had nearly reached the brook. “Well,” said Peter, “will you go into Wales?”
“What should I do in Wales?” I demanded.
“Do!” said Peter, smiling, “learn Welsh.”
I stopped my little pony. “Then I need not go into Wales; I already know Welsh.”
“Know Welsh!” said Peter, staring at me.
“Know Welsh!” said Winifred, stopping her cart.
“How and when did you learn it?” said Peter.
“From books, in my boyhood.”
“Read Welsh!” said Peter, “is it possible?”
“Read Welsh!” said Winifred, “is it possible?”
“Well, I hope you will come with us,” said Peter.
“Come with us, young man,” said Winifred; “let me, on the other side of the brook, welcome you into Wales.”
“Thank you both,” said I, “but I will not come.”
“Wherefore?” exclaimed both simultaneously.
“Because it is neither fit nor proper that I cross into Wales at this time, and in this manner. When I go into Wales, I should wish to go in a new suit of superfine black, with hat and beaver, mounted on a powerful steed, black and glossy, like that which bore Greduv to the fight of Catraeth.197 I should wish, moreover, to see the Welshmen assembled on the border ready to welcome me with pipe and fiddle, and much whooping and shouting, and to attend me to Wrexham, or even as far as Machynllaith, where I should wish to be invited to a dinner at which all the bards should be present, and to be seated at the right hand of the president, who, when the cloth was removed, should arise, and, amidst cries of silence, exclaim﻿—‘Brethren and Welshmen, allow me to propose the health of my most respectable friend the translator of the odes of the great Ab Gwilym, the pride and glory of Wales.’ ”
“How!” said Peter; “hast thou translated the works of the mighty Dafydd?”
“With notes critical, historical and explanatory.”
“Come with us, friend,” said Peter. “I cannot promise such a dinner as thou wishest, but neither pipe nor fiddle shall be wanting.”

我们这时已离群山很近，彼得说：「你若要进威尔士，眼下就得拿定主意，咱们已经快到边界了。」
「边界在哪儿？」我说。
「就是前头那条小溪，」彼得说，「迎面骑马过来的那个人，这会儿正策马蹚进溪里去。」
「看见了，」我说，「溪看见了，那人也看见了；他停在溪当中，像是要让马饮水。」
我们接着往前走，走到差不多挨着溪边。「怎么样，」彼得说，「你到底进不进威尔士？」
「我到威尔士去做什么呢？」我问。
「做什么！」彼得微笑道，「学威尔士语呀。」
我勒住我的小马。「那我就不必进威尔士了；我早就懂威尔士语了。」
「懂威尔士语！」彼得瞪着我说。
「懂威尔士语！」温妮弗雷德说着，停下了车。
「你怎么学的？什么时候学的？」彼得问。
「从书上学来的，小时候。」
「能读威尔士语！」彼得说，「这可当真？」
「能读威尔士语！」温妮弗雷德说，「这可当真？」
「唉，我还是盼你跟我们一起走吧。」彼得说。
「跟我们来吧，年轻人，」温妮弗雷德说，「让我在溪对岸，迎你进威尔士。」
「多谢你们两位，」我说，「可我不去。」
「为什么？」两人同时喊道。
「因为这个时候、这副模样渡进威尔士，既不合宜也不像话。我进威尔士的那天，指望穿一身崭新的上等细呢黑衣，头戴一顶海狸皮帽，骑一匹雄骏的黑马，毛色乌亮，就像当年驮着格勒杜夫去赴卡特拉斯之战197的那匹马。我还指望看见威尔士人齐聚在边界上，备好风笛提琴迎候我，欢呼声、喊叫声闹成一片，一路随我到雷克瑟姆，甚至远到马汉莱思；在那里，我指望有人请我赴宴，满座宾客尽是吟游诗人，请我坐在会长右首；待到撤下桌布，会长便起身，在一片肃静的呼喊声中高声道：『弟兄们，威尔士的同胞们，请允许我为我最敬重的朋友举杯祝酒——伟人阿布·格威利姆颂歌的译者，威尔士的荣光与骄傲。』」
「哎呀！」彼得说；「汝把伟大的达菲德之作译出来了？」
「附有批评、史事与解说诸注。」
「跟我们走吧，朋友，」彼得说。「你盼望的那样一场宴席，我许不了你，可风笛和提琴是断不会少的。」

“Come with us, young man,” said Winifred, “even as thou art, and the daughters of Wales shall bid thee welcome.”
“I will not go with you,” said I. “Dost thou see that man in the ford?”
“Who is staring at us so, and whose horse has not yet done drinking? Of course I see him.”
“I shall turn back with him. God bless you!”
“Go back with him not,” said Peter, “he is one of those whom I like not, one of the clibberty-clabber, as Master Ellis Wyn observes﻿—turn not with that man.”
“Go not back with him,” said Winifred. “If thou goest with that man, thou wilt soon forget all our profitable counsels; come with us.”
“I cannot; I have much to say to him. *Kosko Divvus*, Mr. Petulengro.”
“*Kosko Divvus, Pal*,” said Mr. Petulengro, riding through the water; “are you turning back?”
I turned back with Mr. Petulengro.
Peter came running after me: “One moment, young man, who and what are you?”
“I must answer in the words of Taliesin,” said I; “none can say with positiveness whether I be fish or flesh,198 least of all myself. God bless you both!”
“Take this,” said Peter; and he thrust his Welsh Bible199 into my hand.

「跟我们一起来吧，年轻人，」温妮弗雷德说，「就照你这身装束来也无妨，威尔士的儿女们自会欢迎你。」
「我不跟你们去，」我说。「你看见浅滩里那个人了么？」
「就是直勾勾盯着我们、马还没饮够水的那位？当然看见了。」
「我要跟他往回走。愿上帝保佑你们！」
「别跟他往回走，」彼得说，「他是我瞧不上眼的那号人，正像埃利斯·温师傅讲的，是个喋喋不休的碎嘴子——别跟那个人走。」
「别跟他回去，」温妮弗雷德说，「你若跟了那个人去，不消多久就会把我们有益的教诲忘个干净；跟我们走吧。」
「我不能去；我有许多话要同他讲。*Kosko Divvus*，彼得伦格罗先生。」
「*Kosko Divvus, Pal*，」彼得伦格罗先生策马蹚过溪水，说道，「你要往回走么？」
我便同彼得伦格罗先生掉头往回走了。
彼得追了上来：「且慢，年轻人，你是何人，干的什么营生？」
「我只好拿塔利埃辛的话来回答，」我说，「我是鱼还是肉198，谁也断言不了，顶顶断言不了的是我自己。愿上帝保佑你们两位！」
「拿着这个，」彼得说着，把他那本威尔士语《圣经》199塞进我手里。
