## II / 第二章

===Original===
"It is one fact, sir, that I am a widow," she said. "It is another fact, that I am going to be married again."

There she paused, and smiled at some thought that occurred to her. Doctor Wybrow was not favourably impressed by her smile—there was something at once sad and cruel in it. It came slowly, and it went away suddenly. He began to doubt whether he had been wise in acting on his first impression. His mind reverted to the commonplace patients and the discoverable maladies that were waiting for him, with a certain tender regret.

The lady went on.

===Chinese===
"先生，一个事实是，我是寡妇，"她说。"另一个事实是，我即将再次结婚。"

她停顿了一下，想到什么似地微微一笑。怀布罗医生（Doctor Wybrow）并不喜欢她的笑容——那笑容里既含悲伤又带残忍。它来得缓慢，去得突然。他开始怀疑自己凭第一印象行事是否明智。他不由自主地想起那些寻常的病人和可以确诊的病症正在候诊室等着他，心中涌起一丝温柔的惋惜。

那位女士继续说了下去。

===Original===
"My approaching marriage," she said, "has one embarrassing circumstance connected with it. The gentleman whose wife I am to be, was engaged to another lady when he happened to meet with me, abroad: that lady, mind, being of his own blood and family, related to him as his cousin. I have innocently robbed her of her lover, and destroyed her prospects in life. Innocently, I say—because he told me nothing of his engagement until after I had accepted him. When we next met in England—and when there was danger, no doubt, of the affair coming to my knowledge—he told me the truth. I was naturally indignant. He had his excuse ready; he showed me a letter from the lady herself, releasing him from his engagement. A more noble, a more high-minded letter, I never read in my life. I cried over it—I who have no tears in me for sorrows of my own! If the letter had left him any hope of being forgiven, I would have positively refused to marry him. But the firmness of it—without anger, without a word of reproach, with heartfelt wishes even for his happiness—the firmness of it, I say, left him no hope. He appealed to my compassion; he appealed to his love for me. You know what women are. I too was softhearted—I said, Very well: yes! In a week more (I tremble as I think of it) we are to be married."

===Chinese===
"我即将到来的婚姻，"她说，"附带着一桩尴尬的事。即将成为我丈夫的那位先生，在国外遇见我时，已经与另一位女士订了婚——请注意，那位女士是他自己的血脉至亲，是他的表妹。我无意中夺走了她的爱人，毁掉了她的人生前程。我说无意，是因为直到我接受了他之后，他才告诉我订婚的事。我们后来在英国重逢——毫无疑问，那时事情已经瞒不住了——他才向我坦白。我自然十分愤怒。他早已备好了辩解的理由；他拿出那位女士本人写的一封信，解除与他的婚约。我这辈子从未读过比那更崇高、更高尚的信。我读着信哭了——我这个人连自己的悲伤都不曾掉过一滴眼泪！如果那封信还留给他任何被宽恕的可能，我一定会断然拒绝嫁给他。但那封信的坚定——没有愤怒，没有一句责备，甚至由衷地祝愿他幸福——那种坚定，我说，让他彻底断了念想。他恳求我的怜悯；他诉说对我的爱。你知道女人是什么样的。我也是心软的人——我说，好吧：我答应！再过一周（想到这个我就浑身发抖）我们就要结婚了。"

===Original===
She did really tremble—she was obliged to pause and compose herself, before she could go on. The doctor, waiting for more facts, began to fear that he stood committed to a long story. "Forgive me for reminding you that I have suffering persons waiting to see me," he said. "The sooner you can come to the point, the better for my patients and for me."

The strange smile—at once so sad and so cruel—showed itself again on the lady's lips. "Every word I have said is to the point," she answered. "You will see it yourself in a moment more."

She resumed her narrative.

===Chinese===
她确实浑身发抖——她不得不停下来镇定了一下，才能继续说下去。医生等着听更多的事实，开始担心自己被卷入了一个冗长的故事。"请原谅我提醒您，还有痛苦的病人在等我，"他说。"您越快切入正题，对我的病人和对我都越好。"

那个奇怪的笑容——既悲伤又残忍——再次出现在那位女士的唇边。"我说的每一句话都切题，"她答道。"您马上就会明白的。"

她继续讲述她的故事。

===Original===
"Yesterday—you need fear no long story, sir; only yesterday—I was among the visitors at one of your English luncheon parties. A lady, a perfect stranger to me, came in late—after we had left the table, and had retired to the drawing-room. She happened to take a chair near me; and we were presented to each other. I knew her by name, as she knew me. It was the woman whom I had robbed of her lover, the woman who had written the noble letter. Now listen! You were impatient with me for not interesting you in what I said just now. I said it to satisfy your mind that I had no enmity of feeling towards the lady, on my side. I admired her, I felt for her—I had no cause to reproach myself. This is very important, as you will presently see. On her side, I have reason to be assured that the circumstances had been truly explained to her, and that she understood I was in no way to blame. Now, knowing all these necessary things as you do, explain to me, if you can, why, when I rose and met that woman's eyes looking at me, I turned cold from head to foot, and shuddered, and shivered, and knew what a deadly panic of fear was, for the first time in my life."

===Chinese===
"昨天——您不必担心我会讲太久，先生；就在昨天——我参加了你们英国的一次午宴。一位女士，我完全不认识的陌生人，来得很晚——在我们已经离席、回到客厅之后。她恰好在我旁边坐下；我们被互相介绍。我知道她的名字，她也知道我的名字。她就是那个被我夺走爱人的女人，就是写了那封高尚信件的女人。现在请听好！刚才您对我的话不耐烦，觉得我说了太多无关紧要的事。我说那些话，是为了让您放心，我对那位女士并无任何敌意。我钦佩她，我同情她——我没有任何理由自责。这一点非常重要，您马上就会明白。至于她那边，我有理由相信事情已经向她如实解释清楚了，她也理解我没有任何过错。现在，您既然已经知道了这些必要的前提，就请告诉我——如果您能的话——为什么当我站起来迎上那个女人的目光时，我从头到脚一阵冰凉，浑身战栗、颤抖，生平第一次体会到了那种致命的恐惧感。"

===Original===
The doctor began to feel interested at last.

"Was there anything remarkable in the lady's personal appearance?" he asked.

"Nothing whatever!" was the vehement reply. "Here is the true description of her:—The ordinary English lady; the clear cold blue eyes, the fine rosy complexion, the inanimately polite manner, the large good-humoured mouth, the too plump cheeks and chin: these, and nothing more."

"Was there anything in her expression, when you first looked at her, that took you by surprise?"

===Chinese===
医生终于开始感到兴趣了。

"那位女士的外表有什么特别之处吗？"他问。

"没有任何特别之处！"她激烈地答道。"她的真实样貌是这样的：一个普通的英国女士；清澈冷漠的蓝眼睛，细腻红润的肤色，彬彬有礼却毫无生气的举止，宽大温和的嘴巴，过于丰满的脸颊和下巴：就是这些，没有别的。"

"您第一次看她时，她的表情有什么让您感到意外的地方吗？"

"There was natural curiosity to see the woman who had been preferred to her; and perhaps some astonishment also, not to see a more engaging and more beautiful person; both those feelings restrained within the limits of good breeding, and both not lasting for more than a few moments—so far as I could see. I say, 'so far,' because the horrible agitation that she communicated to me disturbed my judgment. If I could have got to the door, I would have run out of the room, she frightened me so! I was not even able to stand up—I sank back in my chair; I stared horror-struck at the calm blue eyes that were only looking at me with a gentle surprise. I tell you my impression, in all its horror and in all its folly! That woman is destined (without knowing it herself) to be the evil genius of my life. Her innocent eyes saw hidden capabilities of wickedness in me that I was not aware of myself, until I felt them stirring under her look. If I commit faults in my life to come—if I am even guilty of crimes—she will bring the retribution, without (as I firmly believe) any conscious exercise of her own will. In one indescribable moment I felt all this—and I suppose my face showed it. The good artless creature was inspired by a sort of gentle alarm for me. I consented to let the wedding take place as we had arranged it—and left him. The night has passed. ===Original===
Doctor Wybrow rose from his chair, determined to close the interview.

He was strongly and painfully impressed by what he had heard. The longer he had listened to her, the more irresistibly the conviction of the woman's wickedness had forced itself on him. He tried vainly to think of her as a person to be pitied—a person with a morbidly sensitive imagination, conscious of the capacities for evil which lie dormant in us all, and striving earnestly to open her heart to the counter-influence of her own better nature; the effort was beyond him. A perverse instinct in him said, as if in words, Beware how you believe in her!

怀布罗医生从椅子上站起来，决定结束这次谈话。

She put a little paper packet of money on the table. "Thank you, sir. There is your fee."

With those words she rose. Her wild black eyes looked upward, with an expression of despair so defiant and so horrible in its silent agony that the doctor turned away his head, unable to endure the sight of it. The bare idea of taking anything from her—not money only, but anything even that she had touched—suddenly revolted him. Still without looking at her, he said, "Take it back; I don't want my fee."

She neither heeded nor heard him. Still looking upward, she said slowly to herself, "Let the end come. I have done with the struggle: I submit."

She drew her veil over her face, bowed to the doctor, and left the room.

仆人已经回来了。怀布罗医生羞于开口问他。仆人不等主人发问，就报告了差事的结果。

"那位女士名叫纳罗娜伯爵夫人（the Countess Narona）。她住在——"

医生没等听完她住在何处，便以一个无声的点头认可了这个至关重要的发现——她的名字，然后走进了诊室。他徒劳拒绝的诊费仍然放在桌上那个白色小纸包里。他把纸包封进一个信封；写上地址寄往最近治安法庭的"济贫箱"；然后叫来仆人，吩咐他明早把信封交给治安法官。仆人忠于职守，等着问出那个惯例问题："先生，您今天在家用晚餐吗？"

他犹豫了片刻，说："不：我要去俱乐部用餐。"

所有道德品质中最容易变质的，就是所谓的"良心"。在一个人精神状态的某种模式下，他的良心是对他最严厉的审判官；在另一种模式下，他与他的良心则以共犯的身份相处得无比融洽。当怀布罗医生第二次走出家门时，他甚至不想对自己隐瞒：他去俱乐部用餐的唯一目的，就是打听世人对纳罗娜伯爵夫人的评价。
