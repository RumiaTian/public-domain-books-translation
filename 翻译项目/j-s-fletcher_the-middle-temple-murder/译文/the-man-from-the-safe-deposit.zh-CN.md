## VIII The Man from the Safe Deposit / 第八章 保险寄存库来的人

===Original===
Spargo found Rathbury sitting alone in a small, somewhat dismal apartment which was chiefly remarkable for the businesslike paucity of its furnishings and its indefinable air of secrecy. There was a plain writing-table and a hard chair or two; a map of London, much discoloured, on the wall; a few faded photographs of eminent bands in the world of crime, and a similar number of well-thumbed books of reference. The detective himself, when Spargo was shown in to him, was seated at the table, chewing an unlighted cigar, and engaged in the apparently aimless task of drawing hieroglyphics on scraps of paper. He looked up as the journalist entered, and held out his hand.

“Well, I congratulate you on what you stuck in the *Watchman* this morning,” he said. “Made extra good reading, I thought. They did right to let you tackle that job. Going straight through with it now, I suppose, Mr. Spargo?”

Spargo dropped into the chair nearest to Rathbury’s right hand. He lighted a cigarette, and having blown out a whiff of smoke, nodded his head in a fashion which indicated that the detective might consider his question answered in the affirmative.

“Look here,” he said. “We settled yesterday, didn’t we, that you and I are to consider ourselves partners, as it were, in this job? That’s all right,” he continued, as Rathbury nodded very quietly. “Very well﻿—have you made any further progress?”

“要说同本案的关系，那可是实实在在的，”迈尔斯特先生答道。“是的，我断然可以这么说。事实是：6月21日大约——说得确切些——下午三点钟，一位陌生客人光临敝处，报的姓名是约翰·马伯里，现居地址是滑铁卢盎格鲁-东方旅馆，问能否租用一口小型保险箱。他向我说明，想存进保险箱的，是他随身带来的一只小皮盒——顺带一句，那盒子的模样古旧得出奇。我领他看了一口合乎他要求的保险箱，告知了租金和本处的各项规矩，他便租下了那口箱子，预付了一年租金，当场把那只皮盒——约莫一英尺见方的一件物事——存了进去。此后，双方就伦敦今昔之变寒暄了一两句——据我理解，他的意思是，伦敦他已有许多许多年没见着了——然后他取了钥匙，告辞而去。我想，此人就是那位后来遇害的马伯里先生，这一点毋庸置疑。”
