## To the Reader / 致读者

===Original===
This book is not a novel, but rather the confession of a free spirit telling of its mistakes, its sufferings and its struggles from the midst of the tempest; and it is in no sense an autobiography either. Some day I may wish to write of myself, and I will then speak without any disguise or feigned name. Though it is true that I have lent some ideas to my hero, his individuality, his character and the circumstances of his life are all his own; and I have tried to give a picture of the inward labyrinth where a weak spirit wanders, feeling its way, uncertain, sensitive and impressionable, but sincere and ardent in the cause of truth.

===Chinese===
本书不是一部小说，而是一个自由灵魂的忏悔——他在风暴的中心，诉说着自己的迷误、苦难与挣扎；它也绝非一部自传。有朝一日我若想写写自己，到那时，我会不加任何掩饰、不用任何假名，坦然直言。诚然，我曾把一些思想借给我的主人公，但他的个性、他的品格、他一生的境遇，全然是他自己的；我所力图描绘的，是一座内心的迷宫——一个软弱的灵魂在其中摸索徘徊，犹疑不定，敏感而易受撼动，然而在真理的事业上，却真诚而炽热。

===Original===
Some chapters of the book have a family likeness to the meditations of our old French moralists and the stoical essays of the end of the sixteenth century. At a time resembling our own but even exceeding it in tragic horror, amid the convulsions of the League, the Chief-Magistrate Guillaume Du Vair wrote his noble Dialogues, *De la Constance et Consolation ès Calamités Publiques*, with a steadfast mind. While the siege of Paris was at its worst he talked in his garden with his friends, Linus the great traveller, Musée, Dean of the Faculty of Medicine, and the writer Orphée. Poor wretches lay dead of starvation in the streets, women cried out that pike-men were eating children near the Temple; but with their eyes filled with these horrible pictures these wise men sought to raise their unhappy thoughts to the heights where one can reach the mind of the ages and reckon up that which has survived the test. As I reread these Dialogues during the war I more than once felt myself close to that true Frenchman who wrote: Man is born to see and know everything, and it is an injustice to limit him to one place on the earth. To the wise man the whole world is his country. God lends us the world to enjoy in common on one condition only, that we act uprightly.

R. R.

Paris, May, 1920

===Chinese===
本书有若干章节，与我们古老的法兰西道德家们的沉思、与十六世纪末那些斯多噶式的论说，颇有家族的相似。那是一个同我们这个时代相似、而惨祸之烈犹有过之的年代；在天主教同盟（the League）的痉挛动荡之中，首席法官纪尧姆·迪·韦尔（Guillaume Du Vair）怀着一颗坚定不移的心，写下了他那高贵的《对话录》——《论公共灾祸中的坚忍与慰藉》（De la Constance et Consolation ès Calamités Publiques）。巴黎围城最惨酷之际，他仍在自家园中与友朋倾谈：伟大的旅行家利诺斯（Linus）、医学院院长缪赛俄斯（Musée），还有作家俄耳甫斯（Orphée）。可怜的人们饿死在街头，妇人们哭喊着说，长矛兵正在圣殿塔（the Temple）近旁吃孩子；然而，纵使满眼皆是这些骇人的景象，这些智者仍力求将自己不幸的思绪，提升到那样的高处——在那里，人得以触及历代的心灵，掂量出经得住考验而犹存的一切。战争期间重读这部《对话录》，我不止一次感到自己与那位真正的法国人心意相通；他写道：「人生在世，本为观看并知晓万事万物，把他拘囿于大地一隅，乃是不义。对智者来说，整个世界都是他的祖国。上帝把世界借给我们，供我们共同享有，条件只有一条：行事正直。」

R. R.

巴黎，1920 年 5 月
