## VI / 六

===Original===
The Peary plan﻿—A rain of rocks﻿—My friends the Eskimos.

The story of the winter at Cape Sheridan is a story unique in the experience of Arctic exploration. Usually it is the rule to hibernate as much as possible during the period of darkness, and the party is confined closely to headquarters. The Peary plan is different; and constant activity and travel were insisted on.

There were very few days when all of the members of the expedition were together, after the ship had reached her destination. Hunting parties were immediately sent out, for it was on the big game of the country that the expedition depended for fresh meat. Professor Marvin commenced his scientific work, and his several stations were all remote from headquarters; and all winter long, parties were sledging provisions, equipment, etc., to Cape Columbia, ninety-three miles northwest, in anticipation of the journey to the Pole. Those who remained at headquarters did not find life an idle dream. There was something in the way of work going on all of the time. I was away from the ship on two hunting trips of about ten days each, and while at headquarters, I shaped and built over two dozen sledges, besides doing lots of other work.

===Chinese===
皮尔里的方案——一阵石雨——我的因纽特朋友们。

在谢里登角（Cape Sheridan）越冬的这段故事，在北极探险的经历中是独一无二的。通常的规矩是：黑暗时节尽量蛰伏冬眠，全队紧守大本营。皮尔里的方案却另是一套，他坚持要大家不断地活动、不断地赶路。

船抵达目的地之后，全队人马聚在一处的日子屈指可数。狩猎队随即派出，因为探险队的新鲜肉食，全指望这片地方的大猎物。马文教授（Professor Marvin）着手开展他的科学工作，他设的几个观测站都远离大本营；整个冬天，各支小队都在往西北九十三英里外的哥伦比亚角（Cape Columbia）雪运口粮、装备等物，为奔赴北极点的旅程预作准备。留守大本营的人也并没有把日子过成一场清闲的梦，总有些这样那样的活计在进行着。我两次离船外出狩猎，每次约莫十天；留在营地里的时候，我打造了二十多架雪橇，此外还干了许许多多别的活。

Naturally there were frequent storms and intense cold, and in regard to the storms of the Arctic regions of North Greenland and Grant Land, the only word I can use to describe them is “terrible,” in the fullest meaning it conveys. The effect of such storms of wind and snow, or rain, is abject physical terror, due to the realization of perfect helplessness. I have seen rocks a hundred and a hundred and fifty pounds in weight picked up by the storm and blown for distances of ninety or a hundred feet to the edge of a precipice, and there of their own momentum go hurtling through space to fall in crashing fragments at the base. Imagine the effect of such a rainfall of death-dealing boulders on the feelings of a little group of three or four, who have sought the base of the cliff for shelter. I have been there and I have seen one of my Eskimo companions felled by a blow from a rock eighty-four pounds in weight, which struck him fairly between the shoulder-blades, literally knocking the life out of him. I have been there, and believe me, I have been afraid. A hundred-pound box of supplies, taking an aerial joy ride, during the progress of a storm down at Anniversary Lodge in 1894, struck Commander Peary a glancing blow which put him out of commission for over a week. These mighty winds make it possible for the herbivorous animals of this region to exist. They sweep the snow from vast stretches of land, exposing the hay and dried dwarf-willows, that the hare, musk-oxen, and reindeer feed on.

The Eskimo families who came north to Cape Sheridan with us on the *Roosevelt* found life much more ideal than down in their native land. It was a pleasure trip for them, with nothing to worry about, and everything provided. Some of the families lived aboard ship all through the winter, and some in the box-house on shore. They were perforce much cleaner in their personal habits than they were wont to be in their own home country, but never for an instant does the odor or appearance of an Eskimo’s habitation suggest the rose or geranium. The aroma of an East Side lunchroom is more like it.

风暴频繁、酷寒凛冽，自是意料中事。至于北格陵兰（North Greenland）和格兰特地（Grant Land）北极地带的风暴，我能用来形容它们的只有一个词：「可怕」（terrible）——而且要取这个词最十足的含义。这种风雪交加或风雨交加的风暴，造成的是一种令人丧胆的肉体恐怖，因为你意识到自己全然无助。我亲眼见过重达一百磅、一百五十磅的岩石被暴风卷起，刮出九十英尺、一百英尺远，直刮到悬崖边上，再凭着自身的冲势呼啸着腾空飞坠，在崖脚摔得粉碎。试想，三四个人的小小一伙，正躲到崖脚下面避风，这样一场由夺命巨石构成的「石雨」落在他们心头，是何等滋味。我到过那种地方，亲眼看见我的一个因纽特同伴被一块八十四磅重的石头砸倒，那石头不偏不倚砸在他两侧肩胛骨之间，当真险些把他的命都砸出来。我到过那种地方，请相信我，我是害怕过的。1894年，在周年小屋（Anniversary Lodge）那边一场风暴正猛时，一只一百磅重的补给箱飞上天去兜了一圈风，擦着皮尔里指挥官（Commander Peary）撞了一下，弄得他一个多星期动弹不得。也正是这些狂烈的劲风，使这一带的食草动物得以生存：它们把大片大片土地上的积雪扫荡一空，露出干草和干枯的矮柳，供野兔、麝牛和驯鹿取食。

随我们乘罗斯福号（the Roosevelt）北上来谢里登角的那些因纽特人家，觉得这里的生活比他们南边老家理想得多。对他们来说，这趟是一趟逍遥的游乐：无事操心，样样现成。有几个人家整个冬天都住在船上，有几家住在岸上的板箱屋里。他们在个人习惯上自然比在本乡本土时干净得多；不过，因纽特人住处的那股气味也好、那副模样也好，无论何时都绝不会让人联想到玫瑰或天竺葵。要说像什么，纽约东区（East Side）小饭馆里的那股味儿倒更贴切些。

There were thirty-nine Eskimos in the expedition, men, women and children; for the Eskimo travels heavy and takes his women and children with him as a matter of course. The women were as useful as the men, and the small boys did the ship’s chores, sledging in fresh water from the lake, etc. They were mostly in families; but there were several young, unmarried men, and the unattached, much-married and divorced Miss “Bill,” who domiciled herself aboard the ship and did much good work with her needle. She was my seamstress and the thick fur clothes worn on the trip to the Pole were sewn by her. The Eskimos lived as happily as in their own country and carried on their domestic affairs with almost the same carefree irregularity as usual. The best-natured people on earth, with no bad habits of their own, but a ready ability to assimilate the vices of civilization. Twenty years ago, when I first met them, not one used tobacco or craved it. Today every member of the tribe has had experience with tobacco, craves it, and will give most everything, except his gun, to get it. Even little toddlers, three and four years old, will eat tobacco and, strange to say, it has no bad effect. They get tobacco from the Danish missionaries and from the sailors on board the whaling, seal, and walrus-ships. Whisky has not yet gotten in its demoralizing work.

It is my conviction that the life of this little tribe is doomed, and that extinction is nearly due. It will be caused partly by themselves, and partly by the misguided endeavors of civilized people. Every year their number diminishes; in 1894, Hugh J. Lee took the census of the tribe, and it numbered two hundred and fifty-three; in 1906, Professor Marvin found them to have dwindled to two hundred and seven. At this writing I dare say their number is still further reduced, for the latest news I have had from the Whale Sound region informs me that quite a number of deaths have occurred, and the birthrate is not high. It is sad to think of the fate of my friends who live in what was once a land of plenty, but which is, through the greed of the commercial hunter, becoming a land of frigid desolation. The seals are practically gone, and the walrus are being quickly exterminated. The reindeer and the musk-oxen are going the same way, for the Eskimos themselves now hunt inland, when, up to twenty years ago, their hunting was confined to the coast and the life-giving sea.

They are very human in their attributes, and in spite of the fact that their diet is practically meat only, their tempers are gentle and mild, and there is a great deal of affection among them. Except between husband and wife, they seldom quarrel; and never hold spite or animosity. Children are a valuable asset, are much loved, never scolded or punished, and are not spoiled. An Eskimo mother washes her baby the same way a cat washes her kittens. There are lots of personal habits the description of which might scatter the reading circle, so I will desist with the bald statement, that, for them, dirt and filth have no terrors.

队里的因纽特人共有三十九口，男女老幼都有；因为因纽特人出门向来是拖家带口，女人孩子随行是理所当然的事。女人同男人一样顶用，小男孩们干船上的杂务，比如用雪橇从湖上把淡水拉回来，等等。他们大多以家庭为单位；不过也有几个年轻未婚的汉子，还有那位无家无累、结过好几回婚又离了婚的「比尔小姐」（Miss Bill）——她在船上安了身，一双手拿针线做了许多好活计。她就是我的裁缝，赴北极点一路上穿的厚毛皮衣裳都是她缝的。这些因纽特人过得同在老家一样快活，操持家事，也照旧是那副几乎分毫未改的无忧无虑的散漫劲儿。他们是天底下脾气最好的人，自己没有半点恶习，可对文明的种种毛病，学起来倒是一点就通。二十年前我初次结识他们时，没有一个人抽烟，也没有一个人馋烟。如今部落里人人都同烟草打过交道，人人馋它，为了弄到它，除了枪，几乎什么都肯拿出来。连三四岁的小娃娃都会吃烟草，说来奇怪，这竟对他们没有坏处。他们的烟草得自丹麦传教士，也得自捕鲸船、猎海豹船和猎海象船上的水手。威士忌的败坏工夫，倒还没有做到他们身上。

我深信，这个小部落的命脉已经注定，灭绝之期已不远了。这结局，一部分将由他们自己造成，一部分将由文明人那些误入歧途的努力造成。他们的人数逐年减少：1894年，休·J·李（Hugh J. Lee）为部落清点人口，共有二百五十三口；到1906年，马文教授发现他们已减至二百零七口。写到本书的此刻，我敢说人数又进一步少了下去，因为最近一次来自鲸湾（Whale Sound）一带的消息告诉我，那里死了不少人，而出生率又不高。想起我这些朋友的命运，实在令人难过：他们所住的，本是一片富足之乡，如今由于牟利猎人的贪心，正在变成一片苦寒的荒芜之地。海豹实际上已经绝迹，海象也正被迅速赶尽杀绝。驯鹿和麝牛正走着同一条下坡路，因为因纽特人自己也进内陆打猎了——而在直到二十年前的从前，他们的狩猎只限于海岸边和那片养活性命的大海。

就心性而论，他们极富人情。尽管他们的饮食几乎清一色是肉，性情却温厚柔和，彼此之间情爱颇深。除了夫妻之间，他们很少争吵，而且从不记仇怀怨。孩子是一笔宝贵的家当，深受疼爱，从不挨骂受罚，也不被宠坏。因纽特母亲给孩子擦洗，手法同母猫舔洗小猫一模一样。他们还有许多个人习惯，一一描写出来恐怕要把读者吓跑，所以我就直白地说一句作罢：在他们那里，污泥浊水毫不可怕。
