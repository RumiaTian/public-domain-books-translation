## XVIII / 十八

===Original===
After musk-oxen﻿—The doctor’s scientific expedition.

From the time of my arrival at the *Roosevelt*, for nearly three weeks, my days were spent in complete idleness. I would catch a fleeting glimpse of Commander Peary, but not once in all of that time did he speak a word to me. Then he spoke to me in the most ordinary matter-of-fact way, and ordered me to get to work. Not a word about the North Pole or anything connected with it; simply, “There is enough wood left, and I would like to have you make a couple of sledges and mend the broken ones. I hope you are feeling all right.” There was enough wood left and I made three sledges, as well as repaired those that were broken.

The Commander was still running things and he remained the commander to the last minute; nothing escaped him, and when the time came to slowdown on provisions, he gave the orders, and we had but two spare meals a day to sustain us. The whole expedition lived on travel rations from before the time we left Cape Sheridan until we had reached Sydney, NS, and like the keen-fanged hounds, we were always ready and fit.

追猎麝牛——医生的科考远征。

自从抵达*罗斯福号*，将近三个星期里，我整天无所事事。皮尔里指挥官的身影偶尔匆匆一瞥，可整整那段时间，他一句话也没有对我说过。后来他开了口，用的却是最平常不过、就事论事的口气，吩咐我去干活。关于北极点以及一切与之相关的事，一个字也没提；只说：「剩下的木料还够，我想让你打几架雪橇，再把破损的修一修。希望你身子骨还好。」木料确实还够，我打制了三架雪橇，还把那些破损的都修好了。

指挥官依旧统管着一切，直到最后一刻他都是我们的指挥官；什么事都逃不过他的眼睛。到了口粮需要紧缩的时候，命令由他下达，我们一天便只剩两顿饭的口粮勉强撑持。全队从离开谢里登角之前起，直到抵达悉尼（新斯科舍省），一路上吃的都是行军口粮；而我们恰似一群尖牙利齿的猎犬，始终整装待命、劲头十足。

It was late in May when Prof. MacMillan and Mr. Borup, with their Eskimo companions returned from Cape Jesup, where they had been doing highly important scientific work, taking soundings out on the sea-ice north of the cape as high as 84° 15′ north, and also at the cape. They had made a trip that was record-breaking; they had visited the different cairns made by Lockwood and Brainard and by Commander Peary, and they had also captured and brought into the ship a musk-ox calf; and they had most satisfactorily demonstrated their fitness as Arctic explorers, having followed the Commander’s orders implicitly and secured more than the required number of tidal-readings and soundings.

Prof. MacMillan, with Jack Barnes, a sailor, and Kudlooktoo, left for Fort Conger early in June, and continued the work of tidal-observations. They rejoined the *Roosevelt* just before she left Cape Sheridan. A little later in the month, Borup went to Clements Markham Inlet to hunt musk-oxen, and from there he went to Cape Columbia, where he erected the cairn containing the record of the last and successful expedition of the “Peary Arctic Club.” The cairn was a substantial pile of rocks, surmounted by a strong oaken guidepost, with arms pointing “North 413 miles to the Pole”; “East, to Cape Morris K. Jesup, 275 miles”; “West to Cape Thomas H. Hubbard, 225 miles”; while the southern arm pointed south, but to no particular geographical spot; it was labeled “Cape Columbia.” Underneath the arms of the guidepost, which had been made by Mate Gushue, was a small, glass-covered, boxlike arrangement, in which was encased the record of Peary’s successful journey to the Pole, and the roster of the expedition, my name included. From the crossbars, guys of galvanized wire were stretched and secured to heavy rocks, to help sustain the monument from the fury of the storms. Borup did good work, photographed the result, and the picture of the cairn, when exhibited, proved very satisfactory to the Commander.

Dr. Goodsell with two teams, and the Eskimo men, Keshungwah and Tawchingwah, left the ship on May 27, to hunt in the Lake Hazen and Ruggles River regions. They were successful in securing thirteen musk-oxen in that neighborhood, and in Bellows Valley they shot a number of the “Peary” caribou, the species “*Rangifer Pearyi*,” a distinct class of reindeer inhabiting that region.

五月末，麦克米伦教授和博鲁普先生带着各自的因纽特同伴从杰瑟普角（Cape Jesup）归来；他们一直在那里从事极为重要的科学工作：在该角以北的海冰上测深，远达北纬84度15分处，在该角本地也做了观测。他们这一趟行程破了纪录；他们寻访了洛克伍德与布雷纳德以及皮尔里指挥官先后垒起的各座石堆；还活捉了一头小麝牛带回船上；他们绝对听从指挥官的命令，取得的潮位读数与测深数据超出了规定数量，极其圆满地证明了自己堪当北极探险家。

六月初，麦克米伦教授带着水手杰克·巴恩斯（Jack Barnes）和库德卢克图动身前往康格堡（Fort Conger），继续潮汐观测工作。就在*罗斯福号*驶离谢里登角之前，他们赶回船来会合。当月再晚些时候，博鲁普前往克莱门茨·马克姆湾猎麝牛，随后又从那里转赴哥伦比亚角，在那里垒起一座石堆，封存着「皮尔里北极俱乐部」这最后一次圆满成功的探险的记录。那石堆是一大座结实的石垒，顶上竖着一根坚固的橡木指路柱，横臂上标示：「北，距北极点413英里」；「东，至莫里斯·K·杰瑟普角（Cape Morris K. Jesup），275英里」；「西，至托马斯·H·哈伯德角（Cape Thomas H. Hubbard），225英里」；朝南的横臂指向正南，却不指任何具体的地理地点，只标着「哥伦比亚角」。指路柱是大副古休的手笔；柱臂底下装着一个带玻璃罩的小匣子似的装置，里面封存着皮尔里成功抵达北极点的行程记录，还有全队名册——我的名字也名列其中。从横臂的横杆上牵出一条条镀锌铁丝，牢牢系在沉重的岩石上，好帮助这座标碑顶住风暴的狂怒。博鲁普干得漂亮，还把成果拍了照；那石堆的照片一拿出来，指挥官看了十分满意。

5月27日，古德塞尔医生带着两支雪橇队——因纽特人克顺瓦和塔钦瓦（Tawchingwah）——离船前往黑曾湖（Lake Hazen）与拉格尔斯河（Ruggles River）一带行猎。他们在那一带顺利猎获了十三头麝牛；在风箱谷（Bellows Valley），他们又射杀了若干头「皮尔里」驯鹿，即学名*Rangifer Pearyi*的那个种——栖居该地区的一个独特的驯鹿类别。

On the return of Dr. Goodsell, he told of his fascinating experiences in that wonderland. Leaving the *Roosevelt*, he had turned inland at Black Cliff Bay. Past the glaciers he went with his little party, down the Bellows Valley to the Ruggles River, an actual stream of clear-running water, alive with the finest of salmon trout. Adopting the Eskimo methods, he fished for these speckled beauties with joyful success. Here he rounded up and shot the herd of musk-oxen, and here he bagged his caribou. He was in a hunter’s paradise and made no haste to return, but crossed overland to Discovery Harbor and the barn-like structure of Fort Conger, the headquarters of General Greely’s “Lady Franklin Bay Expedition” of 1882﻿–﻿1883. Professor MacMillan was on his way to Fort Conger and it was with much surprise, on arriving there, that he found that Dr. Goodsell had reached it an hour before him. It was an unexpected meeting and quite a pleasure to the Professor to find the Doctor there, ready to offer him the hospitality of the fort.

Dr. Goodsell returned to the *Roosevelt* on June 15, with a load of geological, zoological, and botanical specimens almost as heavy as the loads of meat and skins he brought in. He was an ardent scientist, and viewed nearly every situation and object from the viewpoint of the scientist. Nothing escaped him; a peculiar form of rock or plant, the different features of the animal life, all received his close and eager attention, and he had the faculty of imparting his knowledge to others, like the born teacher that he was. He evinced an eager interest in the Eskimos and got along famously with them.

His physical equipment was the finest; a giant in stature and strength, but withal the gentlest of men having an even, mellow disposition that never was ruffled. In the field the previous spring he had accompanied the expedition beyond the “Big Lead” to 84° 29′, and with the strength of his broad shoulders he had pickaxed the way.

On account of his calm, quiet manner I had hesitated to form an opinion of him at first, but you can rest assured this was a “Tenderfoot” who made good.

During this time I left the ship on short hunting trips, but I was never away from the ship for more than ten or twelve hours.

古德塞尔医生回来后，讲述了他在这片奇境中种种迷人的经历。离开*罗斯福号*，他从黑崖湾（Black Cliff Bay）折入内陆，带着自己那一小队人走过一道道冰川，顺风箱谷而下，来到拉格尔斯河边——那可是一条真正淌着清水的河流，满河都是上好的鲑鳟。他采用因纽特人的钓法钓这些斑驳的美物，竿竿得手，其乐陶陶。就在这里，他围住并射杀了那群麝牛；也是在这里，他猎获了自己的驯鹿。他置身猎人的天堂，全不急着回去，索性取道陆路横穿到发现港（Discovery Harbor），来到康格堡那座谷仓似的建筑——1882至1883年间格里利将军（General Greely）「富兰克林夫人湾探险队」的大本营。麦克米伦教授当时正走在去康格堡的路上，到达后却大为惊讶地发现，古德塞尔医生竟比他早到了一个钟头。这场不期而遇令教授喜出望外：医生已在堡中，正准备尽堡主之谊款待他。

6月15日，古德塞尔医生带着满满一载荷的地质、动物与植物标本回到*罗斯福号*上，那些标本几乎同他运回的一橇橇肉与皮张一样沉。他是个热忱的科学家，几乎无论什么情况、什么物件，都要用科学家的眼光去看。什么都逃不过他：一块形状奇特的岩石或植物，动物界的种种形态特征，无不受到他细致而热切的关注；而且他天生是块当教师的料，有本事把自己的知识传授给别人。他对因纽特人显示出浓厚的兴趣，同他们相处得极为融洽。

他的体格条件是第一流的：身量魁伟，膂力过人，却又是我见过最温和的人之一，性情平顺醇厚，从不着恼。前一年春天在冰原上，他曾随探险队越过「大水道」推进到北纬84度29分，凭着他宽阔双肩的力气，一路抡着冰镐开出路径。

起初，只因他为人沉静安详，我一直踌躇着不敢对他下断语；不过请放心，这位「新手」（tenderfoot）可是实实在在干出名堂来了。

这期间，我也离船作过几次短途行猎，但从没有一次离开船超过十个乃至十二个钟头。

On July 1 quite a lead was opened in the channel south from Cape Sheridan to Cape Rawson. The ice was slowly moving southward, and the prospects for freeing the *Roosevelt* and getting her started on her homeward way were commencing to brighten. The following day a new lead opened much nearer shore, and on July 3 the Eskimos, who had been out hunting, returned from Black Cliff Bay, without game, but with the good news that as far south as Dumb Bell Bay there stretched a lead of open water. July 4, a new lead opened very close to the *Roosevelt*. The spring tides, with a strong southerly wind, had set in so very much earlier, three years before, that on July 4, 1906, the *Roosevelt* had been entirely free of ice, with clear, open water for quite a distance to the south; but this year the ship was still completely packed in the ice, and furthermore she was listed at the same angle as during the winter.

On July 5, I was detailed to help Gushue repair the more or less damaged whaleboats. The heavy and solidly packed snow of the winter had stove them in. On July 6, the anniversary of our departure from New York a year before, the greater part of the day was spent in pumping water from the top of a heavy floeberg into the ship’s boilers. This work was not completed until the morning of the 7th, when the fires were started. Due to the cold, the process of getting up steam was slow work. The ice had been breaking up daily, new leads were noticed, and on this day, July 7, a new lead opened at a distance of fifty yards from the ship, and open water stretched as far south as the eye could see. All hands were put to work reloading the supplies that had been placed on shore the fall previous, for it was easy to see that the time for departure was at hand.

With the boilers in order, an attempt was made to revolve the shaft, but the propeller was too securely frozen in the ice to move, and so Captain Bartlett got out the dynamite and succeeded in freeing the bronze blades.

7月1日，谢里登角以南通往罗森角（Cape Rawson）的海峡里，裂开了一条相当宽的水道。冰在缓缓南移，让*罗斯福号*脱困启程、踏上归途的前景开始露出亮色。第二天，一条新水道在离岸近得多的地方裂开。7月3日，出外行猎的因纽特人从黑崖湾归来，猎物一无所获，却带回好消息：往南直到哑铃湾（Dumb Bell Bay），绵延着一条开阔的水道。7月4日，*罗斯福号*近旁也裂开了一条新水道。三年前，大潮连同强劲的南风来得比这早得多，以至1906年7月4日那天，*罗斯福号*已完全脱冰，往南相当远的一段都是清澈的开阔水面；可今年，船仍被冰结结实实地裹着，而且船身还保持着入冬以来那个角度的倾侧。

7月5日，我奉命去协助古休修理那些损坏程度不一的捕鲸艇。一冬又厚又结实的积雪，把艇身压得塌陷了。7月6日，是我们一年前从纽约启程的周年纪念日，这一天大半工夫都用来从一座厚重浮冰山的顶上抽水，灌进船上的锅炉。这项工作直到7日早晨才收尾，随即便升了火。天气严寒，烧足蒸汽是个缓慢的活计。冰连日崩解，新的水道不断出现；就在这一天——7月7日——离船五十码远处又裂开一条新水道，开阔的水面向南一直延伸到目力尽头。全体人员都被派去重新装载头年秋天存放到岸上的物资，因为谁都看得出来，启碇的日子已经到了眼前。

锅炉收拾停当后，船上试着转动轮轴，但螺旋桨在冰里冻得纹丝不动，于是巴特利特船长搬出炸药，终于把青铜桨叶炸脱了出来。

From the 10th of July to the 13th, a fierce storm raged, clouds of freeing spray broke over the ship, encasing her in a coat of icy mail, and the tempest forced all of the ice out of the lower end of the channel and beyond as far as the eye could see, but the *Roosevelt* still remained surrounded by ice.

The morning of the 15th, a smart breeze from the northeast was blowing, and proved of valuable assistance to us, for it caused the huge blocks of ice that were surrounding the ship to loosen their hold, and for the first time since October, 1908, the *Roosevelt* righted herself to an even keel.

By this time all of our supplies had been loaded and stored, and from the crow’s-nest a stretch of open water could be seen as far as Cape Rawson. From there to Cape Union the ice was packed solid.

15日早晨，东北方吹来一阵劲风，帮了我们大忙：它让围在船四周的巨大冰块松开了钳制；自1908年10月以来，*罗斯福号*头一回回正了身子，龙骨恢复了水平。

这时，我们全部的物资都已装舱入库；从桅顶瞭望台望出去，开阔的水面一直可以望到罗森角。从罗森角到联合角（Cape Union）之间，冰仍旧冻结得严严实实。
