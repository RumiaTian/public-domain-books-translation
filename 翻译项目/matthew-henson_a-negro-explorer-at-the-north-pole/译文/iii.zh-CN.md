## III / 三

===Original===
Finding of Rudolph Franke﻿—Whitney landed﻿—Trading and coaling﻿—Fighting the ice-packs.
At Etah we take on the final load of coal from the *Erik* and the other supplies she has for us, and from now on it will be farewell to all the world; we will be alone with our company, and our efforts will be towards the north and our evasive goal.
At Etah, on going ashore, we were met by the most hopelessly dirty, unkempt, filth-littered human being any of us had ever seen, or could ever have imagined; a white man with long matted hair and beard, who could speak very little English and that only between cries, whimperings, and whines, and whose legs were swollen out of all shape from the scurvy. He was Rudolph Franke and had been left here the year before by Dr. F. A. Cook, an old acquaintance of mine, who had been a member of other expeditions of the Commander’s.
Franke was in a bad way, and the burden of his wail was, “Take me away from this, I have permission, see, here is Dr. Cook’s letter,” and he showed a letter from Dr. Cook, authorizing him to leave, if opportunity offered. Dr. Goodsell looked him over and pronounced him unfit to remain in the Arctic any longer than it would take a ship to get him out, and the Commander had him kindly treated, cleaned, medicated, and placed aboard the *Erik*. The poor fellow’s spirits commenced to rise immediately and there is good chance of his recovery and safe return home.

发现鲁道夫·弗兰克——惠特尼留岸过冬——交易与装煤——与浮冰群搏斗。
在埃塔（Etah），我们从埃里克号（Erik）上装上最后一批煤，以及她替我们捎来的其他补给。从今往后，就要同整个世界道别了：陪伴我们的只剩自己的队伍，我们的一切努力都将朝向北方，朝向那个总在回避我们的目标。
在埃塔上岸时，迎面而来的是我们中间谁都从未见过、也绝难想象的一个无可救药的肮脏腌臜、满身秽物的人：那是个白人，长发长须纠结成毡，几乎不会说英语，仅会的那一点儿，也只夹在哭喊、抽噎与哀诉之间；他的双腿因坏血病肿得完全走了形。他就是鲁道夫·弗兰克（Rudolph Franke），头一年被 F. A. 库克医生（Dr. F. A. Cook）留在了这里。库克是我的一位旧识，曾参加过皮尔里指挥官的其他几次探险。
弗兰克的情形很糟，他哭哭啼啼，翻来覆去只有一个意思：“把我带离这儿吧，我是有许可的，您瞧，这是库克医生的信。”说着他亮出库克医生的一封信，授权他一有机会便可离开。古德塞尔医生（Dr. Goodsell）给他做了检查，断言他已不宜再留居北极——除了等一艘船把他接出去，多一天也不该多待；皮尔里指挥官便吩咐众人好生待他，替他洗净、敷药，安置到埃里克号上。这可怜人的精神头立刻振作起来，很有希望痊愈，平安回家。

===Original===
We learn that Dr. Cook, with two Eskimo boys, is over on the Grant Land side, and in probably desperate circumstances, if he is still alive. The Commander has issued orders in writing to Murphy and Billy Pritchard to be on the lookout for him and give him all the help he may need, and has also instructed the Eskimos to keep careful watch for any traces of him, while on their hunting trips.
There is a cache of Dr. Cook’s provisions here, which Franke turned over to the Commander, and Mr. Whitney has agreed to help Murphy and Billy to guard it.
Mr. Harry Whitney is one of the party of men who came here on the *Erik* to hunt in this region, and he has decided to stay here at Etah for the winter and wait for a ship to take him out next summer. The other two members of the hunting-party, Mr. Larned and Mr. Norton, returned on the *Erik*. If Mr. Whitney had asked me my advice, I would not have suggested that he remain, because, although he has a fine equipment, there will not be much sport in his experience, and there will be a great deal of roughness. He will have to become like the Eskimos and they will be practically his only companions. However, Mr. Whitney has had a talk with the Commander in the cabin of the *Roosevelt*, and the Commander has given his consent and best wishes. Mr. Whitney’s supplies have been unloaded and some additions from the *Erik* made, and there is no reason to fear for his safety.

我们得知，库克医生带着两个因纽特（Eskimo）男孩在格兰特地（Grant Land）那一边，倘若他还活着，处境恐怕已十分险恶。皮尔里指挥官已向墨菲（Murphy）和比利·普里查德（Billy Pritchard）下达书面命令，要他们留心察访库克，他需要什么帮助便给什么帮助；同时又嘱咐因纽特人，出外打猎时仔细留意他的任何踪迹。
这里还封存着库克医生的一批给养，弗兰克已把它们移交给皮尔里指挥官；惠特尼先生（Mr. Whitney）也已答应协助墨菲和比利看守这批物资。
哈里·惠特尼先生（Mr. Harry Whitney）是乘埃里克号来这一带打猎的一行人中的一员，他决意留在埃塔过冬，等来年夏天有船来接他出去。狩猎队的另外两位成员，拉尼德先生（Mr. Larned）和诺顿先生（Mr. Norton），则随埃里克号返程。倘若惠特尼先生来问我的主意，我是不会劝他留下的；因为虽说他的装备精良，这一冬的经历却谈不上多少游猎的乐趣，倒会有数不尽的粗粝艰辛。他将不得不变得同因纽特人一模一样，而且他们实际上将是他仅有的伙伴。不过，惠特尼先生已在罗斯福号的船舱里同皮尔里指挥官谈过，指挥官表示应允，并致以最美好的祝愿。惠特尼先生的给养已经卸下，又从埃里克号上添补了一批，他的安全是没有什么可担忧的。

August 8, 1908: My forty-second birthday. I have not mentioned it to anyone, and there’s only one other besides myself who knows that today I am twice three times seven years of age. Seventeen years ago today, Commander Peary, hobbling about on his crutches with his right leg in a sling, insisted on giving me a birthday party. I was twenty-five years old then, and on the threshold of my Arctic experience. Never before in my life had the anniversary of my birth been celebrated, and to have a party given in my honor touched me deeply. Mrs. Peary was a member of the expedition then, and I suppose that it was due to her that the occasion was made a memorable one for me. Last year, I was aboard the *Roosevelt* in the shadow of the “Statue of Liberty” in New York Bay, and was treated to a pleasant surprise by my wife.
Commander Peary gave me explicit instructions to get Nipsangwah and Myah ashore as quick as the Creator would let them, but to be sure that their seven curs were kept aboard; these two huskies having exalted ideas as to their rights and privileges. Egingwah, or Karko as we knew him, and Koodlootinah and his family were to come aboard.
Acting under orders, I obeyed, but it was not a pleasant task. I have known men who needed dogs less to pay a great deal more for one pup than was paid to Nipsangwah for his pack of seven. The dogs are a valuable asset to this people and these two men were dependent on their little teams to a greater extent than on the plates and cups of tin which they received in exchange for them.

===Chinese===
1908年8月8日：我的四十二岁生日。我没有向任何人提起这事；除了我自己，只有另一个人知道，今天我恰好是二乘三乘七岁。十七年前的今天，皮尔里指挥官右腿吊着悬带、架着双拐蹒跚来去，却执意要为我办一场生日会。那年我二十五岁，正站在北极经历的门槛上。我生平还不曾有人给我庆祝过生日，这样一场专为我举办的聚会，令我深受触动。那时皮尔里夫人（Mrs. Peary）也是探险队的一员，我想，那一天之所以令我难忘，多半是她的功劳。去年今日，我正在纽约湾“自由女神像”的影子下、罗斯福号的船上，我的妻子给了我一个愉快的惊喜。
皮尔里指挥官给我下了明确的指令：把尼普桑瓦（Nipsangwah）和米亚（Myah）尽快送上岸去，造物主容许多快就多快；但务必把他们那七条恶犬留在船上，因为这两个家伙对自己应享的权利与特权，抱着高人一等的观念。埃金瓦（Egingwah）——我们平日叫他卡科（Karko）——连同库德卢蒂纳（Koodlootinah）及其家人，则要接上船来。
我奉命照办了，只是这差事干着并不痛快。我认识一些远不那么需要狗的人，为一条小狗出的价钱，比这次付给尼普桑瓦整整七条狗的还要多得多。对这里的族人来说，狗是宝贵的家当；这两个男人对自己那支小小狗队的倚重，远远超过他们换到手的那几只锡盘锡杯。

August 8﻿–﻿9, 1908: Have been trading with the natives without any trouble; they will give anything I want for anything that I have that they want. “It’s a shame to take the money,” or, as money is unknown up here and has no value, I should say that I should be ashamed to take such an advantage of them, but if I should stop to consider the freight-rates to this part of the world, no doubt a hatchet or a knife is worth just what it can be traded in for.
The ship has been rapidly littering up until it is now in a most perfect state of dirtiness, and in order to get the supplies from the *Erik*, coal, etc., the movable articles, dogs, Eskimos, etc., will have to be shifted and yours truly is helping.
The dogs have been landed on a small island in the bay, where they are safe and cannot run away, and they can have a glorious time, fighting and getting acquainted with each other. Some of the Eskimos’ goods are ashore, some aboard the *Erik*, and the rest forward on the roof of the deck-house, while the *Roosevelt* is getting her coal aboard.
The loading of the meat and coal has been done by the crews of the ships, assisted and *hampered* by some of the Eskimos, and I have been walrus-hunting, and taxidermizing; that is, I have skinned a pair of walrus so that they can be stuffed and mounted. This job has been very carefully, and I think successfully, done and the skins have been towed ashore. The hearts, livers, and kidneys have been brought aboard and the meat is to be loaded tomorrow. Two boatloads of bones have been rowed over to Dog Island for dog-food.

1908年8月8日至9日：一直同当地人交易，毫无磕绊；只要我有的东西正是他们想要的，我要什么，他们便肯拿什么来换。俗话说：“这钱拿着都嫌害臊。”——不过，金钱在这里无人识得、一文不值，所以照实说，倒是我占了他们这么大的便宜，理应惭愧；可若是停下来想想运到这世界一角的运费，那么一把斧头或一把刀，实在就恰好值它能换回的那些东西。

===Original===
Coaling and stowing of whale-meat aboard the *Roosevelt* was finished at noon, August 15, and all day Sunday, August 16, all hands were at the job transferring to the *Erik* the boxes of provisions that were to be left at the cache at Etah. Bos’n Murphy and Billy Pritchard, the cabin-boy, are to stay as guard until the return of the *Roosevelt* next summer. A blinding storm of wind and snow prevented the *Roosevelt* from starting until about two-thirty p.m., when, with all the dogs a-howling, the whistle tooting, and the crew and members cheering, we steamed out of the Harbor into Smith Sound, and a thick fog which compelled half-speed past Littleton Island and into heavy pack-ice.
Captain Bartlett was navigating the ship and his eagle eye found a lane of open water from Cape Sabine to Bache Peninsula and open water from Ellesmere Land halfway across Buchanan Bay, but this lead closed on him, and the *Roosevelt* had to stop. Late in the evening, the ice started to move and grind alongside of the ship, but did no damage except scaring the Eskimos. Daylight still kept up and we went to sleep with our boots on!
From Etah to Cape Sheridan, which was to be our last point north in the ship, consumed twenty-one days of the hardest kind of work imaginable for a ship; actually fighting for every foot of the way against the almost impassable ice. For another ship it would have been impassable, but the *Roosevelt* was built for this kind of work, and her worth and ability had been proven on the voyage of 1905. The constant jolting, bumping, and jarring against the ice-packs, forwards and backwards, the sudden stops and starts and the frequent storms made work and comfort aboard ship all but impossible.

巴特利特船长（Captain Bartlett）正亲自领航，他那只鹰眼找到了一条从萨拜恩角（Cape Sabine）通到巴切半岛（Bache Peninsula）的清水道，还有一条从埃尔斯米尔地（Ellesmere Land）延伸到布坎南湾（Buchanan Bay）中途的清水带；可这条水道随即在他眼前封死，罗斯福号只得停驶。傍晚很晚的时候，浮冰开始在船旁移动、碾磨，但除了吓着因纽特人之外，并未造成什么损伤。天光依旧亮着，我们穿着靴子上了床！
从埃塔到谢里登角（Cape Sheridan）——那里将是我们乘船北行的最后一站——足足耗去二十一天，那是一艘船所能想象的最艰苦的劳作：实际上是一路寸步苦战，对抗着几乎无法穿行的冰层。换作别的船，这里根本无路可通；但罗斯福号正是为这种活计造的，她的价值与能耐，在1905年那次航程中已经得到证明。船身不住地撞上浮冰，或进或退，颠簸震摇，骤停骤起，加上风暴频仍，船上一切正常的工作与安适，几乎都无从谈起。

Had it been possible to be ashore at some point of vantage, to witness the struggles of our little ship against her giant adversaries would have been an impressive sight.
I will not dwell on the trying hours and days of her successful battle, the six days of watching and waiting for a chance to get out of our dangerous predicament in Lincoln Bay, the rounding of the different capes en route, or the horrible jams in Lady Franklin Bay. The good ship kept at the fight and won by sheer bulldogged tenacity and pluck. Life aboard her during those twenty-one days was not one sweet song, but we did not suffer unusually, and a great deal of necessary work was done on our equipments. The Eskimo women sewed diligently on the fur clothing we were to wear during the coming winter and I worked on the sledges that were to be used. Provisions were packed in compact shape and everyone was busy. Two caches of provisions were made ashore in the event of an overland retreat, and the small boats were fully provisioned as a precaution against the loss of the ship. We did not dwell on the thought of losing it, but we took no chances.
Meeting with continual rebuffs, but persistently forging ahead and gaining deliberately day by day, the *Roosevelt* pushed steadily northward through the ice-encumbered waters of Kane Basin, Kennedy and Robeson Channels, and around the northeast corner of Grant Land to the shelter of Cape Sheridan, which was reached early in the afternoon of September 5, 1908.

至于她这场成功苦战中那些煎熬人的时日与钟点——在林肯湾（Lincoln Bay）的险境里守望等待、盼着脱身之机的那六天，沿途一个个海角的绕行，还有富兰克林夫人湾（Lady Franklin Bay）里骇人的冰堵——我就不再细述了。这艘好船始终咬牙苦战，凭着一股死咬不放的顽强与胆气赢得了胜利。那二十一天里，船上的生活绝非一支甜美的歌，但我们并没有格外吃苦，倒是趁着这工夫，把装备上许多必需的活计都做完了。因纽特妇女勤勤恳恳，缝制我们来年冬天要穿的皮衣；我则整备将要使用的雪橇。给养打成紧凑的行装，人人都各有所忙。为防备万一取道陆路撤退，我们在岸上设了两处给养窖藏；小船也都装足了给养，以防船只不测。我们没把失船的事挂在心上，但我们不冒任何风险。
