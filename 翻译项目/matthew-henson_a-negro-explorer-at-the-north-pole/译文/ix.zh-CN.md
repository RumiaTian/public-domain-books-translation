## IX / 九

===Original===
Ready for the dash to the Pole﻿—The commander’s arrival.
The Diary﻿—February 23: Heavy snowfall and furious winds; accordingly intense darkness and much discomfort.
There was a heavy gale blowing at seven o’clock in the morning, on February 22, and the snow was so thick and drifty that we kept close to our igloos and made no attempt to do more than feed the dogs. My igloo was completely covered with snow and the one occupied by Dr. Goodsell was blown away, so that he had to have another one, which I helped to build.
The wind subsided considerably, leaving a thick haze, but after breakfast, Professor MacMillan, Mr. Borup, and their parties, left camp for Cape Colan, to get the supplies they had dumped there, and carry them to Cape Aldrich. I took one Eskimo, Pooadloonah, and one sledge from the Captain’s party, and with my own three boys, Ooblooyah, Ootah, and I-forget-his-name, and a howling mob of dogs, we left for the western side of Cape Columbia, and got the rest of the pemmican and biscuits. On the way back, we met the Captain, who was out taking exercise. He had nothing to say; he did not shake hands, but there was something in his manner to show that he was glad to see us. With the coming of the daylight a man gets more cheerful, but it was still twilight when we left Cape Columbia, and melancholy would sometimes grip, as it often did during the darkness of midwinter.

===Chinese===
为冲刺北极点整装待发——指挥官到来。
日记——2月23日：大雪纷飞，狂风怒号；因而天色格外昏黑，诸多不适。
2月22日早晨七点，狂风刮得正猛，雪又浓又乱，四处飞卷，我们只能紧守着各自的伊格鲁（igloo），除了喂狗，别的什么也不打算做。我那座伊格鲁被雪整个埋没，古德塞尔医生（Dr. Goodsell）住的一座则被风掀跑，他只得再建一座，我也去帮了把手。
风势减退了不少，天边留下一片浓重的雪霭；早饭后，麦克米伦教授（Professor MacMillan）、博鲁普先生（Mr. Borup）和他们各自的队伍便离开营地前往科兰角（Cape Colan），去取先前卸存在那儿的补给，再运往奥德里奇角（Cape Aldrich）。我从船长队里带上一名因纽特人普阿德卢纳（Pooadloonah）和一乘雪橇，连同我自己手下三个小伙子——乌布卢亚（Ooblooyah）、乌塔（Ootah），还有一个名字我忘了的——再加上一群嗥叫不休的狗，动身前往哥伦比亚角西侧，取回了其余的干肉饼和饼干。归途中我们遇见了船长，他正在外面活动腿脚。他什么也没说，也没有握手，但他的神态里透着某种东西，表明他见了我们是高兴的。白昼一来，人总会振奋一些；只是我们离开哥伦比亚角时天色尚是暮光朦胧，忧郁有时仍会攫住人心，正如隆冬的黑暗里常有的那样。

===Original===
Captain Bartlett helped us to push the loaded sledges to Cape Aldrich and nothing was left at Cape Columbia.
When we got back to camp we found Professor Marvin and his party of three Eskimos there. They had just reached the camp and were at work building an igloo.
Professor Marvin came over to our igloo and changed his clothes; that is, in a temperature of at least 45° below zero, by the light of my lantern he coolly and calmly stripped to the pelt, and proceeded to cloth himself in the new suit of reindeerskin and polar bearskin clothing, that had been made for him by the Eskimo woman, Ahlikahsingwah, aboard the *Roosevelt*. It had taken him and his party five days to make the trip from Sheridan to Columbia.

===Chinese===
巴特利特船长（Captain Bartlett）帮我们把满载的雪橇推到奥德里奇角，哥伦比亚角上从此什么都没有留下。
回到营地时，我们发现马文教授（Professor Marvin）和他手下三名因纽特人的队伍已经在那里了。他们刚抵达营地，正动手搭建一座伊格鲁。
马文教授到我们的伊格鲁里来换衣服；也就是说，在至少零下45度的气温里，他就着我提灯的亮光，从容镇定地把一身衣裳脱得只剩皮肉，然后穿上那套新做的驯鹿皮加白熊皮的行头——那是因纽特妇女阿利卡辛瓦（Ahlikahsingwah）在罗斯福号上替他缝制的。从谢里登角到哥伦比亚角这一程，他和他的队伍足足走了五天。

February 26: This from my log: “Clear, no wind, temperature 57° below zero.” Listen! I will tell you about it. At seven a.m. we quit trying to sleep and started the pot a-boiling. A pint of hot tea gave us a different point of view, and Professor Marvin handed me the thermometer, which I took outside and got the reading; 57° below; that is cold enough. I have seen it lower, but after forty below the difference is not appreciable.
I climbed to the highest pinnacle of the cape and in the gathering daylight gazed out over the ice-covered ocean to get an idea of its condition. At my back lay the land of sadness, just below me the little village of snow-houses, the northernmost city on the earth (Commander Peary give it the name Crane City), and, stretching wide and far to the northward, the irresistible influence that beckoned us on; broken ice, a sinister chaos, through which we would have to work our way. Dark and heavy clouds along the horizon gave indication of open water, and it was easy to see that the rough and heavy shore-ice would make no jokes for us to appreciate.
About an hour or so after the midday meal, a loud outcry from the dogs made me go outside to see what was up. This was on the afternoon of February 26. I quickly saw what the dogs were excited about.

===Chinese===
2月26日：以下摘自我的日志：“晴，无风，气温零下57度。”听着！我来把这事讲给你听。早晨七点，我们不再指望睡得着，便起来把壶烧上。一品脱热茶下肚，人看事情的眼光就变了；马文教授把温度计递给我，我拿到外头读出度数：零下57度；这真是够冷的了。比这更低的温度我也见过，不过一过零下40度，那点差别就再觉不出来了。
我攀上海角最高的尖顶，趁着渐亮的日光眺望冰封的大洋，好估量一番它的状况。我背后卧着那片忧伤的陆地，脚下不远处是雪屋搭成的小小村落，地球上最北的城市（皮尔里指挥官给它起名“鹤城”［Crane City］）；而向北铺展开去、无边无际、召唤我们上路的，是那股不可抗拒的引力：碎冰狼藉，一片凶险的混沌，我们必须从中开出一条路来。地平线上暗沉浓重的云表明那边有开阔的水面；而且一眼便看得出，那崎岖厚重的岸冰，可不会同我们开什么好玩的玩笑。
午饭过后约莫一个钟头，狗群忽然大声骚动起来，我便出去看看出了什么事。这是2月26日下午。我很快便看清了狗群在为什么而兴奋。

===Original===
With a “Whoop halloo,” three Komaticks were racing and tearing down the gradient of the land to our camp, and all of us were out to see the finish. Kudlooktoo and Arkeo an even distance apart; and, heads up, tails up, a full five sledge-lengths ahead, with snowdust spinning free, the dog-team of the ever victorious Peary in the lead. The caravan came to a halt with a grandstand finish that it would have done you good to witness.
The Commander didn’t want to stop. He immediately commenced to shout and issue orders, and, by the time he had calmed down, both Captain Bartlett and George Borup had loaded up and pushed forward on to the ice of the Arctic Ocean, bound for the trophy of over four hundred years of effort. The Peary discipline is the iron hand ungloved. From now on we must be indifferent to comfort, and like poor little Joe, in “Bleak House” we must always be moving on.

===Chinese===
只听一声“嗨嗬”的呐喊，三乘科马蒂克雪橇（Komatick）顺着陆地的坡道朝我们营地一路狂奔猛冲而来，我们全都跑出去看那终场的一刻。库德卢克图（Kudlooktoo）与阿尔凯奥（Arkeo）两乘齐头并进、间距均等；而在前面足足领先五个雪橇身位处，昂着头、翘着尾，卷起一路飞旋的雪尘，领头的正是常胜不败的皮尔里那支狗队。车队以一场压轴冲刺般的抵达戛然收势，那场面你若亲眼得见，准会觉得大饱眼福。
指挥官可不想停顿。他随即高声喊叫、连下命令；待到他平静下来时，巴特利特船长和乔治·博鲁普（George Borup）都已装载停当，向前推进到北冰洋的冰面上，直奔那座四百多年心血所系的奖杯而去。皮尔里的纪律，是脱去手套的铁腕。从今往后，我们必须把安逸置之度外，像《荒凉山庄》（“Bleak House”）里可怜的小乔那样，一直往前走，永不停歇。
