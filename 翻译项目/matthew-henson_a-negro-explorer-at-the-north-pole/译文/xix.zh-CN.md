## XIX / 十九

===Original===
The Roosevelt starts for home﻿—Eskimo villages﻿—New dogs and new dog fights.
It was two-thirty p.m., July 17, 1909, that the *Roosevelt* pointed her bow southward and we left our winter quarters and Cape Sheridan. We were on our journey home, all hands as happy as when, a year previous, we had started on our way north, with the added satisfaction of complete success. The ship had steamed but a short distance, when, owing to the rapidly drifting ice in the channel, she had to be made fast to a floeberg. At ten-thirty p.m., the lines were loosed and a new start made. Without further incident, we reached Black Cape.
In rounding the cape the ship encountered a terrific storm, and it was with the greatest difficulty that she made any headway. The storm increased and the *Roosevelt* had to remain in the channel, surrounded by the tightly wedged floes, at the mercy of the wind. The gale continued until the evening of the 20th. The constant surging back and forth of the channel-pack, with the spring tides and the several huge masses of ice, which repeatedly crashed against the ship’s sides, caused a delay of twelve days in Robeson Channel opposite Lincoln Bay. Throughout the width of the entire channel nothing could be seen but small pools of open water; two seals were seen sporting in one of these pools, and one of the Eskimos attempted to kill them, but his aim proved false.
It was not until the 25th that the ship was able to move of her own free will, small leads having opened in close proximity to her. Ootah shot a seal in one of the leads, and also harpooned a narwhal, but he did not succeed in securing either. His brother Egingwah on the following day shot two seals and harpooned a narwhal, and he secured all three of his prizes. The Eskimos had a grand feast off the skin of the narwhal, which they esteem as a great delicacy.
By the 27th the *Roosevelt* had drifted as far south as Wrangell Bay, and it was here that Slocum (Inighito) shot and secured a hood-seal, which weighed over six hundred pounds, and seal-steaks were added to the bill-of-fare.

罗斯福号启程返家——因纽特人的村落——新来的狗和新的狗斗。
1909年7月17日下午两点半，*罗斯福号*调转船头向南，我们离开了越冬驻泊地和谢里登角。我们踏上了归程，全船上下快活得很，同一年前北上启程时一般无二，只是更添了一份大功告成的满足。船开出去没有多远，便因为海峡里浮冰漂流得太快，不得不系牢在一座浮冰山旁。到晚上十点半，缆绳解开，重新启航。此后一路无事，我们抵达了黑角（Black Cape）。
绕过海角时，船遇上一场狂暴的风暴，费了九牛二虎之力才勉强前进。风暴愈演愈烈，*罗斯福号*只得滞留海峡之中，被四周挤得死紧的浮冰团团围住，任凭风摆布。大风一直刮到20日傍晚。海峡里的浮冰群随着大潮来回涌动，又有几大块冰一次又一次撞上船舷，害得我们在罗伯逊海峡、林肯湾对面的水面上耽搁了十二天。整条海峡的宽度之内，除了零星几小片开阔水面，什么也看不见；有两只海豹在其中一片水洼里嬉水，一个因纽特人想猎杀它们，却失了准头。
直到25日，紧挨着船边裂开了几条小水道，船才得以凭自己的意志行动。乌塔在其中一条水道里射中一只海豹，又叉中一头一角鲸，可惜两样都没能弄到手。他的兄弟埃金瓦第二天射得两只海豹、叉中一头一角鲸，三样战利品尽数到手。因纽特人们拿一角鲸皮美美地大吃了一顿，那在他们看来是顶了不起的美味。
到27日，*罗斯福号*已向南漂到弗兰格尔湾（Wrangell Bay）。斯洛克姆（伊尼希托）就是在这里射得并捞起一只冠海豹，重达六百多磅，海豹排由此上了菜单。

The snow storms of the two days ceased on the 28th, and when the weather cleared sufficiently for us to ascertain our whereabouts, we were much surprised to find that we had drifted back north, opposite Lincoln Bay. During the day the wind shifted to the north. Again we drifted southward, until, just off Cape Beechey, the narrowest part of Robeson Channel, a lead stretching southward for a distance of five miles was sighted, and into this open water the ship steamed until the lead terminated in Kennedy Channel, opposite Lady Franklin Bay, where the *Roosevelt* was icebound until August 4, drifting with the pack until we were in a direct line with Cape Tyson and Bellot Isle. Three seals were captured, one a hood-seal weighing 624 pounds, being eight feet eleven inches in length; the other two were small ring-seals.
By ten a.m. of the 4th, the ice had slackened so considerably that the *Roosevelt*, under full steam, set out and rapidly worked her way down Kennedy Channel. From Crozier Island to Cape D’Urville she steamed through practically open water, but a dense fog compelled us to make fast to a large floe when almost opposite Cape Albert. It was not until one a.m. of the 7th, despite several attempts, that the ship got clear and steamed south again. Several small leads were noticed and numerous narwhals were seen, but none were captured.
At three-thirty a.m., when nearing Cape Sabine, we observed that the barometer had dropped to 29.73. A storm was coming, and every effort was made to reach Payer Harbor, but before half of the distance had been covered, the storm broke with terrific violence. The force of the gale was such that, while swinging the boats inboard, we were drenched and thoroughly chilled by the sheets of icy spray, which saturated us and instantly froze. The *Roosevelt* was blown over to starboard until the rails were submerged. To save her, she was steered into Buchanan Bay, under the lee of the cliffs, where she remained until the morning of August 8.
At an early hour, we steamed down Buchanan Bay, passed Cocked Hat Island, and a little later, Cape Sabine. At Cape Sabine was located Camp Clay, the starvation camp of the Lady Franklin Bay expedition of 1881﻿–﻿1883, where the five survivors of the twenty-three members of the expedition were rescued.

接连两天的暴风雪到28日停息了。等天气放晴到足以辨明我们的所在，大家吃惊地发现，我们竟又向北漂了回去，正对着林肯湾。白天里，风转向北面。我们再度向南漂移，直到罗伯逊海峡最窄处的比奇角（Cape Beechey）外，望见一条向南延展五英里的水道，船便驶进这片开阔水，一路开到水道在肯尼迪海峡内到了头、正对富兰克林夫人湾的地方。*罗斯福号*在那里被冰封到8月4日，其间随浮冰群漂移，最后与泰森角（Cape Tyson）和贝洛岛（Bellot Isle）连成了一条直线。被困期间捕得三只海豹：一只冠海豹重624磅，身长八英尺十一英寸；另两只是小个的环斑海豹。
4日上午十点，冰已松动得厉害，*罗斯福号*开足蒸汽启程，顺着肯尼迪海峡迅速南下。从克罗泽岛（Crozier Island）到迪尔维尔角（Cape D’Urville），船几乎一路行驶在开阔水面上；可是浓雾迫使我们差不多行至艾伯特角（Cape Albert）对面时，把船系牢到一大片浮冰上。尽管几度设法，直到7日凌晨一点，船才脱出身来，重新向南开行。途中注意到几条小水道，还看见许多一角鲸，只是一头也没有捕到。
凌晨三点半，临近萨拜恩角时，我们看到气压计降到了29.73。风暴就要来了，我们千方百计想赶到帕耶港（Payer Harbor），可是还没走完一半路程，风暴便以骇人的狂暴之势袭来。风势之猛，使得我们在把小艇吊进舷内的当口，被一片片冰冷的水雾浇得透湿、寒气直沁骨髓；水雾浸透了周身，转眼冻结成冰。*罗斯福号*被吹得向右舷倾侧，连栏杆都没入了水中。为了保住船，我们把她驶进布坎南湾，躲到峭壁的背风面下，在那里一直停到8月8日早晨。
一早，我们沿布坎南湾下行，驶过三角帽岛（Cocked Hat Island），不多时又过了萨拜恩角。萨拜恩角就是克莱营（Camp Clay）的所在——1881至1883年富兰克林夫人湾探险队断粮挨饿的营地，全队二十三名成员中幸存的五人就是在那里获救的。

We entered Smith Sound. Instead of sailing on to Etah, Peary ordered the ship into Whale Sound, in order that walrus-hunting could be done, so that the Eskimos should have a plentiful supply of meat for the following winter. Three walrus were captured, when a storm sprang up with all of the suddenness of storms in this neighborhood, and the ship crossed over from Cape Alexander to Cape Chalon. Cape Chalon is a favorite resort of the Eskimos, and is known as Peter-ar-wick, on account of the walrus that are to be found here during the months of February and March.
At Nerke, just below Cape Chalon, we found the three Eskimo families of Ahsayoo, Tungwingwah, and Teddylingwah, and it was from these people we first learned of Dr. Cook’s safe return from Ellesmere Land. In spite of the fact that the *Roosevelt* was overloaded with dogs, paraphernalia, and Eskimos, these three families were taken aboard.
With them were several teams of dogs. The dogs aboard ship were the survivors of the pack that had been with us all through the campaign, and a number of litters of puppies that had been whelped since the spring season. Our dogs were well acquainted with each other and dog fights were infrequent and of little interest, but the arrival of the first dog of the new party was the signal for the grandest dog fight I have ever witnessed. I feel justified in using the language of the fairy Ariel, in Shakespeare’s “Tempest”: “Now is Hell empty, and all the devils are here.”

我们驶入史密斯海峡。皮尔里没有让船径直开往埃塔，而是下令驶进鲸湾，以便猎捕海象，让因纽特人来年冬天有一份充裕的肉食。捕得三头海象之后，一场风暴骤然而起——这一带的风暴向来来得就是这样突然——船便从亚历山大角（Cape Alexander）渡海到了沙隆角（Cape Chalon）。沙隆角是因纽特人最爱落脚的去处，地名彼得拉维克（Peter-ar-wick），因为每年二月和三月间总有海象聚在此地。
在沙隆角往下一点的内尔克（Nerke），我们遇见了阿萨尤（Ahsayoo）、通温瓦（Tungwingwah）、特迪林瓦（Teddylingwah）三户因纽特人家；我们最先正是从他们嘴里听说，库克医生已从埃尔斯米尔地平安归来。尽管*罗斯福号*上的狗、辎重和因纽特人早已载得超负荷，这三户人家还是被接上了船。
他们还带着好几队狗。船上原有的狗，是随我们走完全程的那群狗中的幸存者，外加开春以来产下的几窝小狗。我们的狗彼此熟稔，狗打架的事难得发生，打起来也少有看头；可新来那伙人的第一条狗一上船，便成了我所见过的最盛大一场狗斗的开场信号。我觉得，借用莎士比亚《暴风雨》里精灵爱丽儿（Ariel）的话来说正合适：“地狱已经空了，群魔都在这里。”

Backward and forward, the foredeck of the ship was a howling, snarling, biting, yelping, moving mass of fury, and it was a long round of fully ten or fifteen minutes before the two king dogs of the packs got together, and then began the battle for supremacy of the pack. It lasted for some time. It would have been useless to separate them. They would decide sooner or later, and it was better to have it over, even if one or both contestants were killed. At length the fight was ended; our old king dog, Nalegaksoah, the champion of the pack, and the laziest dog in it, was still the king. After vanquishing his opponent and receiving humble acknowledgments, King Nalegaksoah went stamping up and down before the pack and received the homage due him; the new dogs, whining and fawning and cringingly submissive, bowed down before him.
The chief pleasure of the Eskimo dogs is fighting; two dogs, the best of friends, will hair-pull and bite each other for no cause whatever, and strange dogs fight at sight; teammates fight each other on the slightest of provocations; and it seems as though sometimes the fights are held for the purpose of educating the young. When a fight is in progress, it is the usual sight to see several mother dogs, with their litters, occupying ringside seats. I have often wondered what chance a cat would stand against an Eskimo dog.
The ship kept on, and I had turned in and slept, and on arising had found that we had reached a place called Igluduhomidy, where a single family was located. Living with this family was a very old Eskimo, Merktoshah, the oldest man in the whole tribe, and not a blood-relation to any member of it. He had crossed over from the west coast of Smith Sound the same year that Hall’s expedition had wintered there, and has lived there ever since. He had been a champion polar bear and big game hunter, and though now a very old man, was still vigorous and valiant, in spite of the loss of one eye.
We stopped at Kookan, the most prosperous of the Eskimo settlements, a village of five tupiks (skin tents), housing twenty-four people, and from there we sailed to the ideal community of Karnah. Karnah is the most delightful spot on the Greenland coast. Situated on a gently southward sloping knoll are the igloos and tupiks, where I have spent many pleasant days with my Eskimo friends and learned much of the folklore and history. Lofty mountains, sublime in their grandeur, overtower and surround this place, and its only exposure is southward toward the sun. In winter its climate is not severe, as compared with other portions of this country, and in the perpetual daylight of summer, life here is ideal. Rivulets of clear, cold water, the beds of which are grass- and flower-covered, run down the sides of the mountains and, but for the lack of trees, the landscape is as delightful as anywhere on earth.

船头甲板上，前前后后滚着一团狂怒的东西，嗥着、龇着、咬着、吠着，蠢蠢而动；这样足足闹了十到十五分钟，两群狗的王这才撞到一起，随即开始了争夺狗群霸主之位的战斗。这一仗打了一阵。要把它们分开是徒劳的。迟早总要见分晓，不如打完了事，哪怕一方或双方丧命也罢。末了，战斗结束；我们的老狗王纳莱加克索阿（Nalegaksoah）——狗群里的冠军，也是狗群里最懒的一条——依旧是王。战胜对手、受了对方低首下心的服输之后，纳莱加克索阿王便在狗群前面踏着重步来回踱着，受它应得的臣服之礼；新来的狗呜咽着、献着媚、卑怯地顺从，在它面前一个个伏低了身子。
因纽特狗最大的乐事就是打架：两条狗即便好得形影不离，也会无缘无故地互相扯毛咬嘴；生狗一照面就斗；同队拉橇的伙伴，稍受些微冒犯便掐起来；而且有时候，那架简直像是特意打来给年幼的受教育的。每当斗得正酣，常见的光景是几只母狗带着自家的崽子占着场边的位子观战。我常常纳闷，一只猫若同因纽特狗交手，能有几分胜算。
船继续前行。我上床睡了一觉，起身时发现我们已经到了一处叫伊格卢杜霍米迪（Igluduhomidy）的地方，那里只住着一户人家。同这户人家住在一起的，有一位年迈的因纽特老人默克托沙（Merktoshah），他是全部落里最年长的人，同部落中任何成员都没有血缘关系。当年霍尔探险队在史密斯海峡西岸越冬，他就是在那一年从西岸渡海过来的，此后一直住在这里。他从前是猎北极熊和大型猎物的顶尖好手，如今虽已年迈，又失了一只眼睛，却依旧筋骨硬朗、胆气不减。
我们在库坎（Kookan）停靠——那是因纽特人聚居地中最兴旺的一处，一个由五顶图皮克（tupiks，兽皮帐篷）组成、住着二十四口人的村落——随后从那里驶往堪称理想聚落的卡尔纳（Karnah）。卡尔纳是格陵兰海岸最讨人喜欢的地方。伊格鲁和图皮克坐落在一座缓缓向南倾斜的小丘上，我曾在那里同因纽特朋友们度过许多快活的日子，听来不少民间传说和史话。高峻的群山雄伟壮丽，高高耸峙，把这个地方四面围拢，只有朝南一面敞向太阳。同这地方的其他各处相比，这里冬天的气候算不得严酷；到了夏日永昼时分，此间的生活直如仙境。一道道清冽的溪水顺着山坡淌下，溪床上长满青草野花；若不是缺少树木，这景致同世上任何地方相比都不逊色。
