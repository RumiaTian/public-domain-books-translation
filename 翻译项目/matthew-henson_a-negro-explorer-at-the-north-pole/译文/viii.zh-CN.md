## VIII / 八

===Original===
In camp at Columbia﻿—Literary igloos﻿—The magnificent desolation of the Arctic.

Our heavy furs had been made by the Eskimo women on board the ship and had been thoroughly aired and carefully packed on the sledges. We were to discard our old clothes before leaving the land and endeavor to be in the cleanest condition possible while contending with the ice, for we knew that we would get dirty enough without having the discomfort of vermin added. It is easy to become vermin-infested, and when all forms of life but man and dog seem to have disappeared, the bedbug still remains. Each person had taken a good hot bath with plenty of soap and water before we left the ship, and we had given each other what we called a “prizefighter’s haircut.” We ran the clippers from forehead back, all over the head, and we looked like a precious bunch but we had hair enough on our heads by the time we came back from our three months’ journey, and we needed a few more baths and new clothes.

When I met Dr. Goodsell at Cape Columbia, about a week after he had left the ship, he had already raised quite a beard, and, as his hair was black and heavy, it made quite a change in his appearance. The effect of the long period of darkness had been to give his complexion a greenish-yellow tinge. My complexion reminded him of a ginger cake with too much saleratus in it.

===Chinese===
驻营哥伦比亚角——书香伊格鲁——北极的壮丽荒凉。

我们厚重的皮裘是船上的因纽特妇女缝制的，早已充分晾晒过，并仔细打包装上了雪橇。离开陆地之前，我们要把旧衣裳一律换掉，力求以尽可能干净的状态去同浮冰较量，因为我们知道，往后够我们脏的，犯不上再添虱虫滋扰的苦楚。人要招上虱虫是很容易的事；当万物生灵仿佛只剩下人和狗的时候，臭虫却依然健在。离船之前，每人都用足肥皂和热水，痛痛快快洗了个好澡，大家还彼此剃了个我们唤作“拳击手式”的发型——推子从前额一路推到后脑，满头推了个遍。我们那副尊容可真够瞧的；不过等三个月的旅程结束回来时，我们头上又长出了够多的头发，而且还少不了再洗几回澡、换几身新衣裳。

我在哥伦比亚角见到古德塞尔医生时，他离船已约有一星期，脸上已蓄起好一把胡须；他头发又黑又密，这一来模样变化着实不小。长时期的黑暗给他的脸色蒙上了一层青黄。至于我的脸色，让他想起一块小苏打搁多了的姜汁蛋糕。

===Original===
February 23: Heavy snowfall but practically no wind this morning at seven o’clock, when Dr. Goodsell left his igloo for Cape Colan to pick up the load he had left there when he lightened his sledges, also some loads of pemmican and biscuits that had been cached. We had supper together and also breakfast this morning, and as we ate we laughed and talked, and I taught him a few tricks for keeping himself warm.

In spite of the snow, which was still falling, I routed out my boys, and in the dark we left camp for the western side of the cape, to get the four sledge-loads of rations that had been taken there the previous November. Got the loads and pushed south to Cape Aldrich, which is a point on the promontory of Cape Columbia. From Cape Aldrich the Commander intends to attack the sea-ice.

After unloading the supplies on the point, we came back to camp at Cape Columbia. Shortly afterwards Captain Bartlett came into camp from his musk-ox-hunt around Parr Bay. He had not shot a thing and was very tired and discouraged, but I think he was glad to see me. He was so hungry that I gave him all the stew, which he swallowed whole.

MacMillan and his party showed up about an hour after the Captain, and very shortly after George Borup came driving in, like “Ann Eliza Johnson, a swingin’ down the line.” I helped Mr. Borup build his igloo, for which he was grateful. He is a plucky young fellow and is always cheerful. He told us that Professor Marvin, according to the schedule, had left the ship on the 20th, and the Commander on the 21st, so they must be well on the way.

===Chinese===
2月23日：今晨七点，大雪纷飞，却几乎没有风；古德塞尔医生离开他的伊格鲁前往科兰角，去取他先前给雪橇减载时卸存在那里的那批东西，还有窖藏的几份干肉饼和饼干。我们一同吃了晚饭，今晨又共进早餐；吃着说着笑着，我还教了他几招保暖的窍门。

尽管雪仍下个不停，我还是把手下那班小伙子从睡处轰了起来，摸着黑离开营地，前往海角西侧，去取头年11月运到那里的四雪橇口粮。取了货便向南推进到奥德里奇角——那是哥伦比亚角海岬上的一个岬尖。指挥官打算就从奥德里奇角向海冰发起攻势。

把补给卸在岬尖上之后，我们回到哥伦比亚角的营地。不多时，巴特利特船长在帕尔湾（Parr Bay）一带猎麝牛归来，进了营。他一无所获，疲惫不堪，意气很是消沉，不过我想，他见到我还是高兴的。他饿极了，我便把炖肉全数给了他，他囫囵着吞了个精光。

船长到达约一小时后，麦克米伦和他的一队人也到了；紧接着，乔治·博鲁普赶着雪橇一路冲进营地，活像歌里唱的那位“安·伊丽莎·约翰逊，一路摇摇晃晃赶过来”（Ann Eliza Johnson, a swingin’ down the line）。我帮博鲁普先生搭他的伊格鲁，他为此很感激我。他是个有胆气的小伙子，永远乐呵呵的。他告诉我们，按日程，马文教授20日离船，指挥官21日动身，这会儿他们必定已经在路上了。

===Original===
While waiting in this camp for the Commander and Professor Marvin to arrive, we had plenty of work; readjusting the sledge-loads and also building snow-houses and banking them with blocks of snow, for the wind had eroded one end of my igloo and completely razed it to the level of the ground, and a more solidly constructed igloo was necessary to withstand the fury of the gale.

We kept a fire going in one igloo and dried our mittens and kamiks. Though the tumpa, tumpa, plunk of the banjo was not heard, and our campfires were not scenes of revelry and joy, I frequently did the double-shuffle and an Old Virginia breakdown, to keep my blood circulating.

The hours preceding our advance from Cape Columbia were pleasantly spent, though we lost no time in literary debates. There were a few books along.

Out on the ice of the Polar ocean, as far as reading matter went, I think Dr. Goodsell had a very small set of Shakespeare, and I know that I had a Holy Bible. The others who went out on the ice may have had reading matter with them, but they did not read it out loud, and so I am not in a position to say what their literary tastes were.

Even on shipboard, we had no pigskin library or five-foot shelf of sleep-producers, but each member had some favorite books in his cabin, and they helped to form a circulating library.

===Chinese===
在营地里等候指挥官和马文教授到来的期间，我们有的是活儿干：重新调整雪橇载重，还要修筑雪屋，并用雪块把屋子四周培固——风把我那座伊格鲁的一端侵蚀殆尽，整个削平到了地面；要顶住狂风的肆虐，就得把伊格鲁筑得更加结实。

我们在其中一座伊格鲁里生着火，烤干我们的连指手套和卡米克（kamiks，海豹皮靴）。虽然听不见班卓琴“嘭啪、嘭啪、咚”的声响，我们的炉火边也不是觥筹宴乐的场面，我却常常跳上一段双曳步，再来一支老弗吉尼亚欢舞，好让血脉流通。

从哥伦比亚角开拔前的那些时辰过得颇为惬意，虽说我们在文学清谈上并没费一点工夫。随行的书倒是有几本。

至于北冰洋冰面上的读物，我想古德塞尔医生带的是一小套莎士比亚，我则确切知道自己带了一本《圣经》。其他上冰的人也许随身带了读物，但他们从不念出声来，所以他们的文学趣味如何，我无从置喙。

即便在船上，我们也没有什么猪皮面文库，也没有一格五英尺长的催眠书架；不过每个队员的舱房里都有几本心爱的书，这些书合起来便凑成了一座流通图书馆。

While we waited here, we had time to appreciate the magnificent desolation about us. Even on the march, with loaded sledges and tugging dogs to engage attention, unconsciously one finds oneself with wits woolgathering and eyes taking in the scene, and suddenly being brought back to the business of the hour by the fiend-like conduct of his team.

There is an irresistible fascination about the regions of northernmost Grant Land that is impossible for me to describe. Having no poetry in my soul, and being somewhat hardened by years of experience in that inhospitable country, words proper to give you an idea of its unique beauty do not come to mind. Imagine gorgeous bleakness, beautiful blankness. It never seems broad, bright day, even in the middle of June, and the sky has the different effects of the varying hours of morning and evening twilight from the first to the last peep of day. Early in February, at noon, a thin band of light appears far to the southward, heralding the approach of the sun, and daily the twilight lengthens, until early in March, the sun, a flaming disk of fiery crimson, shows his distorted image above the horizon. This distorted shape is due to the mirage caused by the cold, just as heat-waves above the rails on a railroad-track distort the shape of objects beyond.

The south sides of the lofty peaks have for days reflected the glory of the coming sun, and it does not require an artist to enjoy the unexampled splendor of the view. The snows covering the peaks show all of the colors, variations, and tones of the artist’s palette, and more. Artists have gone with us into the Arctic and I have heard them rave over the wonderful beauties of the scene, and I have seen them at work trying to reproduce some of it, with good results but with nothing like the effect of the original. As Mr. Stokes said, “it is color run riot.”

在这里等候的当儿，我们有工夫领略四周那壮丽的荒凉了。即便在行军途中，有满载的雪橇和奋力曳行的狗队要人照管，人还是会不知不觉走了神，眼睛只顾把景色收进来，直到自己那一队狗使出魔鬼般的行径，才猛地被拽回眼下的正事上来。

格兰特地（Grant Land）最北端的那片地域，有一种令人无法抗拒的魅力，我实在难以描摹。我灵魂里没有诗意，又在那片冷酷的土地上经年历练、心肠变硬了些，一时竟想不出贴切的字眼，好让你对它独特的美有个概念。你且想象一种绚烂的荒寒、一种美丽的空无吧。那里仿佛从来不是敞亮的大白天，即便正届六月中旬也是如此；从天光最初的微露到最后的残照，晨昏各个时辰的暮光在天空里变幻出不同的景象。2月初的正午，南方天际远远现出一线薄光，预告太阳的临近；暮光一天天延长，到3月初，太阳——一轮殷红如烈火的圆盘——在地平线上露出它变了形的身影。这种变形是严寒造成的蜃景，正如铁路轨道上方蒸腾的热浪会把远处物体的形状扭得走样。

多日以来，巍巍高峰的南坡一直映着那轮将至的太阳的荣光；这般举世无双的壮丽景色，用不着是艺术家也能欣赏。覆满峰顶的积雪，把画家调色板上所有的色彩、层次与调子都呈现了出来，还更有过之。曾有画家随我们同入北极，我听过他们为这奇景之美赞叹不绝，也见过他们挥笔试图重现其中一二——成绩固然不错，却远不及原本的气象。正如斯托克斯先生（Mr. Stokes）所说：“那是色彩在撒野。”

===Original===
To the northward, all is dark and the brighter stars of the heavens are still visible, but growing fainter daily with the strengthening of the sunlight.

When the sun finally gets above the horizon and swings his daily circle, the color effects grow less and less, but then the sky and cloud-effects improve and the shadows in the mountains and clefts of the ice show forth their beauty, cold blues and grays; the bare patches of the land, rich browns; and the whiteness of the snow is dazzling. At midday, the optical impression given by one’s shadow is of about nine o’clock in the morning, this due to the altitude of the sun, always giving us long shadows. Above us the sky is blue and bright, bluer than the sky of the Mediterranean, and the clouds from the silky cirrus mare’s-tails to the fantastic and heavy cumulus are always objects of beauty. This is the description of fine weather.

Almost any spot would have been a fine one to get a round of views from; at Cape Sheridan, our headquarters, we were bounded by a series of land marks that have become historical; to the north, Cape Hecla, the point of departure of the 1906 expedition; to the west, Cape Joseph Henry, and beyond, the twin peaks of Cape Columbia rear their giant summits out to the ocean.

From Cape Columbia the expedition was now to leave the land and sledge over the ice-covered ocean four hundred and thirteen miles north﻿—to the Pole!

===Chinese===
向北望去，一片幽暗，天上较亮的星辰依然可见，只是随着日光日盛，一天比一天黯淡下去。

等太阳终于升上地平线，绕天划出它每日一周的圆弧，色彩的效果便渐渐减退；但这时天空与云彩的景象却愈见其妙：群山和冰隙里的阴影显露出它们的美，是冷的蓝与灰；陆地裸露的斑块，是浓醇的棕；积雪之白，则耀眼夺目。正午时分，人影投在眼里的观感，约莫像上午九点钟的光景——这是太阳高度使然，它总是把我们的人影拉得老长。头顶的天空湛蓝明亮，比地中海的天还要蓝些；从绢丝般的卷云“马尾”，到奇诡厚重的积云，云始终是赏心悦目的景物。这便是晴好天气的写照。

几乎随便站到哪里，环顾一周都是一片好景致。在我们的大本营谢里登角，四望环列着一圈已然载入史册的天然地标：北面是赫克拉角（Cape Hecla），1906年那支探险队的出发之地；西面是约瑟夫·亨利角（Cape Joseph Henry）；再往外，哥伦比亚角的双峰把巨大的峰顶高高探向大洋。

探险队此刻就要从哥伦比亚角告别陆地，驾着雪橇踏上冰封的大洋，向北行进四百一十三英里——直抵北极点！
