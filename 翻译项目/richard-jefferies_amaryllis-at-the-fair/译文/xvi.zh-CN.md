## XVI / 第十六章

===Original===
The man he had seen at the window was young Raleigh Pamment, the son and heir.
He had been sitting in an easy chair, one leg over the arm, busy with a memorandum book, a stump of pencil, and a disordered heap of telegrams, letters, and newspapers.
Everybody writes to Mr. Gladstone, a sort of human lion's mouth for postcards, but Raleigh junior had not got to manage the House of Commons, the revenue, the nation, the Turks, South Africa, the Nile, Ganges, Indus, Afghanistan, sugar, shipping, and Homer.

===Chinese===
窗边那个人正是年轻的拉利·帕门特（Raleigh Pamment），帕门特家的少东家、继承人。
他一直坐在一把安乐椅上，一条腿搭在扶手上，忙着翻一本备忘录、一支铅笔头，还有散乱成一堆的电报、信件和报纸。
人人都给格莱斯顿先生（Mr. Gladstone）写信，他简直成了一个专门接收明信片的人形狮子口，可小拉利还不用去管下议院、税收、国家大事、土耳其人、南非、尼罗河、恒河、印度河、阿富汗、糖、航运和荷马。

===Original===
Yet Raleigh junior had an occasional table beside him, from which the letters, telegrams, newspapers, and scraps of paper had overflowed on to the floor. In a company's offices it would have taken sixteen clerks to answer that correspondence; this idle young aristocrat answered it himself, entered it in his day book, "totted" it up, and balanced the—the residue.
Nothing at all businesslike, either, about him—nothing in the least like those gentlemen who consider that to go in to the "office" every morning is the sum total of life. A most unbusinesslike young fellow.
A clay pipe in his mouth, a jar of tobacco on another chair beside him, a glass of whiskey for a paperweight on his telegrams. An idle, lounging, "bad lot;" late hours, tobacco, whiskey, and ballet-dancers writ very large indeed on his broad face. In short, a young "gent" of the latter half of the nineteenth century.

===Chinese===
然而小拉利身旁有张小茶几，上面的信件、电报、报纸和零散纸片已经溢到了地上。要是哪家公司的办公室里堆着这么多信件，至少得十六个职员才能回完；可这位闲散的年轻贵族自己动手回信，记进流水账，算出总数，然后轧平那个——剩余部分。
他身上毫无精明的生意人气，连一丝一毫都没有——跟那些认为每天早上走进"办公室"就是人生全部的绅士们截然不同。一个极不精明的小伙子。
嘴里叼着根陶土烟斗，旁边椅子上放着一罐烟草，一玻璃杯威士忌压在电报上当镇纸。懒散、散漫、"浪荡子"；晚睡、烟草、威士忌、芭蕾舞女——全都写在他那张宽脸膛上，写得一清二楚。总之，一个十九世纪后半叶的年轻"绅士"。

===Original===
Not the slightest sign of "blue blood" anywhere; not even in the cut of his coat, no Brummel-like elegance; hardly a Bond Street coat at all—rough, large, coarse cloth. If he had stood at the door of a shop he would have done very well indeed for a shopkeeper, the sort that drives about in a cart for orders.
Of his character nothing could be learned from his features. His face was broad, rather flat, with a short but prominent nose; in spite of indulgence, he kept a good, healthy, country colour. His neck was thick, his figure stout, his hands big—a jovial, good-tempered looking man.
His neck was *very* thick, treelike; a drover's neck, no refinement or special intelligence indicated there; great power to eat, drink, and sleep—belly energy.

===Chinese===
哪儿也看不出丝毫"贵族血统"的痕迹；连他衣服的裁剪都没有布鲁梅尔式的优雅；那件大衣简直算不上邦德街的出品——粗糙、宽大、厚实的布料。要是他站在店铺门口，活脱脱就是一个好掌柜的模样，那种赶着马车到处送订单的掌柜。
他的性格从五官上完全看不出来。脸庞宽阔，比较扁平，鼻子短而挺拔；尽管纵情享受，他的气色依然很好，带着健康红润的乡土气。脖子粗壮，体态敦实，双手粗大——一个快活、脾气好的样子。
他的脖子*非常*粗，像树干一样；赶牲口的人才有的脖子，既看不出优雅也看不出什么特别的智慧；吃、喝、睡的本事倒是大得很——纯粹的腹腔精力。

===Original===
But let no one, therefore, suppose that the members of the upper ten thousand are any thicker in the neck, or more abdominal in their proclivities, or beneath the culture of the day. Take five hundred "blue bloods," and you will find among them a certain proportion of thick-necked people; take five hundred very common commoners, you may count exactly the same number interspersed.
The Pamments were simply Englishmen, and liable to be born big, with broad faces, thick necks, and ultimate livers. It was no disgrace to Raleigh, that jolly neck of his.

===Chinese===
不过，可别因此就以为上流社会的人脖子更粗，或者更耽于口腹之欲，或者低于当今的教养水准。挑五百个"贵族血统"的人来，你会发现其中有一定比例的粗脖子；挑五百个再普通不过的平民来，你也能数出完全一样多的粗脖子混在其中。
帕门特家不过是英国人，天生容易长得高大，宽脸膛、粗脖子、最终还有个大肝脏。拉利那条粗脖子，算不上什么丢人的事。

===Original===
Unless you are given to aesthetic crockery, or Francesco de Rimini, I think you would rather have liked him; a sort of fellow who would lend you his dogs, or his gun, or his horse, or his ballet-dancer, or his credit—humph!—at a moment's notice. But he was a very "bad lot;" they whispered it even in dutiful Woolhorton.
He got rid of money in a most surprising way, and naturally had nothing to show for it. The wonderful manner in which coin will disappear in London, like water into deep sand, surpasses the mysteries of the skies. It slips, it slides, it glides, it sinks, it flies, it runs out of the pocket. The nimble squirrel is nothing to the way in which a sovereign will leap forth in town.

===Chinese===
除非你沉迷于唯美派瓷器，或者弗朗切斯科·德·里米尼（Francesco de Rimini）那种调调，否则我想你大概会挺喜欢他；他就是那种随时能借你猎犬、猎枪、马匹、芭蕾舞女，还有他的信用——哼！——一口答应的家伙。但他是个十足的"浪荡子"；连循规蹈矩的伍尔霍顿镇上都有人悄悄议论。
他花钱的方式出奇地大方，自然也就什么也没剩下。金钱在伦敦消失的方式——像水渗入深沙——简直比星空的奥秘还要不可思议。它溜、它滑、它溜走、它沉没、它飞走、它从口袋里跑掉。敏捷的松鼠比起一枚金镑在城里跳出去的速度，简直不值一提。

===Original===
Raleigh had a good allowance, often supplemented by soft aunts, yet he frequently walked for lack of a cab fare. *I* can't blame him; I should be just as bad, if fortune favoured me. How delicious now to walk down Regent Street, along Piccadilly, up Bond Street, and so on, in a widening circle, with a thousand pounds in one's pocket, just to spend, all your own, and no need to worry when it was gone. To look in at all the shops and pick up something here and something yonder, to say, "I'll have that picture I admired ten years ago; I'll have a bit of real old oak furniture; I'll go to Paris—" but Paris is not a patch on London. To take a lady—*the* lady—to St. Peter Robinson's, and spread the silks of the earth before her feet, and see the awakening delight in her eyes and the glow on her cheek; to buy a pony for the "kids" and a diamond brooch for the kind, middle-aged matron who befriended you years since in time of financial need; to get a new gun, and inquire about the price of a deer-stalk in Scotland; whetting the road now and then with a sip of Moet—but only one sip, for your liver's sake—just to brighten up the imagination. And so onwards in a widening circle, as sunlit fancy led: could Xerxes, could great Pompey, could Caesar with all his legions, could Lucullus with all his oysters, ever have enjoyed such pleasure as this—just to spend money freely, with a jolly chuckle, in the streets of London? What is Muhammad's Paradise to *that*?

===Chinese===
拉利有一份丰厚的零花钱，心软的姑妈们还时常接济，可他却常常因为没有马车费而步行。*我*可不能怪他；要是老天眷顾我，我也会一样。此刻想象一下，口袋里揣着一千镑，沿着摄政街往下走，穿过皮卡迪利，登上邦德街，如此这般，越走圈子越大，只管花钱，全是自己的，花完也不用心疼。逛遍每一家店铺，这儿挑一件那儿拣一样，说一声"我要那幅十年前看中的画；我要一套真正的老橡木家具；我要去巴黎——"不过巴黎可比不上伦敦。带着一位女士——*那位*女士——去彼得·罗宾逊百货（St. Peter Robinson's），把世间最美的丝绸铺在她脚下，看她眼中渐渐绽放的欣喜和脸颊上的红晕；给"小家伙们"买一匹小马驹，给多年前在经济困难时伸出援手的那位善良的中年夫人买一枚钻石胸针；弄一把新枪，打听一下在苏格兰猎鹿的费用；走一段路，抿一口莫伊特香槟（Moet）——只为提提神——好让想象力焕发出来。就这样越走越远，随着阳光般的幻想引领：薛西斯（Xerxes）、伟大的庞培（Pompey）、带着全部军团的凯撒（Caesar）、摆满生蚝的卢库卢斯（Lucullus），他们可曾享受过这样的快乐——在伦敦街头痛痛快快地花钱，带着爽朗的笑声？与这相比，穆罕默德的天堂又算什么？

===Original===
The exquisite delight of utterly abandoned extravagance, no counting—anathemas on counting and calculation! If life be not a dream, what is the use of living?
Say what you will, the truth is, we all struggle on in hope of living in a dream some day. This is my dream. Dreadfully, horribly wicked, is it not, in an age that preaches thrift and—twaddle? No joy like waste in London streets; happy waste, imaginative extravagance; to and fro like a butterfly!

===Chinese===
彻底放纵的奢侈带来的美妙快乐，不用数——诅咒那些清点和算计吧！如果生活不是一场梦，活着还有什么意义？
随你怎么说，事实是，我们都在苦苦挣扎，只盼着有一天能活在梦里。这就是我的梦。在一个宣扬节俭和——废话——的时代，这难道不是极其可怕的罪过吗？在伦敦街头挥霍才是最快乐的事；快乐的挥霍，充满想象力的奢侈；像蝴蝶一样翩翩来去！

===Original===
Besides, there's no entertainment in the world like the streets of London on a sunny day or a gas-lit night. The shops, the carriages, the people, the odds and ends of life one sees, the drifting to and fro of folk, the "bits" of existence, glimpses into shadowy corners, the dresses, the women; dear me, where shall we get to? At all events, the fact remains that to anyone with an eye the best entertainment in the world is a lounge in London streets. Theatres, concerts, séances, Albert Halls, museums, galleries, are but set and formal shows; a great weariness, for the most part, and who the deuce would care to go and gaze at them again who could lounge in Piccadilly?
It is well worth a ten-pound note any day in May; fifty pounds sometimes at 1 p.m., merely to look on, I mean, it is worth it; but you can see this living show for nothing. Let the grandees go to the opera; for me, the streets.

===Chinese===
再说了，世间没有什么娱乐比得上阳光下或煤气灯下的伦敦街头。店铺、马车、行人、生活里零零碎碎的片段、人们的来来往往、生活的"碎片"、瞥见的幽暗角落、衣裙、女人；天哪，我们说到哪儿去了？无论如何，事实就是：对于有眼光的人来说，世界上最好的娱乐就是在伦敦街头闲逛。剧院、音乐会、降神会、阿尔伯特音乐厅、博物馆、画廊，不过都是些刻板规矩的表演；多数时候沉闷无聊，能去皮卡迪利闲逛的人，谁还有心思再去看那些玩意儿？
五月份任何一天都值一张十镑钞票；有时候下午一点钟值五十镑，我是说光看看就值；可你花一个子儿不花就能看到这场活生生的表演。让那些大人物去听歌剧吧；对于我，街头就够了。

===Original===
So I can't throw dirt at Raleigh, who often had a hatful of money, and could and did just what seemed pleasant in his sight. But the money went like water, and in order to get further supplies, the idle, good-for-nothing, lazy dog worked like a prime minister with telegrams, letters, newspapers, and so on, worked like a prime minister—at betting. Horse-racing, in short, was the explanation of the memorandum-book, the load of correspondence, and the telegrams, kept flat with a glass of whiskey as a paperweight.

===Chinese===
所以我也不能往拉利身上泼脏水，他时常兜里揣着一大笔钱，想怎么花就怎么花，想干什么就干什么。可钱花得像流水一样，为了弄到更多钱，这个游手好闲、一无是处的懒狗干得跟首相一样起劲——摆弄电报、信件、报纸什么的，干得跟首相一样起劲——全是赌赛马。总之，备忘录、那一大堆信件和电报，全都解释得通了——再加一杯威士忌压在上面当镇纸。

===Original===
While he wrote, and thought, and reckoned up his chances, a loud refrain of snoring arose from the sofa. It was almost as loud as the boom of the fair, but Raleigh had no nerves. His friend Freddie, becoming oppressed with so much labour, had dropped asleep, leaving his whiskey beside him on the sofa, so that the first time he moved over it went on the carpet. With one long leg stretched out, the other knee up, lying on his back, and his mouth wide open to the ceiling, Freddie was very happy.

===Chinese===
正当他写写算算、盘算着自己的胜算时，沙发上响起了一阵响亮的鼾声。那鼾声几乎跟集市上的喧嚣一样大，可拉利神经大条，全不在乎。他的朋友弗雷迪（Freddie）被这么多劳动压垮了，沉沉睡去，威士忌杯就搁在沙发旁，结果他一翻身，杯子就倒翻在地毯上了。弗雷迪一条长腿伸直，另一条腿屈着膝盖，仰面朝天，嘴巴大张对着天花板，好不快活。

===Original===
Raleigh puffed his clay pipe, sipped, and puffed again. Freddie boomed away on the sofa. The family was in London; Raleigh and Freddie got down here in this way: it happened one night there was a row at a superb bar, Haymarket trail. The "chuckers-out" began their coarse horseplay, and in the general melée Raleigh distinguished himself. Rolled about by the crowd, he chanced to find himself for a moment in a favourable position, and punished one of these gigantic brutes pretty severely.
Though stout and short of breath, Raleigh was strong in the arm, he was "up," and he hit hard. The fellow's face was a "picture," coloured in cardinal. Such an opportunity does not occur twice in a lifetime; Raleigh's genius seized the opportunity, and he became great. Actium was a trifle to it.

===Chinese===
拉利叼着陶土烟斗吞云吐雾，抿一口酒，又吸一口。弗雷迪在沙发上鼾声如雷。帕门特一家人都在伦敦；拉利和弗雷迪是这样到这儿来的：一天夜里，干草市场街（Haymarket）一家豪华酒吧里出了乱子。那些"清场人"开始粗野地胡闹，混战之中，拉利大显身手。人群推推搡搡，他碰巧被挤到一个有利位置，狠狠地教训了其中一个彪形大汉。
拉利虽然粗壮又气短，但胳膊有劲，他"站了起来"，下手很重。那家伙的脸成了一幅"画"，染成了枢机主教红。这样的机会一生不会碰到两次；拉利的天才抓住了这个机会，他从此声名大噪。跟这比起来，亚克兴海战（Actium）都算不了什么。

===Original===
There were mighty men before Agamemnon, and there are mighty men who do not figure in the papers.
Raleigh became at once an anaxandron—a King of Men. The history of his feat spread in ten minutes from one end of midnight London to the other: from the policeman in Waterloo Place to—everywhere. Never was such a stir; the fall of Sebastopol—dear me! I can remember it, look at the flight of time—was nothing to it. They would have chaired him, *fêted* him, got a band to play him about the place, literally crowned him with laurel. Ave, Caesar! Evoe! Bacchus! But they could not find him.

===Chinese===
阿伽门农（Agamemnon）之前就有伟大的人，报纸上没有名字的，也有伟大的人。
拉利立刻成了一位阿纳克桑德隆——众人的王者。他那场壮举的传奇故事，十分钟之内从深夜伦敦的一端传到另一端：从滑铁卢广场（Waterloo Place）的警察到——到处都是。从未有过这样的轰动；塞瓦斯托波尔（Sebastopol）的陷落——天哪！我还记得呢，看看时间过得有多快——跟这比起来都不值一提。人们本要抬着他游行、为他举办盛宴、请一支乐队满街演奏、用月桂冠给他加冕。凯撒万岁！酒神万岁！巴克斯（Bacchus）！可他们找不着他了。

===Original===
Raleigh was off with Freddie, who had been in at the death, and was well "blooded." Hansom to Paddington in the small hours; creep, creep, creep, through the raw morning mist, puff, whistle, broad gauge, and they had vanished.
Raleigh was a man of his age; he lost not a moment; having got the glory, the next thing was to elude the responsibility; and, in short, he slipped out of sight till the hue-and-cry was over, and the excitement of the campaign had subsided.
In case anyone should suppose I approve of midnight battle, I may as well label the account at once: "This is a goak."

===Chinese===
拉利带着弗雷迪一走了之——弗雷迪也参与了收尾，而且被"见了血"。凌晨时分雇一辆双轮马车到帕丁顿（Paddington）；在清晨的湿雾中，车轮咕咕、咕咕地碾过，火车呼哧、鸣笛，宽轨哐当哐当，他们就这么消失了。
拉利是这个时代的人；他一刻也不耽误；既然已经得了荣耀，接下来就是要逃避责任；总之，他从人们的视线中溜走了，直到追查的风头过去，那场战役的兴奋劲儿消退。
万一有人以为我赞成午夜斗殴，我不妨给这段叙述贴个标签："此乃笑谈。"

===Original===
I do *not* approve of brawls at the bar, but I have set myself the task to describe a bit of human life exactly as it really is, and I can assure you as a honest fact that Raleigh by that lucky knock became a very great man indeed among people as they really are. People as they really are, are not all Greek scholars.
As I don't wish you to look down upon poor Raleigh too much because he smoked a cutty, and hit a fellow twice as big as himself, and lent his money, and made bets, and drank whiskey, and was altogether wicked, I may as well tell you something in his favour: He was a hero to his valet.

===Chinese===
我*不*赞成在酒吧里打架，但我给自己定下的任务是如实描绘生活本来的样子。我可以向你保证一个不争的事实：拉利因为那次幸运的一击，在真实世界的人们中间成了一个了不起的大人物。真实世界的人，并不全是希腊语学者。
我不想让你太瞧不起可怜的拉利——因为他抽短烟斗、打了比自己大一倍的人、借钱给人、下赌注、喝威士忌、总而言之就是坏透了——我倒不如告诉你他的一条优点：他在他的贴身男仆眼里是个英雄。

===Original===
"No man is a hero to his valet," says the proverb, not even Napoleon, Disraeli, or Solomon.
But Raleigh *was* a hero to his valet.
He was not only a hero to Nobbs the valet; he had perfectly fascinated him. The instant he was off duty Nobbs began to be a Raleigh to himself. He put on a coat cut in the Raleigh careless style; in fact, he dressed himself Raleigh all over. His private hat was exactly like Raleigh's; so was his necktie, the same colour, shape, and bought at the same shop; so were his boots. Why, Raleigh was not only a Hero, he was a Demigod to his valet! Not only Nobbs, but the footmen, and the grooms, and the whole race of servants everywhere who had caught a glimpse of Raleigh looked upon him as the Ideal Man. So did the whole race of "cads" in the bars and at the races, and all over town and country, all of that sort who knew anything of Raleigh sighed to be like "he."
The fellow who said that "No man is a hero to his valet" seemed to suppose that the world worships good and divine qualities only. Nothing of the sort; it is not the heroic, it is the low and coarse and blackguard part the mass of people regard with such deep admiration.
If only Nobbs could have been "he," no doubt whatever he would have "done it" very big indeed. But he would have left out of his copy that part of Raleigh's nature which, in spite of the whiskey and the cutty, and the rest of it, made him still a perfect gentleman at heart. Nobbs didn't want to be a perfect gentleman.
