## X

===Original===
When Mrs. Iden threw his lardy-cake descent in Iden's face she alluded to Grandfather Iden's being a baker and miller, and noted for the manufacture of these articles. A lardy, or larded, cake is a thing, I suppose, unknown to most of this generation; they were the principal confectionery familiar to country folk when Grandfather Iden was at the top of his business activity, seventy years since, in the Waterloo era.

===Chinese===
艾登太太把艾登的猪油蛋糕出身甩在他脸上时，说的就是艾登爷爷（Grandfather Iden）当面包师兼磨坊主、以制作猪油蛋糕闻名的事。猪油蛋糕，恐怕如今这一代人大半都没见过；那是七十年前、滑铁卢时代，艾登爷爷生意最红火时乡下人最熟悉的点心。

===Original===
A lardy-cake is an oblong, flat cake, crossed with lines, and rounded at the corners, made of dough, lard, sugar, and spice. Our ancestors liked something to gnaw at, and did not go in for lightness in their pastry; they liked something to stick to their teeth, and after that to their ribs. The lardy-cake eminently fulfilled these conditions; they put a trifle of sugar and spice in it, to set it going as it were, and the rest depended on the strength of the digestion. But if a ploughboy could get a new, warm lardy-cake, fresh from the oven, thought himself blessed.

===Chinese===
猪油蛋糕是一种长方扁平的糕点，表面划着交叉纹路，四角浑圆，用面团、猪油、糖和香料做成。我们的祖辈喜欢有嚼头的东西，点心不求轻软，只求能粘住牙齿，再沉到肚肠里去。猪油蛋糕完美地满足了这些条件；里头搁了一丁点儿糖和香料权作引子，余下的全凭消化的本事了。不过若一个犁田的小伙子能拿到一块刚出炉、热腾腾的猪油蛋糕，他便觉得自己有福了。

===Original===
Grandfather Iden had long since ceased any serious business, but he still made a few of these renowned cakes for his amusement, and sold a good few at times to the carters' lads who came in to market.

===Chinese===
艾登爷爷早已不再正经经营什么生意了，但偶尔兴起还会做几块这种远近闻名的猪油蛋糕来消遣，赶集时卖给进城的车夫小伙们，也卖出不少。

===Original===
Amaryllis knew the path perfectly, but if she had not, the tom-tomming of drums and blowing of brass, audible two miles away, would have guided her safely to the fair. The noise became prodigious as she approached—the ceaseless tom-tom, the beating of drums and gongs outside the show vans, the shouting of the showmen, the roar of a great crowd, the booing of cattle, the baaing of sheep, the neighing of horses—altogether the "rucket" was tremendous.

===Chinese===
阿玛丽莉斯（Amaryllis）对这条路再熟不过了，就算不熟，两英里外就能听到的鼓声咚咚和铜管号鸣也会把她安全引到集市上去。越走近，那声响便越发惊人——连绵不断的咚咚鼓声、演艺篷车外面锣鼓的敲击声、卖艺人的吆喝声、汹涌人群的喧嚣声、牛的哞叫声、羊的咩咩声、马的嘶鸣声——总而言之，这场"大热闹"着实惊天动地。

===Original===
She looked back from the hill close to the town and saw the people hurrying in from every quarter—there was a string of them following the path she had come, and others getting over distant stiles. A shower had fallen in the night, but the ceaseless wheels had ground up the dust again, and the lines of the various roads were distinctly marked by the clouds hanging above them. For one on business, fifty hastened on to join the uproar.

===Chinese===
她从镇子附近的小丘上回头望去，看见人们从四面八方匆匆赶来——身后小路上走着一长串跟她走同一条路的人，远处还有人在翻越篱笆门。夜里下过一阵雨，但来来往往的车轮又把尘土碾了起来，条条道路上方悬着的尘云清晰地标出了路的走向。去做正经事的一个人，就有五十个人急匆匆赶来凑这份热闹。

===Original===
Suppose the Venus de Medici had been fetched from Florence and had been set up in the town of Woolhorton, or the Laocoön from Rome, or the Milo from Paris, do you think all these people would have scurried in such haste to admire these beautiful works? Nothing of the sort; if you want a crowd you must make a row. It is really wonderful how people do thoroughly and unaffectededly enjoy a fearful disturbance; if the cannon could be shot off quietly, and guns made no noise, battles would not be half so popular to read about. The silent arrow is uninteresting, and if you describe a medieval scramble you must put in plenty of splintering lances, resounding armour, shrieks and groans, and so render it lively.

===Chinese===
倘若有人把佛罗伦萨的维纳斯雕像搬来安放在伍尔霍顿（Woolhorton），或者把罗马的拉奥孔群像、巴黎的断臂维纳斯运来，你想这些人会如此急匆匆赶来欣赏这些绝世美作吗？绝不会；要想聚起人山人海，非得闹出大动静不可。人们真心实意、毫不做作地享受一场可怕的骚动，这实在令人惊叹；假如大炮能悄无声息地发射，枪支不发出声响，战争题材的读物绝不会受欢迎一半。无声的箭乏善可陈，要描写中世纪的混战，你非得写上折断的长矛、铿锵的铠甲、尖叫声和呻吟声不可，这样才热闹。

===Original===
"This is the patent age of new inventions," and someone might make a profit by starting a fête announcing that a drum or a gong would be provided for every individual, to be beaten in a grand universal chorus.

===Chinese===
"这是发明专利的新时代"，说不定有人搞一场庆典来赚上一笔——宣告为每位来宾提供一面鼓或一面锣，大家齐鸣，来一场盛大的全民合奏。

===Original===
Amaryllis had no little difficulty in getting through the crowd till she found her way behind the booths and slipped along the narrow passage between them and the houses. There was an arched entrance, archaeologically interesting, by which she paused a moment, half inclined to go up and inquire for her boots. The shoemaker who lived there had had them since Christmas, and all that wanted doing was a patch on one toe; they were always just going to be done, but never finished. She read the inscription over his door, "Tiras Wise, Shoemaker; Established 1697." A different sort of shoe-maker to your lively Northampton awls; a man who has been in business two hundred years cannot be hurried. She sighed, and passed on.

===Chinese===
阿玛丽莉斯费了好大的劲才穿过人群，找到摊棚后面的通道，沿着摊棚与房屋之间的窄巷溜过去。巷口有一道拱门，颇有考古趣味，她停下来犹豫了片刻，差点想上去问问她的靴子修好了没有。住在那里的鞋匠从圣诞节起就收了她的靴子，一只靴头只需打个补丁；靴子总是"快修好了"，却永远修不完。她读了读门楣上的题字："蒂拉斯·怀斯（Tiras Wise），鞋匠；创立于1697年。"这号鞋匠可跟你在热闹的北安普顿见过的大不一样；一个做了两百年生意的人，你是催不动他的。她叹了口气，继续往前走。

===Original===
The step to Grandfather Iden's door consisted of one wide stone of semicircular shape, in which the feet of three generations of customers had worn a deep groove. The venerable old gentleman, for he was over ninety, was leaning on the hatch (or lower half of the door), in the act of handing some of his cakes to two village girls who had called for them. These innocent, hamlet girls, supposed to be so rurally simple, had just been telling him how they never forgot his nice cakes, but always came every fair day to buy some. For this they got sixpence each, it being well known that the old gentleman was so delighted with anybody who bought his cakes he generally gave them back their money, and a few coppers besides.

===Chinese===
艾登爷爷家门口的台阶是一块半圆形的大石头，三代顾客的脚已磨出了一道深深的凹槽。这位德高望重的老先生——他已年逾九旬——正倚在半截门板上，把几块蛋糕递给两个上门来买的村姑。这两个天真淳朴的乡村姑娘，看上去老实巴交的，方才一直在告诉他她们从没忘记他那好吃的蛋糕，每次逢集都会来买几块。为此每人得了六便士——大家都知道，这位老先生但凡有人来买他的蛋糕，高兴之余总要把钱还给人家，还外搭几个铜板。

===Original===
He took Amaryllis by the arm as she stood on the step and pulled her into the shop, asked her if her father were coming, then walked her down by the oven-door, and made her stand up by a silver-mounted peel, to see how tall she was. The peel is the long wooden rod, broad at one end, with which loaves are placed in the baker's oven. Father Iden being proud of his trade, in his old age had his favourite peel ornamented with silver.

===Chinese===
阿玛丽莉斯站在台阶上时，他一把拉住她的胳膊把她拽进店里，问她父亲来不来，然后领她走到烤炉门旁边，让她靠在一把镶银的铲子旁，量量她长了多高。烤铲是一种长长的木棒，一头扁宽，用来把面包送进炉膛。艾登老爹（Father Iden）以自己的手艺为傲，年老后专门给他最爱的那把烤铲镶上了银饰。

===Original===
"Too fast—too fast," he said, shaking his head, and coughing; "you grow too fast; there's the notch I cut last year, and now you're two inches taller." He lowered the peel, and showed her where his thumb was—quite two inches higher than the last year's mark.

===Chinese===
"太快了——太快了，"他摇着头，咳嗽着说；"你长得太快了；你看这是去年我刻的记号，如今你又高了两英寸。"他放下烤铲，指给她看他的大拇指位置——比去年的刻痕足足高了两英寸。

===Original===
"I want to be tall," said Amaryllis.

===Chinese===
"我就想长得高，"阿玛丽莉斯说。

===Original===
"I daresay—I daresay," said the old man, in the haste manner of feeble age, as he cut another notch to record her height. The handle of the peel was notched all round, where he had measured his grandchildren; there were so many marks it was not easy to see how he distinguished them.

===Chinese===
"那是——那是，"老人以老年人特有的急促语调应道，一边刻下新的记号来记录她的身高。烤铲的柄上四周刻满了记号，都是他量孙子孙女身高时留下的；刻痕多得密密麻麻，真不知他是怎么分辨的。

===Original===
"Is your father coming?" he asked, when he had finished with the knife.

===Chinese===
"你父亲来不来？"他放下刀子后问道。

===Original===
"I don't know." This was Jesuitically true—she did *know*—she could not be certain; but in her heart she was sure he would not come. But she did not want to hear any hard words said about him.

===Chinese===
"我不知道。"这话在耶稣会式的层面上是实话——她确实*不*知道——她不能确定；但心里明白他不会来。只是她不想听到别人说他的坏话。

===Original===
"Has he sent anything? Have you brought anything for me? No. No. Hum!—ha!"—fit of coughing—"Well, well—come in; dinner's late, there's time to hear you read—you're fond of books, you read a great deal at home,"—and so talking, half to himself and half to her, he led the way into the parlour by the shop.

===Chinese===
"他捎了什么没有？你给我带了什么没有？没有。没有。哼！——哈！"——一阵咳嗽——"行了行了——进来吧；晚饭还早，有空听你读书——你爱看书，在家读了不少，"他这样半自言自语半对她说着，领她穿过店铺走进了里屋。

===Original===
Bowed by more than ninety years, his back curved over forwards, and his limbs curved in the opposite direction, so that the outline of his form resembled a flattened capital S. For his chin hung over his chest, and his knees never straightened themselves, but were always more or less bent as he stood or walked. It was much the attitude of a strong man heavily laden and unable to stand upright—such an attitude as big Jack Duck in his great strength might take when carrying two sacks of wheat at once. There was as heavy a load on Grandfather Iden's back, but Time is invisible.

===Chinese===
九十余载的岁月压弯了他的脊背，他的背向前弓起，四肢却向相反方向弯曲，整个身形的轮廓像一个被压扁了的大写S。他的下巴垂在胸口上方，膝盖从来伸不直，无论站着还是走路都多少弯着。这很像一个壮汉负重过沉、直不起腰的样子——大力士大杰克·达克（Jack Duck）扛着两袋麦子时大概也是这副模样。艾登爷爷背上也负着同样沉重的担子，只不过时间是看不见的。

===Original===
He wore a grey suit, as a true miller and baker should, and had worn the same cut and colour for years and years. In the shop, too, he always had a grey hat on, perhaps its original hue was white, but it got to appear grey upon him; a large grey chimney-pot, many sizes too big for his head apparently, for it looked as if forever about to descend and put out his face like an extinguisher. Though his boots were so carefully polished, they quickly took a grey tint from the flour dust as he pottered about the bins in the morning. The ends of his trousers, too long for his antique shanks, folded and creased over his boots, and almost hid his grey cloth under-gaiters.

===Chinese===
他穿着一身灰色套装，正像一个真正的磨坊主兼面包师该穿的样子，而且多年来一直是同样的剪裁和颜色。在店里他也总是戴着一顶灰帽子——或许原本是白色的，但戴在他头上就显得灰扑扑的——一顶灰色的大烟囱帽，看起来比他的脑袋大了好几号，仿佛随时要滑下来把他的脸罩住，像熄灯罩一样。尽管他的靴子擦得一丝不苟，可早上在面粉箱之间走来走去，很快就沾上了一层灰蒙蒙的面粉。他的裤腿对那双古旧的瘦腿来说太长了，折着叠在靴子上面，几乎把灰色的布绑腿都遮住了。

===Original===
A great knobbed old nose—but stay, I will not go further, it is not right to paint too faithfully the features of the very aged, which are repellent in spite of themselves; I mean, they cannot help their faces, their sentiments and actions are another matter; therefore I will leave Father Iden's face as a dim blot on the mirror; you look in it and it reflects everywhere, except one spot.

===Chinese===
一只布满疙瘩的大鼻子——不过且住，我不再往下描摹了，过于逼真地刻画极高龄者的面容并不恰当，它们不由自主地令人望而生畏；我是说，他们对自己的面容无能为力，他们的性情和作为则是另一回事；所以，就让艾登老爹的脸像镜面上一块模糊的斑点吧——你往镜子里看，它到处都能照见，唯独那块斑点照不出来。

===Original===
Amaryllis followed him jauntily—little did she care, reckless girl, for the twenty thousand guineas in the iron box under his bed.

===Chinese===
阿玛丽莉斯轻快地跟在他身后——这个率性的姑娘才不在乎他床底下铁盒子里那两万基尼呢。

===Original===
The cottage folk, who always know so much, had endless tales of Iden's wealth; how years ago bushels upon bushels of pennies, done up in five-shilling packets, had been literally carted like potatoes away from the bakehouse to go to London; how ponies were laden with sacks of silver groats, all paid over that furrowed counter for the golden flour, dust more golden than the sands of ancient Pactolus.

===Chinese===
村里人向来消息灵通，关于艾登家的财富有说不完的旧闻轶事：多少年前，一蒲式耳一蒲式耳的便士，按五先令一扎包好，像运土豆一样从面包房里一车车拉走运往伦敦；小马驮着一袋袋银格罗特，在那布满沟痕的柜台上换取金色的面粉——那面粉的金色比古帕克托洛斯河（Pactolus）的金沙还要灿烂。

===Original===
Reckless Amaryllis cared not a pin for all the spade guineas in the iron box.

===Chinese===
无所顾忌的阿玛丽莉斯对铁盒子里那些铲形基尼一个子儿也不稀罕。

===Original===
The old man sat down by the fire without removing his hat, motioning to her to shut the door, which she was loth to do, for the little room was smothered with smoke. Troubled with asthma, he coughed incessantly, and mopped his mouth with a vast silk handkerchief, but his dull blood craved for warmth, and he got his knees close to the grate, and piled up the coal till it smoked and smoked, and filled the close apartment with a suffocating haze of carbon. To be asked into Father Iden's sanctuary was an honour, but, like other honards, it had to be paid for.

===Chinese===
老人在炉边坐下，帽子也不摘，示意她把门关上。她不太情愿关门，因为这间小屋烟雾弥漫、令人窒息。他患有哮喘，不停地咳嗽，用一块巨大的丝帕抹着嘴，但迟钝的血液渴望温暖，他把膝盖凑近炉篦，不停地添煤，直到浓烟滚滚，整间密不透风的小屋被呛人的碳雾填满。被请进艾登老爹的内室是一种荣耀，但如同其他荣耀一样，是要付出代价的。

===Original===
Amaryllis gasped as she sat down, and tried to breathe as short as possible, to avoid inhaling more than she could bear.

===Chinese===
阿玛丽莉斯坐下来时被烟呛得直喘，尽量浅浅地呼吸，以免吸进超出她承受能力的烟气。

===Original===
"Books," said her grandfather, pointing to the bookcases, which occupied three sides of the room. "Books—you like books; look at them—go and see."

===Chinese===
"书，"她祖父指着占了三面墙的书架说。"书——你喜欢书；看看它们——去看看吧。"

===Original===
To humour him, Amaryllis rose, and appeared to look carefully along the shelves which she had scanned so many times before. They contained very good books indeed, such books as were not to be found elsewhere throughout the whole town of Woolhorton, and perhaps hardly in the county, old and rare volumes of price, such as Sotheby, Wilkinson, and Co. delight to offer to collectors, such as Bernard Quaritch, that giant of the modern auction room, would have written magnificent cheques for.

===Chinese===
为了顺着他，阿玛丽莉斯站起身来，装作仔细打量那些她已看过无数遍的书架。里面确实是些好书，整个伍尔霍顿镇上别处找不到，也许整个郡也难得一见——那些珍贵的古版稀本，正是苏富比-威尔金森公司（Sotheby, Wilkinson, and Co.）乐于卖给收藏家的那种，正是伯纳德·夸里奇（Bernard Quaritch）——现代拍卖场上的巨人——会开出巨额支票的那种。

===Original===
Did you ever see the Giant Quaritch in the auction-room bidding for books? It is one of the sights of London, let me tell you, to anyone who thinks or is alive to the present day. Most sights are reputations merely—the pale reflection of things that were real once. This sight is something of the living time, the day in which we live. Get an *Athenaeum* in the season, examine the advertisements of book auctions, and attend the next great sale of some famous library.

===Chinese===
你见过巨人夸里奇在拍卖场上竞拍图书吗？我告诉你，这对任何有思想、对当下时代有所感知的人来说，都是伦敦的一景。大多数景点不过是虚名——曾经真实存在过的事物的苍白映像。这一景却是活生生的当下、我们身处的这个时代的产物。在拍卖季买一份《雅典娜神殿》杂志（*Athenaeum*），查查书刊拍卖广告，去参加下一场著名藏书的大拍卖吧。

===Original===
You have a recollection of the giant who sat by the highway and devoured the pilgrims who passed? This giant sits in the middle of the ring and devours the books set loose upon their travels after the repose of centuries.

===Chinese===
你可还记得那个坐在大路旁吞食过路朝圣者的巨人？这位巨人坐在拍卖圈的正中央，把那些沉睡了数百年后重获自由、踏上旅程的书籍一一吞入腹中。

===Original===
What prices to give! No one can withstand him. From Paris they send agents with a million francs at their back; from Berlin and Vienna come the eager snappers-up of much considered trifles, but in vain. They only get what the Giant chooses to leave them.

===Chinese===
开什么价都行！没有人能抵挡他。巴黎方面派出背后有百万法郎撑腰的代理人；柏林和维也纳来了那些对琐碎之物趋之若鹜的急切买家，但都是徒劳。他们只能得到巨人挑剩下给他们的东西。

===Original===
Books that nobody ever heard of fetch £50, £60, £100, £200; wretched little books never opened since they were printed; dull duodecimos on the course of the river Wein; nondescript indescribable twaddling local books in Italian, Spanish, queer French, written and printed in some unknown foreign village; read them—you might as well try to amuse yourself with a Chinese pamphlet! What earthly value they are of cannot be discovered. They were composed by authors whose names are gone like the sand washed by the Nile into the sea before Herodotus. They contain no beautiful poetry, no elevated thought, no scientific discovery; they are simply so much paper, printing, and binding, so many years old, and it is for that age, printing, and binding that the money is paid.

===Chinese===
没人听说过的书能卖五十、六十、一百、两百英镑；那些自印刷以来从未被人翻开过的可怜巴巴的小书；关于魏恩河（Wein）水道的枯燥十二开本；用意大利文、西班牙文、古怪法文书写的、在某个无名外国村庄里出版印刷的、不知所云的本地杂书——读它们？你还不如去找一本中文小册子来消遣！它们到底有什么价值，实在无从得知。它们的作者早已被世人遗忘，如同希罗多德之前就被尼罗河冲入大海的泥沙一般。它们不含优美的诗歌、没有崇高的思想、没有科学发现；它们不过是纸张、印刷和装订的产物，存在了那么多年，人们为之付钱，买的就是那份年份、印刷和装订。

===Original===
