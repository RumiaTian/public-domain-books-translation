## Shadow / 影子
===Original===

A Parable

Yea! though I walk through the valley of the *Shadow*.

> Psalm of David

Ye who read are still among the living; but I who write shall have long since gone my way into the region of shadows. For indeed strange things shall happen, and secret things be known, and many centuries shall pass away, ere these memorials be seen of men. And, when seen, there will be some to disbelieve, and some to doubt, and yet a few who will find much to ponder upon in the characters here graven with a stylus of iron.

The year had been a year of terror, and of feelings more intense than terror for which there is no name upon the earth. For many prodigies and signs had taken place, and far and wide, over sea and land, the black wings of the Pestilence were spread abroad. To those, nevertheless, cunning in the stars, it was not unknown that the heavens wore an aspect of ill; and to me, the Greek Oinos, among others, it was evident that now had arrived the alternation of that seven hundred and ninety-fourth year when, at the entrance of Aries, the planet Jupiter is conjoined with the red ring of the terrible Saturnus. The peculiar spirit of the skies, if I mistake not greatly, made itself manifest, not only in the physical orb of the earth, but in the souls, imaginations, and meditations of mankind.

——一则寓言

是啊！我虽行过「影」之幽谷。

——大卫诗篇

你们这些读它的人，还活在世上；而我这个写下它的人，早已走上了去往幽影之域的路。因为确实，将出种种怪事，将泄种种秘辛，许多个世纪将流逝过去，而后这些手记才会落到人的眼前。而一旦见了天日，必有人不信，有人存疑，也必有寥寥几个，在这铁笔凿下的字句里，寻得许多值得沉思的东西。

那一年，是恐怖的一年，是比恐怖更烈、而世上无名可称之情的一年。因为出了许多异象和征兆，海陆四方，瘟疫的黑翼四处张开。然而，精通星象的人并非不知天象之凶；而我——希腊人奥伊诺斯——同别人一样看得明白：那第七百九十四个年头又到了——在白羊宫的入口，木星与可怕的萨图恩的红色光环相合之年。天宇的特有精魂，如果我没有大错，不但显在了大地的有形之球上，也显在了人的灵魂、想象与沉思里。

===Original===

Over some flasks of the red Chian wine, within the walls of a noble hall, in a dim city called Ptolemais, we sat, at night, a company of seven. And to our chamber there was no entrance save by a lofty door of brass: and the door was fashioned by the artisan Corinnos, and, being of rare workmanship, was fastened from within. Black draperies, likewise, in the gloomy room, shut out from our view the moon, the lurid stars, and the peopleless streets﻿—but the boding and the memory of Evil, they would not be so excluded. There were things around us and about of which I can render no distinct account﻿—things material and spiritual﻿—heaviness in the atmosphere﻿—a sense of suffocation﻿—anxiety﻿—and, above all, that terrible state of existence which the nervous experience when the senses are keenly living and awake, and meanwhile the powers of thought lie dormant. A dead weight hung upon us. It hung upon our limbs﻿—upon the household furniture﻿—upon the goblets from which we drank; and all things were depressed, and borne down thereby﻿—all things save only the flames of the seven lamps which illumined our revel. Uprearing themselves in tall slender lines of light, they thus remained burning all pallid and motionless; and in the mirror which their lustre formed upon the round table of ebony at which we sat, each of us there assembled beheld the pallor of his own countenance, and the unquiet glare in the downcast eyes of his companions. Yet we laughed and were merry in our proper way﻿—which was hysterical; and sang the songs of Anacreon﻿—which are madness; and drank deeply﻿—although the purple wine reminded us of blood. For there was yet another tenant of our chamber in the person of young Zoilus. Dead, and at full length he lay, enshrouded;﻿—the genius and the demon of the scene. Alas! he bore no portion in our mirth, save that his countenance, distorted with the plague, and his eyes, in which Death had but half extinguished the fire of the pestilence, seemed to take such interest in our merriment as the dead may haply take in the merriment of those who are to die. But although I, Oinos, felt that the eyes of the departed were upon me, still I forced myself not to perceive the bitterness of their expression, and gazing down steadily into the depths of the ebony mirror, sang with a loud and sonorous voice the songs of the son of Teios.

夜晩，在一座名叫托勒密迈的昏暗城里，一座华贵厅堂的高墙之内，我们七人围坐着，面前是几瓶希俄斯红酒。通往我们这间屋子的路，只有一扇高大的黄铜之门：门出自匠人科林诺斯之手，做工珍罕，从里面闩死。幽暗的屋里，一重重黑色帷幔，又把月亮、惨绿的星和无人的人行街，一概遮在我们眼外——可是凶兆，还有对「恶」的记忆，却是遮不住的。四围有种种东西，我无法分说它们——有形的东西和无形的东西——大气里的滞重——一种窒息之感——焦灼——而尤其可怕的，是神经病患者熟知的那种活状态：感官敏锐地醒着、活着，而思想的官能却沉沉昏睡。一团死沉的重担压着我们。它压在我们的四肢上——压在家具上——压在我们举杯的杯盏上；万物都被它压得垂头丧气——唯独照亮我们宴饮的那七盏灯的火焰不在此列。它们以高挑细瘦的光柱自行直立，烧得一片惨白，纹丝不动；而在那光于我们围坐的黑檀圆桌上凝成的镜面里，我们每一个都照见了自己脸上的死白，照见了同伴垂眼里不安的凶光。然而我们笑，我们以我们特有的方式作乐——那是歇斯底里的方式；我们唱阿那克里翁的歌——那是疯狂的歌；我们痛饮——尽管紫红的酒叫我们想起血。因为我们屋里还有一位住户：年轻的佐伊卢斯。他死了，直挺挺躺着，裹着衾衣——这一幕的天才与恶魔。唉！我们的欢乐里没有他的份儿，只是那张被瘟疫扭曲的脸、那双死亡只扑灭了一半疫火的眼睛，仿佛对我们这场作乐生出了兴趣——正如死人也许会对将死之人的作乐生出兴趣。可是我，奥伊诺斯，虽然觉得亡者的眼睛盯着我，仍强使自己不去察觉它们眼神里的苦涩，只把目光定定沉入黑檀之镜的深处，朗声唱起忒奥斯之子的歌。

But gradually my songs they ceased, and their echoes, rolling afar off among the sable draperies of the chamber, became weak, and undistinguishable, and so faded away. And lo! from among those sable draperies where the sounds of the song departed, there came forth a dark and undefined shadow﻿—a shadow such as the moon, when low in heaven, might fashion from the figure of a man: but it was the shadow neither of man nor of God, nor of any familiar thing. And quivering awhile among the draperies of the room, it at length rested in full view upon the surface of the door of brass. But the shadow was vague, and formless, and indefinite, and was the shadow neither of man nor of God﻿—neither God of Greece, nor God of Chaldaea, nor any Egyptian God. And the shadow rested upon the brazen doorway, and under the arch of the entablature of the door, and moved not, nor spoke any word, but there became stationary and remained. And the door whereupon the shadow rested was, if I remember aright, over against the feet of the young Zoilus enshrouded. But we, the seven there assembled, having seen the shadow as it came out from among the draperies, dared not steadily behold it, but cast down our eyes, and gazed continually into the depths of the mirror of ebony. And at length I, Oinos, speaking some low words, demanded of the shadow its dwelling and its appellation. And the shadow answered, “I am **shadow**, and my dwelling is near to the Catacombs of Ptolemais, and hard by those dim plains of Helusion which border upon the foul Charonian canal.” And then did we, the seven, start from our seats in horror, and stand trembling, and shuddering, and aghast, for the tones in the voice of the shadow were not the tones of any one being, but of a multitude of beings, and, varying in their cadences from syllable to syllable fell duskly upon our ears in the well-remembered and familiar accents of many thousand departed friends.

可是渐渐地，我的歌止了，歌的回声在屋内黑帷之间远远滚过，转弱，模糊，就这样消散了。看哪！从歌声消逝的那些黑帷之间，走出了一团黑暗而无定的影子——一个月亮低垂在天上时，或许会照人影而造出的那种影子：可它既不是人的影子，也不是神的影子，更不是任何熟知之物的影子。它在屋里帷幔间颤了片刻，终于整个停驻在黄铜门的门面上，一览无余。可那影子模糊、无形、无定，既非人也非神之影——既非希腊的神，也非迦勒底的神，也非埃及的任何神。那影子停驻在黄铜的门上，停在门楣的拱下，不动，不言，就此定住，留驻在那里。那扇被影子罩住的门，如果我记得不错，正对着裹衾的年轻佐伊卢斯的脚。而我们，在座的七人，看见那影子从帷幔间出来，谁也不敢正眼看它，只纷纷垂下眼，不断凝望着黑檀之镜的深处。末了，我，奥伊诺斯，低声说了一句什么，向那影子问它的居处和名号。影子答道：「我是『影』，我的居所近挨着托勒密迈的地下墓穴，紧傍着那片同污浊的卡戎运河接壤的昏暗的极乐原野。」于是我们七个，惊怖地从座位上蹦起，站着发抖、战栗、呆若木鸡——因为那影子嗓中的声调，不是任何单独一个存在的声调，而是芸芸众生的声调，音调一字一变，昏昏地落在我们耳中，用的正是那千万个亡友我们耳熟能详的乡音。
