## The Masque of the Red Death / 红死魔的假面舞会
===Original===

The “Red Death” had long devastated the country. No pestilence had ever been so fatal, or so hideous. Blood was its Avatar and its seal﻿—the redness and the horror of blood. There were sharp pains, and sudden dizziness, and then profuse bleeding at the pores, with dissolution. The scarlet stains upon the body and especially upon the face of the victim, were the pest ban which shut him out from the aid and from the sympathy of his fellow-men. And the whole seizure, progress and termination of the disease, were the incidents of half an hour.

But the Prince Prospero was happy and dauntless and sagacious. When his dominions were half depopulated, he summoned to his presence a thousand hale and lighthearted friends from among the knights and dames of his court, and with these retired to the deep seclusion of one of his castellated abbeys. This was an extensive and magnificent structure, the creation of the prince’s own eccentric yet august taste. A strong and lofty wall girdled it in. This wall had gates of iron. The courtiers, having entered, brought furnaces and massy hammers and welded the bolts. They resolved to leave means neither of ingress or egress to the sudden impulses of despair or of frenzy from within. The abbey was amply provisioned. With such precautions the courtiers might bid defiance to contagion. The external world could take care of itself. In the meantime it was folly to grieve, or to think. The prince had provided all the appliances of pleasure. There were buffoons, there were improvisatori, there were ballet-dancers, there were musicians, there was Beauty, there was wine. All these and security were within. Without was the “Red Death.”

It was toward the close of the fifth or sixth month of his seclusion, and while the pestilence raged most furiously abroad, that the Prince Prospero entertained his thousand friends at a masked ball of the most unusual magnificence.

「红死魔」（the Red Death）摧残国土久矣。从来没有一种瘟疫这样致命，这样骇人。血是它的阿凡达与印玺——血的红，血的恐怖。剧痛袭来，接着是一阵突如其来的眩晕，然后毛孔大量渗血，随即消陨。被害人身上，尤其脸上，那些猩红的斑点，便是逐他出境的疫符：从此得不到同类的救助与同情。而这病的侵袭、进程与终结，统共是半个钟头内的事。

普洛斯佩罗亲王（Prince Prospero）却快乐、无畏、精明。他的国土死去一半时，他从宫廷的骑士与贵妇中召来一千名健壮快活的友人，同他们一起退进他一座设堡的修道院的深隐之处。那是一座宏大壮丽的构筑，亲王自己那古怪然而威严的趣味的手笔。一圈高大坚固的墙把它围住。墙上是铁门。朝臣们进了门，便搬来熔炉和沉重的铁锤，把门闩焊死。他们决意不留一丝进出的门路，来应付从内里突然袭来的绝望或疯狂的冲动。修道院里储粮充足。有这样周全的防备，朝臣们尽可向疫气挑战。外面的世界尽可自谋生路。与此同时，悲伤或思考都是蠢事。亲王已备齐一切享乐的器械。有丑角，有即兴诗人，有芭蕾舞者，有乐师，有美，有酒。这一切连同安全都在墙内。墙外是「红死魔」。

在隐居的第五或第六个月将尽、瘟疫在外面肆虐最烈的时候，普洛斯佩罗亲王设了一场极尽奇丽的假面舞会，款待他的一千个朋友。

It was a voluptuous scene, that masquerade. But first let me tell of the rooms in which it was held. There were seven﻿—an imperial suite. In many palaces, however, such suites form a long and straight vista, while the folding doors slide back nearly to the walls on either hand, so that the view of the whole extent is scarcely impeded. Here the case was very different; as might have been expected from the duke’s love of the *bizarre*. The apartments were so irregularly disposed that the vision embraced but little more than one at a time. There was a sharp turn at every twenty or thirty yards, and at each turn a novel effect. To the right and left, in the middle of each wall, a tall and narrow Gothic window looked out upon a closed corridor which pursued the windings of the suite. These windows were of stained glass whose color varied in accordance with the prevailing hue of the decorations of the chamber into which it opened. That at the eastern extremity was hung, for example, in blue﻿—and vividly blue were its windows. The second chamber was purple in its ornaments and tapestries, and here the panes were purple. The third was green throughout, and so were the casements. The fourth was furnished and lighted with orange﻿—the fifth with white﻿—the sixth with violet. The seventh apartment was closely shrouded in black velvet tapestries that hung all over the ceiling and down the walls, falling in heavy folds upon a carpet of the same material and hue. But in this chamber only, the color of the windows failed to correspond with the decorations. The panes here were scarlet﻿—a deep blood color. Now in no one of the seven apartments was there any lamp or candelabrum, amid the profusion of golden ornaments that lay scattered to and fro or depended from the roof. There was no light of any kind emanating from lamp or candle within the suite of chambers. But in the corridors that followed the suite, there stood, opposite to each window, a heavy tripod, bearing a brazier of fire that projected its rays through the tinted glass and so glaringly illumined the room. And thus were produced a multitude of gaudy and fantastic appearances.

那假面舞会是一场穷奢极欲的景象。但先容我讲讲举行它的那几间屋子。共七间——一列帝王气派的套房。然而在许多宫殿里，这样的套房是一条又长又直的纵深，折叠门几乎推到两边的墙上，全局的视野几乎不受阻拦。这里的情形却大不相同——这本在意料之中，凭公爵对*古怪*的癖好。屋子的布置极不规整，视野一次只能收进一间多一点。每二三十码便是一道急转，每一转便是一种新奇的效果。左右两面，在每面墙的中央，一扇高而窄的哥特窗，望着一条封闭的走廊，那走廊沿着套房的曲折蜿蜒。窗是彩色的玻璃，颜色随它开向的那间屋子里占上风的陈设之色而变。东端那间，例如，悬着蓝色——它的窗也是鲜明的蓝。第二间屋的陈设与挂毯是紫色的，这里的窗玻璃便是紫色。第三间通体是绿的，窗槅也是。第四间以橙色陈设并照明——第五间白色——第六间紫色。第七间罩在黑丝绒的挂毯里，从天花板直垂到墙，成沉沉的褶裥落在一块同样料子同样颜色的地毯上。但唯独这间屋子，窗的颜色不与陈设相应。这里的玻璃是猩红——深深的血色。而在七间屋里，任何一间都没有灯或烛台，虽然金饰四处散布或从屋顶垂下，灿然满目。这套房间里没有一点从灯或烛里发出的光。但顺着套房的走廊里，每扇窗的对面立着一只沉重的三脚架，架上承着一只火盆，把光芒投过有色玻璃，把屋子照得炫目。于是便生出无数俗艳而幻怪的景象。

But in the western or black chamber the effect of the firelight that streamed upon the dark hangings through the blood-tinted panes, was ghastly in the extreme, and produced so wild a look upon the countenances of those who entered, that there were few of the company bold enough to set foot within its precincts at all.

It was in this apartment, also, that there stood against the western wall, a gigantic clock of ebony. Its pendulum swung to and fro with a dull, heavy, monotonous clang; and when the minute-hand made the circuit of the face, and the hour was to be stricken, there came from the brazen lungs of the clock a sound which was clear and loud and deep and exceedingly musical, but of so peculiar a note and emphasis that, at each lapse of an hour, the musicians of the orchestra were constrained to pause, momentarily, in their performance, to hearken to the sound; and thus the waltzers perforce ceased their evolutions; and there was a brief disconcert of the whole gay company; and, while the chimes of the clock yet rang, it was observed that the giddiest grew pale, and the more aged and sedate passed their hands over their brows as if in confused reverie or meditation. But when the echoes had fully ceased, a light laughter at once pervaded the assembly; the musicians looked at each other and smiled as if at their own nervousness and folly, and made whispering vows, each to the other, that the next chiming of the clock should produce in them no similar emotion; and then, after the lapse of sixty minutes (which embrace three thousand and six hundred seconds of the Time that flies), there came yet another chiming of the clock, and then were the same disconcert and tremulousness and meditation as before.

But, in spite of these things, it was a gay and magnificent revel. The tastes of the duke were peculiar. He had a fine eye for colors and effects. He disregarded the decora of mere fashion. His plans were bold and fiery, and his conceptions glowed with barbaric lustre. There are some who would have thought him mad. His followers felt that he was not. It was necessary to hear and see and touch him to be *sure* that he was not.

也是在这间屋里，靠着西墙立着一座黑檀的巨钟。它的钟摆来来回回地摆，声调沉闷、滞重、单调；当分针在钟面上走完一圈、报时的时刻一到，钟的黄铜肺腑里便发出一种声音，清晰，响亮，深沉，极富乐感，然而音色与重音如此奇特，以至每逢一点钟过去，乐队的乐师便不得不在演奏里停一瞬，听那声音；于是旋转的舞者也不得不停下他们的回旋；整个欢快的满座宾客起一阵短促的慌乱；而钟声还在响时，便可以看出最轻狂的人脸色发白，年长而稳重些的把手按上额头，仿佛在纷乱的遐想或沉思里。但回声一停，一阵轻笑立刻弥漫全场；乐师们你看看我，我看看你，笑了，仿佛笑自己的紧张与愚蠢，又互相低声发誓：下一次钟响，绝不再起同样的心绪；然后，过了六十分钟（其中包着飞逝的时间的三千六百秒），钟又敲响，于是又是同样的慌乱、颤抖与沉思。

He had directed, in great part, the moveable embellishments of the seven chambers, upon occasion of this great fête; and it was his own guiding taste which had given character to the masqueraders. Be sure they were grotesque. There were much glare and glitter and piquancy and phantasm﻿—much of what has been since seen in *Hernani*. There were arabesque figures with unsuited limbs and appointments. There were delirious fancies such as the madman fashions. There was much of the beautiful, much of the wanton, much of the bizarre, something of the terrible, and not a little of that which might have excited disgust. To and fro in the seven chambers there stalked, in fact, a multitude of dreams. And these﻿—the dreams﻿—writhed in and about, taking hue from the rooms, and causing the wild music of the orchestra to seem as the echo of their steps. And, anon, there strikes the ebony clock which stands in the hall of the velvet. And then, for a moment, all is still, and all is silent save the voice of the clock. The dreams are stiff-frozen as they stand. But the echoes of the chime die away﻿—they have endured but an instant﻿—and a light, half-subdued laughter floats after them as they depart. And now again the music swells, and the dreams live, and writhe to and fro more merrily than ever, taking hue from the many-tinted windows through which stream the rays from the tripods. 为着这场大宴，七间屋的活动装饰大部分由他亲自指点；给化装者定下格调的，也是他自己那主导的趣味。无疑他们怪诞。处处是闪耀、辉煌、辛辣、幻影——许多此后在《埃尔纳尼》（Hernani）里见过的东西。有四肢与配备不称的阿拉伯花式的形影。有狂人编织的那类谵妄的幻想。美的很多，妖冶的很多，古怪的很多，可怕的一些，而足以令人作呕的也不少。实际上，在七间屋里荡来荡去的，是一大群梦。而这些——这些梦——在屋里屋外扭动，从屋子借得颜色，把乐队的狂野的音乐，弄成仿佛他们脚步的回声。而俄顷，绒厅里那座黑檀钟又敲了。于是，有一瞬，一切都停，一切都静，只剩钟的声音。梦们僵着，冻着，站在那里。但钟声的回声渐渐熄灭——它们只延了一瞬——一串轻轻压住的笑，跟着它们飘散。此刻音乐又涌起，梦们复活了，比先前更欢畅地扭来扭去，从三脚架的光线流过的那些五彩的窗上借得颜色。但七间屋里最西边的那间，此刻没有化装者敢去；因为夜在流逝；血色的玻璃里淌进更红的光；黑檀帷幔的黑压得人惊惶；谁的脚一踏上那黑檀的地毯，近旁的黑檀钟便向他发出一声闷沉的轰响，比那在其余几间屋的较远的欢愉里、落进*他们*耳朵的任何一响，都更庄严，更沉重。

But these other apartments were densely crowded, and in them beat feverishly the heart of life. And the revel went whirlingly on, until at length there commenced the sounding of midnight upon the clock. And then the music ceased, as I have told; and the evolutions of the waltzers were quieted; and there was an uneasy cessation of all things as before. But now there were twelve strokes to be sounded by the bell of the clock; and thus it happened, perhaps, that more of thought crept, with more of time, into the meditations of the thoughtful among those who revelled. And thus, too, it happened, perhaps, that before the last echoes of the last chime had utterly sunk into silence, there were many individuals in the crowd who had found leisure to become aware of the presence of a masked figure which had arrested the attention of no single individual before. And the rumor of this new presence having spread itself whisperingly around, there arose at length from the whole company a buzz, or murmur, expressive of disapprobation and surprise﻿—then, finally, of terror, of horror, and of disgust.

In an assembly of phantasms such as I have painted, it may well be supposed that no ordinary appearance could have excited such sensation. In truth the masquerade license of the night was nearly unlimited; but the figure in question had out-Heroded Herod, and gone beyond the bounds of even the prince’s indefinite decorum. There are chords in the hearts of the most reckless which cannot be touched without emotion. Even with the utterly lost, to whom life and death are equally jests, there are matters of which no jest can be made. The whole company, indeed, seemed now deeply to feel that in the costume and bearing of the stranger neither wit nor propriety existed. The figure was tall and gaunt, and shrouded from head to foot in the habiliments of the grave. The mask which concealed the visage was made so nearly to resemble the countenance of a stiffened corpse that the closest scrutiny must have had difficulty in detecting the cheat. And yet all this might have been endured, if not approved, by the mad revellers around. But the mummer had gone so far as to assume the type of the Red Death. His vesture was dabbled in *blood*﻿—and his broad brow, with all the features of the face, was besprinkled with the scarlet horror.

但其余几间屋子挤得密密层层，生命的心在那里发烧般跳动。狂欢旋转着继续，直到午夜开始敲响。于是音乐停了，如我已经讲过；舞者的回旋静了；万物起一阵同先前一样的不安的停顿。但这一回，钟的铃要敲十二下；也许因此，更多的时间带来更多的思绪，爬进狂欢者中间那些善思者的冥想。也许因此，在最后一响的最后回声全然沉入寂静之前，人群里已有许多人得着闲空，察觉一个假面身影的存在——先前它不曾引起任何一个人的注意。这新来的存在的谣言低语着散开，终于从整个人群中升起一片嗡嗡，或曰喃喃，表示不满与惊异——最后，是恐怖，惊骇，与嫌恶。

亲王站在蓝屋里，身旁簇拥着一群脸色发白的朝臣。起初，他一开口，这一群人便有一阵轻轻的冲向那闯入者的移动——那人此刻也在近旁，正迈着从容而威严的步子，向说话的人走近。但由于那化装者的疯狂的僭妄在全体的心里激起的一种无名的敬畏，竟找不到一个人伸手去抓他；于是他畅通无阻，走到离亲王之身一码之内；而那庞大的人群仿佛被同一个冲动驱使，从屋子中央缩到墙边，他便一路不间断地——带着他从头显出的那种庄严而均匀的步子——穿过蓝屋到紫屋——穿过紫屋到绿屋——穿过绿屋到橙屋——再从橙屋到白屋——甚至从那里又到紫屋，才有人下定决心动手拦他。然而这时，普洛斯佩罗亲王——被暴怒和片刻怯懦的羞耻逼疯了——匆匆冲过那六间屋子；一个人也不曾跟着他，因为一种致命的恐怖攫住了所有的人。他高举一柄出鞘的短剑，以疾速的狂怒，逼近到那后退的身影三英尺四英尺之内，后者恰已走到绒厅的尽头，突然转过身来，面对他的追逐者。一声尖利的叫喊——短剑闪着光落在黑檀的地毯上，紧接着，普洛斯佩罗亲王也在那地毯上扑倒死去。于是，绝望之下的狂勇一经召唤，一大群狂欢者立刻冲进那间黑屋，抓住那化装的人——那高大的身影一动不动，笔直立在黑檀钟的阴影里——当他们以这样粗暴的蛮劲抓住那尸衣与尸状的假面，发觉里面空无一物、没有任何有形之体时，不由得发出一阵无法言说的恐怖的抽气。

===Original===

And now was acknowledged the presence of the Red Death. He had come like a thief in the night. And one by one dropped the revellers in the blood-bedewed halls of their revel, and died each in the despairing posture of his fall. And the life of the ebony clock went out with that of the last of the gay. And the flames of the tripods expired. And Darkness and Decay and the Red Death held illimitable dominion over all.
