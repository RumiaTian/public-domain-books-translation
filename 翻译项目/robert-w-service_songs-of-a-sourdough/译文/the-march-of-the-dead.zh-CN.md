## The March of the Dead / 亡者行军

===Original===
The cruel war was over﻿—oh, the triumph was so sweet!
 

 We watched the troops returning, through our tears;
 

 There was triumph, triumph, triumph down the scarlet glittering street,
 

 And you scarce could hear the music for the cheers.
 

 And you scarce could see the housetops for the flags that flew between,
 

 The bells were pealing madly to the sky;
 

 And everyone was shouting for the Soldiers of the Queen,
 

 And the glory of an age was passing by.
And then there came a shadow, swift and sudden, dark and drear;
 

 The bells were silent, not an echo stirred.
 

 The flags were drooping sullenly, the men forgot to cheer;
 

 We waited, and we never spoke a word.
 

 The sky grew darker, darker, till from out the gloomy rack
 

 There came a voice that checked the heart with dread:
 

 “Tear down, tear down your bunting now, and hang up sable black;
 

 They are coming﻿—it’s the Army of the Dead.”
They were coming, they were coming, gaunt and ghastly, sad and slow;
 

 They were coming, all the crimson wrecks of pride;
 

 With faces seared, and cheeks red smeared, and haunting eyes of woe,
 

 And clotted holes the khaki couldn’t hide.
 

 Oh, the clammy brow of anguish! the livid, foam-flecked lips!
 

 The reeling ranks of ruin swept along!
 

 The limb that trailed, the hand that failed, the bloody fingertips!
 

 And oh, the dreary rhythm of their song!
“They left us on the veldt-side, but we felt we couldn’t stop,
 

 On this, our England’s crowning festal day;
 

 We’re the men of Magersfontein, we’re the men of Spion Kop,
 

 Colenso﻿—we’re the men who had to pay.
 

 We’re the men who paid the blood-price. Shall the grave be all our gain?
 

 You owe us. Long and heavy is the score.
 

 Then cheer us for our glory now, and cheer us for our pain,
 

 And cheer us as ye never cheered before.”
The folks were white and stricken, and each tongue seemed weighed with lead;
 

 Each heart was clutched in hollow hand of ice;
 

 And every eye was staring at the horror of the dead,
 

 The pity of the men who paid the price.
 

 They were come, were come to mock us, in the first flush of our peace;
 

 Through writhing lips their teeth were all agleam;
 

 They were coming in their thousands﻿—oh, would they never cease!
 

 I closed my eyes, and then﻿—it was a dream.
There was triumph, triumph, triumph down the scarlet gleaming street;
 

 The town was mad, a man was like a boy.
 

 A thousand flags were flaming where the sky and city meet;
 

 A thousand bells were thundering the joy.
 

 There was music, mirth and sunshine; but some eyes shone with regret:
 

 And while we stun with cheers our homing braves,
 

 O God, in Thy great mercy, let us nevermore forget
 

 The graves they left behind, the bitter graves.

===Chinese===
残酷的战争结束了——哦，胜利多么甘美！
 
 
 我们含着泪看队伍归来；
 
 
 沿着猩红闪耀的街道，凯旋，凯旋，凯旋，
 
 
 欢呼声盖得你几乎听不见乐曲。
 
 
 飘舞的旗子遮得你几乎看不清屋顶，
 
 
 钟声朝着天空疯狂鸣响；
 
 
 人人都在为女王的士兵欢呼，
 
 
 一个时代的荣光正走过。
接着来了个影子，迅疾突然，昏暗凄冷；
 
 
 钟声哑了，一点回响也没有。
 
 
 旗帜阴沉地垂着，人们忘了欢呼；
 
 
 我们等着，谁也不说一句话。
 
 
 天色越来越暗，越来越暗，直到从阴郁的乱云里
 
 
 传出一个声音，吓得人心一紧：
 
 
 「摘下，摘下你们的彩旗，挂上乌黑的丧色；
 
 
 他们来了——那是亡者大军。」
他们来了，他们来了，形容枯槁、惨淡、哀伤而迟缓；
 
 
 他们来了，全是骄傲的血红残骸；
 
 
 焦痕累累的脸，涂抹着红的腮，哀愁的眼睛挥之不去，
 
 
 卡其布遮不住凝着血块的窟窿。
 
 
 哦，苦痛那湿冷的额头！青灰的、溅着白沫的嘴唇！
 
 
 蹒跚的溃败队伍一扫而过！
 
 
 拖着的残肢，废掉的手，血污的指尖！
 
 
 还有哦，他们歌里那凄凉的节拍！
「他们把我们撂在草原上，可我们觉得不能停下，
 
 
 就在今天，英格兰这至高的庆典之日；
 
 
 我们是马赫斯方丹的人，我们是斯皮恩山的人，
 
 
 科伦索——我们是不得不付账的人。
 
 
 我们是付了血价的人。难道坟墓就是我们的全部所得？
 
 
 你们欠我们的。这笔账又长又重。
 
 
 那就为我们的荣光欢呼吧，为我们的创痛欢呼，
 
 
 用你们从未有过的声势给我们喝彩。」
人们脸色惨白，呆若木鸡，每条舌头都像坠了铅；
 
 
 每颗心都被冰的空手攥住；
 
 
 每只眼睛都瞪着亡者的恐怖，
 
 
 瞪着那些付了代价的人的可哀。
 
 
 他们来了，是来嘲弄我们的，在我们和平的初醉里；
 
 
 扭动的嘴唇后面，牙齿森森发亮；
 
 
 他们成千上万地涌来——哦，难道永没有个完！
 
 
 我闭上眼，然后——原来是一场梦。
沿着猩红闪亮的街道，凯旋，凯旋，凯旋；
 
 
 全城都疯了，男人都成了孩子。
 
 
 千百面旗帜在天与城相接处燃烧；
 
 
 千百口钟轰鸣着欢乐。
 
 
 有音乐、欢笑和阳光；可有些眼睛闪着憾恨：
 
 
 当我们用欢呼震晕凯旋的勇士，
 
 
 哦上帝，以祢的大慈悲，愿我们永不要忘记
 
 
 他们留在身后的坟，那些苦涩的坟。
