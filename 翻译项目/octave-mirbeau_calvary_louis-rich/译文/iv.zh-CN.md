## IV / 四

===Original===
“Madame Juliette Roux, if you please?”

“Will Monsieur please come in?” the maid asked.

Without demanding my name or waiting for my answer, she made me cross a small, dark antechamber, and led me into a room where at first I could only distinguish a lamp covered by a large lampshade, which burned low in a corner. The maid raised the flame of the lamp and carried out an otter skin cape which had been thrown on the sofa.

===Chinese===
“劳驾，请问朱丽叶特·鲁夫人在家吗？”

“先生请进，好吗？”女仆问道。

她既不问我的名姓，也不等我答话，便领我穿过一间狭小幽暗的前厅，把我带进一间屋子；起初我只分辨得出角落里一盏罩着大灯罩的灯，灯火燃得很低。女仆把灯芯捻高，又抱走一件扔在沙发上的水獭皮斗篷。

===Original===
“I will go tell madame,” she said.

And she disappeared, leaving me alone in the room.

===Chinese===
“我去禀报夫人。”她说。

说罢人就不见了，把我独自留在屋里。

===Original===
So I was at her house! For eight days the thought of this visit had tortured me. I had no special business, I simply wanted to see Juliette; some kind of keen curiosity, which I did not stop to analyze, drew me to her. Several times I had gone to the Rue Saint Petersbourg with the firm intention of calling on her, but at the last moment my nerve failed me, and I left without mustering sufficient courage to cross her threshold. And now I was the most embarrassed being in the world, and I regretted my foolish step, for obviously it was a foolish step. How would she receive me? What should I say? What caused me the greatest uneasiness was that after I had made a thorough search in my brain I found not a single phrase, not a single word with which to begin our conversation when Juliette entered. What if words should fail me and I should be left standing here with gaping mouth! How ridiculous that would be!

===Chinese===
我居然到了她家里！八天来，登门拜访这个念头一直折磨着我。我并没有什么正经事，只是想见见朱丽叶特；某种我未及细想的强烈好奇心，把我引到了她跟前。有好几回，我已经走到圣彼得堡路（Rue Saint Petersbourg），抱定主意要去登门，可到了最后关头又怯了阵，没敢跨进她的门槛便掉头走了。而此刻，我成了天底下最窘迫的人，直后悔自己走出这愚蠢的一步，因为这明摆着是愚蠢的一步。她会怎样接待我呢？我又该说些什么呢？最叫我惶惶不安的是，我在脑子里翻箱倒柜搜了一遍，竟找不出一句话、一个词，好在朱丽叶特进来时拿来开场。倘若到时候我张口结舌、干瞪眼站在这里，那可怎么好！那有多可笑啊！

===Original===
I examined the room into which Juliette was presently to come. It was a dressing room which also served as a parlor. It made a rather unfavorable impression on me. The toilet table, ostentatiously displayed with its two wash basins of cracked, pink cut glass, shocked me. The walls and ceiling, hung with loud red satin, the furniture, bordered with elaborate plush hangings, the knickknacks, costly and ugly, placed here and there on the furniture, the queer tables serving no apparent purpose, consols weighed down with heavy ornaments﻿—all this bespoke a vulgar taste. I noticed in the center of the mantelpiece, between two massive vases of onyx, a terra cotta statuette of Cupid, smiling with a sort of grimace and offering a flower held at the tips of his outspread fingers. Every detail revealed, on the one hand, a love of expensive and unrefined luxury, and on the other a regrettable predilection for romance and puerile affection. It was at once distressing and sentimental. Nevertheless, and that was a relief to me, I saw here no evidence of that incongruity, that transitory air, that severity of aspect so characteristic of ladies’ boarding houses, those apartments where one is made aware of a haggard existence, where by the number of knickknacks one can count the number of lovers who have passed there, lovers for an hour, a night, a year; where every chair tells of the lack of decency, the unfaithfulness; where on the glass one can see the tragedy of fortune’s fickleness; on the marble, traces of a tear still warm; on the candlestick, blood drops still moist. The door opened and Juliette appeared wearing a white, long flowing dress. I trembled, color came to my face; but she recognized me and, smiling that smile of hers which at last I found again, she stretched out her hand.

===Chinese===
我打量着朱丽叶特即将走进的这间屋子。这是一间梳妆室，兼作客厅。它给我的印象相当坏。梳妆台堂而皇之地摆在那里，上面两只粉色雕花玻璃洗脸盆都已有了裂纹，看得我刺眼。墙上和天花板上张着俗艳的红缎，家具镶着繁复的长毛绒边饰，小摆件又贵又丑，东一件西一件搁在家具上，几张莫名其妙的桌子看不出有什么用处，螺形支柱的小桌上压着沉甸甸的饰物——这一切都透出一股俗气的趣味。我注意到壁炉架正中，在两只厚重的缟玛瑙大花瓶之间，立着一尊陶土的丘比特小像，咧着一副近乎怪相的笑，张开的手指尖上擎着一朵花。每一个细节都显出两样东西：一方面是对于昂贵而不精雅的奢华的爱好，另一方面是一种可叹的偏爱，偏爱浪漫情调和孩子气的柔情。这既叫人心里发堵，又透着多愁善感。不过——这点让我松了口气——我在这里看不出那种不伦不类、那种浮萍过客的气味、那种冷峻的面目；那是女子寄宿公寓特有的标记。在那类公寓里，人能觉出一种憔悴的生活；从摆件的数目，可以数出先后打那里经过的情人的数目——一小时的情人，一夜的情人，一年的情人；那里每一把椅子都诉说着不知廉耻与负心背叛；镜子上看得出财运无常的悲剧；大理石上，泪痕犹温；烛台上，血滴未干。门开了，朱丽叶特走了进来，穿一袭白色的长裙，裙裾飘飘。我浑身一颤，血涌上脸；可她认出了我，脸上漾起那一抹我终于又寻见的微笑，向我伸出手来。

===Original===
“Ah! Monsieur Mintié!” she said, “how nice of you not to have forgotten me! Has it been long since you saw that eccentric Lirat?”

“Why, yes, Madame, I have not seen him since the day I had the honor of meeting you at his place.”

“Ah, my God, I thought you two never separated at all.”

“It is true,” I replied, “that I see him quite often. But I have been working all these days.”

===Chinese===
“啊！曼蒂埃先生！”她说，“您没有忘掉我，真好！您有日子没见那个怪人利拉了吧？”

“呃，是啊，夫人，打那天我有幸在他府上遇见您，就再没见过他。”

“啊，我的天，我还当你们俩压根儿不拆伴儿呢。”

“不错，”我答道，“我确实常同他见面。只是这些天我一直在埋头用功。”

===Original===
As I thought I detected a note of irony in the sound of her voice, I added, to provoke her:

“What a great artist, isn’t he?”

Juliette let this remark pass unanswered.

“So you are always working?” She took up the subject again. “For the rest, I am told you live like a regular recluse. Really, one sees very little of you, Monsieur Mintié!”

===Chinese===
我仿佛从她的嗓音里听出一丝讥诮，便又添上一句，存心撩她：

“多了不起的艺术家，是不是？”

朱丽叶特对这句话未予理睬。

“这么说，您总在用功？”她又拾起这个话头，“还有呢，人家告诉我，您过日子活像个道地的隐士。真的，简直见不着您的人影儿，曼蒂埃先生！”

===Original===
The conversation took a quite ordinary turn, the theatre furnishing food for nearly all of it. A remark which I made seemed to astound her, and she was rather scandalized.

“What, you don’t like the theatre? Is it possible﻿—and you an artist? I am passionately fond of it. The theatre is so amusing! We are going to the Varieté tonight, for the fourth time, mind you.”

A feeble yelp came from behind the door.

===Chinese===
谈话转上了极平常的路子，几乎全靠戏剧充当谈资。我说了一句什么，似乎叫她大吃一惊，她简直有点愤慨了。

“什么，您不喜欢剧院？可能吗——您还是个艺术家呢！我可迷它迷得要命。剧院多有意思呀！今晚我们还去杂剧院，已经是第四回了，您听好。”

门后传来一声细细的狗吠。

===Original===
“Ah, my God!” Juliette exclaimed, hurriedly rising. “My Spy whom I left in my room! Shall I present Spy to you, Monsieur Mintié? Don’t you know Spy?”

She opened the door, drew aside the hangings, which were very wide.

“Come, Spy!” she said coaxingly. “Where have you been, Spy? Come over here, poor thing!”

And I saw a diminutive little animal, with a pointed snout, long ears, advancing, dancing on its thin paws that resembled a spider’s legs and whose whole body, bent and skinny, quivered as though in fever. A ribbon of red silk, carefully tied on the side, encircled its neck in place of a collar.

===Chinese===
“啊，我的天！”朱丽叶特叫起来，慌忙起身。“我的斯派（Spy），我把它独自留在房里了！要不要把斯派介绍给您，曼蒂埃先生？您不认识斯派吗？”

她推开门，撩开很宽大的门帘。

“来，斯派！”她哄着说，“你上哪儿去了，斯派？到这儿来，可怜的小东西！”

于是我看见一只小不点儿的小动物，尖嘴巴，长耳朵，迈着细得像蜘蛛腿的爪子，颠颠地舞着往前走；它那整个身子佝偻干瘦，抖抖索索，像在发烧。脖子上没戴项圈，只在侧面端端正正系着一条红绸带。

===Original===
“Come on, Spy. Say hello to Monsieur Mintié!”

Spy turned on me his round, stupid and cruel eyes which were on a level with his head, and barked viciously.

“That’s right, Spy. Now give your paw. Will you give me your paw? Come, now!”

Juliette bent down and threatened the dog with her finger. Spy finally put his paw in his mistress’ hand. She picked him up, patted and embraced him.

===Chinese===
“来吧，斯派。跟曼蒂埃先生问个好！”

斯派把那双同脑袋一般高低、又圆又蠢又凶的眼睛转向我，恶狠狠地吠起来。

“这就对了，斯派。现在把爪子伸出来。肯把爪子给我吗？来呀！”

朱丽叶特弯下腰，伸出手指吓唬这狗。斯派终于把爪子放进女主人的手心。她把它抱起来，又是拍又是搂。

===Original===
“Oh! the dear little dog! Oh, darling dog! Oh, my love, my dearest Spy!”

She sat up again, still holding the dog in her arms like a child, rubbing her cheek against the snout of the frightful beast, whispering caressing and endearing words into his ears.

“Now show us that you are pleased, Spy! Show it to your little mammy!”

Spy barked again, then licked the lips of Juliette who joyously abandoned herself to these odious caresses.

“Ah, what a dear you are, Spy! Ah, how very, very nice you are!”

===Chinese===
“哦！亲爱的小狗！哦，宝贝狗！哦，我的爱，我最最亲爱的斯派！”

她重新坐直身子，仍像抱孩子似的把狗搂在怀里，拿脸颊蹭着这可怕畜生的嘴巴，对着它的耳朵，低低唤着疼爱的体己话。

“来，做给大家看看你多高兴，斯派！做给你的小妈妈看！”

斯派又叫了几声，随后去舔朱丽叶特的嘴唇；她快快活活地，听任这等可憎的爱抚落到自己身上。

“啊，你真是个宝贝，斯派！啊，你多么、多么乖呀！”

===Original===
And addressing herself to me, whom she seemed to have forgotten completely since Spy’s unfortunate entry, she suddenly asked:

“Do you like dogs, Monsieur Mintié?”

“Very much, Madame,” I answered.

Then she told me, with a wealth of childish detail, the history of Spy, his habits, his urgent needs, his tricks, the scraps with the housekeeper who could not stand him.

===Chinese===
随后她转向我——打斯派倒霉地登场以来，她似乎把我忘了个干净——突然问我：

“您喜欢狗吗，曼蒂埃先生？”

“非常喜欢，夫人。”我答道。

于是她给我讲起斯派的历史，带着一大堆孩子气的细节：它的习惯，它火烧眉毛的急事，它的把戏，还有它同那位容不下它的女管家的一场场口角。

===Original===
“But you ought to see him when he is asleep,” she said to me. “You know he has a bed, sheets, an eiderdown coverlet, like a real person. Every night I put him to bed. And his little head looks so funny on it, all black. Aren’t you very funny, Monsieur Spy?”

Spy chose a comfortable place on Juliette’s dress, and, after turning several times, rolled himself into a black lump, almost entirely lost in the cloth’s silken folds.

“That’s it! Bye-bye, Spy, my little baby!”

===Chinese===
“可您真该看看它睡着的样子，”她对我说，“您知道吗，它有一张床，有床单，有鸭绒被，跟一个真人似的。每天晚上都是我哄它上床。它那颗小脑袋搁在上面，黑黑的一团，滑稽得很。你说是不是很滑稽，斯派先生？”

斯派在朱丽叶特的裙子上挑了块舒服地方，转了几个圈，把自己蜷成一团黑球，几乎整个儿没进绸料的褶子里。

“这就对了！睡吧睡吧，斯派，我的小宝宝！”

===Original===
During this long conversation with Spy, I had a chance to observe Juliette at leisure. She was indeed very beautiful, even more beautiful than I had dreamed she was under her veil. Her face was truly radiant. It had such freshness, such an aurora-like clearness, that the very air about her seemed illumined. Whenever she turned or bent forward I saw her thick hair, very dark, descending along her dress in an enormous tress, which added something peculiarly virginal and youthful to her appearance. I thought I saw a perpendicular, wilful wrinkle furrowed in the middle of her forehead, at the root of her hair, but it was visible only in certain instances of light reflection, and the luminous sweetness of her eyes, the extremely graceful curve of her mouth tempered its rigid aspect. One felt that under her ample garments quivered a supple, nervous body of passionate pliancy; what delighted me most were her hands, delicate, deft and of surprising agility, whose every movement, even of indifference or anger, was a caress.

===Chinese===
在同斯派的这场长谈里，我得以从容地端详朱丽叶特。她确实很美，比我在面纱下梦想过的模样还要美。她的脸真是光彩照人，那样鲜润，那样一种曙光般的明净，连她四周的空气仿佛都被照亮了。每当她转身或俯身，我便看见她那头浓黑的头发，编成一条粗大的发辫顺着长裙垂下去，给她的模样平添了一种特别的处子气与青春气。我恍惚看见她额头正中、发根底下，有一道竖直的、执拗的皱纹；可它只在光线反射的某些时刻才显出来，况且她眼中莹莹的温柔、嘴角极其优雅的弧线，又把那道纹的僵硬冲淡了。人能觉出，在她宽松的衣裙底下，颤动着一具柔韧而神经质的身体，一种热烈的柔顺；最叫我心醉的是她那双手，纤细、灵巧、敏捷得出奇，一举一动，哪怕是漠然或嗔怒的动作，也都像一次爱抚。

===Original===
It was hard for me to form a definite opinion of her. There was in this woman a mixture of innocence and voluptuousness, of shrewdness and stupidity, of kindness and malevolence, which was disconcerting. And a curious thing! At one moment I saw the horrible image of the singer at the Bouffes taking shape near her. And this image formed Juliette’s shadow, so to speak. Far from vanishing, this image, as I looked at it, was assuming in some way a fixed corporeal form. It grimaced, wriggled, leaped with lurid contortions, its foul, obscene lips distended toward Juliette, who seemed to draw the image toward herself and whose hand sank in its hair and passed tremblingly along its body, happy to sully herself with its impure contact. And the sordid juggler was removing Juliette’s clothes and showing her to me in a swoon, in the wretched splendor of sin! I had to shut my eyes and make a painful effort to dispel this abominable image, and Juliette immediately assumed her expression of enigmatic, candid tenderness.

===Chinese===
我很难对她形成一个确定的看法。这个女人身上，天真与肉欲、精明与愚蠢、善良与刻毒搀和在一起，叫人无所适从。还有一桩怪事！一时之间，我看见布夫剧院那个歌女的可怕形象，在她身边渐渐成形。可以说，这形象构成了朱丽叶特的影子。它非但不消散，反而在我的注视下，渐渐凝成一副固定的血肉之躯。它挤眉弄眼，扭扭摆摆，带着狰狞的扭曲跳跃着，那张污秽淫邪的嘴向朱丽叶特张开；朱丽叶特呢，仿佛把这形象往自己身上拽，一只手插进它的头发，颤巍巍地顺着它的身子抚过去，甘愿让这不洁的接触玷污自己。那卑污的要把戏人正扒下朱丽叶特的衣裳，把她昏厥的身子亮给我看，亮在罪恶的凄惨光华里！我不得不闭上眼睛，费一番痛苦的努力，驱散这可惜的形象；朱丽叶特随即又恢复了那副谜一样的、坦白的温柔神情。

===Original===
“And above all, come to see me, often, very often,” she said, seeing me to the door, while Spy, who had followed her into the antechamber, barked and danced on his thin, spider legs.

Outside, I felt the return of a sudden and passionate affection for Lirat and, reproaching myself for being sulky with him, I resolved to ask him to dine with me that very evening. On my way from the Rue Saint Petersbourg to the Boulevard de Courcelles where Lirat lived, I made some bitter reflections. The visit had disillusioned me, I was no longer under the spell of a dream and I quickly returned to desolate reality, to the denial of love. What I had imagined about Juliette was quite vague.

===Chinese===
“千万要来看我，常来，要常常来！”她送我到门口时说；斯派也跟进前厅来了，正踩着蜘蛛似的细胞，又叫又蹦。

走出门外，我对利拉陡然又生出一股热烈的友爱，怪自己先前跟他闹别扭，便决意当晚就邀他共进晚餐。从圣彼得堡路走到利拉住的库尔塞勒林荫大道（Boulevard de Courcelles），一路我不无苦涩地想着心事。这次拜访让我幻灭了；我不再受梦境的蛊惑，很快回到凄凉的现实里，回到对爱情的否定里。我原先对朱丽叶特的种种想象，实在含混得很。

===Original===
My spirit, exalted by her beauty, was ascribing to her moral qualities and mental attainments which I could not define and which I assumed were extraordinary, the more so since Lirat, by attributing to her, without reason, a dishonorable existence and shameful proclivities, had made her a veritable martyr in my eyes, and my heart was moved. Pushing this folly still further, I thought that by some sort of irresistible sympathy she would confide her suffering to me, the grave and sorrowful secrets of her soul; I already saw myself consoling her, speaking to her of duty, virtue, resignation. I looked forward to a series of solemn and touching things.

===Chinese===
我的头脑被她的美貌扇动起来，把种种道德品质、精神造诣都加在她身上；这些品质我说不出名目，却认定非同凡响——更何况利拉无凭无据，把一段不名誉的生活、可耻的癖好安在她头上，叫她在我眼里成了一位真正的殉道者，于是我的心被打动了。我把这痴念又往前推进一步，心想凭着某种不可抗拒的感应，她准会把她的苦楚吐露给我，把她灵魂里那些沉重而悲哀的秘密吐露给我；我仿佛已经看见自己在安慰她，对她讲义务，讲美德，讲忍从。我期待着一连串庄严动人的事情。

===Original===
Instead of all this poesy﻿—a frightful dog who barked at my feet and a woman just like others, without brains, without ideas, occupied solely with pleasures, confining her enthusiasm to the Théâtre des Varietés and the caressing of her Spy, her Spy!﻿ ﻿… Ha!
