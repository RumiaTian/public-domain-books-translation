## Manštin, the Rabbit / 曼斯汀，兔子

===Original===
Manštin was an adventurous brave, but very kindhearted. Stamping a moccasined foot as he drew on his buckskin leggins, he said: “Grandmother, beware of Iktomi! Do not let him lure you into some cunning trap. I am going to the North country on a long hunt.”

With these words of caution to the bent old rabbit grandmother with whom he had lived since he was a tiny babe, Manštin started off toward the north. He was scarce over the great high hills when he heard the shrieking of a human child.

“Wän!” he ejaculated, pointing his long ears toward the direction of the sound; “Wän! that is the work of cruel Double-Face. Shameless coward! he delights in torturing helpless creatures!”

Muttering indistinct words, Manštin ran up the last hill and lo! in the ravine beyond stood the terrible monster with a face in front and one in the back of his head!

This brown giant was without clothes save for a wildcat-skin about his loins. With a wicked gleaming eye, he watched the little black-haired baby he held in his strong arm. In a laughing voice he hummed an Indian mother’s lullaby, “Ä-bōō! Äbōō!” and at the same time he switched the naked baby with a thorny wild-rose bush.

===Chinese===
曼斯汀（Manštin）是个爱冒险的勇士，心肠却非常好。他一边蹬上鹿皮绑腿（leggins），一边跺了跺穿鹿皮鞋（moccasin）的脚，说：「奶奶，当心伊克托米（Iktomi）！别让他把您诱进什么狡猾的圈套里。我要到北边的地方去，打一趟远猎。」

他从小娃娃时起，就一直跟着这位驼背的兔子老奶奶过活。叮嘱完这几句话，曼斯汀便朝北边出发了。他刚翻过大大的高山，就听见一个小孩子的尖声哭喊。

「呀！」他失声叫道，长耳朵指向声音传来的方向；「呀！那是残忍的双面老怪（Double-Face）干的好事。无耻的孬种！他就爱折磨不能还手的小生灵！」

曼斯汀嘴里咕哝着含混不清的话，跑上最后一座山头——看哪！山那边的深谷里站着那个可怕的怪物，脑袋前面一张脸，后面还一张脸！

这棕色巨人不穿衣服，只在腰间围一块野猫皮。他一只眼睛凶光闪闪，望着自己粗壮胳膊里夹着的一个黑头发小娃娃。他用笑呵呵的嗓门哼着印第安母亲的摇篮曲，「啊——噗——！啊噗——！」一边又拿带刺的野玫瑰枝抽打那光着身子的小娃娃。

===Original===
Quickly Manštin jumped behind a large sage bush on the brow of the hill. He bent his bow and the sinewy string twanged. Now an arrow stuck above the ear of Double-Face. It was a poisoned arrow, and the giant fell dead. Then Manštin took the little brown baby and hurried away from the ravine. Soon he came to a teepee from whence loud wailing voices broke. It was the teepee of the stolen baby and the mourners were its heartbroken parents.

When gallant Manštin returned the child to the eager arms of the mother there came a sudden terror into the eyes of both Dakotas. They feared lest it was Double-Face come in a new guise to torture them. The rabbit understood their fear and said: “I am Manštin, the kindhearted—Manštin, the noted huntsman. I am your friend. Do not fear.”

That night a strange thing happened. While the father and mother slept, Manštin took the wee baby. With his feet placed gently yet firmly upon the tiny toes of the little child, he drew upward by each small hand the sleeping child till he was a full-grown man. With a forefinger he traced a slit in the upper lip; and when on the morrow the man and woman awoke they could not distinguish their own son from Manštin, so much alike were the braves.

“Henceforth we are friends, to help each other,” said Manštin, shaking a right hand in farewell. “The earth is our common ear, to carry from its uttermost extremes one’s slightest wish for the other!”

“Ho! Be it so!” answered the newly made man.

===Chinese===
曼斯汀飞快地跳到山头一丛大山艾后面。他弯弓搭箭，筋弦「嘣」的一声。一支箭顿时钉在双面老怪的耳朵上方。那是一支毒箭，巨人倒地死了。曼斯汀抱起棕色的小娃娃，匆匆离开深谷。不多时他来到一座帐篷跟前，里面传出大声的哭嚎。那正是被偷娃娃的家，哭的人是娃娃心碎的爹娘。

豪侠的曼斯汀把孩子送回母亲急切的怀抱时，两个达科他（Dakota）人的眼里突然都露出惊恐。他们生怕那是双面老怪换了新模样，又来折磨他们。兔子明白他们的心思，便说：「我是曼斯汀，好心肠的曼斯汀——曼斯汀，有名的猎人。我是你们的朋友。别怕。」

当天夜里出了一件怪事。趁爹娘熟睡，曼斯汀抱起小小的娃娃。他的两只脚轻轻却牢牢地踩住小孩的小脚趾，攥着每只小手往上拉，把熟睡的孩子拉成了一个身材完全长成的汉子。他用食指在上嘴唇上划出一道人中；第二天早晨男人女人醒来，竟分不出哪个是自己的儿子、哪个是曼斯汀，两个勇士简直像一个模子里刻出来的。

「从今往后咱们是朋友，互相帮衬，」曼斯汀说着，握住一只右手道别。「大地是咱们共同的耳朵，从天涯海角把一方对另一方最细微的心愿传过去！」

「嗬！但愿如此！」新造出来的人答道。

===Original===
Upon leaving his friend, Manštin hurried away toward the North country whither he was bound for a long hunt. Suddenly he came upon the edge of a wide brook. His alert eye caught sight of a rawhide rope staked to the water’s brink, which led away toward a small round hut in the distance. The ground was trodden into a deep groove beneath the loosely drawn rawhide rope.

“Hun-he!” exclaimed Manštin, bending over the freshly made footprints in the moist bank of the brook. “A man’s footprints!” he said to himself. “A blind man lives in yonder hut! This rope is his guide by which he comes for his daily water!” surmised Manštin, who knew all the peculiar contrivances of the people. At once his eyes became fixed upon the solitary dwelling and hither he followed his curiosity—a real blind man’s rope.

Quietly he lifted the door-flap and entered in. An old toothless grandfather, blind and shaky with age, sat upon the ground. He was not deaf however. He heard the entrance and felt the presence of some stranger.

“How, grandchild,” he mumbled, for he was old enough to be grandparent to every living thing, “how! I cannot see you. Pray, speak your name!”

“Grandfather, I am Manštin,” answered the rabbit, all the while looking with curious eyes about the wigwam.

===Chinese===
告别了朋友，曼斯汀匆匆奔向他要去远猎的北边地方。忽然他来到一条宽溪的岸边。机警的眼睛一眼瞥见一条生皮绳拴在水边的木桩上，绳子一直通向远处一座圆圆的小屋。松松拉着的生皮绳底下，地面被踩出一道深深的沟。

「哼嘿！」曼斯汀叫了一声，俯身看着溪边湿岸上新鲜的脚印。「人的脚印！」他自言自语。「那屋里住着一个瞎子！这绳子就是他的引路绳，他天天顺着它来打水！」曼斯汀揣摩道，人们各式各样的古怪机巧他全都认得。他的眼睛顿时盯住了那座孤零零的住所，好奇心驱使他顺着一条真正的瞎子的绳子走了过去。

他悄悄掀开门帘走进去。一位牙齿掉光的老爷爷坐在地上，瞎了眼，老得直打颤。不过他不聋。他听见了进门声，觉出屋里有生人。

「你好，孙儿，」他咕哝道，因为他年纪够大，当得起一切活物的爷爷，「你好！我看不见你。请报上你的名字！」

「爷爷，我是曼斯汀。」兔子答道，一双好奇的眼睛一直把棚屋（wigwam）上下打量个不停。

===Original===
“Grandfather, what is it so tightly packed in all these buckskin bags placed against the tent poles?” he asked.

“My grandchild, those are dried buffalo meat and venison. These are magic bags which never grow empty. I am blind and cannot go on a hunt. Hence a kind Maker has given me these magic bags of choicest foods.”

Then the old, bent man pulled at a rope which lay by his right hand. “This leads me to the brook where I drink! and this,” said he, turning to the one on his left, “and this takes me into the forest, where I feel about for dry sticks for my fire.”

“Grandfather, I wish I lived in such sure luxury! I would lean back against a tent pole, and with crossed feet I would smoke sweet willow bark the rest of my days,” sighed Manštin.

“My grandchild, your eyes are your luxury! you would be unhappy without them!” the old man replied.

“Grandfather, I would give you my two eyes for your place!” cried Manštin.

“How! you have said it. Arise. Take out your eyes and give them to me. Henceforth you are at home here in my stead.”

At once Manštin took out both his eyes and the old man put them on! Rejoicing, the old grandfather started away with his young eyes while the blind rabbit filled his dream pipe, leaning lazily against the tent pole. For a short time it was a most pleasant pastime to smoke willow bark and to eat from the magic bags.

===Chinese===
「爷爷，靠着帐篷杆放的这一只只鹿皮口袋，装得这么鼓鼓囊囊的，都是什么呀？」他问。

「我的孙儿，那是干牛肉和鹿肉。这些是魔法口袋，永远吃不完。我瞎了，不能出去打猎。好心的造物主就给了我这些装满上等吃食的魔法口袋。」

随后，驼背的老人拉了拉右手边放着的一条绳。「这条牵我去溪边喝水！这条，」他说着转向左边那条，「这条领我进林子，我在那儿摸来摸去，捡些干柴生火。」

「爷爷，我要能过上这样安稳的福气日子该多好！我就往帐篷柱上一靠，翘着脚，抽着甜柳皮烟斗，了此余生。」曼斯汀叹道。

「我的孙儿，你的眼睛就是你的福气！没了它们，你可要不痛快的！」老人回答。

「爷爷，我拿我这两只眼睛换您的位置！」曼斯汀喊道。

「好！话可是你说的。起来。把眼睛挖出来给我。从今往后，你替我住在这儿。」

曼斯汀当即挖出双眼，老人把它们装上了！老爷爷欢天喜地，带着一双年轻的眼睛出门去了；瞎兔子则装上一锅梦烟，懒洋洋地靠着帐篷柱。有一小阵子，抽着柳皮、吃着魔法口袋里的吃食，真是再惬意不过的消遣。

===Original===
Manštin grew thirsty, but there was no water in the small dwelling. Taking one of the rawhide ropes he started toward the brook to quench his thirst. He was young and unwilling to trudge slowly in the old man’s footpath. He was full of glee, for it had been many long moons since he had tasted such good food. Thus he skipped confidently along jerking the old weather-eaten rawhide spasmodically till all of a sudden it gave way and Manštin fell headlong into the water.

“En! En!” he grunted kicking frantically amid stream. All along the slippery bank he vainly tried to climb, till at last he chanced upon the old stake and the deeply worn footpath. Exhausted and inwardly disgusted with his mishaps, he crawled more cautiously on all fours to his wigwam door. Dripping with his recent plunge he sat with chattering teeth within his unfired wigwam.

The sun had set and the night air was chilly, but there was no firewood in the dwelling. “Hin!” murmured Manštin and bravely tried the other rope. “I go for some firewood!” he said, following the rawhide rope which led into the forest. Soon he stumbled upon thickly strewn dry willow sticks. Eagerly with both hands he gathered the wood into his outspread blanket. Manštin was naturally an energetic fellow.

When he had a large heap, he tied two opposite ends of blanket together and lifted the bundle of wood upon his back, but alas! he had unconsciously dropped the end of the rope and now he was lost in the wood!

===Chinese===
曼斯汀口渴了，可小屋里没有水。他拿起一条生皮绳，朝溪边走去解渴。他年轻，不肯顺着老人的脚印慢慢磨蹭。他满心欢喜，因为已经有许多个长长的月头没尝过这么好的吃食了。就这样，他一路蹦蹦跳跳，一阵一阵地猛拽那根饱经风雨的旧生皮绳，忽然绳子断了，曼斯汀一头栽进了水里。

「嗯！嗯！」他在溪水当中拼命扑腾，直哼哼。滑溜溜的岸坡他怎么也爬不上，白白试了一路，末了总算摸着了那根旧木桩和踩得深深的脚印小路。又累又懊恼，他四肢着地，小心翼翼地爬回棚屋门口。刚落过水的身子滴着水，他坐在没生火的棚屋里，牙齿咯咯打战。

太阳落了，夜气寒凉，屋里却没有柴。「哼！」曼斯汀嘟囔一声，壮起胆子去摸另一条绳子。「我去弄点柴火！」他说着，顺着那条通进林子的生皮绳走去。不多时他就撞见厚厚铺了一地的干柳枝。他急切地双手并用，把柴拢进张开的毯子。曼斯汀天生是个麻利人。

攒起一大堆，他把毯子对角的两头一系，把柴捆往背上一甩——可是糟啦！他不知不觉松开了绳头，这会儿他在林子里迷了路！

===Original===
“Hin! hin!” he groaned. Then pausing a moment, he set his fan-like ears to catch any sound of approaching footsteps. There was none. Not even a night bird twittered to help him out of his predicament.

With a bold face, he made a start at random.

He fell into some tangled wood where he was held fast. Manštin let go his bundle and began to lament having given away his two eyes.

“Friend, my friend, I have need of you! The old oak tree grandfather has gone off with my eyes and I am lost in the woods!” he cried with his lips close to the earth.

Scarcely had he spoken when the sound of voices was audible on the outer edge of the forest. Nearer and louder grew the voices—one was the clear flute tones of a young brave and the other the tremulous squeaks of an old grandfather.

It was Manštin’s friend with the Earth Ear and the old grandfather. “Here Manštin, take back your eyes,” said the old man, “I knew you would not be content in my stead, but I wanted you to learn your lesson. I have had pleasure seeing with your eyes and trying your bow and arrows, but since I am old and feeble I much prefer my own teepee and my magic bags!”

Thus talking the three returned to the hut. The old grandfather crept into his wigwam, which is often mistaken for a mere oak tree by little Indian girls and boys.

Manštin, with his own bright eyes fitted into his head again, went on happily to hunt in the North country.

「哼！哼！」他哼哼唧唧地呻吟。随后他停了片刻，竖起两把扇子似的耳朵，捕捉有没有走近的脚步声。没有。连一只夜鸟都不肯啁啾一声，帮他走出困境。

他壮起胆子，胡乱挑了个方向开步走。

他跌进一丛缠结的乱木里，被牢牢卡住了。曼斯汀丢了柴捆，哀叹起自己送掉双眼的事来。

「朋友，我的朋友，我需要你！老橡树爷爷带着我的眼睛走了，我在林子里迷了路！」他把嘴唇贴在地上喊道。

话音未落，林子外缘就传来了人声。声音越来越近、越来越响——一个是年轻勇士笛子般清亮的嗓音，另一个是老爷爷颤巍巍的尖叫。

来的是凭着大地之耳听见的曼斯汀的朋友，和老爷爷。「给，曼斯汀，收回你的眼睛，」老人说，「我知道你顶替我不会安生，可我要你受教一课。用你的眼睛看东西、试你的弓箭，我很快活；可我到底又老又乏，还是最疼我自己的帐篷和魔法口袋！」

三个人这样说着回到了小屋。老爷爷爬进他的棚屋——那棚屋，印第安的小姑娘小孩子常把它错当成一棵普普通通的橡树。

曼斯汀把自己亮亮的眼睛重新装回头颅，欢欢喜喜地继续到北边打猎去了。
