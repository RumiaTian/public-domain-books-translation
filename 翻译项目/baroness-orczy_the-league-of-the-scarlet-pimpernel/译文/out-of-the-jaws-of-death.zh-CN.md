## Out of the Jaws of Death / ## 脱离死亡之口

===Original===
Being a fragment from the diary of Valentine Lemercier, in the possession of her great-granddaughter.

We were such a happy family before this terrible Revolution broke out; we lived rather simply, but very comfortably, in our dear old home just on the borders of the forest of Compiegne. Jean and André were the twins; just fifteen years old they were when King Louis was deposed from the throne of France which God had given him, and sent to prison like a common criminal, with our beautiful Queen Marie Antoinette and the Royal children, and Madame Elizabeth, who was so beloved by the poor!

Ah! that seems very, very long ago now. No doubt you know better than I do all that happened in our beautiful land of France and in lovely Paris about that time: goods and property confiscated, innocent men, women, and children condemned to death for acts of treason which they had never committed.

===Chinese===
本篇系瓦朗蒂娜·勒梅西耶（Valentine Lemercier）日记中的一页残章，如今由她的曾孙女珍藏着。

在这场可怕的革命爆发之前，我们原是一个多么幸福的家庭啊；我们住在贡比涅（Compiegne）森林边上那栋亲爱的老宅里，度日虽算简朴，却十分安逸。让（Jean）和安德烈（André）是一对双胞胎兄弟；路易国王（King Louis）被废黜了上帝赐予他的法兰西王位，同我们美丽的王后玛丽·安托瓦内特（Marie Antoinette）、王室的孩子，以及深得穷人爱戴的伊丽莎白夫人（Madame Elizabeth）一道，像寻常罪犯似的被投进了监狱——那时他们俩刚满十五岁！

啊！如今想来，那已是极其遥远的往事了。无疑，那时在我们美丽的法兰西国土上、在可爱的巴黎城里所发生的一切，你比我知道得更清楚：财物家产横遭抄没，无辜的男女乃至孩童，因从未犯下的叛国罪名被判处死刑。

===Original===
It was in August last year that they came to Mon Repos and arrested papa, and maman, and us four young ones and dragged us to Paris, where we were imprisoned in a narrow and horribly dank vault in the Abbaye, where all day and night through the humid stone walls we heard cries and sobs and moans from poor people, who no doubt were suffering the same sorrows and the same indignities as we were.

I had just passed my nineteenth birthday, and Marguerite was only thirteen. Maman was a perfect angel during that terrible time; she kept up our courage and our faith in God in a way that no one else could have done. Every night and morning we knelt round her knee and papa sat close beside her, and we prayed to God for deliverance from our own afflictions, and for the poor people who were crying and moaning all the day.

But of what went on outside our prison walls we had not an idea, though sometimes poor papa would brave the warder’s brutalities and ask him questions of what was happening in Paris every day.

===Chinese===
去年八月，他们来到蒙勒波（Mon Repos），逮捕了爸爸、妈妈和我们四个孩子，把我们拖到巴黎，关进阿贝伊（Abbaye）监狱里一间狭窄而潮气刺骨的地窖式牢房。日日夜夜，透过湿漉漉的石墙，我们听得见可怜人的哭喊、呜咽与呻吟——他们无疑正遭受着与我们相同的苦难和屈辱。

我刚过完十九岁生日，玛格丽特（Marguerite）才十三岁。在那段可怕的日子里，妈妈真是一位十足的天使；她以任何旁人都无法做到的方式，支撑着我们的勇气和对上帝的信心。每天清晨和夜晚，我们跪坐在她的膝旁，爸爸紧挨着她，我们一起祈求上帝把我们从自身的苦难中解救出来，也垂怜那些终日啼哭呻吟的可怜人。

至于高墙之外发生的事，我们一无所知；尽管有时可怜的爸爸会壮起胆子，不顾狱卒的凶横，向他打听巴黎每日的动静。

===Original===
“They are hanging all the aristos to the streetlamps of the city,” the man would reply with a cruel laugh, “and it will be your turn next.”

We had been in prison for about a fortnight, when one day﻿—oh! shall I ever forget it?﻿—we heard in the distance a noise like the rumbling of thunder; nearer and nearer it came, and soon the sound became less confused, cries and shrieks could be heard above that rumbling din; but so weird and menacing did those cries seem that instinctively﻿—though none of us knew what they meant﻿—we all felt a nameless terror grip our hearts.

Oh! I am not going to attempt the awful task of describing to you all the horrors of that never-to-be-forgotten day. People, who today cannot speak without a shudder of the September massacres, have not the remotest conception of what really happened on that awful second day of that month.

===Chinese===
“他们正把贵族佬一个个吊上城里的路灯哪，”那人会带着残忍的狞笑答道，“很快就轮到你们了。”

我们入狱大约两个星期后，有一天——哦！我哪能忘得了那一天？——我们听见远处传来一阵闷雷滚动般的轰响；它越来越近，很快那声响便不再混沌，在隆隆的喧嚣之上，听得出一声声呼叫与尖啸；那些喊声听来如此怪诞而凶险，我们虽谁也不明白它们意味着什么，却都不由自主地感到一种无名的恐怖攥住了心。

哦！我并不打算尝试向你描述那个永志难忘的日子里全部的恐怖——那桩差事太可怕了。如今一提起九月大屠杀（September massacres）仍不免战栗的人们，对那个月可怕的第二天究竟发生了什么，实在连最朦胧的概念也没有。

===Original===
We are all at peace and happy now, but whenever my thoughts fly back to that morning, whenever the ears of memory recall those hideous yells of fury and of hate, coupled with the equally horrible cries for pity, which pierced through the walls behind which the six of us were crouching, trembling, and praying, whenever I think of it all my heart still beats violently with that same nameless dread which held it in its deathly grip then.

Hundreds of men, women, and children were massacred in the prisons of that day﻿—it was a St. Bartholomew even more hideous than the last.

Maman was trying in vain to keep our thoughts fixed upon God﻿—papa sat on the stone bench, his elbows resting on his knees, his head buried in his hands; but maman was kneeling on the floor, with her dear arms encircling us all and her trembling lips moving in continuous prayer.

We felt that we were facing death﻿—and what a death, O my God!

===Chinese===
如今我们全都安居无恙、幸福快乐，可每当我的思绪飞回那个早晨，每当记忆的耳畔重新响起那一片狂怒与仇恨的狰狞吼叫，连同那同样骇人的哀哀乞怜之声——它们穿透墙壁，刺向我们六个蜷缩着、颤抖着、祈祷着的人——每当想起这一切，我的心至今仍会狂跳不已，仍被当年那种攥紧了它的无名恐惧死死扼住。

数以百计的男人、女人和儿童，在那一天各所监狱里惨遭屠戮——那是一场比当年更狰狞的圣巴托罗缪大屠杀（St. Bartholomew）。

妈妈徒劳地想把我们的心思系在上帝身上——爸爸坐在石凳上，两肘撑膝，头埋在双手里；妈妈则跪在地上，用她亲爱的臂膀把我们全都搂着，颤抖的双唇不住地翕动祷告。

我们觉得死亡就在眼前——那是怎样的一个死法啊，我的上帝！

===Original===
Suddenly the small grated window﻿—high up in the dank wall﻿—became obscured. I was the first to look up, but the cry of terror which rose from my heart was choked ere it reached my throat.

Jean and André looked up, too, and they shrieked, and so did Marguerite, and papa jumped up and ran to us and stood suddenly between us and the window like a tiger defending its young.

But we were all of us quite silent now. The children did not even cry; they stared, wide-eyed, paralysed with fear.

Only maman continued to pray, and we could hear papa’s rapid and stertorous breathing as he watched what was going on at that window above.

Heavy blows were falling against the masonry round the grating, and we could hear the nerve-racking sound of a file working on the iron bars; and farther away, below the window, those awful yells of human beings transformed by hate and fury into savage beasts.

===Chinese===
忽然，那扇小小的装着铁栅的窗——开在潮湿石墙的高处——被什么东西遮暗了。我第一个抬起头，可那声自心底涌起的恐怖惊呼，还没到喉咙便噎住了。

让和安德烈也抬起头来，尖声叫出了声，玛格丽特也是；爸爸一跃而起，奔到我们身边，猛地挡在我们与那扇窗之间，像一头护崽的猛虎。

但此刻我们全都鸦雀无声。孩子们连哭都忘了，只是瞪大眼睛呆望着，吓得僵住了。

只有妈妈仍在祷告；爸爸紧盯着上方那扇窗的动静，我们听得见他急促而粗重的呼吸。

沉重的击打一下一下落在铁栅四周的砖石上，我们听得出锉刀啃咬铁栏那令人神经绷裂的声响；而在更远处、窗子的下方，是那一片骇人的咆哮——那是被仇恨与狂怒变成了野兽的人。

===Original===
How long this horrible suspense lasted I cannot now tell you; the next thing I remember clearly is a number of men in horrible ragged clothing pouring into our vault-like prison from the window above; the next moment they rushed at us simultaneously﻿—or so it seemed to me, for I was just then recommending my soul to God, so certain was I that in that same second I would cease to live.

It was all like a dream, for instead of the horrible shriek of satisfied hate which we were all expecting to hear, a whispering voice, commanding and low, struck our ears and dragged us, as it were, from out the abyss of despair into the sudden light of hope.

“If you will trust us,” the voice whispered, “and not be afraid, you will be safely out of Paris within an hour.”

Papa was the first to realise what was happening; he had never lost his presence of mind even during the darkest moment of this terrible time, and he said quite calmly and steadily now:

===Chinese===
这番可怕的煎熬持续了多久，如今我说不上来了；我记得清楚的下一件事，是几个衣衫褴褛得吓人的男人，从上方那扇窗涌进了我们这间地窖似的牢房；转眼间他们一齐朝我们扑来——至少我感觉如此，因为那一刻我正把自己的灵魂交付给上帝，我笃定就在同一秒内，我将不复存活。

这一切恍如一场梦：我们没有听见人人料想中的、仇恨得逞的那种可怕咆哮，耳畔响起的是一个低沉而威严的耳语，它仿佛把我们从绝望的深渊里拖了出来，猛然带进了希望的光亮之中。

“只要你们信任我们，”那声音低低说道，“不要害怕，不出一个钟头，你们就会平平安安地出了巴黎。”

爸爸第一个明白过来出了什么事；即便在这恐怖时日最黑暗的时刻，他也从未失过方寸，此刻他十分平静而沉稳地问道：

===Original===
“What must we do?”

“Persuade the little ones not to be afraid, not to cry, to be as still and silent as may be,” continued the voice, which I felt must be that of one of God’s own angels, so exquisitely kind did it sound to my ear.

“They will be quiet and still without persuasion,” said papa; “eh, children?”

And Jean, André, and Marguerite murmured: “Yes!” whilst maman and I drew them closer to us and said everything we could think of to make them still more brave.

===Chinese===
“我们要做什么？”

“劝住小家伙们，别害怕，别哭，尽量安安静静、一动不动，”那声音继续说。它在我耳中听来如此温良仁慈，我觉得那必定是上帝座前某位天使的声音。

“不用劝，他们自会安安静静、一动不动的，”爸爸说，“对不对，孩子们？”

让、安德烈和玛格丽特低声应道：“对！”妈妈和我把他们搂得更紧，想得出的话都说尽了，只为让他们更加勇敢。

===Original===
And the whispering, commanding voice went on after awhile:

“Now will you allow yourselves to be muffled and bound, and, after that, will you swear that whatever happens, whatever you may see or hear, you will neither move nor speak? Not only your own lives, but those of many brave men will depend upon your fulfilment of this oath.”

Papa made no reply save to raise his hand and eyes up to where God surely was watching over us all. Maman said in her gentle, even voice:

“For myself and my children, I swear to do all that you tell us.”

===Chinese===
过了片刻，那低低的、威严的声音又说道：

“现在，你们可愿意让人蒙住头脸、捆住身子？过后，你们可愿意起誓：无论出了什么事，无论看见什么、听见什么，都绝不挪动、绝不开口？不只你们自己的性命，还有许多勇士的性命，都要看你们是否信守此誓。”

爸爸没有作答，只把手和眼睛抬向上天——上帝必定正在那里看护着我们众人。妈妈用她温柔平和的嗓音说道：

“我代表我自己和我的孩子们起誓：你们吩咐的一切，我们无不照办。”

===Original===
A great feeling of confidence had entered into her heart, just as it had done into mine. We looked at one another and knew that we were both thinking of the same thing: we were thinking of the brave Englishman and his gallant little band of heroes, about whom we had heard many wonderful tales﻿—how they had rescued a number of innocent people who were unjustly threatened with the guillotine; and we all knew that the tall figure, disguised in horrible rags, who spoke to us with such a gentle yet commanding voice, was the man whom rumour credited with supernatural powers, and who was known by the mysterious name of “The Scarlet Pimpernel.”

Hardly had we sworn to do his bidding than his friends most unceremoniously threw great pieces of sacking over our heads, and then proceeded to tie ropes round our bodies. At least, I know that that is what one of them was doing to me, and from one or two whispered words of command which reached my ear I concluded that papa and maman and the children were being dealt with in the same summary way.

I felt hot and stifled under that rough bit of sacking, but I would not have moved or even sighed for worlds. Strangely enough, as soon as my eyes and ears were shut off from the sounds and sights immediately round me, I once more became conscious of the horrible and awful din which was going on, not only on the other side of our prison walls, but inside the whole of the Abbaye building and in the street beyond.

Once more I heard those terrible howls of rage and of satisfied hatred, uttered by the assassins who were being paid by the government of our beautiful country to butcher helpless prisoners in their hundreds.

===Chinese===
一股巨大的信心涌进了她的心里，正如涌进了我的心里。我们对望一眼，便知道彼此想的是同一件事：我们想起了那位勇敢的英国人和他手下那一小队英豪——关于他们，我们听过许许多多神奇的传说，说他们救出过一批又一批无辜的人，那些人平白无故地要被送上断头台；我们全都知道，这个一身骇人破衣、用如此温柔而又威严的声音同我们说话的高大身影，正是人们传言身怀神通的那个人，正是以“红花侠”（the Scarlet Pimpernel）这个神秘名字闻名的那个人。

我们刚刚起誓听命于他，他的伙伴们便毫不客气地把大片大片的麻袋布罩到我们头上，接着又动手用绳子捆我们的身子。至少我知道，其中一个对我就是这么处置的；而凭着一两句飘进我耳朵的低声号令，我断定爸爸、妈妈和孩子们也正受着同样干脆的处置。

蒙在那块粗糙的麻袋布底下，我又热又闷，可说什么我也绝不肯动一动，连一口大气也不敢叹。说来奇怪，我的耳目一旦同身边的声响景象隔绝，便重新清醒地意识到那场骇人的喧嚣正闹得凶——它不仅在狱墙的另一侧，还响彻整座阿贝伊监狱，一直蔓延到墙外的大街。

我又一次听见了那阵阵狂怒与仇恨得逞的可怕嚎叫——发出嚎叫的是那些刺客，我们美丽国家的政府正出钱雇他们，成百成百地宰割手无寸铁的囚徒。

===Original===
Suddenly I felt myself hoisted up off my feet and slung up on to a pair of shoulders that must have been very powerful indeed, for I am no light weight, and once more I heard the voice, the very sound of which was delight, quite close to my ear this time, giving a brief and comprehensive command:

“All ready!﻿—remember your part﻿—en avant!”

Then it added in English. “Here, Tony, you start kicking against the door whilst we begin to shout!”

I loved those few words of English, and hoped that maman had heard them too, for it would confirm her﻿—as it did me﻿—in the happy knowledge that God and a brave man had taken our rescue in hand.

===Chinese===
忽然，我觉得自己被凌空掀起，甩上了一副肩膀——那肩膀必定极其有力，因为我绝不是个身子轻的人。我又一次听见了那个声音，这一回就在我耳边，单听那声响便叫人心喜；它下达了一道简短而周全的号令：

“都准备好了！——记住各自的任务——en avant！（前进！）”

===Original===
But from that moment we might have all been in the very antechamber of hell. I could hear the violent kicks against the heavy door of our prison, and our brave rescuers seemed suddenly to be transformed into a cageful of wild beasts. Their shouts and yells were as horrible as any that came to us from the outside, and I must say that the gentle, firm voice which I had learnt to love was as execrable as any I could hear.

Apparently the door would not yield, as the blows against it became more and more violent, and presently from somewhere above my head﻿—the window presumably﻿—there came a rough call, and a raucous laugh:

“Why? what in the name of —﻿—﻿— is happening here?”

And the voice near me answered back equally roughly: “A quarry of six﻿—but we are caught in this confounded trap﻿—get the door open for us, citizen﻿—we want to get rid of this booty and go in search for more.”
