## VI Beyond the Bamboo Curtain / 六 竹帘那边

===Original===
Had John Quincy been able to see his Aunt Minerva at that moment, he would not have been so sure that he could persuade her to fall in with his plans. He would, indeed, have been profoundly shocked at the picture presented by his supposedly staid and dignified relative.

For Miss Minerva was sitting on a grass mat in a fragrant garden in the Hawaiian quarter of Honolulu. Pale golden Chinese lanterns, inscribed with scarlet letters, hung above her head. Her neck was garlanded with ropes of buff ginger blossoms twined with maile. The sleepy, sensuous music of ukulele and steel guitar rose on the midnight air and before her, in a cleared space under the date palms, Hawaiian boys and girls were performing a dance she would not be able to describe in great detail when she got back to Beacon Street.

===Chinese===
倘若约翰·昆西此刻能亲眼见到他的米纳瓦姑婆，他就未必还那么有把握，认为自己能说动她依从自己的计划了。说实在的，这位向来端庄持重的亲戚眼下呈现的这副模样，准会叫他大为震惊。

因为此刻，米纳瓦小姐正坐在檀香山夏威夷人聚居区一座芬芳花园的草席上。她头顶悬着一盏盏淡金色的中国灯笼，灯笼上题着猩红的字样。她颈间挂着一串串花环，淡黄的姜花间缠着香藤迈莱（maile）。尤克里里和钢吉他那慵懒而撩人的乐声，升上午夜的空中；她面前，海枣树下清出的一块空地上，夏威夷的小伙子姑娘们正跳着一种舞——日后回到灯塔街，这舞她是没法与人细说的。

===Original===
Miss Minerva was, in her quiet way, very happy. One of the ambitions of her life had been realized, and she was present at a luau, or native Hawaiian feast. Few white people are privileged to attend this intimate ceremony, but Honolulu friends had been invited on this occasion, and had asked her to go with them. At first she had thought she must refuse, for Dan was expecting Barbara and John Quincy on Monday afternoon. When on Monday evening he had informed her that the *President Tyler* would not land its passengers until the next day, she had hastened to the telephone and asked to reconsider her refusal.

And she was glad she had. Before her, on another mat, lay the remnants of a dinner unique in her experience. Dan had called her a good sport, and she had this evening proved him to be correct. Without a qualm she had faced the queer food wrapped in brown bundles, she had tasted everything, poi served in individual calabashes, chicken stewed in coconut milk, squid and shrimps, limu, or seaweed, even raw fish. She would dream tonight!

===Chinese===
米纳瓦小姐以她静默的方式，快活极了。她平生的一桩夙愿得了却：她亲临了一场卢奥宴（luau），也就是夏威夷人的本土盛宴。鲜有白人得享殊荣，能参与这般亲近的典礼，但这一回主人家请了檀香山的朋友，朋友们又邀她同去。起初她想自己必须回绝，因为丹正等着芭芭拉和约翰·昆西周一下午抵达。待到周一傍晚，丹告知她泰勒总统号（*President Tyler*）要到次日才让乘客上岸，她便赶紧奔向电话，请求收回先前的谢绝。

而她庆幸自己去了。她面前的另一张席上，摊着一餐她平生仅见的残席。丹曾说她有雅量，这一晚她证明他没有说错。那些棕色叶包里裹着的古怪吃食，她毫不畏缩地一一迎了上去，样样都尝了：各人钵中盛的波伊（poi，芋泥），椰汁炖鸡，鱿鱼和虾，利穆（limu，即海藻），甚至连生鱼也不放过。今夜她怕是要做梦了！

===Original===
Now the feasting had given way to the dance. The moonlight was tracing lacy patterns on the lawn, the plaintive wail of the music rose ever louder, the Hawaiian young people, bashful at first in the presence of strangers, were bashful no longer. Miss Minerva closed her eyes and leaned back against the trunk of a tall palm. Even in Hawaiian love songs there is a note of hopeless melancholy, it touched her emotions as no symphony ever could. A curtain was lifted and she was looking into the past, the primitive, barbaric past of these Islands in the days before the white men came.

A long, heartbreaking crescendo, and the music stopped, the swaying bodies of the dancers were momentarily still. It seemed to Miss Minerva’s friends an opportune moment to depart. They entered the house and in the stuffy little parlor, took leave of their brown, smiling host and hostess. The baby whose arrival in the world was the inspiration for the luau awoke for a second and smiled at them too. Outside in the narrow street their car was waiting.

===Chinese===
此刻宴饮已让位于歌舞。月光在草坪上描出蕾丝般的纹样，乐声哀婉的呜咽一阵高过一阵，夏威夷的年轻人起初当着生人还面带腼腆，如今早已腼腆尽褪。米纳瓦小姐阖上双眼，向后倚在一株高耸棕榈的树干上。即便在夏威夷的情歌里，也总有一缕无望的哀愁，它拨动她的心绪，任何交响乐都不曾这样打动过她。一道帷幕掀起来了，她望进这些岛屿的往昔——白人到来之前，那原始而犷悍的往昔。

一段绵长而令人心碎的渐强音，乐声戛然而止，舞者们摇曳的身姿凝定了片刻。在米纳瓦小姐的朋友们看来，这正是告辞的好时机。他们走进屋去，在闷热的小客厅里，向棕肤含笑的男女主人道别。那个小婴孩——他的降生正是这场卢奥宴的缘起——也醒了一瞬，朝他们笑了笑。外面窄窄的街上，他们的汽车正候着。

===Original===
Through silent, deserted Honolulu they motored toward Waikiki. As they passed the Judiciary Building on King Street the clock in the tower struck the hour of one. She had not been out so late, Miss Minerva reflected, since that night when a visiting company sang “Parsifal” at the Boston Opera House.

The iron gates that guarded the drive at Dan’s house were closed. Leaving the car at the curb, Miss Minerva bade her friends good night and started up the walk toward the front door. The evening had thrilled her, and she moved with the long confident stride of youth. Dan’s scarlet garden was shrouded in darkness, for the moon, which had been playing an in-and-out game with the fast-moving clouds all evening, was again obscured. Exotic odors assailed her nostrils, she heard all about her the soft intriguing noises of the tropic night. She really should get to bed, she knew, but with a happy truant feeling she turned from the front walk and went to the side of the house for a last look at the breakers.

===Chinese===
汽车穿过沉寂无人的檀香山，朝怀基基驶去。经过国王街的司法大楼时，塔楼上的钟敲响了一点。米纳瓦小姐寻思，打从外来剧团在波士顿歌剧院献演《帕西法尔》（Parsifal）的那一晚起，她还不曾在外头待到这般晚。

丹宅车道口的铁门紧闭着。米纳瓦小姐在路边下了车，向朋友们道过晚安，便沿着步道朝正门走去。这个夜晚叫她心旌摇荡，她迈着年轻人那种悠长而笃定的步子。丹那猩红的花园已没入黑暗，整个晚上月亮都在同疾走的云彩玩着藏了又现的把戏，此刻又被遮住了。异香直扑她的鼻端，她听得见四面八方都是热带之夜轻柔而撩人的声息。她知道实在该上床了，却怀着逃学学童般的快活，拐下正前的步道，绕到房子侧面，想最后看一眼碎浪。

===Original===
She stood there under a poinciana tree near the door leading into Dan’s living-room. For nearly two weeks the Kona wind had prevailed, but now on her cheek, she thought she felt the first kindly breath of the trades. Very wide awake, she stared out at the dim foaming lines of surf between the shore and the coral reef. Her mind strayed back to the Honolulu she had known in Kalakaua’s day, to that era when the Islands were so naive, so colorful﻿—unspoiled. Ruined now, Dan had said, ruined by a damned mechanical civilization. “But away down underneath, Minerva, there are deep dark waters flowing still.”

The moon came out, touching with silver the waters at the crossroads, then was lost again under fleecy clouds. With a little sigh that was perhaps for her lost youth and the ’eighties, Miss Minerva pushed open the unlocked door leading into the great living-room, and closed it gently so as not to waken Dan.

===Chinese===
她在凤凰木下站定，树正挨着通往丹家起居室的那扇门。科纳风肆虐了将近两个星期，可此刻她觉得，颊上拂过的是信风最初的一缕慈息。她毫无睡意，凝望着海岸与珊瑚礁之间那一道道朦胧泛沫的浪线。她的心思飘回了她所熟识的、卡拉卡瓦王当政之日的檀香山，飘回那个群岛何等天真、何等绚丽——尚未被糟蹋的年代。如今全毁了，丹说过，毁于一种该死的机械文明。「可是——往下深处，米纳瓦，幽暗的深水依然在流淌呢。」

月亮出来了，给十字路口那片海面镀上一层银辉，随即又没入絮状的云后。米纳瓦小姐轻轻叹了口气——这叹息或许是为了她逝去的青春和八十年代——随后推开那扇没有上锁、通往大起居室的门，又轻轻掩上，免得吵醒丹。

===Original===
An intense darkness engulfed her. But she knew her way across the polished floor and set out confidently, walking on tiptoe. She had gone halfway to the hall door when she stopped, her heart in her mouth. For not five feet away she saw the luminous dial of a watch, and as she stared at it with frightened eyes, it moved.

Not for nothing had Miss Minerva studied restraint through more than fifty years. Many women would have screamed and fainted, Miss Minerva’s heart pounded madly, but that was all. Standing very still, she studied that phosphorescent dial. Its movement had been slight, it was now at rest again. A watch worn on someone’s wrist. Someone who had been on the point of action, but had now assumed an attitude of cautious waiting.

===Chinese===
一团浓黑将她吞没。但她认得穿过那打磨光亮的地板的路，便踮起脚尖，放心大胆地走了进去。走到离大厅门一半的地方，她停住了，心提到了嗓子眼。因为不出五英尺远，她看见一只表发出微光的表盘；她惊恐地瞪着它，它动了一下。

米纳瓦小姐修习克己功夫五十余年，绝非虚功。换了别的女人，早就尖叫着晕过去了；米纳瓦小姐的心狂跳不已，但也仅止于此。她一动不动地站着，端详那磷光闪闪的表盘。它方才动得轻微，此刻又静止了。一只戴在谁手腕上的表。那人本已将动未动，此刻却摆出了一副小心等候的架势。

===Original===
Well, Miss Minerva grimly asked herself, what was she going to do about it? Should she cry out a sharp: “Who’s there?” She was a brave woman, but the foolhardiness of such a course was apparent. She had a vision of that dial flashing nearer, a blow, perhaps strong hands at her throat.

She took a tentative step, and then another. Now, surely, the dial would stir again. But it remained motionless, steady, as though the arm that wore it were rigid at the intruder’s side.

===Chinese===
好吧，米纳瓦小姐冷峻地问自己，眼下她打算怎么办？要不要厉声喝一句：「谁在那儿？」她是个有胆量的女人，可这么办的鲁莽是明摆着的。她仿佛看见那表盘闪着光逼近来，一记重击，也许还有一双有力的大手掐上她的喉咙。

她试着迈了一步，又一步。这下那表盘总该再动一动了吧。它却纹丝不动、稳稳当当，仿佛戴表的那条手臂僵直地垂在这个不速之客的身侧。

===Original===
Suddenly Miss Minerva realized the situation. The wearer of the watch had forgotten the telltale numerals on his wrist, he thought himself hidden in the dark. He was waiting for her to go on through the room. If she made no sound, gave no sign of alarm, she might be safe. Once beyond that bamboo curtain leading into the hall, she could rouse the household.

She was a woman of great will power, but it took all she had to move serenely on her way. She shut her lips tightly and accomplished it, veering a bit from that circle of light that menaced her, looking back at it over her shoulder as she went. After what seemed an eternity the bamboo curtain received her, she was through it, she was on the stairs. But it seemed to her that never again would she be able to look at a watch or a clock and find that the hour was anything save twenty minutes past one!

===Chinese===
突然间，米纳瓦小姐明白了处境。戴表的人忘了腕上那出卖他的数字，自以为藏进了黑暗里。他在等她穿过这个房间。只要她不出声、不露惊慌，兴许就能平安无事。一旦穿过那道通往大厅的竹帘，她就能唤起全家。

她是意志坚强的女人，可要从容不迫地走完这段路，也用尽了她全部的力气。她紧闭双唇，做到了：稍稍绕开那圈威胁着她的光，一边走一边回头用眼角盯住它。挨过仿佛无穷无尽的时辰，竹帘终于接纳了她，她过去了，她踏上了楼梯。只是在她看来，往后无论看表还是看钟，指针除了指向一点二十分，再也不可能是别的时辰了！

===Original===
When she was halfway up the stairs, she recalled that it had been her intention to snap on the lights in the lower hall. She did not turn back, nor did she search for the switch at the head of the stairs. Instead, she went hastily on into her room, and just as though she had been an ordinary woman she closed her door and dropped down, trembling a little, on a chair.

But she was no ordinary woman, and in two seconds she was up and had reopened her door. Her sudden terror was evaporating; she felt her heart beat in a strong regular rhythm again. Action was what was required of her now, calm confident action; she was a Winterslip and she was ready.

===Chinese===
上到楼梯一半，她才想起自己原打算拧亮下面大厅的灯。她没有回头，也没有去摸楼梯顶上的开关，反而匆匆进了自己的房间，而且——活脱脱像个寻常女人似的——关上门，微微发着抖，跌坐进一把椅子里。

可她不是寻常女人，不出两秒钟，她已经站起身，重新打开了房门。骤然的恐惧正在消散；她感到自己的心跳又恢复了沉稳有力的节律。此刻需要她的是行动，沉着而自信的行动；她是温特斯利普家的人，她已准备停当。

===Original===
The servants’ quarters were in a wing over the kitchen, she went there at once and knocked on the first door she came to. She knocked once, then again, and finally the head of a very sleepy Jap appeared.

“Haku,” said Miss Minerva, “there is someone in the living-room. You must go down and investigate at once.”

He stared at her, seeming unable to comprehend.

“We must go down,” amended Miss Minerva.

“Wikiwiki!”

===Chinese===
仆役们的住处在厨房上头的一翼，她径直过去，在遇到的第一扇门上敲了敲。敲了一下，又敲一下，末了，一个睡得迷迷糊糊的日本仆人探出头来。

「哈库，」米纳瓦小姐说，「起居室里有个人。你必须马上下楼去查看。」

他瞪着她，像是听不明白。

「我们必须下去，」米纳瓦小姐改口道。

「快！快！」

===Original===
He disappeared, and Miss Minerva waited impatiently. Where was her nerve, she wondered, why hadn’t she seen this through alone? At home, no doubt, she could have managed it, but here there was something strange and terrifying in the very air. The moonlight poured in through a small window beside her, forming a bright square at her feet. Haku reappeared, wearing a gaudy kimono that he often sported on the beach.

Another door opened suddenly, and Miss Minerva started. Bah! What ailed her, anyhow, she wondered. It was only Kamaikui, standing there a massive figure in the dim doorway, a bronze statue clad in a holoku.

“Someone in the living-room,” Miss Minerva explained again. “I saw him as I came through.”

===Chinese===
他缩了回去，米纳瓦小姐不耐地等着。她的胆气哪儿去了，她纳闷，为何不独自把这事看到底？搁在老家，无疑她一个人就对付了，可在这儿，连空气里都有一种陌生而可怕的东西。月光从她身旁的小窗里倾泻进来，在她脚边铺出一方亮块。哈库再露面时，身上那件花哨的和服，正是他常穿着上海滩招摇的那件。

「起居室里有个人，」米纳瓦小姐又解释了一遍。「我穿过来的时候瞧见他的。」

===Original===
Kamaikui made no reply, but joined the odd little procession. In the upper hall Haku switched on the lights, both upstairs and down. At the head of the stairs there was a brief pause﻿—then Miss Minerva took her rightful place at the head of the line. She descended with a firm step, courageous and competent, Boston at its best. After her followed a stolid little Jap in a kimono gay with passionate poppies, and a Polynesian woman who wore the fearful Mother Hubbard of the missionaries as though it were a robe of state.

===Chinese===

===Original===
In the lower hall Miss Minerva did not hesitate. She pushed on through the bamboo curtain and her hand﻿—it trembled ever so slightly﻿—found the electric switch and flooded the living-room with light. She heard the crackle of bamboo behind her as her strange companions followed where she led. She stood looking curiously about her.

There was no one in sight, no sign of any disturbance, and it suddenly occurred to Miss Minerva that perhaps she was behaving in a rather silly fashion. She had experienced a stirring evening. Then, too, she remembered, there had been that small glass of okolehau. A potent concoction!

Kamaikui and Haku were looking at her with the inquiring eyes of little children. Had she roused them for a fool’s errand? Her cheeks flushed slightly. Certainly in this big brilliant room, furnished with magnificent native woods and green with many potted ferns, everything seemed proper and in order.

“I﻿—I may have been mistaken,” she said in a low voice. “I was quite sure﻿—but there’s no sign of anything wrong. Mr. Winterslip has not been resting well of late. If he should be asleep we won’t waken him.”

She went to the door leading on to the lanai and pushed aside the curtain. Bright moonlight outside revealed most of the veranda’s furnishings, and here, too, all seemed well. “Dan,” Miss Minerva called softly. “Dan. Are you awake?”

No answer. Miss Minerva was certain now that she was making a mountain out of a molehill. She was about to turn back into the living-room when her eyes, grown more accustomed to the semidarkness, noted a rather startling fact.

Day and night, over Dan’s cot in one corner of the lanai, hung a white mosquito netting. It was not there now.

===Chinese===
她走到通往露台的门边，撩开门帘。外面明亮的月光照出了廊下大部分的陈设，这里同样一切如常。「丹，」米纳瓦小姐轻声唤道。「丹。你醒着吗？」

===Chinese===
「来，哈库，」米纳瓦小姐说。「把外头的灯打开。」

哈库过来了，绿灯罩的灯在他手下一亮。正是那盏小灯：那天晚上，丹就在灯下读晚报，忽然显得心绪大乱，冲出去给旧金山的罗杰寄了一封信。米纳瓦小姐站在那儿回想那桩事，又想起了别的种种，因为她极不愿意把身子转向角落里那张帆布床。她觉出卡迈库伊从她身边擦过，接着，她听见一声低低的、半是野性的呜咽，含着惊惧与悲恸。
