## IV A Friend of Tim’s / 四 蒂姆的朋友

===Original===
It was another of those mornings on which the fog maybe did not come. Roger and his guests were in the limousine again; it seemed to John Quincy that they had left it only a few minutes before. So it must have seemed to the chauffeur too as, sleepy-eyed, he hurried them toward the waterfront.

“By the way, John Quincy,” Roger said, “you’ll want to change your money before you go aboard.”

John Quincy gathered his wandering thoughts. “Oh, yes, of course,” he answered.

===Chinese===
又是一个雾没准儿不来的早晨。罗杰和他的客人们再度坐进豪华轿车；在约翰·昆西看来，他们几分钟前才刚从这辆车里下来。司机想必也作如是想，只见他睡眼惺忪，匆匆载着他们往滨水区赶。

「对了，约翰·昆西，」罗杰说，「上船前你总得把钱换一换。」

约翰·昆西收拢起涣散的思绪。「哦，对，当然。」他答道。

===Original===
Roger smiled. “Just what sort of money would you like to change it for?” he inquired.

“Why—” began John Quincy. He stopped. “Why, I always thought—”

“Don’t pay any attention to Roger,” Barbara laughed. “He’s spoofing you.” She was fresh and blooming, a little matter like three a.m. made no difference to her. “Only about one person out of a thousand in this country knows that Hawaii is a part of the United States, and the fact annoys us deeply over in the Islands. Dear old Roger was trying to get you in wrong with me by enrolling you among the nine hundred and ninety-nine.”

“Almost did it, too,” chuckled Roger.

===Chinese===
罗杰微微一笑。「那么您想把您的钱换成哪一国的钱呢？」他问道。

「呃——」约翰·昆西开了口。他顿住了。「呃，我一向以为——」

「别理罗杰，」芭芭拉笑道。「他是逗你玩儿呢。」她神清气爽、容光焕发，凌晨三点那么点小事对她毫无影响。「这个国家里一千个人当中大约只有一个知道夏威夷是美国的一部分，这个事实让我们岛上的人憋气得很。亲爱的老罗杰方才就是想把你编进那九百九十九个里去，好叫你在我这儿失宠。」

「差一点儿就得手了，」罗杰咯咯直笑。

===Original===
“Nonsense,” said Barbara. “John Quincy is too intelligent. He’s not like that congressman who wrote a letter to ‘the American Consul at Honolulu.’ ”

“Did one of them do that?” smiled John Quincy.

“He certainly did. We almost gave up the struggle after that. Then there was the senator who came out on a junket, and began a speech with: ‘When I get home to my country—’ Someone in the audience shouted: ‘You’re there now, you big stiff!’ It wasn’t elegant, of course, but it expressed our feeling perfectly. Oh, we’re touchy, John Quincy.”

“Don’t blame you a bit,” he told her. “I’ll be very careful what I say.”

===Chinese===
「瞎说，」芭芭拉说。「约翰·昆西可聪明着呢。他不像那位给『檀香山的美国领事』写信的众议员。」

「真有议员干出这种事？」约翰·昆西含笑问。

「千真万确。打那以后，我们差点儿不想争这口气了。后来又有一位参议员出来游历，演说一开口便是：『等我回到了我的国家——』听众里有人喊：『你现在就在自个儿的国家，你这个大笨蛋！』话固然说得不雅，可把我们的心情表达得再透彻不过。唉，我们可敏感着呢，约翰·昆西。」

「一点儿也不怪你们，」他对她说。「往后我说话可要留神。」

===Original===
They had reached the Embarcadero, and the car halted before one of the piers. The chauffeur descended and began to gather up the baggage. Roger and John Quincy took a share of it, and they traversed the pier-shed to the gangplank.

“Get along to your office, Roger,” Barbara said.

“No hurry,” he answered. “I’ll go aboard with you, of course.”

===Chinese===
他们到了安巴卡德罗（Embarcadero）滨水码头区，汽车在其中一座码头前停住。司机下车动手搬行李，罗杰和约翰·昆西也各拎了一部分，三人穿过码头货棚，走上跳板。

「快去上你的班吧，罗杰。」芭芭拉说。

「不忙，」他答道。「我当然要送你们上船。」

===Original===
Amid the confusion of the deck, a party of girls swept down on Barbara, pretty lively girls of the California brand. John Quincy learned with some regret that they were there only to see Barbara off. A big broad-shouldered man in white pushed his way through the crowd.

“Hello there!” he called to Barbara.

“Hello, Harry,” she answered. “You, know Roger, don’t you? John Quincy, this is an old friend of mine, Harry Jennison.”

===Chinese===
甲板上一片忙乱，一群姑娘朝芭芭拉涌了过来，是加利福尼亚牌子的那种活泼漂亮姑娘。约翰·昆西略带遗憾地得知，她们只是来给芭芭拉送行的。一个穿白衣裳的宽肩膀大个子从人群中挤了过来。

「嘿，你好啊！」他朝芭芭拉喊道。

「你好，哈利，」她答道。「你认得罗杰的，对不对？约翰·昆西，这是我的一位老朋友，哈里·詹尼森（Harry Jennison）。」

===Original===
Mr. Jennison was extremely good-looking, his face was deeply tanned by the Island sun, his hair blond and wavy, his gray eyes amused and cynical. Altogether, he was the type of man women look at twice and never forget; John Quincy felt himself at once supplanted in the eyes of Barbara’s friends.

Jennison seized the boy’s hand in a firm grip. “Sailing too, Mr. Winterslip?” he inquired. “That’s good. Between us we ought to be able to keep this young woman entertained.”

===Chinese===
詹尼森先生生得极其英俊，脸被岛上的太阳晒成深棕色，一头金色的鬈发，灰眼睛里带着玩世不恭的笑意。总而言之，他是那种女人要看上第二眼、而且永远忘不掉的男人；约翰·昆西顿时感到，在芭芭拉这帮助友眼里，自己已经被人取而代之了。

詹尼森一把紧紧握住小伙子的手。「您也坐船，温特斯利普先生？」他问道。「那敢情好。咱们俩搭伙，保管陪得这位小姐开开心心。」

===Original===
The shore call sounded, and the confusion increased. Along the deck came a little old lady, followed by a Chinese woman servant. They walked briskly, and the crowd gave way before them.

“Hello—this is luck,” cried Roger. “Madame Maynard—just a moment. I want you to meet a cousin of mine from Boston.” He introduced John Quincy. “I give him into your charge. Couldn’t find a better guide, philosopher and friend for him if I combed the Islands.”

The old lady glanced at John Quincy. Her black eyes snapped. “Another Winterslip, eh?” she said. “Hawaii’s all cluttered up with ’em now. Well, the more the merrier. I know your aunt.”

===Chinese===
催客上岸的号声响了，人声愈发嘈杂。甲板上走来一位瘦小的老妇人，身后跟着一个中国女仆。两人步履轻快，人群在她们面前纷纷让路。

「嘿——真是巧了，」罗杰喊道。「梅纳德夫人（Madame Maynard）——请留步。我给您引见一位我波士顿来的堂兄弟。」他把约翰·昆西介绍给她。「我把他托付给您了。就算我把整个群岛翻个遍，也再找不出比您更好的导师、哲人和朋友。」

老妇人朝约翰·昆西瞥了一眼。她的黑眼睛亮光一闪。「又是温特斯利普家的人，嗯？」她说。「夏威夷如今满坑满谷都是你们姓温的。也罢，人越多越热闹。我认得你姑婆。」

===Original===
“Stick close to her, John Quincy,” Roger admonished.

She shook her head. “I’m a million years old,” she protested. “The boys don’t stick so close any more. They like ’em younger. However, I’ll keep my eye on him. My good eye. Well, Roger, run over some time.” And she moved away.

“A grand soul,” said Roger, smiling after her. “You’ll like her. Old missionary family, and her word’s law over there.”

“Who’s this Jennison?” asked John Quincy.

===Chinese===
「一步也别离开她，约翰·昆西。」罗杰叮嘱道。

她摇了摇头。「我都一百万岁了，」她抗议道。「小伙子们如今不肯紧贴着我喽。他们喜欢年轻的。不过，我会替你看着他点的。用我那只好眼睛。对了罗杰，得空过来串门。」说完她走开了。

「一个了不起的人，」罗杰望着她的背影微笑道。「你会喜欢她的。老传教士世家，在那边她一句话就是法律。」

「这个詹尼森是什么人？」约翰·昆西问。

===Original===
“Him?” Roger glanced over to where Mr. Jennison stood, the center of an admiring feminine group. “Oh, he’s Dan’s lawyer. One of the leading citizens of Honolulu, I believe. John J. Adonis himself, isn’t he?” An officer appeared, herding the reluctant throng toward the gangplank. “I’ll have to leave you, John Quincy. A pleasant journey. When you come through on your way home, give me a few more days to try to convince you on my San Francisco offer.”

John Quincy laughed. “You’ve been mighty kind.”

“Not at all.” Roger shook his hand warmly. “Take care of yourself over there. Hawaii’s a little too much like Heaven to be altogether safe. So long, my boy, so long.”

He moved away. John Quincy saw him kiss Barbara affectionately and with her friends join the slow procession ashore.

===Chinese===
「他呀？」罗杰朝詹尼森先生站的地方瞥了一眼——他正被一圈仰慕他的女客团团围住。「哦，他是丹的律师。檀香山的头面人物之一，我想。活脱脱又一位『约翰·杰·阿多尼斯』，对不对？」一名船上的官员出现，把恋恋不舍的人群往跳板那边赶。「我得告辞了，约翰·昆西。祝旅途愉快。回程路过时，再给我几天工夫，让我接着劝你接受我那个旧金山的差事。」

约翰·昆西笑了。「你待我实在太好了。」

「不值一提。」罗杰热乎乎地同他握手。「到那边多保重。夏威夷有点太像天堂，未免不完全保险。再会，孩子，再会。」

他走开了。约翰·昆西看见他深情地吻别芭芭拉，随后同她及她的朋友们一道，汇入缓缓登岸的行列。

===Original===
The young man from Boston stepped to the rail. Several hundred voices were calling admonitions, promises, farewells. With that holiday spirit so alien to John Quincy’s experience, those ashore were throwing confetti. The streamers grew in number, making a tangle of color, a last frail bond with the land. The gangplank was taken up, clumsily the *President Tyler* began to draw away from the pier. On the topmost deck a band was playing—“Aloha-oe,” the sweetest, most melancholy song of goodbye ever written. John Quincy was amazed to feel a lump rising in his throat.

===Chinese===
那个波士顿来的年轻人走到船栏边。岸上几百个嗓音在喊着叮嘱、许诺和道别。岸上的人们撒着彩纸屑，带着那种约翰·昆西全然陌生的节日兴致。彩带越抛越多，缠成一片乱纷纷的色彩，那是与陆地相连的最后一缕脆弱纽带。跳板收了起来，泰勒总统号笨手笨脚地开始驶离码头。最上层甲板上，乐队正奏着《阿罗哈·奥埃》（Aloha-oe）——世上谱写过的最甜美、最忧伤的一支告别曲。约翰·昆西惊讶地发觉，自己的喉咙里涌起一团哽咽。

===Original===
The frail, gay-colored bond was breaking now. A thin veined hand at John Quincy’s side waved a handkerchief. He turned to find Mrs. Maynard. There were tears on her cheeks.

“Silly old woman,” she said. “Sailed away from this town a hundred and twenty-eight times. Actual count—I keep a diary. Cried every time. What about? I don’t know.”

The ship was well out in the harbor now. Barbara came along, Jennison trailing her. The girl’s eyes were wet.

“An emotional lot, we Islanders,” said the old lady. She put her arm about the girl’s slim waist. “Here’s another one of ’em. Living way off the way we do, any goodbye at all—it saddens us.” She and Barbara moved on down the deck.

===Chinese===
这缕脆弱而艳丽的纽带此刻正在扯断。约翰·昆西身旁一只青筋虬结的瘦手挥着手帕。他回过头，看见了梅纳德夫人。她颊上有泪。

「傻老婆子，」她说。「打这城里坐船走了一百二十八回了。实打实数出来的——我记日记呢。回回都哭。哭什么呢？我自己也不知道。」

这会儿船已驶出港湾老远。芭芭拉走过来，詹尼森缀在后头。姑娘的眼睛湿汪汪的。

「我们岛民啊，就是一帮多愁善感的，」老妇人说。她伸臂搂住姑娘纤细的腰。「这不又是一个。像我们这样住得离一切老远老远的，随便哪一种告别——都叫人心里发酸。」她同芭芭拉往甲板那头去了。

===Original===
Jennison stopped. His eyes were quite dry. “First trip out?” he inquired.

“Oh, yes,” replied John Quincy.

“Hope you’ll like us,” Jennison said. “Not Massachusetts, of course, but we’ll do our best to make you feel at home. It’s a way we have with strangers.”

“I’m sure I shall have a bully time,” John Quincy remarked. But he felt somewhat depressed. Three thousand miles from Beacon Street—and moving on! He waved to someone he fancied might be Roger on the dock, and went to find his stateroom.

===Chinese===
詹尼森站住脚。他的眼睛干干的。「头一回坐船出来？」他问。

「哦，是的。」约翰·昆西答道。

「希望你会喜欢我们这儿，」詹尼森说。「当然，比不得马萨诸塞，可我们一定尽力让你觉得跟在家里一样。我们招呼生客，就有这么一手。」

「我保管痛痛快快玩一场。」约翰·昆西说。可他心里有些抑郁。离开灯塔街三千英里——而且还在往前赶！他朝码头上一个他琢磨着也许是罗杰的人影挥了挥手，便去找自己的舱房。

===Original===
He learned that he was to share his cabin with two missionaries. One was a tall, gloomy old man with a lemon-colored face—an honored veteran of the foreign field named Upton. The other was a ruddy-cheeked boy whose martyrdom was still before him. John Quincy suggested drawing lots for a choice of berths, but even this mild form of gambling appeared distasteful to these emissaries of the church.

“You boys take the berths,” said Upton. “Leave me the couch. I don’t sleep well anyhow.” His tone was that of one who prefers to suffer.

John Quincy politely objected. After further discussion it was settled that he was to have the upper berth, the old man the lower, and the boy the couch. The Reverend Mr. Upton seemed disappointed. He had played the role of martyr so long he resented seeing anyone else in the part.

===Chinese===
他这才知道，他的舱房要同两位传教士合住。一位是高个子、阴沉沉的老头，一张柠檬色的脸——海外传教区一位德高望重的老前辈，名叫厄普顿（Upton）。另一位是个红脸膛的小伙子，他的殉道之途还在前头。约翰·昆西提议抽签挑铺位，可就连这种顶温和的赌法，两位教会使者看来也觉得有伤大雅。

「你们两个孩子睡铺位，」厄普顿说。「睡榻留给我。反正我也睡不安稳。」他那口气，活脱是个甘愿受苦的人。

约翰·昆西客气地推让了一番。又商量了一阵，最后议定：他睡上铺，老人睡下铺，小伙子睡睡榻。厄普顿牧师似乎还挺失望。他扮殉道者扮了这么多年，眼看别人顶了这个角色，心里老大不痛快。

===Original===
The Pacific was behaving in a most unfriendly manner, tossing the great ship about as though it were a piece of driftwood. John Quincy decided to dispense with lunch, and spent the afternoon reading in his berth. By evening he felt better, and under the watchful and somewhat disapproving eyes of the missionaries, arrayed himself carefully for dinner.

His name being Winterslip, he had been invited to sit at the captain’s table. He found Madame Maynard, serene and twinkling, at the captain’s right, Barbara at his left, and Jennison at Barbara’s side. It appeared that oddly enough there was an aristocracy of the Islands, and John Quincy, while he thought it quaint there should be such distinctions in an outpost like Hawaii, took his proper place as a matter of course.

===Chinese===
太平洋表现得极不友善，把大轮船颠来簸去，好像它不过是一截浮木。约翰·昆西决定午饭免了，一下午躺在铺位上看书。傍晚时分他觉得好受了些，便在两位传教士留神而颇含非难的目光下，细细穿戴齐整，去进晚餐。

他既姓温特斯利普，便被邀在船长席用膳。他发现梅纳德夫人安详娴雅、眼波闪闪，坐在船长右首；芭芭拉坐左首，詹尼森挨着芭芭拉。说来也怪，岛上居然也有一个贵族阶层，而约翰·昆西一面觉得，在夏威夷这样的天涯前哨竟还讲这等门第，未免古雅，一面照例坐进了自己应得的位置。

===Original===
Mrs. Maynard chatted brightly of her many trips over this route. Suddenly she turned to Barbara. “How does it happen, my dear,” she asked, “that you’re not on the college boat?”

“All booked up,” Barbara explained.

“Nonsense,” said the frank old lady. “*You* could have got on. But then”—she looked meaningly toward Jennison—“I presume this ship was not without its attraction.”

The girl flushed slightly and made no reply.

“What,” John Quincy inquired, “is the college boat?”

===Chinese===
梅纳德夫人兴致勃勃地谈着她在这条航线上的多次旅行。忽然她转身向着芭芭拉。「亲爱的，这是怎么搞的，」她问，「你怎么没搭『大学船』？」

「都订满了，」芭芭拉解释道。

「胡说，」心直口快的老妇人说。「你要想上，总上得去的。不过嘛」——她意味深长地朝詹尼森瞟了一眼——「我估摸着，这条船也自有它的吸引力。」

姑娘微微红了脸，没有作声。

「什么叫『大学船』？」约翰·昆西问。

===Original===
“So many children from Hawaii at school on the mainland,” the old lady explained, “that every June around this time they practically fill a ship. We call it the college boat. This year it’s the *Matsonia*. She left San Francisco today at noon.”

“I’ve got a lot of friends aboard her,” Barbara said. “I do wish we could beat her in. Captain, what are the chances?”

“Well, that depends,” replied the captain cautiously.

“She isn’t due until Tuesday morning,” Barbara persisted. “Wouldn’t it be a lark if you could land us the night before? As a favor to me, Captain.”

===Chinese===
「夏威夷在大陆上念书的孩子那么多，」老妇人解释道，「每年六月赶这时候，他们差不多能坐满一整条船。我们管它叫『大学船』。今年是马特森尼亚号（*Matsonia*）。她今天正午驶离了旧金山。」

「我有一大帮朋友在那条船上，」芭芭拉说。「真盼着咱们能赶在她前头进港。船长，有多大把握？」

「唔，那得看情况，」船长谨慎地答道。

「她按班期礼拜二早上才到，」芭芭拉不肯罢休。「要是您能提前一夜让我们上岸，那该多带劲！就当帮我个忙，船长。」

===Original===
“When you look at me like that,” smiled the officer, “I can only say that I’ll make a supreme effort. I’m just as eager as you to make port on Monday—it would mean I could get off to the Orient that much sooner.”

“Then it’s settled,” Barbara beamed.

“It’s settled that we’ll try,” he said. “Of course, if I speed up there’s always the chance I may arrive off Honolulu after sundown, and be compelled to lay by until morning. That would be torture for you.”

“I’ll risk it,” Barbara smiled. “Wouldn’t dear old dad be pleased if I should burst upon his vision Monday evening?”

“My dear girl,” the captain said gallantly, “any man would be pleased to have you burst upon his vision any time.”

===Chinese===
「你这么望着我，」船长微笑道，「我就只能说：我一定尽最大的努力。我跟你们一样巴望礼拜一进港——那样我也能早些动身去东方。」

「那就说定了，」芭芭拉笑靥如花。

「说定的是我们尽力，」他说。「当然，我一加速，就总有这个可能：日落之后才抵达檀香山外海，只好停船等到天亮。那你们可要活受罪了。」

「这险我甘愿冒，」芭芭拉微笑道。「要是我礼拜一晚上突然显现在亲爱的老爸眼前，他该多高兴呀！」

「亲爱的姑娘，」船长殷勤地说，「任何一个男人，任什么时候让你突然显现在他眼前，都会高兴的。」

===Original===
There was, John Quincy reflected, much in what the captain said. Up to that moment there had been little of the romantic in his relations with girls; he was accustomed to look upon them merely as tennis or golf opponents or a fourth at bridge. Barbara would demand a different classification. There was an enticing gleam in her blue eyes, a hint of the eternal feminine in everything she did or said, and John Quincy was no wooden man.
