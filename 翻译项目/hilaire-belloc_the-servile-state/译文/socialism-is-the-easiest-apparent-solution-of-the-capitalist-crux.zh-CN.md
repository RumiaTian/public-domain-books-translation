## VII Socialism Is the Easiest Apparent Solution of the Capitalist Crux / 第七章 社会主义是资本主义困境最显而易见的表面出路
===Original===

A contrast between the reformer making for distribution and the reformer making for socialism (or collectivism)﻿—The difficulties met by the first type﻿—He is working against the grain﻿—The second is working with the grain﻿—Collectivism a natural development of capitalism﻿—It appeals both to capitalist and proletarian﻿—None the less we shall see that the collectivist attempt is doomed to fail and to produce a thing very different from its object﻿—To wit, the servile state.
I say that the line of least resistance, if it be followed, leads a capitalist state to transform itself into a servile state.
I propose to show that this comes about from the fact that not a *distributive* but a *collectivist* solution is the easiest for a capitalist state to aim at, and that yet, in the very act of attempting *collectivism*, what results is not collectivism at all, but the servitude of the many, and the confirmation in their present privilege of the few; that is, the servile state.
---
Men to whom the institution of slavery is abhorrent propose for the remedy of capitalism one of two reforms.
Either they would put property into the hands of most citizens, so dividing land and capital that a determining number of families in the state were possessed of the means of production; or they would put those means of production into the hands of the political officers of the community, to be held in trust for the advantage of all.
The first solution may be called the attempted establishment of the **distributive state**. The second may be called the attempted establishment of the **collectivist state**.
Those who favour the first course are the conservatives or traditionalists. They are men who respect and would, if possible, preserve the old forms of Christian European life. They know that property was thus distributed throughout the state during the happiest periods of our past history; they also know that where it is properly distributed today, you have greater social sanity and ease than elsewhere. In general, those who would reestablish, if possible, the distributive state in the place of, and as a remedy for, the vices and unrest of capitalism, are men concerned with known realities, and having for their goal a condition of society which experience has tested and proved both stable and good.

===Chinese===

趋向分散所有制的改革者与趋向社会主义（或集体主义）的改革者之间的对照——第一类人所遭遇的困难——他是在逆势而动——第二类人则是在顺水推舟——集体主义乃是资本主义的自然发展——它对资本家和无产者皆有吸引力——然而我们仍将看到，集体主义的企图注定要归于失败，并产生出一种与其目标大相径庭的事物——即奴役国家。
我断言：顺应阻力最小的途径（若予依循），必将引领资本主义国家将其自身转变为奴役国家（the Servile State）。
我打算证明，这是源于这样一个事实：对资本主义国家而言，最容易追求的目标并非*分散所有制*的解决方案，而是*集体主义*的解决方案；然而，正是在试图推行*集体主义*的行为本身之中，其最终结果根本不是集体主义，而是大众沦为奴役、少数人巩固其现存特权；亦即奴役国家。
---
对奴隶制制度深恶痛绝的人们，针对资本主义的救治提出了两种改革方案之一。
他们要么将财产置于大多数公民手中，通过对土地和资本的分散，使国家中具有决定性数量的家庭拥有生产资料；要么将那些生产资料置于共同体的政治官员手中，由其为了所有人的利益代为托管。
第一种解决方案可以称为建立**分散所有制国家（distributive state）**的尝试。第二种则可称为建立**集体主义国家（collectivist state）**的尝试。
赞同第一种途径的人是保守派或传统主义者。他们尊重并渴望在可能的情况下保全欧洲基督教生活的古老形态。他们深知，在我们过去历史上最幸福的时期，财产在整个国家中正是以这种方式分布的；他们也深知，在今日财产分布得当之处，你所见到的社会健全与从容安定远甚于别处。一般而言，那些渴望在可能的情况下重建分散所有制国家以取代资本主义之弊害与骚乱并作为其救治之道的人，皆是关注已知现实的人，并将经验所检验和证明为既稳定又美好的社会形态作为其追求目标。

===Original===

They are then, of the two schools of reformers, the more *practical* in the sense that they deal more than do the collectivists (called also socialists) with things which either are or have been in actual existence. But they are less practical in another sense (as we shall see in a moment) from the fact that the stage of the disease with which they are dealing does not readily lend itself to such a reaction as they propose.
The collectivist, on the other hand, proposes to put land and capital into the hands of the political officers of the community, and this on the understanding that they shall hold such land and capital in trust for the advantage of the community. In making this proposal he is evidently dealing with a state of things hitherto imaginary, and his ideal is not one that has been tested by experience, nor one of which our race and history can furnish instances. In this sense, therefore, he is the *less* practical of the two reformers. His ideal cannot be discovered in any past, known, and recorded phase of our society. We cannot examine socialism in actual working, nor can we say (as we can say of well-divided property): “On such and such an occasion, in such and such a period of European history, collectivism was established and produced both stability and happiness in society.”
In this sense, therefore, the collectivist is far less practical than the reformer who desires well-distributed property.
On the other hand, there is a sense in which this socialist is more practical than that other type of reformer, from the fact that the stage of the disease into which we have fallen apparently admits of his remedy with less shock than it admits of a reaction towards well-divided property.
For example: the operation of buying out some great tract of private ownership today (as a railway or a harbour company) with public funds, continuing its administration by publicly paid officials and converting its revenue to public use, is a thing with which we are familiar and which seemingly might be indefinitely multiplied. Individual examples of such transformation of waterworks, gas, tramways, from a capitalist to a collectivist basis are common, and the change does not disturb any fundamental thing in our society.

于是，在这两派改革者之中，他们在这种意义上更为*务实*：与集体主义者（亦称社会主义者）相比，他们更多地打交道于实际存在或曾经存在过的事物。然而在另一种意义上，他们却不够务实（正如我们片刻之后将看到的那样），因为他们所处理的疾病阶段并不容易接受他们所提出的这种逆转反应。
另一方面，集体主义者提议将土地和资本置于共同体的政治官员手中，其前提是这些官员应当为了共同体的利益托管此类土地和资本。在提出这一建议时，他显然是在打交道于一种迄今纯属想象的事态，他的理想并非某种经受过经验检验的理想，亦非我们的民族与历史能够提供先例的理想。因此，在这种意义上，他是两位改革者中*较不*务实的一位。他的理想无法在我们社会的任何过去、已知和有记录的阶段中被发现。我们无法检验实际运转中的社会主义，我们也不能（像我们谈论分散良好的财产制时那样）断言：“在欧洲历史上的某时某刻、某一时期，集体主义曾经确立，并为社会带来了稳定与幸福。”
因此，在这种意义上，集体主义者远不如渴望财产良好分散的改革者务实。
另一方面，在某种意义上，这位社会主义者却比另一类改革者更为务实，因为我们陷入其中的疾病阶段，在接受他的救治方案时表面上受到的震荡，要少于接受向分散良好财产制倒退所产生的震荡。
例如：今日利用公共资金赎买某一大片私有产业（如铁路或港口公司），继续由公帑雇用的官员进行管理并将其收益转归公用，这是一件我们完全熟悉且看似可以无限复制的事情。将自来水厂、燃气公司、有轨电车从资本主义基础转变为集体主义基础的个案比比皆是，这种变革丝毫不触动我们社会中的任何根本事物。

===Original===

When a private water company or tramway line is bought by some town and worked thereafter in the interests of the public, the transaction is effected without any perceptible friction, disturbs the life of no private citizen, and seems in every way normal to the society in which it takes place.
Upon the contrary, the attempt to create a large number of shareholders in such enterprises and artificially to substitute many partners, distributed throughout a great number of the population, in the place of the original few capitalist owners, would prove lengthy and at every step would arouse opposition, would create disturbance, would work at an expense of great friction, and would be imperilled by the power of the new and many owners to sell again to a few.
In a word, the man who desires to reestablish property as an institution normal to most citizens in the state is *working against the grain* of our existing capitalist society, while a man who desires to establish socialism﻿—that is collectivism﻿—is working *with* the grain of that society. The first is like a physician who should say to a man whose limbs were partially atrophied from disuse: “Do this and that, take such and such exercise, and you will recover the use of your limbs.” The second is like a physician who should say: “You cannot go on as you are. Your limbs are atrophied from lack of use. Your attempt to conduct yourself as though they were not is useless and painful; you had better make up your mind to be wheeled about in a fashion consonant to your disease.” The physician is the reformer, his patient the proletariat.
It is not the purpose of this book to show how and under what difficulties a condition of well-divided property might be restored and might take the place (even in England) of that capitalism which is now no longer either stable or tolerable; but for the purposes of contrast and to emphasise my argument I will proceed, before showing how the collectivist unconsciously makes for the servile state, to show what difficulties surround the distributive solution and why, therefore, the collectivist solution appeals so much more readily to men living under capitalism.
---
If I desire to substitute a number of small owners for a few large ones in some particular enterprise, how shall I set to work?
I might boldly confiscate and redistribute at a blow.

当某座市镇买下一家私营自来水公司或有轨电车线路，并在日后为了公众利益进行运营时，该交易是在没有任何可察觉的摩擦中完成的，不扰乱任何私人公民的生活，且在其发生的社会看来在各方面都显得完全合情合理。
相反，若试图在此类企业中培育大量的小股东，并在绝大多数人口中人为地建立起许多合伙人，以取代原先少数资本家所有者，这一过程将被证明是冗长漫长的，且每走一步都会激起反对、制造动荡、付出巨大摩擦的代价，并且随时面临新出现的众多小所有者再次将股权转卖给少数人的致命危险。
简言之，渴望将财产重建为国家大多数公民之常态制度的人，是在*逆着*我们现有资本主义社会的*纹理而动*；而渴望建立社会主义——亦即集体主义——的人，则是在*顺着*该社会的*纹理行事*。前者犹如一位医生对一个因缺乏使用而四肢部分萎缩的人说：“做这个做那个，进行这样那样的锻炼，你就会恢复四肢的功能。”后者则犹如一位医生说：“你不能按现在的样子硬撑下去了。你的四肢因缺乏使用已经萎缩。你试图表现得好像它们未曾萎缩一样的举动是徒劳且痛苦的；你最好下定决心，按照契合你病况的方式坐轮椅代步。”医生即改革者，他的病人则是无产阶级。
本书的目的并非阐明如何以及在何种困难之下才能恢复良好分散的财产状态，并（即便在英格兰）取代如今既不稳定又不可忍受的资本主义；然而为了形成对比并凸显我的论点，在说明集体主义者如何下意识地迈向奴役国家之前，我将先行阐明围绕着分散所有制解决方案的重重困难，以及生活在资本主义之下的民众为何因此而远更容易为集体主义解决方案所吸引。

我或许可以断然采取没收手段，在一击之下完成重新分配。

But by what process should I choose the new owners? Even supposing that there was some machinery whereby the justice of the new distribution could be assured, how could I avoid the enormous and innumerable separate acts of injustice that would attach to general redistributions? To say “none shall own” and to confiscate is one thing; to say “all should own” and apportion ownership is another. Action of this kind would so disturb the whole network of economic relations as to bring ruin at once to the whole body politic, and particularly to the smaller interests indirectly affected. In a society such as ours a catastrophe falling upon the state from outside might indirectly do good by making such a redistribution possible. But no one working from within the state could provoke that catastrophe without ruining his own cause.
If, then, I proceed more slowly and more rationally and canalise the economic life of society so that small property shall gradually be built up within it, see against what forces of inertia and custom I have to work today in a capitalist society!
If I desire to benefit small savings at the expense of large, I must reverse the whole economy under which interest is paid upon deposits today. It is far easier to save £100 out of a revenue of £1,000 than to save £10 out of a revenue of £100. It is infinitely easier to save £10 out of a revenue of £100 than £5 out of a revenue of £50. To build up small property through thrift when once the mass have fallen into the proletarian trough is impossible unless you deliberately subsidise small savings, offering them a reward which, in competition, they could never obtain; and to do this the whole vast arrangement of credit must be worked backwards. Or, let the policy be pursued of penalising undertakings with few owners, of heavily taxing large blocks of shares and of subsidising with the produce small holders in proportion to the smallness of their holding. Here again you are met with the difficulty of a vast majority who cannot even bid for the smallest share.
One might multiply instances of the sort indefinitely, but the strongest force against the distribution of ownership in a society already permeated with capitalist modes of thought is still the moral one: Will men want to own?

然而，我应当依循何种程序来挑选新的所有者？即便假定存在某种能够确保新分配之公正性的机制，我又该如何避免普遍重新分配所必然伴随的巨大且不可胜数的个别不公正行径？宣称“任何人都不得拥有财产”并予以没收是一回事；宣称“人人都应当拥有财产”并按份额分摊所有权则是另一回事。这种性质的举动将剧烈扰乱整个经济关系网络，以致立即给整个政治体带来毁灭，尤其是间接受其波及的较小利益主体。在像我们这样的社会中，从外部降临于国家的灾难或许可以通过使这种重新分配成为可能而间接促成益处。但从国家内部开展工作的人，若挑起这场浩劫，便绝不可能不毁灭他自身的追求。
那么，若我采取更为缓慢、更为理性的步骤，疏导社会的经济生活，以便在其内部逐步建立起小财产制，请看在今日的资本主义社会中，我必须克服何等巨大的惯性与习俗阻力！
若我想牺牲大额储蓄来扶持小额储蓄，我就必须彻底逆转今日对存款支付利息的全部经济机制。从1,000英镑（£1,000）的收入中省出100英镑（£100），远比从100英镑（£100）的收入中省出10英镑（£10）容易得多。从100英镑（£100）的收入中省出10英镑（£10），又比从50英镑（£50）的收入中省出5英镑（£5）容易得无可比拟。一旦大众跌入了无产阶级的深渊，试图通过勤俭积累小财产便绝无可能，除非你蓄意补贴小额储蓄，向其提供在自由竞争中绝不可能获得的丰厚报酬；而要做到这一点，庞大的信贷体制必须全盘倒转操作。或者，采取对少数所有者企业实施惩罚的政策，对大宗股份征收重税，并按持有份额之微小成比例地补贴小持有者。在此，你又将遭遇绝大多数人甚至连哪怕最微小的份额都无力出资竞购的困境。
人们可以无限列举此类例证，但在早已浸透了资本主义思维方式的社会中，阻碍所有权分散的最强大力量依然是道德层面的阻力：人们究竟还渴望拥有财产吗？

Will officials, administrators, and lawmakers be able to shake off the power which under capitalism seems normal to the rich? If I approach, for instance, the works of one of our great trusts, purchase it with public money, bestow, even as a gift, the shares thereof to its workmen, can I count upon any tradition of property in their midst which will prevent their squandering the new wealth? Can I discover any relics of the cooperative instinct among such men? Could I get managers and organisers to take a group of poor men seriously or to serve them as they would serve rich men? Is not the whole psychology of a capitalist society divided between the proletarian mass which thinks in terms not of property but of “employment,” and the few owners who are alone familiar with the machinery of administration?
I have touched but very briefly and superficially upon this matter, because it needs no elaboration. Though it is evident that with a sufficient will and a sufficient social vitality property could be restored, it is evident that all efforts to restore it have in a capitalist society such as our own a note of oddity, of doubtful experiment, of being uncoordinated with other social things around them, which marks the heavy handicap under which any such attempt must proceed. It is like recommending elasticity to the aged.
On the other hand, the collectivist experiment is thoroughly suited (in appearance at least) to the capitalist society which it proposes to replace. It works with the existing machinery of capitalism, talks and thinks in the existing terms of capitalism, appeals to just those appetites which capitalism has aroused, and ridicules as fantastic and unheard-of just those things in society the memory of which capitalism has killed among men wherever the blight of it has spread.
So true is all this that the stupider kind of collectivist will often talk of a “capitalist phase” of society as the necessary precedent to a “collectivist phase.” A trust or monopoly is welcomed because it “furnishes a mode of transition from private to public ownership.” Collectivism promises employment to the great mass who think of production only in terms of employment. It promises to its workmen the security which a great and well-organised industrial capitalist unit (like one of our railways) can give through a system of pensions, regular promotion, etc.

官员、行政管理者和立法者能否摆脱在资本主义下富人看似习以为常的支配权力？例如，若我走向我们某家大型托拉斯的工场，用公帑将其买下，甚至作为赠礼将其股份分给工人们，我能否指望在他们中间存在任何能够防止其挥霍这笔新财富的财产传统？我能否在这些人中间发现任何合作本能的残存痕迹？我能否让经理人和组织者严肃对待一群穷人，或者像为富人效劳那样为他们服务？资本主义社会的全部心理，难道不是分裂在不以财产而仅以“雇佣”为考量的无产阶级大众、与唯有其自身才熟悉行政运作机制的少数所有者之间吗？
我对这一问题仅做了极简略和浮于表面的触及，因为它无须详加赘述。尽管显而易见的是，若具备充分的意志与充沛的社会活力，财产制度是可以恢复的，但同样显而易见的是：在像我们这样的资本主义社会中，恢复财产的一切努力都带着一种古怪离奇、前途未卜的试验意味，带着与周遭其他社会事物格格不入的脱节感，这构成了任何此类尝试在推行中所背负的沉重掣肘。这犹如建议耄耋老者去追求身轻如燕。
另一方面，集体主义试验与它提议取代的资本主义社会则彻底契合（至少在表面上如此）。它利用资本主义的现有机制运作，以资本主义的现有话语进行言说和思考，迎合的正是资本主义所激起的那些欲望；而且，凡是资本主义的毒害所蔓延之处，它便对资本主义早已在人们心中扼杀其记忆的那些社会事物，冷嘲热讽为异想天开与闻所未闻。
这一切是如此真确，以至于较为愚钝的那类集体主义者经常将社会的“资本主义阶段”称作“集体主义阶段”的必然先决条件。托拉斯或垄断组织受到欢迎，乃是因为它“提供了一种从私人所有制向公共所有制过渡的形式”。集体主义向仅从雇佣角度看待生产的广大群众许诺了就业。它向其工人们许诺了一座庞大且组织优良的工业资本主义单位（如我们的某家铁路公司）通过退休金制度、定期晋升等所能提供的那种安全保障。

, but that security vastly increased through the fact that it is the state and not a mere unit of the state which guarantees it. Collectivism would administer, would pay wages, would promote, would pension off, would fine﻿—and all the rest of it﻿—exactly as the capitalist state does today. The proletarian, when the collectivist (or socialist) state is put before him, perceives nothing in the picture save certain ameliorations of his present position. Who can imagine that if, say, two of our great industries, coal and railways, were handed over to the state tomorrow, the armies of men organised therein would find any change in the character of their lives, save in some increase of security and possibly in a very slight increase of earnings?
The whole scheme of collectivism presents, so far as the proletarian mass of a capitalist state is concerned, nothing unknown at all, but a promise of some increment in wages and a certainty of far greater ease of mind.
To that small minority of a capitalist society which owns the means of production, collectivism will of course appear as an enemy, but, even so, it is an enemy which they understand and an enemy with whom they can treat in terms common both to that enemy and to themselves. If, for instance, the state proposes to take over such and such a trust now paying 4 percent, and believes that under state management it will make the trust pay 5 percent, then the transference takes the form of a business proposition: the state is no harder to the capitalists taken over than was Mr. Yerkes to the Underground. Again, the state, having greater credit and longevity, can (it would seem)5 “buy out” any existing capitalist body upon favourable terms. There is in the whole scheme which proposes to transform the capitalist into the collectivist state no element of reaction, the use of no term with which a capitalist society is not familiar, the appeal to no instinct, whether of cowardice, greed, apathy, or mechanical regulation, with which a capitalist community is not amply familiar.
In general, if modern capitalist England were made by magic a state of small owners, we should all suffer an enormous revolution.
