## I The Grand Master of His Highness’ Wolf Hounds / I 殿下狼犬总管

===Original===
The Seigneur Jean, Baron of Vez, was a hardy and indefatigable sportsman.

If you follow the beautiful valley which runs between Berval and Longpré, you will see, on your left hand, an old tower, which by reason of its isolated position will appear doubly high and formidable to you.

At the present moment it belongs to an old friend of the writer of this tale, and everyone is now so accustomed to its forbidding aspect, that the peasant passing that way in summer has no more fear of seeking shelter from the heat beneath its walls than the martins with their long black wings and shrill cries, and the swallows with their soft chirrupings, have of building their nests under its eaves.

===Chinese===
让领主（the Seigneur Jean），韦兹（Vez）的男爵，是个彪悍刚健、不知疲倦的猎人。

你若沿着贝尔瓦尔（Berval）与龙普雷（Longpré）之间那条秀美的山谷走去，便会在左手边望见一座古塔；只因它孤零零地立在那儿，便显得格外高耸、格外森然可怖。

如今这座塔已归本书作者的一位老友所有；人们也早已看惯了它那拒人千里的模样，以至于夏日里路过的庄稼汉，全不怕在塔墙的阴影下躲避暑热——正如那些拖着长长黑翼、叫声尖锐的岩燕，和那些啁啾呢喃的家燕，全不怕在它的檐下衔泥筑巢一般。

===Original===
But at the time we are now speaking of, somewhere about 1780, this lordly dwelling of Vez was looked upon with different eyes, and, it must be confessed, it did not then offer so safe a place of retreat. It was a building of the twelfth or thirteenth century, rugged and gloomy, its terrifying exterior having assumed no kindlier aspect as the years rolled by. True, the sentinel with his measured tread and flashing steel-cap no longer paced its ramparts, the archer with his shrill-sounding horn no longer kept watch and ward on the battlements; true the postern was no longer guarded by true men at arms, ready at the least signal of danger to lower the portcullis and draw up the bridge; but the solitude alone which surrounded this grim giant of granite was sufficient to inspire the feeling of awe-inspiring majesty awakened by all mute and motionless things.

===Chinese===
然而在我们所讲的那个年月，大约 1780 年光景，人们看这座韦兹的领主宅邸，眼光却大不相同；也不得不承认，那时它绝非一个安稳可靠的避难之所。那是十二、十三世纪遗下来的建筑，粗粝阴沉，岁月流转，它那骇人的外貌不曾变得和善半分。诚然，那踏着匀步、钢盔闪亮的哨兵已不再在城头巡行；那吹着尖厉号角的弓弩手也不再在雉堞间守望；诚然，那角门已不再由忠诚的兵士把守，一有警报便降下铁闸、拉起吊桥；然而单是环绕着这位花岗岩巨人的那一片荒寂，便足以唤起一切沉默不动之物所唤起的那种令人敬畏的庄严之感。

===Original===
The lord of this old fortress, however, was by no means so much to be dreaded; those who were more intimately acquainted with him than were the peasants, and could do him more justice, asserted that his bark was worse than his bite, and that he caused more fear than harm—that is, among his fellow Christians. With the animals of the forest it was different, for he was avowedly their mortal and implacable enemy.

He was chief wolf-hunter to his Royal Highness Louis Philippe of Orleans, the fourth of that name—a post which allowed him to gratify the inordinate passion he had for the chase. Although it was not easy, it was yet possible to bring the Baron to listen to reason in other matters; but as regards the chase, if once he had got a fixed idea in his head, nothing would satisfy him until he had carried it out and had achieved his purpose.

===Chinese===
然而这古堡的主人，倒也并不怎么叫人害怕；那些比乡下庄稼汉更与他相熟、也更肯公道待他的人都说，他是雷声大雨点小，叫人怕得厉害，害人却有限——至少对他的基督徒同类是如此。至于林中的走兽，情形便不同了，因为他摆明就是它们不共戴天、死不休解的仇敌。

他是奥尔良公爵路易·菲利普（Louis Philippe of Orleans）殿下——同名的第四位——的首席猎狼官；这个职位正好遂了他那股子难以遏制的狩猎狂热。在别的事情上，虽说不易，到底还能叫这位男爵（the Baron）听进几句道理；唯独论到狩猎，他脑子里一旦钻进一个主意，便非得照办、非得遂愿不可，不然什么事都堵不住他的心。

===Original===
His wife, according to report, was the natural daughter of the Prince, which, in conjunction with his title of chief wolf-hunter, gave him almost absolute power throughout the domains of his illustrious father-in-law, a power which no one dared to contest with him, especially after the remarriage of his Royal Highness with Madame de Montesson. This had taken place in 1773, since which date he had almost abandoned his castle at Villers-Cotterets for his delightful residence at Bagnolet, where he entertained all the first wits of the day and amused himself with playacting.

And so, whether the sun was shining to rejoice the earth, or the rain was saddening it, whether the winter fields lay hidden beneath a shroud of snow, or the spring had spread her fresh green carpet over the meadows, it was rare, on any day of the year, not to see the great gates of the Castle thrown wide open between eight and nine o’clock in the morning, and first the Baron come forth, and immediately after him his chief pricker, Marcotte, followed by the other prickers. Then appeared the dogs, coupled and held in leash by the keepers of the hounds, under the superintendence of Engoulevent, who aspired to become a pricker. Even as the German executioner walks alone, behind the nobles and in front of the citizens, to show that he is the least of the former and the first of the latter, so he walked immediately after the prickers and ahead of the keepers of the hounds, as being the chief of the whippers-in and least of the prickers.

===Chinese===
据说，他的妻子是亲王的私生女；这重身份，再加上他那首席猎狼官的头衔，便使他在那位显赫岳丈的领地之内，几乎握有绝对的权柄——这权柄无人敢与他相争，尤其是在殿下与蒙特森夫人（Madame de Montesson）再婚之后。这桩婚事成于 1773 年；自那以后，他几乎把维莱-科特雷（Villers-Cotterets）的城堡弃置一旁，搬进了巴尼奥莱（Bagnolet）那座宜人的别业，在那里招待当时第一等的才子名士，又以登台串戏自娱。

因此，无论日光欢腾着照临大地，还是雨水使它黯然神伤，无论冬日的田野在白雪的尸衣下隐没，还是春神在草甸上铺开她那嫩绿的新毯，一年之中鲜有哪一天，你不见城堡的大门在八点到九点之间豁然大开——先是男爵本人骑马而出，紧跟在他身后的，是他的首席猎手马尔科特（Marcotte），其余猎手再鱼贯跟上。随后出现的是猎犬，由犬奴们两两拴着、牵住皮带，归昂古勒旺（Engoulevent）统领——此人正巴望着有朝一日也能当上一名猎手。正如日耳曼的刽子手独自走在贵族之后、百姓之前，以表明他既是前者的末流、又是后者的首位；他也这般走在猎手们之后、犬奴们之前，因为他是犬奴的头儿，又是猎手里的末流。

===Original===
The whole procession filed out of the castle court in full hunting array, with the English horses and the French hounds; twelve horses, and forty dogs.

Before we go any farther, let me say that with these twelve horses and forty dogs the Baron hunted every sort of quarry, but more especially the wolf, in order no doubt to do honour to his title.

===Chinese===
整队人马这般全副狩猎行头，从城堡的庭院里鱼贯而出——英国的马，法国的犬；十二匹马，四十条狗。

在往下细讲之前，且容我说一句：男爵就凭着这十二匹马、四十条狗，猎取各种各样的猎物，而尤以猎狼为甚——这无疑是为了不辱他那猎狼官的头衔。

===Original===
No further proof will be needed by the genuine sportsman of the fine faith he had in the general quality of his hounds, and in their keenness of scent, than the fact that next to the wolf he gave preference to the boar, then to the red deer, then to the fallow-deer, and lastly to the roebuck; finally, if the keepers of the pack failed to sight the animal they had tracked, he uncoupled at random, and went after the first hare that crossed his path. For, as we have already stated, the worthy Baron went out hunting every day, and he would sooner have gone for four-and-twenty hours without food or drink, although he was often thirsty, than have spent that time without seeing his hounds run.

===Chinese===
真正的猎人，只消知道下面这一层，便不必再要什么旁的证据，来印证这位男爵对自己犬群的整体品性、对它们嗅觉之敏锐怀着怎样坚定的信心：那就是——除了狼之外，他猎物的偏好次序，先是野猪，再是赤鹿，其次是扁角鹿，最后才是狍子；到最后，倘若管犬的人没能在追踪中瞧见那畜生，他便随手放开几条犬，去追第一只撞进他路上的野兔。因为，如前所述，这位好男爵天天都要出猎，他宁可整整二十四小时不吃不喝——他本是常常口渴的——也不愿这么长一段时光里，看不见他那群猎犬奔跑。

===Original===
But, as everybody knows, however swift the horses, and however keen the dogs, hunting has its bad times as well as its good.

One day, Marcotte came up to where the Baron was awaiting him, with a crestfallen expression of countenance.

“How now, Marcotte,” asked the Baron frowning, “what is the matter this time? I see by your face we are to expect bad sport today.”

Marcotte shook his head.

“Speak up, man,” continued the Baron with a gesture of impatience.

===Chinese===
然而众所周知，无论马跑得多快，无论犬嗅觉多灵，打猎这桩事，也总有走运和不走运的日子。

有一天，马尔科特走到男爵候着他的地方，一脸垂头丧气。

“怎么啦，马尔科特，”男爵皱着眉头问，“这回又出了什么岔子？我看你这副脸色，今天怕是要扫兴了。”

马尔科特摇了摇头。

“你倒是说话呀，伙计，”男爵带着不耐烦的手势，接着道。

===Original===
“The matter is, my Lord, that the black wolf is about.”

“Ah! ah!” exclaimed the Baron, his eyes sparkling; for you must know that this made the fifth or sixth time that the worthy Baron had started the animal in question, but never once had he been able to get within gunshot of him or to run him down.

“Yes,” Marcotte went on, “but the damned beast has employed himself so well all night crossing his track and doubling, that after having traced him over half the forest, I found myself at the place from which I started.”

“You think then, Marcotte, that there is no chance of getting near him.”

“I am afraid not.”

===Chinese===
“回大人，是这么回事：那头黑狼出动了。”

“啊！啊！”男爵叫出声来，两眼直放光；要知道，这已是这位好男爵第五、第六回惊起这头野兽了，可一次也不曾能够逼近到枪程之内，更不曾把它追得力竭就擒。

“是，”马尔科特接着说，“可这该死的畜生，一整夜把心思都用在了来回穿插、兜圈子折返上头，结果我循着它的踪迹追了大半个林子，到头来又回到了动身的地方。”

“那么照你想，马尔科特，这一回是没指望挨近它了。”

“怕是没指望了。”

===Original===
“By all the devils in hell!” exclaimed the Lord of Vez, who had not had his equal in swearing since the mighty Nimrod, “however, I am not feeling well today, and I must have a burst of some kind, to get rid of these bad humours. What do you think we can hunt, Marcotte, in place of this damned black wolf?”

“Well, having been so taken up with the wolf,” answered Marcotte, “I have not traced any other animal. Will my Lord uncouple at random and hunt the first animal that we come across?”

===Chinese===
“凭地狱里所有的魔鬼发誓！”韦兹领主（the Lord of Vez）嚷道——自有伟大的猎户宁录（Nimrod）以来，论起赌咒发誓，再没人比得上他——“不过，我今天身子不爽，总得痛痛快快地奔上一场，把这些邪火发散出去才好。你说咱们能猎点什么，马尔科特，来顶替这头该死的黑狼？”

“这个么，既然一心都扑在这头狼身上了，”马尔科特答道，“旁的畜生我一概不曾循迹追过。大人可愿随意放几条犬，撞见什么就猎什么？”

===Original===
The Baron was about to express his willingness to agree to this proposal when he caught sight of little Engoulevent coming towards them cap in hand.

“Wait a moment,” he said, “here comes Engoulevent, who, I fancy, has some advice to give us.”

“I have no advice to give to a noble Lord like yourself,” replied Engoulevent, assuming an expression of humility on his sly and crafty face; “it is, however, my duty to inform you that there is a splendid buck in the neighbourhood.”

“Let us see your buck, Engoulevent,” replied the chief wolf-hunter, “and if you are not mistaken about it, there will be a new crown for you.”

“Where is this buck of yours?” asked Marcotte, “but look to your skin, if you make us uncouple to no purpose.”

===Chinese===
男爵正要满口答应这个主意，却一眼瞧见小个子昂古勒旺摘着帽子，朝他们走来。

“且等一等，”他说，“昂古勒旺来了，我瞧他像是有话要向我们进言。”

“像大人这般高贵的领主，小人哪敢有什么进言，”昂古勒旺答道，在那张刁滑狡黠的脸上堆出一副谦卑的神情，“不过，小人有责任禀报大人：邻近有一头极出色的雄鹿。”

“领我们去瞧瞧你那头雄鹿吧，昂古勒旺，”这位首席猎狼官答道，“若是你没看走眼，这儿有一枚崭新的埃居（crown）赏你。”

“你这头雄鹿在哪儿？”马尔科特问，“不过你可得当心你的皮，要是害我们白放了一回犬。”

===Original===
“Let me have Matador and Jupiter, and then we shall see.” Matador and Jupiter were the finest among the hounds belonging to the Lord of Vez. And indeed, Engoulevent had not gone a hundred paces with them through the thicket, before, by the lashing of their tails, and their repeated yelping, he knew that they were on the right scent. In another minute or two a magnificent ten-tined stag came into view. Marcotte cried Tally-ho, sounded his horn, and the hunt began, to the great satisfaction of the Lord of Vez, who, although regretting the black wolf, was willing to make the best of a fine buck in its stead. The hunt had lasted two hours, and the quarry still held on. It had first led its pursuers from the little wood of Haramont to the Chemin du Pendu, and thence straight to the back of Oigny, and it still showed no sign of fatigue; for it was not one of those poor animals of the flat country who get their tails pulled by every wretched terrier.

===Chinese===
“把斗牛士（Matador）和朱庇特（Jupiter）给我，咱们便见分晓。”斗牛士和朱庇特，是韦兹领主那一群猎犬里最出色的两条。果然，昂古勒旺牵着它们在矮树林里还没走上百步，凭它们那不住甩动的尾巴和一声紧似一声的吠叫，他便知道嗅迹已上了正路。不出一两分钟，一头壮美的十杈雄鹿便现出身来。马尔科特一声“发现猎物”的吆喝，又吹响号角，围猎就此开场——韦兹领主好不称心，他虽惋惜那头黑狼，却也乐得拿一头顶刮的雄鹿来将就。这场围猎持续了整整两个钟头，猎物仍在苦苦支撑。它先把追兵从阿拉蒙（Haramont）那片小树林引到“吊死人路”（Chemin du Pendu），又从那里径直奔到瓦尼（Oigny）的背后，却依然不见半点疲乏的迹象；因为它可不是平原上那种任什么蹩脚小犬都能扯住尾巴的可怜畜生。

===Original===
As it neared the low grounds of Bourg-fontaine, however, it evidently decided that it was being run rather hard, for it gave up the bolder measures which had hitherto enabled it to keep ahead, and began to double.

Its first manoeuvre was to go down to the brook which joins the ponds of Baisemont and Bourg, then to walk against stream with the water up to its haunches, for nearly half a mile; it then sprang on to the right bank, back again into the bed of the stream, made another leap to the left, and with a succession of bounds, as vigorous as its failing strength allowed, continued to outdistance its pursuers. But the dogs of my lord Baron were not animals to be put out by such trifles as these. Being both sagacious and well-bred, they, of their own accord, divided the task between themselves, half going up stream, and half down, these hunting on the right those on the left, and so effectually that they ere long put the animal off its changes, for they soon recovered the scent, rallying at the first cry given by one of the pack, and starting afresh on the chase, as ready and eager as if the deer had been only twenty paces in front of them.

===Chinese===
然而它一挨近布尔丰泉（Bourg-fontaine）那片洼地，显然便觉出自己被追得紧了，于是放弃了先前那套使它一直领先的凌厉路数，开始兜起圈子来。

它的头一个花招，是下到那条连通贝斯蒙（Baisemont）池塘与布尔（Bourg）池塘的小溪里，逆流涉水而行，水没到大腿根，走了将近半英里；接着它猛地纵上右岸，又折回河床，再一跃跳到左岸，趁着尚存的气力，一连几纵，竭力甩开追兵。然而男爵大人的犬可不是这般区区小计便能难倒的。它们既聪明又有良种，自行分了工：一半逆流而上，一半顺流而下，两岸彼此呼应搜寻，如此奏效，不多时便搅乱了那畜生的连环诡计——因为它们很快重新嗅到了气味，一听犬群里哪一条开了腔便齐声呼应，重又追了上去，那般精神抖擞、急不可耐，活像那头鹿不过只跑在它们前面二十步开外似的。

===Original===
And so with galloping of horses, with cry of hounds and blare of horn, the Baron and his huntsmen reached the ponds of Saint Antoine, a hundred paces or so from the Confines of Oigny. Between these and the Osier-beds stood the hut of Thibault, the sabot-maker.

We must pause to give some description of this Thibault, the shoemaker, the real hero of the tale.

You will ask why I, who have summoned kings to appear upon the stage, who have obliged princes, dukes, and barons to play secondary parts in my romances, should take a simple shoemaker for the hero of this tale.

===Chinese===
就这样，马蹄翻飞，犬声鼎沸，号角嘹亮，男爵带着他那一众猎手，奔到了圣安托万（Saint Antoine）的几口池塘边——离瓦尼的边界不过百步之遥。在池塘与柳条丛之间，立着木屐匠蒂博（Thibault）的小屋。

这里我们得停一停，把这蒂博——这个鞋匠、本书真正的主角——略作一番描摹。

诸位定要发问：我这个人，曾把一国之君召上舞台，曾逼着亲王、公爵、男爵在我的小说里充任配角，何以这一回，却挑了个区区鞋匠来当主角？

===Original===
First, I will reply by saying that, in my dear home country of Villers-Cotterets, there are more sabot-makers than barons, dukes and princes, and that, as soon as I decided to make the forest the scene of the events I am about to record, I was obliged to choose one of the actual inhabitants of this forest as hero, unless I had wished to represent such fantastic persons as the *Incas* of Marmontel or the *Abencerrages* of M. de Florian.

More than that, it is not the author who decides on the subject, but the subject which takes possession of the author, and, good or bad, this particular subject has taken possession of me. I will therefore endeavour to draw Thibault’s portrait for you, plain shoemaker as he was, as exactly as the artist paints the portrait which a prince desires to send to his ladylove.

===Chinese===
头一句，我要这样回答：在我那可爱的故乡维莱-科特雷，木屐匠比男爵、公爵、亲王加起来还多；而既然我已打定主意，要把即将记述的种种事件安排在林子里发生，我便不得不从这片森林真正的居民里挑出一个来当主角——除非我情愿去描摹马尔蒙泰尔笔下那种《印加人》（*Incas*）、或是弗洛里安先生笔下那种《阿本塞拉赫人》（*Abencerrages*）那样子虚乌有的人物。

更有甚者——并不是作者来择定题材，倒是题材攫住了作者；而这一回的题材，无论好坏，已把我也攫住了。所以我将尽力为诸位描一幅蒂博的肖像：纵使他不过是个寻常鞋匠，我也要像那位画师为亲王绘下要送给意中人的肖像一般，把他画得分毫不差。

===Original===
Thibault was a man between twenty-five and twenty-seven years of age, tall, well made, physically robust, but by nature melancholy and sad of heart. This depression of spirits arose from a little grain of envy, which, in spite of himself, perhaps unconsciously to himself, he harboured towards all such of his neighbours as had been more favoured by fortune than himself.

His father had committed a fault, a serious one at all times, but more especially in those days of absolutism, when a man was not able to rise above his station as nowadays, when with sufficient capacity he may attain to any rank. Thibault had been educated above his position; he had been at school under the Abbé Fortier, at Villers-Cotterets, and had learnt to read, write, and cipher; moreover he knew a little Latin, which made him inordinately proud of himself. Thibault had spent a great part of his time in reading, and his books had been chiefly those which were in vogue at the close of the preceding century. But he had not been a sufficiently clever analyst to know how to separate the good from the bad, or rather he *had* separated what was bad, and swallowed it in large doses, leaving the good to precipitate itself at the bottom of the glass.

蒂博年约二十五到二十七岁之间，身量高挑，体格匀称，筋骨强健，天性却忧郁、心头沉郁。这股子郁郁寡欢，源自他心底里那一星妒意：他对所有命途比自己顺遂的邻里，都不由自主地——或许连他自己也不曾察觉地——怀着一丝嫉恨。

他的父亲犯下过一桩过错——一桩无论何时都称得上严重的过错，而在那个专制的年代尤为致命；那时一个人不能像今日这般逾越自己的本分往上爬，今日只要有足够的才干，便可平步青云，位列任何品级。蒂博受的教育高过了他的出身：他曾在维莱-科特雷的福尔蒂埃神父（the Abbé Fortier）门下求学，学会了读、写、算；此外还懂一点拉丁文，这点学问叫他对自己无比自负。蒂博把大把的光阴都花在了读书上，他所读的，又多半是上世纪末风行一时的那一类书。然而他算不得什么高明的鉴赏家，分不清书中精华与糟粕；不如说，他恰恰是把糟粕挑拣了出来，大口大口吞下肚去，倒让精华沉淀在了杯底。

===Original===
At twenty years of age Thibault had certainly had dreams of being something other than a sabot-maker. He had, for instance, for a very little while, cast his eyes towards the army. But his comrades who had worn the double livery of king and country, had left the service as they entered it, mere soldiers of the ranks, having failed during five or six years of slavery to obtain promotion, even to the not very exalted grade of corporal.

Thibault had also thought of becoming a sailor. But a career in the navy was as much forbidden to the plebeian as one in the army. Possibly after enduring danger, and storm and battle for fifteen or twenty years, he might be made a boatswain’s mate, that was all, and then! besides, it was by no means Thibault’s ambition to wear a short vest and sailcloth trousers, but the blue uniform of the king with red vest and gold epaulettes. He had moreover known of no single case in which the son of a mere shoemaker had become Master of a Frigate, or even Lieutenant. So he was forced to give up all idea of joining the King’s Navy.

===Chinese===
二十岁那年，蒂博无疑做过几场梦，梦见自己总该不至于一辈子做个木屐匠。比方说，曾有那么短短一阵子，他把目光投向过军旅。然而他那些穿过“国王与祖国”那身双重号衣的同伴，进营时是大头兵，出来时依旧是大头兵：五六年苦熬下来，连个算不上多么显赫的班长都没捞上。

蒂博也曾想过当个水手。可是海军这条路，和陆军一样，对平民紧闭着大门。或许熬过十五二十年的风涛险阻、枪林弹雨，他能挣上个水手长副手，也就到头了——再说！况且，蒂博心心念念想穿的，可不是短马甲和帆布裤，而是镶红马甲金肩章的国王蓝军服。况且他也从未听说过，哪位区区鞋匠的儿子当上了巡防舰的舰长，哪怕只是个海军上尉。于是他只得打消了投奔王家海军的念头。

Thibault would not have minded being a Notary, and at one time thought of apprenticing himself to the Royal Scrivener, Maître Niquet, as a stepping-stone, and of making his way up on the strength of his own legs and with the help of his pen. But supposing him to have risen to the position of head clerk with a salary of a hundred crowns, where was he to find the thirty thousand francs which would be required for the purchase of the smallest village practice.

There was, therefore, no better chance of his becoming a scrivener than of becoming an officer on sea or land. Meanwhile, Thibault’s father died, leaving very little ready money. There was about enough to bury him, so he was buried, and this done, there remained some thirty or forty francs over for Thibault.

===Chinese===
当个公证人，蒂博倒也不嫌弃；有一阵子，他想过去给王家文书尼凯师傅（Maître Niquet）当学徒，以此为敲门砖，再凭自己的双腿和手中那管笔，一步步往上爬。可就算他当上了首席书记，拿着一百埃居的年俸，他又上哪儿去凑那三万法郎——那可是买下哪怕最小的乡村公证处所必不可少的数目。

可见他当上文书的指望，并不比当上海陆两军的军官更大。就在这当口，蒂博的父亲撒手人寰，留下的现钱少得可怜。钱刚够殡殓他，于是便把他殡殓了；办完这桩事，蒂博手里还剩下三四十个法郎。

===Original===
Thibault knew his trade well; indeed, he was a first-rate workman; but he had no inclination to handle either auger or parer. It ended, therefore, by his leaving all his father’s tools in the care of a friend, a remnant of prudence still remaining to him, and selling every vestige of furniture; having thus realised a sum of five hundred and forty livres, he determined to make what was then called the tour of France.

Thibault spent three years in travelling; he did not make his fortune during that time, but he learnt a great many things in the course of his journey of which he was previously ignorant, and acquired certain accomplishments which he had previously been without.

He learned amongst other things that, although it was as well to keep one’s word on matters of business with a man, it was no use whatever keeping love vows made to a woman.

===Chinese===
蒂博手艺精熟，实打实是一等一的匠人；可他全无心思再去摸那钻子或刨刀。于是乎，他把父亲的全副工具都寄存在一个朋友处——这是他仅存的一点谨慎——又把每一件家具都变卖净尽；这样凑足了一笔五百四十里弗（livres）的款子，便决意去作当时所谓的“环法之行”（the tour of France）。

蒂博在外游历了三年；三年里他并未发财，却在旅途中学会了许许多多从前一窍不通的事，也添了几样本事，是先前所没有的。

比方说，他从中学到：和男人在生意上打交道，说话固然要算话；可对女人许下的情誓，守着却全无用处。

===Original===
So much for his character and habits of mind. As to his external accomplishments, he could dance a jig beautifully, could hold his own at quarterstaff against four men, and could handle the boar-spear as cleverly as the best huntsman going. All these things had not a little served to increase Thibault’s natural self-esteem, and, seeing himself handsomer, stronger, and cleverer than many of the nobles, he would exclaim against Providence, crying, “Why was I not nobly born? why was not that nobleman yonder born a peasant?”

===Chinese===
以上便是他的性情与心思。至于他外在的本事：他能把号笛舞跳得极漂亮，抡起短棍（quarterstaff）能独敌四人，执起野猪矛，其身手不让当世最好的猎手。这些种种，都大大助长了蒂博与生俱来的自命不凡；他见自己比许多贵族都更俊美、更强壮、更机巧，便不禁怨起老天来，嚷道：“我何以没生在高门？那边那位老爷，又何以没生在农家？”

===Original===
But as Providence took care not to make any answer to these *apostrophés*, and as Thibault found that dancing, playing at quarterstaff, and throwing the boar-spear only fatigued the body, without procuring him any material advantage, he began to turn his thoughts towards his ancient trade, humble though it was, saying to himself, if it enabled the father to live, it would also enable the son. So Thibault went and fetched away his tools; and then, tools in hand, he went to ask permission of the Steward of his Royal Highness Louis Philippe of Orleans, to build a hut in the forest, in which to carry on his trade. He had no difficulty in obtaining this, for the steward knew by experience that his master was a very kindhearted man, expending as much as two hundred and forty thousand francs a year on the poor; he felt sure, therefore, that one who gave away a sum like this, would be willing to let an honest workman who wished to ply his trade, have thirty or forty feet of ground.

As he had leave to establish himself in whatever part of the forest he liked best, Thibault chose the spot near the osier-beds, where the roads crossed, one of the most beautiful parts of the woods, less than a mile from Oigny and about three times that distance from Villers-Cotterets. The shoemaker put up his workshop, built partly of old planks given him by M. Panisis, who had been having a sale in the neighbourhood, and partly of the branches which the steward gave him leave to cut in the forest.

===Chinese===
然而老天爷对这些 *apostrophés*（呼告之词）一向不肯作答；蒂博也渐渐发觉，跳舞、抡短棍、掷野猪矛，只会徒然累乏了筋骨，却带不来半点实在的好处。于是他重新把心思转向了那门古老的行当——纵然卑微，心想：这门手艺既养活了父亲，也就能养活儿子。蒂博便去取回了工具；随后工具在手，便去求奥尔良公爵路易·菲利普殿下的管家恩准，许他在林子里搭一间小屋，好操持他的营生。这事他办得毫不费力，因为那位管家凭经验深知自己的主人为人心善，每年施与穷人的款项多达二十四万法郎；他因而笃信，一个肯如此慷慨解囊的人，断不会不肯让一个诚实的匠人——一个只想安安分分做活的人——占用三四十英尺见方的一块地皮。

既然准他在林子里任择一处最中意的地方安身，蒂博便挑中了柳条丛附近、几条路交汇的那块地——那是整片林子里最美的一隅，离瓦尼不到一英里，离维莱-科特雷大约是这个距离的三倍。鞋匠搭起了他的工棚，材料一部分是帕尼齐先生（M. Panisis）给他的旧木板——那位先生恰在邻近办一场拍卖——另一部分则是管家准他从林子里砍来的树枝。

===Original===
When the building of the hut, which consisted of a bedroom, cosily shut in, where he could work during the winter, and of a lean-to, open to the air, where he could work in the summer, was completed, Thibault began to think of making himself a bed. At first, a layer of fern had to serve for this purpose; but after he had made a hundred pairs of wooden shoes and had sold these to Bedeau, who kept a general shop at Villers-Cotterets, he was able to pay a sufficient deposit to get a mattress, to be paid for in full by the end of three months. The framework of the bed was not difficult to make; Thibault was not the shoemaker he was without being a bit of a carpenter into the bargain, and when this was finished he plaited osiers to take the place of sacking, laid the mattress upon them, and found himself at last with a bed to lie upon.

===Chinese===
小屋落了成：里头是一间卧房，严严实实地封拢，可供他冬天在里面做活；外头搭一个披屋，四面通风，可供他夏天作业。屋成之后，蒂博便开始琢磨给自己置一张床。起初，他只好铺一层蕨草将就。可等他打出一百双木鞋、卖给了在维莱-科特雷开杂货铺的贝多（Bedeau）之后，他便能付下一笔定金，赊来一张床垫，言明三个月内付清全款。床的框架倒不难做：蒂博既是这般手艺的鞋匠，自然也兼带几分木匠的本事；框架一成，他便编了柳条来替代网垫，把床垫铺在上头，临了总算有一张床可躺了。

===Original===
Little by little came the sheets, and then in their turn the coverlids; the next purchase was a chafing-dish, and earthenware pots to cook in, and finally some plates and dishes. Before the year was out Thibault had also made additions to his furniture of a fine oak chest and a fine walnut-wood cupboard, both, like the bed, his own handiwork. All the while he was driving a brisk trade, for none could beat Thibault in turning a block of beech into a pair of shoes, and in converting the odd chips into spoons, saltcellars and natty little bowls.

He had now been settled in his workshop for three years, that is, ever since his return after the completion of his tour round France, and there was nothing for which anyone could have reproached him during this interval except the failing we have already mentioned—that he was rather more envious of the good fortune of his neighbour than was altogether conducive to the welfare of his soul. But this feeling was as yet so inoffensive, that his confessor had no need to do more than awaken in him a sense of shame for harbouring thoughts which had, so far, not resulted in any active crime.

他在工棚里安顿下来，至今已是第三个年头——也就是说，自他环法之行结束后回来便住到了这里。这段日子里，旁人无可指责他什么，除了我们已提到过的那一桩短处：他对邻人的好福气，嫉妒得未免重了些，重到了于他灵魂的安宁颇有妨碍的地步。好在这一点心思至今仍近乎无害，他的听告解神父也只需在他心里唤起一丝羞愧——为那些迄今尚未酿成任何实际罪行的念头而羞愧——便已足够。
