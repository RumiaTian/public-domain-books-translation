## X Following the Tanks Into Battle / 十、跟着坦克上战场

===Original===
The tanks passed beyond us and halfway up to the first line and stopped. Trapdoors in the decks opened, and the crews poured out and began to pile sandbags in front of the machines so that when day broke fully and the mists lifted, the enemy could not see what had been brought up in the night.

Day dawned, and a frisky little breeze from the west scattered the fog and swept the sky clean. There wasn’t a cloud by eight o’clock. The sun shone bright, and we cursed it, for if it had been rainy the attack would not have been made.

We made the usual last preparations that morning, such as writing letters and delivering farewell messages; and the latest rooks made their wills in the little blanks provided for the purpose in the back of the pay books. We judged from the number of dead and the evident punishment other divisions had taken there that the chances of coming back would be slim. Around nine o’clock Captain Green gave us a little talk that confirmed our suspicions that the day was to be a hard one.

===Chinese===
坦克越过我们，朝第一线战壕开到半途便停了下来。顶甲上的舱门打开，乘员们涌出来，动手在车身前头堆起沙袋——这样一来，等天色大亮、雾气散尽，敌人就看不出夜里往前沿运上来的是什么家伙了。

天亮了，一股活泼的小西风把雾气扯散，把天空扫得干干净净。到八点钟，天上连一丝云也没有。太阳明晃晃地照着，我们直骂它——要是赶上下雨，这一仗压根就打不起来。

那天早上，我们照例做了最后一番准备：写家信，托付诀别的话；最新补进来的新兵蛋子（rooks，对新兵的谑称），则在薪饷手册背面专设的小空栏里写下了遗嘱。从尸首的数目和别的师在此地显然吃过的苦头判断，我们估摸着，活着回去的指望相当渺茫。九点钟前后，格林上尉（Captain Green）给我们讲了短短一席话，坐实了我们心头的疑团：这一天，怕是场硬仗。

===Original===
He said, as nearly as I can remember:

“Lads, I want to tell you that there is to be a most important battle﻿—one of the most important in the whole war. High Wood out there commands a view of the whole of this part of the Somme and is most valuable. There are estimated to be about ten thousand Germans in that wood and in the surrounding supports. The positions are mostly of concrete with hundreds of machine guns and field artillery. Our heavies have for some reason made no impression on them, and regiment after regiment has attempted to take the woods and failed with heavy losses. Now it is up to the 47th Division to do the seemingly impossible. Zero is at eleven. We go over then. The best of luck and God bless you.”

We were all feeling pretty sour on the world when the sky pilot came along and cheered us up.

===Chinese===
凭记忆，他大体是这么说的：

“小伙子们，我要告诉你们，今天要打一场极其重要的战役——整个大战里数得着的重要一仗。外头的海伍德（High Wood）居高临下，俯瞰索姆河（Somme）这一带的整个地面，价值极大。据估计，那片树林和周围支援阵地里的德国兵约有上万人，工事大半是混凝土浇筑的，配着几百挺机枪和野战炮。不知为什么，我们的重炮在它们面前毫无建树，一团又一团的部队试图夺下这片树林，都在惨重损失下败下阵来。如今，轮到第47师来干这桩看似不可能的事了。零时定在十一点。到时候我们就翻出壕顶冲锋。祝大伙儿走运，愿上帝保佑你们。”

我们个个正对这世界一肚子怨气，“天国领航员”（sky pilot，大兵对随军牧师的谑称）来了，给我们打气鼓劲。

===Original===
He was a good little man, that chaplain, brave as they make ’em. He always went over the top with us and was in the thick of the fighting, and he had the military cross for bravery. He passed down the line, giving us a slap on the back or a hand grip and started us singing. No gospel hymns either, but any old rollicking, good-natured song that he happened to think of that would loosen things up and relieve the tension.

Somehow he made you feel that you wouldn’t mind going to hell if he was along, and you knew that he’d be willing to come if he could do any good. A good little man! Peace to his ashes.

At ten o’clock things busted loose, and the most intense bombardment ever known in warfare up to that time began. Thousands of guns, both French and English, in fact every available gun within a radius of fifteen miles, poured it in. In the Bedlamitish din and roar it was impossible to hear the next man unless he put his mouth up close to your ear and yelled.

===Chinese===
那位随军牧师是个好样的小个子，天不怕地不怕。他回回跟我们一道翻出壕顶冲锋，哪里打得最凶哪里就有他，还得过军功十字勋章。他顺着队伍一路走过来，不是在这个背上拍一巴掌，就是握握那个的手，还领着我们唱起歌来。唱的也不是什么福音圣歌，全是随口想起的欢快和气的老段子，就为把气氛弄活络，把那股紧绷劲儿松一松。

不知怎么的，他能让你觉得，只要有他同行，下地狱也甘心；你也知道，只要能帮上一点忙，他保准乐意同去。一个多好的小个子！愿他安息。

十点钟，一切轰然炸开了锅，到那时为止战争史上最猛烈的一场炮击开始了。几千门火炮——法国的、英国的，其实是方圆十五英里内所有拉得出来的炮——一齐猛灌。在那疯人院似的喧嚣轰鸣里，身边的人说什么根本听不见，除非他把嘴贴到你耳朵上吼。

===Original===
My ear drums ached, and I thought I should go insane if the racket didn’t stop. I was frightfully nervous and scared, but tried not to show it. An officer or a noncom must conceal his nervousness, though he be dying with fright.

The faces of the men were hard-set and pale. Some of them looked positively green. They smoked fag after fag, lighting the new ones on the butts.

All through the bombardment Fritz was comparatively quiet. He was saving all his for the time when we should come over. Probably, too, he was holed up to a large extent in his concrete dugouts. I looked over the top once or twice and wondered if I, too, would be lying there unburied with the rats and maggots gnawing me into an unrecognizable mass. There were moments in that hour from ten to eleven when I was distinctly sorry for myself.

The time, strangely enough, went fast﻿—as it probably does with a condemned man in his last hour. At zero minus ten the word went down the line “Ten to go” and we got to the better positions of the trench and secured our footing on the side of the parapet to make our climb over when the signal came. Some of the men gave their bayonets a last fond rub, and I looked to my bolt action to see that it worked well. I had ten rounds in the magazine, and I didn’t intend to rely too much on the bayonet. At a few seconds of eleven I looked at my wrist watch and was afflicted again with that empty feeling in the solar plexus. Then the whistles shrilled; I blew mine, and over we went.

===Chinese===
我的耳膜疼得发慌，心想这动静再不停，我就要疯了。我紧张害怕得要命，却尽力不露形迹。当军官、当军士的，哪怕吓得半死，也得把慌张捂得严严实实。

弟兄们的脸绷得紧紧的，煞白，有几个简直泛出绿色。“香烟”（fags）一支接一支地抽，新的一支都用烟屁股点着。

炮击从头到尾，弗里茨（Fritz，指德国兵）都相当安静。他把自己的家伙什全攒着，单等我们冲过去时一股脑儿招呼上来；多半他还大半猫在混凝土掩蔽部里。我朝壕顶外张望了一两回，心里嘀咕：我是不是也会横在那儿无人收殓，任老鼠和蛆虫把我啃成一团认不出模样的东西。十点到十一点那一小时里，有那么几阵子，我确确实实地顾影自怜。

说来奇怪，时间过得飞快——多半死囚的最后一小时也是如此。零前十分钟，口令顺着战线传下来：“还剩十分钟。”我们占到战壕里位置较好的地段，在胸墙一侧踩稳脚跟，单等信号一出便翻墙而上。有几个弟兄把刺刀最后爱怜地擦了一遍，我则查看枪机，确认它动作顺滑。弹仓里压着十发子弹，我不打算太指望刺刀。差几秒十一点时，我看了看腕表，胃底那股空落落的感觉又袭了上来。随即哨音尖啸而起；我吹响自己的哨子，我们翻出壕顶，冲了上去。

===Original===
To a disinterested spectator who was far enough up in the air to be out of range it must have been a wonderful spectacle to see those thousands of men go over, wave after wave.

The terrain was level out to the point where the little hill of High Wood rose covered with the splintered poles of what had once been a forest. This position and the supports to the left and rear of it began to fairly belch machine-gun and shell fire. If Fritz had been quiet before, he gave us all he had now.

Our battalion went over from the second trench, and we got the cream of it.

The tanks were just ahead of us and lumbered along in an imposing row. They lurched down into deep craters and out again, tipped and reeled and listed, and sometimes seemed as though they must upset; but they came up each time and went on and on. And how slow they did seem to move! Lord, I thought we should never cover that five or six hundred yards.

===Chinese===
要是哪个不相干的看客飞得够高、出了炮火射程，眼看这几千人一波接一波地翻出壕顶，想必会觉得是一场叹为观止的奇观。

地势一路平坦，直到海伍德那座小山包拔地而起——山上盖满劈裂的木桩，那里原是一片树林。这座阵地连同它左翼与后方的各处支援阵地，开始简直像喷发一样往外吐机枪火力和炮火。如果说弗里茨先前还算安静，那此刻他是把压箱底的家当全招呼上来了。

我们营从第二线战壕翻出壕顶，炮火里最浓的那份“精华”全让我们赶上了。

坦克就在我们前头，摆出一排威风凛凛的阵势轰隆碾压。它们一头栽进深深的弹坑，又爬出来，歪歪斜斜、摇摇晃晃，有时眼看非翻不可；可每一回它们都稳住了，继续向前，一个劲儿向前。可它们挪得那个慢哟！老天，我以为那五六百码一辈子也走不完了。

===Original===
The tank machine guns were spitting fire over the heads of our first wave, and their Hotchkiss guns were rattling. A beautiful creeping barrage preceded us. Row after row of shells burst at just the right distance ahead, spewing gobs of smoke and flashes of flame, made thin by the bright sunlight. Half a dozen airplanes circled like dragonflies up there in the blue.

There was a tank just ahead of me. I got behind it. And marched there. Slow! God, how slow! Anyhow, it kept off the machine-gun bullets, but not the shrapnel. It was breaking over us in clouds. I felt the stunning patter of the fragments on my tin hat, cringed under it, and wondered vaguely why it didn’t do me in.

Men in the front wave were going down like tenpins. Off there diagonally to the right and forward I glimpsed a blinding burst, and as much as a whole platoon went down.

Around me men were dropping all the time﻿—men I knew. I saw Dolbsie clawing at his throat as he reeled forward, falling. I saw Vickers double up, drop his rifle, and somersault, hanging on to his abdomen.

===Chinese===
坦克上的机枪越过我们第一波的头顶喷吐火舌，哈奇开斯（Hotchkiss）机枪嗒嗒作响。一道漂亮的徐进弹幕在我们前头开路，一排接一排的炮弹在正前方恰到好处的距离上炸开，吐出一团团烟云、一道道火光，在明晃晃的日光里显得稀薄。半打飞机像蜻蜓一般，在蓝天上悠悠打着旋。

正好有一辆坦克在我前头。我跟在它屁股后面走。慢！天哪，真慢！不管怎么说，机枪子弹叫它挡住了，榴霰弹却挡不住。榴霰弹成团成团地在我们头顶炸开，弹片噼噼啪啪打在钢盔上，震得人发懵；我在盔底下缩着脖子，迷迷糊糊直纳闷：它怎么还没把我要了？

前一波的士兵像保龄球瓶一样接连倒下。右前方斜刺里，我瞥见一道晃眼的炸光，足足一整个排的人都倒了下去。

我身边随时有人倒下——都是我认识的人。我看见多布西（Dolbsie）踉跄着朝前栽倒，双手抠着自己的喉咙；看见维克斯（Vickers）弯下腰去，撂下步枪，整个人翻滚出去，双手死死捂着肚子。

===Original===
A hundred yards away, to the right, an officer walked backwards with an automatic pistol balanced on his finger, smiling, pulling his men along like a drum major. A shell or something hit him. He disappeared in a welter of blood and half a dozen of the front file fell with him.

I thought we must be nearly there and sneaked a look around the edge of the tank. A traversing machine gun raked the mud, throwing up handfuls, and I heard the gruff *row, row* of flattened bullets as they ricocheted off the steel armor. I ducked back, and on we went.

Slow! Slow! I found myself planning what I would do when I got to the front trenches﻿—if we ever did. There would be a grand rumpus, and I would click a dozen or more.

And then we arrived.

===Chinese===
右边一百码开外，一名军官倒退着走，手指上稳稳挑着一支自动手枪，满面笑容，像鼓乐队队长领着队伍似的把部下往前带。一发炮弹还是别的什么命中了他。他消失在一片血泊里，前排又有五六个人跟他一同栽倒。

我寻思咱们该快到了，便从坦克边缘偷偷张了一眼。一挺来回扫射的机枪刮着地面，掀起一把把泥土，我还听见打扁的子弹从钢甲上弹开时那低沉的*哐、哐*声。我把头缩回来，继续前进。

慢！真慢！我发现自己已经在盘算进前沿战壕以后要怎么干了——如果我们真能到得了的话。到时候准有一场天翻地覆的大乱战，我怎么说也能“点”掉一打还多。

然后，我们到了。

===Original===
I don’t suppose that trip across No Man’s Land behind the tanks took over five minutes, but it seemed like an hour.

At the end of it my participation in the battle of High Wood ended. No, I wasn’t wounded. But when we reached the Boche front trenches a strange thing happened. There was no fight worth mentioning. The tanks stopped over the trenches and blazed away right and left with their all-around traverse.

A few Boches ran out and threw silly little bombs at the monsters. The tanks, noses in air, moved slowly on. And then the Graybacks swarmed up out of shelters and dugouts, literally in hundreds, and held up their hands, whining “Mercy, *kamarad*.”

We took prisoners by platoons. Blofeld grabbed me and turned over a gang of thirty to me. We searched them rapidly, cut their suspenders and belts, and I started to the rear with them. They seemed glad to go. So was I.

===Chinese===
我估摸着，跟着坦克穿越无人地带（No Man’s Land）这一趟统共花不了五分钟，可当时竟像熬了一个钟头。

这一段路走完，我在海伍德之战里的戏份便到此为止了。不，我没负伤。只是我们抵达德国佬（Boche）前沿战壕时，怪事出现了：压根没有一场像样的战斗。坦克横骑在战壕上方，凭着周向旋转的火力朝左右两边猛轰。

有几个德国兵跑出来，朝这些钢铁怪物扔了几颗不顶用的小炸弹。坦克昂着鼻子，缓缓前行。接着，灰军装德国兵便从隐蔽处和掩蔽部里蜂拥而出——实打实的数以百计——高举双手，嘴里哀告：“饶命，*卡玛拉德*（kamarad，德语‘同志’，德兵投降时的喊话）。”

我们整排整排地收俘虏。布洛菲尔德（Blofeld）一把拽住我，把三十个俘虏交给我。我们火速搜了他们的身，割断背带和腰带，我便押着他们动身后撤。他们瞧着挺乐意走。我也乐意。

===Original===
As we hurried back over the five hundred yards that had been No Man’s Land and was now British ground, I looked back and saw the irresistible tanks smashing their way through the tree stumps of High Wood, still spitting death and destruction in three directions.

Going back we were under almost as heavy fire as we had been coming up. When we were about halfway across, shrapnel burst directly over our party and seven of the prisoners were killed and half a dozen wounded. I myself was unscratched. I stuck my hand inside my tunic and patted Dinky on the back, sent up a prayer for some more luck like that, and carried on.

After getting my prisoners back to the rear, I came up again but couldn’t find my battalion. I threw in with a battalion of Australians and was with them for twenty-four hours.

When I found my chaps again, the battle of High Wood was pretty well over. Our company for some reason had suffered very few casualties, less than twenty-nine. Company B, however, had been practically wiped out, losing all but thirteen men out of two hundred. The other two companies had less than one hundred casualties. We had lost about a third of our strength. It is a living wonder to me that any of us came through.

===Chinese===
我们匆匆折返，穿过那五百码地皮——前不久还是无人地带，如今已归英军。我回头望去，只见势不可当的坦克正在海伍德的树桩丛中碾开一条路，依旧朝三个方向喷吐着死亡与毁灭。

回程路上，我们头顶的炮火几乎同来时一样猛。走到半途，一发榴霰弹正在我们这一队人头顶炸开，七个俘虏丢了命，还有六个挂了彩。我自己倒毫发无伤。我把手伸进军上衣，拍了拍丁奇（Dinky）的背，暗暗祷告老天爷再多赏几回这样的运气，然后接着赶路。

把俘虏押到后方之后，我又折上前线，却怎么也找不着我们营。我便跟一个澳大利亚营搭了伙，随他们待了二十四小时。

等我再找到自己那帮弟兄，海伍德之战差不多已经收尾。不知什么缘故，我们连伤亡极少，不到二十九人。可B连几乎全军覆没，两百人里只活下十三个。另外两个连的伤亡都不到一百。我们损失了大约三分之一的兵力。我们这帮人里居然有人活着回来，在我看来简直是活生生的奇迹。

===Original===
I don’t believe any of us would have if it hadn’t been for the tanks.

The net result of the battle of High Wood was that our troops carried on for nearly two miles beyond the position to be taken. They had to fall back but held the wood and the heights. Three of the tanks were stalled in the farther edge of the woods﻿—out of fuel﻿—and remained there for three days unharmed under the fire of the German guns.

Eventually someone ventured out and got some juice into them, and they returned to our lines. The tanks had proved themselves, not only as effective fighting machines, but as destroyers of German morale.

===Chinese===
我敢说，若不是有坦克，我们当中一个也活不下来。

海伍德之战的最终战果是：我们的部队一鼓作气，越过预定夺取的阵地又推进了近两英里，虽然随后不得不后撤，却守住了树林和高地。有三辆坦克趴窝在林子远端的边上——燃料耗尽——在德军炮火底下停了三天，毫发未损。

最后总算有人壮着胆子摸出去，给它们灌足了油（juice，兵俚指燃料），它们便开回了我方防线。坦克就此证明了自己——不只是得力的战斗机器，更是德国人士气的克星。
