## XII. The Captain Objects / 十二　上校提出异议

===Original===

Up the old stairway to the courtroom Jerk followed the captain, wondering why he had been called, what the captain knew about last night, and whatever Doctor Syn would advise him to say if he were questioned. These were nutty problems for Jerry’s young teeth to crack, and though somewhat nervous in consequence, he was on the whole highly delighted at seeing the fun.

The procedure of the inquiry was evidently biding the captain’s presence, for as soon as he had taken his seat at the high table the squire rose and in a few well-chosen words announced the inquiry to be set and open. The captain seemed to have forgotten the presence of Jerk, who was left standing in the doorway surveying the august company. There was an attorney-at-law and a doctor of medicine from Hythe, an attorney from Romney and a doctor from Romney. At the high table these four gentlemen sat facing the squire, who was in the centre, with Doctor Syn upon his right. On his left was the chair just occupied by the captain, and on fixed oak benches round the room sat the leading lights of Dymchurch: the head preventive officer, three or four well-to-do farmers, two owners of fishing luggers, Denis Cobtree, Mrs. Waggetts, and the schoolmaster, besides two or three other villagers. Nobody took much notice of Jerk when he came in, for all eyes were on the captain, but Doctor Syn not only took notice but the trouble to point out an empty space on one of the benches.

===Chinese===

杰尔克（Jerk）跟着上校，一路登上通往法庭的那道古旧楼梯，心里直犯嘀咕：叫自己来做什么？上校对昨夜的事知道多少？万一被问起话来，辛医生（Doctor Syn）又会教他说什么才好？这些难题，够小杰里那口嫩牙好好嗑上一阵的了。他因此有几分发怵，可总的说来，眼见有热闹可瞧，他心里乐得很。

查讯的程序显然一直在恭候上校大驾：他刚在上首长桌落座，乡绅便站起身来，用几句措辞得体的话，宣布查讯就此设立、正式开庭。上校似乎把杰尔克忘了个干净，任他杵在门口，打量着满堂威严的人物。这里有海斯（Hythe）来的一位执业律师、一位内科大夫，还有罗姆尼（Romney）来的一位律师和一位大夫。四位先生在上首长桌对面坐成一排，正对着乡绅；乡绅居中，辛医生坐在他右手；他左手便是上校方才坐过的那把椅子。屋子四壁下摆着一圈固定的橡木长凳，迪姆彻奇的头面人物齐聚其上：首席缉私员、三四位家境殷实的农场主、两位单桅渔帆船的船主，还有丹尼斯·科布特（Denis Cobtree）、瓦格茨太太（Mrs. Waggetts）和学堂先生，另添两三个别的村人。杰尔克进来时，谁也没怎么留意他，因为满堂的眼睛都盯着上校；唯独辛医生不但留意到了，还不辞劳烦地指点他，说长凳上有一块空位。

===Original===

“Are all those summoned for this inquiry present?” asked the captain, looking round at the assemblage.

“All but Mr. Mipps,” said the squire, referring to a list of names before him. “While we were waiting for you, he took the opportunity of viewing the body next door.”

The captain signed to one of the two sailors who were guarding the door of the adjoining room, and he accordingly summoned the undertaker, who with an eye to business was measuring the corpse. Jerk caught a glimpse of this as the door opened, and of the form of Sennacherib Pepper lying on a table. The undertaker, with a footrule in his hands, took his place on one of the benches. Mipps’s entrance seemed to revive the tragedy of the whole business, for there was a pause pending the squire’s opening speech; but the captain was the first to speak. He arose and to the astonishment of everybody took up and lit a pipe which had been lying upon the table in front of him.

===Chinese===

“奉召出席这次查讯的，人都到齐了么？”上校环视满堂，问道。

“只差米普斯（Mipps）先生一位，”乡绅一面答，一面翻着面前的名单，“等你这会儿工夫，他抽空到隔壁验看遗体去了。”

上校朝把守着隔壁房门的两个水兵中的一个打了个手势，那水兵便进去传唤殡葬承办人——这位老兄不忘自家生意，正拿尺量着尸身。门开的一瞬，杰尔克瞥见了这一幕，也瞥见了西拿基立·佩珀（Sennacherib Pepper）的遗体躺在一张桌上。殡葬承办人手里握着一支英尺木尺，寻了条长凳坐定。米普斯这一进来，仿佛把整桩事的悲剧气氛又带了回来，满场静默，单等乡绅致开场辞；不想头一个开口的，却是上校。他站起身来，竟当众拿起面前桌上搁着的一杆烟斗，装烟点着，抽了起来，惊得满座愕然。

===Original===

“Sir Antony Cobtree and gentlemen,” he said in his great husky sea voice, as he drew the smoke deliberately through the long clay stem and volleyed it back from his set mouth in blue battle clouds across the table, “we have met here to discuss, as Sir Antony Cobtree has already said better than I ever could, the sad and sudden death of Doctor Sennacherib Pepper, killed violently last night on Romney Marsh. The form of this inquiry I leave to the lawyers whose business it is, but before they get busy I’ve got a few things bottled up that I must and will say. I don’t possess the knack of a crafty tongue myself, I’ve the reputation among my colleagues of being the most tactless man in the service; but I’ve also a reputation as a fighter, and when I do fight, it’s a hard fight—a straightforward, open fight. So what I’ve got to say will like enough cause offence to every man in this room from Sir Antony Cobtree downward. I’m no good at strategy; as I say, I fight open; and when I think things—well, I can’t bottle them up; I say ’em out bluntly at the risk of offence. So here it is: I don’t like this business—this Doctor Pepper business—” The captain here paused to roll a large volume of smoke across the room.

The squire took advantage of the pause and said: “If that’s all it is, Captain, come now—which of us do?”

The captain thought a moment and added: “If the party or parties who committed the crime didn’t like it, why, in thunder’s name, did they do it?”

“You should know that better than we do,” returned the squire hotly, “for that the murderer was under your employment is fairly obvious.”

===Chinese===

“安东尼·科布特爵士（Sir Antony Cobtree），各位先生，”他说道，嗓门是那副粗哑洪亮的水手嗓门；一面不慌不忙地透过长长的陶土烟管吸着烟，再从抿紧的嘴里连珠般齐射回去，一蓬蓬蓝色的战云滚滚掠过桌面，“我们今天聚在这里，是要商议西拿基立·佩珀医生惨死的事——他昨夜在罗姆尼湿地上横遭暴亡；这件事，安东尼·科布特爵士方才已经说过，说得比我这辈子能办到的都好。这查讯的章程怎么立，我留给律师们去张罗，那是他们的本行；可趁他们还没忙起来，我心里憋着几句话，不能不说，也一定要说。我这人没有巧舌如簧的本事；在同僚中间，我顶着个名声，是这一行里最不识时务的家伙；可我还有另一个名声：好斗。真打起来，就是一场硬仗——一场明来明往、当面锣对面鼓的硬仗。所以，我要说的这些话，只怕会把这屋子里上自安东尼·科布特爵士、下至在座每一位先生，统统得罪个遍。我不擅长使心计；方才说了，我打仗讲究公开；心里一想事——唉，就憋不住，哪怕得罪人，也要直统统地说出来。那么，话就在这儿了：这桩事，我看不上眼——这桩佩珀医生的案子——”上校说到这里顿了顿，把一大团烟慢慢滚过整间屋子。

乡绅趁着这停顿开口道：“上校，倘若不过是这么回事，得了吧——我们中间又有哪个喜欢呢？”

上校想了想，又添上一句：“要是干下这桩罪行的某人或某些人也并不喜欢它，那看在雷霆的份上，他们干吗还要去干？”

“这层道理，你该比我们明白得多，”乡绅火冒三丈，回敬道，“那凶手是在你手下当差，这不是明摆着的么。”

===Original===

“You are referring to the mulatto seaman,” said the captain. “In the first place, I consider that you should have asked my permission before you issued that public notice affixed to the church door. Until the mulatto is found and can be examined, I deny your right or any man’s right to brand him as a murderer.”

“You remarked just now, sir,” cried the squire, “that you preferred to leave the business of lawyers to the lawyers. Please do so, and remember that while I am head of this jurisdiction on Romney Marsh I’ll brook no dictation from Admiralty men—no, sir, not from the First Lord downward.”

“Come, come, gentlemen,” said Doctor Syn, drumming with his fingers on the table, “I think that this is an ill-fitting time and place for wrangling. The captain has got a bee in his bonnet somehow, and the sooner we get it out for him the better. Let us please hear, sir, what he has to say.”

The squire nodded his head roughly and sat silent, while the rest of the company waited for the captain to continue, which he presently did, still pulling vigorously at his long clay pipe.

===Chinese===

“你说的是那个黑白混血水手，”上校说，“头一条，我认为你把那张公告贴上教堂大门之前，本该先征得我的准许。在那黑白混血儿缉拿到案、接受讯问之前，我不承认你有权——也不承认任何人有权——把凶手这罪名烙在他身上。”

“先生，你方才亲口说过，情愿把律师的营生留给律师去办，”乡绅嚷道，“那就请便吧。还请你记着：只要罗姆尼湿地这一方辖地还由我做主，就容不得海军部的人在这儿指手画脚——不错，先生，上自海军大臣（First Lord），下至无名小卒，一概如此。”

“好了，好了，二位先生，”辛医生说着，手指在桌上笃笃地敲着，“这样的时辰、这样的地方，我看都不宜斗气拌嘴。上校不知怎么钻了个牛角尖，我们越早替他把这尖儿拔出来越好。先生，还是请让我们听听他有什么要说的话吧。”

乡绅重重地点一下头，闷坐不语；其余众人便等着上校续说下去。上校果然接着开口，一面兀自起劲地嘬着他那杆长陶土烟斗。

===Original===

“The next thing I don’t like,” he went on, “is Dymchurch itself. I don’t like the Marsh behind it, and I don’t like the flat, open coastline; it looks a deal too innocent for me on the surface, and, not being a strategist, I don’t like it.”

The squire was on edge with irritation.

“I am sure, sir,” he said sarcastically, “that had the Almighty been notified of your objection during the process of the creation he would have extended Dover Cliffs round Dungeness.” The captain didn’t seem to notice the interruption.

“Next, I don’t like the people here, leaving Doctor Syn out of it—for he’s a parson and I never could make head or tail of parsons. I say that, from the squire down, you’re none of you swimming the surface. Sir Antony Cobtree went to great pains to lavishly entertain me yesterday, in order that he might politely imprison me last night. I enjoy good entertainment and the conversation of witty, clever men, but not at the price of a locked door.”

===Chinese===

“还有一样我不喜欢的，”他接着说，“就是迪姆彻奇这地方本身。它背后那片湿地，我不喜欢；那道又平又敞的海岸线，我也不喜欢。这地方表面上清白得太过分，照我看就有鬼；我又不是个讲策略的人，所以我不喜欢它。”

乡绅心里的火气已经按捺不住。

“我相信，先生，”他话里带刺，“倘若造物主在创世的时候收到过你老这封异议书，他老人家准会把多佛的悬崖一路延伸过去，把邓杰内斯（Dungeness）整个围将起来。”上校却似乎压根没理会这声插话。

“再往下数，这里的人我也不喜欢——辛医生不在此列，因为他是位牧师，而牧师那种人，我一辈子也摸不着头脑。依我说，从乡绅往下，你们没一个不是沉在水面底下过活的。安东尼·科布特爵士昨天好不费事地大排筵席款待我，图的就是昨夜客客气气地把我软禁起来。好席面我爱吃，机灵人的妙语我爱听，可要拿一扇上了锁的门作价钱，恕不奉陪。”

===Original===

“I don’t know what you are talking about!” said the squire, livid with rage.

“Don’t you, sir?” retorted Captain Collyer. “Well, I do, as I had to risk breaking my neck when I climbed down the ivy from your top window.”

“You had only to tell me of your eccentric habits,” said the squire, “and I would have set a ladder against your window in case the door stuck.”

“The door was locked, and well you know it, sir,” cried the captain, suddenly turning on the squire, “for half an hour after I had climbed back through the window—to be exact, at half-past four—I heard stealthy feet come along the passage and unlock it, by which I know that for a period of the night you wanted to make sure of me inside my room, and when on inquiring from your servants I discover that I am the first guest who has ever slept in that particular room, and that the furniture was put into it for the occasion from one of the spare rooms, I begin to see your wisdom, for that room contained no view of the high road, no view of the Marsh or sea.”

===Chinese===

“我不明白你在说些什么！”乡绅嚷道，气得脸色铁青。

“您不明白吗，先生？”科利尔上校（Captain Collyer）回敬道，“哼，我明白得很：从您顶楼窗口攀着常春藤溜下来的时候，我可是冒着摔断脖子的险。”

“您只要跟我打声招呼，说您有这些古怪习性，”乡绅说，“我便会叫人在您窗下架一架梯子，以防房门卡住。”

“那扇门是上了锁的，您心里雪亮，先生。”上校突然转向乡绅，发作起来，“我攀窗爬回屋里之后半个钟头——说得确切些，是四点半钟——我听见有贼头贼脑的脚步顺着走廊过来，把锁捅开。由此我便知道，夜里有一段时辰，您是要拿稳我关在自己房里的。后来我向您的仆人一打听，才知道我是头一个睡进那间屋子的客人，那屋里的家具还是专为此番从一间空房里搬置进去的。这下我可见识到您的‘高明’了——原来那间屋子，望不见大路，望不见湿地，也望不见海。”

===Original===

“Gad! sir, you are the first man who has dared to question my hospitality. Perhaps you expected me to give up my room for your accommodation.”

“Nothing of the kind,” answered the captain, “but I expected to be dealt straight with. And this brings me to the end of my complaints, and let me tell you this: I saw enough last night on the Marsh to keep Jack Ketch busy for an hour or so. Gentlemen, I am warning you. You’ll not be the first I’ve sent from the coast to the sessions, nor will you be the last. I warn you, one and all, that I’m going to strike soon. I’m not afraid of your tales of Marsh devils and demon riders. I’ll rout ’em out and see how they look by daylight. I’ve men behind me that I can trust, and they’re pretty hardy fighters. If your demon riders are not of this world, then they’ll do our good steel no harm; but if they are just men playing hanky-panky tricks to frighten fools from the Marsh, well, all I’ve got to say to them is, if they relish British cutlasses in their bowels, let them continue with such pranks as they played upon poor Pepper, and they’ll get Pepper back and be damned to them, for it’s Jack Ketch or the cold steel and nothing else.” And having thus hurled his challenge at the assembly the captain put his pipe upon the table and sat down.

You can imagine that a speech of so staggering a nature had a strange effect upon the company. So sudden was it, so ferocious, so uncalled for, that nearly a minute elapsed before anyone moved. At last the squire rose, speaking quietly but in that clear voice that everybody in Dymchurch knew so well and respected:

===Chinese===

“嗬！先生，敢质疑我的待客之道，您是头一个。莫非您还指望我把自己的房间腾出来给您安顿不成？”

“压根儿没有那个意思，”上校答道，“可我指望的是别人跟我明来明往打交道。我的牢骚到此为止。下面这句话，请各位记牢：昨夜我在湿地上瞧见的东西，足够让刽子手杰克·凯奇（Jack Ketch，旧时英国刽子手的通称）忙活上一个来钟头。诸位先生，我这是在警告你们。从这片海岸经我的手送上巡回法庭的，你们不是头一批，也不会是末一批。我正告你们，在座每一个人：我很快就要动手了。你们那一套湿地魔鬼、魔骑的故事，吓不倒我。我自会把他们从窝里掏出来，瞧瞧大白日头底下他们是什么嘴脸。我背后有人手，个个信得过，也都是能打硬仗的角色。倘若你们那些魔骑当真不是阳世间的，那自然伤不着我们的好钢刀；可倘若他们不过是几个耍弄装神弄鬼的把戏、吓唬湿地傻瓜的凡人，那么我撂给他们的话便是：要是他们乐意让英国弯刀在肚肠里开花，那就随他们接着耍那些作践可怜佩珀的把戏——保管连本带利，原样讨回一顿‘胡椒’（原文 Pepper 一语双关：既是死者之名，亦指一顿痛惩），被咒下地狱去；反正等着他们的，不是杰克·凯奇，便是冷冰冰的钢刀，别无其他。”上校就这样把战书掷向满堂，随即将烟斗搁回桌上，坐了下来。

可以想见，这样一篇惊雷也似的演说，落在满堂人等身上，激起了何等古怪的效果。它来得太突然，太凶悍，又太没有来由，足足过了将近一分钟，才有人动弹。末了，乡绅站起身来，语调平静，用的却是全迪姆彻奇人都再熟悉不过、也素来敬重的那副清朗嗓音：

“Gentlemen, Doctor Syn spoke very wisely, as it is ever his wont to do, when he rebuked us for wrangling, for, as he said, both time and place are ill fitting. This is the first time that I have been insulted during my long sojourn in Romney Marsh, and I am glad that it has been in the presence of my friends and tenants of Dymchurch, who know me well and will do me right in their own minds, never allowing themselves to be warped for a single instant by the scathing and unjust remarks of a stranger upon whom I have, to the best of my ability, bestowed hospitality and every mark of friendship. On the other hand, I most honestly affirm that Captain Howard Collyer has given me insult in a straightforward way. In his defence I must say that the Admiralty have chosen a bad man to do their spying for them; when I say bad, I mean, of course, the ‘wrong’ man. I know the captain to be a brave and a good sailor. The splendid though tactless drubbing that he gave to the French man-o’-war *Golden Lion* in the mouth of the St. Lawrence River describes exactly the sort of character that Collyer carries; and if the Admiralty had left him in command of the *Resistance* we should have been at war with the odious French long ago. I now give the Admiralty credit for being weatherwise seamen and diplomatists, and think them shrewd in depriving him of a ‘command.’ Having now, as it were, given the devil his due, I say to him, in the presence of you all, that his words here this morning have been foolish, ridiculous, and altogether preposterous. It is not in accordance with either my private or public dignity that I should answer the vague, hinted accusation of this captain. As I said before, I am judge here, and while I hold the most honourable position of ‘Leveller of Marsh Scotts,’ I decline to entertain any imputations, for should I ever consider myself to be in the position of being rationally accused of any crime of lawlessness, I should, for the honour of my office and the general welfare of Romney Marsh, regard myself compelled to resign. This I have no intention of doing, for it is clearly now my bounden duty to see my poor friend Sennacherib Pepper righted and avenged; and for that duty I sweep aside Captain Collyer’s statements as trivial and impertinent. You gentlemen in this courthouse are all good Marshmen, and one and all know me better than I know myself. When you consider me unfit to be your judge I will retire, but not till then.”

A storm of applause greeted the squire as he sat down, but it was checked by Doctor Syn, who again reminded the assemblage of the sad event that had brought them to the courthouse and begged them out of respect for the dead gentleman in the next room to abstain from any further acclamation.

“诸位先生，辛医生责备我们不该斗气拌嘴，说得极是明白——他向来如此；诚如他所言，此时此地，均不相宜。我在罗姆尼湿地这方水土上住了这许多年，遭人当面羞辱，这是头一遭。我倒庆幸这场羞辱是当着我迪姆彻奇的朋友和佃户们的面来的：他们深知我的为人，自会在心里还我一个公道，断不会让一个外乡人的刻薄不公之词，哪怕在一霎之间撼动他们的心——而我待那位外乡人，已是竭尽绵薄，东道之谊、朋友之礼，一样不缺。不过话说回来，我也须最坦白地承认：霍华德·科利尔（Howard Collyer）上校给我的这场羞辱，倒确是明来明往的。要替他说句公道话，我得说，海军部挑选替他们当眼线的人，挑了个坏人选；我说‘坏’，当然是指挑了个‘不对路’的人。我知道这位上校是个勇敢的水手，也是个好水手。当年在圣劳伦斯河（St. Lawrence River）口，他把法国战舰‘金狮号’（Golden Lion）结结实实痛揍了一顿——揍得漂亮，只是不识时务——这一仗恰巧画出科利尔是副什么脾性；倘若海军部当初留他继续统带‘抵抗号’（Resistance），我们同那可恶的法国人早就开战了。如今我倒要称赞海军部几句：他们真是些善观风色的水手兼外交家，剥掉他一个‘指挥权’，手段着实精明。如今，就算我把该给魔鬼的那份公道给了他，可我还要当着你们大家的面对他说：他今天早晨在这里放出的这番话，愚蠢，可笑，而且荒谬绝伦。这位上校那影影绰绰、含沙射影的指控，无论按我私人的体面，还是按我公开的身份，都不值得我来作答。方才已经说过，我是此地的大法官；只要‘湿地斯科特人的平定者’（Leveller of Marsh Scotts，旧时裁定湿地讼案的荣衔）这一至为荣耀的职分还在我身上一日，我便一日拒绝理会任何血口喷人的影射。倘若哪一天我自认确已被人以像样的凭据指控犯了什么目无王法的罪行，那么，为了我这职分的尊严，也为了罗姆尼湿地的公共福祉，我自当视引退为义不容辞。可我全无此意。因为如今分明摆着一桩我义不容辞的天职：替我可怜的朋友西拿基立·佩珀昭雪冤屈，报此血仇；为了这桩天职，科利尔上校的种种说法，我只当是琐碎无礼的废话，一扫而过。这座议事厅里的诸位先生都是地地道道的湿地汉子，你们个个都比我更了解我自己。哪一天你们觉得我不配做你们的裁判官了，我自会告退——在那之前，绝不。”

乡绅落座，满堂喝彩骤起，声如骤雨；辛医生却把它按了下去。他再度提请众人记起把他们召到这议事厅里来的那桩哀事，恳请大家体念隔壁那位死者的体面，按下掌声，勿再喝彩。
