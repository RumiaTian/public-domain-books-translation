## II. The Coming of the King's Frigate / 二　国王的护卫舰来了

===Original===
Now Doctor Syn was very fond of the sea, and he was never far away from it. Even in winter time he would walk upon the seawall with a formidable telescope under his arm, his hands thrust deep into the pockets of a long sea-coat, and his old black three-cornered parson's hat cocked well forward and pulled down over his eyes. And although the simple old fellow would be mentally working out his dry-as-dust sermons, he would be striding along at a most furious speed, presenting to those who did not know him an altogether alarming appearance, for in tune to his brisk step he would be humming the first verse of an old-time sea chanty that he had picked up from some ruffianly seadog of a parishioner; and as he strode along, with his weather eye ever on the lookout for big ships coming up the Channel, the rough words would roll from his gentle lips with the most perfect incongruity:

===Chinese===
话说辛医生（Doctor Syn）生性酷爱大海，从来不肯离海太远。即便在冬天，他也照旧在海堤上散步，腋下夹着一支威风凛凛的大望远镜，双手深深插在长身航海大衣的口袋里，头上那顶旧黑三角牧师帽向前压得很低，直遮到眼睛上。这位朴实的老人家心里尽管在推敲他那些枯燥得掉灰的布道文，脚下却迈着大步、走得飞快，叫不认识他的人瞧着不免心惊：因为他总合着轻快的步点，哼一支老式水手号子的头一节——那是从教区里哪个刁悍的老海狗教民嘴里学来的；他一路阔步前行，那只阅尽风浪的眼睛始终留神着驶上英吉利海峡（the Channel）的大船，粗野的字眼便从他温文的唇间滚滚而出，说不出的不协调：

===Original===
"Oh, here's to the feet that have walked the plank,
Yo ho! for the dead man's throttle,
And here's to the corpses floating round in the tank,
And the dead man's teeth in the bottle."

===Chinese===
“哦，这一杯，敬那走过跳板的脚，
哟嗬！为死人的咽喉干杯，
再敬那漂满水舱、团团打转的尸首，
和瓶子里死人的牙。”

===Original===
He was as proud of this song as if he had written it himself, and it was a continual source of amusement to the fishermen to hear him sing it, which he frequently did of an evening in the parlour of the old Ship Inn when he went there for a chat and a friendly pipe; for Doctor Syn was, as I have said, broad-minded, and held views that would certainly have been beyond those of the diocesan dignitaries. The very daring of a parson drinking with the men in a public inn had a good effect, he declared, upon the parish, for a good parson, as a good sailor, should know when he has had enough. The squire would back him up in this, and there they would both sit every evening laughing and talking with the fishermen, very often accompanying some crew down to the beach to help them launch their boat—and of course all this added to their popularity. But on Sunday nights they dined at the courthouse, leaving the field open for the redoubtable Mipps, who, as has been said, took full advantage of it.

===Chinese===
这支歌他引以为傲，倒像是出自他本人手笔；渔民们听他唱这支歌，也总能乐上一阵——傍晚时分，他常到老“船”酒馆（the Ship Inn）的店堂里去聊聊天、随和地共抽一斗烟，兴之所至便唱上一曲。因为辛医生，正如我方才所说，是个心胸开阔的人，他持的那些见解，恐怕远远超出了教区神职显贵们的境界。他宣称，牧师敢在酒馆里同汉子们对饮，单这份大胆就对教区大有好处；因为一个好的牧师，正如一个好的水手，应当知道几时算喝够了量。乡绅在这上头也总替他帮腔，于是每天傍晚，他们俩便坐在那里同渔民们有说有笑，还常常跟着哪一伙船员下到海滩，帮他们把小船推下水——这一切自然叫他们大得人心。可是每逢礼拜天晚上，他们要在议事厅（courthouse）用餐，便把整片场子让给了那位了不得的米普斯（Mipps）；前文说过，他把这个便宜占了个十足。

===Original===
Now the ungainly little sexton had a great admirer in the person of Mrs. Waggetts, the landlady of the Ship. Her husband had been dead for a number of years, and she was ever on the lookout for another. She perceived in the person of Mipps her true lord and master. He was enterprising, he had also money of his own, for he was parish undertaker as well as sexton, and ran from his small shop in the village every trade imaginable. You could buy anything, from a bottle of pickles to a marlinspike in that dirty little store, and get a horrible anecdote thrown in with your bargain from the ready lips of the old fellow, who would continue to hammer away at an unfinished coffin as he talked to you.

But the burning passion that smouldered in the breast of the Ship landlady was in no way shared by the little sexton.

===Chinese===
话说这位笨手笨脚的小个子司事，有一位热心的仰慕者——“船”酒馆的老板娘瓦格茨太太（Mrs. Waggetts）。她丈夫死了好些年，她时时刻刻都在物色下一任，并在米普斯身上认出了自己真正的主人与当家人。此人有闯劲，手里也自有几个钱，因为他不但是司事，还兼着教区的殡葬承办，村子里那间小铺子在他手上做着天底下想得出来的各行买卖。从一瓶腌黄瓜到一支穿索铁锥，你什么都能在那间又脏又小的铺子里买到；成交之余，这老家伙那张利嘴还会饶上一段骇人的奇闻，他一边同你说话，一边还在一口没完工的棺材上敲个不停。

然而“船”酒馆老板娘胸中那团闷烧的烈火，小个子司事却半点也无。

===Original===
"Missus Waggetts," he would say, "folk in the death trade should keep single; they gets their fair share of misery, Lord above knows, in these parts with the deaths so uncommon few."

"Well," Mrs. Waggetts would sigh, "I often wish as how it had been me that had been took instead of Waggetts. I fair envy him lying up there all so peaceful like, just a-rottin' slowly along in his coffin."

But the sexton would immediately fly into a rage with: "Waggetts' coffin rottin', did you say, Missus Waggetts? Not mine. I undertook Waggetts, I'd have you remember, and I don't undertake to rot. I loses money on my coffins, Missus Waggetts. I undertakes, ma'am, undertakes to provide a suitable affair wot'll keep out damp and water, and cheat worm, grub, slug, and slush."

===Chinese===
“瓦格茨太太，”他总这么说，“干死人这一行的，就该打单身。老天爷知道，在这块地方死人不常死，咱们受的晦气已经够份儿了。”

“唉，”瓦格茨太太会叹口气，“我常常巴不得当年被带走的不是瓦格茨，倒是我。我真眼馋他，安安静静躺在坡顶上，在棺材里慢慢地、慢慢地烂着。”

可司事一听立刻火冒三丈：“瓦格茨的棺材在烂，你是这么说的吗，瓦格茨太太？绝不是我的活儿。我提醒你记着，瓦格茨的丧事是我承办的，可我从来没应承过叫它烂。我做的棺材是赔本的，瓦格茨太太。我承办的，太太，我承办的是一件像样的物件——挡得住潮气、隔得开雨水，叫蛀虫、蛆虫、蛞蝓和烂泥统统占不到便宜。”

===Original===
"Nobody would deny, Mister Mipps," the landlady would answer in a conciliatory tone, "as to how you're a good undertaker. Anyone with half an eye could see as how you knocks 'em up solid."

But Mipps didn't encourage Mrs. Waggetts when she was pleased to flatter, so he would take himself off in high dudgeon to avoid her further attentions.

This actual conversation took place one November afternoon, and the sexton, after slamming the inn door to give vent to his irritation, hurried along the seawall toward his shop, comforting himself that he could sit snug inside a coffin and cheer himself up with hammering it.

===Chinese===
“谁也不能否认，米普斯先生，”老板娘会用缓和的口气答话，“你这殡葬承办人的手艺是没得挑的。谁只要长着半只眼，就看得出你敲打出来的东西瓷实。”

可米普斯并不领受瓦格茨太太高兴时的奉承，他往往怒冲冲地抬腿就走，躲开她进一步的殷勤。

上头这场对话，就发生在一个十一月的下午。司事摔上酒馆的门，出了出这口恶气，便沿着海堤急匆匆往自己的铺子赶，一路安慰自己：待会儿可以舒舒服服坐在一口棺材里头，敲敲打打，给自己解解闷。

===Original===
On the way he met Doctor Syn, who was standing silhouetted against the skyline with his telescope focused upon some large vessel that was standing in off Dungeness.

"Ah, Mr. Mipps," said the cleric, handing his telescope to the sexton, "tell me what you make of that?"

Mipps adjusted the lens and looked. "The Devil!" he ejaculated.

===Chinese===
半路上他遇见了辛医生。医生立在海堤上，身形衬着天际，望远镜正对着一艘大船——那船正打邓杰内斯（Dungeness）外海朝岸边驶来。

“啊，米普斯先生，”牧师一边把望远镜递给司事一边说，“给我瞧瞧，你看那是什么？”

米普斯调了调镜片，看了一眼。“见鬼！”他失声骂了出来。

===Original===
"I beg your pardon?" said the Doctor. "What did you say?" One of the King's preventer men had come out of his cottage and was approaching them.

"I don't make no head nor tale of it," replied the sexton. "Perhaps you do, sir?"

"Well, it looks to me," continued the parson, "it—looks—to—me—uncommonly like a King's frigate. Can't you make out her guns on the port side?"

===Chinese===
“你说什么？”医生问。“你刚说什么来着？”原来替国王当差的一名缉私员（preventer man）正从小屋里出来，朝两人走来。

“我是一点头绪也瞧不出来，”司事答道。“兴许您瞧得出来，先生？”

“嗯，照我看，”牧师接着说，“它——照——我——看——活脱脱是一艘国王的护卫舰。你瞧不见它左舷那排炮吗？”

===Original===
"Yes!" cried the sexton; "I'll be hanged if you're not right, sir; it's a damned King's ship as ever was."

"Mr. Mipps," corrected the parson, "again I must ask you to repeat your remark."

"I said, sir," replied the sexton, meekly handing back the glass, "that you're quite right: it's a King's ship, a nice King's ship!"

===Chinese===
“瞧出来啦！”司事喊道，“先生，您要是说得不对，我情愿上绞架；这是一艘十足的、该死的国王军舰。”

“米普斯先生，”牧师纠正道，“我还得再请您把方才那句话重说一遍。”

“我说的是，先生，”司事乖乖地把望远镜递还回去，“您说得一点儿不错：这是一艘国王军舰，一艘漂亮的国王军舰！”

===Original===
"And she's standing in, too," went on the parson. "I can make her out plainly now, and, good gracious! she's lowering a longboat!"

"Oh!" said Mr. Mipps, "I wonder wot that's for?"

"A revenue search," volunteered the preventer.

Mipps started. He hadn't seen the preventer.

===Chinese===
“而且它正往岸这边驶来，”牧师又说。“这下我看得清清楚楚了——哎呀，我的老天！它放下一条长艇了！”

“哦！”米普斯先生说，“不知这是要干什么？”

“缉私搜查，”那缉私员自告奋勇地搭了腔。

米普斯一激灵。他压根没瞧见这缉私员。

===Original===
"Hello!" he said, turning round; "didn't know you was there, Sir Francis Drake. What do you make of that there ship?"

"A King's frigate," replied the preventer man. "She's sending a boat's crew ashore."

"What for?" asked the sexton.

"I told you: a revenue search; to look for smugglers."

"Smugglers," laughed the parson, "here in Dymchurch?"

===Chinese===
“哟！”他转过身来说，“没瞧见您在场呢，弗朗西斯·德雷克爵士（Sir Francis Drake）。您给瞧瞧，那条船是什么名堂？”

“国王的护卫舰，”缉私员答道。“它正派一船水手上岸。”

“干什么？”司事问。

“方才说过了：缉私搜查；查走私贩子。”

“走私贩子，”牧师笑了，“在咱们迪姆彻奇（Dymchurch）？”

===Original===
"Aye, sir, so they say. Smugglers here in Dymchurch."

"God bless my soul!" exclaimed the parson incredulously.

"How silly!" said the sexton.

"That remains to be seen, Mister," retorted the preventer.

"What do you say?" said the sexton.

"I say, Mister, it remains to be seen."

===Chinese===
“可不是嘛，先生，人家都这么说。走私贩子就在迪姆彻奇。”

“我的老天爷！”牧师难以置信地惊呼。

“多傻呀！”司事说。

“那有待分晓，先生，”缉私员回敬道。

“你说什么？”司事问。

“我说，先生，这事有待分晓。”

===Original===
"'Course it does!" went on the sexton. "Let's have another blink at her. Well," he said at length, closing the telescope with a snap, and returning it, "King's ship or no, they looks to me more like a set of mahogany pirates, and I'm a-goin' to lock up the church. King's men's one thing, but havin' the plate took's another, and one that I don't fancy, being held responsible; so good afternoon, sir"—touching his hat to the vicar—"and good afternoon to you, Christopher Columbus." And with this little pleasantry, which struck him as being the height of humour, the grotesque little man hopped off at high speed in the direction of the inn.

"Odd little man that, sir," said the preventer.

"Very odd little man," said the vicar.

===Chinese===
“那还用说！”司事接着说。“再让我瞭它一眼。”末了，他啪的一声合上望远镜，递还回去，说道：“管它是不是国王的军舰，我看那伙人倒更像一伙红木脸皮的海盗，我得去把教堂锁上。国王的人马是一码事，教堂的银器被抢是另一码事——还要我担这份干系，我可不愿意。那么，先生，下午好。”——他朝教区牧师碰了碰帽檐——“也祝您下午好，克里斯托弗·哥伦布（Christopher Columbus）。”说完这句在他自己看来妙到毫巅的俏皮话，这古怪的小个子便一蹦一跳，飞快地朝酒馆去了。

“那小个子可真怪，先生，”缉私员说。

“怪得很的小个子，”教区牧师说。
