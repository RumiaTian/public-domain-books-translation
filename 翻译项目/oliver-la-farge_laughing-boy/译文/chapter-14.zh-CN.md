## Chapter XIV / 第十四章

### I / I

===Original===
It began to snow on the morning of the third day of their trip home, not far from Kintiel. The ground, where it had any dampness in it, had been frozen since the night before, and they had hurried under a threatening sky, having still a good day’s ride before them. The storm came down like timber-wolves, rushing. A mountaintop wind sent the dry flakes whirling past, stinging their ears and the sides of their faces; there was no sun, they could see only a few yards ahead of them. Pulling their blankets up over their heads, they guided themselves by the wind at their backs.

===Chinese===
回家行程的第三天早晨，离金提尔（Kintiel）不远，天下起雪来。地面但凡存着几分潮气的，前一夜里就冻住了；他们顶着一派凶险欲变的天空匆匆赶路，前面还有整整一天的好路程。暴风雪压将下来，像成群的林狼，奔突急袭。一股山巅之风卷着干雪片打着旋扫过，刺扎着他们的耳朵和脸颊两侧；没有太阳，往前只望得见几步远。两人把毯子拽起来罩在头上，凭着背后吹来的风辨着方向。

===Original===
An Indian takes the weather passively, accepting and enduring it without the European’s mental revolt or impatience. Comfort and fat living had changed this to some degree in Laughing Boy; he was unusually aware of discomfort, and resentful, rating the blizzard as colder than it was. Slim Girl was simply miserable. They did not speak, but jogged on, punishing their horses.

===Chinese===
印第安人对待天气是被动的：领受它，捱过它，心里没有欧洲人那种抗拒和不耐。安逸的日子、肥甘的吃喝，在笑娃身上把这性情改变了几分；他对苦楚格外知觉，心怀怨怼，把这场暴风雪估摸得比实际的更冷。瘦姑娘则干脆是苦透了。两人不说话，只顾一路颠行，狠使着他们的马。

===Original===
Time passed and the wind slackened, so that the snow about their ponies’ hooves stayed still, although the fall of flakes continued. Laughing Boy was preoccupied with thoughts of the road, but his wife contrasted this ride with the other time when they had ridden this way together. First it is the top of a stove and then it is an ice-machine, she thought; yet I am beginning to love it.

===Chinese===
时间过去，风势松了下来，小马蹄边旋起的雪落定了，虽然雪片还在下。笑娃一心惦记着路径，他妻子却把这一趟同上回两人一路同行时比较着。前一会儿是炉子面，后一会儿又成了制冰机，她想；可我竟开始爱上这儿了。

===Original===
Cliffs loomed before them, duskily blue with snowflakes rebounding and zigzagging before they touched the rock. The snow was beginning to drift.

===Chinese===
崖壁在前面隐隐现出，带着一层昏昧的蓝，雪片未及触岩便弹起，折出之字。雪开始吹积起来。

===Original===
“These are not the right cliffs,” he said; “the wind must have shifted, I think. I was afraid it would.”

===Chinese===
“这些不是原先那几道崖壁，”他说；“风向准是变了，我想。我原本就怕它变。”

===Original===
“What shall we do, then?”

===Chinese===
“那咱们怎么办？”

===Original===
“I think this is Inaiyé Cletso’i; we follow to the left.”

===Chinese===
“我想这里是伊奈耶·克莱措伊（Inaiyé Cletso’i）；咱们往左边走。”

===Original===
“Why not camp here?”

===Chinese===
“干吗不在这儿宿营？”

===Original===
“We must find firewood. We might just sleep here and not wake up. Come along, little sister, perhaps we shall find a hogan.”

===Chinese===
“咱们得找柴火。在这儿睡下去，怕就再也醒不过来了。走吧，小妹妹，兴许能寻着一座霍根屋。”

===Original===
They continued, he fully occupied, she miserable with nothing to do save follow. Sometimes the snow whirled up at them, sometimes a flaw would sting their faces with fine, white dust. Their heavy blankets felt thin as cotton over their shoulders.

===Chinese===
两人接着走，他全副心神都在路上，她一腔苦楚，除了跟随无事可做。雪有时打着旋扑上他们，有时一阵斜风挟着细白的雪粉刺他们的脸。厚重的毯子披在肩上，却薄得像棉布一般。

===Original===
“There’s a hogan.” She pushed forward.

===Chinese===
“那儿有座霍根屋。”她催马赶前。

===Original===
“*Hogay-gahn*, bad. Do not stop here!”

===Chinese===
“霍盖-冈（Hogay-gahn），不祥。别在这儿停！”

===Original===
“What do you mean?”

===Chinese===
“你这话什么意思？”

===Original===
“Don’t you see it is deserted? Don’t you see the hole in the north side? Someone has died here. Come along.”

===Chinese===
“你没瞧见这屋子是废弃了的吗？没瞧见北面墙上那个窟窿？有人死在这屋里。走吧。”

===Original===
She sighed in anger, gritted her teeth, swore under her breath, and turned her horse back. Nothing on earth would make a Navajo stop there; he would not even use the dry timbers for firewood to save his life. Well, it was part of the rest.

===Chinese===
她愤懑地叹了口气，咬紧牙关，压着嗓子咒骂了一句，拨转马头。天底下任凭什么也休想叫一个纳瓦霍人在那儿停留；就是救命，他也不会拿那些干木去生火。罢了，这也是这一切里应有的一部分。

===Original===
“We are coming somewhere now,” he called to her.

===Chinese===
“咱们这就要走到有人烟的地方了。”他向她喊道。

===Original===
“How?”

===Chinese===
“怎么说？”

===Original===
“I smell smoke. There, you can see.”

===Chinese===
“我闻见烟味了。喏，你也看得见。”

===Original===
It was a well-built hut beside a corral. Smoke issued from the hole in the roof. The dome of daubed mud and untrimmed logs looked beautiful just then. Laughing Boy shouted at the door, and a middle-aged man crawled out.

===Chinese===
那是一座修得齐整的小屋，旁边带一道畜栏，烟从屋顶的孔洞里冒出来。抹泥的穹顶、未削整的原木，这会儿看上去竟很美。笑娃朝门口喊了一声，一个中年汉子爬了出来。

===Original===
“Where are you going?”

===Chinese===
“你们上哪儿去？”

===Original===
“To Chiziai.”

===Chinese===
“去奇扎伊。”

===Original===
“You are out of the trail; it is far.”

===Chinese===
“你们偏离了大路；还远着呢。”

===Original===
“This snow confused us.”

===Chinese===
“这场雪把我们搅糊涂了。”

===Original===
“Where from?”

===Chinese===
“从哪儿来？”

===Original===
“To Tlakai.”

===Chinese===
“特莱凯。”

===Original===
“Where’s that?”

===Chinese===
“那在哪儿？”

===Original===
“Between Seinsaidesah and Agathla.”

===Chinese===
“在塞恩赛德萨和阿加斯拉之间。”

===Original===
“*Ei-yei!* You come far! Just beyond, there, is a box canyon. There is shelter and feed. Put your horses there, Grandfather. Drop your saddles here, I shall bring them in. Come in, Grandmother.”

===Chinese===
“咿-耶！你们来得可远啦！就在那边过去一点，有个箱形峡谷，能避风，也有草料。把马安置在那儿吧，老爷爷。鞍子卸在这儿，我来搬进去。进来吧，老奶奶。”

===Original===
They lost no time over the horses, and crawled gladly into the smoky, fetid, warm hogan. There were the man, two women, four children between eight and fifteen, and two dogs. The space was a circle some twelve feet in diameter﻿—the average size; with the people, the fire in the middle, saddles, cooking utensils, a loom and blankets, it was well filled.

===Chinese===
两人在马身上不敢耽搁，随即欢欢喜喜地钻进那烟腾腾、气味浊、暖烘烘的霍根屋。屋里有一个男人、两个女人、四个八岁到十五岁之间的孩子，外加两条狗。地面是一块直径约十二英尺的圆——这是寻常的尺寸；再算上当中那堆火、鞍子、炊具、一架织机和些毯子，已经填得满满当当。

===Original===
“You live at T’o Tlakai?”

===Chinese===
“你们住在特奥·特莱凯？”

===Original===
“No, at Chiziai. My parents live there. There was a Night Chant; for that we went. It was a full ten days’ chant. Mountain Singer conducted it.”

===Chinese===
“不，在奇扎伊。我父母住在那儿。那边办了一场夜颂仪，我们就是为这个去的。是一场足足十天的颂仪，山地颂唱师主持的。”

===Original===
“Beautiful!”

===Chinese===
“美啊！”

===Original===
“Yes.”

===Chinese===
“是啊。”

===Original===
The elder wife served them a pot of boiled mutton and corn, with a chunk of the usual tough wheat bread. They ate readily. It flashed through Laughing Boy’s mind that he had not enjoyed a meal so much since his arrival at Tsé Lani, but then he thought that that was silly. The foods to which he was accustomed!

===Chinese===
年长的那位妻子端给他们一锅羊肉煮玉米，还有一大块家常的那种硬麦面面包。他们吃得畅快。笑娃心头闪过一个念头：自从到了特塞拉尼，他还从没一顿饭吃得这么香过；可转念又想，这念头真傻。这些本是他吃惯了的饭食呀！

### II / II

===Original===
They were storm-bound for all the next day. He was anxious to be home again, now that the restraint of the ceremony and after-ceremony was ended. He wanted to have Slim Girl to himself, at leisure, and to enjoy their own special kind of life once more. So he was impatient and ready to find fault.

===Chinese===
转天一整天，他们都被风雪困住了。如今仪式和仪式后那些拘束既已完结，他急着要回家。他想把瘦姑娘独占下来，从容自在，再享受一回他们两个特有的那种生活。于是他等得不耐烦，动辄就要挑剔。

===Original===
It was a long time since he had been confined in a winter hogan, with its crowded things and people and close-packed smells. Their house at Los Palos was always aired. At T’o Tlakai it still had been warm enough to leave the door unblanketed during the day, and he had spent most of his time in the brush-walled medicine-lodge. He found it too close here, and was made self-conscious by fearing what she might think of it.

===Chinese===
他已经很久没有困守在过冬的霍根屋里了——物挤、人多、气味稠密。他们在洛斯帕洛斯的屋子总是透气的。在特奥·特莱凯，白天还暖和得用不着拿毯子遮门，他的时光大半都消磨在那枝条围壁的医药霍根屋里。他嫌这里太气闷，又心里发窘，生怕她看了这地方不知会怎么想。

===Original===
The modern Navajo diet, boiled mutton and tough bread, tough bread and boiled mutton, a little corn and squash, coffee with not enough sugar, tea as black as coffee, had none of the delicacy of the old ceremonial dishes. He went outside only on rising, when they all rolled in the snow (it had never occurred to him to warn Slim Girl of that custom, but she followed suit without a sign), and again for half an hour to look at his ponies. The thick air inside weighed upon him; he felt dull after a heavy breakfast, and had no more appetite.

===Chinese===
时下纳瓦霍人的吃食——煮羊肉就硬面包，硬面包就煮羊肉，一点玉米和南瓜，糖搁得不够的咖啡，黑得跟咖啡一样的茶——全没有古时仪式菜肴的那份精细。他只在起身时出去一趟，那时一家人都要在雪里打滚（他压根儿不曾想到要提醒瘦姑娘这个习俗，她却毫无异样地照做了），再有就是出去半个钟头看看他的小马。屋里浑浊的空气压在他身上；一顿早饭下肚，他便觉昏沉，再无胃口。

===Original===
Then there were the lice. His wife had rid him of them, conquering his sincere belief that they were a gift from Old Couple in the World Below to enable people to sleep. He had rated that as one of her minor magics. No new ones had got on to him at T’o Tlakai, but in this crowded place they stormed him. He was not used to being bitten, so he was tormented, and he scratched a great deal.

===Chinese===
再说还有虱子。他妻子早替他把它们除尽了，还降服了他一片虔敬的信念——虱子是下界老夫妇（Old Couple in the World Below）赐下来、好叫人睡得着觉的礼物。他一向把这一桩算作她的小法术之一。在特奥·特莱凯不曾有新的虱子上过他的身，可在这挤挤挨挨的地方，虱子朝他大举袭来。他不惯受咬，于是大受其苦，挠个不住。

===Original===
His host asked him naively, “You have many lice, Grandfather?”

===Chinese===
主人天真地问他：“老爷爷，你身上虱子多吗？”

===Original===
He caught his tongue in time, answering, “No, but they nearly froze yesterday. Now they have waked up again, and they are hungry.”

===Chinese===
他及时收住了舌头，答道：“不多，只是昨天它们险些冻死了。这会儿又都醒了，正饿着呢。”

===Original===
Slim Girl gave him a look of approval and sympathy, with a little gesture of scratching furiously at herself. He smiled.

===Chinese===
瘦姑娘朝他投来一记赞许又体恤的眼色，还悄悄做了个小手势，在自己身上狠狠挠了一把。他笑了。

===Original===
The afternoon and early evening were better, for his host recounted the second part of the Coming Up story to his children, the part about the Twin Gods, Slayer of Enemy Gods, and Child of the Waters, which Laughing Boy loved best. He noticed that Slim Girl listened intently. Some day he would be telling his children. It seemed a long time for them not to have had any, but he really did not know very much about these things. It was the woman’s business; the children were hers, after all. She would arrange it in due time, according to her wisdom. He drowsed and was soothed by the tale of the familiar, strange adventures, the gate of the Clashing Rocks, the trail over Boiling Sands, Monster Eagle and Monster Elk and Big God, lightning-arrows and cloud-blankets. After supper the close air drugged him; his eyes were nearly closed as he listened to the last of the myth.

===Chinese===
午后和傍晚初头好过一些，因为主人给他的孩子们讲起上升传说的第二部分，讲孪生神——诛敌神者与水之子（Child of the Waters）——的那部分，那正是笑娃最爱听的。他留意到瘦姑娘听得入神。有朝一日，他也要这样讲给自己的孩子们听。他们成亲这么久还没有孩子，日子似乎显得太长了些，不过这类事情他实在懂得不多。这是女人分内的事；孩子终究是她的。她自会依着她的智慧，到时候一一安排。他昏昏欲睡，被那些既熟稔又奇异的故事抚慰着——撞岩之门，沸沙之上的路，怪鹰、怪麋与大神，闪电之箭与云之毯。晚饭后，窒闷的空气像药力一样迷了他；听到神话末尾时，他的眼睛几乎阖上了。

===Original===
The snowflakes, drifting through the smoke-hole, fell into the fire with little hisses. The even voice went on, telling the end.

===Chinese===
雪片从排烟孔里飘下来，落进火中，嘶嘶地轻响。那平匀的声音还在往下讲，讲到结尾。

===Original===
“Slayer of Enemy Gods came to the Hunger People, they say﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“诛敌神者来到了饥饿之民那里，他们说……”

===Original===
But it was not his dream, there was nothing portentous about this voice. Slim Girl had slain the Hunger People. He smiled and listened, cradled in drowsiness, distantly conscious of a louse biting him, and comforting himself with the thought that tomorrow all that would be attended to, tomorrow they would be home again. These poor people, they could not know. He half-opened his eyes, seeing his wife’s thoughtful, delicate face, and said, as sleepy people will, much louder than he realized,

===Chinese===
可这不是他的梦，这声音里没有任何凶兆。瘦姑娘早已把饥饿之民斩除净尽了。他含笑听着，困倦像摇篮一样摇着他，远远觉出有一只虱子在咬他，又宽慰自己说，明天这一切自会料理妥当，明天他们就又到家了。这些可怜的人，他们无从知晓。他半睁开眼睛，望见妻子那张若有所思的、秀致的脸，像所有犯困的人那样，说得远比他自己觉察的响亮——

===Original===
“*Hasché Lto’i!*”

===Chinese===
“Hasché Lto’i（狩猎女神）！”

===Original===
“What was that, Grandfather?” asked the man.

===Chinese===
“你说什么来着，老爷爷？”那人问。

===Original===
“Nothing.”

===Chinese===
“没什么。”

===Original===
“I thought you said something about Hunting Goddess.”

===Chinese===
“我当你说到狩猎女神的什么来着。”

===Original===
“No, I said ‘*hashké yei itei*,’ the gods are brave.”

===Chinese===
“不，我说的是‘hashké yei itei’，诸神是英勇的。”

===Original===
“Unh! That is well said.”

===Chinese===
“嗯！这话在理。”

===Original===
Slim Girl reflected. Hashché Lto’i was one of the few real goddesses, but she had nothing at all to do with the Coming Up story. He had covered his slip neatly, that man of hers. He was no child. They two would go far, far, under her direction.

===Chinese===
瘦姑娘心里寻思。Hashché Lto’i 本是寥寥可数的几位真女神之一，可她同上升传说毫不相干。他把口误遮掩得干净利落，她这个男人。他可不是个孩子了。他们两个，在她的指引之下，会走得远，远远的。

===Original===
The story-telling ended, and the flakes had ceased falling through the smoke-hole. Tomorrow would be clear. The banked fire became a dull redness, scarcely glowing.

===Chinese===
讲故事结束了，雪片也不再从排烟孔里飘落。明天会放晴。封压的火堆暗成了一团深红，几乎不吐光焰。

### III / III

===Original===
They covered the fifteen miles home at a racing pace, on a morning of clear, brilliant air and dry, fresh snow. They both felt glorious, released from the cramped hogan, glad to be approaching their goal. Though she had no great endurance, Slim Girl rode well, and now, with their ponies prancing in the cold, played tricks and frolicked on horseback as Indian men and women rarely do together. They both yipped and waved their arms; she snatched his hat, and threw it for him to pick from the ground on the run; he swung low under his pony’s neck and sent an arrow skimming ahead of them. Before they reached it, the special quality and privacy of their home reached out to them, and they were almost definitely conscious of reentering their own way of living as though one entered an enclosure.

===Chinese===
这天早晨天清气朗，空气明净，积雪干爽新鲜，他们一路竞驰，赶完了回家的十五英里。两人都觉得美妙极了——从那局促的霍根屋里放出来，又庆幸眼看就要到家。瘦姑娘耐力虽算不得出众，马却骑得好；这会儿两匹小马在寒气里昂首踏步，两人便在马背上耍起花样、嬉闹起来，这是印第安人的男女极少一道玩的。两人都尖声吆喝、挥舞胳膊；她一把抢过他的帽子扔出去，叫他跑着马从地上拾起；他侧身低荡到小马颈子底下，一箭嗖地掠出，送到他们前头去。还没赶到那支箭跟前，他们这个家独有的气韵与幽静已经迎面伸来，两人几乎分明意识到，自己正重新踏进自家那种过活里去，仿佛跨进一座围场。

===Original===
He admired anew the fireplace, with its smokelessness, its draft that set the flame quickly blazing, the heat thrown out from its shallow back. She prepared food while he tended to the ponies. The house became warm, but the air was sweet, the adobe walls and clay floor were clean, and now, with lively appetites, they smelt the good food cooking. She sat back from the fire while things stewed and bubbled. Kneeling beside her, he kissed her﻿—to him perhaps more than anything else the act symbolic of their life apart﻿—and they smiled at each other with grave pleasure. For a minute she was limp in his arms, then she pushed him away with an affectionate, scolding word and returned to a pot that was boiling over. He lay back on the sheepskins, watching. Domesticity, his wife, his home, perfection.

===Chinese===
他再度赞叹起那座火炉——不冒烟，风道一畅，火苗便迅速蹿旺，热力从浅浅的炉膛后壁发散出来。她备饭，他照料小马。屋子暖了起来，空气却是甜净的，土坯的墙、泥墁的地都干净；这时两人胃口正好，闻着饭食烹煮的香味。锅里的东西咕嘟咕嘟炖着滚着，她从火边退坐下来。他跪到她身旁，吻了她——在他心里，这一吻或许比什么都更是他们那离群生活的象征——两人带着庄重的愉悦相视而笑。有一分钟，她软软地瘫在他怀里，随后把他推开，嗔了一句亲昵的骂，回到那口正沸溢的锅边。他仰躺在羊皮上望着。居家的日子，他的妻子，他的家，圆满至极。

===Original===
The loom-frame hung near the door; on the other side was the anvil. The place was permeated with an excited happiness, fullness, completion. Had any religious-minded Navajo, sensitive enough to the reiterated doctrine of *hozoji* to feel it without words, entered that place, he would have felt that he had, indeed, entered the “house of happiness.”

===Chinese===
织机的木架挂在门边；另一侧是铁砧。这地方浸透着一种兴冲冲的幸福、充盈与圆满。倘若有哪个虔敬的纳瓦霍人，对那反复申说的“霍卓吉”义理敏感到不必借言辞便能觉出，走进这地方，他准会觉得，自己当真走进了一座“幸福之屋”。
