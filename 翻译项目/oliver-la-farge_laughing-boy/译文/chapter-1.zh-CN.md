## I / 第一章

### I / I

===Original===
He was riding the hundred miles from T’o Tlakai to Tsé Lani to attend a dance, or rather, for the horse-racing that would come afterwards. The sun was hot and his belly was empty, but life moved in rhythm with his pony loping steadily as an engine down the miles. He was lax in the saddle, leaning back, arm swinging the rope’s end in time to the horse’s lope. His new red headband was a bright colour among the embers of the sun-struck desert, undulating like a moving graph of the pony’s lope, or the music of his song﻿—

===Chinese===
他骑马赶着一百英里的路，从特奥·特莱凯（T’o Tlakai）到特塞拉尼（Tsé Lani）去参加一场舞会——或者不如说，是为着舞会之后的那场赛马。日头毒，肚里空，可生命正随着他的小马合着节拍涌动——那小马像一台机器，平稳地一英里又一英里大步跑下去。他松松地坐在鞍上，身子后仰，一条胳膊甩着绳头，跟马的步子合着拍。他那条崭新的红头带，在被烈日烤成余烬色的荒漠里一点鲜亮，随着小马的跑动一起一伏，像一道描出马步的活动波形线，又像他歌里的乐线——

===Original===
“*Nashdui bik’é dinni, eya-a, eyo-o﻿ ﻿…*
						

						Wildcat’s feet hurt, *eya-a, eyo-o﻿ ﻿…*”

===Chinese===
“Nashdui bik’é dinni，哎呀-啊，哎哟-哦……

						野猫的脚疼啊，哎呀-啊，哎哟-哦……”

===Original===
Rope’s end, shoulders, song, all moved together, and life flowed in one stream. He threw his head back to sing louder, and listened to the echo from the cliffs on his right. He was thinking about a bracelet he should make, with four smooth bars running together, and a turquoise in the middle﻿—if he could get the silver. He wished he could work while riding; everything was so perfect then, like the prayers, *hozoji nashad*, travelling in beauty. His hands, his feet, his head, his insides all were *hozoji*, all were very much alive. He whooped and struck up the Magpie Song till the empty desert resounded﻿—

===Chinese===
绳头、肩膀、歌，全都一起动，生命流成一道。他把头往后一仰，唱得更响，听那歌声从右边的峭壁上撞回的回声。他心里正盘算着要打的一只手镯：四道光滑的银条并排奔流，当中嵌一颗绿松石——只要他弄得到银子。他巴不得能一边骑马一边做活计；那时候样样都那么圆满，就像那些祷词，霍卓吉·纳沙德（hozoji nashad），在美之中行进。他的手、他的脚、他的脑袋、他的五脏六腑，全都霍卓吉，全都活得旺旺的。他高声呼哨，唱起喜鹊歌，唱得空荡荡的沙漠四处回响——

===Original===
“A-a-a-iné, a-a-a-iné,
						

						Ya-a-iné-ainé, ko-ya-ainé﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“啊-啊-啊-伊内，啊-啊-啊-伊内，

						呀-啊-伊内-伊内，阔-呀-伊内……”

===Original===
He was lean, slender, tall, and handsome, Laughing Boy, with a new cheap headband and a borrowed silver belt to make ragged clothes look fine.

===Chinese===
他精瘦，细条，高挑，俊朗，这就是笑娃（Laughing Boy）；一条崭新的廉价头带，一条借来的银腰带，让一身破衣裳显出体面。

===Original===
At noon, having no money, he begged coffee from a trader at Chinlee and went on, treasuring his hunger because of the feasting to come. Now he began to meet Navajos of all ages, riding to the dance. The young men bunched together﻿—a line of jingling bridles, dark, excited faces, flashing silver, turquoise, velveteen shirts, dirty, ragged overalls, a pair of plaid calico leggings, a pair of turkey-red ones. Some of them were heavy with jewelry; Horse Giver’s Son wore over four hundred dollars in silver alone; most of them had more than Laughing Boy. They stopped to look at his bow-guard, which he himself had made.

===Chinese===
正午时分，他身无分文，在钦利（Chinlee）向一个开铺子的讨了些咖啡，又上了路，把饥饿当宝贝似的攒着，因为前头有一场盛宴等着。这会儿他开始遇到各色年纪的纳瓦霍人，都骑马往舞会去。小伙子们扎成堆——一长串叮当作响的笼头，一张张黑亮兴奋的脸，闪闪烁烁的银器、绿松石，平绒衬衫，脏污破烂的工装裤，一条格子印花布的绑腿，一条火鸡红的绑腿。有些人浑身披挂着重饰；赠马人之子（Horse Giver’s Son）单是身上戴的银器就值四百多块；大多数人戴的都比笑娃的多。他们停下来看他腕上的弓护腕（bow-guard），那是他自己亲手做的。

===Original===
“I am a good jeweller,” he said, elated; “I make silver run like a song.”

===Chinese===
“我是个好银匠，”他得意地说；“我打的银子，跑起来像一支歌。”

===Original===
“You should make a song about yourself,” they told him, “and teach the burros to sing it.”

===Chinese===
“你该给自己编一支歌，”他们跟他说，“再教毛驴们去唱。”

===Original===
“Have you had any rain up by T’o Tlakai?”

===Chinese===
“特奥·特莱凯那边落过雨吗？”

===Original===
“No, it is just like last year. It is the devil. The grass is all dried up and the sheep are dying.”

===Chinese===
“没有，跟去年一个样。准是魔鬼作祟。草全枯死了，羊也快死了。”

===Original===
“They had a cloudburst over by T’isya Lani. It washed out the dam.”

===Chinese===
“提斯亚·拉尼（T’isya Lani）那边下过一场骤雨，把水坝冲垮了。”

===Original===
“It washed out the missionary’s house, they say. His wife ran out in something thin and got wet, they say.”

===Chinese===
“还把传教士的房子冲垮了，听说。他老婆穿着件薄衫跑出来，淋了个透，听说。”

===Original===
“*Ei-yei!*”

===Chinese===
“咿-耶！”

===Original===
Tall Hunter and his wife drove past in a brand-new buckboard behind two fast-trotting, grey mules. He owned over five hundred head of horses, and his wife had thick strings of turquoise and coral around her neck.

===Chinese===
高猎人（Tall Hunter）和他老婆赶着一辆崭新的四轮轻便马车过去了，拉车的是两匹快步小跑的灰骡子。他有五百多匹马，他老婆脖子上挂着一串串厚实的绿松石和珊瑚。

===Original===
“His brother is in jail for stealing cattle, they say.”

===Chinese===
“听说他兄弟偷牛，蹲了监狱。”

===Original===
“What is jail?” asked Laughing Boy.

===Chinese===
“监狱是什么？”笑娃问。

===Original===
Slender Hair explained: “It is something the American Chief does to you. He puts you in a room of stone, like a Moqui house, only it is dark and you can’t get out. People die there, they say. They haven’t any room; they can’t see anything, they say. I do not like to talk about it.”

===Chinese===
细发（Slender Hair）解释道：“那是美国酋长（American Chief）用来治你的一样东西。他把人关进一间石头屋子，像莫基人（Moqui）的房子，只是里头黑黢黢的，出不去。人会死在里头，听说。人在里头没有一点空儿；什么都看不见，听说。我不爱说这个。”

===Original===
Laughing Boy thought, I should rather die. He wanted to ask more, but was ashamed to show his ignorance before these southern Navajos, many of whom wore hats like Americans, and who knew so much of Americans’ ways.

===Chinese===
笑娃心想，我宁可死。他还想多问，却羞于在这些南边的纳瓦霍人面前露怯——他们当中好多人都戴着美国人那样的帽子，懂得那么多美国人的门道。

===Original===
They raced. His horse was tired, but it won by a nose, which was just as well, since he had bet his bow-guard. Now he had six dollars. He hoped there would be gambling.

===Chinese===
他们赛了一场。他的马累了，还是以一鼻之差赢了——这也算万幸，因为他把弓护腕押上去打了赌。这下他手里有六块钱了。他盼着有赌局。

===Original===
Tsé Lani showed a distant bonfire in the dusk, with mounted Indians moving in on it like spokes of a wheel. About two hundred young men came together half a mile away, making their ponies prance, exchanging greetings. Crooked Ear carried the ceremonial wand. Now they all lined up, with the dull, red sunset behind their black figures. They started going like getting off to a race, right into a gallop, yelling. Over by the fire was shouting, and another line tearing towards them. The world was full of a roar of hooves and two walls of noise rushing together, the men leaning forward over their horses’ necks, mouths wide. “*E-e-e-e-e!*” They met in a great swirl of plunging, dodging horses, and swept on all together, whooping for dear life, with the staff in front of them, almost onto the fire, then dissolved with jingling of bits, laughter, and casual jokes as they unsaddled by the pool.

===Chinese===
暮色里，特塞拉尼远远现出一堆篝火，骑马的印第安人从四面八方涌向火堆，像车轮的辐条。约莫两百个小伙子在半英里外聚拢，催得小马踏着碎步，彼此招呼。歪耳朵（Crooked Ear）捧着仪杖。这会儿他们全体排成列，黑黢黢的身影背后衬着暗红的落日。他们起跑就像出赛一般，一上来便纵蹄飞奔，齐声呐喊。火堆那边喊声大作，另一列人马朝他们猛冲过来。满世界都是马蹄的轰响，两堵声浪的墙迎头相撞，人人伏在马颈上，嘴张得老大。“咿-咿-咿-咿-咿！”两队人马撞进一处，卷起好大一团人仰马翻、左躲右闪的漩涡，又合成一股冲将过去，豁出命似的呼哨，仪杖在前头开路，险些冲上火堆，随后哗啦散开，笼头嚼子叮当乱响，一片笑声和随口的打趣，在池边卸下了马鞍。

===Original===
The steady motion of excitement was slowed then, in the last of the day, by the rocks and the piñons, by the reflection of the sky in the pool where flat, vague silhouettes of horses stooped to drink. The voices of many people, the twinkling of fires continued the motif, joining the time of quiet with elation past and to come; a little feeling of expectation in Laughing Boy’s chest, a joyful emptiness, part hunger and part excitement.

===Chinese===
随后，在这天将尽的时分，兴奋的奔流被乱石和皮尼翁松放缓了，被池水里映着的天空放缓了——马匹低头饮水，在水面投下扁扁的、影影绰绰的轮廓。众人的语声、火光的明灭延续着这个调子，把已过的欢腾和将来的欢腾缝合进此刻的安静里；笑娃胸口有一点小小的期待，一种快活的空落，一半是饿，一半是兴奋。

===Original===
He tended his pony minutely. The little mare had had two days of loping; shortly he wanted to race her; three days of rest would not be too much. She was his only horse; he had traded two others for her. She was tough, as a horse had to be to live at all in the North country. He ran his hands down her withers, feeling the lean, decisive muscles. In all that section, from Dennihuitso to Biltabito, from T’o Tlikahn to To Baka, where he knew every horse by sight, she was the best, but she would meet some competition here. He felt as if she were his own creation, like the bow-guard; at least he had selected her, as he had chosen the soft blue turquoise in the ornament. Little, compact, all black save for the tiny white spot on her forehead, she had the ugly Roman nose of character. She was like an arrow notched to a taut bowstring﻿—a movement of the hand would release level flight swiftly to a mark.

===Chinese===
他把小马照料得无微不至。这匹小母马已经连着两天大步跑；过不多久他还要让她出赛；歇上三天也不嫌多。她是他仅有的一匹马；是拿另外两匹换来的。她皮实——在北边那地方，一匹马要活命就得这么皮实。他的手顺着她的耆甲往下捋，摸着那精瘦而果断的肌肉。那一带地方，从德内霍佐（Dennihuitso）到比尔塔比托（Biltabito），从特奥·特莱凯（T’o Tlikahn）到托巴卡（To Baka），哪匹马他见了都认得，她是顶好的一匹，只是到了这儿怕是要遇上些对手。他觉得她就像自己造出来的东西，像那只弓护腕；至少她是自己挑中的，就像他从饰物上挑中那颗柔和的蓝绿松石。小小个头，结实的身量，浑身乌黑，只有额心一点白星，长着一只鹰钩鼻，丑，却丑出个性。她像一支扣在绷紧弓弦上的箭——手一动，平直的箭去便迅疾地直奔标的。

===Original===
He was thinking some of these things, half hearing the noises of the people. Just like the prayer, “travelling in beauty.” It would be good to be a singer as well, to express all these things through the prayers. He would like to know many of them, to learn to conduct the Mountain Chant, and know all the beautiful stories behind the songs and ceremonies inside the Dark Circle of Branches. That would be really on the trail of beauty; to work in silver and turquoise, own soft-moving ponies, and lead the Mountain Chant. Just thinking about it was good. It made him feel cool inside.

===Chinese===
他心里转着这些念头，人声嘈嘈，一半进耳一半不闻。就像那祷词，“在美之中行进”。要是还能做个颂唱师（Singer），也很好，借祈祷把这些东西一一唱出来。他想多懂得些，学到能主持山地颂仪（Mountain Chant），把暗枝圈（Dark Circle of Branches）里那些歌与仪式背后的美故事统统知道。那才算真正踏上了美的小径；以银和绿松石做活计，养几匹步子轻柔的小马，再执掌山地颂仪。光是想想就是好的。想得他心里一片清凉。

===Original===
“*Hozho hogan ladin nasha woyen﻿ ﻿…*
						

						In the house of happiness there I wander﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“Hozho hogan ladin nasha woyen……

						在幸福之屋里，我在那里游荡……”

===Original===
All the time he was passing his hand along the pony’s neck, along her back, feeling the lines of tough muscles.

===Chinese===
他的手一路顺着小马的脖颈、顺着她的背抚过去，摸着一道道坚韧的肌肉。

===Original===
“*E-ya,* Grandfather, are you going to dance with the horse?” Jesting Squaw’s Son called over to him, “food is ready.”

===Chinese===
“咿呀，老爷爷，你要搂着马跳舞吗？”打趣婆娘之子（Jesting Squaw’s Son）朝他喊道，“饭好了。”

===Original===
“*Hakone!*” He returned abruptly to the quick-moving life of the dance. “I can eat it. I did not know you were coming.”

===Chinese===
“哈科内！”他猛地回到舞会上那活泛欢腾的日子里。“我吃得下。先前不知道你们也来了。”

===Original===
“I came when I heard you were to race your mare. I think there is money to be made, then, and I want to see her race.”

===Chinese===
“听说你要让母马出赛，我就赶来了。我想这回有钱可赚，也想看看她跑。”

===Original===
They went up arm in arm into the crowd, pushing their way into the circle around one of the fires. Busy housewives gave them coffee, the big pot of meat was passed over, and a flat, round loaf of rubbery, filling bread. The meat was the backbone of a yearling calf, boiled with corn. It was good. He munched joyfully, feeling his empty stomach fill, wadding himself with bread, washing it down with bitter coffee. A couple of Americans carrying their own plates dipped in gingerly. A Hopi, having collected everything he could possibly eat, sat down officiously beside them to air his school English and his bourgeois superiority.

===Chinese===
两人挽着胳膊走进人群，挤进一堆篝火四周的圈子。忙碌的主妇们递给他们咖啡，一大锅肉从头顶传过来，还有一块又圆又扁、筋道管饱、像胶皮似的面包。肉是一岁牛犊的脊骨，跟玉米一块儿煮的。好吃。他嚼得欢畅，觉出空肚子一点点填满，拿面包把自己塞得严严实实，再用苦咖啡往下冲。两个端着自家盘子的美国人小心翼翼地凑进来蘸了蘸。一个霍皮人（Hopi）把能吃得下的东西搜罗了个遍，煞有介事地在他俩旁边坐下，卖弄他的学堂英语和他那小市民的优越感。

### II / II

===Original===
A small drum beating rapidly concentrated the mixed noises into a staccato unison. Young men gathered about the drummer. Laughing Boy might have eaten more, but he left the fire immediately with Jesting Squaw’s Son. Someone led off high-pitched at full voice,

===Chinese===
一面小鼓急促地敲起来，把嘈嘈的声响收拢成一板一眼的齐一。小伙子们围到鼓手身边。笑娃本来还能再吃些，却立刻同打趣婆娘之子离了火堆。有人起了头，尖着嗓门放声唱：

===Original===
“Yo-o galeana, yo-o galeana, yo-o galeana﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“哟-哦，加莱亚纳，哟-哦，加莱亚纳，哟-哦，加莱亚纳……”

===Original===
By the end of the second word the crowd was with him; more young men hurried up to join the diapason,

===Chinese===
唱到第二个词的末尾，人群已经跟上了他；更多小伙子赶上来，汇入那一片洪音，

===Original===
“Galeana ena, galeana eno, yo-o ay-e hena ena﻿ ﻿…”

===Chinese===
“加莱亚纳-埃纳，加莱亚纳-埃诺，哟-哦 啊伊-埃 赫纳-埃纳……”

===Original===
They put their arms over each other’s shoulders, swaying in time to the one drum that ran like a dull, glowing thread through the singing, four hundred young men turning loose everything they had.

===Chinese===
他们胳膊搭着彼此的肩，随着那唯一的一面鼓摇荡；鼓声像一根幽幽发亮的线，从歌声里穿行而过，四百个小伙子把浑身的一切全都放开来。

===Original===
A bonfire twenty feet long flared to the left of them. Opposite, and to the right, the older people sat wrapped in their blankets. Behind them, men crouched in their saddles, heads and shoulders against the night sky, nodding time to the rhythm, silent, with here and there a reflection of firelight on a bit of silver, a dark face, or a horse’s eye.

===Chinese===
一堆二十英尺长的篝火在他们左边熊熊烧着。对面和右边，上了年纪的人裹着毯子坐着。他们身后，男人们蜷在马鞍上，头和肩膀衬着夜空，随节奏一点一点头，一声不响，四下里偶有一星火光，映在一小块银器上、一张黑脸膛上，或是一只马眼睛上。

===Original===
Twelve girls in single file stole into the open space, moving quietly and aloof as though the uproar of singing were petrified into a protective wall before it reached them. Only the pulse of the drum showed in their steps. They prowled back and forth before the line of young men, considering them with predatory judgment.

===Chinese===
十二个姑娘排成一列，悄悄溜进空场，步子又轻又冷，仿佛那鼎沸的歌声还没挨到她们身边，就先凝成了一堵护身的墙。只有鼓的脉动显在她们的脚步里。她们在小伙子们那一列跟前来回逡巡，用掂量猎物的眼光打量他们。

===Original===
Laughing Boy at the back of the crowd looked at them with mild interest; he liked to watch their suave movements and the rich display of blankets and jewelry. One caught his attention; he thought she had on more silver, coral, turquoise, and white shell than he had ever seen on any one person. He speculated on its value﻿—horses﻿—she must have a very rich mother, or uncles. She was too slender, seeming frail to dance in all that rich, heavy ornamentation. He wished she would move more into the firelight. She was well dressed to show off what she wore; silver and stones with soft highlights and deep shadows glowed against the night-blue velveteen of her blouse; oval plaques of silver were at her waist, and ceremonial jewels in the fringe of her sash. Her blue skirt swung with her short, calculated steps, ankle-length, above the dull red leggings and moccasins with silver buttons. The dark clothing, matching the night, was in contrast to the other dancers, even her blanket was mainly blue. He felt animosity towards her, dark and slight, like a wisp of grass﻿—only part of a woman. Her gaze, examining the singers, was too coolly appraising. Now she was looking at him. He threw his head back, losing himself in the singing. He wished he, too, had an American hat.

===Chinese===
