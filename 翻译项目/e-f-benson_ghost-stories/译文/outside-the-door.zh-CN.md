## Outside the Door / 门外

===Original===
The rest of the small party staying with my friend Geoffrey Aldwych in the charming old house which he had lately bought at a little village north of Sheringham on the Norfolk coast had drifted away soon after dinner to bridge and billiards, and Mrs. Aldwych and myself had for the time been left alone in the drawing-room, seated one on each side of a small round table which we had very patiently and unsuccessfully been trying to turn. But such pressure, psychical or physical, as we had put upon it, though of the friendliest and most encouraging nature had not overcome in the smallest degree the very slight inertia which so small an object might have been supposed to possess, and it had remained as fixed as the most constant of the stars. No tremor even had passed through its slight and spindle-like legs. In consequence we had, after a really considerable period of patient endeavour, left it to its wooden repose, and proceeded to theorise about psychical matters instead, with no stupid table to contradict in practice all our ideas on the subject.
This I had added with a certain bitterness born of failure, for if we could not move so insignificant an object, we might as well give up all idea of moving anything. But hardly were the words out of my mouth when there came from the abandoned table, a single peremptory rap, loud and rather startling.

===Chinese===
那日在我朋友杰弗里·奥尔德威奇（Geoffrey Aldwych）不久前于诺福克（Norfolk）海岸舍林汉姆（Sheringham）以北一座小村里买下的那幢迷人老宅中借宿小聚的几位客人，晚饭后不久便三三两两散去了，有的去打桥牌，有的去打台球；奥尔德威奇太太（Mrs. Aldwych）和我一时落了单，留在起居室里，分坐在一张小圆桌的两边——这张桌子，我们一直极有耐心地想把它转动起来，却毫无成效。然而，任凭我们对它施加的是心灵的压力还是物质的压力，纵然这压力再友善、再鼓舞人心，也分毫未能克服这样一件小物件理应仅有的一丁点惯性；它纹丝不动，稳如最恒定的星辰，连它那细长纺锤似的桌腿都不曾颤动一下。于是，经过一段着实不算短的耐心努力之后，我们便由它去继续享受它木质的安息，转而玄谈起心灵的事体来——再没有那张愚蠢的桌子来一一驳回我们在这题目上的种种见解。
我这几句是带着几分因失败而生的苦涩补上的，因为倘若我们连这样一件无足轻重的物件都挪它不动，那索性打消挪动任何东西的念头也罢。可是，话才离口，那张被弃之不顾的桌子便发出了一声短促而不容分说的叩响，又响又颇为吓人。

===Original===
“What’s that?” I asked.
“Only a rap,” said she. “I thought something would happen before long.”
“And do you really think that is a spirit rapping?” I asked.
“Oh dear no. I don’t think it has anything whatever to do with spirits.”
“More perhaps with the very dry weather we have been having. Furniture often cracks like that in the summer.”
Now this, in point of fact, was not quite the case. Neither in summer nor in winter have I heard ever furniture crack as the table had cracked, for the sound, whatever it was, did not at all resemble the husky creak of contracting wood. It was a loud sharp crack like the smart concussion of one hard object with another.

===Chinese===
「那是什么？」我问。
「不过是一声叩响罢了，」她说，「我早就觉得，过不了多久总会有点什么事发生。」
「你当真以为那是亡灵在叩响吗？」我问。
「哎呀，不是的。我一点也不觉得那和亡灵有什么相干。」
「也许跟这段日子天气极干更有些关系。夏天里家具常常那样地发出脆响。」
然而事实上，这话并不大对头。无论冬夏，我都未曾听见过家具发出过方才那桌子那样的脆响，因为那声响——无论它究竟是什么——全然不似木材干缩时那嘶哑的吱嘎声。那是一声又响又尖的脆响，像是一件硬物与另一件硬物清脆地相撞。

===Original===
“No, I don’t think it had much to do with dry weather either,” said she smiling. “I think, if you wish to know, that it was the direct result of our attempt to turn the table. Does that sound nonsense?”
“At present, yes,” said I, “though I have no doubt that if you tried you could make it sound sense. There is, I notice, a certain plausibility about you and your theories—”
“Now you are being merely personal,” she observed.
“For the good motive, to goad you into explanations and enlargements. Please go on.”
“Let us stroll outside, then,” said she, “and sit in the garden, if you are sure you prefer my plausibilities to bridge. It is deliciously warm, and—”
“And the darkness will be more suitable for the propagation of psychical phenomena. As at séances,” said I.
“Oh, there is nothing psychical about my plausibilities,” said she. “The phenomena I mean are purely physical, according to my theory.”

===Chinese===
「不，我想这跟天气干也没什么关系，」她微笑着说，「你要是想知道的话，我以为这正是我们方才试图转动桌子的直接结果。听着像胡说八道吧？」
「眼下嘛，是的，」我说，「不过我毫不怀疑，只要你肯费心，准能把它说得头头是道。我倒注意到，你和你那一套理论颇有几分振振有词的味道——」
「你这可就只剩人身攻击了。」她评论道。
「出于一片好心，意在激你把话讲讲清楚、再作些发挥。请接着说吧。」
「那咱们就出去走走吧，」她说，「到花园里坐坐，要是你确实觉得我这套振振有词的说法比桥牌更对你的胃口的话。外头暖融融的，宜人得很，而且——」
「而且黑暗更利于心灵现象的繁衍滋生。就像降神会上那样。」我说。
「哦，我这套振振有词的说法里可没有什么心灵的东西，」她说，「按我的理论，我所指的那种现象，纯粹是物质性的。」

===Original===
So we wandered out into the transparent half light of multitudinous stars. The last crimson feather of sunset, which had hovered long in the west, had been blown away with the breath of the night wind, and the moon, which would presently rise, had not yet cut the dim horizon of the sea, which lay very quiet, breathing gently in its sleep with stir of whispering ripples. Across the dark velvet of the close-cropped lawn, which stretched seawards from the house, blew a little breeze full of the savour of salt and the freshness of night, with, every now and then, a hint so subtly conveyed as to be scarcely perceptible of its travel across the sleeping fragrance of drowsy garden-beds, over which the white moths hovered seeking their night-honey. The house itself, with its two battlemented towers of Elizabethan times, gleamed with many windows, and we passed out of sight of it, and into the shadow of a box-hedge, clipped into shapes and monstrous fantasies, and found chairs by the striped tent at the top of the sheltered bowling-alley.

===Chinese===
于是我们信步走到屋外，走进那满天繁星之下澄澈的微光里。落日最后一缕绯红的羽翼，原已在西天流连了许久，此刻被夜风的气息吹散了去；眼看就要升起的月亮，尚未划破海面那朦胧的天际线——海面极是宁静，仿佛在睡梦中轻轻呼吸，激起一阵阵低语的涟漪。从宅子向海伸展的那片修剪得短短的草坪，黑天鹅绒似的，一阵微风掠过其上，满带着咸味的海气与夜的清新，间或还极其微妙、几不可察地透出它方才拂过那些沉睡的、芬芳的花圃时所沾染的气息——白色的飞蛾在花圃上方盘旋，寻觅着夜花的蜜。宅子本身，连同它那两座伊丽莎白时代（Elizabethan times）带雉堞的塔楼，在许多扇窗户里闪着光；我们走出它的视线，没入一道黄杨木树篱的阴影里——那树篱被修剪成种种形状与怪诞的奇形怪状；随后，我们在那片有遮挡的保龄球场顶端、一顶条纹帐篷旁找到了椅子。

“And this is all very plausible,” said I. “Theories, if you please, at length, and, if possible, a full length illustration also.”
“By which you mean a ghost-story, or something to that effect?”
“Precisely: and, without presuming to dictate, if possible, firsthand.”
“Oddly enough, I can supply that also,” said she. “So first I will tell you my general theory, and follow it by a story that seems to bear it out. It happened to me, and it happened here.”
“I am sure it will fill the bill,” said I.
She paused a moment while I lit a cigarette, and then began in her very clear, pleasant voice. She has the most lucid voice I know, and to me sitting there in the deep-dyed dusk, the words seemed the very incarnation of clarity, for they dropped into the still quiet of the darkness, undisturbed by impressions conveyed to other senses.

===Chinese===
「这一切都很有几分道理，」我说，「那么，就请你把理论详详细细地讲一讲，如有可能，再附上一幅足尺码的实例作说明。」
「你的意思是要个鬼故事，或者那一类的东西咯？」
「一点不错：而且，恕我不敢僭越地定规矩，如有可能，要亲历亲闻的那种。」
「说来也巧，这个我恰好也供应得起，」她说，「那么，我先把我的一般理论讲给你听，再讲一个似乎恰好能印证它的故事。这事是我亲身经历的，而且就发生在这里。」
「我相信这一定够分量。」我说。
她略停了片刻，等我点上一支烟，随后便以她那极清朗、悦耳的嗓音讲了起来。她的嗓音是我所知最为澄澈的；对坐在那浓重暮色里的我而言，那些字句仿佛便是清明本身的化身，因为它们一滴滴落进黑夜那寂静的静谧之中，不因触及其他感官的印象而有半分搅扰。

===Original===
“We are only just beginning to conjecture,” she said, “how inextricable is the interweaving between mind, soul, life, call it what you will—and the purely material part of the created world. That such interweaving existed has, of course, been known for centuries; doctors, for instance, knew that a cheerful optimistic spirit on the part of their patients conduced towards recovery; that fear, the mere emotion, had a definite effect on the beat of the heart, that anger produced chemical changes in the blood, that anxiety led to indigestion, that under the influence of strong passion a man can do things which in his normal state he is physically incapable of performing. Here we have mind, in a simple and familiar manner, producing changes and effects in tissue, in that which is purely material. By an extension of this—though, indeed, it is scarcely an extension—we may expect to find that mind can have an effect, not only on what we call living tissue, but on dead things, on pieces of wood or stone. At least it is hard to see why that should not be so.”

===Chinese===
「我们才刚刚开始揣测，」她说，「心灵，或灵魂，或生命，随你怎么称呼，与受造世界中纯粹物质的那一部分之间，其相互交织究竟纠缠到何等地步。这种交织的存在，自然早已为人所知有几百年了；譬如医生们就知道，病人若怀有乐观开朗的心境，便有助于康复；恐惧——单是这种情绪——便会对心跳有确定无疑的影响；愤怒会使血液产生化学变化；焦虑会导致消化不良；一个人在强烈激情的驱使下，能做到他在常态下体力所不能为之事。在这里，我们看到心灵以简单而常见的方式，在组织之中、在纯粹物质之中产生了变化与效果。由此再推进一步——其实这几乎算不上什么推进一步——我们大可指望发现：心灵不仅能影响我们所谓活的组织，还能影响死物，影响木石之类。至少，很难看出为什么情形不该如此。」

===Original===
“Table-turning, for instance?” I asked.
“That is one instance of how some force, out of that innumerable cohort of obscure mysterious forces with which we human beings are garrisoned, can pass, as it is constantly doing, into material things. The laws of its passing we do not know; sometimes we wish it to pass and it does not. Just now, for instance, when you and I tried to turn the table, there was some impediment in the path, though I put down that rap which followed as an effect of our efforts. But nothing seems more natural to my mind than that these forces should be transmissible to inanimate things. Of the manner of its passing we know next to nothing, any more than we know the manner of the actual process by which fear accelerates the beating of the heart, but as surely as a Marconi message leaps along the air by no visible or tangible bridge, so through some subtle gateway of the body these forces can march from the citadel of the spirit into material forms, whether that material is a living part of ourselves or that which we choose to call inanimate nature.”

===Chinese===
「比如转桌术（table-turning）之类？」我问。
「这便是其中一例，可见某种力量——出自驻守于我们人体之内那支由晦暗神秘的力量所组成的无数大军——能够怎样地注入物质的事物之中，而且这种注入正时时都在发生。至于它注入所依的法则，我们一无所知；有时我们盼它注入，它却偏偏不肯。比方说方才，你和我试图转动那桌子时，通路之上便有某种阻碍，尽管我把随后那声叩响算作我们一番努力的效应。但在我看，再没有什么比这些力量可以传递到无生命之物上更为顺理成章的了。至于它传递的方式，我们几乎一无所知，一如我们不知恐惧加速心跳这一实际过程的方式究竟若何；然而，正如马可尼（Marconi）的电报凭着一道目不可见、手不可触的桥梁跃过长空，同样地，通过身体某处隐秘的关隘，这些力量也能从心灵的堡垒开拔出去，进驻到物质的形相之中，无论那物质是我们自身有生命的一部分，还是我们愿意称作无生命自然的那些。」

===Original===
She paused a moment.
“Under certain circumstances,” she went on, “it seems that the force which has passed from us into inanimate things can manifest its presence there. The force that passes into a table can show itself in movements or in noises coming from the table. The table has been charged with physical energy. Often and often I have seen a table or a chair move apparently of its own accord, but only when some outpouring of force, animal magnetism—call it what you will—has been received by it. A parallel phenomenon to my mind is exhibited in what we know as haunted houses, houses in which, as a rule, some crime or act of extreme emotion or passion has been committed, and in which some echo or reenactment of the deed is periodically made visible or audible. A murder has been committed, let us say, and the room where it took place is haunted. The figure of the murdered, or less commonly of the murderer, is seen there by sensitives, and cries are heard, or steps run to and fro. The atmosphere has somehow been charged with the scene, and the scene in whole or part repeats itself, though under what laws we do not know, just as a phonograph will repeat, when properly handled, what has been said into it.”

===Chinese===

“Usually I sleep solidly: it is very rarely indeed that I wake, when once I have gone to sleep, before I am called.
