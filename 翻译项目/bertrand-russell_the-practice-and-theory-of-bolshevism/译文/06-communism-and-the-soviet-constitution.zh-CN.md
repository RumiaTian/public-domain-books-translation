## V Communism and the Soviet Constitution / V 共产主义与苏维埃宪法
===Original===

Before I went to Russia I imagined that I was going to see an interesting experiment in a new form of representative government. I did see an interesting experiment, but not in representative government. Everyone who is interested in Bolshevism knows the series of elections, from the village meeting to the All-Russian Soviet, by which the people’s commissaries are supposed to derive their power. We were told that, by the recall, the occupational constituencies, and so on, a new and far more perfect machinery had been devised for ascertaining and registering the popular will. One of the things we hoped to study was the question whether the Soviet system is really superior to Parliamentarism in this respect.
We were not able to make any such study, because the Soviet system is moribund.4 No conceivable system of free election would give majorities to the Communists, either in town or country. Various methods are therefore adopted for giving the victory to Government candidates. In the first place, the voting is by show of hands, so that all who vote against the Government are marked men. In the second place, no candidate who is not a Communist can have any printing done, the printing works being all in the hands of the State. In the third place, he cannot address any meetings, because the halls all belong to the State. The whole of the press is, of course, official; no independent daily is permitted. In spite of all these obstacles, the Mensheviks have succeeded in winning about 40 seats out of 1,500 on the Moscow Soviet, by being known in certain large factories where the electoral campaign could be conducted by word of mouth. They won, in fact, every seat that they contested.
But although the Moscow Soviet is nominally sovereign in Moscow, it is really only a body of electors who choose the executive committee of forty, out of which, in turn, is chosen the Presidium, consisting of nine men who have all the power. The Moscow Soviet, as a whole, meets rarely; the Executive Committee is supposed to meet once a week, but did not meet while we were in Moscow. The Presidium, on the contrary, meets daily. Of course, it is easy for the Government to exercise pressure over the election of the executive committee, and again over the election of the Presidium.

在我前往俄国之前，我曾设想自己将亲眼目睹一场关于代议制政府新形式的有趣实验。我确实目睹了一场有趣的实验，但并非发生在代议制政府领域。每一个对布尔什维主义（Bolshevism）感兴趣的人，都知晓从村级会议到全俄苏维埃（All-Russian Soviet）的一系列选举程序，人民委员们据称正是从这一程序中获得权力的。曾有人告诉我们，通过罢免权、职业选区等等机制，人们已经设计出一种用于查明和记录民众意愿的崭新且更加完善的机制。我们希望研究的课题之一，就是苏维埃制度在这方面是否确实优越于议会主义（Parliamentarism）。

我们未能进行任何此类研究，因为苏维埃制度已经濒临死亡。4 无论在城市还是乡村，任何可以设想的自由选举制度都绝不可能让共产党人获得多数席位。因此，当局采取了各种方法来让政府候选人获胜。首先，投票是通过举手表决进行的，因此所有投票反对政府的人都会被标记出来。其次，任何非共产党员的候选人都不可能印发任何材料，因为印刷厂全部掌握在国家手中。第三，他无法举行任何集会，因为所有礼堂都归国家所有。当然，所有的出版报刊都是官方性质的；没有任何独立的日报被获准发行。尽管存在所有这些障碍，孟什维克依然通过在某些可以通过口耳相传进行竞选宣传的大型工厂中享有知名度，在莫斯科苏维埃（Moscow Soviet）的 1,500 个席位中成功赢得了大约 40 个席位。事实上，凡是他们参与竞选的席位，他们全部获胜了。

然而，尽管莫斯科苏维埃名义上在莫斯科拥有最高权力，但它实际上只是一个选举团，负责选出由四十人组成的执行委员会（Executive Committee），再从执行委员会中选出由掌握全部权力的九人组成的主席团（Presidium）。作为一个整体的莫斯科苏维埃极少召开会议；执行委员会理应每周开会一次，但在我们逗留莫斯科期间从未开过会。相反，主席团每天都举行会议。当然，政府在执行委员会的选举中施加压力是轻而易举的，在主席团的选举中同样如此。

===Original===

It must be remembered that effective protest is impossible, owing to the absolutely complete suppression of free speech and free Press. The result is that the Presidium of the Moscow Soviet consists only of orthodox Communists.
Kamenev, the President of the Moscow Soviet, informed us that the recall is very frequently employed; he said that in Moscow there are, on an average, thirty recalls a month. I asked him what were the principal reasons for the recall, and he mentioned four: drinking, going to the front (and being, therefore, incapable of performing the duties), change of politics on the part of the electors, and failure to make a report to the electors once a fortnight, which all members of the Soviet are expected to do. It is evident that the recall affords opportunities for governmental pressure, but I had no chance of finding out whether it is used for this purpose.
In country districts the method employed is somewhat different. It is impossible to secure that the village Soviet shall consist of Communists, because, as a rule, at any rate in the villages I saw, there are no Communists. But when I asked in the villages how they were represented on the Volost (the next larger area) or the Guberniya, I was met always with the reply that they were not represented at all. I could not verify this, and it is probably an overstatement, but all concurred in the assertion that if they elected a non-Communist representative he could not obtain a pass on the railway and, therefore, could not attend the Volost or Guberniya Soviet. I saw a meeting of the Guberniya Soviet of Saratov. The representation is so arranged that the town workers have an enormous preponderance over the surrounding peasants; but even allowing for this, the proportion of peasants seemed astonishingly small for the centre of a very important agricultural area.
The All-Russian Soviet, which is constitutionally the supreme body, to which the People’s Commissaries are responsible, meets seldom, and has become increasingly formal. Its sole function at present, so far as I could discover, is to ratify, without discussion, previous decisions of the Communist Party on matters (especially concerning foreign policy) upon which the constitution requires its decision.
All real power is in the hands of the Communist Party, who number about 600,000 in a population of about 120 millions.

必须记住的是，由于言论自由和新闻出版自由遭到了彻头彻尾的完全取缔，进行有效的抗议是根本不可能的。结果就是，莫斯科苏维埃的主席团完全由正统的共产党员组成。

莫斯科苏维埃主席加米涅夫（Kamenev）告知我们，罢免权被非常频繁地使用；他说在莫斯科平均每月有三十次罢免。我问他罢免的主要原因是什么，他提到了四点：酗酒、开赴前线（从而无法履行职责）、选民政治立场的改变，以及未能做到每两周向选民汇报一次工作——而这是所有苏维埃成员都必须履行的职责。显而易见，罢免权为政府施加压力提供了机会，但我没有机会查明它是否确实被用于这一目的。

在农村地区，所采用的方法略有不同。确保村苏维埃由共产党员组成是不可能的，因为通常来说——无论如何在我见过的村庄里——根本就没有共产党员。但当我在村庄里询问他们在乡（沃尔斯特，Volost，即上一级行政区划）或省（Guberniya）苏维埃中是如何被代表的时，我得到的回答始终是他们根本没有任何代表。我无法核实这一点，这或许是夸大其词，但所有人都一致断言：如果他们选举了一名非共产党员代表，该代表就无法获得火车通行证，从而无法出席乡或省苏维埃会议。我旁听过一次萨拉托夫（Saratov）省苏维埃的会议。那里的代表名额分配被安排成使城市工人比周边农民占据压倒性优势；但即便考虑到这一点，对于一个极其重要的农业区中心而言，农民代表的比例仍然低得令人吃惊。

全俄苏维埃在宪法上是人民委员向其负责的最高权力机构，但它极少开会，而且已经变得越来越流于形式。据我所知，它目前唯一的职能就是对共产党在宪法要求其做出决定的事项（尤其是涉及外交政策的事项）上先前做出的决定进行不加讨论的追认。

一切真正的权力都掌握在共产党手中，在约 1.2 亿的总人口中，党员人数约为 60 万人（约 600,000 人，总人口约 120,000,000）。

I never came across a Communist by chance: the people whom I met in the streets or in the villages, when I could get into conversation with them, almost invariably said they were of no party. The only other answer I ever had was from some of the peasants, who openly stated that they were Tsarists. It must be said that the peasants’ reasons for disliking the Bolsheviks are very inadequate. It is said﻿—and all I saw confirmed the assertion﻿—that the peasants are better off than they ever were before. I saw no one﻿—man, woman, or child﻿—who looked underfed in the villages. The big landowners are dispossessed, and the peasants have profited. But the towns and the army still need nourishing, and the Government has nothing to give the peasants in return for food except paper, which the peasants resent having to take. It is a singular fact that Tsarist roubles are worth ten times as much as Soviet roubles, and are much commoner in the country. Although they are illegal, pocketbooks full of them are openly displayed in the market places. I do not think it should be inferred that the peasants expect a Tsarist restoration: they are merely actuated by custom and dislike of novelty. They have never heard of the blockade; consequently they cannot understand why the Government is unable to give them the clothes and agricultural implements that they need. Having got their land, and being ignorant of affairs outside their own neighbourhood, they wish their own village to be independent, and would resent the demands of any Government whatever.
Within the Communist Party there are, of course, as always in a bureaucracy, different factions, though hitherto the external pressure has prevented disunion. It seemed to me that the personnel of the bureaucracy could be divided into three classes. There are first the old revolutionists, tested by years of persecution. These men have most of the highest posts. Prison and exile have made them tough and fanatical and rather out of touch with their own country. They are honest men, with a profound belief that Communism will regenerate the world.

我从未偶然碰见过一名共产党员：在街头或村庄里遇到的那些人，每当我能同他们攀谈起来时，他们几乎毫无例外地表示自己不属于任何党派。我得到的唯一其他回答来自一些农民，他们公开宣称自己是沙皇的支持者。必须指出的是，农民讨厌布尔什维克的理由是非常站不住脚的。据说——而且我所见到的一切都证实了这一说法——农民的生活境况比以往任何时候都要好。在村庄里，我没有看到任何一个人——无论是男人、女人还是儿童——看起来营养不良。大地主被剥夺了土地，农民从中获了利。但城市和军队依然需要粮食维系，而政府除了纸币之外没有任何东西可以提供给农民以换取粮食，农民对不得不接受纸币感到愤慨。一个奇特的事实是，沙皇时期的卢布价值是苏维埃卢布的十倍，并且在农村普遍得多。尽管使用沙皇卢布是非法的，但装满这种货币的皮夹却在集市上公开展示。我认为不应当据此推断农民期待沙皇复辟：他们纯粹是受习惯驱使并厌恶新鲜事物。他们从未听说过封锁（blockade）；因此，他们无法理解为什么政府无法向他们提供所急需的衣物和农具。既然已经分得了土地，又对自身邻里之外的事务一无所知，他们便希望自己的村庄能够保持独立，并会抵制任何政府提出的要求。

在共产党内部，正如在任何官僚体制中一样，自然存在着不同的派系，尽管迄今为止外部压力防止了分裂。在我看来，官僚体制的人员可以划分为三个阶层。首先是经受了多年迫害考验的老革命家。这些人占据着绝大多数最高职位。监狱与流放使他们变得坚强、狂热，并且相当脱离本国的实际国情。他们是诚实的人，深信共产主义（Communism）将使世界重获新生。

They think themselves utterly free from sentiment, but, in fact, they are sentimental about Communism and about the regime that they are creating; they cannot face the fact that what they are creating is not complete Communism, and that Communism is anathema to the peasant, who wants his own land and nothing else. They are pitiless in punishing corruption or drunkenness when they find either among officials; but they have built up a system in which the temptations to petty corruption are tremendous, and their own materialistic theory should persuade them that under such a system corruption must be rampant.
The second class in the bureaucracy, among whom are to be found most of the men occupying political posts just below the top, consists of arrivistes, who are enthusiastic Bolsheviks because of the material success of Bolshevism. With them must be reckoned the army of policemen, spies, and secret agents, largely inherited from the Tsarist times, who make their profit out of the fact that no one can live except by breaking the law. This aspect of Bolshevism is exemplified by the Extraordinary Commission, a body practically independent of the Government, possessing its own regiments, who are better fed than the Red Army. This body has the power of imprisoning any man or woman without trial on such charges as speculation or counterrevolutionary activity. It has shot thousands without proper trial, and though now it has nominally lost the power of inflicting the death penalty, it is by no means certain that it has altogether lost it in fact. It has spies everywhere, and ordinary mortals live in terror of it.
The third class in the bureaucracy consists of men who are not ardent Communists, who have rallied to the Government since it has proved itself stable, and who work for it either out of patriotism or because they enjoy the opportunity of developing their ideas freely without the obstacle of traditional institutions. Among this class are to be found men of the type of the successful business man, men with the same sort of ability as is found in the American self-made Trust magnate, but working for success and power, not for money. There is no doubt that the Bolsheviks are successfully solving the problem of enlisting this kind of ability in the public service, without permitting it to amass wealth as it does in capitalist communities.

===Chinese===

他们自认为完全不带感情色彩，但事实上，他们对共产主义以及他们正在创造的政权充满了感伤主义情绪；他们无法正视这样一个事实：他们所创造的并非彻底的共产主义，而且共产主义对那些只想要属于自己的土地而别无所求的农民来说宛如诅咒。当他们在官员中发现贪腐或酗酒行为时，他们会施以毫不留情的惩罚；然而他们建立起来的体制，却滋生着巨大的微小腐败诱惑，他们自己的唯物主义理论本应让他们相信，在这样的体制下腐败必然猖獗泛滥。

官僚体制中的第二个阶层——占据着仅次于最高层政治职位的多数人便属于此类——由投机钻营者（arrivistes）组成，他们之所以成为狂热的布尔什维克，是由于布尔什维主义在物质上取得的成功。与他们归为一类的，还有在很大程度上继承自沙皇时代的大批警察、间谍和特工，他们从“不违法便无法生存”这一事实中牟取私利。布尔什维主义的这一面向由契卡（Cheka，全俄肃反委员会）体现得淋漓尽致，该机构实际上独立于政府，拥有自己的军团，其伙食待遇优于红军（Red Army）。该机构有权以投机倒把或从事反革命（counterrevolutionary）活动等罪名，不经审判便将任何人投入监狱。它未经正当审判就枪决了数以千计的人，尽管如今它名义上已丧失判处死刑的权力，但事实上它是否完全丧失了这一权力则绝不能确定。它的密探遍布各处，普通凡人生活在其恐怖阴影之下。

官僚体制中的第三个阶层由那些并非狂热共产党员的人组成，自政府证明了自身的稳固以来，他们便团结在政府周围，他们为政府效力要么是出于爱国心，要么是因为他们享受能够不受传统制度阻碍、自由施展其抱负的机会。在这一阶层中，可以找到成功商业人士类型的人，他们具备在白手起家的美国托拉斯巨头身上所见的那种能力，但他们为成功和权力劳作，而非为了金钱。毫无疑问，布尔什维克正成功地解决将此类人才吸纳进公共服务中的问题，且不允许他们像在资本主义社会中那样聚敛财富。

This is perhaps their greatest success so far, outside the domain of war. It makes it possible to suppose that, if Russia is allowed to have peace, an amazing industrial development may take place, making Russia a rival of the United States. The Bolsheviks are industrialists in all their aims; they love everything in modern industry except the excessive rewards of the capitalists. And the harsh discipline to which they are subjecting the workers is calculated, if anything can, to give them the habits of industry and honesty which have hitherto been lacking, and the lack of which alone prevents Russia from being one of the foremost industrial countries.

在战争领域之外，这或许是他们迄今为止取得的最大成功。这使得人们有理由设想，如果俄国得以享有和平，可能会出现惊人的工业发展，使俄国成为美国的竞争对手。布尔什维克在他们所有的目标中都是工业主义者；除了资本家过分的回报之外，他们热爱现代工业中的一切。他们正对工人们施加的严酷纪律，如果说还有什么能起作用的话，正是旨在赋予他们迄今为止所欠缺的勤勉与诚信习惯——而唯独这种欠缺阻碍了俄国成为首屈一指的工业国之一。
