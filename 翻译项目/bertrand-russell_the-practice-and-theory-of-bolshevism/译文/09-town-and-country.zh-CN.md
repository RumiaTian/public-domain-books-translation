## VIII Town and Country / VIII 城市与乡村
===Original===

The problem of inducing the peasants to feed the towns is one which Russia shares with Central Europe, and from what one hears Russia has been less unsuccessful than some other countries in dealing with this problem. For the Soviet Government, the problem is mainly concentrated in Moscow and Petrograd; the other towns are not very large, and are mostly in the centre of rich agricultural districts. It is true that in the North even the rural population normally depends upon food from more southerly districts; but the northern population is small. It is commonly said that the problem of feeding Moscow and Petrograd is a transport problem, but I think this is only partially true. There is, of course, a grave deficiency of rolling-stock, especially of locomotives in good repair. But Moscow is surrounded by very good land. In the course of a day’s motoring in the neighbourhood, I saw enough cows to supply milk to the whole child population of Moscow, although what I had come to see was children’s sanatoria, not farms. All kinds of food can be bought in the market at high prices. I travelled over a considerable extent of Russian railways, and saw a fair number of goods trains. For all these reasons, I feel convinced that the share of the transport problem in the food difficulties has been exaggerated. Of course transport plays a larger part in the shortage in Petrograd than in Moscow, because food comes mainly from south of Moscow. In Petrograd, most of the people one sees in the streets show obvious signs of under-feeding. In Moscow, the visible signs are much less frequent, but there is no doubt that under-feeding, though not actual starvation, is nearly universal.
The Government supplies rations to everyone who works in the towns at a very low fixed price. The official theory is that the Government has a monopoly of the food and that the rations are sufficient to sustain life. The fact is that the rations are not sufficient, and that they are only a portion of the food supply of Moscow. Moreover, people complain, I do not know how truly, that the rations are delivered irregularly; some say, about every other day. Under these circumstances, almost everybody, rich or poor, buys food in the market, where it costs about fifty times the fixed Government price. A pound of butter costs about a month’s wages.

促使农民向城市提供粮食的问题，是俄国与中欧共同面临的问题，而据人们所闻，俄国在处理这一问题上比其他某些国家还要少一些失败。对于苏维埃（Soviet）政府而言，这一问题主要集中在莫斯科（Moscow）和彼得格勒（Petrograd）；其他城镇规模都不是很大，且大多位于富饶农业区的中心。诚然，在北部，即便农村人口通常也依赖来自偏南地区的粮食；但北部人口并不多。人们通常说莫斯科和彼得格勒的粮食供应问题是一个运输问题，但我认为这只是部分属实。当然，铁路车辆严重短缺，特别是状况良好的机车更是匮乏。但莫斯科周边环绕着极其优良的土地。在邻近地区乘车游览的一天中，我看到的奶牛足够向莫斯科的全体儿童供应牛奶，尽管我当时去参观的是儿童疗养所而非农场。在集市上可以以高价买到各种食物。我在俄国铁路网上走过了相当长的路程，看到了相当数量的货运列车。鉴于所有这些原因，我深信运输问题在粮食困难中所占的比重被夸大了。当然，与莫斯科相比，运输在彼得格勒的匮乏中扮演了更大的角色，因为粮食主要来自莫斯科以南。在彼得格勒，街上看到的多数人都有明显的营养不良迹象。在莫斯科，这种肉眼可见的迹象要少得多，但毫无疑问的是，营养不良——尽管并非实际上的彻底挨饿——几乎是普遍存在的。

政府以极低的统购统销价格向城市里所有工作的人供应配给粮。官方的理论是政府垄断了粮食供应，而且配给足以维持生命。实际情况是配给并不充足，而且只占莫斯科粮食供应的一部分。此外，人们抱怨配给的发放极不规律，我不知道这有多属实，有人说大约隔天发放一次。在这些情况下，几乎每个人，无论贫富，都在市场上购买食物，而市场上的价格大约是政府固定价格的五十倍。一磅黄油的价格大约相当于一个月的工资。

In order to be able to afford extra food, people adopt various expedients. Some do additional work, at extra rates, after their official day’s work is over. For, though there is supposed to be by law an eight-hours day, extended to ten in certain vital industries, the wage paid for it is not a living wage, and there is nothing to prevent a man from undertaking other work in his spare time. But the usual resource is what is called “speculation,” i.e., buying and selling. Some person formerly rich sells clothes or furniture or jewellery in return for food; the buyer sells again at an enhanced price, and so on through perhaps twenty hands, until a final purchaser is found in some well-to-do peasant or nouveau riche speculator. Again, most people have relations in the country, whom they visit from time to time, bringing back with them great bags of flour. It is illegal for private persons to bring food into Moscow, and the trains are searched; but, by corruption or cunning, experienced people can elude the search. The food market is illegal, and is raided occasionally; but as a rule it is winked at. Thus the attempt to suppress private commerce has resulted in an amount of unprofessional buying and selling which far exceeds what happens in capitalist countries. It takes up a great deal of time that might be more profitably employed; and, being illegal, it places practically the whole population of Moscow at the mercy of the police. Moreover, it depends largely upon the stores of goods belonging to those who were formerly rich, and when these are expended the whole system must collapse, unless industry has meanwhile been reestablished on a sound basis.
It is clear that the state of affairs is unsatisfactory, but, from the Government’s point of view, it is not easy to see what ought to be done. The urban and industrial population is mainly concerned in carrying on the work of government and supplying munitions to the army. These are very necessary tasks, the cost of which ought to be defrayed out of taxation. A moderate tax in kind on the peasants would easily feed Moscow and Petrograd. But the peasants take no interest in war or government. Russia is so vast that invasion of one part does not touch another part; and the peasants are too ignorant to have any national consciousness, such as one takes for granted in England or France or Germany.

为了能够负担额外的食物，人们采取了各种应急手段。有些人会在官方规定的全天工作结束后，从事报酬更高的额外工作。因为尽管法律上规定了八小时工作制（在某些关键行业延长至十小时），但为此支付的工资并不能维持生计，而且没有任何规定阻止一个人在闲暇时间从事其他工作。但最常见的门路是所谓的“投机倒把”，即倒买倒卖。某个从前的富人出售衣物、家具或珠宝以换取食物；买家又以更高的价格转手卖出，如此可能经手二十人之多，直到最终在某个富裕农民或暴发户投机商手中找到买主。此外，大多数人在农村都有亲戚，他们不时前往探望，带回大袋大袋的面粉。私人将粮食带入莫斯科是非法的，火车会遭到搜查；但通过行贿或狡黠，经验丰富的人往往能逃避搜查。食品市场是非法的，偶尔也会遭到查抄；但通常情况下官方对此是睁一只眼闭一只眼的。因此，压制私人商业的尝试反而导致了大量远超资本主义国家发生的非职业买卖。这耗费了大量本可用于更有成效用途的时间；而且由于是非法的，它实际上使整个莫斯科人口都受制于警察的支配。此外，这在很大程度上依赖于旧富人所拥有的物资存量，一旦这些存量耗尽，整个体制便必定崩溃，除非工业在此期间已在健全的基础上重建起来。

显而易见，这种局面是不令人满意的，但从政府的角度来看，很难看清究竟应当做些什么。城市与工业人口主要从事维系政府运转以及为军队提供弹药的工作。这些都是非常必要的任务，其成本理应由税收来承担。向农民征收适度的实物税，本可以轻而易举地养活莫斯科和彼得格勒。但农民对战争或政府毫无兴趣。俄国是如此广袤，以至于入侵某一地区并不会触及另一地区；而且农民过于无知，以至于不具备任何国家意识——而这在英国、法国或德国是被视为理所当然的。

The peasants will not willingly part with a portion of their produce merely for purposes of national defence, but only for the goods they need﻿—clothes, agricultural implements, etc.﻿—which the Government, owing to the war and the blockade, is not in a position to supply.
When the food shortage was at its worst, the Government antagonized the peasants by forced requisitions, carried out with great harshness by the Red Army. This method has been modified, but the peasants still part unwillingly with their food, as is natural in view of the uselessness of paper and the enormously higher prices offered by private buyers.
The food problem is the main cause of popular opposition to the Bolsheviks, yet I cannot see how any popular policy could have been adopted. The Bolsheviks are disliked by the peasants because they take so much food; they are disliked in the towns because they take so little. What the peasants want is what is called free trade, i.e., decontrol of agricultural produce. If this policy were adopted, the towns would be faced by utter starvation, not merely by hunger and hardship. It is an entire misconception to suppose that the peasants cherish any hostility to the Entente. The *Daily News* of July 13th, in an otherwise excellent leading article, speaks of “the growing hatred of the Russian peasant, who is neither a Communist nor a Bolshevik, for the Allies generally and this country in particular.” The typical Russian peasant has never heard of the Allies or of this country; he does not know that there is a blockade; all he knows is that he used to have six cows but the Government reduced him to one for the sake of poorer peasants, and that it takes his corn (except what is needed for his own family) at a very low price. The reasons for these actions do not interest him, since his horizon is bounded by his own village. To a remarkable extent, each village is an independent unit. So long as the Government obtains the food and soldiers that it requires, it does not interfere, and leaves untouched the old village communism, which is extraordinarily unlike Bolshevism and entirely dependent upon a very primitive stage of culture.

农民不会心甘情愿地仅仅为了国防目的就交出一部分农产品，他们只愿意用粮食换取自己所需的商品——衣服、农具等等——而政府由于战争和封锁（blockade），根本无力提供这些物资。

在粮食短缺最为严重的时期，政府通过红军（Red Army）以极其严酷的手段执行余粮强征，从而激化了与农民的矛盾。这种方法后来有所缓和，但农民依然不情愿交出粮食，鉴于纸币的一文不值以及私人买家开出的高得多的价格，这是很自然的。

粮食问题是民众反对布尔什维克的主要原因，然而我看不出有什么顺应民心的政策可行。农民厌恶布尔什维克，是因为他们拿走的粮食太多；城市里厌恶布尔什维克，是因为他们拿进来的粮食太少。农民想要的是所谓的自由贸易，即取消对农产品的统制。如果采纳这项政策，城市面临的将不是仅仅饥饿与困苦，而是彻底的断粮绝户。认为农民对协约国（Entente）怀有任何敌意完全是一种误解。7 月 13 日的《每日新闻》（*Daily News*）在一篇其他方面均属优秀的社论中谈到了“既非共产党员也非布尔什维克的俄国农民，对整个盟国、尤其是对本国日益增长的仇恨”。典型的俄国农民从未听说过盟国或这个国家；他不知道存在封锁这回事；他所知道的一切就是：他过去拥有六头奶牛，但政府为了接济更贫困的农民而将他的奶牛削减到只剩一头，而且政府以极低的价格征收他的粮食（除了他自己一家所需的部分）。这些行为背后的理由他毫无兴趣，因为他的眼界仅限于自己的村庄。在惊人的程度上，每一个村庄都是一个独立的单元。只要政府能够获得其所需的粮食和兵源，它就不加干涉，听任古老的乡村村社共产主义保留原样，这种共产主义与布尔什维主义（Bolshevism）有着天壤之别，且完全依赖于一种非常原始的文化发展阶段。

The Government represents the interests of the urban and industrial population, and is, as it were, encamped amid a peasant nation, with whom its relations are rather diplomatic and military than governmental in the ordinary sense. The economic situation, as in Central Europe, is favourable to the country and unfavourable to the towns. If Russia were governed democratically, according to the will of the majority, the inhabitants of Moscow and Petrograd would die of starvation. As it is, Moscow and Petrograd just manage to live, by having the whole civil and military power of the State devoted to their needs. Russia affords the curious spectacle of a vast and powerful Empire, prosperous at the periphery, but faced with dire want at the centre. Those who have least prosperity have most power; and it is only through their excess of power that they are enabled to live at all. The situation is due at bottom to two facts: that almost the whole industrial energies of the population have had to be devoted to war, and that the peasants do not appreciate the importance of the war or the fact of the blockade.
It is futile to blame the Bolsheviks for an unpleasant and difficult situation which it has been impossible for them to avoid. Their problem is only soluble in one of two ways: by the cessation of the war and the blockade, which would enable them to supply the peasants with the goods they need in exchange for food; or by the gradual development of an independent Russian industry. This latter method would be slow, and would involve terrible hardships, but some of the ablest men in the Government believe it to be possible if peace cannot be achieved. If we force this method upon Russia by the refusal of peace and trade, we shall forfeit the only inducement we can hold out for friendly relations; we shall render the Soviet State unassailable and completely free to pursue the policy of promoting revolution everywhere. But the industrial problem is a large subject, which has been already discussed in Chapter VI.

政府代表着城市和工业人口的利益，它仿佛是扎营在一个农民国度之中，它与农民的关系与其说是通常意义上的政府治理，倒不如说是外交与军事性质的。经济局势如同在中欧一样，有利于乡村而不利于城市。如果俄国按照多数人的意愿实行民主治理，莫斯科和彼得格勒的居民将会饿死。而在目前的情况下，莫斯科和彼得格勒勉强维持生存，依靠的是将国家的全部民政和军事权力用于满足其需求。俄国呈现出一种奇特的景象：一个庞大而强大的帝国，在边缘地带繁荣昌盛，在核心地带却面临着极度的匮乏。那些最不繁荣的人掌握着最大的权力；而唯有通过其过度的权力，他们才能勉强生存下去。这一局面归根结底是由于两个事实：几乎全体居民的全部工业精力都不得不用于战争，以及农民既不理解战争的重要性，也不了解封锁的事实。

为了布尔什维克无法避免的不快和困境而去责难他们，是徒劳无益的。他们的问题唯有两种解决途径：要么停止战争和封锁，从而使他们能够向农民提供换取粮食所需的商品；要么逐步发展出独立的俄国工业。后一种方法将是缓慢的，并会伴随着可怕的艰难困苦，但政府中一些最有才干的人相信，如果无法实现和平，这种方法是可行的。如果我们通过拒绝和平与贸易而将这种方法强加给俄国，我们将丧失我们所能用来维系友好关系的唯一筹码；我们将使苏维埃国家变得坚不可摧，并且能够完全自由地在各地推行输出革命的政策。但工业问题是一个宏大的课题，已在第六章中作了探讨。
