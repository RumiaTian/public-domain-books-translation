## III Lenin, Trotsky and Gorky / III 列宁、托洛茨基与高尔基
===Original===

Soon after my arrival in Moscow I had an hour’s conversation with Lenin in English, which he speaks fairly well. An interpreter was present, but his services were scarcely required. Lenin’s room is very bare; it contains a big desk, some maps on the walls, two bookcases, and one comfortable chair for visitors in addition to two or three hard chairs. It is obvious that he has no love of luxury or even comfort. He is very friendly, and apparently simple, entirely without a trace of hauteur. If one met him without knowing who he was, one would not guess that he is possessed of great power or even that he is in any way eminent. I have never met a personage so destitute of self-importance. He looks at his visitors very closely, and screws up one eye, which seems to increase alarmingly the penetrating power of the other. He laughs a great deal; at first his laugh seems merely friendly and jolly, but gradually I came to feel it rather grim. He is dictatorial, calm, incapable of fear, extraordinarily devoid of self-seeking, an embodied theory. The materialist conception of history, one feels, is his lifeblood. He resembles a professor in his desire to have the theory understood and in his fury with those who misunderstand or disagree, as also in his love of expounding. I got the impression that he despises a great many people and is an intellectual aristocrat.
The first question I asked him was as to how far he recognized the peculiarity of English economic and political conditions? I was anxious to know whether advocacy of violent revolution is an indispensable condition of joining the Third International, although I did not put this question directly because others were asking it officially. His answer was unsatisfactory to me. He admitted that there is little chance of revolution in England now, and that the working man is not yet disgusted with Parliamentary government. But he hopes that this result may be brought about by a Labour Ministry. He thinks that, if Mr. Henderson, for instance, were to become Prime Minister, nothing of importance would be done; organized Labour would then, so he hopes and believes, turn to revolution.

抵达莫斯科（Moscow）后不久，我同列宁（Lenin）用英语进行了一小时的交谈，他的英语讲得相当不错。现场有一名译员，但几乎不需要他发挥作用。列宁的房间陈设非常简朴；除了一张大书桌、墙上的几张地图、两个书柜以及两三把硬木椅子外，只有一把供来客使用的舒适椅子。显而易见，他不喜欢奢华，甚至不在意舒适。他待人十分随和，外表看起来简朴平易，全无半点傲慢神色。如果一个人在不知晓他是谁的情况下遇到他，绝不会猜到他手握巨大权力，甚至不会觉得他有什么显赫之处。我从未见过如此毫无自我膨胀感的大人物。他非常专注地注视着来访者，眯起一只眼睛，这似乎使另一只眼睛的穿透力增强得令人心悸。他经常笑；起初他的笑似乎纯粹是亲切友善、令人愉快的，但渐渐地我开始觉得这种笑相当冷峻。他专断、沉着、不知恐惧为何物，异乎寻常地毫无私心，宛如一种理论的化身。人们能感觉到，唯物史观（历史唯物主义）就是他的生命之源。他在渴望让人理解该理论、对误解或反对该理论之人感到愤怒，以及热衷于阐释理论方面，极像一位大学教授。我得到的印象是，他鄙视许多人，是一个知识上的贵族。

我问他的第一个问题是：他在多大程度上认识到英国经济和政治状况的特殊性？我非常渴望知道，鼓吹暴力革命是否是加入第三国际（the Third International）不可或缺的条件，尽管我并没有直接提出这个问题，因为其他人正在以官方身份询问此事。他的回答并未让我感到满意。他承认，目前在英国爆发革命的机会微乎其微，工人阶级尚未对议会制政府感到彻底厌恶。但他希望这一结果能由工党内阁促成。他认为，例如若亨德森先生（Mr. Henderson）出任首相，将一事无成；于是，有组织的工人阶级——正如他所希望和确信的那样——将转向革命。

===Original===

On this ground, he wishes his supporters in this country to do everything in their power to secure a Labour majority in Parliament; he does not advocate abstention from Parliamentary contests, but participation with a view to making Parliament obviously contemptible. The reasons which make attempts at violent revolution seem to most of us both improbable and undesirable in this country carry no weight with him, and seem to him mere bourgeois prejudices. When I suggested that whatever is possible in England can be achieved without bloodshed, he waved aside the suggestion as fantastic. I got little impression of knowledge or psychological imagination as regards Great Britain. Indeed the whole tendency of Marxianism is against psychological imagination, since it attributes everything in politics to purely material causes.
I asked him next whether he thought it possible to establish Communism firmly and fully in a country containing such a large majority of peasants. He admitted that it was difficult, and laughed over the exchange the peasant is compelled to make, of food for paper; the worthlessness of Russian paper struck him as comic. But he said﻿—what is no doubt true﻿—that things will right themselves when there are goods to offer to the peasant. For this he looks partly to electrification in industry, which, he says, is a technical necessity in Russia, but will take ten years to complete.2 He spoke with enthusiasm, as they all do, of the great scheme for generating electrical power by means of peat. Of course he looks to the raising of the blockade as the only radical cure; but he was not very hopeful of this being achieved thoroughly or permanently except through revolutions in other countries. Peace between Bolshevik Russia and capitalist countries, he said, must always be insecure; the Entente might be led by weariness and mutual dissensions to conclude peace, but he felt convinced that the peace would be of brief duration. I found in him, as in almost all leading Communists, much less eagerness than existed in our delegation for peace and the raising of the blockade. He believes that nothing of real value can be achieved except through world revolution and the abolition of capitalism; I felt that he regarded the resumption of trade with capitalist countries as a mere palliative of doubtful value.

===Chinese===

基于这一理由，他希望本国的支持者竭尽全力争取工党在议会中占据多数；他不主张放弃参与议会竞选，而是主张通过参选使议会显而易见地变得为人所不齿。在大多数人看来使本国暴力革命的尝试既不可能也非所望的种种理由，在他看来毫无分量，仅仅是资产阶级的偏见而已。当我提出在英国任何可能实现的目标都可以通过和平手段实现时，他挥了挥手，将这一提议斥为空想。对于英国，我几乎看不出他有多少了解或心理上的想象力。事实上，马克思主义的整个倾向都是排斥心理想象力的，因为它将政治中的一切都归因于纯粹的物质原因。

接下来我问他，在一个农民占绝大多数的国家，是否有可能牢固而全面地建立共产主义（Communism）。他承认这很困难，并嘲笑了农民被迫以粮食换取纸币的做法；俄国纸币的一文不值在他看来颇具滑稽意味。但他说道——这无疑是事实——当有商品可以提供给农民时，情况就会好转。为此，他部分指望于工业电气化（electrification），他说这在俄国是一项技术上的必然要求，但需要十年时间才能完成。2 他同所有人一样，热情洋溢地谈到了利用泥炭发电的宏伟计划。当然，他将解除封锁（blockade）视为唯一的根治之道；但他对于彻底或持久地实现这一点并不抱太大希望，除非其他国家也爆发革命。他说，布尔什维克俄国与资本主义国家之间的和平注定始终是不牢靠的；协约国（Entente）可能会因疲惫和内部纷争而走向缔和，但他深信这种和平将是短暂的。我在他身上——正如在几乎所有主要的共产党员身上一样——发现他们对于和平与解除封锁的渴望，远不如我们代表团那么迫切。他认为，除了通过世界革命（world revolution）和废除资本主义之外，无法取得任何具有真正价值的成果；我觉得，他将与资本主义国家恢复贸易仅仅视为一种价值存疑的权宜缓和手段。

He described the division between rich and poor peasants, and the Government propaganda among the latter against the former, leading to acts of violence which he seemed to find amusing. He spoke as though the dictatorship over the peasant would have to continue a long time, because of the peasant’s desire for free trade. He said he knew from statistics (what I can well believe) that the peasants have had more to eat these last two years than they ever had before, “and yet they are against us,” he added a little wistfully. I asked him what to reply to critics who say that in the country he has merely created peasant proprietorship, not Communism; he replied that that is not quite the truth, but he did not say what the truth is.3
The last question I asked him was whether resumption of trade with capitalist countries, if it took place, would not create centres of capitalist influence, and make the preservation of Communism more difficult? It had seemed to me that the more ardent Communists might well dread commercial intercourse with the outer world, as leading to an infiltration of heresy, and making the rigidity of the present system almost impossible. I wished to know whether he had such a feeling. He admitted that trade would create difficulties, but said they would be less than those of the war. He said that two years ago neither he nor his colleagues thought they could survive against the hostility of the world. He attributes their survival to the jealousies and divergent interests of the different capitalist nations; also to the power of Bolshevik propaganda. He said the Germans had laughed when the Bolsheviks proposed to combat guns with leaflets, but that the event had proved the leaflets quite as powerful. I do not think he recognizes that the Labour and Socialist parties have had any part in the matter. He does not seem to know that the attitude of British Labour has done a great deal to make a first-class war against Russia impossible, since it has confined the Government to what could be done in a hole-and-corner way, and denied without a too blatant mendacity.
He thoroughly enjoys the attacks of Lord Northcliffe, to whom he wishes to send a medal for Bolshevik propaganda. Accusations of spoliation, he remarked, may shock the bourgeois, but have an opposite effect upon the proletarian.

他描述了富农与贫农之间的分化，以及政府在后者当中针对前者开展的宣传，从而引发了各种暴力行径，这些行径在他看来似乎颇有意思。他谈话的语气表明，由于农民对自由贸易的渴望，对农民的专政将不得不持续很长一段时间。他说根据统计数据得知（这一点我深信不疑），过去两年里农民的口粮比以往任何时候都要充足，“然而他们还是反对我们，”他略带惆怅地补充道。我问他如何反驳那些声称他在农村仅仅创造了农民小土地所有制（peasant proprietorship）而非共产主义的批评者；他回答说那并非完全是实情，但他没有说实情究竟是什么。3

我问他的最后一个问题是：如果与资本主义国家恢复贸易，是否会产生资本主义影响力的中心，从而使维持共产主义变得更加困难？在我看来，更为狂热的共产党员很可能会畏惧同外部世界进行商业交往，因为这会导致异端思想的渗透，并使当前体制的僵化变得几乎难以维系。我想知道他是否有这种感觉。他承认贸易会带来困难，但说这些困难将小于战争带来的困难。他说两年前他和他的同事们从未想过能够在全世界的敌意面前幸存下来。他将他们的幸存归功于各个资本主义国家之间的嫉妒和利益分歧；同时也归功于布尔什维克宣传的威力。他说，当布尔什维克提出用传单对抗大炮时，德国人曾嗤之以鼻，但事实证明传单具有同样的威力。我认为他并没有认识到工党和社会党在此事中发挥过任何作用。他似乎并不知道，英国劳工的态度在很大程度上使得对俄发动一场全面战争成为不可能，因为这种态度迫使政府只能鬼鬼祟祟地行事，并且在不至于过分明目张胆地撒谎的情况下予以否认。

他非常乐于见到诺思克利夫勋爵（Lord Northcliffe）的攻击，甚至想为后者颁发一枚布尔什维克宣传奖章。他评论道，关于掠夺的指控或许会震惊资产阶级，但对无产者却会产生截然相反的效果。

I think if I had met him without knowing who he was, I should not have guessed that he was a great man; he struck me as too opinionated and narrowly orthodox. His strength comes, I imagine, from his honesty, courage, and unwavering faith﻿—religious faith in the Marxian gospel, which takes the place of the Christian martyr’s hopes of Paradise, except that it is less egotistical. He has as little love of liberty as the Christians who suffered under Diocletian, and retaliated when they acquired power. Perhaps love of liberty is incompatible with wholehearted belief in a panacea for all human ills. If so, I cannot but rejoice in the sceptical temper of the Western world. I went to Russia a Communist; but contact with those who have no doubts has intensified a thousandfold my own doubts, not as to Communism in itself, but as to the wisdom of holding a creed so firmly that for its sake men are willing to inflict widespread misery.
Trotsky, whom the Communists do not by any means regard as Lenin’s equal, made more impression upon me from the point of view of intelligence and personality, though not of character. I saw too little of him, however, to have more than a very superficial impression. He has bright eyes, military bearing, lightning intelligence and magnetic personality. He is very good-looking, with admirable wavy hair; one feels he would be irresistible to women. I felt in him a vein of gay good humour, so long as he was not crossed in any way. I thought, perhaps wrongly, that his vanity was even greater than his love of power﻿—the sort of vanity that one associates with an artist or actor. The comparison with Napoleon was forced upon one. But I had no means of estimating the strength of his Communist conviction, which may be very sincere and profound.
An extraordinary contrast to both these men was Gorky, with whom I had a brief interview in Petrograd. He was in bed, apparently very ill and obviously heartbroken. He begged me, in anything I might say about Russia, always to emphasize what Russia has suffered. He supports the Government﻿—as I should do, if I were a Russian﻿—not because he thinks it faultless, but because the possible alternatives are worse. One felt in him a love of the Russian people which makes their present martyrdom almost unbearable, and prevents the fanatical faith by which the pure Marxians are upheld.

我想，如果我在不知晓他是谁的情况下遇到他，绝不会猜到他是一个伟大的人物；他给我的印象是过于固执己见且抱守狭隘的正统。我猜想，他的力量源于他的诚实、勇气和坚定不移的信仰——对马克思主义福音的宗教般信仰，这种信仰取代了基督教殉道者对天堂的期盼，不同之处在于它较少带有自我中心的成分。他对于自由的热爱，正如戴克里先（Diocletian）统治下受迫害、而在掌权后施加报复的基督徒一样微薄。或许对自由的热爱与全心全意相信一种医治人类一切疾苦的万应灵药（panacea）是水火不容的。如果是这样，我不能不对西方世界的怀疑精神感到庆幸。我前往俄国时是一个共产主义者；但与那些毫无怀疑之人接触后，使我自身的怀疑加剧了一千倍——不是针对共产主义本身，而是针对把一种信条坚守到为了它而甘愿让世人遭受广泛苦难的这种做法是否明智。

托洛茨基，共产党员无论如何都不认为他在地位上能与列宁平起平坐，但从智力和个性的角度（尽管不是从品格的角度）来看，他给我留下的印象更为深刻。不过我见到他的时间太短，只留下了非常肤浅的印象。他目光炯炯，带着军人的风度，思维敏捷，具有令人着迷的人格魅力。他仪表堂堂，拥有一头令人赞叹的鬈发；人们会觉得他对女性具有难以抗拒的吸引力。我感觉到他有一种欢快幽默的气质，只要在任何方面都不受到违逆。我认为——也许是错误的——他的虚荣心甚至超过了他对权力的渴望，那是人们通常会与艺术家或演员联系在一起的虚荣心。人们不由自主地会将其与拿破仑（Napoleon）相提并论。但我无法评估他的共产主义信念有多么坚定，这种信念也许是非常真诚且深刻的。

与这两个人形成极其鲜明对比的是高尔基（Gorky），我在彼得格勒与他进行了一次简短的会面。他躺在病榻上，显然病得很重，而且明显心碎欲绝。他恳求我，无论我对俄国发表什么言论，都要始终强调俄国所承受的苦难。他支持政府——正如我如果是俄国人也会支持一样——不是因为他认为政府完美无缺，而是因为可能替代它的选择更为糟糕。人们能感受到他对俄国人民的热爱，这种热爱使得人民当下的苦难几乎令他无法忍受，并阻止了他产生纯粹马克思主义者借以支撑自身的那种狂热信仰。

I felt him the most lovable, and to me the most sympathetic, of all the Russians I saw. I wished for more knowledge of his outlook, but he spoke with difficulty and was constantly interrupted by terrible fits of coughing, so that I could not stay. All the intellectuals whom I met﻿—a class who have suffered terribly﻿—expressed their gratitude to him for what he has done on their behalf. The materialistic conception of history is all very well, but some care for the higher things of civilization is a relief. The Bolsheviks are sometimes said to have done great things for art, but I could not discover that they had done more than preserve something of what existed before. When I questioned one of them on the subject, he grew impatient, and said: “We haven’t time for a new art, any more than for a new religion.” Unavoidably, although the Government favours art as much as it can, the atmosphere is one in which art cannot flourish, because art is anarchic and resistant to organization. Gorky has done all that one man could to preserve the intellectual and artistic life of Russia. I feared that he was dying, and that, perhaps, it was dying too. But he recovered, and I hope it will recover also.

在我见到的所有俄国人中，我觉得他是最可爱、也最令我同情的人。我渴望对他的人生观有更多的了解，但他说话十分困难，并且不断被阵阵剧烈的咳嗽打断，以至于我无法久留。我遇到的所有知识分子——这是一个承受了可怕苦难的阶层——都对他为他们所做的一切表达了感激之情。唯物史观固然不错，但对文明中更高层次事物的些许关照却是一种莫大的宽慰。人们有时说布尔什维克在艺术方面成就卓著，但我未能发现他们除了保存此前已有的一部分成果之外还做过什么。当我向其中一人询问此事时，他变得不耐烦起来，说道：“我们没有闲暇去搞什么新艺术，正如我们没有闲暇去搞什么新宗教一样。”不可避免的是，尽管政府尽可能优待艺术，但整体氛围却是艺术无法繁荣的氛围，因为艺术是崇尚自由且抗拒组织的。高尔基竭尽一人之力，保留了俄国的知识与艺术生命。我曾担心他快要死去了，而艺术生命或许也将随之消逝。但他康复了，我希望艺术生命也能同样复苏。
