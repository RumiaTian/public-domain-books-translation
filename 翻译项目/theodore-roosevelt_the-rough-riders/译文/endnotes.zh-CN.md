4. The total Spanish force in Santiago under General Linares was 6,000: 4,000 regulars, 1,000 volunteers, and 1,000 marines and sailors from the ships. (Diary of the British Consul, Frederick W. Ramsden, entry of July 1st.) Four thousand more troops entered next day. Of the 6,000 troops, 600 or thereabouts were at El Caney, and 900 in the forts at the mouth of the harbor. Lieutenant Tejeiro states that there were 520 men at El Caney, 970 in the forts at the mouth of the harbor, and 3,000 in the lines, not counting the cavalry and civil guard which were in reserve. He certainly very much understates the Spanish force; thus he nowhere accounts for the engineers mentioned on p. 135; and his figures would make the total number of Spanish artillerymen but 32. He excludes the cavalry, the civil guard, and the marines which had been stationed at the Plaza del Toros; yet he later mentions that these marines were brought up, and their commander, Bustamente, severely wounded; he states that the cavalry advanced to cover the retreat of the infantry, and I myself saw the cavalry come forward, for the most part dismounted, when the Spaniards attempted a forward movement late in the afternoon, and we shot many of their horses; while later I saw and conversed with officers and men of the civil guard who had been wounded at the same time﻿—this in connection with returning them their wives and children, after the latter had fled from the city. Although the engineers are excluded, Lieutenant Tejeiro mentions that their colonel, as well as the colonel of the artillery, was wounded. Four thousand five hundred is surely an understatement of the forces which resisted the attack of the forces under Wheeler. Lieutenant Tejeiro is very careless in his figures. Thus in one place he states that the position of San Juan was held by two companies comprising 250 soldiers. Later he says it was held by three companies, whose strength he puts at 300﻿—thus making them average 100 instead of 125 men apiece. He then mentions another echelon of two companies, so situated as to cross their fire with the others. Doubtless the blockhouse and trenches at Fort San Juan proper were only held by three or four hundred men; they were taken by the Sixth and Sixteenth Infantry under Hawkins’s immediate command; and they formed but one point in the line of hills, trenches, ranch-houses, and blockhouses which the Spaniards held, and from which we drove them. When the city capitulated later, over 8,000 unwounded troops and over 16,000 rifles and carbines were surrendered; by that time the marines and sailors had of course gone, and the volunteers had disbanded.
 In all these figures I have taken merely the statements from the Spanish side. I am inclined to think the actual numbers were much greater than those here given. Lieutenant Wiley, in his book *In Cuba with Shafter*, which is practically an official statement, states that nearly 11,000 Spanish troops were surrendered; and this is the number given by the Spaniards themselves in the remarkable letter the captured soldiers addressed to General Shafter, which Wiley quotes in full. Lieutenant Tejeiro, in his chap. XIV, explains that the volunteers had disbanded before the end came, and the marines and sailors had of course gone, while nearly a thousand men had been killed or captured or had died of wounds and disease, so that there must have been at least 14,000 all told. Subtracting the reinforcements who arrived on the 2nd, this would mean about 10,000 Spaniards present on the 1st; in which case Kent and Wheeler were opposed by at least equal numbers.
 In dealing with the Spanish losses, Lieutenant Tejeiro contradicts himself. He puts their total loss on this day at 593, including 94 killed, 121 missing, and 2 prisoners﻿—217 in all. Yet he states that of the 520 men at Caney but 80 got back, the remaining 440 being killed, captured, or missing. When we captured the city we found in the hospitals over 2,000 seriously wounded and sick Spaniards; on making inquiries, I found that over a third were wounded. From these facts I feel that it is safe to put down the total Spanish loss in battle as at least 1,200, of whom over a thousand were killed and wounded.
 Lieutenant Tejeiro, while rightly claiming credit for the courage shown by the Spaniards, also praises the courage and resolution of the Americans, saying that they fought, “*con un arrojo y una decision verdaderamente admirables*.” He dwells repeatedly upon the determination with which our troops kept charging though themselves unprotected by cover. As for the Spanish troops, all who fought them that day will most freely admit the courage they showed. At El Caney, where they were nearly hemmed in, they made a most desperate defence; at San Juan the way to retreat was open, and so, though they were seven times as numerous, they fought with less desperation, but still very gallantly. ↩︎

4. 利纳雷斯将军（General Linares）麾下驻圣地亚哥的西班牙军队总计 6,000 人：正规军 4,000 人，义勇军 1,000 人，另有来自各舰的海军陆战队和水兵 1,000 人。（录自英国领事弗雷德里克·W·拉姆斯登〔Frederick W. Ramsden〕日记，7 月 1 日条。）次日又有 4,000 名援军开入。这 6,000 人中，约有 600 人在埃尔卡内，900 人在港湾入口处的炮台里。特赫伊罗中尉称，埃尔卡内有 520 人，港湾入口炮台有 970 人，防线之内有 3,000 人，且不计留作预备队的骑兵和治安警卫队。他无疑大大低报了西军兵力：比如他对第 135 页提到的工兵处处未作交代；照他的数字算来，西班牙炮兵总共不过 32 人。他把骑兵、治安警卫队和原先驻扎斗牛场（Plaza del Toros）的海军陆战队一概排除在外，可他后来又提到，这些陆战队被调了上来，其指挥官布斯塔门特（Bustamente）身负重伤；他写道，骑兵曾前出掩护步兵撤退，而西班牙人那天傍晚试图向前推进时，我亲眼看见他们的骑兵开了上来——大多是下了马的——我们还打死了他们的许多马匹；再后来，我又见到并同当时一并负伤的治安警卫队军官和士兵谈过话﻿——那是在他们的妻小逃出城后，我把这些妻小送还给他们时的事。工兵虽被排除在外，特赫伊罗中尉却又提到，工兵上校同炮兵上校一样负了伤。四千五百这个数字，对抵抗惠勒所部进攻的西军兵力而言，无疑仍是少报。特赫伊罗中尉的数字漫不经心：他在一处说圣胡安阵地由两个连共 250 名士兵据守，随后又改称由三个连据守，兵力记作 300﻿——这么一来每连平均 100 人，而非 125 人。接着他又提到另一梯队的两个连，位置恰与其他各连构成交叉火力。毫无疑问，圣胡安堡本堡的碉堡与堑壕仅由三四百人据守；它们是霍金斯（Hawkins）亲率第六和第十六步兵团攻取的；而且它们不过是西班牙人所据守的那条由丘陵、堑壕、牧场屋舍与碉堡构成的防线上的一个点，我们把西军从整条防线上逐了出去。后来城池投降时，交出的未负伤部队逾 8,000 人，步枪和卡宾枪逾 16,000 支；到那时，海军陆战队和水兵当然早已撤走，义勇军也已解散。
以上这些数字，我一概取自西班牙一方的说法。我倾向于认为，实际数目远大于此处所列。怀利中尉（Lieutenant Wiley）在其著作《随沙夫特在古巴》（*In Cuba with Shafter*）——此书实际上近乎一份官方陈述——中说，投降的西班牙军队将近 11,000 人；被俘士兵致沙夫特将军的那封引人瞩目的信里，西班牙人自己给出的也是这个数字，怀利将该信全文引录。特赫伊罗中尉在其书第十四章解释说，义勇军在战事结束前业已解散，海军陆战队和水兵当然早已撤走，另有近千人阵亡、被俘或死于创伤与疾病，如此算来，西军总数当不少于 14,000 人。扣除 2 日开到的援军，这意味着 1 日当天在场的西班牙人约有 10,000 名；果真如此，肯特和惠勒当面之敌至少与他们兵力相当。
在西班牙人的伤亡数字上，特赫伊罗中尉自相矛盾。他把西军当日总损失记作 593 人，包括阵亡 94 人、失踪 121 人、被俘 2 人﻿——合计 217 人。可他又说，埃尔卡内的 520 人中仅有 80 人生还，其余 440 人或死或俘，或下落不明。我们攻陷城池后，在各医院里发现重伤和患病的西班牙人逾 2,000 名；一经查问，我发现其中超过三分之一是伤员。由这些事实看来，我认为把西班牙人的战斗总损失记作至少 1,200 人是稳妥的，其中死伤合计逾千。
特赫伊罗中尉在为西班牙人的勇烈正当争誉之余，也称许美国人的胆气与果决，说他们以「一种真正令人叹服的勇猛与决绝」（*con un arrojo y una decision verdaderamente admirables*）作战。他反复致意于我军自身毫无掩体护身、却仍冲锋不辍的那股决绝。至于西班牙军队，凡当天与之交手的人，无不乐于承认他们展现的勇气。在埃尔卡内，他们几乎被团团围住，作了极为殊死的抵抗；在圣胡安，退路敞开，因此尽管他们人多七倍，作战便少了那股拼死之劲，但仍十分英勇。 ↩︎
