## Introduction / 导言

===Original===
Though it is quite possible, and indeed probable, that the Epinician Odes do not represent Pindar’s highest achievement in the realm of pure poetry, the student of history has every reason to be thankful that, of all his works, it is these that have escaped the ruin that has overtaken the rest. For they furnish us, perhaps more fully and more convincingly than any other remains of antiquity, with a solution of a problem which has troubled historians. Some of these have exclaimed against what they consider the narrow, shortsighted, parochial spirit of the Greeks generally, in failing to see that their material prosperity, their safety and true independence would be best secured, if not by frankly enrolling themselves under the hegemony of one strong state (preferably Athens), at least by a close federation as binding on each member as that of the United States of America. Still more writers have denounced what they consider the unpatriotic selfishness of the aristocratical party in the several states, their unscrupulous plottings and alliances with the enemies of their respective cities, and, when they gained the upper hand, their ruthless treatment of the democracies.

Does not Pindar furnish us with the key to this jealous isolation, by showing us how each city cherished a belief in its divine origin, how the first parent of each state, or its founder, was a divine being, whose very name it perpetuated—for in Pindar the name of the goddess-founder and that of the state are interchangeable—Thebe, Aegina, Cyrene; or how its founders were demigods who settled there in obedience to divine commands? To subordinate this heavenly guardianship to other powers might well seem sacrilege. And the tradition of the sacredness of an independence thus hallowed, when once established, might well survive the age of unquestioning faith.

For the families of the aristocracy, to whom a thousand memories called far more powerfully and insistently than to any other order of nobility in the world’s history, there were incentives to political exclusiveness whose cogency we cannot appreciate unless we take the Olympian hierarchy as seriously as Pindar did. The chronique scandaleuse of Olympus, which shocked Plato, inspired Pindar. While repudiating belief in anything derogatory to the Gods, he does not consider their amours with mortal women (or even their paederasty) in that light. The men whose praises he sang had in their veins the blood of Gods: their human ancestors were half divine, and their achievements were worthy of their high descent. The sons of the great houses whose lineage was from Heracles, Aeacus, Perseus, counted themselves of different clay from the common herd: they recognised in their hearts that they owed no duty to a democratic constitution: they never ceased to chafe under the yoke of equality with beings whom they held scarce fit to be their servants, and to intrigue against a system which placed their personal liberty at the mercy of the caprice of the “base,” and allowed their wealth to be exploited for purposes with which they had no sympathy. No wonder that they accepted with complacency the poet’s digressions into the old heroic myths that to us are but fairy tales: for them these were unassailable fact. Their records in song were the charter of their superiority to the world around them, of their right divine to govern their fellow-men.

尽管完全可能、甚至颇有可能，《竞技凯歌》（Epinician Odes）并不代表品达（Pindar）在纯粹诗歌王国中的最高成就，治史者却极有理由庆幸：在他全部的著作之中，得以免遭殃及其余诸作的覆亡之厄的，正是这批颂歌。因为它们为我们解答了一个久已困扰史家的难题，其详备与令人信服的程度，或许超过了古代遗存中的任何其他文献。有些史家曾痛斥他们所认为的希腊人普遍具有的狭隘、短视、偏守一隅的精神：希腊人竟未能看出，纵使不以坦然自附于某一强邦霸权之下的方式（最好是雅典〔Athens〕），至少也当以一个如美利坚合众国那般对各成员具有同等约束力的紧密联邦，来最稳妥地保障他们的物质繁荣、安全与真正的独立。更有为数更多的论者抨击他们所认为的各邦贵族党派不忠于邦国的自私自利：其肆无忌惮的阴谋密划、与各自城邦之敌的结盟，以及一旦得势之后对民主政体的残酷蹂躏。

品达岂非正为我们提供了理解这种猜忌的孤立状态的钥匙？他向我们昭示：每一城邦如何珍藏着自身源出神明的信仰；各邦的始祖——亦即其建邦者——如何是一位神祇，邦国永世奉守其名——因为在品达笔下，女神建邦者之名与城邦之名可以互代——忒拜（Thebe）、埃伊纳（Aegina）、昔兰尼（Cyrene）；或者，建邦者如何是奉神命而卜居其地的半神。将这种上天的护佑屈置于其他权力之下，很可能被视为渎神之举。而如此被神圣化的独立，其神圣性之传统一经确立，纵使在无所疑虑的信仰时代逝去之后，也极可能绵延不绝。

对于贵族世家而言——较之世界历史上任何其他贵族等级，千百种记忆对他们的召唤都更为有力、更为急切——政治上的排他自守自有其动因；除非我们像品达那样郑重看待奥林匹斯（Olympian）诸神的等序体系，否则便无从领会这些动因的效力。奥林匹斯山上那部令柏拉图（Plato）为之震骇的“丑闻编年史”（chronique scandaleuse），却使品达灵感泉涌。他虽然摈斥任何有损诸神的说法，却并不以那种眼光看待诸神与凡间女子的情爱（甚至他们对少年的眷爱）。他所歌颂的人们，血管中奔流着神明的血液：他们的人类祖先是半神，他们的功业亦无愧于高贵的世系。家世溯自赫拉克勒斯（Heracles）、埃阿科斯（Aeacus）、珀尔修斯（Perseus）的豪门子弟，自视与凡俗之辈非同一抔泥土所塑：他们心中明白，自己对民主政体不欠丝毫义务；与那些被他们看作用作仆役都嫌不配之辈同处平等，这副轭下他们从无一日不愤懑难平；而对于那种使其个人自由任凭“贱民”反复无常之心摆布、又使其财富被榨取以供他们毫无同感之用途的制度，他们的阴谋倾轧也从未止息。难怪他们心满意足地领受诗人旁骛于古老英雄神话的闲笔——那些神话在我们不过是童话，在他们却是无可攻讦的事实。这些载诸歌诗的功业记录，便是他们对周遭世界之优越地位的特许状，便是他们治理同胞之“神授权利”的特许状。

It must also be remembered that these highborn men were superior to the lower classes not only in pride of lineage, but they bore about with them the witness to this in their bodily development. The aristocrat was a stronger man, and far more skilled in the use of that strength for personal encounters than the average democrat. He was a man of leisure, and we may say that practically all such men made it their aim, their daily practice, to perfect their physical condition in the gymnasiums which were in every town. In many states, perhaps in all which belonged to the Peloponnesian League, a small organized body of aristocrats kept the far more numerous commonalty in subjection, solely by their fighting superiority.1 We must not lose sight of the transcendent importance of bodily vigour in an age when all fighting was hand-to-hand, and where the numbers on each side were so small that a few abnormally strong men might, by breaking the enemies’ line, decide the issue. Each successful champion in the great athletic contests means a large number of men who went through a course of training in which they were wrought up to the highest pitch of perfection not only of muscular development, but also of pluck and endurance.

Among reasons which have been assigned for the discursiveness of these triumphal odes, in which a very small space is given to the victor, and none to the details of the contest, perhaps the most important was this: excessive praise was universally regarded as mischievous to its objects, as tending directly to provoke the jealousy of the Gods, and to invite their nemesis. In the mouth of an enemy it was malicious, tactless in that of a friend. Hence the praise in an ode was distributed widely: the bard celebrated the champion, his family, former victors, the city or island of his birth, his ancestors, the ancient heroes of the land, so that the *φθόνος* might fall heavily on none, especially as all success was ascribed to the Gods, to whom the competitors sacrificed before engaging in the contest. A religious halo surrounded the Great Games. “Not unto us” is the burden of more than one ode, in which success is expressly attributed to the help of a God, as Poseidon, and often to the Graces.

A feature of the Games which strikes us moderns unpleasantly is the absence among the Greeks of what we call the sporting spirit. While no praise was too high, no reward too splendid for the victor, his unsuccessful competitors neither expected nor obtained any sympathy even from their fellow-townsmen. Even the victor was sometimes assailed by envious disparagement, especially where a state was rent by political factions; while the vanquished had to hide his head from the storm of derision which greeted his failure.

还须记住，这些出身高贵的人不仅在门第的自负上优于下层阶级，而且他们体格的发育本身便是随身携带的优越性之明证。贵族是更强壮的人，而且把这种膂力用于近身搏斗时，其娴熟程度远非寻常平民可比。他是有闲之人；我们可以说，这类人几乎无一不把在每座城镇皆设的体育馆（gymnasium）中完善体魄奉为目标与日课。在许多城邦——或许在隶属于伯罗奔尼撒同盟（Peloponnesian League）的全部城邦——一小支组织起来的贵族武装，单凭其战斗上的优势，便使人数远为众多的平民大众俯首臣服。1 我们切不可无视体魄之雄健在那种时代的超凡意义：其时一切战斗皆是白刃相接，双方人数又如此之少，以致数名异乎寻常的壮士便可能冲破敌阵而决出胜负。大赛之中每产生一名成功的优胜者，便意味着有大批的人经历过一整套训练；在那样的训练中，他们不仅肌肉之发达，连同胆气与坚忍，都被砥砺至尽善的极诣。

在这些庆捷颂歌之所以枝蔓旁骛——留给优胜者的篇幅极少，于竞赛的详情则绝口不提——人们曾举出种种缘由，其中最重要的或许是这样一条：过分的赞美被普遍认为有害于受赞之人，易于直接招致诸神的嫉恨，并引来它们的报应。此语出于仇敌之口便是恶毒，出于朋友之口便是冒昧失言。因此，颂歌中的赞美便广泛铺排：歌者既颂优胜者本人，亦颂其家族、昔日的优胜者、他诞生的城邦或岛屿、他的列祖列宗、故土的上古英雄，好让*φθόνος*（嫉恨）不致沉重地压到任何一人头上——尤其因为一切成功皆归于诸神，而竞技者赴赛之前都要向诸神献祭。神圣的光晕环绕着大赛会。“非归于我们”是不止一首颂歌的叠唱题旨：诗中把成功明白归之于某位神明的佑助——如波塞冬（Poseidon）——且时常归之于美惠女神（the Graces）。

赛会中有一特征令我们今人殊觉不快，那便是希腊人缺少我们所谓的“体育精神”（sporting spirit）。对优胜者而言，再高的赞誉不为过，再隆重的奖赏不为奢；而他那些失利的竞争者，纵在本邦同胞之间，也既不曾指望、亦不曾得到半点同情。甚至优胜者本人有时也会遭到出于嫉妒的贬损之词的攻讦，尤其在邦国为政治党争所撕裂之处；而落败者面对其失败所招致的讥嘲风暴，唯有掩首敛迹而已。
