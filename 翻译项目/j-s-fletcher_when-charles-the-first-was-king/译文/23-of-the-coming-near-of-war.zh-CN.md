## XXIII Of the Coming Near of War / 二十三 战争的逼近
===Original===

Even as I had expected, Philip Lisle consented willingly to give up his Rose to my keeping. From our first time of meeting there had been a kind of fellow-feeling ’twixt him and me, and we had grown to love each other in more than ordinary friendliness, so that he felt that I was to be trusted with his daughter’s happiness. But neither he nor my mother would hear aught of marriage yet awhile. Better to wait, said they, even were it ten years, until the land was settled, than to marry in the midst of so much anxiety and trouble. For at any time, said Philip, the tide of war might surge round us with flame and bloodshed, and things might occur which would be harder to cope with married than single. Better to wait until the land was at rest and we could marry with peace all around us.
So now there was a double inducement to me to long for the ending of the war, and I looked forward to the coming of peace with a hot impatience. But peace was not to come upon us for many a long day yet. With the advent of spring in that year, 1643, the rival armies began manoeuvring once more, and both sides became active in soliciting help and assistance. The King had taken up his headquarters at Oxford in readiness for the campaign, and had there gathered his army around him. Thither, ere spring was over, repaired Philip Lisle and Jack, having remained but a short time with us, and from them, at irregular intervals, came news of the war. At first their news was not over good. The King lost Reading. But then came tidings of success. The royal army overcame its opponents at Roundway Down and laid siege to Bristol. By the end of June that great city was in the King’s hands, and the counties of Devon, Somerset, Hampshire, and Wiltshire came under his power. The Parliamentarians began to lose heart. Hampden, one of their principal leaders, had been slain in a skirmish. The royal troops laid siege to Gloucester and seemed likely to take it. Everything looked rosy for the King’s cause, and loyal people began to pluck up a good spirit. There was some serious talk amongst the Parliamentarians of a treaty, which no doubt would have been carried out but for the pressure put upon the Commons by the London crowds, who swarmed round the House and refused to have any treaty. Then, however, came reverses to the King’s cause. The Earl of Essex raised the siege of Gloucester.

不出我所料，菲利普·利尔（Philip Lisle）慨然应允，把他的罗斯交给我照管。自我们初次见面起，他同我之间就有一种知已之情，情谊早已超出寻常的朋友，所以他放心把女儿的幸福托付给我。可婚姻一事，他同母亲此刻都听不得半个字。他们说，等吧——哪怕等十年——等这国度安定了再办，也强似在这样忧患多事的时候成亲。菲利普说，战火随时可能裹着烈焰与血泊席卷到我们头上；有些事，独身应付得来，成了家反倒难了。等国土安宁、四下太平，再办喜事不迟。
于是我盼战争早日结束，又添了一重理由，怀着灼热的焦盼等待和平。可和平还迟迟不来。1643年春天一到，两军重又开始调动，双方都四处求援。国王把大营设在了牛津（Oxford），备战迎敌，大军已聚在身边。春天还没过完，菲利普·利尔同杰克便辞别我们，赶赴那里去了；此后，隔三差五地，便有战地消息传来。起初消息不算好：国王丢了雷丁。随后传来捷报：王军在朗道唐击溃敌军，围攻布里斯托尔。到六月底，那座大城已落入国王之手，德文、萨默塞特、汉普郡、威尔特郡诸郡也归了他的势力。议会党人心开始涣散：他们的主将之一汉普登，在一场小接战中阵亡。王军围困格洛斯特，眼看就要拿下。国王的事业处处玫瑰色，忠王的人心也壮了起来。议会党人一度认真议起和约——若不是伦敦民众围住下院、坚决不允，和约多半就成了。可接着，国王一方连遭败绩：埃塞克斯伯爵解了格洛斯特之围。

Turning away from that city, he found the Royalist army barring his path at Newbury. A battle followed which neither side could claim. Essex continued his march. If it had not been for the fine powers of the Parliamentary infantry he would have suffered defeat, but the foot had been finely drilled and officered and withstood the fiery impulse of the Royalist charge with rocklike firmness. In this battle Falkland fell, weary, men said, of the war and its horrors. So matters stood after the battle of Newbury, each side being pretty much as it was before the campaign began.
But meanwhile there were other matters at work, as we heard through various channels. On the first day of July in that year the Synod known as the Westminster Assembly began to sit. It had two objects﻿—to make England Presbyterian, and to bring the Scots to the help of the Parliamentary army. Perhaps the latter was the real object, as Parson Drumbleforth said, and the former the means whereby it was to be achieved. For with the Scots it was a case of no Presbyterianism, no Scottish army. Then came the making of that famous matter the Solemn League and Covenant, which was signed by members of Parliament who thereby bound themselves to bring the religion of England, Scotland, and Ireland into conformity. This Solemn League and Covenant became everywhere the test of faithfulness to the Parliamentary cause. Its effect began to be seen at once. The fierce, persecuting spirit of the Presbyterian broke out on every side. Anything that savoured of Episcopacy was ruthlessly destroyed or mutilated. The Cross at Cheapside was broken to pieces, and a similar fate soon overtook Charing Cross. The images, crucifixes, and altars of the ancient parish churches were destroyed; the painted windows were broken, and the spirit of wanton mischief seemed to be let loose throughout the land. The Earl of Manchester went to Cambridge and drove out from the University all who refused to fall in with the Covenant. There had been much talk about the persecution of the King, but under the new regime men were not even permitted to think for themselves. Anything more intolerant of other men’s opinions than the Presbyterianism of that day there could not be.
Now, however, rose up a new party, which combated the cruel intolerance of the Presbyterians, and finally vanquished it.

他离开那城，却见保王军在新伯里拦住去路。随之而来的一仗，双方谁也夸不得口。埃塞克斯继续行军。若不是议会军的步兵争气，他几乎吃了败仗；那支步兵操练精良、军官得力，抵住保王军冲锋的烈焰，稳如磐石。就在这一仗里，福克兰阵亡了——人们说，他早已厌倦了这场战争同它的惨酷。新伯里一战之后，形势大致如故：双方同开战前差不多，谁也没占大便宜。
但与此同时，还有别的事在酝酿，我们从各种渠道听到了。那年7月1日，人称威斯敏斯特会议的宗教会议开会了。它有两个目标：使英格兰长老会化；把苏格兰人拉来援助议会军。兴许后者才是真目的——德拉姆尔福思牧师就这么说——前者只是达成的手段：对苏格兰人，是不长老会，就不出兵。接着便炮制出了那桩著名的玩意儿，「誓约同盟与圣约」，议员们纷纷签署，以此自缚，要把英格兰、苏格兰、爱尔兰三国的宗教归于一律。这誓约同盟与圣约，随即处处成了检验是否忠于议会事业的试金石。效果立刻显了出来：长老会派那凶暴的、迫害的性子四处发作。凡带一点圣公会气味的东西，都被横加捣毁或残损。奇普赛德的十字架被砸成碎片，查令十字随即遭了同样的命运。古老教区教堂里的圣像、十字架和祭坛被毁，彩绘玻璃被砸，肆意的破坏之魂仿佛在全国放了闸。曼彻斯特伯爵进了剑桥，把不肯附和圣约的人一律逐出大学。从前总听说国王怎样迫害人；如今在新规矩底下，人们连自行思考都不被允许。世上再没有比那个年代的长老会更不容异见的了。
然而这时兴起了一个新的派别，同长老会的残暴不宽容相抗，并最终战胜了它。

===Original===

The Independents, as they were called, were for liberty of conscience. They wanted it for themselves, and what they desired for themselves they were just enough to wish others to enjoy. They pleaded for full liberty for all men, Turks, Papists, Socinians, as for themselves. The Puritans, iron-bound and rigid in their love of mere outward uniformity, regarded this new doctrine with horror. They wished to keep the three kingdoms under their own rule, and to stamp out every other form of worship than their own Presbyterianism. They cried out loudly at the teachings of the Independents, and complained that such a dangerous doctrine as liberty of conscience should have arisen from a body which in a sense formed part of themselves. But the doctrine of liberty steadily gained ground. It was not probable that the gloomy religion of Scotland would ever be forced upon the English people, and men who saw their parish churches mutilated and destroyed by the fanatics of the League and Covenant, gained from what they saw an unconquerable aversion to the system which it was attempted to force upon them.
Now, at this time there were really three forces fighting for the supremacy in England﻿—the King, the Presbyterians, and the Independents, and men who had eyes to see and ears to hear looked on at the three-cornered contest with eager hearts. The Parliamentary leaders on their side watched it with more than ordinary anxiety, for the new force, the Independents, lay in their own army under the command of that extraordinary farmer of Huntingdon, Oliver Cromwell. Whatever were this man’s faults, and whatever his virtues﻿—and both have been doubtless exaggerated by his friends and enemies﻿—there was one thing about him in which he was great above all Englishmen, namely, his resolute determination not to allow the Presbyterians to stamp out national life and thought in this land which is our own. How he and his Ironsides put the Presbyterian power aside, and indirectly made for the restoration of the English spirit you will see as this history advances. Already Cromwell was becoming a power in the land. The counties of Norfolk, Suffolk, Essex, Cambridge, Huntingdonshire, and Hertfordshire had at his instigation bound themselves together under the name of the Eastern Association.

===Chinese===

这派人叫独立派，主张良心自由。他们自己要这个自由；他们要给自己的，也公道地愿别人同享。他们为所有人——土耳其人、教皇派、索齐尼派，同为他们自己——争取完全的自由。清教徒们呢，死板僵硬，只爱外表的划一，把这套新道理看作洪水猛兽。他们要把三个王国攥在自己手心，把自家长老会之外的一切礼拜形式统统扑灭。他们大声嚷嚷独立派的学说，抱怨这样危险的思想——良心自由——竟会从某种意义上出自他们自身内部的一群人里。可自由的学说稳步得势。苏格兰那阴郁的宗教，本就不可能强加给英格兰人民；亲眼看着自己教区的教堂被圣约的狂热分子们砸烂的人们，从所见所闻中，对那套要强塞给他们的体制生出了无法折服的厌恶。
此时此刻，英格兰其实有三股力量在争霸：国王、长老会、独立派；有眼看的、有耳听的人，都提着一颗心旁观这场三角争斗。议会派的领袖们更是格外焦虑地注视着它，因为那股新力量——独立派——就藏在他们自己的军队里，领军的是亨廷登那位非同寻常的农夫，奥利弗·克伦威尔（Oliver Cromwell）。这个人无论有怎样的过失、怎样的美德——两者无疑都被他的朋友和敌人各自夸大了——有一样他是全英格兰无人能及的伟大之处：他铁了心，绝不容长老会把民族的生命与思想从这个我们自己的国度里抹杀。他和他的铁骑军怎样把长老会的势力报开，又怎样间接为英格兰精神的复兴铺了路，读下去自会看到。这时的克伦威尔，已渐成国中一股势力。诺福克、萨福克、埃塞克斯、剑桥、亨廷登、赫特福德诸郡，在他的鼓动下结成一体，号称东部联盟。

In August, 1643, the Earl of Manchester took command of the associated forces, Cromwell being really his guiding spirit. Then within these counties Royalism was put down, and after beating the Royalist forces at Winceby on October 11, Cromwell held himself in readiness to attack the King’s army in Yorkshire.
But in Yorkshire at that time men were for the King, and the Royalist forces were largely spread over the county. The Marquis of Newcastle had raised the siege of Hull, and had succeeded in establishing a fair degree of power in many important districts and centres. In Yorkshire, Sir Thomas Fairfax, commanding the Parliamentary troops, was making a hard struggle to retain his ground, and was watching such towns as Leeds and Bradford, while at the same time he kept an eye on the fortified places, like Pontefract and Knaresborough. Little by little, however, Fairfax was being driven back, when an event happened which brought matters to a crisis. The Solemn League and Covenant was signed. A committee of Englishmen and Scotchmen were appointed to superintend the operations of the army. These arrangements duly carried out, the Scotch forces under the command of Leslie, Earl of Leven, crossed the border and advanced into Yorkshire. In June, 1644, Leven joined his forces to those of Manchester and Fairfax, and laid siege to the Marquis of Newcastle at York.
So now at last the red fire of war was close upon us, for the rival armies were, so to speak, but a stone’s-throw away. All day long we heard messages of some sort; now that the Scots were fifty thousand strong and would infallibly sweep away the King and all his army; now that the Royalist troops were on the march northwards to relieve Newcastle at York; and now that ere four-and-twenty hours had passed we should hear the booming of the rival artillery. But we heard no news from Philip or Jack; nay, we had no news of them ’twixt January and June in that year 1644, so that we were oft anxious about their welfare and wondered what had become of them.

1643年8月，曼彻斯特伯爵统领联盟各军，克伦威尔实际是他背后的主心骨。此后，保王势力在这几郡被压了下去；10月11日在温斯比击败保王军之后，克伦威尔厉兵秣马，准备攻打国王在约克郡的军队。
可那时的约克郡，人心向着国王，保王的兵力遍布全郡。纽卡斯尔侯爵解了赫尔之围，在许多重要地区和重镇站稳了脚跟。在约克郡，托马斯·费尔法克斯爵士（Sir Thomas Fairfax）统率议会军苦苦支撑，一面盯着利兹、布拉德福德这样的城镇，一面还提防着庞蒂弗拉克特（Pontefract）、纳尔斯伯勒这些设防之地。然而费尔法克斯步步被逼后退，正在此时，一件大事把局面推到了关口：誓约同盟与圣约签署了。英苏两方合组了一个委员会，督办军事。诸事办妥，苏格兰大军便在利文伯爵莱斯利统率下越过边界，推进到约克郡。1644年6月，利文同曼彻斯特、费尔法克斯的军队会师，围攻纽卡斯尔侯爵于约克（York）城。
至此，战争的赤焰终于逼到了我们眼前：两军相隔，可说只有一石之遥。成天价各种消息灌进耳朵：一会儿说苏格兰人有五万之众，必定把国王连同他的大军扫荡干净；一会儿说保王军正挥师北上，去救被困约克的纽卡斯尔；一会儿说不出二十四小时，我们就能听见两边大炮的对轰。可菲利普同杰克音信全无——不，从那年一月到六月，我们没得到他们半点消息，常为他们悬心，不知他们到底怎样了。

Those months indeed were remarkably trying ones to all of us, for my mother was anxious and afraid of the war coming our way, and Rose was troubled about her father, and Lucy fretted lest the enemy should shoot Ben in any of their skirmishes round Pontefract, which they seemed likely to besiege; and I was impatient and fretful, wishing that they would make an end of the matter, so that I might marry my dear one and live in peace on my land. For it seemed a long time to wait for her, and I was more impatient every day, and wished Roundhead and Cavalier could adjust their differences more speedily than they seemed desirous of doing.
But perhaps none of us were so anxious and troubled at this time as worthy Ben Tuckett, who, by reason of his residence in Pontefract marketplace, did hear much news on both sides of the question. For Ben never by word or deed let either party see that he favoured or disfavoured them, but talked little and listened much, saying in excuse that it is a deal better to be seen than to be heard, and that silence is pure gold, while speech is but ordinary silver. Nevertheless, whatever Ben heard he turned over in his mind with no little diligence, never forgetting anything nor letting any little matter slip. He mixed freely with men of both parties, each side curiously enough believing him to be one of themselves, and in this way he got to hear matters which ordinary folk did not hear of. But Master Ben’s love of knowledge ere long brought him into much trouble and perplexity of mind, for he presently found out that the Parliamentary troops would certainly besiege the Castle, which was now strongly garrisoned and equipped by the Royalists under Colonel Lowther. This news was disquieting to a peaceable and quiet tradesman like Ben, for he could not help foreseeing there would be bullets and cannonballs flying about the town, and sallies and skirmishes going on, and it occurred to him that a rough-handed soldiery might not be very nice about taking goods out of his shop without paying for what they took. What with thoughts like this and fears lest a stray bullet should some day find a billet in his brain as he stood at his shop-door, poor Ben grew careworn and anxious-looking, and lost a good deal of his sleek appearance.

那几个月，对我们全家实在难熬：母亲忧心忡忡，怕战火烧到家门；罗斯为父亲牵肠挂肚；露西惦着本，生怕议会军在庞蒂弗拉克特——看样子是非围它不可了——的零星冲突里伤了她的本。我呢，又急又躁，巴不得他们早点把事情了结，好叫我娶了我的亲人，在自家地上安生度日。等她等得实在太久，我一天比一天心焦，只愿圆颅党同保王党把分歧早些摆平，别像他们眼下这样不紧不慢。
不过，那时候，只怕谁都不如可敬的本·塔基特焦愁：他住在庞蒂弗拉克特集市广场上，两头的消息听得多。本呢，言行上从不让任何一方看出他向着谁、不向着谁，只是少说多听，还自有说辞：被人看见，胜过被人听见；沉默是纯金，言语不过是寻常的银子。可本听来的每一件事，都在心里翻来覆去掂量，勤勉得很，一件不忘，一点不漏。他在两党人中廷混，说来也怪，两边都把他当自己人；就这样，许多平常人听不到的事，都进了他的耳朵。可是本小主人这份求知的爱好，不久便给他惹来大烦恼：他探听明白，议会军必围城堡无疑——此时城堡已由劳瑟上校率保王军重兵驻守、器械齐全。这消息对这样一个安分守己的买卖人，实在报忧：他不由得预见到，子弹炮弹要在镇里乱飞，突围和零战不断；又想到大兵的手脚粗，从他铺子里拿了东西，未必肯照价付钱——兴许分文不付。想着这些，又怕哪天往店门口一站，一颗流弹正好在他脑壳里安家，可怜的本愁容满面，先前养出的那身膘也掉了不少。

But he never abated one jot of his curiosity about coming events, and whenever he came over to Dale’s Field he had always some fresh scrap of news for us, gleaned from Royalist or Parliamentarian. And whatever it was, there was always one burden to Ben’s song concerning it﻿—the war was coming upon us.

可是他对时局的好奇心，一点没有诚少；每逢来戴尔田庄，总能从保王党或议会党那里，给我们捎来几样新闻。而无论新闻是什么，本唱来唱去总归是那一个调门：战争就要压到我们头上来了。
