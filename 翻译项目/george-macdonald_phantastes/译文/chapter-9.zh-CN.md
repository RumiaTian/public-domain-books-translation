## IX / 第九章

===Original===

===Original===
From this time, until I arrived at the palace of Fairy Land, I can attempt no consecutive account of my wanderings and adventures. Everything, henceforward, existed for me in its relation to my attendant. What influence he exercised upon everything into contact with which I was brought, may be understood from a few detached instances. To begin with this very day on which he first joined me: after I had walked heartlessly along for two or three hours, I was very weary, and lay down to rest in a most delightful part of the forest, carpeted with wildflowers. I lay for half an hour in a dull repose, and then got up to pursue my way. The flowers on the spot where I had lain were crushed to the earth: but I saw that they would soon lift their heads and rejoice again in the sun and air. Not so those on which my shadow had lain. The very outline of it could be traced in the withered lifeless grass, and the scorched and shriveled flowers which stood there, dead, and hopeless of any resurrection. I shuddered, and hastened away with sad forebodings.

===Chinese===
从这时起，直到我抵达仙境的仙宫，我已无法再把我的漫游与历险作连贯的记述了。从此以后，一切事物之于我，都只在与我那位随从的关系中存在。他对我所接触到的一切施加着怎样的影响，从几件孤立的实例中便可以明白了。就从他初来附随的这一天说起：我意兴索然地走了两三个钟头之后，已疲乏之极，便在林中最令人愉悦的一段躺下歇息，那里满地铺着野花。我木然地静卧了半个钟头，随后起身赶路。我躺过之处，花儿都被压伏在地；但我看得出，它们很快便会重新抬头，再度在阳光与空气里欢欣。我的影子躺过之处，却全然不是这样。影子的轮廓竟在枯死无生的草叶间历历可辨，那里立着的花都焦黑皱缩——死了，对任何复活都不再抱希望。我打了个寒噤，怀着悲哀的预感匆匆离去。

===Original===
In a few days, I had reason to dread an extension of its baleful influences from the fact, that it was no longer confined to one position in regard to myself. Hitherto, when seized with an irresistible desire to look on my evil demon (which longing would unaccountably seize me at any moment, returning at longer or shorter intervals, sometimes every minute), I had to turn my head backwards, and look over my shoulder; in which position, as long as I could retain it, I was fascinated. But one day, having come out on a clear grassy hill, which commanded a glorious prospect, though of what I cannot now tell, my shadow moved round, and came in front of me. And, presently, a new manifestation increased my distress. For it began to coruscate, and shoot out on all sides a radiation of dim shadow. These rays of gloom issued from the central shadow as from a black sun, lengthening and shortening with continual change. But wherever a ray struck, that part of earth, or sea, or sky, became void, and desert, and sad to my heart. On this, the first development of its new power, one ray shot out beyond the rest, seeming to lengthen infinitely, until it smote the great sun on the face, which withered and darkened beneath the blow. I turned away and went on. The shadow retreated to its former position; and when I looked again, it had drawn in all its spears of darkness, and followed like a dog at my heels.

===Chinese===
不出几日，我便有理由惧怕它那祸殃的影响扩张开来了，因为事实是：相对于我，它已不再固守一个位置。在此之前，每当我被一种不可抗拒的欲望攫住，要看一看我那邪恶的魔怪（这渴望会在任何时刻莫名其妙地攫住我，间隔或长或短地反复来袭，有时一分钟一次），我只得把头扭向后方，越过肩头去看；只要还能保持这姿势，我便被摄住，目不能移。可是有一天，我走上一座明净的青草山冈，冈上视野壮丽——虽则壮丽在何处，如今已说不上来——我的影子却转动过来，到了我的面前。不多时，一种新的显形又加深了我的痛苦。因为它开始迸闪，向四面八方射出幽影的辐射。这些幽暗的光线从中央那影子发出，宛如出自一轮黑太阳，忽长忽短，变化不歇。但光线击中何处，何处的那一片地、那一片海、那一片天，便化作虚空，化作荒漠，令我心中悲凉。在这新威力的首次施展之中，有一缕光线射得比其余都远，仿佛无限伸长，直到击中大太阳的面门；太阳在这击打之下枯萎、晦暗了。我转身走开，继续赶路。影子退回原位；我再回望时，它已把所有黑暗的长矛尽数收回，像狗一样跟在我的脚跟。

===Original===
Once, as I passed by a cottage, there came out a lovely fairy child, with two wondrous toys, one in each hand. The one was the tube through which the fairy-gifted poet looks when he beholds the same thing everywhere; the other that through which he looks when he combines into new forms of loveliness those images of beauty which his own choice has gathered from all regions wherein he has traveled. Round the child’s head was an aureole of emanating rays. As I looked at him in wonder and delight, round crept from behind me the something dark, and the child stood in my shadow. Straightway he was a commonplace boy, with a rough broad-brimmed straw hat, through which brim the sun shone from behind. The toys he carried were a multiplying-glass and a kaleidoscope. I sighed and departed.

One evening, as a great silent flood of western gold flowed through an avenue in the woods, down the stream, just as when I saw him first, came the sad knight, riding on his chestnut steed. But his armor did not shine half so red as when I saw him first. Many a blow of mighty sword and axe, turned aside by the strength of his mail, and glancing adown the surface, had swept from its path the fretted rust, and the glorious steel had answered the kindly blow with the thanks of returning light. These streaks and spots made his armor look like the floor of a forest in the sunlight. His forehead was higher than before, for the contracting wrinkles were nearly gone; and the sadness that remained on his face was the sadness of a dewy summer twilight, not that of a frosty autumn morn. He, too, had met the Alder-maiden as I, but he had plunged into the torrent of mighty deeds, and the stain was nearly washed away. No shadow followed him. He had not entered the dark house; he had not had time to open the closet door. “Will he ever look in?” I said to myself. “*Must* his shadow find him some day?” But I could not answer my own questions.

===Chinese===
有一次，我经过一座小屋，一个可爱的仙童走了出来，两手各执一件奇妙的玩具。一件是那种镜筒，受仙赐的诗人透过它观看时，处处看见的都是同一事物；另一件则是他透过它观看时，能把自己亲手拣选、采自行历诸方的种种美的意象，组合成新的可爱的形相。孩子的头上环绕着一圈放射的光轮。我正又惊又喜地看他，那黑黢黢的一团从我背后悄悄绕了过来，孩子便立在了我的影子里。转眼之间，他成了一个寻常的男孩，戴着一顶粗糙的宽檐草帽，阳光从背后透过帽檐照在他身上。他手中的玩具，成了一具多重镜和一只万花筒。我叹息一声，走开了。

一天傍晚，正当一大股金色的洪流自西方静静涌过林间一条夹道，顺着那流光，一如我初见他时，悲哀的骑士骑着他的栗色骏马来了。但他甲胄的红亮，已不及我初见他时的一半。巨剑与巨斧的多少次斫击，都被他锁子甲的坚固所偏转，滑掠过甲面，沿途扫去了蚀蚀的锈迹；那光华的精钢便以重返的光辉，答谢这番善意的击打。这些条纹与光斑，使他的甲胄看上去宛如阳光筛落的林间地面。他的额头比先前开阔了，因为聚拢的皱纹几乎消失；他脸上残存的悲哀，是凝露的夏日暮色的悲哀，而非降霜的秋日清晨的悲哀。他也同我一样遇见过赤杨树少女，但他已纵身投入壮举的激流，那污痕几乎洗净了。没有影子跟随他。他不曾走进那黑暗的小屋；他也不曾来得及去开那壁橱的门。“他日后会不会朝里张望呢？”我自忖道。“他的影子就*必定*会在某一天找到他吗？”可我无法回答自己的问题。

===Original===
We traveled together for two days, and I began to love him. It was plain that he suspected my story in some degree; and I saw him once or twice looking curiously and anxiously at my attendant gloom, which all this time had remained very obsequiously behind me; but I offered no explanation, and he asked none. Shame at my neglect of his warning, and a horror which shrunk from even alluding to its cause, kept me silent; till, on the evening of the second day, some noble words from my companion roused all my heart; and I was at the point of falling on his neck, and telling him the whole story; seeking, if not for helpful advice, for of that I was hopeless, yet for the comfort of sympathy—when round slid the shadow and inwrapt my friend; and I could not trust him. The glory of his brow vanished; the light of his eye grew cold; and I held my peace. The next morning we parted.

But the most dreadful thing of all was, that I now began to feel something like satisfaction in the presence of the shadow. I began to be rather vain of my attendant, saying to myself, “In a land like this, with so many illusions everywhere, I need his aid to disenchant the things around me. He does away with all appearances, and shows me things in their true color and form. And I am not one to be fooled with the vanities of the common crowd. I will not see beauty where there is none. I will dare to behold things as they are. And if I live in a waste instead of a paradise, I will live knowing where I live.” But of this a certain exercise of his power which soon followed quite cured me, turning my feelings towards him once more into loathing and distrust. It was thus:—

===Chinese===
我们同行了两天，我开始爱他。显然，他多少已猜到我的遭遇；我有一两次看见他好奇而不安地望着缀在我身后的那团幽暗——它这一路始终极其恭顺地随在我背后；但我不作解释，他也未问。我因辜负了他的警告而羞惭，又怀着一种连起因都不愿触及的恐怖，因此缄默不言；直到第二天傍晚，同伴的几句高贵言辞唤醒了我整个的心；我几乎要扑上去搂住他的脖颈，把整段故事向他倾吐；所求的纵然不是有益的忠告——那我是绝望的——至少也是同情的慰藉——就在此刻，影子滑转过来，把我的朋友裹了进去；我再也信不过他了。他额上的光辉隐没了；他眼中的光芒冷了下来；我便沉默不语。次日清晨，我们分手了。

而最可怕的事情是：如今我开始在这影子的随伴里感到一种近似满足的东西。我对这位随从竟渐渐颇为自负，自言自语道：“在这样的地方，四周幻象重重，我需要他的襄助，来解除周围事物的魔咒。他把一切表象尽数除去，把事物的真色真相显给我看。而我绝非那种肯被俗众的浮华愚弄的人。无美之处，我不肯看见美。我敢于直视事物的本相。倘若我住在一片荒漠而非乐园里，我也要明明白白地知道自己住在何处。”但这念头，很快便被他威力的某一次施展治得烟消云散，使我对他的情感重新化作憎恶与猜疑。事情是这样：—

===Original===
One bright noon, a little maiden joined me, coming through the wood in a direction at right angles to my path. She came along singing and dancing, happy as a child, though she seemed almost a woman. In her hands—now in one, now in another—she carried a small globe, bright and clear as the purest crystal. This seemed at once her plaything and her greatest treasure. At one moment, you would have thought her utterly careless of it, and at another, overwhelmed with anxiety for its safety. But I believe she was taking care of it all the time, perhaps not least when least occupied about it. She stopped by me with a smile, and bade me good day with the sweetest voice. I felt a wonderful liking to the child—for she produced on me more the impression of a child, though my understanding told me differently. We talked a little, and then walked on together in the direction I had been pursuing. I asked her about the globe she carried, but getting no definite answer, I held out my hand to take it. She drew back, and said, but smiling almost invitingly the while, “You must not touch it;”—then, after a moment’s pause—“Or if you do, it must be very gently.” I touched it with a finger. A slight vibratory motion arose in it, accompanied, or perhaps manifested, by a faint sweet sound. I touched it again, and the sound increased. I touched it the third time: a tiny torrent of harmony rolled out of the little globe. She would not let me touch it any more.

一个晴朗的正午，一个小姑娘来与我同行，她穿过树林而来，来的方向与我的路径恰成直角。她一路又唱又跳，快乐得像个小孩子，虽然她看上去几乎已是个成年女子。她手里——忽而这只手，忽而那只手——拿着一颗小小的球，明净澄澈，宛如最纯的水晶。这球仿佛既是她的玩物，又是她最珍视的宝物。有一刻你会以为她对它全然漫不经心，另一刻又见她为它的安危忧心忡忡。但我相信她无时无刻不在照管着它，也许在她最不着意的时候，照管得最紧。她含笑在我身旁停下，用最甜美的嗓音向我道了声日安。我对这孩子生出一种奇妙的喜爱——因为她给我的印象更像孩子，尽管我的理知告诉我并非如此。我们谈了片刻，随后便一道朝我原本行进的方向走去。我问她所拿的这球的来历，却得不到确定的回答，我便伸出手去接它。她缩身退让，同时脸上却带着近乎引人相邀的笑意，说道：“你不可以碰它；”——顿了一顿——“不过你若要碰，也得轻轻地。”我用一根手指碰了碰它。球内泛起一丝微颤的动，伴着——或者不如说是显现为——一缕清甜的声音。我再一碰，声音便增强了。第三次碰它时，一股细小的和声之流从那小小的球里滚滚涌出。她再也不许我碰它了。

We traveled on together all that day. She left me when twilight came on; but next day, at noon, she met me as before, and again we traveled till evening. The third day she came once more at noon, and we walked on together. Now, though we had talked about a great many things connected with Fairy Land, and the life she had led hitherto, I had never been able to learn anything about the globe. This day, however, as we went on, the shadow glided round and inwrapt the maiden. It could not change her. But my desire to know about the globe, which in his gloom began to waver as with an inward light, and to shoot out flashes of many-colored flame, grew irresistible. I put out both my hands and laid hold of it. It began to sound as before. The sound rapidly increased, till it grew a low tempest of harmony, and the globe trembled, and quivered, and throbbed between my hands. I had not the heart to pull it away from the maiden, though I held it in spite of her attempts to take it from me; yes, I shame to say, in spite of her prayers, and, at last, her tears. The music went on growing in intensity and complication of tones, and the globe vibrated and heaved; till at last it burst in our hands, and a black vapor broke upwards from out of it; then turned, as if blown sideways, and enveloped the maiden, hiding even the shadow in its blackness. She held fast the fragments, which I abandoned, and fled from me into the forest in the direction whence she had come, wailing like a child, and crying, “You have broken my globe; my globe is broken—my globe is broken!” I followed her, in the hope of comforting her; but had not pursued her far, before a sudden cold gust of wind bowed the treetops above us, and swept through their stems around us; a great cloud overspread the day, and a fierce tempest came on, in which I lost sight of her. It lies heavy on my heart to this hour. At night, ere I fall asleep, often, whatever I may be thinking about, I suddenly hear her voice, crying out, “You have broken my globe; my globe is broken; ah, my globe!”

那一整天我们继续同行。暮色四合时她离开了我；但第二天正午，她又像先前那样来会我，我们又同行到傍晚。第三天她又在正午来了，我们继续同行。话说回来，虽然我们谈过许多与仙境相关的事，谈过她迄今所过的生活，关于那颗球，我却始终未能探得一点消息。然而这一天，走着走着，影子滑转过来，把少女裹住了。它却改变不了她。但我想要探知这球底细的欲望，变得不可抗拒——那球在他的幽暗里开始微微明灭，仿佛内蕴光华，迸射出五彩火焰的闪光。我伸出双手，把它抓住。它像先前一样鸣响起来。声音迅速增强，直到汇成一场低回的和声风暴；那球在我两手之间颤抖、悸动、搏跳。我不忍把它从少女身边夺走，然而我仍握着它，不顾她要把它夺回去的挣扎；是啊，说来可耻，不顾她的恳求，最后，不顾她的眼泪。乐声不断在强度与音调的繁复上增长，那球震颤着、起伏着；终于它在我们手中迸裂，一股黑色的雾气从中向上腾起；随即像被侧风吹送似的横转过来，把少女裹住，连那影子也掩没在它的黑暗里。她紧攥着那些碎片——我早已弃之不顾——朝她来的方向逃进森林，像孩子一般哀哭，喊着：“你把我的球打破了；我的球破了——我的球破了！”我跟在她后面，指望安慰她；但没追出多远，一阵骤起的冷风蓦地把我们头顶的树梢吹得俯首，又从我们四周的树干间横扫而过；一朵大云遮暗了白昼，一场猛烈的暴风雨袭来，我便望不见她了。时至今日，这事仍沉沉压在我心头。夜里，在我入睡之前，不论我正想着什么，我常常会突然听见她的声音，喊着：“你把我的球打破了；我的球破了；啊，我的球！”

Here I will mention one more strange thing; but whether this peculiarity was owing to my shadow at all, I am not able to assure myself. I came to a village, the inhabitants of which could not at first sight be distinguished from the dwellers in our land. They rather avoided than sought my company, though they were very pleasant when I addressed them. But at last I observed, that whenever I came within a certain distance of any one of them, which distance, however, varied with different individuals, the whole appearance of the person began to change; and this change increased in degree as I approached. When I receded to the former distance, the former appearance was restored. The nature of the change was grotesque, following no fixed rule. The nearest resemblance to it that I know, is the distortion produced in your countenance when you look at it as reflected in a concave or convex surface—say, either side of a bright spoon. Of this phenomenon I first became aware in rather a ludicrous way. My host’s daughter was a very pleasant pretty girl, who made herself more agreeable to me than most of those about me. For some days my companion-shadow had been less obtrusive than usual; and such was the reaction of spirits occasioned by the simple mitigation of torment, that, although I had cause enough besides to be gloomy, I felt light and comparatively happy. My impression is, that she was quite aware of the law of appearances that existed between the people of the place and myself, and had resolved to amuse herself at my expense; for one evening, after some jesting and raillery, she, somehow or other, provoked me to attempt to kiss her. But she was well defended from any assault of the kind. Her countenance became, of a sudden, absurdly hideous; the pretty mouth was elongated and otherwise amplified sufficiently to have allowed of six simultaneous kisses. I started back in bewildered dismay; she burst into the merriest fit of laughter, and ran from the room. I soon found that the same undefinable law of change operated between me and all the other villagers; and that, to feel I was in pleasant company, it was absolutely necessary for me to discover and observe the right focal distance between myself and each one with whom I had to do. This done, all went pleasantly enough. Whether, when I happened to neglect this precaution, I presented to them an equally ridiculous appearance, I did not ascertain; but I presume that the alteration was common to the approximating parties. 这里我还要提一件更奇的事；但这一特性究竟是否该归因于我的影子，我自己也无从断言。我来到一座村庄，村里的居民乍看之下，竟分辨不出与我们本土的居民有何不同。他们与其说是寻求与我作伴，不如说是避着我，不过我一开口攀谈，他们便都十分和悦。可我终于注意到，每当我与其中任何人接近到某段距离之内——这段距离却因人而异——那人的整个容貌便开始改变；我愈走近，这改变便愈甚。待我退回原先的距离，原先的容貌便告复原。这变化的性质是怪诞的，没有固定的规则。就我所知，与它最相近的，是你从凹面或凸面——譬如一把光亮汤匙的任一面——里照见自己面容时所生的那种变形。这现象我最初察觉的方式颇为滑稽。我房东的女儿是位极可爱、极标致的姑娘，她待我比周围大多数人都和悦亲热。那几天，我那随行的影子不像往常那样碍眼了；折磨稍有减轻，精神便起了这样的反拨，以致我虽然另有足够的理由阴郁，却觉得轻快，相对而言还算快乐。我的印象是，她十分清楚这地方的人与我之间存在的形貌法则，并打定主意要拿我来取乐；因为一天傍晚，一番玩笑戏谑之后，她不知怎地竟惹得我试图去吻她。但她对这一类的进袭，防卫得十分周全。她的面容霎时变得荒唐地狰狞；那张秀美的嘴拉长了，并以别的方式放大开来，足以容人同时吻上六下。我惊惶错乱地向后一缩；她却爆发出最欢畅的一阵大笑，跑出屋去了。我很快发现，同样那一条难以名状的变化法则，也在我与所有其他村民之间起作用；而若要觉得相与的人令人愉快，我绝对必须找出并遵守我与每个交往之人之间恰当的焦距。做到了这一点，一切都进行得相当愉快。至于我偶尔疏忽了这重防范时，在他们的眼中我是否也呈现出一副同样可笑的模样，我未曾查明；但我推想，这变化是为彼此趋近的双方所共有的。我同样无法断定，我对于这奇怪变形的产生是否为一方必要因素；抑或在既有的情形之下，这变形也会在村民们彼此之间发生。
