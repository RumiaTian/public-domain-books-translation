## XVI / 第十六章

===Original===
> Selbst der Styx, der neunfach sie umwindet,
> Wehrt die Rückkehr Ceres Tochter nicht;
> Nach dem Apfel greift sie, und es bindet
> Ewig sie des Orkus Pflicht
> Ev’n the Styx, which ninefold her infoldeth
> Hems not Ceres’ daughter in its flow;
> But she grasps the apple—ever holdeth
> Her, sad Orcus, down below.

===Chinese===
> Selbst der Styx, der neunfach sie umwindet,
> Wehrt die Rückkehr Ceres Tochter nicht;
> Nach dem Apfel greift sie, und es bindet
> Ewig sie des Orkus Pflicht
> 纵是九重缠裹她的斯提克斯，
> 它的流水也围不住刻瑞斯的女儿；
> 但她攫住了那苹果——悲哀的俄耳库斯便永远
> 把她攫在幽冥之下。

===Original===
Ever as I sang, the veil was uplifted; ever as I sang, the signs of life grew; till, when the eyes dawned upon me, it was with that sunrise of splendor which my feeble song attempted to re-imbody. The wonder is, that I was not altogether overcome, but was able to complete my song as the unseen veil continued to rise. This ability came solely from the state of mental elevation in which I found myself. Only because uplifted in song, was I able to endure the blaze of the dawn. But I cannot tell whether she looked more of statue or more of woman; she seemed removed into that region of phantasy where all is intensely vivid, but nothing clearly defined. At last, as I sang of her descending hair, the glow of soul faded away, like a dying sunset. A lamp within had been extinguished, and the house of life shone blank in a winter morn. She was a statue once more—but visible, and that was much gained. Yet the revulsion from hope and fruition was such, that, unable to restrain myself, I sprang to her, and, in defiance of the law of the place, flung my arms around her, as if I would tear her from the grasp of a visible Death, and lifted her from the pedestal down to my heart. But no sooner had her feet ceased to be in contact with the black pedestal, than she shuddered and trembled all over; then, writhing from my arms, before I could tighten their hold, she sprang into the corridor, with the reproachful cry, “You should not have touched me!” darted behind one of the exterior pillars of the circle, and disappeared. I followed almost as fast; but ere I could reach the pillar, the sound of a closing door, the saddest of all sounds sometimes, fell on my ear; and, arriving at the spot where she had vanished, I saw, lighted by a pale yellow lamp which hung above it, a heavy, rough door, altogether unlike any others I had seen in the palace; for they were all of ebony, or ivory, or covered with silver-plates, or of some odorous wood, and very ornate; whereas this seemed of old oak, with heavy nails and iron studs. Notwithstanding the precipitation of my pursuit, I could not help reading, in silver letters beneath the lamp: “No one enters here without the leave of the Queen.” But what was the Queen to me, when I followed my white lady? I dashed the door to the wall and sprang through. Lo! I stood on a waste windy hill. Great stones like tombstones stood all about me. No door, no palace was to be seen. A white figure gleamed past me, wringing her hands, and crying, “Ah! you should have sung to me; you should have sung to me!” and disappeared behind one of the stones. I followed. A cold gust of wind met me from behind the stone; and when I looked, I saw nothing but a great hole in the earth, into which I could find no way of entering. Had she fallen in? I could not tell. I must wait for the daylight. I sat down and wept, for there was no help.

===Chinese===
我唱着，那帷幕便一路升起；我唱着，生命的征兆便一路增长；直到那双眼睛在我面前睁开时，宛如破晓，带着那样一轮壮丽辉煌的日出——我那支微弱的歌所力图重新赋形的，正是这日出。奇怪的是，我竟没有被全然压倒，还能在那看不见的帷幕继续升起之时，把我的歌唱完。这能力唯独来自我发现自己置身其中的那种精神高扬的状态。只因为我已在歌中高升，才受得住那黎明的烈焰。但我说不清她看上去更像石像，还是更像女人；她仿佛已被移入幻想的那个境地，在那里一切都极度鲜活，却没有一样轮廓分明。末了，当我唱到她垂落的头发时，那灵魂的光辉渐渐黯淡下去，如一场消逝的日落。内在的一盏灯熄灭了，生命的宅子在冬日的清晨里泛出一片空白。她又成了一尊石像——但看得见了，这已是极大的收获。然而，从希望与圆满遽然跌回的这一阵震荡如此剧烈，以致我再也无法克制自己，一跃扑到她跟前，不顾那地方的法则，张开双臂抱住她，仿佛要把她从一个看得见的死神手中夺回来，把她从基座上抱下来，贴到我的心口。可她的双脚刚一离开那黑色的基座，她便浑身战栗起来；接着，不等我收紧双臂的环抱，她便从我的怀抱里挣脱出去，一声嗔责的叫喊——「你不该碰我！」——跃入长廊，闪身躲到那圆环外侧的一根柱子后面，不见了。我几乎同样迅疾地追去；但我还未赶到那柱子跟前，一声关门的声音——那有时是一切声音中最悲哀的声音——落进我的耳朵；待我赶到她消失的地方，我看见一扇沉重而粗粝的门，被悬在它上方的一盏淡黄色的灯照亮着，与我在仙宫里见过的所有门全然不同；因为那些门不是乌木的，便是象牙的，或者包着银片，或者用某种芬芳的木料制成，装饰得十分华美；而这扇门却似乎是老橡木的，钉着粗大的钉子和铁泡钉。尽管我追赶得那样急迫，我还是不由自主地读到了灯下那几个银色的字：「非女王允准，任何人不得入内。」可我追随的是我的白衣女郎，女王于我何干？我把那门猛力甩向墙壁，一跃而过。看哪！我站在一座荒凉多风的山丘上。四周耸立着一块块宛如墓碑的巨石。门没有了，仙宫也不见了。一个白色的身影从我身旁闪过，绞着双手，哭喊道：「啊！你本该对我唱歌的；你本该对我唱歌的！」随即消失在一块石头后面。我追了过去。一阵冷风从石头后面迎面扑来；待我张望，只见地上有一个大洞，此外什么也没有，而我找不到任何能进到那洞里去的路径。她是掉进去了吗？我说不上来。我必须等天亮。我坐下来哭泣，因为无计可施。
