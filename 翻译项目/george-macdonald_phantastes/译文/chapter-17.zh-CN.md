## XVII / 第十七章

===Original===

===Original===
When the daylight came, it brought the possibility of action, but with it little of consolation. With the first visible increase of light, I gazed into the chasm, but could not, for more than an hour, see sufficiently well to discover its nature. At last I saw it was almost a perpendicular opening, like a roughly excavated well, only very large. I could perceive no bottom; and it was not till the sun actually rose, that I discovered a sort of natural staircase, in many parts little more than suggested, which led round and round the gulf, descending spirally into its abyss. I saw at once that this was my path; and without a moment’s hesitation, glad to quit the sunlight, which stared at me most heartlessly, I commenced my tortuous descent. It was very difficult. In some parts I had to cling to the rocks like a bat. In one place, I dropped from the track down upon the next returning spire of the stair; which being broad in this particular portion, and standing out from the wall at right angles, received me upon my feet safe, though somewhat stupefied by the shock. After descending a great way, I found the stair ended at a narrow opening which entered the rock horizontally. Into this I crept, and, having entered, had just room to turn round. I put my head out into the shaft by which I had come down, and surveyed the course of my descent. Looking up, I saw the stars; although the sun must by this time have been high in the heavens. Looking below, I saw that the sides of the shaft went sheer down, smooth as glass; and far beneath me, I saw the reflection of the same stars I had seen in the heavens when I looked up. I turned again, and crept inwards some distance, when the passage widened, and I was at length able to stand and walk upright. Wider and loftier grew the way; new paths branched off on every side; great open halls appeared; till at last I found myself wandering on through an underground country, in which the sky was of rock, and instead of trees and flowers, there were only fantastic rocks and stones. And ever as I went, darker grew my thoughts, till at last I had no hope whatever of finding the white lady: I no longer called her to myself *my* white lady. Wherever a choice was necessary, I always chose the path which seemed to lead downwards.

===Chinese===
天光来临时，它带来了行动的可能，却几乎没带来什么慰藉。亮光刚一显出增长，我便朝那裂壑深处凝望，可是在一个多钟头里，我都没能看得足够清楚，辨不明它的形貌。后来我终于看出，那几乎是一道垂直的开口，像一口粗加开凿的井，只是极其巨大。我望不见底；直到太阳真正升起，我才发现有一道天然石阶——许多处所不过依稀可辨——绕着深壑一圈又一圈，螺旋下行，通入它的深渊。我立刻明白，这就是我的路；我一刻也没有迟疑，甘愿撇下那无情瞪视着我的日光，开始了迂曲的下行。这条路极难走。有几处，我不得不像蝙蝠一样攀附在岩石上。有一处，我从径道上跌落下去，跌在下一圈折返的石阶尖脊上；那一段石阶恰好宽阔，又与岩壁成直角向外突出，便把我双脚安然接住——只是那一震令我有些发懵。下行了极深之后，我发现石阶尽头是一道狭窄的开口，水平没入岩体。我爬了进去；进去之后，刚有转身的余地。我把头探回我方才降下来的竖井里，察看我一路下行的来路。抬头仰望，我看见了星辰——虽然此刻太阳想必已高悬天际。俯身下望，只见竖井四壁笔直而下，光滑如镜；而在我脚下极深之处，我看见了同一些星辰的倒影——正是我抬头时在天上看见的那些。我重新转回身，向内又爬了一段，甬道渐渐开阔，我终于能够站立起来，直身而行。路越走越宽、越走越高；四面八方岔出新径；一座座敞开的大殿次第显现；到最后，我发觉自己已在一个地底国度里漫游，那里的天空是岩石做的，没有树木花草，只有奇形怪状的岩与石。而且我一路走着，思虑便一路转暗，到最后，我对于寻见白衣女郎已全然不抱希望：我不再在心里称她为*我的*白衣女郎。凡在必须抉择之处，我总是拣那条似乎通向更深处的路。

===Original===
At length I began to find that these regions were inhabited. From behind a rock a peal of harsh grating laughter, full of evil humor, rang through my ears, and, looking round, I saw a queer, goblin creature, with a great head and ridiculous features, just such as those described, in German histories and travels, as Kobolds. “What do you want with me?” I said. He pointed at me with a long forefinger, very thick at the root, and sharpened to a point, and answered, “*He! he! he!* what do *you* want here?” Then, changing his tone, he continued, with mock humility—“Honored sir, vouchsafe to withdraw from thy slaves the luster of thy august presence, for thy slaves cannot support its brightness.” A second appeared, and struck in: “You are so big, you keep the sun from us. We can’t see for you, and we’re so cold.” Thereupon arose, on all sides, the most terrific uproar of laughter, from voices like those of children in volume, but scrannel and harsh as those of decrepit age, though, unfortunately, without its weakness. The whole pandemonium of fairy devils, of all varieties of fantastic ugliness, both in form and feature, and of all sizes from one to four feet, seemed to have suddenly assembled about me. At length, after a great babble of talk among themselves, in a language unknown to me, and after seemingly endless gesticulation, consultation, elbow-nudging, and unmitigated peals of laughter, they formed into a circle about one of their number, who scrambled upon a stone, and, much to my surprise, and somewhat to my dismay, began to sing, in a voice corresponding in its nature to his talking one, from beginning to end, the song with which I had brought the light into the eyes of the white lady. He sang the same air too; and, all the time, maintained a face of mock entreaty and worship; accompanying the song with the travestied gestures of one playing on the lute. The whole assembly kept silence, except at the close of every verse, when they roared, and danced, and shouted with laughter, and flung themselves on the ground, in real or pretended convulsions of delight. When he had finished, the singer threw himself from the top of the stone, turning heels over head several times in his descent; and when he did alight, it was on the top of his head, on which he hopped about, making the most grotesque gesticulations with his legs in the air. Inexpressible laughter followed, which broke up in a shower of tiny stones from innumerable hands. They could not materially injure me, although they cut me on the head and face. I attempted to run away, but they all rushed upon me, and, laying hold of every part that afforded a grasp, held me tight. Crowding about me like bees, they shouted an insect-swarm of exasperating speeches up into my face, among which the most frequently recurring were—“You shan’t have her; you shan’t have her; he! he! he! She’s for a better man; how he’ll kiss her! how he’ll kiss her!”

我终于开始发觉，这些地带是有居民栖息的。一块岩石背后，爆出一阵刺耳磨人的大笑，满含恶毒的兴味，响彻我的两耳；我回头一看，只见一个古怪的妖怪生物，大脑袋，五官滑稽，正像德国史地著作与游记里描述的那种科博尔德（Kobolds）。「你们要我怎样？」我说。他伸出一根长长的食指指着我——指根极粗，尖端削得锐细——答道：「*嘻！嘻！嘻！*倒是*你*要在这里怎样？」接着，他换了一副腔调，装出谦卑的样子继续道：「尊贵的老爷，求您容您的奴仆们避开您威仪赫赫的临在的光辉吧，因为您的奴仆们消受不起它的光亮。」第二个现出身来，插嘴道：「您太大了，把太阳都给我们挡住了。您在，我们就什么也看不见，我们还这么冷。」话音未落，四面八方腾起了最骇人的哄笑，论音量像孩童的嗓音，却又干瘪沙哑，赛过老朽衰年——可惜并没有老年的虚弱。仿佛仙境群魔的整个巢窟——形与貌无奇不有、其丑百态，身量从一英尺到四英尺不等——忽然一齐聚拢到我周围。他们先用一种我听不懂的语言彼此喧聒了好一大通，又做了仿佛无尽无休的手势、商议、以肘相碰、放肆地哄笑，末了围成一个圆圈，圈住他们中间的一个；那家伙爬上一块石头，令我大吃一惊、也颇为惶惑的是，他竟从头到尾唱起那支歌来——正是我用来把光明送进白衣女郎眼里的那一支；连曲调也唱得一样；而且自始至终摆着一副假意恳求与崇拜的面孔，一边唱，一边配着弹琉特琴者的歪拟手势。全体鸦雀无声，只是每唱完一节，他们便咆哮起来，又跳又叫，狂笑高呼，扑倒在地，作真心或假意的狂喜痉挛。他唱完了，歌者便从石头顶上翻跌下来，坠落途中连打了好几个跟斗；而他落地时，偏偏用头顶着地，就这样单凭脑袋蹦跳不休，两腿在空中做着最古怪滑稽的姿态。随之而来的是难以言表的哄笑，笑声里，无数只手抛来一阵小石子的急雨。这些石子伤不了我的筋骨，却把我头脸划破了几处。我试图逃走，他们便一齐扑上来，凡抓得住的地方一概抓住，把我紧紧拽住。他们像蜂群一样簇拥着我，把一窝昆虫般恼人的话朝我脸上一阵一阵嚷来，其中出现得最频繁的是——「你休想得到她；你休想得到她；嘻！嘻！嘻！她是留给一个更高贵的男人的；看他要怎样吻她！看他要怎样吻她！」

===Original===
The galvanic torrent of this battery of malevolence stung to life within me a spark of nobleness, and I said aloud, “Well, if he is a better man, let him have her.”

They instantly let go their hold of me, and fell back a step or two, with a whole broadside of grunts and humphs, as of unexpected and disappointed approbation. I made a step or two forward, and a lane was instantly opened for me through the midst of the grinning little antics, who bowed most politely to me on every side as I passed. After I had gone a few yards, I looked back, and saw them all standing quite still, looking after me, like a great school of boys; till suddenly one turned round, and with a loud whoop, rushed into the midst of the others. In an instant, the whole was one writhing and tumbling heap of contortion, reminding me of the live pyramids of intertwined snakes of which travellers make report. As soon as one was worked out of the mass, he bounded off a few paces, and then, with a somerset and a run, threw himself gyrating into the air, and descended with all his weight on the summit of the heaving and struggling chaos of fantastic figures. I left them still busy at this fierce and apparently aimless amusement. And as I went, I sang—

===Chinese===

他们立刻松开了我，向后退开一两步，发出一阵舷炮齐射般的咕哝与哼啧，像是意外而又怅然的赞许。我向前走了一两步，一条通路立时在这些龇牙咧嘴的小丑物中间让了出来；我经过时，他们还向两边极其有礼地朝我鞠躬。走出几码之后，我回头一望，只见他们全都一动不动地站着，目送着我，像一大群学童；忽然，其中一个转过身，大吼一声，扑进其余那些中间。霎时间，全场化作一堆扭结翻滚、蜷曲蠕动的乱团，让我想起旅人们记载过的那种活蛇盘绕的蛇塔。一个刚从堆里挣脱出来，便向前蹿出几步，接着一个跟斗加一段疾跑，把自己旋转着抛上空中，再整个身子砸落在那堆起伏挣扎、奇形怪状的乱象顶上。我离开时，他们还忙着这种凶猛而显然毫无目的的嬉戏。我一路走着，唱道——

===Original===
A crash of laughter, more discordant and deriding than any I had yet heard, invaded my ears. Looking on in the direction of the sound, I saw a little elderly woman, much taller, however, than the goblins I had just left, seated upon a stone by the side of the path. She rose, as I drew near, and came forward to meet me. She was very plain and commonplace in appearance, without being hideously ugly. Looking up in my face with a stupid sneer, she said: “Isn’t it a pity you haven’t a pretty girl to walk all alone with you through this sweet country? How different everything would look! wouldn’t it? Strange that one can never have what one would like best! How the roses would bloom and all that, even in this infernal hole! wouldn’t they, Anodos?
