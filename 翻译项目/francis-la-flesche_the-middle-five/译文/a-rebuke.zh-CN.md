## XIV A Rebuke / 一顿训斥

===Original===
It was Saturday, a day of delight for the boys and girls of the Mission school, for to them it was a day of rest from the toil of study, and a visit home was permitted. On this morning the allotted chores were performed with redoubled energy; for the sooner the tasks were done, the earlier would be the start for home, the sooner the pleasures laid out for the day would begin.

The boys who had finished their work and had reported to the superintendent were already on their way to the village, shouting and singing as they went. Edwin watered the horses, and I started the hydraulic ram; then, having received our formal leave to go, we chased each other up the hill toward the village, and wrestled until we came to the place where the path branched; he took one way and I the other, but we continued to chaff back and forth until we were out of hearing.

After greeting my father and mother, the first thing I did was to run over to the barn and see the horses. When I had rubbed the noses of Kushas and Hintu and the rest, and had pitched down from the loft a lot of hay for them, I stepped over to Ka-heʻ-num-ba’s house and looked in at the door, which stood wide open. His wife was sitting near the stove, quietly working on a pair of moccasins.

“Where is Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe?” I shouted.

===Chinese===
那是星期六，传教所学校的男孩女孩们欢喜的一天，因为对他们来说，这一天可以从学习的辛劳中歇息下来，还获准回家探望。这天早晨，分派的杂活大家干得格外卖力；因为活儿越早干完，就能越早上路回家，这一天安排好的乐事也能越早开始。

干完活并向校监报告过的男孩们已经上路往村里去了，一路又喊又唱。埃德温饮了马，我发动了水锤泵；随后领到正式的外出许可，我们俩便互相追逐着朝村子方向的坡上跑，一路扭打，直到走到小路分岔的地方；他走一条道，我走另一条，可我们还继续隔着距离斗嘴，直到谁也听不见谁。

向父亲母亲问过安之后，我头一件事就是跑去看马棚里的马。在摩挲过 Kushas 和 Hintu 以及其余几匹马的鼻子、又从草料棚里扔下一大捆干草之后，我便踱到 Ka-heʻ-num-ba 家，朝敞开的房门里张望。他的妻子坐在炉子旁，安静地缝着一双鹿皮鞋。

“Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 在哪儿？”我喊道。

===Original===
“Oh! how you startled me!” she said. “Your uncle has gone to the stable with other boys; he left word for you to go there when you came.”

Hardly had she finished speaking before I was off like the wind. On the ground by a fire sat Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe and the boys, all busy making game sticks, the Indian name of which we Mission boys translated into English as “bone slides.” These were made out of willow saplings. After cutting the stick the proper length, the bark was removed, and a narrow strip of it wound around the peeled stick, which was then held over the blaze of the fire until the exposed part was scorched. When the binding was removed, the game-stick presented a mottled appearance, something like a snake.

The brown bodies of these partly nude little savages glistened against the sun as they worked, while the breezes played with their black totemic locks. They were not aware of my approach until I pitched a corncob into their midst, when they all threw up their heads to see who was coming.

“Ho! Little White-chest!” exclaimed Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe. “Have you come home?”

===Chinese===
“哟！吓我一跳！”她说，“你叔叔跟别的男孩上马棚去了；他留了话，你来了就到那儿找他。”

她话音未落，我已一阵风似的跑掉了。火堆旁的地上坐着 Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 和男孩们，都在忙着做游戏棒——它的印第安名字，我们传教所的男孩们把它译成英文，叫“滑骨”（bone slides）。这些游戏棒用柳树苗做成。把棒砍到合适的长度后，剥去外皮，再取一条窄窄的树皮缠在去皮的棒上，然后把棒举在火苗上燎，直到裸露的部分烤焦。解去缠皮之后，游戏棒便现出斑驳的花纹，有点像一条蛇。

这些半裸的棕色小野蛮人干着活，棕色的身体在太阳底下发亮，微风拂弄着他们乌黑的、依图腾留出的发绺。直到我把一块玉米芯扔进他们中间，他们才发觉我来了，全都抬起头看是谁来了。

“嗬！小白胸（White-chest）！”Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 喊道，“你回家啦？”

===Original===
“Yes, I have come home,” I replied; “but I don’t want you to call me White-chest.”

“Sit down,” said one of the little brownies. “When we have done, we will give you some, then you can play with us.”

When the sticks were finished, I was given five or six of them. The tallest boy led the game. He grasped the small end of the game-stick with his right hand, bracing the top with a finger, then he took two or three quick sidelong steps and threw the stick against the ground with all the force he could command; it bounded up and shot through the air like an arrow. The next boy threw one of his sticks in the same manner, and from the same place. All the others played, each in his turn. Then one of the boys shouted, “Your turn, little White-chest. Throw hard!”

I was familiar with the game, and by practice had acquired some skill in throwing the sticks. I selected one that seemed to have the proper weight and feeling, took the usual position, and crouching almost to the earth, I threw my stick with all the force that I could muster. We watched its flight until it touched the ground and slid along, far beyond any stick that had been thrown.

“Woo-hoo!” exclaimed the boys, “he has beaten us all; he’s won all our sticks!”

===Chinese===
“是的，我回家了，”我答道，“可我不许你再叫我白胸。”

“坐下吧，”一个棕色小家伙说，“等我们做完了，分你几根，你就能跟我们一块儿玩了。”

棒做好后，分了我五六根。个子最高的男孩开局面。他右手攥住游戏棒的细端，用一根手指抵住顶端，接着侧身疾跨两三步，使出他能调动起来的全部力气把棒朝地上掷去；棒弹起来，像箭一样穿过空中。下一个男孩以同样的姿势、在同一位置掷出他的一根棒。其余的人依次各掷一回。然后一个男孩喊道：“该你了，小白胸。使劲掷！”

这游戏我熟，练习中也练出了几分掷棒的功夫。我挑了一根分量和手感都合适的，摆好惯常的姿势，身子几乎蹲到地上，使出我能集聚的全部力气把棒掷了出去。我们望着它飞行，直到它落地又滑出去老远，把所有掷过的棒都远远甩在后面。

“呜——呼！”男孩们喊道，“他把我们全赢了；他把我们的棒全赢走啦！”

===Original===
“Kill him! kill him! He’s nothing but a thieving Winnebago!” This cry came from the west end of the village, not far from where we were playing. Startled by the angry words, we paused in our sport, and looked in that direction. A crowd began to gather and move along the path that led out of the village.

“What are they doing? Let’s go and see,” cried Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe.

We all rushed forward on a keen run, and reached the crowd; there we saw a lad, a little larger than we were, struggling to get away from a swarm of boys and young men who were throwing stones and sticks at him. He was a pitiful object, and why they should abuse him so was more than we could understand. His legs and feet were bare; he carried on his arm something that resembled a worn-out blanket, and in his hand he held tightly a piece of bread. He belonged to the Winnebago tribe, against whom at that time there was much prejudice among the Omahas. Mud was thrown at him; he was pushed and jostled by the crowd, and some persons kicked him. Slowly the boy retreated, at times stopping to look with pleading eyes at his merciless persecutors. When he started to run, someone threw a stick of wood before him; he struck his foot against it and fell; then the crowd laughed.

“They are doing wrong!” exclaimed Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe. “They ought not to do that!”

===Chinese===
“打死他！打死他！他不过是个偷东西的温内巴戈（Winnebago）！”这喊声从村子西头传来，离我们玩的地方不远。我们被这怒气冲冲的话吓了一跳，停下游戏朝那边望去。一群人开始聚拢，沿着出村的小路移动。

“他们在干什么？走，瞧瞧去。”Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 喊道。

我们全都撒腿飞跑，赶到了人群跟前；只见一个比我们略大些的少年，正挣扎着想从一群朝他扔石块木棍的男孩和年轻人中间脱身。他那样子真可怜，他们为什么这样虐待他，我们实在不明白。他腿脚赤裸；胳膊上搭着一件破毯子模样的东西，手里紧紧攥着一块面包。他属于温内巴戈部落，那时候奥马哈人对这个部落偏见很深。泥块朝他砸去；人群推他搡他，还有人踢他。那男孩慢慢退让，不时停下脚步，用恳求的眼光望着那些狠心的迫害者。他刚要跑，有人把一根木棍扔到他前面；他脚上一绊，摔倒了；于是人群哄笑起来。

“他们这是作恶！”Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 喊道，“不该这样对待他！”

===Original===
“I think so, too,” I added; “but what can we do?”

Just then I felt a tug at my sleeve. I turned to see who it was, and there stood the boy that did errands for my father. “Your father wants you to come home,” he said.

I was a little troubled at this, for the boy spoke in a frightened tone. At that moment a man came up and cried in a loud voice:

“You are commanded to cease molesting the boy!”

===Chinese===
“我也这么想，”我附和道，“可我们能做什么呢？”

正在这时，我觉得有人扯我的衣袖。我回头看是谁，原来是替我父亲跑腿的那个男孩。“你父亲叫你回家去。”他说。

这让我有些不安，因为这男孩说话的声音发慌。就在那时，一个男人走上前来，大声喊道：

“奉命尔等不得再骚扰这男孩！”

===Original===
Recognizing the speaker as a messenger coming from the chief, the rabble dispersed in groups, like angry wolves.

My mind was uneasy as I went toward home, and I felt guilty, though I could not understand why. As I entered the house I was ushered into my father’s presence. He was talking earnestly to a number of men who sat on the floor smoking a pipe which they passed from hand to hand. Among them I recognized Ka-heʻ-num-ba (the father of Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe), Te-oʻ-ke-ha, Duʻ-ba-mo-ne, Wa-honʻ-i-ge (Edwin’s father), and other prominent men of the village. My father seemed to take no notice of my entrance, but kept on talking. When he had finished speaking, his eyes rested on me, and after a moment’s pause, he said, “Son, step to the middle of the floor.” I did so. Then in a low tone he began:

“I speak not boastfully; all who are here have known me from boyhood, and will know what I am about to say to you is true. Even before I grew to be your size I was left to face the difficulties of life. I have felt the pangs of hunger and the chills of winter, but, by ceaseless struggles, I overcame poverty and gathered about me, as I grew to manhood, many of the things that make life bearable; yet I did not cease to struggle. I have won honors and position among our people, and the respect of the tribes having friendly relations with us. Success has attended me; but, remembering my early struggles, I suppressed vanity, and gave help to the poor. When journeying with my people, if I saw any of them weary and footsore, I gave them horses, and sent them away singing for joy. The stranger who entered my door never left it hungry. No one can accuse me of having tormented with abuse the poverty-stricken man. Early I sought the society of those who knew the teaching of the chiefs. From them I learned that kindness and hospitality win the love of a people. I culled from their teachings their noblest thoughts, and treasured them, and they have been my guide. You came into existence, and have reached the age when you should seek for knowledge. That you might profit by the teachings of your own people and that of the white race, and that you might avoid the misery which accompanies ignorance, I placed you in the House of Teaching of the White-chests, who are said to be wise and to have in their books the utterances of great and learned men. I had treasured the hope that you would seek to know the good deeds done by men of your own race, and by men of the white race, that you would follow their example and take pleasure in doing the things that are noble and helpful to those around you. Am I to be disappointed?”

As his talk progressed, he grew eloquent, and louder and louder became his tones. My eyes were riveted upon him. In every feature of his handsome face there was reflected a mind, a will, a determination that nothing could break. He arose to his feet and continued, pointing his finger at me:

===Chinese===
人群认出说话的是酋长派来的使者，便三三两两散去，像一群恶狼。

我往家走，心里七上八下，觉得自己像是有罪，却又说不清为什么。一进屋，我就被领到父亲面前。他正跟几个男人恳切地谈话，他们坐在地上，轮流传着一根烟斗吸烟。其中我认出了 Ka-heʻ-num-ba（Ga-imʻ-ba-zhe 的父亲）、Te-oʻ-ke-ha、Duʻ-ba-mo-ne、Wa-honʻ-i-ge（埃德温的父亲），还有村里其他几位有名望的人。父亲似乎没有留意我进来，只顾说下去。他说完了，目光落在我身上，顿了片刻，说：“儿子，站到屋子中间来。”我照办了。于是他低声开口：

“我说的不是自夸的话；在座的各位从小就认识我，会知道我将对你说的是真是假。还没长到你这么高的时候，我就独自面对生活的艰难了。我尝过饥饿的绞痛、冬天的寒颤，但凭着不懈的奋斗，我战胜了贫穷，成年后又积攒起许多使生活过得下去的东西；可我并没有停止奋斗。我赢得了本族的荣誉和地位，也赢得了与我们有友好关系的各部落的敬重。我一直顺遂成功；但想起早年的挣扎，我便压住虚荣，济助穷人。与本族人同行时，只要看见谁疲乏伤脚，我就把马送给他，让他欢歌而去。进我家门的陌生人，从没有饿着肚子离开的。谁也不能指控我曾经用恶语折磨过穷苦的人。我早早地去亲近那些通晓酋长教导的人。从他们那里，我懂得了仁慈和好客能赢得人民的爱戴。我从他们的教诲中采撷最崇高的思想，珍藏起来，它们一直是我的指引。你出世了，也到了该寻求知识的年纪。为了让你从本族人和白人的教诲中受益，让你免于无知所带来的苦难，我把你送进了白胸人（White-chests）的学堂——据说他们有智慧，他们的书里记载着伟大而博学之人的言论。我一直怀着一个希望：你会去了解本族人和白人做下的善举，效法他们，以做高尚的、有益于身边人的事为乐。难道我要失望吗？”

他说着说着，言辞越来越雄辩，声调也越来越高。我的眼睛牢牢盯在他身上。他那英俊面庞的每一处，都映出一种任何东西都打不垮的心智、意志和决心。他站起身来，指着我，继续说：

===Original===
“Only today there crept to the door of my house a poor boy driven thither by hunger; he was given food by my command. Having satisfied for the time being his craving, he went away happy. Hardly had he left the village, when a rabble gathered about him and persecuted him. They threw mud at him, pointed at him their fingers in derision, and laughed rudely at his poverty, and you, a son of E-staʻ-ma-za, joined the tormentors and smiled at the poor boy’s tears.”

I winced at this accusation. He could accuse me of almost anything; but of this I was not guilty. A hesitating small voice at the door said, “He did not join them!” It was the little boy that came after me who spoke. I was grateful for this defence, but, as though he had not heard it, my father continued.

“By your presence you aided and encouraged those wicked boys. He who is present at a wrongdoing, and lifts not a hand to prevent it, is as guilty as the wrongdoers. The persecution of the poor, the sneer at their poverty is a wrong for which no punishment is too severe. I have finished. Go, and think of my words.”

Those at the door made way for me; I passed out and entered my mother’s room. She looked up at me with a kindly smile; but I flung myself down on her bed, buried my face in the pillows, sobbing. My mother did not speak, but went on with her work. When I had regained my composure, she bade me come and sit beside her. I did so. She put an arm around me, and said in a caressing tone, “What is it that makes my little boy cry?” I told her. She sat in silence for a while, and then spoke:

===Chinese===
“就在今天，有一个被饥饿驱赶到我家门前的穷孩子；我吩咐人给了他饭食。他一时填饱了辘辘饥肠，欢欢喜喜地走了。可他刚出村子，一群暴徒就围上去迫害他。他们朝他扔泥，用手指着他讥笑，粗暴地嘲笑他的贫穷；而你，E-staʻ-ma-za 的儿子，竟然加入了这些折磨他的人，对着穷孩子的眼泪微笑。”

听到这指控，我浑身一缩。他几乎可以指控我任何事；唯独这件事我是无辜的。门口一个犹犹豫豫的细小声音说：“他没有加入他们！”说话的是平时跟着我的那个小男孩。我很感激这番辩白，可父亲像没有听见似的，继续说道：

“你在场，就是给那些恶少帮腔壮胆。眼见恶行发生而不抬手阻止的人，与作恶者同罪。迫害穷人、讥笑其贫穷，是怎样的罪过——对它再重的惩罚也不为过。我说完了。去吧，好好想想我的话。”

门口的人给我让开路；我出去进了母亲的房间。她抬起头朝我慈爱地一笑；我却一头扑倒在她床上，把脸埋进枕头里抽泣。母亲没有说话，只管做她的活计。等我平静下来，她叫我过去坐在她身旁。我照办了。她用一条胳膊搂住我，用抚爱的语气说：“是什么让我的小男孩哭啦？”我把事情告诉了她。她默默坐了一会儿，然后开口：

===Original===
“Your father is right; you must be guided by his words. You had a chance to do good; you let it slip away from you. That poor boy came and sat at the door, the humblest place in your house; he did not beg, but the eyes he turned upon your father and on me told a tale of suffering. At your father’s bidding, I placed food with my own hands before the boy; when he had finished eating he arose without a word and, taking with him what was left of the food, he went out, giving me a look that bespoke his gratitude. My boy must learn to be good and kind. When you see a boy barefooted and lame, take off your moccasins and give them to him. When you see a boy hungry, bring him to your home and give him food.”

The mild words and the gentle touch of her hand were like ointment to my wounds. When she had finished speaking, I put my arms around her neck and kissed her.

On my way back to the Mission I saw a lad standing at the fork of the paths. It was little Bob. “Come, hurry!” he said; “I’ve been waiting for you.” Together we returned to the school.

After supper I went out and lay on the grass, looking up into the blue sky, thinking. Twilight came, then darkness. A bell rang, and all the boys went upstairs to bed. I followed. We knelt by our beds; Graybeard rapped on the banister with his penknife; when there was silence, he said slowly, and in a low tone, “Our Father who art in heaven, Hallowed be Thy name.” We repeated with him this prayer that was taught over a thousand years ago. The tardy ones with labored breathing cut almost every word; but I repeated them carefully, and, although I had said them a hundred times before, now, for the first time since I had been in the school, I began to wonder what they meant.

“你父亲说得对；你要记住他的话。你原有机会行善；你却让它从手边溜走了。那可怜的孩子来了，坐在门口——你家最卑微的位置；他没有乞讨，可他望向你父亲和我的那双眼睛，道尽了他的苦难。你父亲一吩咐，我便亲手把饭食摆在那孩子面前；他吃完，一言不发地站起来，带着剩下的食物走了出去，临走望了我一眼，那眼神流露出他的感激。我的孩子必须学会善良仁慈。看见赤脚的跛脚男孩，就脱下你的鹿皮鞋送给他。看见挨饿的男孩，就把他带回家里给他饭吃。”

她温和的话语、她手上轻轻的抚慰，像膏油一样敷在我的伤口上。她说完，我搂住她的脖子，亲了亲她。

回传教所的路上，我看见一个少年站在岔路口。是小鲍勃（Bob）。“来，快点！”他说，“我等你半天了。”我们一起回到了学校。

晚饭后我出去躺在草地上，仰望着蓝天想心事。暮色降临，接着是黑夜。铃响了，男孩们都上楼睡觉去。我跟着上去。我们跪在床边；灰胡子用铅笔刀敲着栏杆；等静下来，他缓慢地、低声地说：“我们在天上的父，愿人都尊你的名为圣。”我们跟着他念这千余年前传下来的祷词。迟来的男孩们气喘吁吁，几乎每个字都念不全；我却一字一字认真跟着念；虽然这些话我从前念过上百遍，如今，进学校以来头一回，我开始琢磨它们究竟是什么意思。

Before I could answer Edwin began tugging at the sheet, saying, “Lester, you’ve too much sheet over there!”

“Edwin, tell us a story,” I said.

“Do!” exclaimed the other boys. “Tell us a story.”

莱斯特（Lester）、埃德温和我上了床；我睡中间。

“弗兰克，你怎么这么不吭声？”莱斯特用胳膊肘捅了捅我，问。

我还没来得及回答，埃德温先扯起床单来，说：“莱斯特，你那边床单占太多啦！”

两人拉扯着被子、好脾气地互相踢腾了一阵，便安静下来。我也挨了几脚，但没有加入这场嬉闹。静了一会儿，埃德温说：“哎，伙计们，我今天下午心里一直不好受。我从河边回家，我父亲把我狠狠骂了一顿。他念叨了一通，说我和几个男孩一起欺负了一个可怜的温内巴戈男孩，还对我说了好半天。他根本不给我机会说，我今天根本没见过什么温内巴戈男孩；我一早上都在河里游泳来着。我弄不明白，现在也不明白。哎，弗兰克，你父亲有时候也骂你吗？”

“埃德温，给我们讲个故事吧。”我说。

“讲吧！”其他男孩喊道，“讲个故事。”
