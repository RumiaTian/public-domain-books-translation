## I A Letter with a Postscript / 一 附有附言的信

===Original===
“A gentleman called to see you when you were out last night, sir,” said Mrs. Medley, my landlady, removing the last of the breakfast things.

“Yes?” I said, in my affable way.

“A gentleman,” said Mrs. Medley meditatively, “with a very powerful voice.”

“Caruso?”

“Sir?”

“I said, did he leave a name?”

===Original===
“Yes, sir. Mr. Ukridge.”

“Oh, my sainted aunt!”

“Sir?”

“Nothing, nothing.”

“Thank you, sir,” said Mrs. Medley, withdrawing from the presence.

Ukridge! Oh, hang it! I had not met him for years, and, glad as I am, as a general thing, to see the friends of my youth when they drop in for a chat, I doubted whether I was quite equal to Ukridge at the moment. A stout fellow in both the physical and moral sense of the words, he was a trifle too jumpy for a man of my cloistered and intellectual life, especially as just now I was trying to plan out a new novel, a tricky job demanding complete quiet and seclusion. It had always been my experience that, when Ukridge was around, things began to happen swiftly and violently, rendering meditation impossible. Ukridge was the sort of man who asks you out to dinner, borrows the money from you to pay the bill, and winds up the evening by embroiling you in a fight with a cabman. I have gone to Covent Garden balls with Ukridge, and found myself legging it down Henrietta Street in the grey dawn, pursued by infuriated costermongers.

厄克里奇！哦，要命！我已多年没见过他。虽说一般而言，少年时代的朋友登门闲谈，我总是欢迎的，可眼下我怀疑自己消受不起厄克里奇。此君无论按肉身还是按道义的讲法，都是一条壮汉；对于过我这种隐修式文人生活的人，他未免太蹦跶了些——何况我正构思一部新小说，那是个精细活儿，要求绝对的清静与遁世。我一向的经验是：厄克里奇一在场，事态便开始急转直下、风雷并作，叫人无从静思。厄克里奇是那样一种人：请你出去吃饭，跟你借钱付账单，最后再让你跟马车夫卷进一场恶斗，为这一晚收尾。我同厄克里奇去过考文特花园的舞会，结果在灰白的黎明，发现自己正撒腿狂奔在海丽埃塔街上，身后是怒火中烧的水果贩子们。

===Original===
I wondered how he had got my address, and on that problem light was immediately cast by Mrs. Medley, who returned, bearing an envelope.

“It came by the morning post, sir, but it was left at Number Twenty by mistake.”

“Oh, thank you.”

“Thank *you*, sir,” said Mrs. Medley.

I recognised the handwriting. The letter, which bore a Devonshire postmark, was from an artist friend of mine, one Lickford, who was at present on a sketching tour in the west. I had seen him off at Waterloo a week before, and I remember that I had walked away from the station wishing that I could summon up the energy to pack and get off to the country somewhere. I hate London in July.

The letter was a long one, but it was the postscript which interested me most.

===Chinese===
我正纳闷他怎么弄到我的地址，梅德利太太便立刻为这谜题投下一线光明——她回来了，手里拿着一只信封。

“是早晨邮班送来的，先生，只是错投到了二十号。”

“哦，谢谢你。”

“该谢*您*才对，先生。”梅德利太太说。

我认得这笔迹。信上盖着德文郡的邮戳，寄信人是我的一位画家朋友，姓利克福德，眼下正在西部写生旅行。一星期前我到滑铁卢车站为他送行，我记得自己走出车站时一路盼着，盼自己能鼓起劲头，收拾行装，也去乡下什么地方躲一躲。我讨厌七月的伦敦。

信写得很长，可最让我感兴趣的是那条附言。

===Original===
> “… By the way, at Yeovil I ran into an old friend of ours, Stanley Featherstonehaugh Ukridge, of all people. As large as life﻿—quite six foot two, and tremendously filled out. I thought he was abroad. The last I heard of him was that he had started for Buenos Aires in a cattle ship, with a borrowed pipe by way of luggage. It seems he has been in England for some time. I met him in the refreshment-room at Yeovil Station. I was waiting for a down train; he had changed on his way to town. As I opened the door, I heard a huge voice entreating the lady behind the bar to ‘put it in a pewter’; and there was S. F. U. in a villainous old suit of grey flannels (I’ll swear it was the one he had on last time I saw him), with pince-nez tacked on to his ears with ginger-beer wire as usual, and a couple of inches of bare neck showing between the bottom of his collar and the top of his coat﻿—you remember how he could never get a stud to do its work. He also wore a mackintosh, though it was a blazing day.

> “He greeted me with effusive shouts. Wouldn’t hear of my standing the racket. Insisted on being host. When we had finished, he fumbled in his pockets, looked pained and surprised, and drew me aside. ‘Look here, Licky, old horse,’ he said, ‘you know I never borrow money. It’s against my principles. But I *must* have a couple of bob. Can you, my dear good fellow, oblige me with a couple of bob till next Tuesday? I’ll tell you what I’ll do. (In a voice full of emotion). I’ll let you have this (producing a beastly little threepenny bit with a hole in it which he had probably picked up in the street) until I can pay you back. This is of more value to me than I can well express, Licky my boy. A very, very dear friend gave it to me when we parted, years ago﻿ ﻿… It’s a wrench﻿ ﻿… Still﻿—no, no﻿ ﻿… You must take it, you must take it. Licky, old man, shake hands, old horse. Shake hands, my boy.’ He then tottered to the bar, deeply moved, and paid up out of the five shillings which he had made as an afterthought. He asked after you, and said you were one of the noblest men on earth. I gave him your address, not being able to get out of it, but if I were you I should fly while there is yet time.”

It seemed to me that the advice was good and should be followed. I needed a change of air. London may have suited Doctor Johnson, but in the summer time it is not for the ordinary man. What I wanted, to enable me to give the public of my best (as the reviewer of a weekly paper, dealing with my last work, had expressed a polite hope that I would continue to do) was a little haven in the country somewhere.

I rang the bell.

“Sir?” said Mrs. Medley.

> “……顺带一提，在约维尔，我竟撞见了咱们的一位老熟人——偏偏是斯坦利·费瑟斯通豪·厄克里奇。还是那副活生生的原样——足足六英尺二，还发福得可观。我以为他还在国外。我最后听到的消息，是他搭一艘运牛船动身去了布宜诺斯艾利斯，全部行李是一支借来的烟斗。看样子他在英国已待了不少日子。我是在约维尔车站的茶点室遇见他的。我在等下行车，他是进城途中换车。一推开门，就听见一条洪亮的大嗓门正恳求柜台后的女士‘把它倒进锡杯里’；S. F. U. 果然就在那儿，穿一身邪门歪道的灰色法兰绒旧衣（我敢起誓，就是我上回见他穿的那身），夹鼻眼镜照例用姜汁汽水的铁丝箍在耳朵上，领子下沿和上衣上沿之间露出两英寸光脖子——你总记得，他从没能让一颗领扣恪尽职守。那天烈日当空，他还罩着一件雨衣。

> “他大呼小叫地招呼我，热情四溢。我说什么也不肯让我破费，非要尽地主之谊。吃喝完毕，他在口袋里摸索一阵，面露痛楚与讶异，把我拉到一边。‘听我说，利基老马，’他说，‘你知道我从不借钱。这有违我的原则。可我*非*得要两枚先令不可。亲爱的好伙计，能否行个好，借我两先令，撑到下星期二？我告诉你我要怎么办。（声音满怀深情）我把我这个押给你（掏出一枚恶劣的小小三便士，中间还带着窟窿，八成是街上捡的），等我回头还你钱。这东西于我的价值，实非言语所能表达，利基我的孩子。多年前分别时，一位顶顶亲爱的朋友把它送给了我……我真舍不得……不过——不，不成……你必须收下，你必须收下。利基老兄，握个手，老马。握个手，我的孩子。’随后他百感交集，踉跄着走向柜台，用他临时又讨来的五先令付了账。他问起你的近况，还说你是世上最高贵的男子汉之一。我把你的地址给了他——实在推脱不掉——可我要是你，就趁还来得及，赶紧远走高飞。”

我看这忠告很有道理，应当遵行。我正需要换换空气。伦敦或许合约翰逊博士的意，可到了夏天，它不属于寻常人。我需要的——好让我把最好的自己献给大众（某家周刊的评论家评到我上一部作品时，曾客客气气地表示希望我继续如此）——是乡间某处的一个小小避风港。

我按了铃。

“您叫我，先生？”梅德利太太应道。
