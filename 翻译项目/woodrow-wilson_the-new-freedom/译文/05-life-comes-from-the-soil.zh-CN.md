## IV Life Comes from the Soil / 生机源于沃土
===Original===

When I look back on the processes of history, when I survey the genesis of America, I see this written over every page: that the nations are renewed from the bottom, not from the top; that the genius which springs up from the ranks of unknown men is the genius which renews the youth and energy of the people. Everything I know about history, every bit of experience and observation that has contributed to my thought, has confirmed me in the conviction that the real wisdom of human life is compounded out of the experiences of ordinary men. The utility, the vitality, the fruitage of life does not come from the top to the bottom; it comes, like the natural growth of a great tree, from the soil, up through the trunk into the branches to the foliage and the fruit. The great struggling unknown masses of the men who are at the base of everything are the dynamic force that is lifting the levels of society. A nation is as great, and only as great, as her rank and file.
So the first and chief need of this nation of ours today is to include in the partnership of government all those great bodies of unnamed men who are going to produce our future leaders and renew the future energies of America. And as I confess that, as I confess my belief in the common man, I know what I am saying. The man who is swimming against the stream knows the strength of it. The man who is in the melee knows what blows are being struck and what blood is being drawn. The man who is on the make is the judge of what is happening in America, not the man who has made good; not the man who has emerged from the flood; not the man who is standing on the bank looking on, but the man who is struggling for his life and for the lives of those who are dearer to him than himself. That is the man whose judgment will tell you what is going on in America; that is the man by whose judgment I, for one, wish to be guided.
We have had the wrong jury; we have had the wrong group﻿—no, I will not say the wrong group, but too small a group﻿—in control of the policies of the United States. The average man has not been consulted, and his heart had begun to sink for fear he never would be consulted again.

===Chinese===

当我回顾历史进程、审视美国的起源时，我看到每一页上都赫然写着：国家的生机源于底层，而非来自顶层；从默默无闻之辈的行列中涌现出来的天才，正是重焕人民（the people）之青春与活力的天才。凡我所掌握的历史知识，以及所有滋养我思想的经验与观察，都使我更加坚信：人类生活的真正智慧，乃是凝练自普通人的经历。生命的功用、生机与果实，绝非自上而下流动；它犹如参天大树的自然生长，从土壤中生发，向上穿过树干，流入枝桠，直至繁茂的枝叶与累累硕果。身处万物基石之中的苦苦奋斗的无名大众，正是推动社会层级不断提升的原动力。一个国家的伟大，恰恰取决于其广大普通民众的伟大，而且仅以此为限。
因此，今日我们这个国家首要且核心的需求，是将所有那些孕育我们未来领袖、重焕美国未来生机的大批无名人士纳入政府的共同治理之中。当我坦言这一点、当我坦陈我对普通人的坚实信仰时，我深知自己在说些什么。逆流而泳者深知水流的湍急；身处混战者深知拳脚落于何处、鲜血因何而流；正在奋力打拼的人，才最有资格评判美国正在发生的一切，而不是那些已经功成名就的人；不是那些已经从洪流中脱险的人；不是那些站在岸边冷眼旁观的人，而是那些正在为自己的生存以及为比自身性命更珍贵的挚爱之人的生存而奋战的人。正是这个人的判断能够告诉你美国正在发生什么；正是这个人的判断，成为我个人所渴望据以接受指引的准则。
我们曾经选错了陪审团；曾经由错误的群体——不，我不想说是错误的群体，而是过于狭隘的极少数群体——掌控了美利坚合众国的政策。普通人从未被征询过意见，而他的心已经开始下沉，唯恐自己再也不会被咨询意见了。

Therefore, we have got to organize a government whose sympathies will be open to the whole body of the people of the United States, a government which will consult as large a proportion of the people of the United States as possible before it acts. Because the great problem of government is to know what the average man is experiencing and is thinking about. Most of us are average men; very few of us rise, except by fortunate accident, above the general level of the community about us; and therefore the man who thinks common thoughts, the man who has had common experiences, is almost always the man who interprets America aright. Isn’t that the reason that we are proud of such stories as the story of Abraham Lincoln﻿—a man who rose out of the ranks and interpreted America better than any man had interpreted it who had risen out of the privileged classes or the educated classes of America?
The hope of the United States in the present and in the future is the same that it has always been: it is the hope and confidence that out of unknown homes will come men who will constitute themselves the masters of industry and of politics. The average hopefulness, the average welfare, the average enterprise, the average initiative, of the United States are the only things that make it rich. We are not rich because a few gentlemen direct our industry; we are rich because of our own intelligence and our own industry. America does not consist of men who get their names into the newspapers; America does not consist politically of the men who set themselves up to be political leaders; she does not consist of the men who do most of her talking﻿—they are important only so far as they speak for that great voiceless multitude of men who constitute the great body and the saving force of the nation. Nobody who cannot speak the common thought, who does not move by the common impulse, is the man to speak for America, or for any of her future purposes. Only he is fit to speak who knows the thoughts of the great body of citizens, the men who go about their business every day, the men who toil from morning till night, the men who go home tired in the evenings, the men who are carrying on the things we are so proud of.

===Chinese===

因此，我们必须组建一个同理心向全体美利坚合众国人民敞开的政府，一个在采取行动之前尽可能广泛征求合众国人民意见的政府。因为政府治理的核心难题，在于了解普通人正在经历什么、在思考什么。我们大多数人都是普通人；除了幸运的机缘巧合之外，我们当中极少有人能够超然拔萃于周围社群的普遍水准；因此，思想与大众相通的人、拥有普通经历的人，几乎总是能够准确阐释美国的人。这难道不正是我们为诸如亚伯拉罕·林肯（Abraham Lincoln）的故事而深感自豪的原因吗？——这位出身草莽平民的人物，对美国的理解远比任何出自美国特权阶级或受精英教育阶级的人都要深刻得多。
美利坚合众国无论在当下还是在未来的希望，都与它一贯的希望别无二致：那就是坚信并期盼从默默无闻的家庭中，必将走出成为工业与政治主宰者的人物。美利坚合众国普通民众的希望、普遍的福祉、大众的进取心以及寻常百姓的开创精神，才是使国家富强的唯一源泉。我们之所以富强，并非因为少数绅士在指挥我们的工业；我们之所以富强，是因为我们自身的智慧与辛勤劳作。美国绝非由那些名字频频见诸报端的人构成；在政治上，美国绝非由那些自命为政治领袖的人构成；她也绝非由那些夸夸其谈的大多数演说者构成——他们之所以重要，仅仅在于他们能够替构成国家主体与中流砥柱的那群庞大而沉默的大众代言。凡是不能表达大众共同心声、不为共同冲动所驱使的人，就绝无资格代表美国，也无资格代表她未来的任何抱负。唯有了解广大公民群体思想的人，才配发表演说——那些每天忙于生计的人，那些从早到晚辛勤劳作的人，那些傍晚疲惫归家的人，那些支撑着令我们深感自豪之伟业的人。

You know how it thrills our blood sometimes to think how all the nations of the earth wait to see what America is going to do with her power, her physical power, her enormous resources, her enormous wealth. The nations hold their breath to see what this young country will do with her young unspoiled strength; we cannot help but be proud that we are strong. But what has made us strong? The toil of millions of men, the toil of men who do not boast, who are inconspicuous, but who live their lives humbly from day to day; it is the great body of toilers that constitutes the might of America. It is one of the glories of our land that nobody is able to predict from what family, from what region, from what race, even, the leaders of the country are going to come. The great leaders of this country have not come very often from the established, “successful” families.
I remember speaking at a school not long ago where I understood that almost all the young men were the sons of very rich people, and I told them I looked upon them with a great deal of pity, because, I said: “Most of you fellows are doomed to obscurity. You will not do anything. You will never try to do anything, and with all the great tasks of the country waiting to be done, probably you are the very men who will decline to do them. Some man who has been ‘up against it,’ some man who has come out of the crowd, somebody who has had the whip of necessity laid on his back, will emerge out of the crowd, will show that he understands the crowd, understands the interests of the nation, united and not separated, and will stand up and lead us.”
If I may speak of my own experience, I have found audiences made up of the “common people” quicker to take a point, quicker to understand an argument, quicker to discern a tendency and to comprehend a principle, than many a college class that I have lectured to﻿—not because the college class lacked the intelligence, but because college boys are not in contact with the realities of life, while “common” citizens are in contact with the actual life of day by day; you do not have to explain to them what touches them to the quick.
There is one illustration of the value of the constant renewal of society from the bottom that has always interested me profoundly.

你们深知，每当思及地球上的所有国家都在拭目以待美国将如何运用其力量——物质力量、巨大资源与惊人财富时，我们的血液有时是何等澎湃。各国屏息以待，想看看这个年轻的国家将如何施展其年轻而纯洁的力量；我们不能辜负他们的期望。
有时有人问我：“你认为一个普通的受教育程度不高的公民能够理解复杂的政治问题吗？”我的回答是：“他们理解得比那些被特定偏见蒙蔽了双眼的人要深刻得多。”我曾多次在讲演中发现，“普通民众”领会要点之敏捷、理解论证之迅速、洞察趋势与领悟原理之敏锐，往往胜过我曾授课的诸多大学班级——这并非因为大学班级的学生缺乏智力，而是因为大学生尚未接触现实生活的严峻磨砺，而“普通”公民每天都在与真实的现实生活直接碰撞；凡是触及他们痛处的事物，你根本无需向他们多作解释。
关于社会自下而上不断更新的价值，有一则例证向来令我深感兴趣。

The only reason why government did not suffer dry rot in the Middle Ages under the aristocratic system which then prevailed was that so many of the men who were efficient instruments of government were drawn from the church﻿—from that great religious body which was then the only church, that body which we now distinguish from other religious bodies as the Roman Catholic Church. The Roman Catholic Church was then, as it is now, a great democracy. There was no peasant so humble that he might not become a priest, and no priest so obscure that he might not become Pope of Christendom; and every chancellery in Europe, every court in Europe, was ruled by these learned, trained and accomplished men﻿—the priesthood of that great and dominant body. What kept government alive in the Middle Ages was this constant rise of the sap from the bottom, from the rank and file of the great body of the people through the open channels of the priesthood. That, it seems to me, is one of the most interesting and convincing illustrations that could possibly be adduced of the thing that I am talking about.
The only way that government is kept pure is by keeping these channels open, so that nobody may deem himself so humble as not to constitute a part of the body politic, so that there will constantly be coming new blood into the veins of the body politic; so that no man is so obscure that he may not break the crust of any class he may belong to, may not spring up to higher levels and be counted among the leaders of the state. Anything that depresses, anything that makes the organization greater than the man, anything that blocks, discourages, dismays the humble man, is against all the principles of progress. When I see alliances formed, as they are now being formed, by successful men of business with successful organizers of politics, I know that something has been done that checks the vitality and progress of society. Such an alliance, made at the top, is an alliance made to depress the levels, to hold them where they are, if not to sink them; and, therefore, it is the constant business of good politics to break up such partnerships, to reestablish and reopen the connections between the great body of the people and the offices of government.

在中世纪，在当时盛行的贵族制度下，政府之所以没有彻底朽坏崩溃，唯一的原因在于：众多行之有效的政府治理人才都是从教会选拔出来的——出自那个在当时是唯一教会的庞大宗教团体，即我们今天有别于其他宗教团体所称的罗马天主教会。当时的罗马天主教会正如今天一样，是一座伟大的民主殿堂。没有任何农夫地位卑微到不能成为神甫，也没有任何神甫默默无闻到不能晋升为基督教世界的教皇；欧洲的每一处内阁宰辅、每一个宫廷，都由这些博学多才、训练有素且造诣深厚的人士——即那个庞大而主导的团体的神职人员所治理。中世纪政府之所以能够保持生机，正是因为生命汁液通过神职人员的畅通渠道，从底层、从广大人民群众中源源不断地向上涌流。在我看来，对于我所论述的事理，这正是所能引证的最具说服力且引人入胜的例证之一。
保持政府纯洁的唯一途径，就是保持这些渠道畅通无阻，使任何人都不至因出身卑微而认为自己不属于政治机体的一部分，使政治机体的脉络中不断涌入新鲜血液；使任何人都不至因默默无闻而无法打破其所属阶级的藩篱，无法跃升至更高阶层并跻身国家领袖之列。凡是压抑人性、凡是使体制凌驾于个人之上、凡是阻碍、打击并令卑微者沮丧的一切，都违背了进步（progress）的一切原则。当我看到成功的商界人士与成功的政治操盘手结成同盟（正如他们当下正在结盟的那样）时，我深知已经出现了一种扼杀社会活力与进步的勾当。这种在顶层达成的同盟，旨在压制底层水准，将其禁锢在原地，甚至将其进一步沉沦；因此，良善政治恒久的使命，就在于打破这种利益集团（the interests）的伙伴关系，重建并重新打通广大人民（the people）与政府公职之间的血脉联系。

Today, when our government has so far passed into the hands of special interests; today, when the doctrine is implicitly avowed that only select classes have the equipment necessary for carrying on government; today, when so many conscientious citizens, smitten with the scene of social wrong and suffering, have fallen victims to the fallacy that benevolent government can be meted out to the people by kindhearted trustees of prosperity and guardians of the welfare of dutiful employees﻿—today, supremely, does it behoove this nation to remember that a people shall be saved by the power that sleeps in its own deep bosom, or by none; shall be renewed in hope, in conscience, in strength, by waters welling up from its own sweet, perennial springs. Not from above; not by patronage of its aristocrats. The flower does not bear the root, but the root the flower. Everything that blooms in beauty in the air of heaven draws its fairness, its vigor, from its roots. Nothing living can blossom into fruitage unless through nourishing stalks deep-planted in the common soil. The rose is merely the evidence of the vitality of the root; and the real source of its beauty, the very blush that it wears upon its tender cheek, comes from those silent sources of life that lie hidden in the chemistry of the soil. Up from that soil, up from the silent bosom of the earth, rise the currents of life and energy. Up from the common soil, up from the quiet heart of the people, rise joyously today streams of hope and determination bound to renew the face of the earth in glory.
I tell you, the so-called radicalism of our times is simply the effort of nature to release the generous energies of our people. This great American people is at bottom just, virtuous, and hopeful; the roots of its being are in the soil of what is lovely, pure, and of good report, and the need of the hour is just that radicalism that will clear a way for the realization of the aspirations of a sturdy race.
