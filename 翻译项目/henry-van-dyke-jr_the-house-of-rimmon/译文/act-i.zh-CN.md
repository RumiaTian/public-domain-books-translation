## Act I / 第一幕

### Scene I / 第一场

===Original===
Night, in the garden of Naaman at Damascus. At the left, on a slightly raised terrace, the palace, with softly gleaming lights and music coming from the open latticed windows. The garden is full of oleanders, roses, pomegranates, abundance of crimson flowers; the air is heavy with their fragrance: a fountain at the right is plashing gently: behind it is an arbour covered with vines. Near the centre of the garden stands a small, hideous image of the god Rimmon. Back of the arbour rises the lofty square tower of the House of Rimmon, which casts a shadow from the moon across the garden. The background is a wide, hilly landscape, with a high road passing over the mountains toward the snow-clad summits of Mount Hermon in the distance. Enter by the palace door, the lady Tsarpi, robed in red and gold, and followed by her maids, Khamma and Nubta. She remains on the terrace: they go down into the garden, looking about, and returning to her.

Khamma: There's no one here; the garden is asleep.

Nubta: The flowers are nodding, all the birds abed, And nothing wakes except the watchful stars!

Khamma: The stars are sentinels discreet and mute: How many things they know and never tell!

Tsarpi: (Impatiently.) Unlike the stars, how many things you tell And do not know! When comes your master home?

Nubta: Lady, his armour-bearer brought us word An hour ago, the master will be here At moonset, not before.

Tsarpi: He haunts the camp And leaves me much alone; yet I can pass The time of absence not unhappily, If I but know the time of his return. An hour of moonlight yet! Khamma, my mirror! These curls are ill arranged, this veil too low— So—that is better, careless maids! Withdraw— But warn me if your master should appear.

Khamma: Mistress, have no concern; for when we hear The clatter of his horse along the street, We'll run this way and lead your dancers down With song and laughter—you shall know in time.

Tsarpi: My guest is late; but he will surely come! Hunger and thirst will bring him to my feet. The man who burns to drain the cup of love— The priest whose greed of glory never fails— Both, both have need of me, and he will come. And I—what do I need? Why everything That helps my beauty to a higher throne; All that a priest can promise, all a man Can give, and all a god bestow, I need: This may a woman win, and this will I.

Rezon: By these, the eyes Of Tsarpi; and by this, the rod of Rimmon.

Tsarpi: Speak clearly; tell your plan.

Rezon: You know the host Of the Assyrian king has broken forth Again to conquer us. Envoys have come From Shalmaneser to demand surrender. Our king Benhadad wavers, for he knows His weakness. All the nobles, all the rich, Would purchase peace that they may grow more rich: Only the people and the soldiers, led By Naaman, would fight for liberty. Blind fools! For we two will rise Upon the city's fall. The common folk Shall suffer; Naaman shall sink with them In wreck; but I shall rise, and you shall rise Above me! You shall climb, through incense-smoke, And days of pomp, and nights of revelry, Glorious rites and ecstasies of love, Unto the topmost room in Rimmon's tower, The secret, lofty room, the couch of bliss, And the divine embraces of the god.

Tsarpi: (Throwing out her arms in exultation.) All, all I wish! What must I do for this?

Rezon: Turn Naaman away from thoughts of war; Or purchase him with love's delights to yield This point—I care not how—and afterwards The future shall be ours.

Tsarpi: And if I fail?

Rezon: I have another shaft. The last appeal, Before the king decides, is to the oracle Of Rimmon. You shall read the signs! A former priestess of his temple, you Shall be the interpreter of heaven, and speak A word to melt this brazen soldier's heart Within his breast.

Tsarpi: But if it flame instead?

Rezon: I know the way to quench that flame. The cup, The parting cup your hand shall give to him! What if the curse of Rimmon should infect That wine with sacred venom, secretly To work within his veins, week after week Corrupting all the currents of his blood, Dimming his eyes, wasting his flesh? What then? Would he prevail in war? Would he come back To glory, or to shame? What think you?

Tsarpi: I? I do not think; I only do my part. But can the gods bless this?

Rezon: The gods can bless Whatever they decree; their will makes right; And this is for the glory of the house Of Rimmon—and for thee, my queen. Come, come! The night grows dark: we'll perfect our alliance.

便哈达：（顺从地颓然靠回宝座。）我是临门之家忠信之子。求问神谕吧。但由谁来解读？

利汛：乃缦之妻瑟璧，少女时代曾在神庙中事奉，她将作这位大能之神的喉舌，作今日的高等女祭司。把祭牲带上来！

瑟璧：（单调地吟诵。）祭牲之血发黑，临门不悦，亚斯拉吐不悦；他们不愿与亚述交战，他们要与尼尼微之神立约。大马士革祸患将临，强敌将荒芜此地。故当与那公牛言和；听从临门之声。

如阿玛：不要惧怕！有一位更大的神，要以他全能的翅膀遮蔽你：你要投靠在他的盾牌和藤牌之下。

便哈达：回心转意吧，我的儿，你不可冒犯这咒诅。

乃缦：我的王啊，没有哪一种咒诅，比点燃一颗胸中烈火更为可怖——那是为出卖荣誉以苟延怯懦之命、每一口呼吸皆是耻辱的人所点燃！我若出卖追随我的人、出卖信赖我的城、出卖我誓言所系的国家，有什么王能护我免于我内心深处的鄙夷，有什么神能将我从那自造的地狱中释放？亚述的慈悲我晓得；那如同潜行的猛虎一般残忍。交出大马士革，她的街道将流淌无辜之血的江河；这城的心脏，那颗强健劳作的心，在野牛残暴的蹄下受伤、碾碎，将控告她的元帅——他正安然端坐于贵族之间，在某个惬意之处。我倒是安全了——免于未识之神们所威吓的愤怒，却要被我所认识的人永远咒骂——那才是我所怕的咒诅。

便哈达：我的儿，不要把话说的这样高。我们岂不应当在天上的众主权之前低头？那些看不见的掌权者，乃是神圣的。

乃缦：王啊，我在祭司的学问上未曾受教；然而我深知，在我们四围有隐秘的权势，主宰着凡人的祸福，非人力所能左右。倘若这些权势以爱与真理显现，我想他们必是神，我必敬拜他们。但若他们隐秘的旨意，显在纯粹全能之盲目的命令之中——无罪之处施罚，以不应受的灾祸击打穷人，在暗中以不义之箭刺穿无防备之人，注定无辜者于无尽痛苦中焚烧——我便不称这凶猛的全能为神圣。虽然我必须与每个人一样，承担我所受之生命的重担，我仍要保全我的灵魂不受惊吓，以稳定之心踏上真理与荣誉之路！但若我于此有误；倘若真有意愿残酷、不公、喜怒无常而又至高无上的神，我愿弃绝这些神的恩宠，站在人这一边，与人同受苦，为人而死。诸位大人可曾听见？那神谕向我——只向我一人——宣告：我若率军出征，便要遭报应。"非胜即败！"我紧握这一线生机！大马士革得自由，她的一切仇敌被击退，百姓得免于奴役，王在他古老的宝座上受尊崇——哦，这代价为何？凡存在的神，无论他们为何这一次胜利索取何等代价，我都甘愿支付。把这镀金的耻辱之记交给我带回去；我要在亚述王面前摇晃它，把它摔碎在他的牙齿上。
