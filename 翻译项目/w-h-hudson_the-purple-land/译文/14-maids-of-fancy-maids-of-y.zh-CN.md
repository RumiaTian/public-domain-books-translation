## XIV Maids of Fancy: Maids of Yí / 第十四章 遐想中的少女与伊河的少女
===Original===

The girl I have mentioned, whose name was Monica, and the child, called Anita, were the only persons there besides myself who were not carried away by the warlike enthusiasm of the moment. Monica, silent, pale, almost apathetic, was occupied serving maté to the numerous guests; while the child, when the shouting and excitement was at its height, appeared greatly terrified, and clung to Alday’s wife, trembling and crying piteously. No notice was taken of the poor little thing, and at length she crept away into a corner to conceal herself behind a faggot of wood. Her hiding-place was close to my seat, and after a little coaxing I induced her to leave it and come to me. She was a most forlorn little thing, with a white, thin face and large, dark, pathetic eyes. Her mean little cotton frock only reached to her knees, and her little legs and feet were bare. Her age was seven or eight; she was an orphan, and Alday’s wife, having no children of her own, was bringing her up, or, rather, permitting her to grow up under her roof. I drew her to me, and tried to soothe her tremors and get her to talk. Little by little she gained confidence, and began to reply to my questions; then I learnt that she was a little shepherdess, although so young, and spent most of the time every day in following the flock about on her pony. Her pony and the girl Monica, who was some relation﻿—cousin, the child called her﻿—were the two beings she seemed to have the greatest affection for.
“And when you slip off, how do you get on again?” I asked.
“Little pony is tame, and I never fall off,” she said. “Sometimes I get off, then I climb on again.”
“And what do you do all day long﻿—talk and play?”
“I talk to my doll; I take it on the pony when I go with the sheep.”
“Is your doll very pretty, Anita?”
No answer.
“Will you let me see your doll, Anita? I know I shall like your doll, because I like you.”
She gave me an anxious look. Evidently doll was a very precious being and had not met with proper appreciation. After a little nervous fidgeting she left me and crept out of the room; then presently she came back, apparently trying to screen something from the vulgar gaze in her scanty little dress. It was her wonderful doll﻿—the dear companion of her rambles and rides. With fear and trembling she allowed me to take it into my hands.

“那你从马上滑下来的时候，又是怎么重新骑上去的呢？”我问道。
“小马很温驯，我从来不会摔下来，”她说，“有时候我自己跳下来，然后又能爬上去。”
“那你整天都在做什么呢——跟谁说话，跟谁玩耍？”
“我跟我的洋娃娃说话；我赶着羊群骑马出去的时候，都会带上它。”
“你的洋娃娃生得很俊俏吗，安妮塔？”
没有回音。

她用充满警惕与焦虑的目光打量了我一眼。显然，那个洋娃娃是个极其珍贵的宝贝，且此前从未得到过大人们应有的赏识。局促不安地扭捏了一小会儿后，她离开了我，悄步溜出了房间；不多时她折返回来，在单薄短小的裙子下遮掩着什么，生怕被俗人的粗鄙目光窥见。这便是她那神奇非凡的洋娃娃——她在旷野漫游与驰骋时的挚友旅伴。带着惶恐与战栗，她终于战战兢兢地将它递到了我的手中。

It was, or consisted of, the forefoot of a sheep, cut off at the knee; on the top of the knee part a little wooden ball wrapped in a white rag represented the head, and it was dressed in a piece of red flannel﻿—a satyr-like doll, with one hairy leg and a cloven foot. I praised its pleasing countenance, its pretty gown and dainty little boots; and all I said sounded very precious to Anita, filling her with emotions of the liveliest pleasure.
“And do you never play with the dogs and cats and little lambs?” I asked.
“Not with the dogs and cats. When I see a very little lamb asleep I get down and go softly, softly and catch it. It tries to get away; then I put my finger in its mouth, and it sucks, and sucks; then it runs away.”
“And what do you like best to eat?”
“Sugar. When uncle buys sugar, aunt gives me a lump. I make doll eat some, and bite off one small piece and put it in pony’s mouth.”
“Which would you rather have, Anita﻿—a great many lumps of sugar, or a beautiful string of beads, or a little girl to play with?”
This question was rather too much for her neglected little brain, which had fed itself with such simple fare; so I was obliged to put it in various ways, and at last, when she understood that only one of the three things could be chosen, she decided in favour of a little girl to play with.
Then I asked her if she liked to hear stories; this also puzzled her, and after some cross-questioning I discovered that she had never heard a story, and did not know what it meant.
“Listen, Anita, and I will tell you a story,” I said. “Have you seen the white mist over the Yí in the morning﻿—a light, white mist that flies away when the sun gets hot?”
Yes, she often saw the white mist in the morning, she told me.
“Then I will tell you a story about the white mist and a little girl named Alma.”
“Little Alma lived close to the River Yí, but far, far from here, beyond the trees and beyond the blue hills, for the Yí is a very long river. She lived with her grandmother and with six uncles, all big tall men with long beards; and they always talked about wars, and cattle, and horse-racing, and a great many other important things that Alma could not understand. “既然如此，先生，那我再去替你采一束（ramo）百合花，趁这工夫你好静心琢磨一个讲给我听。”
“别去，”我说，“你不许再去替我采百合花了。瞧，我把我这枝还给你。”说着，我顺手将那束鲜花插戴在她乌黑的发辫之间，黑红辉映之下显得异样惊艳夺目，更赋予了这姑娘一种全新的动人风姿。“啊，莫妮卡，它们把你衬得太美了——还是让我替你取下来吧。”
可她硬是不肯由我取下。“我现在便走开一会儿，好留你一人替我琢磨故事，”她说着，红着脸别过身去准备迈步。
我于是拉住她的双手，强引着她面朝向我。“听着，莫妮卡，”我注视着她说，“你可知这些百合花蕴含着某种奇异的魔力吗？看它们是何等猩红似火；那是激情的色泽，因为它们饱蘸了热烈的痴情，能把我的心灼烧成一团烈火。如果你再采来哪怕一朵，莫妮卡，我便要给你讲一个吓得你浑身颤抖的故事——让你抖得像风中的柳叶，吓得脸色苍白如伊河（Yí 河）水面上的晨雾。”
听了我这番话，她抿嘴嫣然一笑；那笑容宛如一抹细碎的阳光穿透林荫倾洒在她明艳的面庞上。接着，她用近乎耳语般的轻柔嗓音说道：“那会是一个怎样的故事呢，先生？快讲给我听听吧，这样我才晓得究竟该不该再去替你采百合花呀。”
“那将是一个关于异乡行客的故事，他在树荫下偶遇了一位面色苍白温婉的姑娘，姑娘低垂着黑眸，纤手里攥着红百合；她向他祈求一个故事，可他能对她倾诉的，却唯有爱，爱，永无休止的爱。”
当我吐完最后一字，她温柔地将一双纤手从我掌心缓缓抽离，转身隐入了林木掩映之间，毫无疑问，她是被我这番炽热的话语吓得芳心乱颤，像一只受惊的小鹿躲避猎人般落荒而逃了。
那一刻我心底确实如此作想。然而并非如此，那些原本为宗教祈愿而采摘的百合花依然静静躺在我脚边的草地上，当她回眸匆匆瞥我的一瞬，那双羞涩的乌黑眼眸里毫无半分责怨；因为，尽管听了我那番严厉的警告，她离开却仅仅是为了替我寻觅更多这般危险炽烈的猩红花朵。
并非在那个我心如鹿撞、静候她归来的激荡时刻，而是在日后更平静释怀的岁月里，当莫妮卡已然化作过往岁月里一帧娇美恬淡的剪影之时，我才终于提笔谱就了如下的诗行。

===Original===

I am not so vain as to believe that they possess any great poetical merit, and introduce them principally to let the reader know how to pronounce the pretty name of that Oriental river, which it still keeps in remembrance of a vanished race.

> Standing silent, pale her face was,
> Pale and sweet to see:
> ’Neath the willows waiting for me,
> Willow-like was she,
> Smiling, blushing, trembling, bashful
> Maid of Yí.
>
> Willow-like she trembled, yet she
> Never fled from me;
> But her dove-like eyes were downcast,
> On the grass to see
> White feet standing: white thy feet were,
> Maid of Yí.
>
> Stalks of lilies in her hands were:
> Crimson lilies three,
> Placed I in her braids of black hair﻿—
> They were bright to see!
> Lift thy dark eyes, for I love thee,
> Maid of Yí!

===Chinese===

我还不至于自负到认定这几行诗句具备多么高超绝伦的诗意造诣，将其录于此处，主要不过是想让读者明了该如何呼唤那条东岸区河流的美丽芳名，它至今仍铭记着一个早已湮灭消逝的古老部族。
