## III Materials for a Pastoral / 三 田园诗的素材
===Original===

Leaving the eloquent old horse-tamer’s rancho early next morning, I continued my ride, jogging quietly along all day and, leaving the Florida department behind me, entered upon that of the Durazno. Here I broke my journey at an estancia where I had an excellent opportunity of studying the manners and customs of the Orientals, and where I also underwent experiences of a mixed character and greatly increased my knowledge of the insect world. This house, at which I arrived an hour before sunset to ask for shelter (“permission to unsaddle” is the expression the traveller uses), was a long, low structure, thatched with rushes, but the low, enormously thick walls were built of stone from the neighbouring sierras, in pieces of all shapes and sizes, and presenting, outwardly, the rough appearance of a stone fence. How these rudely piled-up stones, without cement to hold them together, had not fallen down was a mystery to me; and it was more difficult still to imagine why the rough interior, with its innumerable dusty holes and interstices, had never been plastered.
I was kindly received by a very numerous family, consisting of the owner, his hoary-headed old mother-in-law, his wife, three sons, and five daughters, all grown up. There were also several small children, belonging, I believe, to the daughters, notwithstanding the fact that they were unmarried. I was greatly amazed at hearing the name of one of these youngsters. Such Christian names as Trinity, Heart of Jesus, Nativity, John of God, Conception, Ascension, Incarnation, are common enough, but these had scarcely prepared me to meet with a fellow-creature named﻿—well, Circumcision! Besides the people, there were dogs, cats, turkeys, ducks, geese, and fowls without number. Not content with all these domestic birds and beasts, they also kept a horrid, shrieking parakeet, which the old woman was incessantly talking to, explaining to the others all the time, in little asides, what the bird said or wished to say, or, rather, what she imagined it wished to say. There were also several tame young ostriches, always hanging about the big kitchen or living-room on the lookout for a brass thimble, or iron spoon, or other little metallic bonne bouche to be gobbled up when no one was looking.

翌日大清早告别了那位雄辩的驯马老汉的农舍（*rancho*），我继续策马前行，整日里优哉游哉地慢跑着，将佛罗里达省抛在身后，踏入了杜拉斯诺省（Durazno）境内。在此处的一座大牧场（*estancia*），我歇足小憩，这让我获得了一个考察东岸人风俗人情的极好机会，也让我有了一番喜忧参半的体验，并大大增长了对昆虫世界的见识。我在日落前一小时赶到这处宅院请求留宿（过路旅人惯用的客套话叫“乞准卸鞍”）。那是一座狭长低矮的建筑，屋顶铺着芦苇，但那低矮却异常厚重的墙壁，则是就地取材自邻近山丘的石块砌成，碎石大小各异、形状不一，从外面看宛如一道粗粝的石围墙。这些未经泥灰黏合便粗笨堆叠而成的石墙究竟是如何屹立不倒的，于我委实是个谜团；而更令人费解的是，屋内那遍布尘土、千疮百孔的粗糙石缝与间隙，竟从未曾抹过泥灰。

迎接我的是一大家子人，人口极为兴旺，包括户主本人、他白发苍苍的老岳母、他的妻子、三个儿子以及五个皆已成年的闺女。此外还有几个垂髫小儿，据我推测，多半是那几个虽未婚配的闺女所生。在听到其中一个孩童的名讳时，我着实大吃一惊。诸如三位一体、耶稣之心、圣灵降孕、天主若望、圣母受孕、耶稣升天、道成肉身之类的教名在当地早已屡见不鲜，但即便如此，我也绝未料到世上竟真有人给同胞起名叫——嗯，割礼！除了这一大家子人，院里院外还有数不清的家犬、花猫、火鸡、鸭子、鹅和土鸡。有了这一大群家禽家畜犹嫌不足，他们居然还养了一只嘶声怪叫、烦人至极的长尾小鹦鹉；老太太整天没完没了地同它说话，还时不时压低声音朝旁人解释这鸟儿说了什么、想说什么，或者更确切地说，是她自己臆想这鸟儿想表达什么。此外还有好几只驯养的小美洲鸵，成天在大灶房或起居室里晃荡，眼睛贼溜溜地盯着黄铜顶针、铁勺子或其他细小金属零碎，只要有人稍不留神，便被它们一口吞下肚去。

A pet armadillo kept trotting in and out, in and out, the whole evening, and a lame gull was always standing on the threshold in everybody’s way, perpetually wailing for something to eat﻿—the most persistent beggar I ever met in my life.
The people were very jovial, and rather industrious for so indolent a country. The land was their own, the men tended the cattle, of which they appeared to have a large number, while the women made cheeses, rising before daylight to milk the cows.
During the evening two or three young men﻿—neighbours, I imagine, who were paying their addresses to the young ladies of the establishment﻿—dropped in; and after a plentiful supper, we had singing and dancing to the music of the guitar, on which every member of the family﻿—excepting the babies﻿—could strum a little.
About eleven o’clock I retired to rest, and, stretching myself on my rude bed of rugs, in a room adjoining the kitchen, I blessed these simple-minded, hospitable people. Good heavens, thought I to myself, what a glorious field is waiting here for some new Theocritus! How unutterably worn out, stilted, and artificial seems all the so-called pastoral poetry ever written when one sits down to supper and joins in the graceful *Cielo* or *Pericon* in one of these remote, semi-barbarous South American estancias! I swear I will turn poet myself, and go back some day to astonish old blasé Europe with something so﻿—so﻿—What the deuce was that? My sleepy soliloquy was suddenly brought to a most lame and impotent conclusion, for I had heard a sound of terror﻿—the unmistakable *zz‑zzing* of an insect’s wings. It was the hateful vinchuca. Here was an enemy against which British pluck and six-shooters are of no avail, and in whose presence one begins to experience sensations which are not usually supposed to enter into the brave man’s breast. Naturalists tell us that it is the *Connorhinus infectans*, but, as that information leaves something to be desired, I will proceed in a few words to describe the beast. It inhabits the entire Chilean, Argentine, and Oriental countries, and to all the dwellers in this vast territory it is known as the vinchuca; for, like a few volcanoes, deadly vipers, cataracts, and other sublime natural objects, it has been permitted to keep the ancient name bestowed on it by the aborigines.

一整晚，一只当宠物饲养的犰狳（armadillo）不停地在屋里屋外小跑穿梭，进进出出；一只跛足的海鸥则始终伫立在门槛当中挡着路，没完没了地哀鸣讨食——这真是我平生所见最执迷不悟的乞丐。

这家人格外欢畅豁达，在这样一个闲适怠惰的国度里也算得上颇为勤快。土地是他们自家的产业，男人们照料牲畜——他们拥有的牛群数量似乎相当可观；女人们则操持奶酪作坊，天色未明便起身挤奶。

入夜时分，两三个年轻小伙子——我想大概是邻近的后生，特来向这家的年轻姑娘献殷勤——串门溜达了进来；在享用了一顿丰盛的晚餐后，我们便伴着吉他的乐声载歌载舞，全家人除了襁褓中的婴孩，人人都能在琴弦上胡乱拨弄几下。

大约十一点光景我便退席歇息，在紧邻灶房的一间屋里，我将身子舒展在铺着粗毯的简陋卧榻上，心中虔诚祝福着这群淳朴好客的人家。天哪，我暗自思忖，此地岂不是在翘首以盼一位当代的忒奥克里托斯（Theocritus）降临吗！当你坐下来共进晚餐，在这荒僻半蛮荒的南美牧场里跳起优美的谢洛舞（*Cielo*）或贝里孔舞（*Pericon*）时，世间那些所谓的田园诗听来是何等陈腐、造作而生硬！我发誓自己也要改行当诗人，有朝一日回到那暮气沉沉的老欧洲，用某种如此——如此——见鬼，那是什么动静？我昏昏欲睡中的喃喃自语骤然落得个荒唐至极的收场，因为我听见了一声令人毛骨悚然的声响——那是绝不会听错的、某种昆虫振翅的嗡嗡怪鸣。那便是可憎的锥蝽（*vinchuca*）。面对这种强敌，英国人的血气与转轮手枪统统无济于事；在它的阴影下，哪怕最无畏的勇士心头也不免泛起寻常英雄绝不该有的骇异。博物学家告诉我们此物学名叫 *Connorhinus infectans*，但鉴于这番解释未免辞不达意，我姑且用几句话来描画描画这只恶兽。它遍布智利、阿根廷与东岸全境，在这片辽阔疆域上的所有居民口中，它一概被称为“锥蝽”；盖因它与少数几座火山、致命毒蛇、巨瀑及其他崇高的大自然造物一样，有幸保留了土著原住民赐予它的古老芳名。

===Original===

It is all over of a blackish-brown colour, as broad as a man’s thumbnail, and flat as the blade of a table-knife﻿—when fasting. By day it hides, bug-like, in holes and chinks, but no sooner are the candles put out, than forth it comes to seek whom it may devour; for, like the pestilence, it walks in darkness. It can fly, and in a dark room knows where you are and can find you. Having selected a nice tender part, it pierces the skin with its proboscis or rostrum, and sucks vigorously for two or three minutes, and, strange to say, you do not feel the operation, even when lying wide awake. By that time the creature, so attenuated before, has assumed the figure, size, and general appearance of a ripe gooseberry, so much blood has it drawn from your veins. Immediately after it has left you the part begins to swell up and burn as if stung by nettles. That the pain should come after and not during the operation is an arrangement very advantageous to the vinchuca, and I greatly doubt whether any other bloodsucking parasite has been equally favoured by nature in this respect.
Imagine then my sensations when I heard the sound of not one, but two or three pairs of wings! I tried to forget the sound and go to sleep. I tried to forget about those rough old walls full of interstices﻿—a hundred years old they were, my host had informed me. Most interesting old house, thought I; and then very suddenly a fiery itching took possession of my great toe. There it is! said I; heated blood, late supper, dancing, and all that. I can almost imagine that something has actually bitten me, when of course nothing of that kind has happened. Then, while I was furiously rubbing and scratching it, feeling a badger-like disposition to gnaw it off, my left arm was pierced with red-hot needles. My attentions were quickly transferred to that part; but soon my busy hands were called elsewhere, like a couple of hard-worked doctors in a town afflicted with an epidemic; and so all night long, with only occasional snatches of miserable sleep, the contest went on.
I rose early, and, going to a wide stream, a quarter of a mile from the house, took a plunge which greatly refreshed me and gave me strength to go in quest of my horse. Poor brute!

它浑身呈焦黑褐色，足有一枚男人的大拇指指甲盖宽，在空腹绝食时，扁平得犹如餐刀的刀刃。白日里它如臭虫般藏匿于墙洞石缝之间，然而蜡烛刚一吹熄，它便倾巢而出，寻觅可吞噬的血肉；盖因它如同瘟疫一般，在黑暗中肆虐游荡。它没有臭虫那股难闻的刺鼻气味，反倒散发着一股类似青香蕉的淡淡清香；它下口叮咬时轻微得令人毫无觉察，甚至比蚊蚋的刺痛还要隐秘；然而它的胃口却深不可测。它吸足了血，身躯便膨胀成一颗饱满圆润的浆果；一旦被它盯上，次日你便会发现身上肿起一块婴儿拳头大小的硬块，灼痛发痒，至少两三日方能消退。

我刚在黑暗中辨认出那声不祥的振翅声，便腾地从卧榻上跳将起来，划亮一根火柴，只见一只巨大的锥蝽正泰然自若地停在我的枕头旁。我当即将其斩于枕上；可未等我再次躺倒，又是一阵嗡嗡声在耳畔回响，紧接着是第二声、第三声。石墙那些未经抹灰的孔洞深处，显然栖息着成千上万只这般恶灵。那一整夜，我都在同这群吸血狂魔作着一场惊心动魄、难分难解的恶斗；到了黎明破晓时分，我不仅精疲力竭、遍体鳞伤，而且怒不可遏、痛苦不堪，只觉得那座农舍里的一切——老妇人、鹦鹉、美貌姑娘、犰狳，乃至我昨夜赞不绝口的诗意田园风光——全都变得可憎可鄙、不可理喻。我一瘸一拐地踱出门，只想着赶紧套马扬长而去，可祸不单行，昨夜还活蹦乱跳的坐骑，不知何时竟也瘸了一条腿。

I had intended giving him a day’s rest, so pleasant and hospitable had the people shown themselves; but now I shuddered at the thought of spending another night in such a purgatory. I found him so lame that he could scarcely walk, and so returned to the house on foot and very much cast down. My host consoled me by assuring me that I would sleep the siesta all the better for having been molested by those “little things that go about,” for in this very mild language he described the affliction. After breakfast, at noon, acting on his hint, I took a rug to the shade of a tree and, lying down, quickly fell into a profound sleep, which lasted till late in the afternoon.
That evening visitors came again, and we had a repetition of the singing, dancing, and other pastoral amusements, till near midnight; then, thinking to cheat my bedfellows of the night before, I made my simple bed in the kitchen. But here also the vile vinchucas found me, and there were, moreover, dozens of fleas that waged a sort of guerilla warfare all night, and in this way exhausted my strength and distracted my attention, while the more formidable adversary took up his position. My sufferings were so great that before daybreak I picked up my rugs and went out a distance from the house to lie down on the open plain, but I carried with me a smarting body and got but little rest. When morning came I found that my horse had not yet recovered from his lameness.
“Do not be in a hurry to leave us,” said my host, when I spoke of it. “I perceive that the little animals have again fought with and defeated you. Do not mind it; in time you will grow accustomed to them.”
How *they* contrived to endure it, or even to exist, was a puzzle to me; but possibly the vinchucas respected them, and only dined when, like the giant in the nursery rhyme, they “smelt the blood of an Englishman.”
I again enjoyed a long siesta, and when night came resolved to place myself beyond the reach of the vampires, and so, after supper, went out to sleep on the plain. About midnight, however, a sudden storm of wind and rain drove me back to the shelter of the house, and the next morning I rose in such a deplorable state that I deliberately caught and saddled my horse, though the poor beast could scarcely put one foot on the ground.

鉴于这家人待我如此亲善好客，我原本打算让坐骑在此歇息一日；可眼下一想到还要在这样的人间地狱里再熬上一宿，我不禁不寒而栗。我发现马匹瘸得几乎无法迈步，只得垂头丧气地徒步踱回宅中。我的房东瞧出了我的狼狈窘相，并立刻猜出了缘由。

“啊，朋友，”他体谅地叹道，“昨夜那些小飞虫（*bichitos*）定然是折腾得您够呛吧。”他竟用如此轻描淡写的词句来描摹这场酷刑。晌午用罢便饭，依照他的指点，我抱了一领毡毯挪到大树阴凉下，刚躺倒便沉沉睡去，这一觉直睡到日头偏西。

入夜时分，昨夜那帮后生再度登门，唱歌、跳舞与其他田园风雅之戏又重演了一遍，直至夜深子时；随后，我心想着避开昨夜那帮床笫之客，便把铺盖卷挪到了灶房泥地上。然而在此处，那些该死的锥蝽依然嗅迹而来，更有数以百计的跳蚤整夜打着游击战，教我力竭神散、顾此失彼，好让那更为致命的大敌从容就位。我受尽折磨，天色未明便收拾起铺盖，远远跑出屋外，躺在露天荒原上；可我浑身焦灼刺痛，压根合不上眼。清晨重返马厩，我的坐骑瘸腿依旧毫无起色。

“休要急着动身离去，”当我提及此事时，房东劝阻道，“我瞧得出，那些小生灵昨夜又同您杀了一场，且把您杀得大败。无须介怀，假以时日，您自会习以为常的。”

他们全家究竟是如何忍受这般煎熬乃至存活下来的，于我简直是个谜；但兴许锥蝽对他们还留有一丝敬意，唯有在嗅到“英国人的血肉”时——正如同童谣里的吃人巨魔一般——才肯放开肚皮大快朵颐。

白天我又舒舒服服补了一大觉，待夜幕降临，我下定决心彻底摆脱吸血鬼的魔爪，晚饭后便径直走入辽阔荒原露宿。然而半夜时分，一场突如其来的狂风骤雨将我逼回屋檐下避雨。次日清晨醒来时，我浑身狼狈凄凉，顾不得许多，执意抓来坐骑备上鞍鞯，尽管可怜的牲口几乎连一只蹄子也无法着地。

My friends laughed good-humouredly when they saw me making these resolute preparations for departure. After partaking of bitter maté, I rose and thanked them for their hospitality.
“You surely do not intend leaving us on that animal!” said my host. “He is unfit to carry you.”
“I have no other,” I replied, “and am anxious to reach my destination.”
“Had I known this I would have offered you a horse before,” he returned, and then he sent one of his sons to drive the horses of the estancia into the corral.
Selecting a good-looking animal from the herd, he presented it to me, and as I did not have money enough to buy a fresh horse whenever I wanted one, I accepted the gift very gladly. The saddle was quickly transferred to my new acquisition, and, once more thanking these good people and bidding adieu, I resumed my journey.
When I gave my hand before leaving to the youngest, and also, to my mind, the prettiest of the five daughters of the house, instead of smiling pleasantly and wishing me a prosperous journey, like the others, she was silent, and darted a look at me, which seemed to say, “Go, sir; you have treated me badly, and you insult me by offering your hand; if I take it, it is not because I feel disposed to forgive you, but only to save appearances.”
At the same moment, when she bestowed that glance on me which said so much, a look of intelligence passed over the faces of the other people in the room. All this revealed to me that I had just missed a very pretty little idyllic flirtation, conducted in very novel circumstances. Love cometh up as a flower, and men and charming women naturally flirt when brought together. Yet it was hard to imagine how I could have started a flirtation and carried it on to its culminatory point in that great public room, with all those eyes on me; dogs, babes, and cats tumbling about my feet; ostriches staring covetously at my buttons with great vacant eyes; and that intolerable parakeet perpetually reciting “How the waters came down at Lodore,” in its own shrieky, beaky, birdy, hurdy-gurdy, parrot language. Tender glances, soft whispered words, hand-touchings, and a thousand little personal attentions, showing which way the emotions tend, would scarcely have been practicable in such a place and in such conditions, and new signs and symbols would have to be invented to express the feelings of the heart.

他从群马中挑选了一匹相貌堂堂的好牲口馈赠予我；既然我囊中羞涩，无法逢遇难关便随手买马，我便万分欣喜地笑纳了这份重礼。马鞍很快换到了我的新坐骑背上；我再次谢过这群好心人，互道珍重，重踏征途。

临行前，我向这户人家五个女儿中最年幼、且在我眼里生得最标致的一位伸出手去，她却未曾像其他人那样绽开笑颜祝我一路顺风，反而一言不发，狠狠剜了我一眼，那目光仿佛在说：“走吧，先生；你待我薄情寡义，此刻伸手同我作别纯属轻薄折辱；我若握了你的手，绝非出于宽宥之意，不过是逢场作戏遮人耳目罢了。”

恰在此时，当她向我投来那饱含深意的一瞥之际，屋里其他人的脸上掠过了一抹心照不宣的会意神色。这一切让我恍然大悟：我方才竟在极其离奇的境遇下，生生错过了一场极为甜美动人的田园艳遇！爱情如花般破土萌生，男女风云际会，眉目传情本是水到渠成。然而叫人难以想象的是，在那间大庭广众的公用屋厅里，在众目睽睽之下——脚下群狗、婴孩与猫儿乱作一团，几只美洲鸵睁着空洞硕大的眼珠贪婪地觊觎着我的衣纽，那只该死的长尾小鹦鹉则用它那尖厉、多嘴、鸟里鸟气、像手摇风琴般的破锣嗓门没完没了地朗诵着《洛多尔的瀑布如何奔流》（*How the waters came down at Lodore*）——我又该如何掀开一场罗曼史的序幕，并将其引向高潮呢？脉脉含情的凝眸、软语呢喃的耳语、指尖轻触以及千百种表露心迹的细腻体贴，在那样一个场合、那样一种境况下，几乎毫无立足之地；若要抒发心曲，恐怕非得独创一套全新的肢体哑谜不可。

===Original===

And doubtless these Orientals, living all together in one great room, with their children and pets, like our very ancient ancestors, the pastoral Aryans, do possess such a language. And this pretty language I should have learnt from the most willing of teachers, if those venomous vinchucas had not dulled my brain with their persecutions and made me blind to a matter which had not escaped the observation of even unconcerned lookers-on. Riding away from the estancia, the feeling I experienced at having finally escaped from these execrable “little things that go about” was not one of unmixed satisfaction.

===Chinese===

毫无疑问，这些东岸人全家老小与孩童宠物同聚一堂生活在一间硕大屋宇之下，正如我们极古老的先祖——那些过着游牧生活的雅利安人一样，确实掌握着这套独特的哑谜语言。倘若不是那些恶毒的锥蝽百般迫害、把我的脑仁折磨得麻木迟钝，教我看不穿连旁观者都心知肚明的情愫，我本可以向这位最热心的女先生把这门美妙的语言学得透彻纯熟。策马驰离大牧场时，回味着终于逃脱这群可恶“小爬虫”魔爪的经历，我心头泛起的滋味，却并非单纯的庆幸与快慰。
