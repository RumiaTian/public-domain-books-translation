## XX A Ghastly Gift / 第二十章 骇人的赠礼
===Original===

Day had just dawned when I rose to join Mariano at the fire he had already kindled to heat the water for his early maté. I did not like the idea of lying there concealed amongst the trees like some hunted animal for an indefinite time; moreover, I had been advised by Santa Coloma to proceed directly to the Lomas de Rocha, on the south coast, in the event of a defeat, and this now seemed to me the best thing to do. It had been very pleasant lying there “under the greenwood tree,” while those veracious stories of hags, *lampalaguas*, and apparitions had proved highly entertaining; but a long spell, a whole month perhaps, of that kind of life was not to be thought of; and if I did not get to Rocha now, before the rural police were set to catch runaway rebels, it would perhaps be impossible to do so later on. I determined, therefore, to go my own way, and, after drinking bitter maté, I caught and saddled the dun horse. I really had not deserved the severe censure Lechuza had passed on me the previous evening in reference to horse-stealing, for I had taken the dun with very little more compunction than one is accustomed to feel in England when “borrowing” an umbrella on a rainy day. To all people in all parts of the world, a time comes when to appropriate their neighbour’s goods is held not only justifiable, but even meritorious; to Israelites in Egypt, Englishmen under a cloud in their own moist island, and to Orientals running away after a fight. By keeping the dun over thirty hours in my possession I had acquired a kind of prescriptive right to it, and now began to look on it as my very own; subsequent experience of his endurance and other good qualities enables me to endorse the Oriental saying that a “stolen horse carries you well.”
Bidding farewell to my companions in defeat, who had certainly not been frightened out of their imaginations, I rode forth just when it was beginning to grow light. Roads and houses I studiously avoided, travelling on at an easy gallop, which took me about ten miles an hour, till noon; then I rested at a small rancho, where I fed and watered my horse and recruited my own energies with roast beef and bitter maté. On again till dark; by that time I had covered about forty miles, and began to feel both hungry and tired.

破晓方至，我便爬起身来走到火堆旁同马里亚诺会合，他早已生起了火，正忙着烧水冲泡清晨的第一剂马黛茶（maté）。我打心眼里厌恶像被猎犬穷追不舍的野兽那般、毫无指望地无限期潜伏在深林灌木丛里的念头；加之圣科洛马早先曾千叮咛万嘱咐，一旦战败，便让我径直奔赴南部海岸的罗恰丘陵（Lomas de Rocha），眼下看来这无疑是上上之策。尽管“在绿林树荫之下”高卧荒野确实惬意无比，而那些关于恶婆娘、兰帕拉瓜巨蚺（*lampalagua*）和孤魂还魂的逼真奇谈亦教人乐不可支；但若要长年累月——哪怕整整一个月——过这等东躲西藏的亡命生涯，那是无论如何也教人消受不起的；况且若是眼下趁着乡勇警宪尚未大规模出动缉拿叛乱逃犯之际逃往罗恰，日后恐怕便再难有这般生路了。思虑至此，我决意独自行路，在咽下一盅苦涩的马黛茶之后，我套上并备好了那匹黄膘马的马鞍。就盗马一事而言，前一天傍晚猫头鹰对我施加的严厉斥责委实有失公允，皆因我牵走那匹黄马时的内疚自责，并不比一个英国绅士在阴雨绵绵的日子里随手“顺走”一把雨伞时的愧疚多上多少。凡是生息在天南海北各个角落的苍生黎庶，终归会迎来那么一个将侵吞占有邻人财物不仅视为天经地义、甚至引为功勋壮举的特定关头：正如在埃及寄居的以色列人，在自己阴湿多雨的本岛上倒了大霉的英格兰臣民，以及在沙场折戟后仓皇跑路的东岸汉子。鉴于这匹黄膘马已被我据为己有超过了三十多个小时（30 多个小时），我仿佛已然在法理上对其享有了某种时效占有权，如今索性心安理得地将其视作自己的私产；而日后它在长途跋涉中所展现出的惊人耐力与诸般神骏品质，更是让我由衷印证了东岸那句古老谚语——“偷来的骏马最脚下生风”。
同那几位并未在沙场败局中被吓破丰富想象力的难兄难弟们依依作别之后，正当东方既白之际，我翻身上马启程远行。一路上我煞费苦心地刻意回避了官道与村舍，任由坐骑以舒缓自如的颠步疾行，每小时约莫能跑出十英里（每小时约 10 英里）的程程，直至正午时分；随后我在一座僻静的小农舍（rancho）歇脚，给战马喂了草料饱饮了清泉，自己亦就着烤牛肉与苦马黛茶重振了周身元气。饭后扬鞭继续赶路直至天色向晚；到彼时为止我已纵马奔驰了约四十英里（约 40 英里），腹中雷鸣饥肠辘辘，浑身上下亦开始泛起阵阵倦意。

I had passed several ranchos and estancia houses, but was shy of seeking entertainment at any of them, and so went farther, only to fare worse. When the brief twilight was darkening to night I came upon a broad cart-track, leading, I suppose, to Montevideo from the eastern part of the country, and, seeing a long, low rancho near it, which I recognized as a pulperia, or store, by the flagstaff planted before it, I resolved to purchase some refreshment for myself, then to ride on a mile or two and spend the night under the stars﻿—a safe roof if an airy one. Tying my horse to the gate, I went into the porch-like projection at the end of the rancho, which I found divided from the interior by the counter, with its usual grating of thick iron bars to protect the treasures of gin, rum, and comestibles from drunken or quarrelsome customers. As soon as I came into the porch I began to regret having alighted at the place, for there, standing at the counter, smoking and drinking, were about a dozen very rough-looking men. Unfortunately for me, they had tied their horses under the shadow of a clump of trees some distance from the gate, so that I had missed seeing them on my arrival. Once amongst them, however, my only plan was to disguise my uneasiness, be very polite, get my refreshments, then make my escape as speedily as possible. They stared rather hard at me, but returned my salutation courteously; then going to a disengaged corner of the counter, I rested my left elbow on it and called for bread, a box of sardines, and a tumbler of wine.
“If you will join me, señores, the table is spread,” said I; but they all declined my invitation with thanks, and I began to eat my bread and sardines.
They appeared to be all persons living in the immediate neighbourhood, for they addressed each other familiarly and were conversing about love matters. One of them, however, soon dropped out of the conversation, and, edging away from the others, stood a little space apart, leaning against the wall on the side of the porch farthest from me. I began to notice this man very particularly, for it was plain to see that I had excited his interest in an extraordinary manner, and I did not like his scrutiny.

沿途我曾途经数座农舍和大庄园（estancia）府邸，但我生性多疑，唯恐自投罗网，不敢冒失前往任何一处打尖求宿，只得硬着头皮继续向前摸索，孰料竟是一步步滑向了更加险恶的境地。当那短暂的黄昏薄暮渐次被漆黑夜幕吞噬之际，我撞上了一条宽阔的车马大道，依我揣度，这大概是从东部地区通往蒙得维的亚（Montevideo）的交通要道；见近旁耸立着一座狭长低矮的农舍，且门前竖立着一根高耸的旗杆，我一眼便认出这是一间杂货铺兼小酒馆（pulperia），于是决计在此采购些果腹口粮，随后策马再往前跑上一两英里，在璀璨繁星之下露天露宿——纵然通风透气了些，好歹算是一顶安全可靠的天幕。我把缰绳系在篱笆门桩上，迈步跨入了农舍尽头一处宛如门廊般的凸出前厅，发觉前厅与内室之间被一道厚重的柜台隔开，柜台上方照例竖着一排粗壮的铁栅栏，以防酗酒滋事或寻衅斗殴的泼皮无赖劫掠里头珍藏的金酒、朗姆酒与各类罐头口粮。然而甫一踏入这处门廊，我心头便立刻泛起了一阵悔意，暗怪自己不该冒失在此下马停留，皆因就在柜台前，正倚着十来个（约 12 个）面相极其凶悍粗鄙的粗鲁汉子，一边吞云吐雾一边开怀畅饮。倒霉的是，他们把坐骑拴在了距离篱笆门有些距离的一丛树荫底下，以至于我进门前竟未曾察觉半点风吹草动。然而既然已然误入狼窝，眼下唯一的上策便是极力掩饰内心的不安与慌乱，举止务必温文尔雅，买齐干粮之后便以迅雷不及掩耳之势溜之大吉。众人拿毒辣的目光狠狠上下打量了我一番，倒也还算客客气气地回应了我的颔首致意；随后我自顾自踱步到柜台一处无人的闲置角落，左肘斜支在台面上，张口要了干面包、一盒沙丁鱼罐头与满满一杯红酒。
“列位爷台若是不嫌弃，酒菜已然齐备，且请同赏光吧，”我客套地让道；众人皆道谢婉拒了我的美意，于是我便自顾自啃起面包与沙丁鱼来。
瞧这伙人的神态言谈，大抵全是近乡近邻的土著熟客，彼此间推心置腹嬉笑怒骂，正唾沫横飞地高谈阔论着男女风月私情。然而未消多时，其中一人便悄然退出了谈话圈子，不动声色地同众人拉开了距离，独自抱臂斜倚在距离我最远的外侧门廊土墙边。我开始格外警惕地留意起这个汉子，皆因任谁都能一眼看穿，我显然以一种异乎寻常的诡异方式激起了他强烈的兴趣，而他那道肆无忌惮的毒辣窥视，更是教我脊背发凉、浑身不自在。

He was, without exception, the most murderous-looking villain I have ever had the misfortune to meet: that was the deliberate opinion I came to before I formed a closer acquaintance with him. He was a broad-chested, powerful-looking man of medium height; his hands he kept concealed under the large cloth poncho he wore, and he had on a slouch hat that just allowed his eyes to be seen under the rim. They were truculent, yellowish-green eyes, that seemed to grow fiery and dim and fiery again by turns, yet never for a single instant were they averted from my face. His black hair hung to his shoulders, and he also had a bristly moustache, which did not conceal his brutal mouth, nor was there any beard to hide his broad, swarthy jowl. His jaws were the only part of him that had any motion, while he stood there, still as a bronze statue, watching me. At intervals he ground his teeth, after which he would slap his lips together two or three times, while a slimy froth, most sickening to see, gathered at the corners of his mouth.
“Gandara, you are not drinking,” said one of the gauchos, turning to him. He shook his head slightly without speaking or taking his eyes off my face; whereupon the man who had spoken smiled and resumed his conversation with the others.
The long, intense, soul-trying scrutiny this brutal wretch had subjected me to came to a very sudden end. Quick as lightning a long, broad knife flashed out from its concealment under his poncho, and with one catlike bound he was before me, the point of his horrid weapon touching my poncho just over the pit of my stomach.
“Do not move, rebel,” he said in a husky voice. “If you move one hair’s breadth, that moment you die.”
The other men all ceased talking and looked on with some interest, but did not offer to interfere or make any remark.
For one moment I felt as if an electric shock had gone through me, and then instantly I was calm﻿—never, in fact, have I felt more calm and collected than at that terrible moment. ’Tis a blessed instinct of self-preservation which nature has provided us with; feeble, timid men possess it in common with the strong and brave, as weak, persecuted wild animals have it as well as those that are fierce and bloodthirsty.

毫无悬念，这是我这辈子有幸撞见过的恶棍之中，模样最为凶神恶煞、杀气腾腾的刽子手雏形：这是在同他深交过招之前，我心头经过深思熟虑后得出的确凿定论。他身材中等，却生着一副极其宽阔敦实的膀臂胸膛，浑身上下透着一股骇人的悍勇蛮力；双手始终深藏在身上那件宽大的粗布毛毡大氅（poncho）底下，头顶歪戴着一顶软呢宽檐帽，帽檐压得极低，仅能勉强露出帽子底下的两只招子。那是一双穷凶极恶、泛着黄绿凶光的狠毒眼珠，眸中的凶芒时而暴涨炽烈、时而晦暗阴沉，却自始至终连半刻都未曾从我的面庞上挪开过分毫。他如墨的乱发披垂及肩，蓄着一抹硬扎扎如钢针般的胡须，却丝毫遮掩不住那张残忍嗜血的血盆大口，亦无长髯去遮盖那张宽阔黝黑的下颚骨。就在他宛如一尊青铜凶神般凝固在原地死死锁定我之际，全身上下唯一尚在蠕动的，便唯有他那副下巴颏了。每隔一阵工夫，他便咬牙切齿地磨磨后槽牙，随后上下嘴唇吧唧作响地猛碰两三下，嘴角边随即泛起一圈黏稠恶心的白沫，令人观之欲呕。
“甘达拉（Gandara），你怎的不喝两口？”身旁一位高乔牧人（gaucho）扭过头冲他嚷嚷道。他仅仅是微微摇了摇头，既未发一言，眼珠子亦未曾离开我的脸庞半寸；方才发问的汉子见状讪讪一笑，扭头继续同其他人闲扯起来。
然而，这个嗜血畜生对我施加的这番漫长、凌厉且直刺灵魂的酷刑般凝视，却以一种猝不及防的爆烈势头骤然迎来了终局。快若闪电一般，一柄宽大修长的凶残利刃倏地从大氅底下的阴影中撕空出鞘，伴随着宛如猎豹扑食般的一记猫腰疾跃，他已然鬼魅般瞬移到了我的鼻尖跟前，那柄狰狞兵刃那冰凉森寒的锋芒尖端，正端端正正顶在我的毛毡大氅之上，直抵我的心窝下腹软肋。
“休得动弹，叛贼，”他喉咙里挤出一阵沙哑粗粝的嘶吼，“你若是敢动一根毫毛，当场便要你的狗命。”
周遭的闲汉们顷刻间全都闭上了嘴巴，饶有兴致地在一旁冷眼旁观，却无一人上前劝阻，亦无一人多嘴置评。
在那么一个刹那，我只觉得仿佛有一道狂暴的高压电光狠狠穿透了我的四肢百骸，但紧接着，我整个人奇迹般地重归于绝对的冰冷沉着——事实上，哪怕回溯平生，我也从未在任何凶险万分的灭顶绝境中，体味过比这一刻更为镇定自若的死寂宁静。这真是大自然赋予万灵的自卫保命天赐本能；怯懦柔弱之辈与骁勇善战的豪杰同样享有这份恩赐，正如任人宰割的孱弱野兽同那些嗜血残暴的丛林猛兽一样，骨子里皆深藏着这份生存警觉。

It is the calm which comes without call when death suddenly and unexpectedly rises up to stare us in the face; it tells us that there is one faint chance which a premature attempt to escape or even a slight agitation will destroy.
“I have no wish to move, friend,” I said, “but I am curious to know why you attack me?”
“Because you are a rebel. I have seen you before, you are one of Santa Coloma’s officers. Here you shall stand with this knife touching you till you are arrested, or else with this knife in you here you shall die.”
“You are making a mistake,” I said.
“Neighbours,” said he, speaking to the others, but without taking his eyes from my face, “will you tie this man hand and foot while I stand before him to prevent him from drawing any weapon he may have concealed under his poncho?”
“We have not come here to arrest travellers,” returned one of the men. “If he is a rebel it is no concern of ours. Perhaps you are mistaken, Gandara.”
“No, no, I am not mistaken,” he returned. “He shall not escape. I saw him at San Paulo with these eyes﻿—when did they ever deceive me? If you refuse to assist me, then go one of you to the Alcalde’s house and tell him to come without delay, while I keep guard here.”
After a little discussion one of the men offered to go and inform the Alcalde. When he had left, I said, “My friend, may I finish my meal? I am hungry, and had just begun to eat when you drew your knife against me.”
“Yes; eat,” he said; “only keep your hands well up so that I can see them. Perhaps you have a weapon at your waist.”
“I have not,” I said, “for I am an inoffensive person and do not require weapons.”
“Tongues were made to lie,” he returned, truly enough. “If I see you drop your hand lower than the counter I shall rip you up. We shall then be able to see whether you digest your food or not.”
I began to eat and sip my wine, still with those brutal eyes on my face and the keen knife-point touching my poncho. There was now a ghastly look of horrible excitement on his face, while his teeth-grinding performances became more frequent and the slimy froth dropped continually from the corners of his mouth on to his bosom. I dared not look at the knife, because a terrible impulse to wrest it out of his hands kept rising in me.

“皆因你是个谋反作乱的逆贼。老子早先见过你，你是圣科洛马（Santa Coloma）手底下的带兵军官。你且给我老老实实在这儿立着，任由这把刀顶着你的肉，直到官差将你缉拿归案；否则这柄刀锋一旦捅穿你的皮囊，你今晚便得横尸当场。”
