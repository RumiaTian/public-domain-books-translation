## XI The Woman and the Serpent / 十一 妇人与长蛇
===Original===

I walked thoughtfully back, because, after rendering that unimportant service to Marcos, I began to experience sundry qualms of conscience and inward questionings concerning the strict morality of the whole proceeding. Allowing that I had done something very kind, charitable, and altogether praiseworthy in getting the poor fellow’s unfortunate feet out of the stocks, did all that justify the cajolery I had practised to attain my object? Or, to put it briefly in the old familiar way: Does the end sanctify the means? Assuredly it does in some cases, very easy to be imagined. Let us suppose that I have a beloved friend, an ailing person of a nervous, delicate organisation, who has taken it into his poor cracked brains that he is going to expire at the stroke of twelve on a given night. Without consulting the authorities on ethical questions, I should, in such a case, flit about his room secretly manipulating his timepieces, till I had advanced them a whole hour, and then, just before the stroke of midnight, triumphantly produce my watch and inform him that death had failed to keep the appointment. Such an acted lie as that would weigh nothing on the conscience of any man. The fact of the matter is, the circumstances must always be considered and every case judged on its own particular merits. Now, this affair of getting the key was not one for me to judge, since I had been a chief actor in it, but rather for some acute and learned casuist. I therefore made a mental note of it, with the intention of putting it impartially before the first person of that description I should meet. Having thus disposed of a troublesome matter, I felt greatly relieved in mind, and turned into the kitchen once more. I had scarcely sat down, however, before I round that one disagreeable consequence of my performance﻿—the fat señora’s claim on my undying devotion and gratitude﻿—had yet to be faced. She greeted my entrance with an effusive smile; and the sweetest smiles of some people one meets are less endurable than their black looks. In self-defence I assumed as drowsy and vacant an expression as I could summon on the instant to a countenance by nature almost too ingenuous.

I pretended not to hear, or to misunderstand, everything that was said to me; finally I grew so sleepy that I was several times on the point of falling off my chair, then, after each extravagant nod, I would start up and stare vacantly around me. My grim little host could scarcely conceal a quiet smile, for never had he seen a person so outrageously sleepy before. At length he mercifully remarked that I seemed fatigued, and advised me to retire. Very gladly I made my exit, followed in my retreat from the kitchen by a pair of sad, reproachful eyes.
I slept soundly enough in the comfortable bed, which my obese Gulnare had provided for me, until the numerous cocks of the establishment woke me shortly after daybreak with their crowing. Remembering that I had to secure Marcos in the stocks before the irascible little magistrate should appear on the scene, I rose and hastily dressed myself. I found the greasy man of the brass buttons already in the kitchen sipping his matutinal *maté-amargo*, and asked him to lend me the key of the prisoner’s room; for this was what I had been instructed to do by the señora. 自那以后，我便得以在芬芳繁花丛中得享片刻耳根清净的太平漫步；然而鉴于这只斑纹小战俘方才立下的殊勋，我实在舍不得当场将它放生。我心头灵光一闪：倘若我随身携带此物，它岂不是成了一枚百邪不侵的护身符，恰好能将我从那位夫人的肉麻纠缠中彻底拯救出来？发觉它是一条极机敏黠慧的小长蛇，且同身陷囹圄时的马科斯·马科一般满腹皆是狡黠伪诈，我便将它稳妥地塞进了我的帽子里，将帽檐死死压在头顶，不留半点缝隙供它那支如箭簇般纤细的三角脑袋探出头来。在溪谷草甸中考察采集了两个多钟头的草木标本之后，我悠哉游哉地踱步还家。我正坐在灶房里品啜苦马黛茶解渴，只见女房东满面堆笑地款款迈步进门——我想大概此时此刻她心头的邪火早已烟消云散了吧。我极为绅士地起立致意，优雅地脱帽鞠躬。孰料我一时疏忽竟彻底忘了长蛇这桩要命的底牌，只见那小生灵啪嗒一声自帽兜里滑落，在泥地上欢快地扭动起来！顷刻间，整间灶房炸了营——女当家、孩童与仆役爆发出一阵震耳欲聋的尖叫恐号，推搡践踏着抱头鼠窜，连滚带爬逃出了屋门！事已至此，我只得无奈地拾起长蛇步出户外将其放归荒原，这久违的自由滋味，在经历了这场密闭囚禁之后，想必于它而言定然甜美甘冽至极。

On my return to the house, one of the servants informed me that the señora was too much offended to sit in the same room with me again, so that I was obliged to have my breakfast alone; and for the remainder of the time during which I was a prisoner I was avoided by everyone (except Brass Buttons﻿—who appeared indifferent to everything on earth), as if I had been a leper or a dangerous lunatic. They thought, perhaps, that I still had other reptiles concealed about my person.
Of course, one always expects to find a cruel, unreasoning prejudice against snakes amongst ignorant people, but I never knew before to what ridiculous lengths it will carry them. The prejudice makes me angry, but on this occasion it had a use, for it enabled me to pass the day unmolested.
In the evening the Juez returned, and I soon heard him loud in a stormy altercation with his wife. Perhaps she wanted him to have me decapitated. How it ended I cannot say; but when I saw him his manner towards me was freezing, and he retired without giving me an opportunity of speaking to him.
Next morning I got up resolved not to be put off any longer. Something would have to be done, or I would know the reason why. On stepping out I was very much surprised to see my horse standing saddled at the gate. I went into the kitchen and asked Brass Buttons, the only person up, what it meant.
“Who knows?” he returned, giving me a maté. “Perhaps the Juez desires you to leave the house before he is up.”
“What did he say?” I demanded.
“Say? Nothing﻿—what should he say?”
“But you saddled the horse, I suppose?”
“Of course. Who else would do it?”
“Were you told to do so by the Juez?”
“Told? Why should he tell me?”
“How, then, am I to know that he wishes me to leave his beautiful house?” I asked, getting angry.
“The question!” he returned, shrugging his shoulders. “How do you know when it is going to rain?”
Finding there was nothing more to be got out of the fellow, I finishing taking maté, lit a cigar, and left the house. It was a lovely morning, without a cloud, and the heavy dew sparkled on the grass like drops of rain. What a pleasant thing it was to be able to ride forth again free to go where I liked!
And so ends my snake-story, which is perhaps not very interesting; but it is true, and therefore has one advantage over all other snake-stories told by travellers.

===Chinese===

待我踱回大宅，一名家仆神色严峻地向我通报：夫人深感蒙受了奇耻大辱，此生绝不肯再同我共处一室进膳，是以我唯有独自一人凄凉地打发了早饭；在此后我继续充当囚徒的剩余时光里，宅中上下所有人等（唯独那个似乎对世间万物皆漠不关心的“黄铜纽扣”除外）皆对我避之唯恐不及，仿佛我乃是身染恶疾的麻风病患抑或是嗜血发狂的疯魔一般。想必在他们眼里，我的衣衫襟袍深处指不定还私藏着多少条致命的毒蛇猛兽呢。

当然，在愚昧无知的乡野氓隶心中，对蛇类生物向来抱有一种残忍而荒诞不经的莫名戒惧；但我平生却委实从未料到，这等偏见竟能将人驱策至如此荒唐可笑的滑稽地步。这种偏见平日里固然教我心生厌恶，然而在眼下这般境遇里倒也大显神通，至少它教我得以在不受任何滋扰骚扰的清静中安然打发了整整一天。

入夜时分，法官大人终于还家，未过多久我便听见他与妻子在房内爆发出一阵惊天动地的激烈争吵。保不齐那妇人正枕边吹风执意要他将我推出辕门斩首示众呢。这场争吵究竟如何收场我无从探知；但当我面见他之际，他待我的神色冰冷得犹如寒霜，且未等我寻得半点答话的契机，便拂袖而去回房安寝。

次日清晨我翻身而起，打定主意绝不再任由这帮人这般无限期拖延搪塞。今天非得做个了断不可，否则老子定要问个水落石出！迈出房门的刹那，眼前展现的景象教我大吃一惊：我的坐骑不知何时早已备齐了鞍鞯，正端端正正拴在院门口！我阔步迈进灶房，询问已然起身的唯一活人——“黄铜纽扣”——这究竟唱的是哪一出戏。

“天晓得呢？”他递给我一盅马黛茶，含混答道，“想必是法官大人希望在自己爬出被窝之前，您早已识趣地离宅远遁了吧。”

“他究竟有何示下？”我追问道。

“示下？半个字也没有——他又能有何示下？”

“但马鞍总是你备上的吧？”

“那是自然。除了老子，这宅里还有谁肯动手干这等粗活？”

“是法官大人吩咐你这般做的？”

“吩咐？他平白无故吩咐老子作甚？”

“既然如此，我又岂能得知他究竟是盼着我卷铺盖滚蛋呢？”我被他这通车轱辘鬼话气得七窍生烟，怒喝道。

“这话问得新鲜！”他耸了耸肩膀，反唇相讥道，“天上何时要下暴雨，难道非得有人通知您不可？”

发觉从这块滚刀肉嘴里再也榨不出半句人话，我仰脖饮尽了马黛茶，点燃一支雪茄，大步流星跨出了门庭。那是一个晴空万里、澄澈无云的迷人清晨，草叶上凝结的沉甸甸朝露宛如万千碎钻般在晨曦中熠熠生辉。当一个人能再度跨上马背、天高任鸟飞任意驰骋之际，那滋味是何等心旷神怡啊！

我的长蛇历险记至此便落下了帷幕，此案听来或许算不上多么惊心动魄；但它却千真万确字字属实，是以比起天下行脚旅人编造的其余蛇类神话，至少兼具了一桩无可匹敌的胜处。
