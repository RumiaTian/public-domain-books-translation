### I / 一

===Original===
Then I went to Oxford, and when the war broke out I joined the Navy. But just before the revolution, Admiral Butt, who had gone on a special mission to Russia, applied for my “Anglo-Russian” services.

I still remember very vividly the morning following on my arrival in Petrograd, when I had to meet the Admiral for the first time at the British Embassy. I ascended the broad staircase with its worn red carpet to the Chancery. Very perfect young men, very perfectly dressed, were conversing in very perfect intonations about love among monkeys. It struck me as delightfully human for diplomats. When I descended, the Admiral had not yet arrived. I talked to Yuri, the hall-porter, a clean-shaven individual of uncertain nationality, violently pro-British, and speaking several languages all very badly. Every now and then the great heavy door would open﻿—it was snowing heavily outside﻿—and some man or woman would come in and inquire if this was the Military District Staff. “It is the British Embassy,” replied Yuri proudly. And he explained the error. The Military District Staff was 4 Palace Square; the Embassy 4 Palace Quay. In peacetime people did come in occasionally and inquire if this was the Military District Staff; but since war had been declared they seemed to be doing little else. I pondered over the possibility of Yuri, unable one day to withstand the increasing pressure of inquiries, going mad and holding forth on this subject to his brother inmates henceforth indefinitely in a lunatic asylum.

===Chinese===
后来我上了牛津（Oxford）大学，战争一爆发便参加了海军。可就在革命前夕，因特别使命赴俄的巴特（Butt）海军上将，申请调用我去，要的就是我这份「英俄」之才。

我至今清清楚楚记得初到彼得格勒（Petrograd）后的那个早晨——那天我得去英国大使馆，头一回拜见海军上将。我踏上铺着磨旧红地毯的宽大楼梯，上到使馆办公处（Chancery）。几位十分完美的年轻人，衣着十分完美，正用十分完美的语调，谈论猴子之间的爱情。在我看来，这在外交官身上，可真是人情味十足，可爱得很。我下楼时，海军上将还没有到。我便同门房尤里（Yuri）攀谈起来。这人脸刮得精光，国籍成谜，狂热亲英，会讲好几国语言，可惜每一国都讲得很糟。那扇沉重的大门隔三差五就打开一回——外面大雪下得正紧——总有男人或女人走进来，问这里是不是军区参谋部（Military District Staff）。「这里是英国大使馆。」尤里自豪地答道，并且把误会解释一番：军区参谋部在宫殿广场（Palace Square）4号；大使馆在宫殿滨河街（Palace Quay）4号。和平年月里，偶尔也有人进来问这里是不是军区参谋部；可自打宣战以来，人们仿佛别的什么都不干了，专干这一件事。我不禁琢磨起一种可能：有朝一日，尤里抵不住与日俱增的问讯重压，发了疯，从此在疯人院里对着同院的病友，就这个题目无休无止地大发议论。

===Original===
Helping me on with my coat, Yuri was suddenly seized by a strange panic. He dropped the coat on the floor and dashed to the door. I followed him, thinking it was the revolution. I was rewarded for my exertion. The Ambassador’s car drove up, and sitting in it were Sir George Buchanan and the French Ambassador. Yuri sprang up and pulling off his cap opened the door of the vehicle and stood still in a paroxysm of reverence and awe. But the two great men within continued talking, the Frenchman in that agitated, agile manner that Frenchmen have, the Englishman with a fine superiority of distinction. The Ambassadors of the two friendly powers sat talking, evidently unaware that they had arrived. Yuri held the door open, still bareheaded, the incarnation of servility and devotion. Then they entered. Yuri made a dash for Sir George’s feet, and began hastily to unbuckle his felt goloshes, while the great diplomat with his fur collar still up to his temples and his round fur cap cocked over one ear stood panting in his great fur coat. I had an absurd idea that something great must be happening on the political horizon.

Finally the Admiral arrived. He was a tall, imperious figure. His movements were powerful and sweeping. He had the air of a man engaged in winning the war while everybody else about him was obstructing him in his patriotic task. His voice was the voice of such a man. His look seemed specially selected to match his voice. That war-winning quality was clearly manifest in his personality, but his actual work towards that end was all very obscure.﻿ ﻿…

===Chinese===
尤里帮我穿大衣时，忽然被一阵莫名的惊慌攫住。他把大衣往地上一撂，直奔门口而去。我以为革命来了，便跟着他跑了过去。我这一趟没有白跑。大使的汽车驶到门前，车里坐着乔治·布坎南爵士（Sir George Buchanan）和法国大使。尤里一跃而起，一把拽下帽子，拉开车门，随即一动不动地伫立着，崇敬与惶恐到了痉挛的地步。可车内两位大人物谈兴正浓：法国人用的是法国人特有的那种亢奋敏捷的做派，英国人则是一派优雅超然的气度。两个友邦的大使坐在车里谈个不停，分明没有觉察自己已经到了。尤里扶着车门，仍旧光着头，活脱脱是卑屈与忠心的化身。随后他们走了进去。尤里一个箭步扑到乔治爵士脚前，急急忙忙去解他毡套鞋的搭扣；那位大外交官毛皮领子还直竖到太阳穴，圆毛皮帽歪扣在一只耳朵上，裹在那件大皮大衣里直喘气。我心里冒出一个荒唐的念头：政治地平线上必定正有什么大事发生。

海军上将终于到了。他身材高大，气派专断，一举一动都威猛豪迈。他浑身上下透着一股劲头：这场战争全靠他一人去打赢，而他身边的每一个人都在给他的爱国大业使绊子。他的嗓音正是这样一个人的嗓音，他的目光又仿佛是特意挑选来与嗓音相配的。那种「打赢战争」的气质在他身上展露无遗，至于他为达此目的究竟做着什么实际工作，那就十分晦暗不明了……

===Original===
Then one morning, as I was about to cross the Troitski Bridge to meet the Admiral, I was stopped by the police and was compelled to go home and change into uniform. When I returned the revolution had already broken out. The Admiral had just witnessed the sacking of the Arsenal by a disorderly crowd. Regiment after regiment was going over to the revolution. Solitary shots, and now and then machine-gun fire, were heard from various quarters of the city. The Admiral and I stood at the window and watched. Lorry after lorry packed with armed soldiery and workmen, some lying in a “ready” attitude along the mudguards, went past us in a kind of wild and dazzling joy ride, waving red flags and revolutionary banners to shouts of “Hurrah!” from the crowds in the street. The Admiral stood with his hands folded on the windowsill, unable to withhold his enthusiasm. It was a clear, bright day, I think, and very cold.

===Chinese===
后来一个早晨，我正要穿过特罗伊茨基桥（Troitski Bridge）去见海军上将，被警察拦下，只得回家换上军装。待我再出门，革命已经爆发。海军上将刚刚亲眼目睹一群乱民洗劫了兵工厂。一个团又一个团的士兵倒向革命。零星的枪声，间或夹着机关枪声，从城中四面八方传来。海军上将和我站在窗前观望。一辆接一辆的卡车载满武装的士兵和工人，有的摆出「预备」姿势趴在挡泥板上，以一种狂野而炫目的兜风架势从我们面前驶过，车上的人挥着红旗和革命的横幅，迎着街上人群「乌拉！」「乌拉！」的欢呼。海军上将双手交叠搭在窗台上，按捺不住心头的兴奋。我想，那是个晴朗明媚的日子，而且很冷。

===Original===
That evening following the outbreak of the revolution was vividly impressed upon my memory. During the day I had listened to innumerable speeches, some of a Liberal loftiness; others of a menacingly proletarian character, threatening death to capital and revolution to the world at large. There was a tendency to flamboyant extravagance and exaggeration. “Down with Armies and Navies!” shouted one speaker hysterically. “Down with militarism! Through red terror to peace, freedom and brotherhood!” There were placards and banners and processions. “Land and Liberty!” was a popular watchword. Red was the dominant colour, and the opening bars of the Marseillaise were a kind of recurring leitmotif in the tumult. Crossing a bridge I passed a company of soldiers newly revolted. They marched alert and joyous to the sound of some old familiar marching song till they came to the words “for the Czar.” Having sung these words they stopped somewhat abruptly and perplexed. “*How* for the Czar?” one of them asked. “*How* for the Czar?” they repeated, looking at each other sheepishly. Then they marched on without singing. There were peasants who did not know the word “revolution” and thought it was a woman who would supersede the Czar. Others wanted a republic with a czar. And there were others still who interpreted the word republic as “rieszshpublicoo,” thinking that it meant “cut up the public.” In the Troitski Square I was stopped by a young enthusiastic Russian officer who, attracted by my British uniform, spoke to me in English, his eyes glittering with excitement. “Sir,” he said, “you now will have more vigorous Allies.” And then in the Nevski I passed a procession of Anarchists who are regarded by the Bolsheviks with about the same degree of unmitigated horror as the Bolsheviks are regarded by the *Morning Post*. They marched with a gruesome look about their faces, bearing their horrible colours of black, crested with a human skull and crossbones.

And somewhat later in the day I sat at dinner with Zina’s people on the Petrograd side, and the presence of a score of students, male and female, an engineer, a lawyer, and a journalist or two, all of that revolutionary intelligentsia, probably accounted for the Liberal atmosphere that prevailed. Yesterday they had been revolutionaries; today they were contented Liberals, hailing Lvov and Miliukov as the heroes of the day. The engineer drank to the future: “The old world is dead: long live the new world!”

===Chinese===
革命爆发后的那个夜晚，在我的记忆里烙下了鲜活的印迹。白天我听了数不清的演说：有的是自由派的高腔远调，有的带着咄咄逼人的无产阶级色彩，扬言要叫资本死，要革命闹遍全世界。字里行间有一种浮夸炫饰、言过其实的倾向。「打倒陆军和海军！」一位演讲者歇斯底里地喊道。「打倒军国主义！通过红色恐怖，走向和平、自由、博爱！」满眼是标语、横幅和游行队伍。「土地与自由」是一句风行的口号。红色是压倒一切的色调，《马赛曲》（Marseillaise）开头的几小节，成了这片喧嚣中反复回响的主导旋律。过一座桥时，我迎面遇上一小队新近哗变的士兵。他们伴着一支熟悉的老进行曲行进，机敏而欢快，一直唱到「为了沙皇」那句词。这句一唱完，他们便有些突兀地停了下来，一脸茫然。「*怎么*个『为了沙皇』法？」其中一个问道。「*怎么*个『为了沙皇』法？」他们面面相觑，讪讪地重复。随后便不再唱，继续行军。有庄稼汉不知道「革命」这个词，还当它是个女人，要来接沙皇的班。有人想要一个留着沙皇的共和国。更有人把「共和国」这个词念成「雷什普布利库」（rieszshpublicoo），以为意思是「把公众剁碎」。在特罗伊茨基广场（Troitski Square），一位热情的俄国青年军官拦住了我；他被我这身英国军装吸引，对我讲起英语来，双眼兴奋得发亮。「先生，」他说，「你们如今要有一批更强有力的盟友了。」接着在涅夫斯基大街（Nevski），我又遇上一队无政府主义者的游行——布尔什维克（Bolsheviks）看待他们的那种毫不打折扣的恐惧，同《晨邮报》（Morning Post）看待布尔什维克的恐惧不相上下。他们脸上带着阴森可怖的神情行进，扛着那面骇人的黑旗，旗首顶着一颗人的骷髅和交叉的白骨。

当天晚上再晚些时候，我在彼得格勒区（Petrograd side）季娜（Zina）家里吃饭；席间有二十来个男女学生、一位工程师、一位律师，还有一两个记者，全属于革命知识界——满座的自由派气氛，多半就是这么来的。昨天他们还是革命者；今天已经心满意足地当起自由派，把李沃夫（Lvov）和米留科夫（Miliukov）奉为当日的英雄。工程师为未来干杯：「旧世界死了：新世界万岁！」

===Original===
The two ancient grandfathers were much too old and feeble to intervene on behalf of the old order of things; they had exhausted their Liberal aspirations with the liberation of the serfs in 1861 and could not see what in the world more anybody wanted. Zina’s father, underpaid and ill as he was, had lost forever the hope of seeing better days, and failed to see how the revolution could affect his own position. Nikolai Vasilievich was still keeping him so far. These Liberals interpreted the revolution as a protest against the pro-German tendency at court, and as an attempt to get into line with the Western Democracies in this hitherto unconvincing struggle against militarism and autocracy. The news was rumoured that the Czar had abdicated. Again, it was said that a section of the court had been planning a revolution to depose the Emperor and substitute his brother Michael in order to carry on the war more vigorously; and that the people’s revolution had preceded it by two days. Some monarchists now wanted to put down the revolution in order to carry on the war; other monarchists wanted to put down the war in order to put down the revolution; and still other monarchists wanted to put down the revolution and did not care a hang about the war. The Liberals wanted the revolution to carry on the war; the Czar wanted to put down the revolution; the Socialists and workmen wanted to put down the war and to put down the Czar; and the soldiers and sailors wanted to put down their officers. The Liberal gathering drank to Russia’s Allies; and then Uncle Kostia, obviously moved by the great event, rose and said in a slow, melodious voice:

===Chinese===
两位老祖父年纪太大、身子太弱，再也无力出面维护旧秩序了；他们那点自由派的抱负，早在一八六一年解放农奴那阵就已用尽，实在想不明白世人到底还想要什么。季娜的父亲薪水微薄，又疾病缠身，早已永远断了过上好日子的指望，也看不出革命能给他自己的处境带来什么变化——到那时为止，一直是尼古拉·瓦西里耶维奇（Nikolai Vasilievich）在养活他。这些自由派把革命解释为对宫廷亲德倾向的抗议，解释为在这场迄今难服人心的反军国主义、反专制斗争中同西方民主国家看齐的一种努力。有传闻说，沙皇已经退位。又有人说，宫廷里有一派人原也密谋着一场革命，要废黜皇帝，让他弟弟米哈伊尔（Michael）取而代之，好把仗打得更起劲，却不料人民的革命抢在了前头两天。于是，一些保皇党人想镇压革命，好把战争打下去；另一些保皇党人想结束战争，好镇压革命；还有一些保皇党人想镇压革命，至于战争，则全不放在心上。自由派想借着革命把战争打下去；沙皇想镇压革命；社会党人和工人想结束战争、打倒沙皇；士兵和水兵想打倒他们的长官。这场自由派的聚会为俄国的盟友们举了杯；随后，科斯佳叔叔（Uncle Kostia）显然被这桩大事打动了，站起身来，用缓慢而悦耳的嗓音说道：

===Original===
“We will not talk about or criticize the past. We will carry it gently into the depths of the garden and bury it there among the flowers. And then, carefully, we will look into the cradle and nurse very tenderly the slumbering future.﻿ ﻿…”

This attitude, we all felt, was befitting to the great bloodless revolution.

===Chinese===
「过去的事，我们不谈，也不指摘。我们要轻轻地把它捧起来，送进花园深处，葬在那里的花丛中间。然后，我们再小心翼翼地凑近摇篮，无比温柔地守护那沉睡中的未来……」

我们大家都觉得，这样的态度，正与这场伟大的不流血的革命相称。
