## Castaways Working / 落难者的劳作

===Original===
We had been away from the boat for about two hours; when we got back, I saw that Abdullah and his helpers had gotten the deck plates off the engine well and used them to build a more substantial barricade at the ruptured stern. The heater was going and the boat was warm inside, not just relatively to the outside, but actually comfortable. It was even more crowded, however, because there was a ton of collapsium shielding, in four sections, and the generator and power unit, piled in the middle. Abdullah and Tom and Hans Cronje were looking at the converters, which to my not very knowing eye seemed to be in a hopeless mess.

There was some more work going on up at the front. Cesário Vieira had found a small portable radio that wasn’t in too bad condition, and had it apart. I thought he was doing about the most effective work of anybody, and waded over the pile of engine parts to see what he was doing. It wasn’t much of a radio. A hundred miles was the absolute limit of its range, at least for sending.

===Chinese===
我们离船大约两个钟头；回来时，我看见阿卜杜拉（Abdullah）和他的帮手们已经把机舱井上的甲板盖板拆了下来，拿去在破裂的船尾垒起一道更结实的屏障。加热器烧得正旺，船舱里暖烘烘的——不只是相对于外头而言，而是实实在在的舒服。不过舱里也更挤了，因为正当中堆着一吨分作四扇的坍缩素（collapsium）防护屏，外加发电机和动力装置。阿卜杜拉、汤姆（Tom）和汉斯·克龙涅（Hans Cronje）正对着变换器琢磨，在我这双不算内行的眼睛看来，那玩意儿已经糟得没了指望。

船头那边还有些活儿在进行。塞萨里奥·维埃拉（Cesário Vieira）找出一台小型便携电台，成色不算太坏，已经把它拆开了。我觉得在所有人里头，他干的差不多是最有实效的活儿，便蹚过一堆发动机部件过去看他弄的什么。那电台实在不怎么样。一百英里是它通讯距离的绝对极限——至少发话是这样。

===Original===
“Is this all we have?” I asked, looking at it. It was the same type as the one I carried on the job, camouflaged in a camera case, except that it wouldn’t record.

“There’s the regular boat radio, but it’s smashed up pretty badly. I was thinking we could do something about cannibalizing one radio out of parts from both of them.”

We use a lot of radio equipment on the *Times*, and I do a good bit of work on it. I started taking the big set apart and then remembered the receiver for the locator and got at that, too. The trouble was that most of the stuff in all the sets had been miniaturized to a point where watchmaker’s tools would have been pretty large for working on them, and all we had was a general-repair kit that was just about fine enough for gunsmithing.

===Chinese===
「我们就只有这一台？」我瞧着它问。它跟我外出采访随身带的那台是同一个型号——那台伪装在照相机盒里——只是这一台不能录音。

「还有艇上那台正式配的电台，不过砸得相当厉害。我一直在琢磨，能不能从两台机器上拆拆拼拼，凑出一台能用的来。」

《时报》（*Times*）用着大量无线电设备，其中不少活儿都出自我手。我动手拆那台大机子，拆着拆着想起定位器的接收机，便把它也拆了。麻烦在于，这几台机器里的元件大都微型化到了连钟表匠的家什使起来都嫌大的地步，而我们手头只有一个通用修理工具包，那精细程度也就勉强够干枪匠活儿。

===Original===
While we were fooling around with the radios, Ramón Llewellyn was telling the others what we found up the other branch of the fjord. Joe Kivelson shook his head over it.

“That’s too far from the boat. We can’t trudge back and forth to work on the engines. We could cut firewood down there and float it up with the lifters, and I think that’s a good idea about using slabs of the soft wood to build a hut. But let’s build the hut right here.”

“Well, suppose I take a party down now and start cutting?” the mate asked.

“Not yet. Wait till Abe gets back and we see what he found upstream. There may be something better up there.”

===Chinese===
我们折腾电台的当儿，拉蒙·卢埃林（Ramón Llewellyn）正在给其他人讲我们在峡湾另一条汊子上游的发现。乔·基维尔森（Joe Kivelson）听了直摇头。

「那地方离船太远。修发动机可经不起来回徒步跑。柴可以到下面去砍，再用升举器（lifter）浮运上来；用软木板子搭棚子这个主意，我觉得也不赖。不过棚子就搭在这儿吧。」

「那么，我现在带一队人下去先砍起来？」大副问。

「先别。等艾布（Abe）回来，看看他在上游找到了什么。说不定那上头还有更好的去处。」

===Original===
Tom, who had been poking around in the converters, said:

“I think we can forget about the engines. This is a machine-shop job. We need parts, and we haven’t anything to make them out of or with.”

That was about what I’d thought. Tom knew more about lift-and-drive engines than I’d ever learn, and I was willing to take his opinion as confirmation of my own.

“Tom, take a look at this mess,” I said. “See if you can help us with it.”

===Chinese===
一直在变换器里翻翻弄弄的汤姆开了口：

「我看发动机就别指望了。这是机加工车间的活儿。咱们需要零件，可既没有做零件的料，也没有做零件的家什。」

这跟我心里的想法差不多。论升举-驱动引擎（lift-and-drive engines），汤姆懂得的比我这辈子能学到的还多，我乐意拿他的判断给我自己的想法作个佐证。

「汤姆，来瞧瞧这摊乱摊子，」我说。「看能不能搭把手。」

===Original===
He came over, looked at what we were working on, and said, “You need a magnifier for this. Wait till I see something.” Then he went over to one of the lockers, rummaged in it, and found a pair of binoculars. He came over to us again, sat down, and began to take them apart. As soon as he had the two big objective lenses out, we had two fairly good magnifying glasses.

That was a big help, but being able to see what had to be done was one thing, and having tools to do it was another. So he found a sewing kit and a piece of emery stone, and started making little screwdrivers out of needles.

After a while, Abe Clifford and Piet Dumont and the other man returned and made a beeline for the heater and the coffeepot. After Abe was warmed a little, he said:

===Chinese===
他走过来，看了看我们手上的活计，说：「干这个得有放大镜。等着，我看看能找出点什么。」说着他走到一只储物柜前，翻捡了一通，找出一副双筒望远镜。他又回到我们这边坐下，动手把它拆开。两块大物镜一卸下来，我们便有了两枚蛮像样的放大镜。

这帮了大忙，不过能看清该干的活儿是一回事，有家伙去干又是另一回事。于是他又找出一个针线包和一小块砂石，动手用缝衣针磨起小螺丝刀来。

过了一阵，艾布·克利福德（Abe Clifford）、皮特·杜蒙（Piet Dumont）和另外那个人回来了，径直奔加热器和咖啡壶而去。艾布身上暖过来一点儿之后，说道：

===Original===
“There’s a little waterfall about half a mile up. It isn’t too hard to get up over it, and above, the ground levels off into a big bowl-shaped depression that looks as if it had been a lake bottom, once. The wind isn’t so bad up there, and this whole lake bottom or whatever it is is grown up with trees. It would be a good place to make a camp, if it wasn’t so far from the boat.”

“How hard would it be to cut wood up there and bring it down?” Joe asked, going on to explain what he had in mind.

“Why, easy. I don’t think it would be nearly as hard as the place Ramón found.”

===Chinese===
「往上约半英里有道小瀑布。翻上去不算太难，上头地势平展开来，成了一大片盆状洼地，看样子早先是个湖底。那上头风也不这么凶，整片湖底——管它到底是什么——都长满了树。要不是离船太远，倒是扎营的好地方。」

「在那上头砍柴、再把木头弄下来，费多大劲？」乔问，接着把自己心里的打算讲了一遍。

「嗨，容易得很。我看比拉蒙找的那个地方省事多了。」

===Original===
“Neither do I,” the mate agreed. “Climbing up that waterfall down the stream with a half tree trunk would be a lot harder than dropping one over beside the one above.” He began zipping up his parka. “Let’s get the cutter and the lifters and go up now.”

“Wait till I warm up a little, and I’ll go with you,” Abe said.

Then he came over to where Cesário and Tom and I were working, to see what we were doing. He chucked appreciatively at the midget screwdrivers and things Tom was making.

===Chinese===
「我也这么想，」大副附和道。「拖着半截树干爬下游那道瀑布，可比在上游瀑布旁边把木头放下去难得多。」他开始拉风雪大衣的拉链。「这就拿上切割器和升举器，上那边去吧。」

「等我暖和一小会儿，就跟你们一块儿去，」艾布说。

随后他踱到塞萨里奥、汤姆和我干活的地方，看我们在做什么。见着汤姆正做着的那些微型螺丝刀之类的物件，他赏识地轻声直乐。

===Original===
“I’ll take that back, Ramón,” he said. “I can do a lot more good right here. Have you taken any of the radio navigational equipment apart, yet?” he asked us.

We hadn’t. We didn’t know anything about it.

“Well, I think we can get some stuff out of the astrocompass that can be used. Let me in here, will you?”

===Chinese===
「拉蒙，我收回刚才那话，」他说。「我留在这儿能顶的用场大得多。」接着问我们：「无线电导航设备，你们拆过没有？」

没拆过。我们对那玩意儿一窍不通。

「呃，我想能从天文罗盘（astrocompass）里拆出些可用的东西来。让个位置给我挤挤，行吗？」

===Original===
I got up. “You take over for me,” I said. “I’ll go on the wood-chopping detail.”

Tom wanted to go, too; Abe told him to keep on with his toolmaking. Piet Dumont said he’d guide us, and Glenn Murell said he’d go along. There was some swapping around of clothes and we gathered up the two lifters and the sonocutter and a floodlight and started upstream.

The waterfall above the boat was higher than the one below, but not quite so hard to climb, especially as we had the two lifters to help us. The worst difficulty, and the worst danger, was from the wind.

===Chinese===
我站起身。「你来接我的手，」我说。「我去砍柴那队。」

汤姆也想去，艾布叫他接着做他的工具。皮特·杜蒙说他来给我们带路，格伦·穆雷尔（Glenn Murell）说他也去。大伙儿把衣物调换了一番，我们收拾起那两具升举器、声波切割器（sonocutter）和一盏泛光灯，沿着溪流往上游出发。

船上方那道瀑布比下方那道高，爬起来倒不那么吃力，何况还有两具升举器帮忙。最大的难处、最大的凶险，都来自风。

===Original===
Once we were at the top, though, it wasn’t so bad. We went a couple of hundred yards through a narrow gorge, and then we came out onto the old lake bottom Abe had spoken about. As far as our lights would shine in the snow, we could see stubby trees with snaky branches growing out of the tops.

We just started on the first one we came to, slicing the down-hanging branches away to get at the trunk and then going to work on that. We took turns using the sonocutter, and the rest of us stamped around to keep warm. The first trunk must have weighed a ton and a half, even after the branches were all off; we could barely lift one end of it with both lifters. The spongy stuff, which changed from bark to wood as it went in to the middle, was two feet thick. We cut that off in slabs, to use for building the hut. The hardwood core, once we could get it lit, would make a fine hot fire. We could cut that into burnable pieces after we got it to camp. We didn’t bother with the slashings; just threw them out of the way. There was so much big stuff here that the branches weren’t worth taking in.

We had eight trees down and cut into slabs and billets before we decided to knock off. We didn’t realize until then how tired and cold we were. A couple of us had taken the wood to the waterfall and heaved it over at the side as fast as the others got the trees down and cut up. *If we only had another cutter and a couple more lifters*, I thought. *If we only had an airworthy boat.…*

===Chinese===
不过一登上顶，情况就不那么糟了。我们穿过一道狭窄的隘谷走了两三百码，便来到艾布所说的那片老湖底上。在灯光照得到的雪野范围内，满眼都是矮墩墩的树，树顶上披散着蛇一般虬曲的枝条。

我们碰到头一棵树就开了工，先把垂挂下来的枝条削掉好够着树干，然后对树干下家伙。大家轮着使声波切割器，其余的人就在一旁跺脚转悠取暖。头一根树干就算把枝条去个精光，也足有一吨半重；两具升举器齐上，才勉强抬得起它一头。那层海绵似的软材——从外皮往中心渐渐变成了木头——足足有两英尺厚。我们把它切成一块块板材，留着搭棚子用。当中的硬木芯子，一旦引着了火，准能烧出一炉又旺又持久的火；那个可以运回营地后再截成耐烧的柴段。枝条废料我们懒得理会，随手扔开便是——这地方大材多得是，枝条不值得往回搬。

等我们决定收工时，已经放倒了八棵树，全切成了板材和柴段。到那时才觉出来，自己又累又冷到了什么地步。这边的伙计每放倒一棵、切碎一棵，那边就有人把木头运到瀑布口，尽快从瀑布旁边抛下去。*哪怕再有一台切割器、再有两具升举器呢*，我心里想。*哪怕那条船还能上天呢……*

===Original===
When we got back to camp, everybody who wasn’t crippled and had enough clothes to get away from the heater came out and helped. First, we got a fire started—there was a small arc torch, and we needed that to get the dense hardwood burning—and then we began building a hut against the boat. Everybody worked on that but Dominic Silverstein. Even Abe and Cesário knocked off work on the radio, and Joe Kivelson and the man with the broken wrist gave us a little one-handed help. By this time, the wind had fallen and the snow was coming down thicker. We made snow shovels out of the hard outer bark, although they broke in use pretty often, and banked snow up against the hut. I lost track of how long we worked, but finally we had a place we could all get into, with a fireplace, and it was as warm and comfortable as the inside of the boat.

===Chinese===
回到营地，凡是没挂彩、衣裳又穿得足以离开加热器的人，都出来搭手。我们先生了火——有一支小型电弧炬，要引燃那致密的硬木非它不可——接着便贴着船身搭起棚子来。除了多米尼克·西尔弗斯坦（Dominic Silverstein），人人都在这上头出了力。连艾布和塞萨里奥都撂下了电台的活儿，乔·基维尔森和那个断了手腕的汉子也用一只手给我们帮了点小忙。到这时，风已经息了，雪倒下得更密。我们用坚硬的外层树皮做了些雪铲——虽然使不了几下就得断——把雪培到棚子外沿挡风。干了多久我记不清了，反正最后我们有了一处大家挤得下的所在，还带着一座火塘，和船舱里一样温暖舒服。

===Original===
We had to keep cutting wood, though. Before long it would be too cold to work up in the woods, or even go back and forth between the woods and the camp. The snow finally stopped, and then the sky began to clear and we could see stars. That didn’t make us happy at all. As long as the sky was clouded and the snow was falling, some of the heat that had been stored during the long day was being conserved. Now it was all radiating away into space.

The stream froze completely, even the waterfall. In a way, that was a help; we could slide wood down over it, and some of the billets would slide a couple of hundred yards downstream. But the cold was getting to us. We only had a few men working at woodcutting—Cesário, and old Piet Dumont, and Abe Clifford and I, because we were the smallest and could wear bigger men’s parkas and overpants over our own. But as long as any of us could pile on enough clothing and waddle out of the hut, we didn’t dare stop. If the firewood ran out, we’d all freeze stiff in no time at all.

===Chinese===
可砍柴的活儿断不得。要不了多久，天就会冷得没法在林子里干活，甚至没法在林子和营地之间来回。雪总算停了，跟着天开始放晴，我们看见了星星。这可一点儿也让人高兴不起来。只要天上还阴着、雪还下着，长昼里蓄下的热量多少还存得住；如今，这些热量正一股脑儿朝太空里辐射散逸。

溪水彻底冻结了，连瀑布也不例外。往好里说，这也算个帮手：我们可以让木头顺着冰面往下滑，有的柴段一口气能滑出几百码开外。可寒冷渐渐往我们骨头里钻。伐木的只剩寥寥几个人——塞萨里奥、老皮特·杜蒙，还有艾布·克利福德和我，因为我们几个块头最小，能把大个子那号人的风雪大衣和套裤套在自己那身外头。但只要还有谁裹得上足够的衣裳、摇摇摆摆走得出去，我们就一天也不敢停。柴火一断，大伙儿转眼就得冻僵。

===Original===
Abe Clifford got the radio working, at last. It was a peculiar job as ever was, but he thought it would have a range of about five hundred miles. Somebody kept at it all the time, calling Mayday. I think it was Bish Ware who told me that Mayday didn’t have anything to do with the day after the last of April; it was Old Terran French, *m’aidez*, meaning “help me.” I wondered how Bish was getting along, and I wasn’t too optimistic about him.

Cesário and Abe and I were up at the waterfall, picking up loads of firewood—we weren’t bothering, now, with anything but the hard and slow-burning cores—and had just gotten two of them hooked onto the lifters. I straightened for a moment and looked around. There wasn’t a cloud in the sky, and two of Fenris’s three moons were making everything as bright as day. The glisten of the snow and the frozen waterfall in the double moonlight was beautiful.

===Chinese===
艾布·克利福德总算把电台修得能用了。那台机器拼凑得也是古怪至极，不过他估计通讯距离能有五百英里上下。总有人守在旁边，一声接一声地呼叫「梅戴」（Mayday，无线电求救呼号）。记得就是比什·韦尔（Bish Ware）告诉过我，「梅戴」跟四月过完头一天那个五朔节（May Day）没有半点干系；那是古特拉法语 *m’aidez*，意思是「救救我」。我不知道比什如今光景如何，反正对他乐观不起来。

塞萨里奥、艾布和我到瀑布上头装柴火——这会儿除了又硬又耐烧的木芯，别的我们一概不碰——刚把两块挂上升举器。我直起腰歇了片刻，四下里张望。天上没有一丝云，芬尼斯（Fenris）的三轮月亮有两轮正悬在天际，把万物照得亮如白昼。双月交映之下，积雪和冰封的瀑布莹莹闪光，美极了。

===Original===
I turned to Cesário. “See what all you’ll miss, if you take your next reincarnation off Fenris,” I said. “This, and the long sunsets and sunrises, and—”

Before I could list any more sights unique to our planet, the 7 mm machine gun, down at the boat, began hammering; a short burst, and then another, and another and another.

===Chinese===
我转向塞萨里奥。「瞧瞧吧，下辈子投胎你要是投到芬尼斯以外的地方，得错过多少好光景，」我说。「这个，还有那长长的日出日落，还有——」

我这颗行星独有的景致还没数上几样，下面船上那挺 7 毫米机枪就嗒嗒地砸响了；短短一串点射，接着又一串，又是一串，再一串。
