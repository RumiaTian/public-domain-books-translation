## XXVI The Horn of Alveric / 二十六 阿尔韦里克的号角

===Original===
Northward to lonely lands through wearying years Alveric wandered, where windy fragments of his grey gaunt tent added a gloom to chill evenings. And the folk upon lonely farms, as they lit the lights in their houses, and the ricks began to darken against the pale green of the sky, would sometimes hear the rap of the mallets of Niv and Zend coming clear through the hush from the land that no others trod. And their children peering from casements to see if a star was come would see perhaps the queer grey shape of that tent flapping its tatters above the last of the hedgerows, where a moment before was only the grey of the gloaming. On the next morning there would be guesses and wonderings, and the joy and fear of the children, and the tales that their elders told them, and the explorations by stealth to the edge of the fields of men, shy peerings through dim green gaps in the last of the hedgerows (though to look toward the east was forbidden), and rumours and expectations; and all these things were blended together by this wonder that came from the east, and so passed into legend, which lived for many a year beyond that morning; but Alveric and his tent would be gone.

So day by day and season after season that company wandered on, the lonely mateless man, the moonstruck lad and the madman, and that old grey tent with its long twisted pole. And all the stars became known to them, and all the four winds familiar, and rain and mist and hail, but the flow of yellow windows all warm and welcome at night they knew only to say farewell to: with the earliest light in the first chill of dawn Alveric would awake from impatient dreams, and Niv would arise shouting, and away they would go upon their crazed crusade before any sign of awakening appeared on the quiet dim gables. And every morning Niv prophesied that they would surely find Elfland; and the days wore away and the years.

===Chinese===
岁岁年年，阿尔韦里克（Alveric）向北漂泊，走入寂寥之地；他那灰白枯瘦的帐篷被风吹得支离破碎，给清冷的暮色平添一层阴郁。孤零零的田庄上，人家在屋中点起灯火，干草垛在苍绿色的天幕前渐渐化作黑影，那时人们有时会听见尼夫（Niv）与曾德（Zend）的木槌声，清晰地穿过那片再无他人涉足之地的静谧，传进耳中。他们的孩子趴在窗前，想看看是不是有星星落下来，却也许会瞥见那顶古怪的灰色帐篷的影儿，在最后一道树篱之上扬着它的破片飘荡——而片刻之前那里还只有暮霭的灰。次日清晨便有种种揣测与惊异，有孩子们的欢喜与畏惧，有长辈们讲给他们听的故事，有偷偷摸摸地探到人间田野尽头去的小小冒险，有从最后一道树篱昏绿的缝隙间怯怯地东张西望（虽说朝东望是被禁止的），还有种种传闻与期盼；这一切都被那来自东方的奇事搅在一起，就此沉入传说，在那个清晨之后还流传了许多年；而阿尔韦里克和他的帐篷，早已不见了踪影。

日复一日，季复一季，这一行人就这样走下去：那孤身无伴的男子，那中了月魔的少年，那疯子，还有那顶灰白旧帐篷和它那根又长又扭曲的帐杆。所有的星宿他们都认识了，四方的风也全都熟稔，还有雨、雾、冰雹；唯有夜里那一扇扇泄出暖光、满含欢迎的黄灿灿的窗户，他们只懂得与之作别：黎明的第一缕清寒之光初现时，阿尔韦里克便从不耐烦的梦中醒来，尼夫一跃而起大声呼喝，于是他们便在那一片静谧昏暗的山墙还毫无醒觉之象以前，踏上这疯狂的远征。每个早晨尼夫都预言他们必能寻见精灵国；而日子一天天磨蚀，岁月一年年流逝。

===Original===
Thyl had long left them; Thyl who prophesied victory to them in burning song, whose inspirations cheered Alveric on coldest nights and led him through rockiest ways, Thyl sang one evening suddenly songs of some young girl’s hair, Thyl who should have led their wanderings. And then one day in the gloaming, a blackbird singing, the may in bloom for miles, he turned for the houses of men, and married the maiden and was one no more with any band of wanderers.

The horses were dead; Niv and Zend carried all they had on the pole. Many years had gone. One autumn morning Alveric left the camp to go to the houses of men. Niv and Zend eyed each other. Why should Alveric seek to ask the way of others? For somehow or other their mad minds knew his purpose more swiftly than sane intuitions. Had he not Niv’s prophecies to guide him, and the things that Zend had been told on oath by the full moon?

===Chinese===
锡尔（Thyl）早已离他们而去；那个曾在烈火般的歌中为他们预言胜利的锡尔，那个在最冷的夜里鼓舞过阿尔韦里克、领他走过最崎岖的路途的锡尔，某天傍晚忽然唱起了一个少女头发的歌——这本该是引领他们漂泊的人啊。后来有一天，在暮霭之中，一只乌鸫正唱着，五月花盛开绵延数里，他便转身奔人间的宅舍而去，娶了那少女，从此再不是任何流浪者之队中的人了。

马都死了；尼夫和曾德把他们所有的一切都挑在那根帐杆上。许多年过去了。一个秋日的清晨，阿尔韦里克离开营地，往人间的宅舍去。尼夫和曾德对望一眼。阿尔韦里克何必去问旁人路呢？因为不知怎的，他们那疯癫的心智比清醒的直觉更快地便猜到了他的用意。难道他没有尼夫的预言可作指引，没有曾德听满月起誓所闻的那些话么？

===Original===
Alveric came to the houses of men, and of the folk he questioned few would speak at all of things that lay to the east, and if he spoke of the lands through which he had wandered for years they gave as little heed as if he were telling them that he had pitched his tent on the coloured layers of air that glowed and drifted and darkened in the low sky over the sunset. And the few that answered him said one thing only: that only the wizards knew.

When he had learned this Alveric went back from the fields and hedgerows and came again to his old grey tent in the lands of which none thought; and Niv and Zend sat there silent, eying him sideways, for they knew he mistrusted madness and things said by the moon. And next day when they moved their camp in the chill of dawn Niv led the way without shouting.

===Chinese===
阿尔韦里克来到人间的宅舍，他所问及的人里，肯开口谈及东方之事的寥寥无几；若他说起自己多年来所漂泊的那些土地，他们便不甚理会，仿佛他在对他们讲，自己曾把帐篷扎在了日落时分低垂天空中那层层发光、飘移、暗淡下去的彩色空气之上。而那少数回答他的人只说一句话：唯有巫师知道。

阿尔韦里克得知此理，便从田野与树篱之间折返，又回到他那顶灰白的旧帐篷所在、那片无人挂念之地；尼夫和曾德坐在那里一言不发，斜着眼瞧他，因为他们知道，他既不信疯癫，也不信月亮所言。第二天他们在黎明的寒意中拔营时，尼夫不再呼喝着在前引路了。

===Original===
They had not gone for many more weeks upon their curious journey when Alveric met one morning, at the edge of the fields men tended, one filling his bucket at a well, whose thin high conical hat and mystical air proclaimed him surely a wizard. “Master,” said Alveric, “of those arts men dread, I have a question that I would ask of the future.”

And the wizard turned from his bucket to look at Alveric with doubtful eyes, for the traveller’s tattered figure seemed scarce to promise such fees as are given by those that justly question the future. And, such as those fees are, the wizard named them. And Alveric’s wallet held that which banished the doubts of the wizard. So that he pointed to where the tip of his tower peered over a cluster of myrtles, and prayed Alveric to come to his door when the evening star should appear; and in that propitious hour he would make the future clear to him.

And again Niv and Zend knew well that their leader followed after dreams and mysteries that came not from madness nor from the moon. And he left them sitting still and saying nothing, but with minds full of fierce visions.

===Chinese===
他们这古怪的旅程又走了没几个星期，某天早晨，阿尔韦里克在人们所耕种的田野尽头，遇见一个正在井边打水的人，那人又高又尖的瘦帽子和一身神秘的气派分明宣告着他是个巫师。「大师，」阿尔韦里克说，「您所通晓的是凡人所畏惧的那门学问，我有一个问题要向未来请教。」

那巫师从水桶边转过身来，用犹疑的目光打量阿尔韦里克，因为这个旅人衣衫褴褛的模样，似乎不大可能付得起那些向未来公正问询之人所奉上的酬金。巫师便照那种酬金的数目开了一个价。而阿尔韦里克的背袋里恰好装着足以打消巫师疑虑的东西。于是巫师指了指自己塔尖从一丛桃金娘间探出的所在，请阿尔韦里克在昏星升起时到他的门前；在那个吉时，他会把未来为他看得明明白白。

尼夫和曾德又一次清清楚楚地知道，他们的首领所追寻的乃是来自疯癫与月亮之外的梦与奥秘。他让他们两个坐着不动、一声不吭，可他们的心里满是猛烈的幻象。

===Original===
Through pale air waiting for the evening star Alveric walked over the fields men tended, and came to the dark oak door of the wizard’s tower which myrtles brushed against with every breeze. A young apprentice in wizardry opened the door and, by ancient wooden steps that the rats knew better than men, led Alveric to the wizard’s upper room.

The wizard had on a silken cloak of black, which he held to be due to the future; without it he would not question the years to be. And when the young apprentice had gone away he moved to a volume he had on a high desk, and turned from the volume to Alveric to ask what he sought of the future. And Alveric asked him how he should come to Elfland. Then the wizard opened the great book’s darkened cover and turned the pages therein, and for a long while all the pages he turned were blank, but further on in the book much writing appeared, although of no kind that Alveric had ever seen. And the wizard explained that such books as these told of all things; but that he, being only concerned with the years to be, had no need to read of the past, and had therefore acquired a book that told of the future only; though he might have had more than this from the College of Wizardry, had he cared to study the follies already committed by man.

===Chinese===
阿尔韦里克穿过人们耕种的田野，走过那等候着昏星升起的苍白空气，来到了巫师塔那扇深色的橡木门前，一阵阵微风掠过，桃金娘便轻轻拂着门扉。一个年轻的巫术学徒开了门，沿着那段老鼠比人更熟稔的古老木梯，把阿尔韦里克领到了巫师楼上的房间。

巫师披着一件黑色丝质斗篷，他认为这是对未来应尽的礼数；少了它，他便不肯探问将临的岁月。等那年轻的学徒退下，他便走向高几上的一卷书，从书页间抬起头来问阿尔韦里克，他要从未来寻求什么。阿尔韦里克便问他：自己当如何才能抵达精灵国。于是巫师翻开那部大书暗沉的封皮，翻动其中的书页，有很长一段时间，他所翻过的页上全是一片空白，但再翻到书的更深处，便现出许多文字，只是其字形为阿尔韦里克平生所未见。巫师解释道，这样的书乃是记载万事的；而他既只关心将来的岁月，便无须去读过往之事，于是搜求了一部专述未来的书；倘若他肯钻研人类已经犯下的那些蠢事，原可从巫术学院（the College of Wizardry）再多得些别的卷册。

===Original===
Then he read for a while in his book, and Alveric heard the rats returning softly to the streets and houses that they had made in the stairs. And then the wizard found what he sought of the future, and told Alveric that it was written in his book how he never should come to Elfland while he carried a magical sword.

When Alveric heard this he paid the wizard’s fees and went away doleful. For he knew the perils of Elfland, which no common sabre forged on the anvils of men could ever avail to parry. He did not know that the magic that was in his sword left a flavour or taste on the air like that of lightning, which passed through the border of twilight and spread over Elfland, nor knew that the Elf King learned of his presence thus and drew his frontier away from him, so that Alveric should trouble his realm no more; but he believed what the wizard had read to him out of his book, and so went doleful away. And, leaving the stairs of oak to time and the rats, he passed out of the grove of myrtles and over the fields of men, and came again to that melancholy spot where his grey tent brooded mournfully in the wilderness, dull and silent as Niv and Zend sitting beside it. And after that they turned and wandered southwards, for all journeys now seemed equally hopeless to Alveric, who would not give up his sword to meet magical perils without magical aid; and Niv and Zend obeyed him silently, no longer guiding him with raving prophesies or with things said by the moon, for they knew he had taken counsel with another.

然后他在书中读了一会儿，阿尔韦里克听见老鼠轻轻地回到它们在楼梯里所筑的街巷与房屋。接着巫师找到了他要向未来探求的内容，便告诉阿尔韦里克，书上写得明白：他只要佩着那把魔法剑，便永远到不了精灵国。

阿尔韦里克闻言，付了巫师的酬金，满怀愁苦地离去了。因为他深知精灵国的凶险，凡间铁砧上锻出的寻常刀剑，绝无从抵挡。他并不知道，他剑中所蕴的魔法在空气里留下了一缕如闪电般的气息，这气息穿透了暮色的边境，弥漫到精灵国之上；他也不知道精灵国王正是借此察觉了他的临近，于是把边境从他面前远远挪开，好让阿尔韦里克再不能搅扰他的疆土；可他相信了巫师从那本书里念给他听的话，便这样忧愁地走开了。他把那道橡木楼梯留给时光与老鼠，走出桃金娘的小林，越过人间的田野，又回到那片萧索之地——他那顶灰白的帐篷正在荒野里愁闷地伫立，像坐在它旁边的尼夫与曾德一样沉闷无言。此后他们转身向南漂泊，因为此时在阿尔韦里克看来，所有的旅途都已同样无望，他又不愿舍下自己的剑、在无所凭依之下去应对魔法的凶险；尼夫和曾德默默听他调遣，不再以狂乱的预言或月亮的话语为他引路，因为他们知道他已去请教过别人。

By weary ways with lonely wanderings they came far to the south, and never the border of Elfland appeared with its heavy layers of twilight; yet Alveric would never give up his sword, for well he guessed that Elfland dreaded its magic, and had poor hope of recapturing Lirazel with any blade that was dreadful only to men. And after a while Niv prophesied again, and Zend would come late on nights of the full moon to wake Alveric with his tales. And for all the mystery that was in Zend when he spoke, and for all the exultation of Niv when he prophesied, Alveric knew by now that the tales and the prophecies were empty and vain and that neither of these would ever bring him to Elfland. With this mournful knowledge in a desolate land he still struck camp at dawn, still marched, still sought for the frontier, and so the months went by.

And one day where the edge of Earth was a wild untended heath, running down to the rocky waste in which Alveric had camped, he saw at evening a woman in the hat and cloak of a witch sweeping the heath with a broom. And each stroke as she swept the heath was away from the fields we know, away to the rocky waste, eastwards towards Elfland. Big gusts of black dried earth and puffs of sand were blowing towards Alveric from every powerful stroke. He walked towards her from his sorry encampment and stood near and watched her sweeping; but still she laboured at her vigorous work, striding away behind dust from the fields we know, and sweeping as she strode. And after a while she lifted her face as she swept and looked at Alveric, and he saw that it was the witch Ziroonderel. After all these years he saw that witch again, and she saw beneath the flapping rags of his cloak that sword that she had made for him once on her hill. Its scabbard of leather could not hide from the witch that it was that very sword, for she knew the flavour of magic that rose from it faintly and floated wide through the evening.

他们沿着疲惫的路途、伴着孤寂的漂泊，远远地来到南方，精灵国的边境及其那沉沉的暮霭层层却始终不曾出现；然而阿尔韦里克绝不肯抛下他的剑，因为他很清楚精灵国畏惧它的魔法，而若改用一柄只能吓唬凡人的刀刃，要再夺回莉拉泽尔（Lirazel）便希望渺茫。过了些时候，尼夫又开始发预言，曾德也常在满月之夜深更来到阿尔韦里克床前，用他的故事把他吵醒。可无论曾德开口时带着多少神秘，无论尼夫发预言时多么激昂，阿尔韦里克到如今已明白：那些故事与预言都是空而无用的，二者都不能把他带进精灵国。在这片荒凉之地上怀着这悲哀的认知，他仍在黎明时拔营，仍在行军，仍在寻找边境，月份便这样一月一月地过去。

有一天，大地的尽头是一片荒未经人照料的石楠荒原，向下延展到阿尔韦里克扎营的那片乱石荒滩。傍晚时分，他看见一个头戴女巫之帽、身披女巫斗篷的女子，正用一把扫帚扫着那片荒原。她每一帚扫去，都是从我们所知的原野扫开，扫向那片乱石荒滩，向东，朝着精灵国的方向。她每一帚用力扫下，便有大团大团干黑的泥土和阵阵飞沙朝着阿尔韦里克扑来。他从他那寒酸的营地朝她走去，就近站定，看她扫地；可她只顾奋力地干她的活，在从我们所知原野卷起的尘土后头大步迈着，一边走一边扫。过了一会儿，她一边扫一边抬起脸来望向阿尔韦里克，他这才看清，那是女巫齐龙德尔泽尔（Ziroonderel）。这么多年之后他又见到了这个女巫，而她也在他斗篷飘扬的破布下，看见了那把她昔日在自己的山丘上为他铸造的剑。那皮革的剑鞘瞒不过女巫——正是那一把剑，因为她认得那从剑上隐隐升起、在暮色里远远荡开的那一缕魔法的气息。

“Mother Witch!” said Alveric.

And she curtsied low to him, magical though she was and aged by the passing of years that had been before Alveric’s father, and though many in Erl had forgotten their lord by now; yet she had not forgotten.

He asked her what she was doing there, on the heath with her broom in the evening.

“Sweeping the world,” she said.

And Alveric wondered what rejected things she was sweeping away from the world, with grey dust mournfully turning over and over as it drifted across our fields, going slowly into the darkness that was gathering beyond our coasts.

“Why are you sweeping the world, Mother Witch?” he said.

“There’s things in the world that ought not to be here,” said she.

He looked wistfully then at the rolling grey clouds from her broom that were all drifting towards Elfland.

“Mother Witch,” he said, “can I go too? I have looked for twelve years for Elfland, and have not found a glimpse of the Elfin Mountains.”

And the old witch looked kindly at him, and then she glanced at his sword.

“He’s afraid of my magic,” she said; and thought or mystery dawned in her eyes as she spoke.

“Who?” said Alveric.

And Ziroonderel lowered her eyes.

“The King,” she said.

And then she told him how that enchanted monarch would draw away from whatever had worsted him once, and with him draw all that he had, never supporting the presence of any magic that was the equal of his.

And Alveric could not believe that such a king cared so much for the magic he had in his old black scabbard.

“It is his way,” she said.

And then he would not believe that he had waved away Elfland.

“He has the power,” said she.

And still Alveric would face this terrible king and all the powers he had; but wizard and witch had warned him that he could not go with his sword, and how go unarmed through the grizzly wood against the palace of wonder? For to go there with any sword from the anvils of men was but to go unarmed.

“Mother Witch,” he cried. “May I come no more to Elfland?”

And the longing and grief in his voice touched the witch’s heart and moved it to magical pity.

“You shall go,” she said.

He stood there half despair in the mournful evening, half dreams of Lirazel. While the witch from under her cloak drew forth a small false weight which once she had taken away from a seller of bread.

“Draw this along the edge of your sword,” she said, “all the way from hilt to point, and it will disenchant the blade, and the King will never know what sword is there.”

“Will it still fight for me?” said Alveric.

“No,” said the witch. “But once you are over the frontier take this script and wipe the blade with it on every spot that the false weight has touched.” And she fumbled under her cloak again and drew forth a poem on parchment. “It will enchant it again,” she said.

And Alveric took the weight and the written thing.

“Let not the two touch,” warned the witch.

And Alveric set them apart.

“Once over the frontier,” she said, “and he may move Elfland where he will, but you and the sword will be within his borders.”

“Mother Witch,” said Alveric, “will he be wroth with you if I do this?”

“Wroth!” said Ziroonderel. “Wroth? He will rage with a most exceeding fury, beyond the power of tigers.”

“I would not bring that on you, Mother Witch,” said Alveric.

“Ha!” said Ziroonderel. “What care I?”

Night was advancing now, and the moor and the air growing black like the witch’s cloak. She was laughing now and merging into the darkness. And soon the night was all blackness and laughter; but he could see no witch.

Then Alveric made his way back to his rocky camp by the light of its lonely fire.
