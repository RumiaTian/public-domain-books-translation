## The Solitude of Childhood / 童年的孤独

===Original===
As nothing which is impassioned escapes the eye of poetry, neither has this escaped it—that there is, or may be, through solitude, "sublime attractions of the grave." But even poetry has not perceived that these attractions may arise for a child. Not, indeed, a passion for the grave *as* the grave—from *that* a child revolts; but a passion for the grave as the portal through which it may recover some heavenly countenance, mother or sister, that has vanished. Through solitude this passion may be exalted into a frenzy like a nympholepsy. At first, when in childhood we find ourselves torn away from the lips that we could hang on forever, we throw out our arms in vain struggles to snatch at them, and pull them back again. But when we have felt for a time how hopeless is that effort, and that they cannot come to *us*, we desist from that struggle, and next we whisper to our hearts, Might not we go to *them*?

Such in principle and origin was the famous *Dulce Domum*48 of the English schoolboy. Such is the *Heimweh* (homesickness) of the German and Swiss soldier in foreign service. Such is the passion of the Calenture. Doubtless, reader, you have seen it described. The poor sailor is in tropical latitudes; deep, breathless calms have prevailed for weeks. Fever and delirium are upon him. Suddenly from his restless hammock he starts up; he will fret no longer in darkness; he ascends upon deck. How motionless are the deeps! How vast—how sweet are these shining *zaarrahs* of water! He gazes, and slowly under the blazing scenery of his brain the scenery of his eye unsettles. The waters are swallowed up; the seas have disappeared. Green fields appear, a silent dell, and a pastoral cottage. Two faces appear—are at the door—sweet female faces, and behold they beckon him. "Come to us!" they seem to say. The picture rises to his wearied brain like a *sanctus* from the choir of a cathedral, and in the twinkling of an eye, stung to madness by the cravings of his heart, the man is overboard. He is gone—he is lost for this world; but if he missed the arms of the lovely women—wife and sister—whom he sought, assuredly he has settled into arms that are mightier and not less indulgent.

===Chinese===
凡属深挚激越之情，无一能逃过诗的眼目；这一层同样不曾逃过——那就是，经由孤独，会有、或可能会有「坟墓的崇高吸引」。然而就连诗也不曾察觉，这种吸引竟能在一个孩子身上生起。诚然，那不是对坟墓*之为*坟墓的激情——*那*是孩子所畏避的；而是对坟墓作为一道门户的激情，经由这道门户，他或可重又迎回某张属天的面容，母亲或姊姊，那张已经消逝的面容。经由孤独，这种激情可以被激扬为一种癫狂，近似山泽女神附体的迷狂（nympholepsy）。起初，当我们在童年发觉自己被人从那双我们本可以永远依偎的唇边硬生生夺开时，我们张开双臂，徒然挣扎，想一把攫住他们，重新拉回到自己身边。但过了一段时间，我们已尝出这番努力何等无望，尝出他们不能到*我们*这里来，我们便止息了那番挣扎，随后便向自己心底悄声探问：何不能是*我们*到*他们*那里去？

英国学童那首著名的《Dulce Domum》（「甜美之家」）（48），其原理与根源正是如此。在异邦服役的德意志与瑞士士兵所怀的 *Heimweh*（思乡病）正是如此。谵妄热（calenture）的激情也正是如此。读者，你无疑见过对它的描写。可怜的水手正漂在热带的纬度；深沉而窒人的静风已绵延数周。热病与谵妄缠住了他。蓦地，他从躁动不宁的吊床上直起身来；他不肯再在黑暗中煎熬；他登上甲板。深渊何其凝定不动！这些闪耀于水面的 *zaarrahs*（细雨般的水光）何其浩渺——何其甘美！他凝望着，渐渐地，在他脑中灼燃的景物底下，眼中的景物涣然消散了。海水被吞没；诸海不见了。绿野显现，一方幽寂的溪谷，一座田园的茅舍。两副面容显现——就在门边——甜美的女子面容，看哪，她们在向他招手。「到我们这里来！」她们仿佛这样说。这幅图景升上他倦极的脑际，如同从大教堂的唱诗班中升起的一曲圣咏（*sanctus*）；而一眨眼之间，那人已被自己心底的渴念刺成癫狂，纵身翻入海中。他去了——他已失落于这个世界；然而，纵使他错过了他所寻访的那两副可爱的臂膀——妻子与姊姊——他确凿无疑地已安顿进另一些臂膀之中，那是更雄健的臂膀，而其温存并不稍减。

===Original===
I, young as I was, had one feeling not learned from books, and that *could* not have been learned from books, the deepest of all that connect themselves with natural scenery. It is the feeling which in "The Hart-leap Well" of Wordsworth, in his "Danish Boy," and other exquisite poems is brought out, viz., the breathless, mysterious, Pan-like silence that haunts the noonday. If there were winds abroad, then I was roused myself into sympathetic tumults. But if this dead silence haunted the air, then the peace which was in nature echoed another peace which lay in graves, and I fell into a sick languishing for things which a voice from heaven seemed to say "*cannot* be granted."

There is a German superstition, which eight or ten years after I read, of the Erl-king and his daughter. The daughter had power to tempt infants away into the invisible world; but it is, as the reader understands, by collusion with some infirmity of sick desire for such worlds in the infant itself.

===Chinese===
至于我自己，虽则年少，却已怀有一种并非从书本学来的情感——那也*断然*不是书本所能教的——在一切同自然景物相系连的情感之中，它是最深沉的一种。这就是华兹华斯（Wordsworth）在《鹿跳泉》（The Hart-leap Well）里、在他的《丹麦少年》（The Danish Boy）以及其他绝妙诗篇中所烘托出的那种情感，也就是那种屏息的、神秘的、潘神（Pan）一般的静默，在正午时分盘桓不去。若四野有风游行，我自己也会被激荡起同感的骚动。可若这种死寂盘桓在空气里，那么自然之中的安宁便应和起蛰伏于坟墓之内的另一种安宁，我便堕入一种病恹的渴慕，渴慕那些事物——仿佛有天上的声音在说，那些事物*断然*不能蒙允。

德意志有一则迷信，说的是桤木王（Erl-king）与他的女儿——桤木王之女；那传说我在八年或十年之后才读到。这位女儿有一种力量，能把婴孩诱引到无形的世界里去；然而——读者自能领会——这须凭婴孩自身对这类世界所怀的某种病弱渴念的弱点、与之串通，方能奏效。

===Original===
> "Who is that rides through the forest so fast?"

It is a knight who carries his infant upon his saddlebow. The Erl-king's daughter rides by his side; and, in words audible only when she means them to be heard, she says:

> "If thou wilt, dear baby, with me go away,
> We will see a fine show, we will play a fine play."

===Chinese===
> 「是谁骑着马儿，这般疾驰地穿过森林？」

那是一位骑士，鞍桥上抱带着他的婴孩。桤木王之女骑行在他身旁；她开口说话，那些话语唯有在她意欲让人听见时方能听见：

> 「亲爱的宝贝，你若肯跟我走，
> 好景致看不尽，好戏文玩不休。」

===Original===
That sounds lovely to my ears. Oh yes, that collusion with dim sleeping infancy is lovely to me; but I was too advanced in intellect to have been tempted by *such* temptations. Still there was a perilous attraction for me in worlds that slept and rested; and if the Erl-king's daughter had revealed herself to my perceptions, there was one "show" that she might have promised which would have wiled me away with her into the dimmest depths of the mightiest and remotest forests.

===Chinese===
这在我耳中听来是可爱的。哦，是的，同蒙昧酣睡的婴孩之间的这种串通，在我听来是可爱的；可我那时心智已开得太进，不致被*这样的*诱惑所诱动。然而，在那些沉睡着、安息着的世界里，于我总有一种危险的引力；倘若桤木王之女曾向我的知觉显现她自己，原有一种「景致」是她满可以许诺于我的——那景致会将我诱引而去，随她一同没入那最雄伟、最辽远的森林的至深至暗的幽处。
