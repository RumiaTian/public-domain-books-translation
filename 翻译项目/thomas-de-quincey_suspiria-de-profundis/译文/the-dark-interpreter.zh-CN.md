## The Dark Interpreter / 黑暗的阐释者

===Original===
> “Oh, eternity with outstretched wings, that broodest over the secret truths in whose roots lie the mysteries of man﻿—his whence, his whither﻿—have I searched thee, and struck a right key on thy dreadful organ!”

===Chinese===
> 「哦，永恒啊，你这张开铺展的双翼、孵伏于诸般隐秘真理之上的永恒——在那些真理的根柢之中，埋藏着人的奥秘：他从何而来，向何而去——我曾将你搜寻，并在你那可畏的管风琴上，按下了一个准确的琴键！」

===Original===
Suffering is a mightier agency in the hands of nature, as a Demiurgus creating the intellect, than most people are aware of.

The truth I heard often in sleep from the lips of the Dark Interpreter. Who is he? He is a shadow, reader, but a shadow with whom you must suffer me to make you acquainted. You need not be afraid of him, for when I explain his nature and origin you will see that he is essentially inoffensive; or if sometimes he menaces with his countenance, that is but seldom: and then, as his features in those moods shift as rapidly as clouds in a gale of wind, you may always look for the terrific aspects to vanish as fast as they have gathered. As to his origin﻿—what it is, I know exactly, but cannot without a little circuit of preparation make *you* understand. Perhaps you are aware of that power in the eye of many children by which in darkness they project a vast theatre of phantasmagorical figures moving forwards or backwards between their bed-curtains and the chamber walls. In some children this power is semi-voluntary﻿—they can control or perhaps suspend the shows; but in others it is altogether automatic. I myself, at the date of my last confessions, had seen in this way more processions﻿—generally solemn, mournful, belonging to eternity, but also at times glad, triumphal pomps, that seemed to enter the gates of Time﻿—than all the religions of paganism, fierce or gay, ever witnessed. Now, there is in the dark places of the human spirit﻿—in grief, in fear, in vindictive wrath﻿—a power of self-projection not unlike to this. Thirty years ago, it may be, a man called Symons committed several murders in a sudden epilepsy of planet-struck fury. According to my recollection, this case happened at Hoddesdon, which is in Middlesex. “Revenge is sweet!” was his hellish motto on that occasion, and that motto itself records the abysses which a human will can open. Revenge is *not* sweet, unless by the mighty charm of a charity that seeketh not her own it has become benignant.49 And what he had to revenge was woman’s scorn. He had been a plain farm-servant; and, in fact, he was executed, as such men often are, on a proper point of professional respect to their calling, in a smock-frock, or blouse, to render so ugly a clash of syllables. His young mistress was every way and by much his superior, as well in prospects as in education. But the man, by nature arrogant, and little acquainted with the world, presumptuously raised his eyes to one of his young mistresses. Great was the scorn with which she repulsed his audacity, and her sisters participated in her disdain. Upon this affront he brooded night and day; and, after the term of his service was over, and he, in effect, forgotten by the family, one day he suddenly descended amongst the women of the family like an Avatar of vengeance. Right and left he threw out his murderous knife without distinction of person, leaving the room and the passage floating in blood.

The final result of this carnage was not so terrific as it threatened to be. Some, I think, recovered; but, also, one, who did *not* recover, was unhappily a stranger to the whole cause of his fury. Now, this murderer always maintained, in conversation with the prison chaplain, that, as he rushed on in his hellish career, he perceived distinctly a dark figure on his right hand, keeping pace with himself. Upon *that* the superstitious, of course, supposed that some fiend had revealed himself, and associated his superfluous presence with the dark atrocity. Symons was not a philosopher, but my opinion is, that he was too much so to tolerate that hypothesis, since, if there was one man in all Europe that needed no tempter to evil on that evening, it was precisely Mr. Symons, as nobody knew better than Mr. Symons himself. I had not the benefit of his acquaintance, or I would have explained it to him. The fact is, in point of awe a fiend would be a poor, trivial *bagatelle* compared to the shadowy projections, *umbras* and *penumbras*, which the unsearchable depths of man’s nature is capable, under adequate excitement, of throwing off, and even into stationary forms. I shall have occasion to notice this point again. There are creative agencies in every part of human nature, of which the thousandth part could never be revealed in one life.

===Chinese===
在自然的手中——自然恰似一位创造智性的巨匠造物主（Demiurgus）——苦难乃是一种远比多数人所觉察的更为强有力的动因。

这一真理，我常在睡梦中从黑暗阐释者（Dark Interpreter）的唇间听到。他是谁？读者啊，他是一个暗影；但请你务须容我引荐，让你与他相识。你无须怕他，因为待我说明了他的性质与来历，你便会看到：他本质上是无害的；即便他有时以面色相威吓，那也属罕见；而且，每当他陷于那种心绪，他的容貌便如疾风中的云影，变换得同样迅疾，你尽可指望那些可怖的面相，聚拢得多么快，消散得也多么快。至于他的来历——它究竟是什么，我了然于心；只是若不先绕一小段路作些铺垫，便无法让*你*明白。或许你知道许多孩子的眼睛里有一种力量：凭这力量，他们在黑暗中能投映出整座幻影横生的剧场，种种魔影在床帐与四壁之间或前或后地移动。在有些孩子身上，这力量是半自主的——他们能节制这些光影之戏，或许还能叫它暂停；但在另一些孩子身上，它却全然出于自动。就我自己而言，直到我上一部自白所记的时日为止，我以这种方式见过的行列——大多肃穆哀戚，属于永恒；但偶尔也有欢欣的、凯旋的盛典，仿佛正要跨入时间的门户——比一切异教宗教，无论狞厉的还是欢娱的，曾经亲见的还要多。而在人类灵魂的幽暗之地——在悲恸里，在恐惧里，在复仇的怒火里——也藏着一种自我投射的力量，与此相去不远。大约三十年前，有一个名叫西蒙斯（Symons）的人，在一场中了星煞般的狂怒骤然发作之际，连行数起凶杀。据我的记忆，这桩案子发生在霍兹登（Hoddesdon），属米德尔塞克斯（Middlesex）。「复仇是甜蜜的！」便是他那一回的地狱箴言；而这句箴言本身，便记下了人的意志所能张开的深渊。复仇*并*不甜蜜，除非借着那「不求自己益处」的仁爱之大能的魅化，它才变而为仁慈（49）。而他所要复仇的，是一个女子的轻蔑。他原是个寻常的农庄雇工；而事实上，他受刑时的穿着——照这类人常有的、对其行当表示应分敬意的规矩——还是一件农家罩衫（smock-frock），或者唤作罩衣（blouse），好把这么刺耳的音节冲突换个说法。他的年轻女主人，无论在哪一方面，无论前程还是教养，都远远胜过他。但此人天性倨傲，于世事又所见甚浅，竟狂妄地抬眼企及他年轻的女主人之一。她以何等的轻蔑斥退了他的放肆，而她的姊妹们也分有了她的鄙夷。对这一番羞辱，他昼夜郁结于怀；待得服役期满，他实际上已被那家人遗忘，一日之间，他猝然降临于那家的女眷之中，恰似复仇之神临凡的化身（Avatar）。他左右挥出杀人的刀子，不分皂白，任那间屋子与过道漂浸在血泊之中。

这场屠戮的最终结果，并不像它所预示的那般可怖。有几位，我想，痊愈了；但也有一位不曾*复原*，而不幸的是，对于他那场怒火的全部缘由，她竟是全然的局外人。这位凶手与狱中牧师交谈时始终坚称：当他沿那地狱的行程狂奔之时，他分明看见右手边有一个幽暗的身影，与自己亦步亦趋。听到*这个*，迷信的人们自然断定是有恶魔现身了，便把这不请自来的在场与那桩幽暗的暴行牵连在一起。西蒙斯算不得哲学家；但我的看法是：他倒是太哲学家了，断乎容不下那个假设；因为，倘若说全欧上下有一个人在那个晚上无须恶魔的诱惑便足以行恶，那人恰恰就是西蒙斯先生——这一点，无人比西蒙斯先生本人更清楚。我无缘与他相识，不然我原可以把这层道理讲给他听。事实是：论到可畏，比起人类天性那不可测度的深处——在足够的激扰之下——所能抛掷出来、甚至凝为定形的那些幽影投映、*本影*（umbras）与*半影*（penumbras）来，一个恶魔不过是件寒碜而琐屑的*小玩意儿*（bagatelle）。这一层意思，日后我还有机会论及。人性的每一部分都藏着创造的动因；纵使穷尽一生，也决计无法把这些动因的千分之一揭示出来。

===Original===
You have heard, reader, in vision which describes our Ladies of Sorrow, particularly in the dark admonition of Madonna, to her wicked sister that hateth and tempteth, what root of dark uses may lie in moral convulsions: not the uses hypocritically vaunted by theatrical devotion which affronts the majesty of God, that ever and in all things loves Truth﻿—prefers sincerity that is erring to piety that cants. Rebellion which is the sin of witchcraft is more pardonable in His sight than speechifying resignation, listening with complacency to its own self-conquests. Show always as much neighbourhood as thou canst to grief that abases itself, which will cost thee but little effort if thine own grief hath been great. But God, who sees thy efforts in secret, will slowly strengthen those efforts, and make that to be a real deed, bearing tranquillity for thyself, which at first was but a feeble wish breathing homage to *Him*.

In afterlife, from twenty to twenty-four, on looking back to those struggles of my childhood, I used to wonder exceedingly that a child could be exposed to struggles on such a scale. But two views unfolded upon me as my experience widened, which took away that wonder. The first was the vast scale upon which the sufferings of children are found everywhere expanded in the realities of life. The generation of infants which you see is but part of those who belong to it; were born in it; and make, the world over, not one half of it. The missing half, more than an equal number to those of any age that are now living, have perished by every kind of torments. Three thousand children per annum﻿—that is, three hundred thousand per century; that is (omitting Sundays), about ten every day﻿—pass to heaven through flames50 in this very island of Great Britain. And of those who survive to reach maturity what multitudes have fought with fierce pangs of hunger, cold, and nakedness! When I came to know all this, then reverting my eye to *my* struggle, I said oftentimes it was nothing! Secondly, in watching the infancy of my own children, I made another discovery﻿—it is well known to mothers, to nurses, and also to philosophers﻿—that the tears and lamentations of infants during the year or so when they have no *other* language of complaint run through a gamut that is as inexhaustible as the cremona of Paganini. An ear but moderately learned in that language cannot be deceived as to the rate and *modulus* of the suffering which it indicates. A fretful or peevish cry cannot by any efforts make itself impassioned. The cry of impatience, of hunger, of irritation, of reproach, of alarm, are all different﻿—different as a chorus of Beethoven from a chorus of Mozart. But if ever you saw an infant suffering for an hour, as sometimes the healthiest does, under some attack of the stomach, which has the tiger-grasp of the Oriental cholera, then you will hear moans that address to their mothers an anguish of supplication for aid such as might storm the heart of Moloch. Once hearing it, you will not forget it. Now, it was a constant remark of mine, after any storm of that nature (occurring, suppose, once in two months), that always on the following day, when a long, long sleep had chased away the darkness and the memory of the darkness from the little creature’s brain, a sensible expansion had taken place in the intellectual faculties of attention, observation, and animation. It renewed the case of our great modern poet, who, on listening to the raving of the midnight storm, and the crashing which it was making in the mighty woods, reminded himself that all this hell of trouble

> “Tells also of bright calms that shall succeed.”

Pain driven to agony, or grief driven to frenzy, is essential to the ventilation of profound natures. A sea which is deeper than any that Count Massigli51 measured cannot be searched and torn up from its sleeping depths without a levanter or a monsoon. A nature which is profound in excess, but also introverted and abstracted in excess, so as to be in peril of wasting itself in interminable reverie, cannot be awakened sometimes without afflictions that go to the very foundations, heaving, stirring, yet finally harmonizing; and it is in such cases that the Dark Interpreter does his work, revealing the worlds of pain and agony and woe possible to man﻿—possible even to the innocent spirit of a child.

读者啊，你在那篇描写忧伤三圣母（Our Ladies of Sorrow）的幻象里——尤其在圣母（Madonna）对她那心怀憎恨、又专事诱惑的邪恶姊妹所作的幽暗训诫里——已经听到：道德的剧变之中，可以埋着何等幽暗的功用之根——这功用，绝非做戏般的虔诚所伪善地夸耀的那一种——那虔诚冒犯上帝的威严，而上帝永远、且在万事之中爱真理——祂宁取迷误中的真诚，不取假仁假义的虔敬。悖逆——那与行邪术同罪的悖逆——在祂眼中，比演说腔的顺服更可宽宥；那种顺服，正沾沾自得地聆听它自己克胜自身的动静。对于那自卑自抑的悲恸，你总要倾尽全力与之亲近；倘若你自己的悲恸曾经深重，这于你不过费些微力。但那在暗中察看你的努力的神，必渐渐坚固这些努力，使那起初不过是向*祂*吐送敬意的软弱心愿，成为一桩真实的作为，为你自己结出安宁。

在日后的人生里，从二十岁到二十四岁，每当我回望童年那些挣扎，总不免深觉诧异：一个孩子竟会被抛掷到如此规模的挣扎之中。但随着阅历渐广，有两层见识在我眼前展开，把这份诧异驱散了。第一层是：孩童的苦难，在生活的真实之中，处处铺展于何等浩大的规模。你眼前所见的这一代婴孩，只是属于这一代的人中的一部分；他们生于这一代；而在全世界，他们构不成它的一半。那失落的一半——比现今在世的任何年龄段的人数还要多——已在种种折磨之下夭亡。每年三千孩童——也就是每世纪三十万；再算下去（除去主日），每天约莫十个——就在这不列颠大岛上，经由烈焰（50）升入天国。而在那些得以存活至成年的人当中，又有多少人在饥饿、寒冷与裸露的剧痛里搏斗过！待我尽知这一切，再把目光转回*我自己的*挣扎，我便常常说：那算不得什么！其次，在照看我自己几个孩子的婴幼岁月里，我另有一番发现——这发现之于母亲、之于乳母、也之于哲学家，都并非陌生——婴孩在他们尚无*别种*诉苦言语的那一年上下之中，他们的眼泪与哀哭贯通着一整副音域，其无穷无尽，恰如帕格尼尼（Paganini）的克里莫纳名琴（cremona）。一只对那言语稍受训练的耳朵，对于哭声所示之苦的强度与*模量*（modulus），决不会受欺。烦躁或乖张的啼哭，任凭怎样用力，也装不出满怀激情的样子。不耐的啼哭、饥饿的啼哭、恼怒的啼哭、责备的啼哭、惊惶的啼哭，各个不同——其不同，恰如贝多芬（Beethoven）的一阕合唱之异于莫扎特（Mozart）的一阕合唱。然而，倘若你曾见过一个婴孩——有时连最健壮的也在所难免——在胃疾的侵袭下痛楚一个钟头，那病痛有着东方式霍乱那种虎攫之势，那你便会听到阵阵呻吟，向母亲们发出哀恳救助的剧痛呼声，足以强攻摩洛克（Moloch）的心。这样的哭声，一经入耳，便永难忘记。而每逢那样一场风暴过后（姑且说，两个月一回），我总有一个不变的心得：到了第二天，当一场长长的、长长的睡眠把黑暗连同黑暗的记忆一并从小生灵的脑中赶走之后，那孩子在注意、观察与活泼这些心智官能上，便有了一种可以察觉的舒展与扩充。这情形重演了我们那位伟大的现代诗人的境事——他在聆听午夜风暴的狂号、聆听它在莽莽林间摧折树木的爆裂声时，提醒自己：这一场地狱般的纷扰

> 「也诉说着行将接踵而至的明丽安宁。」

痛楚被驱迫至剧痛，悲恸被驱迫至癫狂，对于深邃天性的疏泄畅达，乃是不可或缺的。一片海，若比马西利伯爵（Count Massigli）（51）所测过的任何海域更深，便非有列万特狂风（levanter）或季风（monsoon）来袭，无从探测，也无从把它沉睡的深处翻搅撕裂起来。一种天性，深邃至于极度，而内倾与出神也至于极度，以致有在无穷无尽的幻想中虚耗自身的危险；有时，非有直抵根基的苦难——翻腾着，搅动着，而终归于谐和——便无从唤醒；而正是在这样的情形之下，黑暗阐释者行他的工：他把疼痛、剧痛与悲苦的诸般世界揭示出来，指明它们之于人是可能的——甚至之于一个孩子的无辜心灵，也是可能的。
