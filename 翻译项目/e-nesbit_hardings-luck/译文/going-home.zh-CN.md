## VIII Going Home / 第八章 归家

===Original===
In Deptford the seven months had almost gone by; Dickie had worked much, learned much, and earned much. Mr. Beale, a figure of cleanly habit and increasing steadiness, seemed like a plant growing quickly towards the sun of respectability, or a lighthouse rising bright and important out of a swirling sea﻿—of dogs.

For the dog-trade prospered exceedingly, and Mr. Beale had grown knowing in thoroughbreeds and the prize bench, had learned all about distemper and doggy fits, and when you should give an ailing dog sal-volatile and when you should merely give it less to eat. And the money in the bank grew till it, so to speak, burst the bankbook, and had to be allowed to overflow into a vast sea called Consols.

The dogs also grew, in numbers as well as in size, and the neighbors, who had borne a good deal very patiently, began, as Mr. Beale said, to “pass remarks.”

===Chinese===
在德普特福德（Deptford），七个月的光阴眼看快要过完了；迪基（Dickie）做了许多工，学了许多本事，也挣了许多钱。比尔先生（Mr. Beale）如今是个干净利落、一天比一天稳重的人物，像一株飞快朝着「体面」这轮太阳生长的植物，又像一座灯塔，从一片打着漩涡的大海——狗的大海——里耸起，明亮而神气。

因为狗这一行生意兴隆得很。比尔先生在纯种犬和赛场评奖上已经成了行家，犬瘟热、狗抽风的门道样样精通，病狗什么时候该喂嗅盐（sal-volatile），什么时候只消让它少吃点儿东西，他一清二楚。银行里的存款越长越多，长到可以说把存折都撑破了，只好任它漫溢出去，漫进一片叫做统一公债（Consols）的大海里。

狗也在长，个头在长，数目也在长。街坊四邻原本耐心地担待了许多，如今照比尔先生的说法，开始「说闲话」了。

===Original===
“It ain’t so much the little ’uns they jib at,” said Mr. Beale, taking his pipe out of his mouth and stretching his legs in the backyard, “though to my mind they yaps far more aggravatin’. It’s the cocker spannel and the Great Danes upsets them.”

“The cocker spannel has got rather a persevering bark,” said Dickie, looking up at the creeping-jenny in the window-boxes. No flowers would grow in the garden, now trampled hard by the india-rubber-soled feet of many dogs; but Dickie did his best with window-boxes, and every window was underlined by a bright dash of color﻿—creeping-jenny, Brompton stocks, stonecrop, and late tulips, and all bought from the barrows in the High Street, made a brave show.

“I don’t say as they’re actin’ unneighborly in talking about the pleece, so long as they don’t do no *more* than talk,” said Beale, with studied fairness and moderation. “What I do say is, I wish we ’ad more elbow-room for ’em. An’ as for exercisin’ of ’em all every day, like the books say﻿—well, ’ow’s one pair of ’ands to do it, let alone legs, and you in another line of business and not able to give yer time to ’em?”

===Chinese===
「街坊们看不惯的倒不是那些小狗，」比尔先生说，从嘴里拿下烟斗，在后院里舒舒服服地伸开两腿，「虽说依我看，小狗叫唤起来才真真的更烦人。叫他们受不住的，是那只可卡犬和那几条大丹犬。」

「可卡犬的叫声相当缠人，」迪基说着，抬头望望窗台花箱里的金钱草。花园里如今是种不出花了——多少只狗的橡胶底脚早把地踩得又硬又实；可迪基在花箱上用尽了心思，每扇窗底下都描上一道鲜亮的颜色——金钱草、布朗普顿紫罗兰、景天、晚郁金香——全是从大街上的货摊上买来的，摆出来倒也煞是好看。

「街坊们念叨着要惊动警察，这算不算不近人情，我不敢说；只要他们不*再进一步*，光是动动嘴，」比尔说，摆出一副用心装出来的公允和克制。「我要说的只是：但愿咱们能有一块更宽敞的地方安置它们。至于书上说，狗得天天遛——嘿，就凭一双手，怎么遛得过来？还不说两条腿呢。何况你又改了行，另做一门生意，腾不出工夫照管它们。」

===Original===
“I wish we had a bigger place, too,” said Dickie; “we could afford one now. Not but what I should be sorry to leave the old place, too. We’ve ’ad some good times here in our time, farver, ain’t us?” He sighed with the air of an old man looking back on the long-ago days of youth.

“You lay to it we ’as,” said Mr. Beale; “but this ’ere backyard, it ain’t a place where dogs can what you call exercise, not to *call* it exercise. Now is it?”

“Well, then,” said Dickie, “let’s get a move on us.”

===Chinese===
「我也盼着有个更大点儿的地方，」迪基说；「咱们如今也住得起了。不过话虽这样说，要离开老地方，我心里也舍不得。咱们爷儿俩在这儿过了些好日子，爸爸，是不是？」他叹了口气，一副老人家回望久远年轻岁月的神气。

「这你可说准了，咱们过过好日子，」比尔先生说；「可这块后院哪，狗在这儿那点儿活动，你不好*管它叫*运动，压根儿称不上运动。是不是这个理儿？」

「那么，」迪基说，「咱们就赶紧动起来吧。」

===Original===
“Ah,” said Mr. Beale, laying his pipe on his knee, “now you’re talkin’. Get a move on us. That’s what I ’oped you’d say. ’Member what I says to you in the wintertime that night Mr. Fuller looked in for his bit o’ rent﻿—about me gettin’ of the fidgets in my legs? An’ I says, ‘Why not take to the road a bit, now and again?’ an’ you says, ‘We’ll see about that, come summer.’ And ’ere *is* come summer. What you say, matey, eh?”

Dickie looked lovingly at the smart back of the little house﻿—its crisp white muslin blinds, its glimpses of neat curtains, its flowers; and then another picture came to him﻿—he saw the misty last light fainting beyond the great shoulders of the downs, and the “little starses” shining so bright and new through the branches of fir trees that interlaced above, a sweet-scented bed of soft fallen brown pine-needles.

“What say, mate?” Mr. Beale repeated; and Dickie answered﻿—

===Chinese===
「啊，」比尔先生说，把烟斗搁在膝盖上，「这才像话。赶紧动起来。我就盼着你说这句话。还记得冬天里那天晚上，富勒先生（Mr. Fuller）上门来收他那点儿房租，我跟你说我的两条腿犯了坐立不安的毛病？我说：『干吗不隔三差五上路上走走？』你说：『等到夏天再说。』喏，夏天*当真*来了。咱们要是上路上走一遭呢，伙计——那种地方宽敞，人尽可以把腿伸直了走，既踢不着狗，也碰不倒锅碗。楼上后屋里还搁着那辆旧婴儿车，精神头跟从前一样足——只要刷上点儿漆，就配得上公爵坐，真的。再说我在这儿，你在这儿，还有狗群里的几只尖子。想想吧，伙计——那张挂着绿帐子的床，自己烤的鲱鱼那股好闻的香味儿，拾树枝生起来的火，小星星一颗一颗钻出来冲你眨眼，四下里那么安静，烟斗抽到瞌睡上来，从嘴里掉下去也不碍事，再不怕烧着床罩。你说怎么样，伙计，啊？」

迪基满心爱意地打量着这所小房子的漂亮后墙——挺括的白纱帘，从窗口瞥得见的整齐帘幕，还有那些花；随后，另一幅图景浮上他的心头——他望见朦胧的残照在丘陵巨大的肩头那边渐渐淡去，「小星星」从头顶交搭的枞树枝间钻出来，那么亮，那么新，底下一张香气扑鼻的软床，铺满褐色的落松针。

「你说怎么样，伙计？」比尔先生又问了一遍；迪基答道——

===Original===
“Soon as ever you like’s what I say. And what I say is, the sooner the better.”

Having made up his mind to go, Mr. Beale at once found a dozen reasons why he could not leave home, and all the reasons were four-footed, and wagged loving tails at him. He was anxious, in fact, about the dogs. Could he really trust Amelia?

“Dunno oo you *can* trust then,” said Amelia, tossing a still handsome head. “Anybody ’ud think the dogs was babbies, to hear you.”

===Chinese===
「你拣什么时候都成，我就是这话。而且我这话是：越快越好。」

比尔先生一拿定主意要走，立刻就找出了十来条离不开家的理由，而这些理由条条都是四条腿的，还冲他亲亲热热地摇着尾巴。其实呢，他惦记的是那些狗。他当真能放心阿米莉亚（Amelia）吗？

「那我就不知道你*还能*信得过谁了，」阿米莉亚说着，把一颗依旧标致的脑袋一甩。「听你这么念叨，人家还当那些狗是吃奶的娃娃呢。」

===Original===
“So they are﻿—to me﻿—as precious as, anyway. Look here, you just come and live ’ere, ’Melia﻿—see? An’ we’ll give yer five bob a week. An’ the nipper ’e shall write it all down in lead-pencil on a bit o’ paper for you, what they’re to ’ave to eat an’ about their physic and which of ’em’s to have what.”

This took some time to settle, and some more time to write down. And then, when the lick of paint was nearly dry on the perambulator and all their shirts and socks were washed and mended, and lying on the kitchen window-ledge ready for packing, what did Mr. Beale do but go out one morning and come back with a perfectly strange dachshund.

“An’ I can’t go and leave the little beast till he knows ’imself a bit in ’is noo place,” said Mr. Beale, “an’ ’ave ’im boltin’ off gracious knows where, and being pinched or carted off to the Dogs’ Home, or that. Can I, now?”

===Chinese===
「对我来说就是娃娃——反正一样金贵。这么着吧，你就搬来住在这儿，米莉娅——听见没？我们一礼拜给你五先令。再让那孩子拿铅笔把一切都给你写在纸上：它们该吃什么，该吃什么药，哪一只该吃什么。」

这些事情商量了好一阵，写下来又花了好一阵。后来呢，等婴儿车上那一层新漆快要干透，他们的衬衫袜子也都洗好补好，摊在厨房窗台上只等打包裹扎，比尔先生却干出了什么事？一天早上出门去，回来时竟带回来一只素不相识的腊肠犬。

「再说，这小畜生在生地方还没待熟之前，我不能扔下它就走哇，」比尔先生说，「任它一溜烟跑到老天爷才知道的地方去，叫人逮了去，或是给装车送进狗狗收容所什么的。你说我能吗，啊？」

===Original===
The new dog was very long, very brown, very friendly and charming. When it had had its supper it wagged its tail, turned a clear and gentle eye on Dickie, and without any warning stood on its head.

“Well,” said Mr. Beale, “if there ain’t money in that beast! A trick dog ’e is. ’E’s wuth wot I give for ’im, so ’e is. Knows more tricks than that ’ere, I’ll be bound.”

He did. He was a singularly well-educated dog. Next morning Mr. Beale, coming downstairs, was just in time to bang the front door in the face of Amelia coming in, pail-laden, from “doing” the steps, and this to prevent the flight of the new dog. The door of one of the dog-rooms was open, and a fringe of inquisitive dogs ornamented the passage.

===Chinese===
这条新狗身子很长，毛色深棕，又和气又惹人疼。吃过晚饭，它摇了摇尾巴，把一只清亮温和的眼睛转向迪基，然后毫无预兆地来了个倒立。

「瞧见没，」比尔先生说，「这畜生身上可藏着钱呢！它是条会耍把戏的狗。我给它的那几个钱，值，就是这么值。会的把戏还不止这一手，我敢打包票。」

它果然不止这一手。这是一条教养出奇好的狗。第二天早晨，比尔先生下楼时，恰好来得及把前门砰地带上，堵住了阿米莉亚的脸——她拎着水桶，「擦洗」完台阶正要进门——为的是拦住新狗逃跑。有一间狗屋的门敞开着，一长溜好奇的狗脑袋在走廊上排成了流苏似的饰边。

“I didn’t,” she said, and stuck to it.

That afternoon Beale, smoking in the garden, got up, as he often did, to look through the window at the dogs. He gazed a moment, muttered something, and made one jump to the back door. It was closed. Amelia was giving the scullery floor a “thorough scrub over,” and had fastened the door to avoid having it opened with suddenness against her steaming pail or her crouching form.

But Mr. Beale got in at the backdoor and out at the front just in time to see the dachshund disappearing at full speed, “like a bit of brown toffee-stick,” as he said, round the end of the street. They never saw that dog again.

“Trained to it,” Mr. Beale used to say sadly whenever he told the story; “trained to it from a pup, you may lay your life. I see ’im as plain as I see you. ’E listens an’ ’e looks, and ’e doesn’t ’ear nor see nobody. “It’s a mean trick,” said Dickie when Beale told him of the loss of the dog; “that’s what I call it. I’m sorry you’ve lost the dog.”

“This ’ere walking tower of ours. We pays our own way? No cadging?”

And now they were really on the road. And they were going to Arden﻿—to that place by the sea where Dickie’s uncle, in the other life, had a castle, and where Dickie was to meet his cousins, after his seven months of waiting.

You may think that Dickie would be very excited by the thought of meeting, in this workaday, nowadays world, the children with whom he had had such wonderful adventures in the other world, the dream world﻿—too excited, perhaps, to feel really interested in the little everyday happenings of “the road.” But this was not so. The present was after all the real thing. The dreams could wait. The knowledge that they were there, waiting, made all the ordinary things more beautiful and more interesting. The feel of the soft dust underfoot, the bright, dewy grass and clover by the wayside, the lessening of houses and the growing wideness of field and pasture, all contented and delighted Dickie. He felt to the full all the joy that Mr. Beale felt in “ ’oofing it,” and when as the sun was sinking they overtook a bent, slow-going figure, it was with a thrill of real pleasure that Dickie recognized the woman who had given him the blue ribbon for True.

True himself, now grown large and thick of coat, seemed to recognize a friend, gambolled round her dreadful boots, sniffed at her withered hand.
