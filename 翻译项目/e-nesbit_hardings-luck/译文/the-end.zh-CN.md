## XII The End / 第十二章 终局

===Original===
What a triumph for little lame Dickie of Deptford!
---
You think, perhaps, that he was happy as well as proud, for proud he certainly was, with those words and those cheers ringing in his ears. He had just done the best he could, and tried to help Beale and the dogs, and the man who had thought himself to be Lord Arden had said, “I am proud that he should be the head of our house,” and all the Arden folk had cheered. It was worth having lived for.
The unselfish kindness and affection of the man he had displaced, the love of his little cousins, the devotion of Beale, the fact that he was Lord of Arden, and would soon be lord of all the old acres﻿—the knowledge that now he would learn all he chose to learn and hold in his hand some day the destinies of these village folk, all loyal to the name of Arden, the thought of all that he could be and do﻿—all these things, you think, should have made him happy.

德普特福德（Deptford）的瘸腿小迪基（Dickie），这是何等的荣耀啊！

你也许会想，他不但骄傲，而且快活；骄傲是千真万确的——那些话、那些欢呼还在他耳边回响。他刚刚竭尽所能，去帮比尔（Beale）和那群狗，而那位原本自以为是阿登勋爵（Lord Arden）的先生说了：「由他来做我们家族的当家人，我骄傲。」于是全体阿登的人都为他欢呼。一生为了这个而活，也就值了。
他挤掉了的那个人待他无私而恩厚；他的小堂弟堂妹们疼他；比尔对他忠心耿耿；他身为阿登勋爵，不久便要成为全部古老田产的主人；他知道，从此自己想学什么就能学什么，有朝一日还能把这些村人的命运握在自己手里，而他们都忠心于阿登这个名号；再加上对自己将来能成为什么、能成就什么的全部憧憬——你想，这一切本该让他快活的。

===Original===
They would have made him happy, but for one thing. All this was won at the expense of those whom he loved best﻿—the children who were his dear cousins and playfellows, the man, their father, who had moved heaven and earth to establish Dickie’s claim to the title, and had been content quietly to stand aside and give up title, castle, lands, and treasure to the little cripple from Deptford.
Dickie thought of that, and almost only of that, in the days that followed.
The life he had led in that dreamworld, when James the First was King, seemed to him now a very little thing compared with the present glory, of being the head of the house of Arden, of being the Providence, the loving overlord of all these good peasant folk, who loved his name.
Yet the thought of those days when he was plain Richard Arden, son of Sir Richard Arden, living in the beautiful house at Deptford, fretted at all his joy in his present state. That, and the thought of all he owed to him who had been Lord of Arden until he came, with his lame foot and his heirship, fretted his soul as rust frets steel. These people had received him, loved him, been kind to him when he was only a tramp boy. And he was repaying them by taking away from them priceless possessions. For so he esteemed the lordship of Arden and the old lands and the old Castle.

这一切本该让他快活的，只是有一样东西从中作梗。这一切，全是以他最爱的人为代价换来的——那几个孩子，是他亲爱的堂弟堂妹，也是他的玩伴；还有那个人，他们的父亲——为了证实迪基对这爵位的权利，他曾经上天入地，随后又心甘情愿地悄悄退到一旁，把爵位、城堡、田产和宝藏，统统让给了德普特福德来的小瘸子。
在此后的日子里，迪基想着这件事，而且几乎满心只想着这件事。
如今，比起眼前的荣光——做阿登家族的当家人，做这些善良乡民爱戴的守护天意、满怀仁爱的庇主——他当年在那个梦境之国里、詹姆斯一世在位时过的生活，就显得微末极了。
可是一想到往昔——想到自己那时只是一个平平常常的理查德·阿登（Richard Arden），是理查德·阿登爵士的儿子，住在德普特福德那座美丽的宅子里——他眼下境况中的全部欢乐便被搅得不安。这心事，加上一想到自己欠下那个人的一切——在他拖着瘸腿、带着继承权到来之前，那人是阿登的领主——他的灵魂便受着侵蚀，像钢让锈蚀了。当年他不过是个流浪儿，这些人接待了他，疼爱他，待他好。而他的报答，却是把无价的家当从他们手里拿走。因为在他看来，阿登的领主权位、古老的田地、古老的城堡，正是这样的无价之宝。

===Original===
Suppose he gave them up﻿—the priceless possessions? Suppose he went away to that sure retreat that was still left him﻿—the past? It was a sacrifice. To give up the here and now, for the far off, the almost forgotten. All that happy other life, that had once held all for which he cared, seemed thin and dreamlike beside the vivid glories of the life here, now. Yet he remembered how once that life, in King James’s time, had seemed the best thing in the world, and how he had chosen to come back from it, to help a helpless middle-aged ne’er-do-weel of a tramp﻿—Beale. Well, he had helped Beale. He had done what he set out to do. For Beale’s sake he had given up the beautiful life for the sordid life. And Beale was a new man, a man that Dickie had made. Surely now he could give up one beautiful life for another﻿—for the sake of these, his flesh and blood, who had so readily, so kindly, so generously set him in the place that had been theirs?
More and more it came home to Dickie that this was what he had to do. To go back to the times when James the First was King, and never to return to these times at all. It would be very bitter﻿—it would be like leaving home never to return. It was exile. Well, was Richard Lord Arden to be afraid of exile﻿—or of anything else? He must not just disappear either, or they would search and search for him, and never know that he was gone forever. He must slip away, and let the father of Edred and Elfrida be, as he had been, Lord Arden. He must make it appear that he, Richard Lord Arden, was dead. He thought over this very carefully. But if he seemed to be dead, Edred and Elfrida would be very unhappy. Well, they should not be unhappy. He would tell them. And then they would know that he had behaved well, and as an Arden should. Don’t be hard on him for longing for just this “little human praise.” There are very few of us who can do without it; who can bear not to let someone, very near and dear, know that we have behaved rather decently on those occasions when that is what we have done.
It took Dickie a long time to think out all this, clearly, and with no mistakes. But at last his mind was made up.

迪基越来越真切地明白到，这就是他非做不可的事。回到詹姆斯一世在位的年代去，从此再也不回到这个年代来。那会十分苦楚——就像离家而去，永不回来。那是流放。可是，理查德·阿登勋爵难道会怕流放——会怕任何别的什么吗？他也不能只是凭空消失，否则他们会找呀找呀，永远不知道他已一去不返。他必须悄悄走开，让埃德雷德（Edred）和埃尔弗里达（Elfrida）的父亲像从前那样做阿登勋爵。他必须造成一种假象，让人以为他理查德·阿登勋爵已经死了。这件事他想了又想，极其仔细。可是，他若是显得死了，埃德雷德和埃尔弗里达会非常难过。那么，就不该让他们难过。他要告诉他们。这样他们就会知道，他行事端正，不愧是阿登家的人。不要因为他渴求这一点点「小小的人间褒奖」就苛责他。我们当中很少有人离了它还能活；很少有人能忍受不让某个至亲至爱的人知道：在那些我们确实做得体面的场合，我们做得体面。
迪基花了很长时间，才把这一切想得清清楚楚、不出差错。但他终于拿定了主意。
