## XXI / 二十一

===Original===
Habits of the Cubans, beckoning, smoking, etc.﻿—Visit to the Bishop, at Jesus del Monte﻿—Coolie mart, in the Cerro﻿—The condition and prospects of the Coolies; their importation, contracts, and treatment.
===Chinese===
古巴人的习惯：招手、吸烟等——往访赫苏斯德尔蒙特（Jesus del Monte）的主教——塞罗的苦力市场——苦力的境况与前途；他们的输入、契约与待遇。

===Original===
The people of Cuba have a mode of calling attention by a sound of the tongue and lips, a sort of “P﻿—s﻿—t!” after the fashion of some parts of the continent of Europe. It is universal here; and is used not only to servants and children, but between themselves, and to strangers. It has a mean sound, to us. They make it clear and penetrating; yet it seems a poor, effeminate sibilation, and no generous, open-mouthed call. It is the mode of stopping a volante, calling a waiter, attracting the attention of a friend, or calling the notice of a stranger. I have no doubt, if a fire were to break out at the next door, a Cuban would call “P﻿—s﻿—t!”

They beckon a person to come to them by the reverse of our motion. They raise the open hand, with the palm outwards, bending the fingers toward the person they are calling. We should interpret it to be a sign to go away.

Smoking is universal, and all but constant. I have amused myself, in the street, by seeing what proportion of those I meet have cigars or *cigarettos* in their mouths. Sometimes it has been one half, sometimes one in three. The cigar is a great leveller. Any man may stop another for a light. I have seen the poor porters, on the wharf, bow to gentlemen, strangers to them, and hold out a cigar, and the gentlemen stop, give a light, and go on﻿—all as of course.
===Chinese===
古巴人有一种用舌头和嘴唇发声唤人注意的法子，类似「噗——嘶——特！」（P—s—t!），是欧洲大陆某些地方的做派。这里人人用它；不但对仆人和孩子，彼此之间、对生人也用。在我们听来，这声音鄙俗。他们发得清脆而穿透力十足；可在我们听来，那总是一种寒酸的、女人气的嘶声，算不得爽朗开口的呼喊。叫停一辆沃兰特马车，唤一名侍者，引一位朋友的注意，或提醒一个陌生人，都用这一套。我毫不怀疑，倘若隔壁失了火，古巴人喊的也会是「噗——嘶——特！」

他们招手叫人过来的姿势，同我们正相反。他们抬起摊开的手，掌心向外，朝所唤的人屈动手指。换了我们，倒要以为这是叫人走开的手势。

吸烟人人如此，几乎无时或辍。我在街上曾拿这个自娱：数数迎面而来的人里，嘴上叼着雪茄或纸烟（*cigarettos*）的占几成。有时是一半，有时是三分之一。雪茄是了不起的平等之物。无论什么人，都可以拦住别人借个火。我见过码头上穷苦的脚夫，向素不相识的绅士们鞠一躬，递上一支雪茄，绅士们便站住，给他点上火，再走开——一切理所当然。

===Original===
In the evening, called on the Señoritas F﻿—﻿—, at the house of Mr. B﻿—﻿—, and on the American young lady at Señor M﻿—﻿—’s, and on Mrs. Howe, at Mde. Almy’s, to offer to take letters or packets.

At Mrs. Almy’s, there is a gentleman from New York, Mr. G﻿—﻿—, who is dying of consumption. His only wish is to live until the *Cahawba* comes in, that he may at least die at sea, if he cannot survive until she reaches New York. He has a horror of dying here, and being buried in the Potter’s Field.﻿—Dr. Howe has just come from his chamber.
===Chinese===
傍晚，拜访了B——先生宅里的F——家小姐们、M——先生宅里的美国小姐，还有阿尔米夫人（Mde. Almy）宅里的豪太太（Mrs. Howe），提出可以代带书信或包裹。

阿尔米夫人宅里有一位纽约来的绅士，G——先生，已是痨病垂危。他唯一的愿望是活到卡哈瓦号（*Cahawba*）进港，纵然熬不到纽约，好歹也要死在海上。他一想到死在这里、葬进义冢（Potter's Field），就不寒而栗。——豪博士刚从他房里出来。

===Original===
I drove out to the bishop’s, to pay my parting respects. It is about half-past eight in the evening. He has just returned from his evening drive, is dressed in a cool, cambric dressing gown, after a bath, and is taking a quiet cigar in his high-roofed parlor. He is very cordial and polite, and talks again about the Thirty Millions Bill, and asks what I think of the result, and what I have seen of the island, and my opinion of the religious and charitable institutions. I praise the Belen and the Sisters of Charity, and condemn the prison, and he appears to agree with me. He appreciates the learning and zeal of the Brothers of Belen; speaks in the highest terms of the devotedness of the Sisters of Charity; and admits the great faults of the prison, but says it was built recently, at an enormous outlay, and he supposes the government is reluctant to be at the expense of abandoning it and building another. He charges me with messages of remembrance and respect to acquaintances we have in common. As I take my leave, he goes with me to the outer gate, which is kept locked, and again takes leave, for two leave takings are the custom of the country, and returns to the solitude of his house.
===Chinese===
我驱车前往主教寓所，作辞行之拜。时约晚上八点半。他刚兜完晚风回来，浴罢穿一件凉快的细麻布晨衣，正在高顶的客厅里安安静静地抽雪茄。他极为恳切有礼，又谈起三千万法案（Thirty Millions Bill），问我对结果的看法，问我在岛上见到了什么，问我对宗教与慈善机构的意见。我称赞了贝伦学校（Belen）和慈善修女会，批评了那座监狱，他似乎同意我的话。他推重贝伦修士们的学问与热忱；对慈善修女会的献身精神称颂备至；也承认监狱弊病深重，只是说它新近才建成，耗费浩大，他料想政府舍不得弃之不用、再费一笔钱另建新狱。他托我向我们共同相识的友人致意问安。临别时，他一直送我到外门——那门平日上着锁——再次道别，因为道别两次是这个国家的习俗——然后回到他宅子的清寂中去。

===Original===
Yesterday I drove out to the Cerro, to see the Coolie jail, or market, where the imported Coolies are kept for sale. It is a well-known place, and open to all visitors. The building has a fair-looking front; and through this I enter, past two porters, into an open yard in the rear, where, on the gravel ground, are squatting a double line of Coolies, with heads shaved, except a tuft on the crown, dressed in loose Chinese garments of blue and yellow. The dealer, who is a calm, shrewd, heartless looking man, speaking English as well as if it were his native tongue, comes out with me, calls to the Coolies, and they all stand up in a double line, facing inward, and we pass through them, preceded by a driver armed with the usual badge of the plantation driver, the short, limber whip. The dealer does not hesitate to tell me the terms on which the contracts are made, as the trade is not illegal. His account is this﻿—The importer receives $340 for each Coolie, and the purchaser agrees to pay the Coolie four dollars per month, and to give him food, and two suits of clothes a year. For this, he has his services for eight years. The contract is reduced to writing before a magistrate, and two originals are made, one kept by the Coolie and one by the purchaser, and each in Chinese and Spanish.
===Chinese===
昨天我驱车到塞罗，去看苦力监，或者说苦力市场——输入的苦力就关在那里待售。这是个人人皆知的地方，对一切来客开放。房子正面还算体面；我从这里进去，过了两个门房，来到后面的一个敞院，沙砾地上蹲着两行苦力，剃光了头，只头顶留一撮头发，穿一身宽大的蓝黄二色的中国衣裤。经销商是个神色沉静、精明而无情的人，英语说得同母语一样好。他陪我出来，朝苦力们吆喝一声，他们便全体起立，排成两行，相向而立；我们穿过行间，前面引路的是一个监工，带着种植园监工惯常的标帜——那条短而柔韧的鞭子。经销商并不讳言契约的条款，因为这买卖并不违法。他的说法是这样的——进口商每名苦力收 340 美元，买主则允诺每月付给苦力四美元，给他饭吃，每年两身衣裳。以此为代价，他得到苦力八年的劳役。契约当着治安法官的面写成文字，正本一式两份，苦力和买主各执一份，每份都用中文和西班牙文写就。

===Original===
This was a strange and striking exhibition of power. Two or three white men, bringing hundreds of Chinese thousands of miles, to a new climate and people, holding them prisoners, selling their services to masters having an unknown tongue and an unknown religion, to work at unknown trades, for inscrutable purposes!

The Coolies did not look unhealthy, though some had complaints of the eyes; yet they looked, or I fancied they looked﻿—some of them, unhappy, and some of them stolid. One I am sure had the leprosy; although the dealer would not admit it. The dealer did not deny their tendency to suicide, and the anger of attempting to chastise them, but alleged their great superiority to the Negro in intelligence, and contended that their condition was good, and better than in China, having four dollars a month, and being free at the end of eight years. He said, which I found to be true, that after being separated and employed in work, they let their hair grow, and adopt the habits and dress of the country. The newly arrived Coolies wear tufts, and blue-and-yellow, loose, Chinese clothes. Those who have been here long are distinguishable from the whites only by the peculiar tinge of the cheek, and the form of the eye.

The only respect in which his account differed from what I heard elsewhere, was in the amount the importer receives, which has always been stated to me at $400.
===Chinese===
这是权力的一场奇特而触目的展示。两三个白人，把成百的中国人从几千里外载来，置于新的气候和新的种族中间，把他们当囚犯拘着，把他们的劳役卖给语言不通、宗教不一的主人，去做闻所未闻的营生，为着不可测度的目的！

苦力们看上去并不羸弱，虽然有些人害眼病；可他们的神情——或者是我自以为看出的神情——有的郁郁不乐，有的麻木不仁。有一个我敢断定是害着麻风，尽管经销商不肯承认。经销商也不否认他们动辄自杀，也不否认惩罚他们会激起暴怒；但他强调他们在智力上远胜黑人，并且力言他们的境遇是好的，比在中国还好：每月有四美元，八年期满便得自由。他还说——后来我发现确是如此——他们被分派出去做工之后，便蓄起头发，习染本地的风俗衣着。新到的苦力留着一撮头发，穿蓝黄的宽大中国衣裤；来得久了的，除了脸颊特有的色泽和眼睛的形状，同白人几乎分辨不出。

他的说法同我别处听来的唯一出入，在进口商所得的数目上——别处向来对我说是 400 美元。

===Original===
While I am talking with him, a gentleman comes and passes down the line. He is probably a purchaser, I judge; and I leave my informant to follow what is more for his interest than talking with me.

The importation has not yet existed eight years. So the question, what will become of these men, exotics, without women or children, taking no root in the land, has not come to a solution. The constant question is﻿—will they remain and mix with the other races? Will they be permitted to remain? Will they be able to go back?

So far as I can learn, there is no law in China regulating the contracts and shipment of Chinese Coolies, and none in Cuba regulating their transportation, landing, or treatment while here. The trade has grown up and been permitted and recognized, but not regulated. It is yet to be determined how far the contract is enforceable against either party. Those Coolies that are taken from the British East Indies to British islands, are taken under contracts, with regulations, as to their exportation and return, understood and enforced. Not so the Chinese Coolies. Their importers are *lege soluti*. Some say the government will insist on their being returned. But the prevailing impression is that they will be brought in debt, and bound over again for their debts or in some other way secured to a lifelong servitude, Mr. —﻿—﻿—, a very wealthy and intelligent planter, tells me he is to go over to Regla, tomorrow morning, to see a lot of slaves offered for sale to him, and asks me if I have ever seen a sale of slaves. I never have seen that sight, and accept his invitation. We are to leave here at half-past six, or seven, at the latest. All work is early here; I believe I have mentioned that the hour of ’Change for merchants is 7:30 a.m.
===Chinese===
正同他说话间，一位绅士走来，沿着队列走过去。我猜他多半是买主；我便别过这位消息灵通之士——同我闲谈，终究不如那桩买卖于他有利。

这种输入至今还不满八年。因此，这些人——一群客居的异乡之种，没有妻儿，在此地扎不下根——将来会怎样，这个问题还没有答案。人们不断在问：他们会留下来，同别的种族混血吗？他们会获准留下吗？他们回得去吗？

据我所知，中国没有管理华工契约与装运的法律，古巴也没有管理他们的转运、登岸和居留期间待遇的法律。这门买卖自行滋长起来，得到默许和承认，却从未受到规制。契约对双方究竟有多大约束力，也尚待分晓。从英属东印度运往英属各岛的苦力，是依契约招运的，其出口与遣返自有章程，为人共知，切实执行。华工却不然。他们的进口商是 *lege soluti*（法外之人）。有人说政府会坚持把他们遣返。但流行的看法是：他们会背上债务，为偿债再度被立契卖身，或用别的法子被拴住，落得终身为奴。———先生，一位极富而聪明的种植园主，告诉我他明天早晨要过海到雷格拉（Regla）去，看一批向他出售的奴隶，问我可曾见过奴隶买卖。这景象我从未见过，便应了他的约。我们明晨六点半动身，最迟七点。这里样样活计都起得早——我记得提过，商家上交易所（'Change）的时刻是上午七点半。
