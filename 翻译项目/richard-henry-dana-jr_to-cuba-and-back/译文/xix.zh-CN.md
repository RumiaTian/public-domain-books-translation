## XIX / 十九

===Original===
Drive over the Paseo de Tacon﻿—Count de la Fernandina﻿—Cuban nobility﻿—Hospital of San Juan de Dios﻿—The Presidio and grand prison; its inmates, discipline, etc.

===Chinese===
驱车畅游塔孔大道——费尔南迪纳伯爵——古巴的贵族——圣胡安·德·迪奥斯医院——要塞监狱与大监狱；其囚犯、纪律等。

===Original===
Drove out over the Paseo de Tacon to the Cerro, a height, formerly a village, now a part of the suburbs of Havana. It is high ground, and commands a noble view of Havana and the sea. Coming in, I met the Bishop, who introduced me to the Count de La Fernandina, a dignified Spanish nobleman, who owns a beautiful villa on this Paseo, where we walked a while in the grounds. This house is very elegant and costly, with marble floors, high ceilings, piazzas, and a garden of the richest trees and flowers coming into the courtyard, and advancing even into the windows of the house. It is one of the most beautiful villas in the vicinity of Havana.

There are several noblemen who have their estates and titles in Cuba, but are recognized as nobles of Spain;﻿—in all, I should say, about fifty or sixty. Some of these have received their titles for civil or military services; but most of them have been raised to their rank on account of their wealth, or have purchased their titles outright. I believe there are but two grades, the marquis and the count. Among the titles best known to strangers are Villanueva, Fernandina, and O’Reilly. The number of Irish families who have taken rank in the Spanish service and become connected with Cuba, is rather remarkable. Beside O’Reilly, there are O’Don nel, O’Farrel, and O’Lawlor, descendants of Irishmen who entered the Spanish service after the battle of the Boyne.

===Chinese===
驱车出城，沿塔孔大道（Paseo de Tacon）前往塞罗（Cerro）。那是一处高地，从前是个村子，如今成了哈瓦那郊外的一部分。这里地势高敞，哈瓦那全城与大海尽收眼底，景色壮阔。归途中我遇见主教，他把我引见给费尔南迪纳伯爵（Count de La Fernandina）。伯爵是位气度庄重的西班牙贵族，在这条大道上拥有一座美丽的别墅，我们便在园中漫步了片刻。这座宅邸精美华贵，大理石的地面，高高的天花，环列的游廊，还有满园繁盛的花木，一直漫进庭院，甚至直抵宅子的窗前。这是哈瓦那近郊最美的别墅之一。

有几位贵族，领地和封号都在古巴，却被承认是西班牙的贵族；——总计起来，我估摸约有五六十位。其中有的因文官职守或军功而获封；但大多数是因家资豪富而跻身此列，或是干脆花钱把头衔买了下来。据我所知，贵族只有两等：侯爵与伯爵。外人最熟知的封号有比利亚努埃瓦（Villanueva）、费尔南迪纳（Fernandina）和奥赖利（O’Reilly）。在西班牙王家效力、又与古巴结缘的爱尔兰家族，数目相当可观。除奥赖利外，还有奥唐奈（O’Don nel）、奥法雷尔（O’Farrel）和奥劳勒（O’Lawlor），都是博因河之役（battle of the Boyne）后投效西班牙的爱尔兰人的后裔。

===Original===
Dr. Howe had seen the Presidio, the great prison of Havana, once; but was desirous to visit it again; so he joined me, under the conduct of our young friend, Señor —﻿—﻿—, to visit that and the hospital of San Juan de Dios. The hospital we saw first. It is supported by the government﻿—that is to say, by Cuban revenues﻿—for charity patients chiefly, but some, who can afford it, pay more or less. There are about two hundred and fifty patients. This, again, is in the charge of the Sisters of Charity. As we came upon one of the Sisters, in a passageway, in her white cap and cape, and black and blue dress, Dr. Howe said, “I always take off my hat to a Sister of Charity,” and we paid them all that attention, whenever we passed them. Dr. Howe examined the book of prescriptions, and said that there was less drugging than he supposed there would be. The attending physician told us that nearly all the physicians had studied in Paris, or in Philadelphia. There were a great many medical students in attendance, and there had just been an operation in the theatre. In an open yard we saw two men washing a dead body, and carelessly laying it on a table, for dissection. I am told that the medical and surgical professions are in a very satisfactory state of advancement in the island, and that a degree in medicine, and a license to practise, carry with them proofs of considerable proficiency. It is always observable that the physical and the exact sciences are the last to suffer under despotisms.

===Chinese===
豪博士（Dr. Howe）从前去过一次哈瓦那的大监狱——要塞监狱（Presidio），但还想再去看一趟，于是由我们那位年轻的朋友———先生（Señor ————）带领，与我同去参观监狱和圣胡安·德·迪奥斯医院（San Juan de Dios）。我们先看的是医院。它由政府供养——也就是说，由古巴的税收供养——收治的主要是慈善病人，不过也有些人付得起钱，多少缴纳一些。院里约有二百五十名病人，这里同样由慈善修女会（Sisters of Charity）照管。我们在过道里迎面遇见一位修女，白帽白披肩，穿着黑蓝两色的会衣，豪博士说：“见了慈善会的修女，我总要脱帽致敬。”此后每逢修女经过，我们都一一脱帽为礼。豪博士查看了处方簿，说用药比他预想的要少。主治医师告诉我们，几乎所有的医生都在巴黎或费城念过书。在场的医学生很多，手术示教室里刚做完一台手术。在一个露天的院子里，我们看见两个男人在冲洗一具尸体，又漫不经心地把它抬上桌子，供解剖之用。听说岛上的内外科医术均精进可观，令人满意；要取得医学学位和行医执照，都须证明确有相当的造诣。在专制制度下，自然科学与精密科学总是最后受害——这向来是引人注目的事实。

The Presidio and Grand Cárcel of Havana is a large building, of yellow stone, standing near the fort of the Punta, and is one of the striking objects as you enter the harbor. It has no appearance of a jail without, but rather of a palace or court; but within, it is full of live men’s bones and of all uncleanness. No man, whose notions are derived from an American or English penitentiary of the last twenty years, or fifty years, can form an idea of the great Cuban prison. It is simply horrible. There are no cells, except for solitary confinement of “incomunicados,”﻿—who are usually political offenders. The prisoners are placed in large rooms, with stone floors and grated windows, where they are left, from twenty to fifty in each, without work, without books, without interference or intervention of anyone, day and night, day and night, for the weeks, months or years of their sentences. The sights are dreadful. In this hot climate, so many beings, with no provision for ventilation but the grated windows﻿—so unclean, and most of them naked above the waist﻿—all spend their time in walking, talking, playing, and smoking; and, at night, without bed or blanket, they lie down on the stone floor, on what clothes they may have, to sleep if they can. The whole prison, with the exception of the few cells for the “incomunicados,” was a series of these great cages, in which human beings were shut up. Incarceration is the beginning, middle and end of the whole system. Reformation, improvement, benefit to soul or body, are not thought of. We inquired carefully, both of the officer who was sent to attend us, and of a *capitan de partido*, who was there, and were positively assured that the only distinction among the prisoners was determined by the money they paid. Those who can pay nothing, are left to the worst. Those who can pay two reals (twenty-five cents) a day, are placed in wards a little higher and better. Those who can pay six reals (seventy-five cents) a day, have better places still, called the “Salas de distincion,” and some privileges of walking in the galleries. The amount of money, and not the degree of criminality, determines the character of the punishment. There seems to be no limit to the right of the prisoners to talk with any whom they can get to hear them, at whatever distance, and to converse with visitors, and to receive money from them. In fact, the whole scene was a Babel. All that was insured was that they should not escape. When I say that no work was done, I should make the qualification that a few prisoners were employed in rolling tobacco into cigars, for a contractor; but they were very few. Among the prisoners was a *capitan de partido* (a local magistrate), who was committed on a charge of conniving at the slave trade. He could pay his six reals, of course; and had the privileges of a “Sala de distincion” and of the galleries. He walked about with us, cigar in mouth, and talked freely, and gave us much information respecting the prison. My last request was to see the *garrotte*; but it was refused me.

哈瓦那的要塞监狱与大监狱（Presidio and Grand Cárcel）是一座黄色石料建成的大厦，坐落在蓬塔要塞（fort of the Punta）近旁，是进港时最惹眼的建筑之一。它外表全无监狱的样子，倒像一座宫殿或官署；可是里面，却满是活人的骸骨和种种污秽。凡是从近二十年乃至近五十年的英美感化院中得来监狱概念的人，都无法想象这座古巴大监狱。它简直就是骇人。狱中没有牢房，只有用来单独禁闭“incomunicados”（禁绝交往者）的暗室——这些人通常是政治犯。囚犯们被关进大间里，石头的地面，装着铁栅的窗户，每间塞进二十到五十人，没有活计，没有书本，无人照管也无人干预，日复一日、夜复一夜地熬过刑期中的几个星期、几个月或几年。景象骇人听闻。在这样炎热的气候里，这么多的人挤在一起，除了铁栅窗外再无通风设施——浑身污垢，大半上身赤裸——全靠踱步、闲谈、嬉戏和抽烟打发时光；到了夜里，没有床铺，没有毯子，便躺倒在石地上，垫着自己仅有的几件衣裳，能睡着便睡。整座监狱，除了几间关押“incomunicados”的暗室外，就是一连串这样的大笼子，活人被锁在里面。监禁，就是这套制度的开头、中间和全部；改过迁善、教化改良、于灵魂或身体有何益处，一概无人考虑。我们仔细询问了奉命陪同的军官，也问了在场的*capitan de partido*（地方治安官），得到确凿的答复：囚犯之间唯一的等差，全由他们出钱多少而定。一个钱也付不出的，就落入最坏的境地。付得起每天两雷亚尔（real，合二十五美分）的，住进稍稍高爽洁净些的病室。付得起每天六雷亚尔（合七十五美分）的，住处更好，称作“Salas de distincion”（优待室），还有在回廊里走动的特权。决定刑罚轻重的，是钱的数目，而不是罪行的轻重。囚犯似乎享有毫无限制的权利，隔着任意远的距离同任何听得见他们的人说话，同来探视的人交谈，收受他们带来的钱财。实际上，整个场面就是一座巴别塔（Babel）。唯一被确保的，是他们逃不出去。我说狱中无活可做时，还应补一句：有少数囚犯受雇于一名承包商，卷制雪茄；但人数寥寥。囚犯中有一位*capitan de partido*（地方治安官），因涉嫌纵容奴隶贸易而被收押。他自然付得出每天六雷亚尔，享有“优待室”和回廊的特权。他嘴里叼着雪茄，陪我们四处走动，谈吐随便，向我们讲了不少有关这座监狱的情形。我最后请求看一看*garrotte*（绞刑具），遭到了拒绝。

It was beginning to grow dark before we got to the gate, which was duly opened to us, and we passed out, with a good will, into the open air. Dr. Howe said he was nowise reluctant to be outside. It seemed to bring back to his mind his Prussian prison, a little too forcibly to be agreeable. He felt as if he were in keeping again, and was thinking how he should feel if, just as we got to the gate, an officer were to bow and say, “Dr. Howe?” “Yes, sir.” “You may remain here. There is a charge against you of seditious language, since you have been in the island.” No man would meet such a danger more calmly, and say less about it, than he, if he thought duty to his fellow beings called him to it.

The open air, the chainless ocean, and the ships freely coming and going, were a pleasant change to the eye, even of one who had never suffered bonds for conscience’ sake. It seemed strange to see that all persons outside were doing as they pleased.

===Chinese===
我们走到大门口时，天色已经暗下来了。大门照章为我们开启，我们心甘情愿地跨出去，回到露天里。豪博士说，置身狱外，他绝无半点惋惜。这地方似乎把他记忆里的普鲁士监狱又带了回来，那股力量未免太猛，叫人不快。他觉得自己仿佛又被看押了起来，心里正盘算着：倘若我们刚走到门口，就有军官一躬身，说道：“豪博士？”“是的，先生。”“那就请您留步。您入境以来，有发表煽动性言论之嫌。”那时自己会作何感想。然而，倘若他认为对同胞的责任如此召唤他，再没有人能比他更沉着地面对这种危险，也更少把它挂在嘴边了。

露天的空气，不系锁链的大海，自由进出往来的船只——即便对一个从未为良心而身陷缧绁的人，眼前这一变换也令人愉快。看到狱外的人个个各行其是，反倒叫人觉得奇异了。
