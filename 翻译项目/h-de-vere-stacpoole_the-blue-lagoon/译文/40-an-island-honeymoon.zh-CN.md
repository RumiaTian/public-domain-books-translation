## X An Island Honeymoon / X 岛上蜜月

===Original===
One day Dick climbed on to the tree above the house, and, driving Madame Koko off the nest upon which she was sitting, peeped in. There were several pale green eggs in it. He did not disturb them, but climbed down again, and the bird resumed her seat as if nothing had happened. Such an occurrence would have terrified a bird used to the ways of men, but here the birds were so fearless and so full of confidence that often they would follow Emmeline in the wood, flying from branch to branch, peering at her through the leaves, lighting quite close to her﻿—once, even, on her shoulder.

The days passed. Dick had lost his restlessness: his wish to wander had vanished. He had no reason to wander; perhaps that was the reason why. In all the broad earth he could not have found anything more desirable than what he had.

Instead now of finding a half-naked savage followed doglike by his mate, you would have found of an evening a pair of lovers wandering on the reef. They had in a pathetic sort of way attempted to adorn the house with a blue flowering creeper taken from the wood and trained over the entrance.

===Chinese===
有一天，迪克爬上屋旁的树上，把正孵蛋的科科夫人从巢里赶开，探头往里看。巢里有好几枚淡绿色的蛋。他没有动它们，又爬了下来，母鸟回到巢里坐好，仿佛什么也没发生过。这样的事若发生在一只熟知人类习性的鸟身上，早把它吓坏了；可这里的鸟毫无惧色、满怀信任，常会跟着埃米琳进林子，从一根枝飞到另一根枝，隔着叶子窥看她，落在离她很近的地方——有一次，甚至落在她肩上。

日子一天天过去。迪克不再躁动：四处游荡的愿望消失了。他没有了游荡的理由；也许这正是缘故。在这广袤大地上，他再也找不到比眼前所有更可心的了。

如今你要是来寻，傍晚找到的不再是那个身后跟着影子般的女伴的半裸野人，而是一对流连礁石上的恋人。他们还笨拙而可爱地试着装点房子，从林中移来一株开蓝花的藤蔓，引它爬满门洞上方。

===Original===
Emmeline, up to this, had mostly done the cooking, such as it was; Dick helped her now, always. He talked to her no longer in short sentences flung out as if to a dog; and she, almost losing the strange reserve that had clung to her from childhood, half showed him her mind. It was a curious mind: the mind of a dreamer, almost the mind of a poet. The Cluricaunes dwelt there, and vague shapes born of things she had heard about or dreamt of: she had thoughts about the sea and stars, the flowers and birds.

Dick would listen to her as she talked, as a man might listen to the sound of a rivulet. His practical mind could take no share in the dreams of his other half, but her conversation pleased him.

He would look at her for a long time together, absorbed in thought. He was admiring her.

===Chinese===
在那之前，做饭多半是埃米琳的事——无非那几样；如今迪克总给她打下手。他同她说话，再也不是像对狗一样甩出的短句；而她，几乎褪尽了自幼缠着她的那种奇怪的矜持，把心思向他半掩半露地打开了。那是颗奇妙的心：一颗梦想者的心，几乎是一颗诗人的心。鞋妖精（Cluricaunes）住在里面，还有种种由传闻和梦境生出的朦胧形影：她有关于海和星星、花和鸟儿的种种念头。

埃米琳说话时，迪克听着，像一个人听溪水的声响。他那一颗讲求实际的心，参与不了另一半的梦，可她的话让他欢喜。

他会久久地凝望她，想得入神。他是在欣赏她。

===Original===
Her hair, blue-black and glossy, tangled him in its meshes; he would stroke it, so to speak, with his eyes, and then pull her close to him and bury his face in it; the smell of it was intoxicating. He breathed her as one does the perfume of a rose.

Her ears were small, and like little white shells. He would take one between finger and thumb and play with it as if it were a toy, pulling at the lobe of it, or trying to flatten out the curved part. Her breasts, her shoulders, her knees, her little feet, every bit of her, he would examine and play with and kiss. She would lie and let him, seeming absorbed in some faraway thought, of which he was the object, then all at once her arms would go round him. All this used to go on in the broad light of day, under the shadow of the artu leaves, with no one to watch except the bright-eyed birds in the leaves above.

Not all their time would be spent in this fashion. Dick was just as keen after the fish. He dug up with a spade﻿—improvised from one of the boards of the dinghy﻿—a space of soft earth near the taro patch and planted the seeds of melons he found in the wood; he rethatched the house. They were, in short, as busy as they could be in such a climate, but lovemaking would come on them in fits, and then everything would be forgotten. Just as one revisits some spot to renew the memory of a painful or pleasant experience received there, they would return to the valley of the idol and spend a whole afternoon in its shade. The absolute happiness of wandering through the woods together, discovering new flowers, getting lost, and finding their way again, was a thing beyond expression.

===Chinese===
她的头发蓝黑发亮，把他缠在了发网里；他简直是在用目光抚摩它，然后把她拉到跟前，把脸埋进去；那发香醉人。他呼吸着她，像人呼吸玫瑰的芬芳。

她的耳朵小小的，像两只白色的小贝壳。他会用拇指和食指捏住一只，当玩具摆弄，扯扯耳垂，或者想把耳廓按平。她的胸乳，她的肩，她的膝，她的小脚，她的每一寸，他都要端详、抚弄、亲吻。她会躺着任他摆布，仿佛沉浸在某个遥远的思绪里——那思绪的终点正是他——然后忽然之间，她的双臂会一下圈住他。这一切都在光天化日之下进行，在阿尔图树叶的荫下，除了头顶叶间那些目光晶亮的鸟儿，没有谁看见。

他们的时光也并不全这样打发。迪克对鱼的兴致丝毫不减。他用一把铁锹——用小艇的一块船板临时改的——在芋头地旁翻出一块松土，种下他在林中找到的甜瓜子；他又给屋顶重新苫了草。总之，在这样的气候里，他们已经忙得不能再忙了；可爱情会一阵阵袭来，一袭来，一切便都被忘掉。正如人会重访某处，重温在那里领受过的苦乐记忆，他们会回到神像的山谷，在它的荫下消磨整整一个下午。两人一同在林间漫游，寻见新的花，迷了路，又找回路来——那份圆满的幸福，非言语所能表达。

===Original===
Dick had suddenly stumbled upon Love. His courtship had lasted only some twenty minutes; it was being gone over again now, and extended.

One day, hearing a curious noise from the tree above the house, he climbed it. The noise came from the nest, which had been temporarily left by the mother bird. It was a gasping, wheezing sound, and it came from four wide-open beaks, so anxious to be fed that one could almost see into the very crops of the owners. They were Koko’s children. In another year each of those ugly downy things would, if permitted to live, be a beautiful sapphire-coloured bird with a few dove-coloured tail feathers, coral beak, and bright, intelligent eyes. A few days ago each of these things was imprisoned in a pale green egg. A month ago they were nowhere.

Something hit Dick on the cheek. It was the mother bird returned with food for the young ones. Dick drew his head aside, and she proceeded without more ado to fill their crops.

===Chinese===
迪克是猝然撞见爱情的。他的求爱统共只持续了约莫二十分钟；如今这段情事被重新演绎，且不断加长。

一天，他听见屋旁树上有个古怪的声音，便爬了上去。声音来自巢里，母鸟恰好暂时离开。那是喘息般的、嘶嘶的声音，出自四张张到最大的喙——急等着喂，急得几乎能看进它们主人的喉咙里去。它们是科科的孩子们。再过一年，这些丑兮兮的绒球，若是容许它们活下去，每一只都会变成一只美丽的宝蓝色的鸟，带着几根鸽灰色的尾羽、珊瑚色的喙，和一双聪明明亮的眼睛。几天之前，这些东西还各自囚在一枚淡绿色的蛋里。一个月之前，它们哪里都不存在。

什么东西打在迪克脸上。是母鸟衔着雏鸟的食物回来了。迪克把头闪开，她便不再耽搁，径自喂起孩子们来。
