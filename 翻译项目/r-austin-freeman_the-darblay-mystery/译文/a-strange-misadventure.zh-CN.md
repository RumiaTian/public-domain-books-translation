## IX A Strange Misadventure / 第九章 一场离奇的意外

===Original===
Although I had been in harness but a few weeks, it was with a pleasant sense of freedom that I turned from the door and crossed the road towards the alley. My time was practically my own, for, though I was remaining with Dr. Cornish until the end of the week, he was now in charge, and my responsibilities were at an end.

===Chinese===
尽管我套上挽具不过几个星期，可当我从那扇门转身出来、穿过马路走向小巷时，心头却怀着一种愉快的自由之感。我的时间实际上已归自己支配；因为虽说还要在康尼什博士家住到周末，主事的如今已是他本人，我的责任已经卸尽了。

===Original===
The alley was entered by an arched opening, so narrow that I had never suspected it of being a public thoroughfare, and I now threaded it with my shoulders almost touching the walls. Whither it finally led I have no idea, for when I reached another arched opening in the left hand wall and saw that this gave on a flight of stone steps, I descended the latter and found myself on the towpath. At the foot of the steps I stood awhile and looked about me. The moon was nearly full, and shone brightly on the opposite side of the canal, but the towpath was in deep shadow, being flanked by a high wall, behind which were the houses of the adjoining streets. Looking back﻿—that is, to my left﻿—I could just make out the bridge and the adjoining buildings, all their unlovely details blotted out by the thin night-haze, which reduced them to mere flat shapes of grey. A little nearer, one or two spots of ruddy light with wavering reflections beneath them, marked the cabin windows of the sloop, and her mast, rising above the grey obscurity, was clearly visible against the sky.

===Chinese===
进入小巷须经一道拱形的口子，那口子窄得出奇，以致我从不曾疑心它竟是一条公共通道；此刻我侧身穿行其间，双肩几乎擦着两侧的墙壁。它最终通向何处，我毫无概念，因为当我走到左壁又一道拱形开口前，看见它通向一段石阶时，我便顺阶而下，发现自己站到了纤道上。我在石阶脚下伫立片刻，环顾四周。月亮将近圆满，清辉朗朗地照在运河对岸，纤道这边却没入浓重的暗影里——一旁高墙夹峙，墙后便是邻街的房屋。回望来路——也就是朝左手边望——我只能勉强辨认出那座桥和毗邻的房屋，它们一切不入眼的细节都被薄薄的夜霭抹去了，只剩下几团扁平的灰色轮廓。再近一点，一两团暖红的光，底下曳着晃动的倒影，标出单桅帆船的舱窗；它的桅杆从灰蒙蒙的夜色中拔起，衬着天穹，历历可见。

===Original===
Naturally, I turned in that direction, sauntering luxuriously and filling my pipe as I went. Doubtless, by day the place was sordid enough in aspect﻿—though it is hard to vulgarize a navigable waterway﻿—but now, in the moonlit haze, the scene was almost romantic. And it was astonishingly quiet and peaceful. From above, beyond the high wall, the noises of the streets came subdued and distant, like sounds from another world; but here there was neither sound nor movement. The towpath was utterly deserted, and the only sign of human life was the glimmer of light from the sloop.

===Chinese===
我自然朝那个方向走去，一路信步，惬意非常，边走边往烟斗里装烟。白天此地想必够腌臜的——虽说一条可通舟楫的水道，想俗气也难——可此刻，在月色薄雾中，这景象几乎有几分浪漫。而且它出奇地宁静安谧。头顶高墙之外的街市喧声传到这里，已被滤得低沉而遥远，宛如来自另一个世界的声响；这边却是声息全无，动静皆无。纤道上渺无人迹，唯一的人间迹象，便是帆船上透出的那一点灯光。

===Original===
It was delightfully restful. I found myself treading the gravel lightly not to disturb the grateful silence, and as I strolled along, enjoying my pipe, I let my thoughts ramble idly from one topic to another. Somewhere above me, in that rather mysterious house, Simon Bendelow was lying in his narrow bed, the wasted, yellow face looking out into the darkness through that queer little celluloid window, or perhaps Miss Dewsnep and her friend were even now taking their farewell peep at him. I looked up, but, of course, the house was not visible from the towpath, nor was I now able to guess at its position.

===Chinese===
这地方真令人心神俱畅。我发觉自己放轻脚步踏着砂砾，惟恐惊扰这份可感的静谧；一路缓行，吸着烟斗，我任思绪懒懒地从一个题目荡到另一个题目。在我头顶的什么地方，在那座颇有几分神秘的房子里，西蒙·本德洛（Simon Bendelow）正躺在他那窄窄的床上，枯瘦蜡黄的脸透过那扇古怪的小赛璐珞窗凝望着黑暗；又或许，杜斯内普小姐（Miss Dewsnep）和她的朋友此刻还在向他投去诀别的一瞥。我抬头望去，可那房子自然是从纤道上望不见的，此刻我也已猜不出它的方位了。

===Original===
A little farther, and the hull of the sloop came clearly into view, and nearly opposite to it, on the towpath, I could see some kind of shed or hut against the wall, with a derrick in front of it overhanging a little quay. When I had nearly reached the shed, I passed a door in the wall, which apparently communicated with some house in one of the streets above. Then I came to the shed, a small wooden building which probably served as a lighterman’s office, and I noticed that the derrick swung from one of the corner-posts. But at this moment my attention was attracted by sounds of mild revelry from across the canal. Someone in the sloop’s deck-house had burst into song.

===Chinese===
再往前走一程，帆船的船身清晰地映入眼帘；与它几乎正相对的纤道边上，靠墙立着一座棚屋似的小房，房前一根起重吊杆，探出一座小小驳岸的上空。快到棚屋时，我经过墙上的一道门，它显然通着上首某条街里的一所房子。随后我便到了棚屋跟前——一座小小的木屋，多半充作驳船工的办公处——我注意到，那根吊杆是枢挂在其中一根角柱上的。可就在这时，运河对岸一阵轻度欢闹的声息吸引了我的注意。帆船的甲板室里有人放声唱起歌来。

===Original===
I stepped out on to the little quay and stood at the edge, looking across at the homely curtained windows and wondering what the interior of the deck-house looked like at this moment. Suddenly my ear caught an audible creak from behind me. I was in the act of turning to see whence it came when something struck me a heavy, glancing blow on the arm, crashed to the ground, and sent me flying over the edge of the quay.

===Chinese===
我迈步踏上那座小驳岸，站在边上，望着对岸那几扇挂着家常窗帘的窗户，猜想这会儿甲板室里头是什么光景。突然，我耳中捕捉到身后一声听得真切的吱嘎响。我正待转身看它从何而来，有什么东西已擦着我的胳膊沉沉一击，轰然砸在地上，把我整个儿掀过了驳岸的边沿。

===Original===
Fortunately the water here was not more than four feet deep, and as I had plunged in feet first, and am a good swimmer, I never lost control of myself. In a moment I was standing up with my head and shoulders out of water, not particularly alarmed, though a good deal annoyed and much puzzled as to what had happened. My first care was to recover my hat, which was floating forlornly close by, and the next was to consider how I should get ashore. My left arm was numb from the blow and was evidently useless for climbing. Moreover, the face of the quay was of smooth concrete, as was also the wall below the towpath. But I remembered having passed a pair of boat-steps some fifty yards back, and decided to make for them. I had thought of hailing the sloop, but as the droning song still came from the deck-house, it was clear that the Dutchmen had heard nothing, and I did not think it worth while to disturb them. Accordingly, I set forth for the steps, walking with no little difficulty over the soft, muddy bottom, keeping close to the side and steadying myself with my right hand, with which I could just reach the edge of the coping.

===Chinese===
所幸这里的水深不过四英尺，而我又是双脚先栽下去的，水性也过得去，自始至终不曾乱了方寸。转眼间我已站起身来，头和双肩露出水面，倒不怎么惊慌，只是恼火得紧，而且满腹狐疑，不知究竟出了什么事。我的头一桩要务是捞回帽子——它正凄凄惶惶地漂在手边——其次便是思量如何上岸。左臂挨了那一击，已经麻木，显然攀爬是指望不上它了。况且驳岸的立面是光滑的水泥，纤道下的护墙也是。好在我记得约莫五十码开外经过过一道船梯，便决意向它进发。我本想朝帆船呼救，可甲板室里那单调的歌声仍未停歇，显然那些荷兰人什么也没听见，我想犯不着去惊动他们。于是我便向船梯进发，在松软泥泞的底床上深一脚浅一脚地挪，紧贴着岸边走，右手勉力够着压顶石的边沿，借此稳住身子。

===Original===
It seemed a long journey, for one cannot progress very fast over soft mud with the water up to one’s armpits; but at last I reached the steps and managed to scramble up on to the towpath. There I stood for a moment or two irresolute. My first impulse was to hurry back as fast as I could and seek the Morrises’ hospitality; for I was already chilled to the bone and felt as physically wretched as the proverbial cat in similar circumstances. But I was devoured by curiosity as to what had happened, and moreover I believed that I had dropped my stick on the quay. The latter consideration decided me, for it was a favourite stick, and I set out for the quay at a very different pace from that at which I had approached it the first time.

===Chinese===
这一程似觉漫长，因为腋下没在水里、脚底又是软泥，任谁也快不了；可我总算挨到了船梯跟前，连滚带爬地上了纤道。我在那里踌躇了片刻。第一阵冲动是撒腿奔回去，求得莫里斯一家人（the Morrises）的殷勤收留；因为我已经冻彻骨髓，一身狼狈凄惶，活脱脱是俗话里那只同类处境下的猫儿。可我好奇心大炽，非要弄明白出了什么事不可；再者，我记得手杖是掉在驳岸上了。后一层考虑定了乾坤——那是一根我心爱的手杖——我便重新向驳岸进发，这一回的步子，同头一回趋近它时可大不一样了。

===Original===
The mystery was solved long before I arrived at the quay; at least it was solved in part. For the derrick which had overhung the quay, now lay on the ground. Obviously it had fallen﻿—and had missed my head only by a matter of inches. But how had it come to fall? Again, obviously, the guy-rope had given way. As it could not have broken, seeing that the derrick was unloaded and the rope must have been strong enough to bear the last load, I was a good deal puzzled as to how the accident could have befallen. Nor was I much less puzzled when I had made my inspection. The rope was, of course, unbroken and its “fall”﻿—the part below the pulley-blocks﻿—passed into the shed through a window-like hole. This I could see as I approached, and also that a door in the end of the shed nearest to me was ajar. Opening it, I plunged into the dark interior, and partly by touch and partly by the faint glimmer that came in at the window, I was able to make out the state of affairs. Just below the hole through which the rope entered was a large cleat, on which the fall must have been delayed. But the cleat was vacant, the rope hung down from the hole and its end lay in an untidy raffle on the floor. It looked as if it had been cast off the cleat; but as there had apparently been no one in the shed, the only possible supposition was that the rope had been badly secured, that it had gradually worked loose and had at last slipped off the cleat. But it was difficult to understand how it had slipped right off.

===Chinese===
未到驳岸，这桩疑团的谜底早已揭晓；至少揭晓了一半。因为先前探出驳岸上空的那根吊杆，如今躺在地上。不言而喻，它倒了下来——而且离我的脑袋不过差着几英寸。可它怎么会倒呢？同样不言而喻：那根拉索松脱了。它不可能断——吊杆空载着，绳子既承受得住上一趟的载重，想必足够结实——于是这桩事故究竟如何临头，我便大惑不解起来。待我察看一番之后，疑惑也未见稍减。绳索自然完好未断，它的“垂头”——滑轮组以下的那一段——穿过一个窗也似的孔洞通进棚屋。走近时我便看清了这一节，还看见离我最近那端的棚屋门虚掩着。推开门，我一头扎进黑黢黢的屋内，半凭触摸，半凭窗口透进的那一线微光，把屋里的情势摸了个大概。绳索入洞处的正下方有一只大系缆桩，垂头想必原是缠系在它上面的。可如今桩上空空，绳索从洞口直垂下来，末端乱蓬蓬地堆在地板上。看那样子，倒像是被人从桩上解了下来的；但棚里分明不曾有人，那么唯一说得通的假设，只能是绳索当初就没系牢，渐渐松动，终于从桩上滑脱。只是它何以能滑脱得如此干净利落，仍旧教人费解。

===Original===
I found my stick lying at the edge of the quay and close by it my pipe. Having recovered these treasures, I set off to retrace my steps along the towpath, sped on my way by a jovial chorus from the sloop. A very few minutes brought me to the steps, which I ascended two at a time, and then, having traversed the alley, I came out sheepishly into Market Street. To my relief, I saw a light in Mr. Morris’ shop, and could even make out a moving figure in the background. I hurried across, and, opening the glazed door, entered the shop, at the back of which Mr. Morris was seated at a bench filing some small object which was fixed in a vice. He looked round at me with no great cordiality, but suddenly observing my condition, he dropped his file on the bench, and exclaimed:

===Chinese===
我的手杖就躺在驳岸边上，紧挨着它的是我的烟斗。寻回这两件宝物，我便沿纤道循原路折返，帆船上一阵欢畅的合唱为我送行。不过几分钟我就到了船梯跟前，两级一跨地蹬了上去；穿出小巷，我讪讪地现身于市场街（Market Street）。让我宽慰的是，我望见莫里斯先生的铺子里亮着灯，甚至辨得出里头有个人影在动。我急忙穿街而过，推开那扇镶玻璃的门走进店里；店堂后部，莫里斯先生正坐在工作台前，锉一件用老虎钳夹着的小物件。他回头看我，神情说不上热络，可蓦地瞧见我这副光景，便把锉刀往台上一撂，失声道：

===Original===
“Good Lord, Doctor! What on earth have you been doing?”

===Chinese===
“老天爷，大夫！您这到底是干了些什么呀？”

===Original===
“Nothing on earth,” I replied, with a feeble grin, “but something in the water. I’ve been into the canal.”

===Chinese===
“陆上的事一件没干，”我咧开一个无力的笑，答道，“水里的事倒干了一桩。我下运河去了。”

===Original===
“But what for?” he demanded.

===Chinese===
“下去干什么呀？”他追问。

===Original===
“Oh, I didn’t go in intentionally,” I replied; and then I gave him a sketch of the incident, as short as I could make it, for my teeth were chattering and explanations were chilly work. However, he rose nobly to the occasion. “You’ll catch your death of cold!” he exclaimed, starting up. “Come in here, and slip off your things at once, while I go for some blankets.”

===Chinese===
“哦，我不是成心下去的，”我答道；接着我把这桩变故的始末，拣最短的说法讲了一遍——牙齿直打颤，解释缘由实在是桩寒气逼人的差事。好在他慨然当此局面。“您要冻出个好歹来的！”他霍地起身，嚷道，“快到这边来，赶紧把衣裳脱了，我去取几条毯子来。”

===Original===
He led me into a little den behind the shop, and, having lighted a gas fire, went out by a back door. I lost no time in peeling off my dripping clothes, and by the time that he returned I was in a state in which I ought to have been when I took my plunge.

===Chinese===
他领我进店堂后面一间小斗室，点着了煤气炉，便从后门出去了。我手脚也不慢，赶紧剥下淋漓的衣裳；等他回来时，我已处于当初跳水时本该保持的那种状态了。

===Original===
“Here you are,” said he. “Put on this dressing-gown and wrap yourself in the blankets. We’ll draw this chair up to the fire, and then you will be all right for the present.”

===Chinese===
“喏，给您，”他说，“把这件晨衣穿上，拿毯子裹住身子。咱们把这把椅子挪到炉边，您眼下就算安顿妥当了。”

===Original===
I followed his directions, pouring out my thanks as well as my chattering teeth would let me.

===Chinese===
我一一照办，一面倾吐谢忱——尽我那副捉对儿厮打的牙关所容许的程度。

===Original===
“Oh, that’s all right,” said he. “If you will empty your pockets, the missus can put some of the things through the wringer, and then they’ll soon dry. There happens to be a good fire in the kitchen; some advance cooking on account of the funeral. You can dry your hat and boots here. If anyone comes to the shop you might just press that electric bell-push.”

===Chinese===
“哦，这算不得什么，”他说，“您把口袋都掏空吧，有些东西内人可以拿到轧水机里过一道，很快就能干。厨房里恰好生着旺火——为着丧事的缘故，提前动灶做了些吃食。您的帽子和靴子就在这儿烤着。要是有人上门，您就按一按那只电铃按钮。”

===Original===
When he had gone, I drew the Windsor armchair close to the fire and made myself as comfortable as I could, dividing my attention between my hat and my boots, which called for careful roasting, and the contents of the room. The latter appeared to be a sort of store for the reserve stock-in-trade, and certainly this was a most amazing collection. I could not see a single article for which I would have given sixpence. The array on the shelves suggested that the shop had been stocked with the sweepings of all the stalls in Market Street, with those of Shoreditch High Street thrown in. As I ran my eye along the ranks of dial-less clocks, cracked fiddles, stopperless decanters, and tattered theological volumes, I found myself speculating profoundly on how Mr. Morris made a livelihood. He professed to be a “dealer in antiques,” and there was assuredly no question as to the antiquity of the goods in this room. But there is little pecuniary value in the kind of antiquity that is unearthed from a dust bin.

===Chinese===
他走后，我把那把温莎扶手椅拖到炉边，尽可能安顿得舒服些，将注意力一剖为二：一半给帽子和靴子——它们需要细心烘烤——一半给这屋里的存货。后者似乎是一间存放备用营业货色的库房，而这一批收藏也真教人叹为观止。我瞧不出其中任何一件东西值得我掏六便士。架上那一溜阵仗，给人的印象是：这家铺子的货源，是市场街各家摊位的扫地余货，还饶上了肖尔迪奇大街（Shoreditch High Street）各家的一份。我的目光扫过一排排没有表盘的时钟、开裂的小提琴、丢了塞子的细颈酒瓶和褴褛的神学书册，不由得深深琢磨起莫里斯先生的生计来。他自称“古董商人”，而这屋里货色的古旧，确实毋庸置疑。只可惜，从垃圾箱里刨出来的那一种古董，是换不来几个铜子的。

===Original===
It was really rather mysterious. Mr. Morris was a somewhat superior man, and he did not appear to be poor. Yet this shop did not seem capable of yielding an income that would have been acceptable to a ragpicker. And during the whole of the time in which I sat warming myself, there was not a single visitor to the shop. However, it was no concern of mine; and I had just reached this sage conclusion when Mr. Morris returned with my clothes.

===Chinese===
这事实在颇有几分神秘。莫里斯先生是个品位略高一等的人物，瞧着也不像穷。可这家铺子，看起来连产出一份拾荒者都肯将就的进项都难。而且我坐着烤火的这整段时间里，铺子里连一位客人也没有。不过，这与我何干；我刚刚抵达这条明哲的结论，莫里斯先生便抱着我的衣裳回来了。

===Original===
“There,” he said, “they are very creased and disreputable, but they are quite dry. They would have had to be cleaned and pressed, in any case.”

===Chinese===
“喏，”他说，“皱得一塌糊涂，不成体统，好在干透了。横竖它们也免不了要送洗熨烫一番。”

===Original===
With this he went out into the shop and resumed his filing, while I put on the stiff and crumpled garments. When I was dressed, I followed him and thanked him effusively for his kind offices, leaving also a grateful message for his wife. He took my thanks rather stolidly, and having wished me “good night,” picked up his file and fell to work again.

===Chinese===
说完他便回到店堂里继续锉他的活计，我则套上那身又硬又皱的行头。穿戴停当，我跟出去，为他的殷勤照拂连声道谢，又托他向太太转达感激之意。他领受我的道谢时神情颇为木然，道了声“晚安”，便拾起锉刀，重新埋头干起活来。

===Original===
I decided to walk home; principally, I think, to avoid exhibiting myself in a public vehicle. But my self-consciousness soon wore off, and when, in the neighbourhood of Clerkenwell, I perceived Dr. Usher, on the opposite side of the street, I crossed the road and touched his arm. He looked round quickly, and, recognizing me, shook hands cordially.

===Chinese===
我决定步行回家；我想，主要是不愿把自己当展品陈列在公共车辆上。可这份忸怩不久便磨没了；走到克莱肯维尔（Clerkenwell）一带时，我望见厄舍博士（Dr. Usher）在街对面，便穿过马路，碰了碰他的胳膊。他霍然回头，认出是我，热忱地握起手来。
