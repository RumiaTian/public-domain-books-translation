## XV Sir Clinton's Solution / 第十五章 克林顿爵士的解答

===Original===
“It’s a pleasure to meet Sir Clinton again,” Joan observed when they had finished their coffee. “For the last ten days or so, I’ve been dealing with a man they call the Chief Constable. I don’t much care for him. These beetle-browed officials are not my sort. Too stiff and overbearing for me, altogether.”
===Chinese===
“又见到克林顿爵士本人，真是一桩乐事，”喝完咖啡，琼说道。“这十来天，我一直同一位他们称作郡警察局长的人物打交道。我不大喜欢他。这号眉头紧锁的官老爷不合我的胃口。总的说来，对我是又生硬，又霸道。”

===Original===
Sir Clinton laughed at the hit.

“Sorry,” he said. “I’ve invited one of your aversions to join us. In fact, I think I hear him at the door now.”

“Inspector Armadale?” Joan demanded. “Well, I’ve nothing against him. You never let him get a word in edgeways at our interviews. Grasping, I call it.”
===Chinese===
克林顿爵士被这一击逗笑了。

“抱歉，”他说。“我请了你讨厌的人之一来加入我们。说起来，我想这会儿他已经到门口了。”

“阿玛代尔探长？”琼问。“唔，我跟他无冤无仇。只是咱们几回问话，你从不让他插上半句话。我管这叫独揽一切。”

===Original===
The door opened and the Inspector was ushered in. As he entered, a glance passed between him and Sir Clinton. In reply, Armadale made a furtive gesture which escaped the rest of the company.

“Passed in his checks,” Sir Clinton interpreted it to himself. “That clears the road.”
===Chinese===
门开了，探长被引进屋来。他进门时，同克林顿爵士交换了一个眼色。作为回应，阿玛代尔做了一个隐秘的手势，旁人都没有察觉。

“筹码交割了，”克林顿爵士心里如此解读，“这一来，路上就清净了。”

===Original===
Joan poured out coffee for the Inspector and then turned to the Chief Constable.

“Cecil promised that you’d tell us all about everything. Don’t linger over it. We’re all in quite good listening form and we look to you not to be boring. Proceed.”
===Chinese===
琼给探长倒上咖啡，随后转向郡警察局长。

“塞西尔答应过，你会把一切原原本本讲给我们听。别拖拖拉拉。我们都处于极佳的听讲状态，就指望你别讲得乏味。开始吧。”

===Original===
Sir Clinton refused to be disconcerted.

“Inspector Armadale’s the last authority on the subject,” he remarked. “He’s got the confession of the master mind in his pocket. I haven’t seen it yet. Suppose I give you my account of things, and the Inspector will check it for us where necessary? That seems a fair division of labour.”

“Very fair,” Una Rainhill put in. “Now, Joan, be quiet and let’s get on with the tale.”
===Chinese===
克林顿爵士偏不肯乱了方寸。

“在这个题目上，阿玛代尔探长才是最高的权威，”他说。“主谋的供词就揣在他口袋里。我还没有拜读过。不如由我把事情讲一遍，需要之处请探长替我们核对核对？这分工听起来还算公道。”

“公道得很，”尤娜·雷恩希尔插话道。“好了，琼，安静些，让故事讲下去。”

===Original===
“Before the curtain goes up,” Sir Clinton suggested, “you’d better read your programmes. First of all you find the name of Thomas Pailton, alias Cocoa Tom, alias, J. B. Foss, alias The Wizard of Woz: a retired conjurer, gaolbird, confidence-trick sharp, etc. As I read his psychology, he was rather a weak character and not over straight even in dealing with his equals. In the present play, he was acting under the orders of a gentleman of much tougher fibre.
===Chinese===
“开幕之前，”克林顿爵士提议道，“你们最好先读一读节目单。头一个名字是托马斯·佩尔顿（Thomas Pailton），化名『可可汤姆』（Cocoa Tom），化名 J. B. 福斯，化名『沃兹国的巫师』（The Wizard of Woz）：退休的魔术师、蹲过班房的老客、骗术行里的老手，等等。照我对他人心的解读，他是个相当软弱的角儿，即便同平辈的同伙打交道，也不见得多么正派。在眼下这出戏里，他是奉一位筋骨硬得多的先生之命行事。

===Original===

I’m sure he wasn’t a prentice hand. As to his character, I believe he was rather a violent person when roused, and he had a deplorable lack of control over a rather bad temper.
===Chinese===
“节目单上的第二个名字是托马斯·马登（Thomas Marden）。警方没有他早年行迹的记录，不过我疑心，马登先生在这一节上更该感激的是自己的运气，而非自己的清白。我敢肯定他不是生手。至于他的品性，我相信此人一旦被惹起，相当暴戾，而且对自己那副坏脾气，可悲地缺乏克制。

===Original===
“The third name is …?”
===Chinese===
“第三个名字是……？”

===Original===
“Stephen Racks,” the Inspector supplied in answer to Sir Clinton’s glance of inquiry.

===Chinese===
“斯蒂芬·拉克斯（Stephen Racks），”探长应着克林顿爵士探询的目光，补上了这一位。

“没错，”探长插话道。

===Original===

He was employed by Mr. Kessock in some capacity or other which gave him access to Mr. Kessock’s correspondence. I’ve no details on that point as yet.”

===Chinese===
“最后，还有一位无名氏先生（Mr. Blank），姓名我不知道，眼下正因伪造百万富翁凯索克先生的签名，在美国被捕。他在凯索克先生手下担任过这样那样的职务，得以经手凯索克先生的信件。这一点我尚无细节。”

===Original===

I’ll merely mention in passing that an American tourist came here a while ago and asked to see the Leonardo medallions, because he was writing a book on Leonardo. He, I believe, was Mr. Blank from America; and his job was to see the safe in the museum and note its pattern.
===Chinese===
“琼，你活像《猎蛇鲨记》（The Hunting of the Snark）里那个敲钟人。『噢，跳过你亲爱的叔叔吧！』好吧，遵命，我就跳过去。我只顺带提一句：前些时候有位美国游客到此一行，要求看看达·芬奇徽章，因为他正在写一部关于达·芬奇的书。我相信，这位就是来自美国的无名氏先生；他的差事，是看一眼博物馆里的保险柜，记下它的式样。

===Original===

His story was true enough, I’ve no doubt; but it set me thinking at once. I may not have shown it, Joan, but I quite agreed with you about his methods. It seemed a funny business to come straight to the police over a thing of that sort. Of course he had his reason ready; but it didn’t ring quite true, somehow. I might have put it down to tactlessness, if it hadn’t suggested something else to my mind.
===Chinese===
“我还得再跳一次；这一跳，就到了博物馆失窃的那天夜里。后来出了什么事，你们都知道。福斯先生来找我，说了那套偷听到你们几个人策划一场恶作剧的说辞。他的故事想必大体不假；可它立刻让我动了心思。琼，我也许没有露在脸上，但他那套做法，我的看法同你完全一致。为这种事径直跑来报警，未免蹊跷。他当然备好了理由；只是不知怎么，听着不太像真话。若不是它让我想起了别的什么，我兴许会把这归结为不懂分寸。

===Original===
“That pistol-shot which smashed the lamp was too neatly timed for my taste. It was fired by someone who knew precisely when the keeper was going to be gripped, and it was fired just in time to get ahead of Foxton Polegate in the raid on the showcase. That meant, if it meant anything, that the man who fired the shot was a person who knew of the practical joke. But on the face of it, Foss was the only person who knew about the joke, bar the jokers themselves. So naturally I began to suspect Foss of having a hand in the business. It was the usual mistake of the criminal—trying to be too clever and throw suspicion on to someone else.
===Chinese===
“击碎灯的那一枪，时机掐得太巧，不合我的口味。开枪的人一清二楚，知道看管人什么时候会被揪住，而枪又发得恰是时候，抢在福克西·波尔盖特动手劫陈列柜之前。这倘若有什么含义，便是：放枪的人知晓那场恶作剧。可是明面上，除了开玩笑的人自己，只有福斯一个人知道这场玩笑。于是很自然地，我开始怀疑福斯在这桩事里插了一手。这是罪犯常犯的老毛病——过分卖弄聪明，反倒把嫌疑引到别人身上。

===Original===
“Now Foss wasn’t the man in white, obviously; for he came to see me while the manhunt was still in full cry. So at that stage in the business I was fairly certain that at least two people were in the game: Foss and someone else, who was the man in white. That looked like either the valet or the chauffeur, since they were the only people I knew about who were directly associated with Foss while he was here. But this incognito business at the masked ball had made it possible for outsiders to come in unrecognized; so the man in white might be a confederate quite outside our range of knowledge. One couldn’t assume that either Marden or Brackley was in the show at all.
===Chinese===
“不过福斯显然不是白衣人；大搜捕还闹得沸反盈天的时候，他来见过我。所以在那个阶段，我已相当有把握：局中至少有两个人——福斯，还有另一个，就是那白衣人。看样子，这位不是男仆便是司机，因为据我所知，福斯住在此地期间，直接同他有瓜葛的只有这两个人。可假面舞会上隐匿姓名的那一套，让外人得以混进来而不被认出；所以白衣人也可能是完全在我们认知范围之外的同伙。谁也不能断定马登或者布拉克利到底入没入戏。

===Original===
“I learned, later on, that Foss had synchronized his watch with yours, Cecil; and that, of course, made it pretty plain that he was in the game. There was also another bit of evidence which suggested something. If either the valet or the chauffeur was the confederate, then they could easily enough have found out from the servants what costume Maurice meant to wear that night—a few questions to his valet would have got the information—and they could have chosen the Pierrot costume for their own runner in order to confuse things. That suggested that Foss’s servants might be in the business; but it proved nothing really. The white Pierrot costume was chosen mainly for its conspicuousness, I’m sure.
===Chinese===
“后来我得知，福斯曾把他的表同你的表对过时，塞西尔；这一来，他身在局中就已相当明白了。还有一小点证据也颇有暗示。倘若男仆或司机是同伙，那他们很容易从仆人嘴里打听到莫里斯那晚打算穿什么行头——向他自己的男仆问上几句，就能问到消息——然后给自己那名跑腿的挑上皮埃罗戏装，好把水搅浑。这说明福斯的仆人可能也在这桩事里；但严格说来，这证明不了什么。白色的皮埃罗戏装被选中，主要是图它显眼，这一点我敢肯定。

===Original===
“Now I come to the disappearance of the man in white.”

“Thank goodness!” Joan commented. “It gets more interesting as it goes on, doesn’t it? That’s something to be thankful for.”
===Chinese===
“现在我讲到白衣人的消失。”

“谢天谢地！”琼评点道。“越讲越有意思了，是不是？这便值得感谢了。”

===Original===
“One does one’s best,” Sir Clinton retorted, unperturbed. “Now the vanishing of that fellow could be accounted for in various ways, so far as I could see. First of all, he might have slipped down the rope into the little lake. That was what the rope was meant to suggest, obviously. But unfortunately one of the hunters had the wit to keep an eye on the lake; and it was pretty clear the man in white didn’t go that way. Then there was the possibility of his being concealed in the cave; but that was ruled out by the search of the cave. Thirdly, the gang might have hit on the opening of one of the secret passages of Ravensthorpe. Candidly, I ruled that out also. It seemed next door to impossible. But if you exclude all these ways, then there seem to be only two possibilities left. The first of these depends on the man in white having a confederate in the cordon who let him slip through. But the chance of a slip-through of that sort escaping the notice of the rest of the hunters seemed very small. It seemed to me too risky a business for them to have tried.
===Chinese===
“我尽力而为了，”克林顿爵士不动声色地回敬道。“那家伙的消失，依我看可以有好几种解释。首先，他可能顺着绳子滑进了小湖。那根绳子的用意，显然就是要人这么想。不幸的是，猎人当中有一位有头脑，始终盯着湖面；白衣人没有走那条路，这一点相当清楚。其次，他可能藏进了山洞；但洞里搜过了，这一条也就排除。第三，这伙人可能摸到了雷文索普某条暗道的入口。坦白说，这一条我也排除了。看起来几乎不可能。可要是把这些路尽数排除，剩下的似乎只有两种可能。第一种，前提是白衣人在包围圈里有个同伙，放他钻了过去。可这种钻空子的事要瞒过其余猎人的眼睛，机会似乎很小。依我看，他们不至于冒这个险。

===Original===
“The final possibility was that the fugitive disguised himself as something else. Well, what disguise would be the best? It’s a question of camouflage, and they had only a few seconds to do the camouflaging. You can’t dress up as a drainpipe or a garden-seat in a couple of seconds. So we come down to something that’s human in shape but isn’t really human. In a garden, you might pretend to be a scarecrow; but up on that terrace a scarecrow was out of the question. And then I remembered the statues.
===Chinese===

===Original===

===Chinese===
“设想有人在傍晚上了露台，把一座雕像从底座上凿了下来。碎成块的大理石可以抛进小湖，光溜溜的基座便留给逃亡者。”

===Chinese===

他眺望园林开阔的景致，随后抬眼望向高天——大朵发亮的云，正列着庄严的队伍驶过蓝天。

===Original===
“Looks peaceful, Cecil, doesn’t it? Makes one rather glad to be alive, when one gets into a scene like this. And yet, to poor Maurice it was a mere torture-chamber of nausea and torment, a horror that drove him to burrowing into holes and crannies, anywhere to escape from the terrors of the open sky. I don’t suppose that we normal people can even come near the thing in our imaginations. It’s too rum for our minds—outside everything we know. Poor devil! No wonder he went off the rails a bit in the end.”
===Chinese===
“看着很安宁，是不是，塞西尔？置身这样的景致，倒叫人颇乐于活着。可是对可怜的莫里斯，这不过是一间恶心与煎熬的刑房，一种恐怖，逼得他钻洞觅缝，只要能逃开露天长空的恐吓，钻到哪里都行。我想，我们这些正常人，连在想象里朝它走近一步都办不到。对我们的头脑，它太古怪了——完全在我们所知的一切之外。可怜的人！难怪他末了有点儿出了轨。”
