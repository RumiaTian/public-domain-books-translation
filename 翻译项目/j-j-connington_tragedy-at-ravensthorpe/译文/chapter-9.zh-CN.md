## IX The Muramasa Sword / 第九章 村正刀

===Original===
As the door closed behind Michael Clifton, the Chief Constable turned to the Inspector.

“Now we can get to business, Inspector. Let’s have a look round the place at leisure, and perhaps the surgeon will turn up before we reach the body itself.”

Followed by Armadale, he stepped over to the bay containing the corpse of Foss and began methodically to inspect the surroundings.
===Chinese===
迈克尔·克利夫顿（Michael Clifton）出去，房门在他身后合上，郡警察局长（Chief Constable）便转向探长。

“这下咱们可以办正事了，探长。先从容地把这地方四下看一看，等咱们走到尸体跟前，法医说不定也就到了。”

他身后跟着阿玛代尔（Armadale），举步走向停放福斯（Foss）尸身的那处凹室，开始有条不紊地察看四周。

===Original===
“This must have been the case that Marden slipped against when he cut his hand,” the Inspector pointed out. “There’s a big hole in the glass and some blood on the broken edges of the gap.”

“Oh, yes, there’s blood enough to suit most people,” Sir Clinton admitted, with a glance towards the shattered case. But he seemed less interested in the glass than in the floor surface; for he moved slowly to and fro, evidently trying to place himself so that the sunlight from the window was reflected up to him from the parquet. After a moment or two, he seemed satisfied.

“That part of Marden’s story seems true enough. He did slip here. If you come across, you’ll see a line where the polish of the parquet has been taken off by some hard part of his shoe. You won’t be able to spot it unless you make a mirror of the floor.”
===Chinese===
“马登（Marden）割破手时滑倒撞上的，必定就是这口柜子，”探长指出。“玻璃上有个大窟窿，破口的边缘上还沾着血。”

“哦，是啊，这儿的血够大多数人受用的，”克林顿爵士（Sir Clinton）应道，朝那口砸破的柜子瞥了一眼。可他对玻璃的兴趣似乎不及对地面；只见他慢慢来回踱着，显然是在挪动身子，好让窗口的日光从镶木地板上反照到自己眼里。过了一两分钟，他像是满意了。

“马登的话里，这一节看来不假。他确实是在这儿滑倒的。你过来瞧，可以看见一道印子，是鞋上什么硬物把镶木地板的抛光蹭掉了。不把地板当镜子使，你是瞧不出来的。”

===Original===
The Inspector in his turn moved over and satisfied himself of the existence of the faint mark.

“That confirms part of his story,” he admitted, grudgingly. “There’s a lot of blood about, quite apart from the stuff from the body. One might make something out of that.”

“Suppose we try,” Sir Clinton suggested. “Assume that he cut his hand here on the glass. He’d be all asprawl on the floor; and the first thing he’d do would be to put his hands down to help himself up. That would account for these biggish patches here, under the case. Then a foot or so away you see those round marks of droplets with tiny splashes radiating from them with a fair regularity all round. These must have been made by drops falling from his hand while he stood still﻿—no doubt while he was feeling with the other hand for his handkerchief to stanch the bleeding.”
===Chinese===
探长也走过去，亲眼认明了那道淡淡的痕迹。

“这证实了他话里的一部分，”他不情愿地承认。“且不说尸体里流出的血，这四下里血迹也不少。说不定能从这上头做点文章。”

“那咱们就试试，”克林顿爵士提议。“姑且假定他是在这儿被玻璃割破了手。他当时准是手脚摊开摔在地板上；头一件事必定是双手撑地爬起来。柜子底下这几块稍大的血印，正可由此解释。再往外一尺上下，你瞧那些圆圆的滴痕，四周匀匀称称地溅着细小的血星。那必是他站定不动时从手上滴落的——不用说，正是他腾出另一只手摸手帕、要止住血的时候。”

===Original===
The Inspector indicated his agreement.

“After he’d got it fixed up, one might expect him to go over and look at Foss. He’d gone down on the floor, you remember, while he was hurrying to Foss’s assistance.”

“There’s no sign of that,” Armadale hastened to point out. “I can’t see any blood-drops round about the body.”

“Oh, don’t be in too much of a hurry, Inspector. Perhaps they fell in the pool of Foss’s own blood or, more probably, his handkerchief soaked up any blood that flowed just then.”
===Chinese===
探长表示同意。

“包扎停当之后，按理会指望他走过去瞧瞧福斯。您记得吧，他急着赶去救福斯那会儿，人是跪伏在地板上的。”

“那可没有迹象，”阿玛代尔连忙指出。“尸体四周我看不到一点血滴。”

“哦，探长，别太性急。兴许血滴落进了福斯本人那摊血泊里；再不然，多半是他那块手帕把当时流出的血都吸掉了。”

===Original===
Sir Clinton, still with his eyes on the ground, began to cast about in search of further traces.

“Ah, here are a couple of drops at the end of the bay. Have a look at them, Inspector.”

Armadale knelt down and examined the clots.

“Made on his way to the door, probably,” he suggested.

“They might have been, if he was swinging his arms as one does when one walks freely; but one doesn’t usually swing the arm when there’s a fresh wound in the hand, I think. These aren’t round blobs like the others; they’re elongated, and all the splashing from them is at one end﻿—the end towards the safe. His hand, when they were made, was moving towards the safe’s bay, whatever his body was doing.”
===Chinese===
克林顿爵士仍旧盯着地面，开始四处搜寻别的痕迹。

“啊，凹室这头有两滴血。来看看，探长。”

阿玛代尔跪下身，细察那两粒血块。

“大概是他往门口走的时候滴下的，”他揣测道。

“若是像常人自在行走时那样甩着胳膊，倒也可能；可依我看，手里带着新伤口，人通常是不会甩那胳膊的。这两滴不像方才那些滚圆的血珠，而是拉长了的，溅出的血星全集中在一头——朝着保险柜的那一头。滴下这两滴血时，他的手正向保险柜那处凹室移动，身子在做什么且不管它。”

===Original===
Sir Clinton made a rough measurement of the distance between the two drops.

“If they’d been nearer together or further apart, then each of them might have been made while his arm was going backwards in its natural swing while he was walking towards the door. But the distance between them won’t fit that. You’ll see at once if you try walking over the ground yourself, Inspector; for you’re just about Marden’s height and your stride must be nearly the same as his.”

“He said something about going to the safe and trying the handle,” the Inspector admitted, grudgingly. “So far, his tale’s got some support.”

Sir Clinton smiled covertly at Armadale’s obvious desire to pick holes in the valet’s narrative.

“Well, let’s find out how it happened,” Sir Clinton suggested. “He evidently passed this bay and went on towards the next one, where the safe is. We’ll follow his example.”
===Chinese===
克林顿爵士约略量了量两滴血之间的距离。

“这两滴若是再近些或再远些，那倒可能是他朝门口走时、胳膊顺势向后一甩各滴下一滴。可这个间距对不上。探长，你自己走一遍这块地面就立时明白了；你的个头跟马登差不多，步幅也必定和他相仿。”

“他倒是提到过去保险柜那儿试了试把手，”探长又不情愿地承认。“到眼下为止，他的话总算有了几分佐证。”

阿玛代尔分明一心想在这男仆的叙述里挑刺，克林顿爵士暗自莞尔。

“好，咱们来看看究竟是怎么个经过，”克林顿爵士提议。“他分明是走过这处凹室，继续朝隔壁那一处——保险柜所在的去处——去了。咱们也照他的路线走一遭。”

===Original===
They turned the corner of the showcase and stepped over to the safe door.

“There’s a trace of blood on the handle, true enough,” the Inspector admitted. “But I’m not sure he told the truth about why he came to the safe.”

Sir Clinton inspected the smear of blood on the handle, but he seemed to attach very little importance to it.

“I suppose one mustn’t jump to conclusions and assume that everything’s all aboveboard,” he conceded. “But even if we keep open minds, wouldn’t it be the most natural thing in the world for Marden to try the safe door? Remember what had happened according to his story. Mr. Chacewater was in the room, for Marden saw him with his own eyes. Mr. Chacewater turned the corner of a bay﻿—the one next this; and then Marden lost him for good. If you’d been in Marden’s place, wouldn’t you have searched about, and then, finding no trace of the missing man, wouldn’t you have jumped to the conclusion that he might be hidden in the safe? And wouldn’t you have given the handle a pull, just to make sure the safe was really locked and that Mr. Chacewater wasn’t hiding inside it?”
===Chinese===
两人绕过展柜的拐角，走到保险柜门前。

===Original===
“I suppose so,” conceded the Inspector, evidently dissatisfied.

“I expect his tale isn’t complete, of course. He could hardly give every detail. It would be a bit suspicious if he had, I think. If his tale had been absolutely complete in every detail, I’d be inclined to suspect a previously prepared recitation rather than an account of the facts. In a case of this sort, one could hardly expect a watertight narrative, could one?”

He continued his examination of the floor; but there seemed to be no other bloodstains of any importance.

“Now let’s have a glance at the body,” he suggested. “We needn’t shift it till the surgeon comes; but we can see what’s to be seen without altering its position in the meanwhile.”
===Chinese===

“现在咱们来瞧瞧尸体吧，”他提议。“法医来之前不必挪动它；不过在这当口，不动它的位置，能看的咱们也尽可以看。”

===Original===
The Inspector was the first to reach the spot, and as he knelt down beside the corpse he gave an exclamation of surprise.

“Here’s an automatic pistol, sir. It’s lying almost under the body, but I can see the muzzle. It looks like a .38 calibre.”

Sir Clinton examined the body itself. The cause of death seemed obvious enough, for the weapon still remained in the wound. A glance at it set the Chief Constable’s eye ranging over the museum cases. He retreated from the bay and searched for a time until he found what he was looking for: an empty sheath in an unlocked case. Without touching the sheath, he scanned the Japanese inscription on its surface.

“什么无线电小玩意儿，”探长脱口而出。“他要这么个东西，究竟派什么用场呢？”

克林顿爵士兴致盎然地察看这仪器，随后把箱盖合上了。

“这个咱们带走，探长。”

===Original===
Then, with a sudden change of mind, he contradicted himself.

“No, we’ll leave it here for the present. That will be much better.”

Somewhat mystified by this change of intention, the Inspector agreed. Sir Clinton’s manner did not invite questions.

“I think we had better see Miss Chacewater again. There are one or two questions I’d like to put to her, Inspector; and you had better be there.”
===Chinese===
随即他念头一转，又自己推翻了自己。

“不，眼下还是留在这儿。那样好得多。”

探长对这意图的突变有些纳闷，却也应承了。克林顿爵士那副神气，不容人发问。

“我想咱们最好再见一见蔡斯沃特小姐。有一两个问题我要问她，探长；你最好也在场。”

===Original===
In a minute or two, Joan was found, with Michael Clifton in attendance. Sir Clinton did not think it worth while to sit down.

“Just a couple of points I want to ask about. First of all, is there any record of the combination which opens the lock of the safe in the museum?”

Joan shook her head.

“Maurice was the only one of us who knew it. My father did leave a note of it; but I remember that Maurice destroyed that. He specially wished to keep it to himself.”
===Chinese===
一两分钟后，找到了琼（Joan），迈克尔·克利夫顿陪在一旁。克林顿爵士觉得犯不着坐下。

“只想问两点事。首先，博物馆里那口保险柜转字密码锁的密码，可有留过什么记录？”

琼摇了摇头。

“我们家只有莫里斯（Maurice）一个人知道。父亲原先留过一张字条，可我记得让莫里斯毁掉了。他特意要把这密码独揽在自己手里。”

===Original===
“Another point,” Sir Clinton went on. “Did Foss know, on the night of the burglary, which of the rows contained the real medallions and which row the replicas were in?”

Joan reflected for a moment or two before replying.

“He must have known. Maurice had shown him the things once at least, if not oftener; and I know there was no secret as to which were the real things and which were the counterfeits.”

Sir Clinton seemed satisfied with this information.
===Chinese===
“再有一点，”克林顿爵士接着说。“失窃那晚，福斯知不知道哪一排放的是真徽章、哪一排放的是复制品？”

琼思忖了片刻才作答。

“他必定知道。莫里斯给他看过那些东西，少说也有一次；而且据我所知，哪些是真品、哪些是赝品，从来不是什么秘密。”

克林顿爵士对这答话似乎感到满意。

===Original===
“One last thing,” he continued. “I suppose you could show me where your brother keeps his correspondence. We must get hold of Kessock’s address and notify him about Foss’s death; and there seems no way of doing it as quick as this one. If the papers aren’t locked up, perhaps I could see them now?”

It appeared that the letters were available and Sir Clinton turned them over rapidly.

“Fifth Avenue? That’s satisfactory.”

He put the papers back in their place.
===Chinese===
“最后一件事，”他继续道。“你哥哥存放来往信件的地方，你能领我去看看吗？我们必须弄到凯索克（Kessock）的地址，把福斯的死讯通知他；看来再没有比这更快的法子了。要是那些信没上锁，我现在就可以看看吗？”

信件倒是现成的，克林顿爵士飞快地翻了翻。

“第五大道（Fifth Avenue）？这就妥了。”

他把信件放回原处。

===Original===
“There’s just one thing more. I’m going to put a constable on guard at the door of the museum for a while﻿—day and night for a day or two, perhaps. You won’t mind?”

“Certainly not. Do as you wish.”

Sir Clinton acknowledged the permission. Then, as though struck by an afterthought, he inquired:

“Have you Cecil’s address?”
===Chinese===
“还有一件小事。我要派一名警士在博物馆门口守上一阵子——兴许一两天，昼夜不离人。你们不会介意吧？”

“当然不会。随你的便。”

克林顿爵士谢过了这层应允。随后，像是临时又想起什么，他问道：

“塞西尔（Cecil）的地址，你有吗？”

===Original===
Joan shook her head.

“He said he’d let me know where he was staying, but he hasn’t written. Perhaps he hasn’t settled down yet. He may be staying at an hotel for a day or two.”

“Please ring me up as soon as he sends word.”

Joan promised to do this, and Sir Clinton continued:

“By the way, Inspector Armadale wishes to take the fingerprints of everyone in the house. Would you mind setting an example and having yours taken along with the rest? If you do it, then it will be easier for us to get the others. They won’t be suspicious when they hear that it’s a general inquisition.”
===Chinese===
琼摇了摇头。

“他说过会把落脚的地方告诉我，可至今没来信。也许他还没安顿下来。兴许还要在旅馆里住上一两天。”

“他一有音信，请立刻给我来电话。”

琼应承下来，克林顿爵士又接着说：

“对了，阿玛代尔探长要取宅里每个人的指纹。你肯不肯带个头，同大家一起也让取一份？你若带了头，我们取旁人的就容易了。他们一听说是人人有份的例行查问，就不会起疑。”

===Original===
Both Joan and Michael consented without ado.

“The Inspector will be with you in a moment or two,” Sir Clinton said, as he took his leave. “Just a word with you, Inspector.”

Armadale followed him from the room.

“Now, Inspector, there’s a lot for you to do yet. First of all, get these fingerprints. Then telephone to London and get Kessock’s business address. As soon as you get it, let me know.”
===Chinese===
琼和迈克尔二话不说都应允了。

“探长过一两分钟就到你们这儿来，”克林顿爵士说着告辞。“跟你说句话，探长。”

阿玛代尔随他出了房门。

“好了，探长，你还有一大堆事要办。首先，取齐这些指纹。然后打电话到伦敦，查凯索克的办公地址。一查到，立刻告诉我。”

===Original===
“But you got his address from the correspondence, sir, surely. It’s in Fifth Avenue.”

“I want his other address﻿—his office in New York, you understand?”

“His office will be shut by now, if you’re going to cable,” the Inspector pointed out, thoughtlessly.

“No, it won’t. You forget that their time is some hours behind ours. We’ll catch him in office hours if you hurry. Then when you’ve done that, get Foss’s face photographed; and arrange for a constable and reliefs to be posted at the museum door till further orders. The museum door is to be left open and the light is to be left burning at night, so that he can keep his eye on things.”
===Chinese===
“可是先生，他的地址您不是从信件里得到了吗。就在第五大道。”

“我要的是他另一个地址——他在纽约（New York）的办公处，你明白吗？”

“要是打算发电报，这会儿他办公室早关门了，”探长冒失地指出。

“不，关不了。你忘了他们那边的时间比我们晚好几个钟头。动作快些，正好赶在他的办公时间逮着他。办完这桩，再拍下福斯的面容照片；再安排警士带着轮班的人守在博物馆门口，直到另行通知。博物馆的门要敞着，夜里灯也要亮着，好让他盯得住里头的东西。”

===Original===
Inspector Armadale jotted some notes in his pocketbook. As he closed this, he seemed to think of something.

“There’s just one thing, sir. You want to get into the safe? Couldn’t we get the number of the lock combination from the makers? They must know it.”

Sir Clinton shook his head.

“Unfortunately the safe has no maker’s nameplate on it, Inspector. I looked at the time we examined it. It’s a fairly old pattern, though, I noticed; and if it hasn’t got a balanced fence arbour, I think I can guarantee to find the combination of it with a little assistance.”
===Chinese===
阿玛代尔探长在记事本上草草记下几条。合上本子时，他像是又想起了什么。

“还有一件事，先生。您想打开那保险柜？咱们不能向制造厂家要密码吗？他们必然知道。”

克林顿爵士摇了摇头。

“可惜那保险柜上没有厂家的铭牌，探长。我们查验时我看过了。不过我留意到，它是相当老式的货色；只要它没装平衡制栓轴（balanced fence arbour），稍有人搭把手，我敢担保能把密码找出来。”

===Original===
Armadale looked rather blank.

“I thought these things were too stiff to tackle,” he said.

Sir Clinton suppressed a smile.

“You ought to read Edgar Allan Poe, Inspector. ‘Human ingenuity cannot concoct a cipher which human ingenuity cannot resolve,’ was a dictum of his. If I’m not mistaken about that safe, I think I could guarantee to open it in less than ten minutes. The resources of science, and all that, you know. But I think it would be better to wait a while and see if Mr. Chacewater turns up to open it for us himself.”

“But perhaps Mr. Chacewater’s body is inside it now,” the Inspector suggested. “There may have been a double murder, for all we know.”

“In that case, we shall find him when we open it,” Sir Clinton assured him lightly. “If he’s inside, he’ll hardly be likely to shift his quarters.”
===Chinese===
阿玛代尔显得一脸茫然。

“我原以为这种锁具硬得没法下手呢，”他说。

克林顿爵士忍住一抹笑意。

“你该读读埃德加·爱伦·坡（Edgar Allan Poe），探长。『人类的巧智，造不出人类的巧智解不开的密码』，便是他的一句格言。要是我没看错那口保险柜，我敢担保十分钟之内打开它。科学的种种手段嘛，你懂的。不过我想，还是等一等的好——瞧瞧蔡斯沃特先生会不会自己回来，替咱们把它打开。”

“可说不定蔡斯沃特先生的尸首眼下就在里头，”探长提醒道。“据我们所知，兴许是桩双重凶案呢。”

“真那样，咱们开柜时自会找到他，”克林顿爵士轻描淡写地宽慰他。“他若在里头，大约也挪不了窝了。”
