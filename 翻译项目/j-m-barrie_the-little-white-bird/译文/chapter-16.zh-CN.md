## XVI / 第十六章

===Original===
Lockout Time

===Chinese===
闭园时分

===Original===
It is frightfully difficult to know much about the fairies, and almost the only thing known for certain is that there are fairies wherever there are children. Long ago children were forbidden the Gardens, and at that time there was not a fairy in the place; then the children were admitted, and the fairies came trooping in that very evening. They can't resist following the children, but you seldom see them, partly because they live in the daytime behind the railings, where you are not allowed to go, and also partly because they are so cunning. They are not a bit cunning after Lockout, but until Lockout, my word!

===Chinese===
要了解仙女（fairy）们的事情实在太难了，几乎唯一可以确定的真相是：凡是孩子们所在的地方，必有仙女。很久以前，孩子们被禁止进入花园，那时此地一只仙女也没有；后来孩子们获准入内，当晚仙女们便成群结队地涌了进来。她们忍不住要跟随孩子们，只是你难得看见她们，一方面因为她们白天藏在栏杆后面——那是你去不了的地方——另一方面也因为她们太狡猾了。闭园之后，她们就一点也不狡猾了；可在此之前，天哪！

===Original===
When you were a bird you knew the fairies pretty well, and you remember a good deal about them in your babyhood, which it is a great pity you can't write down, for gradually you forget, and I have heard of children who declared that they had never once seen a fairy. Very likely if they said this in the Kensington Gardens, they were standing looking at a fairy all the time. The reason they were cheated was that she pretended to be something else. This is one of their best tricks. They usually pretend to be flowers, because the court sits in the Fairies' Basin, and there are so many flowers there, and all along the Baby Walk, that a flower is the thing least likely to attract attention. They dress exactly like flowers, and change with the seasons, putting on white when lilies are in and blue for bluebells, and so on. They like crocus and hyacinth time best of all, as they are partial to a bit of colour, but tulips (except white ones, which are the fairy-cradles) they consider garish, and they sometimes put off dressing like tulips for days, so that the beginning of the tulip weeks is almost the best time to catch them.

===Chinese===
当你还是一只鸟的时候，你对仙女们颇为熟悉，你幼年时记得不少关于她们的事，可惜你没法把它们写下来，因为你渐渐忘记了。我听过有些孩子声称自己从未见过一只仙女。很可能，他们说这话时正站在肯辛顿花园（Kensington Gardens）里，面前就杵着一只仙女。他们被骗，是因为仙女装成了别的东西。这是她们最拿手的把戏之一。她们通常伪装成花，因为宫廷坐落在仙女盆（Fairies' Basin）里，那里以及婴儿小径（Baby Walk）沿线花朵遍布，花是最不引人注目的东西。她们穿得和花一模一样，随季节更替：百合花盛开时穿白，蓝铃花时节穿蓝，诸如此类。她们最喜欢番红花和风信子的时节，因为她们偏爱一点色彩，但郁金香——除了白色的，那是仙女的摇篮——她们觉得俗艳，有时接连几天都不肯扮成郁金香，所以郁金香盛开的头几周几乎是最容易捉住她们的时候。

===Original===
When they think you are not looking they skip along pretty lively, but if you look and they fear there is no time to hide, they stand quite still, pretending to be flowers. Then, after you have passed without knowing that they were fairies, they rush home and tell their mothers they have had such an adventure. The Fairy Basin, you remember, is all covered with ground-ivy (from which they make their castor-oil), with flowers growing in it here and there. Most of them really are flowers, but some of them are fairies. You never can be sure of them, but a good plan is to walk by looking the other way, and then turn round sharply. Another good plan, which David and I sometimes follow, is to stare them down. After a long time they can't help winking, and then you know for certain that they are fairies.

===Chinese===
她们以为你没看的时候，会蹦蹦跳跳地跑得飞快；可你若一看，她们怕来不及躲藏，就会纹丝不动地站住，假装是花。等你走过、浑然不知她们是仙女，她们便冲回家去，告诉妈妈自己经历了一场多么惊险的冒险。你还记得仙女盆吧？那里遍铺地钱草——她们的蓖麻油就是从这种草里榨出来的——零星点缀着许多花。其中大多数确实是花，但有一些是仙女。你永远拿不准，不过有个好办法：走路时故意看向别处，然后猛地回头。还有一个好办法，我和大卫（David）有时也用，就是死盯着她们看。盯久了她们忍不住会眨眼睛，那时你就可以断定她们是仙女了。

===Original===
There are also numbers of them along the Baby Walk, which is a famous gentle place, as spots frequented by fairies are called. Once twenty-four of them had an extraordinary adventure. They were a girls' school out for a walk with the governess, and all wearing hyacinth gowns, when she suddenly put her finger to her mouth, and then they all stood still on an empty bed and pretended to be hyacinths. Unfortunately, what the governess had heard was two gardeners coming to plant new flowers in that very bed. They were wheeling a handcart with the flowers in it, and were quite surprised to find the bed occupied. "Pity to lift them hyacinths," said the one man. "Duke's orders," replied the other, and, having emptied the cart, they dug up the boarding-school and put the poor, terrified things in it in five rows. Of course, neither the governess nor the girls dare let on that they were fairies, so they were carted far away to a potting-shed, out of which they escaped in the night without their shoes, but there was a great row about it among the parents, and the school was ruined.

===Chinese===
婴儿小径沿线也有大量的仙女，那是一处著名的温柔之地——仙女出没的地方都叫这个名字。有一次，二十四个仙女遭遇了一场奇遇。她们是一所女子学校的姑娘，正跟着家庭教师出来散步，全都穿着风信子色的长裙。突然老师竖起手指放在嘴边，于是她们齐刷刷地站在一块空花圃里，假装是风信子。不幸的是，老师听到的是两位园丁正朝这块花圃走来，要栽种新花。他们推着一辆手推车，里面装着花苗，发现花圃里已经有人占了，颇为惊讶。"把那些风信子挖掉怪可惜的，"一个园丁说。"公爵的命令，"另一个答道。清空手推车后，他们把这所寄宿学校整个挖了出来，把那些可怜的、吓得魂飞魄散的姑娘们排成五排栽了进去。当然，无论是老师还是学生都不敢暴露她们是仙女，于是她们被远运到一间温室棚屋里，夜里才光着脚逃了出来。但家长们为此大吵一架，那所学校也就此倒闭了。

===Original===
As for their houses, it is no use looking for them, because they are the exact opposite of our houses. You can see our houses by day but you can't see them by dark. Well, you can see their houses by dark, but you can't see them by day, for they are the colour of night, and I never heard of anyone yet who could see night in the daytime. This does not mean that they are black, for night has its colours just as day has, but ever so much brighter. Their blues and reds and greens are like ours with a light behind them. The palace is entirely built of many-coloured glasses, and is quite the loveliest of all royal residences, but the queen sometimes complains because the common people will peep in to see what she is doing. They are very inquisitive folk, and press quite hard against the glass, and that is why their noses are mostly snubby. The streets are miles long and very twisty, and have paths on each side made of bright worsted. The birds used to steal the worsted for their nests, but a policeman has been appointed to hold on at the other end.

===Chinese===
至于她们的房子，找是没用的，因为它们和我们的房子恰好相反。我们的房子白天看得见，夜里看不见。她们的房子则夜里看得见，白天看不见，因为它们的颜色就是夜色——我还从未听说过谁能白日里看见夜。这并不是说它们是黑色的，夜自有夜的颜色，就像白天有白天的颜色一样，只不过夜色要鲜亮得多。她们的蓝、红、绿就像我们的，只不过背后好像打着光。那座宫殿完全由五颜六色的玻璃筑成，是所有皇家住所中最可爱的，不过女王有时会抱怨，因为百姓总爱探头窥看她在干什么。百姓们实在好奇，把玻璃贴得死死的，所以他们的鼻子大多是塌的。街道有数英里长，弯弯曲曲，两侧铺着亮晶晶的毛线小径。从前鸟儿们会偷毛线筑巢，但后来任命了一名警察守在另一头。

===Original===
One of the great differences between the fairies and us is that they never do anything useful. When the first baby laughed for the first time, his laugh broke into a million pieces, and they all went skipping about. That was the beginning of fairies. They look tremendously busy, you know, as if they had not a moment to spare, but if you were to ask them what they are doing, they could not tell you in the least. They are frightfully ignorant, and everything they do is make-believe. They have a postman, but he never calls except at Christmas with his little box, and though they have beautiful schools, nothing is taught in them; the youngest child being chief person is always elected mistress, and when she has called the roll, they all go out for a walk and never come back. It is a very noticeable thing that, in fairy families, the youngest is always chief person, and usually becomes a prince or princess; and children remember this, and think it must be so among humans also, and that is why they are often made uneasy when they come upon their mother furtively putting new frills on the basinette.

===Chinese===
仙女和我们之间一个很大的不同是：她们从不做任何有用的事。当第一个婴儿第一次笑出声来，他的笑声碎成了百万片，仙女们便四下蹦跳着捡拾起来。那就是仙女的起源。她们看上去忙碌极了，好像片刻不得闲，可你要是问她们在做什么，她们半点也答不上来。她们什么都不懂，做的一切都是假装的。她们有一个邮差，但他除了圣诞节提着小盒子来一趟之外从不登门；虽然她们有漂亮的学校，里面什么也不教；最小的孩子总是当上"主人"，被选为女教师，她点完名之后，大家都出门散步，然后就再也不回来了。有一件事非常明显：在仙女家庭里，最小的总是掌权的那个，通常会成为王子或公主；孩子们记住了这一点，以为人类世界也一定如此，所以当他们撞见母亲偷偷给婴儿床装新褶边时，常常感到不安。

===Original===
You have probably observed that your baby-sister wants to do all sorts of things that your mother and her nurse want her not to do: to stand up at sitting-down time, and to sit down at standing-up time, for instance, or to wake up when she should fall asleep, or to crawl on the floor when she is wearing her best frock, and so on, and perhaps you put this down to naughtiness. But it is not; it simply means that she is doing as she has seen the fairies do; she begins by following their ways, and it takes about two years to get her into the human ways. Her fits of passion, which are awful to behold, and are usually called teething, are no such thing; they are her natural exasperation, because we don't understand her, though she is talking an intelligible language. She is talking fairy. The reason mothers and nurses know what her remarks mean, before other people know, as that "Guch" means "Give it to me at once," while "Wa" is "Why do you wear such a funny hat?" is because, mixing so much with babies, they have picked up a little of the fairy language.

===Chinese===
你大概注意到过，你那小妹妹总想做你妈妈和保姆不想让她做的事：该坐下的时候要站着，该站起来的时候要坐下；该睡觉的时候偏要醒着；穿着最好的裙子偏要在地上爬，诸如此类。也许你觉得这是淘气。其实不是；这仅仅说明她在照着仙女的样子做——她起先学的是仙女的做派，大约要两年才能把她调教成人的习惯。她那些发作起来很吓人的脾气——人们总说是长牙闹的——其实根本不是；那是她自然的恼怒，因为我们不理解她，虽然她说的明明是一种可以听懂的语言。她说的是仙女语。妈妈和保姆之所以比别人更早明白她的话——比如"咕"是"马上给我"，"哇"是"你为什么戴这么滑稽的帽子？"——是因为她们和婴儿相处太久了，不知不觉学到了一点仙女的语言。

===Original===
Of late David has been thinking back hard about the fairy tongue, with his hands clutching his temples, and he has remembered a number of their phrases which I shall tell you some day if I don't forget. He had heard them in the days when he was a thrush, and though I suggested to him that perhaps it is really bird language he is remembering, he says not, for these phrases are about fun and adventures, and the birds talked of nothing but nest-building. He distinctly remembers that the birds used to go from spot to spot like ladies at shopwindows, looking at the different nests and saying, "Not my colour, my dear," and "How would that do with a soft lining?" and "But will it wear?" and "What hideous trimming!" and so on.

===Chinese===
最近大卫一直使劲回想仙女语，双手抱着脑袋，他回忆起不少她们的短语，哪天我忘了就把它们告诉你。那是他还是画眉鸟时听到的。虽然我提醒他，也许他记起的其实是鸟语，但他说不是，因为那些短语讲的都是好玩和冒险，而鸟儿们谈论的无非是筑巢。他清清楚楚地记得，鸟儿们从一个地方逛到另一个地方，就像女士们逛橱窗，端详各式各样的鸟巢，说："颜色不对，亲爱的，""配上软衬里会怎样？""耐不耐磨？""多难看的装饰！"等等。

===Original===
The fairies are exquisite dancers, and that is why one of the first things the baby does is to sign to you to dance to him and then to cry when you do it. They hold their great balls in the open air, in what is called a fairy-ring. For weeks afterward you can see the ring on the grass. It is not there when they begin, but they make it by waltzing round and round. Sometimes you will find mushrooms inside the ring, and these are fairy chairs that the servants have forgotten to clear away. The chairs and the rings are the only telltale marks these little people leave behind them, and they would remove even these were they not so fond of dancing that they toe it till the very moment of the opening of the gates. David and I once found a fairy-ring quite warm.

仙女们是出色的舞者，这就是为什么婴儿最早做的事情之一就是向你比划让你跳舞给他看，而你真的跳了，他反而哭起来。她们在露天举行盛大舞会，舞会的场地叫做仙女环（fairy-ring）。舞会过后好几个星期，你都能在草地上看到那个环。她们开始跳舞时那里还没有环，但她们一圈一圈地跳华尔兹，就跳出了一个环。有时你会在环里发现蘑菇，那是仆人们忘了收走的仙女椅。椅子和舞环是这些小精灵留下的唯一痕迹，要不是她们那么爱跳舞，一直跳到花园大门开启的那一刻，她们甚至连这些痕迹也会抹去。我和 once composedegache. ===Original===
The tablecloth varies according to the seasons, and in May it is made of chestnut-blossom. The ways the fairy-servants do is this: The men, scores of them, climb up the trees and shake the branches, and the blossom falls like snow. Then the lady servants sweep it together by whisking their skirts until it is exactly like a tablecloth, and that is how they get their tablecloth.

===Chinese===
桌布随季节变化，五月里用的是栗子花。仙女仆从们是这样做的：数十名男仆爬上树摇晃树枝，花瓣像雪一样纷纷飘落；然后女仆们用裙摆把花瓣扫拢，直到铺得恰似一张桌布——她们就是这样弄到桌布的。

===Original===
They have real glasses and real wine of three kinds, namely, blackthorn wine, berberris wine, and cowslip wine, and the Queen pours out, but the bottles are so heavy that she just pretends to pour out. There is bread and butter to begin with, of the size of a threepenny bit; and cakes to end with, and they are so small that they have no crumbs. The fairies sit round on mushrooms, and at first they are very well-behaved and always cough off the table, and so on, but after a bit they are not so well-behaved and stick their fingers into the butter, which is got from the roots of old trees, and the really horrid ones crawl over the tablecloth chasing sugar or other delicacies with their tongues. ===Chinese===
