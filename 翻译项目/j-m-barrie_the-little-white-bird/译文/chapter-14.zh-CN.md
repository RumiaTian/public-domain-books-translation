## XIV / 第十四章

===Original===

Peter Pan

If you ask your mother whether she knew about Peter Pan when she was a little girl she will say, "Why, of course, I did, child," and if you ask her whether he rode on a goat in those days she will say, "What a foolish question to ask; certainly he did." Then if you ask your grandmother whether she knew about Peter Pan when she was a girl, she also says, "Why, of course, I did, child," but if you ask her whether he rode on a goat in those days, she says she never heard of his having a goat. Perhaps she has forgotten, just as she sometimes forgets your name and calls you Mildred, which is your mother's name. Still, she could hardly forget such an important thing as the goat. Therefore there was no goat when your grandmother was a little girl. This shows that, in telling the story of Peter Pan, to begin with the goat (as most people do) is as silly as to put on your jacket before your vest.

===Chinese===

彼得·潘（Peter Pan）

如果你问你的妈妈，她小时候是否听说过彼得·潘，她会说："哎呀，当然听说过，孩子。"如果你问她那时候他是不是骑着一只山羊，她会说："问的什么傻问题，他当然骑过。"再如果你问你的奶奶，她小时候是否听说过彼得·潘，她也会说："哎呀，当然听说过，孩子。"可如果你问她那时候他是不是骑着山羊，她会说她从来没听说过他有山羊。也许她忘了，就像她有时忘了你的名字，叫你米尔德丽德（Mildred）——那是你妈妈的名字。不过，山羊这么重要的事她恐怕不会忘。所以你奶奶小时候彼得并没有山羊。这说明讲彼得·潘的故事时，像大多数人那样从山羊讲起，就像先把外套穿在马甲外面一样愚蠢。

===Original===

Of course, it also shows that Peter is ever so old, but he is really always the same age, so that does not matter in the least. His age is one week, and though he was born so long ago he has never had a birthday, nor is there the slightest chance of his ever having one. The reason is that he escaped from being a human when he was seven days' old; he escaped by the window and flew back to the Kensington Gardens.

===Chinese===

当然，这还说明彼得已经非常非常老了，但他实际上永远是同一个年龄，所以这一点也无关紧要。他的年龄是一周。虽然他出生在很久很久以前，却从来没有过过生日，以后也绝无可能过上一次。原因是他刚满七天就逃脱了做人的命运；他从窗户逃走，飞回了肯辛顿花园（Kensington Gardens）。

===Original===

If you think he was the only baby who ever wanted to escape, it shows how completely you have forgotten your own young days. When David heard this story first he was quite certain that he had never tried to escape, but I told him to think back hard, pressing his hands to his temples, and when he had done this hard, and even harder, he distinctly remembered a youthful desire to return to the treetops, and with that memory came others, as that he had lain in bed planning to escape as soon as his mother was asleep, and how she had once caught him halfway up the chimney. All children could have such recollections if they would press their hands hard to their temples, for, having been birds before they were human, they are naturally a little wild during the first few weeks, and very itchy at the shoulders, where their wings used to be. So David tells me.

===Chinese===

如果你以为他是唯一一个想要逃跑的婴儿，那说明你把自己的幼年忘得一干二净了。大卫（David）第一次听到这个故事时，非常肯定自己从未试图逃跑过，但我让他使劲往回想，双手按着太阳穴使劲再使劲，他便清晰地忆起了幼年时渴望飞回树梢的愿望，随之而来的还有其他记忆：他曾躺在床上盘算着等妈妈一睡着就逃跑，以及他有一次被妈妈在烟囱半截截住。所有孩子只要使劲按住太阳穴，都能唤起这样的回忆——因为他们在成为人类之前都曾是鸟，所以在最初几个星期里天性有点野，肩膀处——那里曾经是翅膀所在的位置——会很痒。这是大卫告诉我的。

===Original===

I ought to mention here that the following is our way with a story: First, I tell it to him, and then he tells it to me, the understanding being that it is quite a different story; and then I retell it with his additions, and so we go on until no one could say whether it is more his story or mine. In this story of Peter Pan, for instance, the bald narrative and most of the moral reflections are mine, though not all, for this boy can be a stern moralist, but the interesting bits about the ways and customs of babies in the bird-stage are mostly reminiscences of David's, recalled by pressing his hands to his temples and thinking hard.

===Chinese===

这里我应该说明一下我们讲故事的方式：首先由我讲给他听，然后他再讲给我听——约定俗成地认定这是一个完全不同的故事；接着我用他添加的内容再讲一遍，如此反复，直到谁也说不清这究竟是他的故事还是我的故事了。就拿这个彼得·潘的故事来说，干巴巴的叙述和大部分道德感怀是我的，但也不全是——这孩子有时候可是个严厉的道德家——而那些关于鸟儿阶段婴儿习性的有趣片段，则大多是大卫的回忆，是他双手按着太阳穴使劲回想出来的。

===Original===

Well, Peter Pan got out by the window, which had no bars. Standing on the ledge he could see trees far away, which were doubtless the Kensington Gardens, and the moment he saw them he entirely forgot that he was now a little boy in a nightgown, and away he flew, right over the houses to the Gardens. It is wonderful that he could fly without wings, but the place itched tremendously, and, perhaps we could all fly if we were as dead-confident-sure of our capacity to do it as was bold Peter Pan that evening.

===Chinese===

好了，彼得·潘从一扇没有栏杆的窗户逃了出去。站在窗台上他能看到远处的树木，毫无疑问那就是肯辛顿花园。一看到它们，他立刻忘了自己此刻是一个穿着睡衣的小男孩，径直飞了出去，飞过那些房屋，飞向花园。没有翅膀竟然能飞，这真了不起——但那个地方痒极了。也许，如果我们像那个夜晚大胆的彼得·潘一样，对自己的飞行能力确信不疑，我们都能飞起来。

===Original===

He alighted gaily on the open sward, between the Baby's Palace and the Serpentine, and the first thing he did was to lie on his back and kick. He was quite unaware already that he had ever been human, and thought he was a bird, even in appearance, just the same as in his early days, and when he tried to catch a fly he did not understand that the reason he missed it was because he had attempted to seize it with his hand, which, of course, a bird never does. He saw, however, that it must be past Lockout Time, for there were a good many fairies about, all too busy to notice him; they were getting breakfast ready, milking their cows, drawing water, and so on, and the sight of the water-pails made him thirsty, so he flew over to the Round Pond to have a drink. He stooped, and dipped his beak in the pond; he thought it was his beak, but, of course, it was only his nose, and, therefore, very little water came up, and that not so refreshing as usual, so next he tried a puddle, and he fell flop into it. When a real bird falls in flop, he spreads out his feathers and pecks them dry, but Peter could not remember what was the thing to do, and he decided, rather sulkily, to go to sleep on the weeping beech in the Baby Walk.

===Chinese===

他欢快地落在婴儿宫殿（Baby's Palace）和九曲湖（Serpentine）之间的开阔草坪上，做的第一件事就是仰面朝天，蹬着腿乱踢。他已然完全忘了自己曾经是人，以为自己是一只鸟——甚至在外表上也和早年一模一样。他试图抓一只苍蝇，没抓到却不明白原因是他用了手去抓——鸟儿当然不会这么做。不过他注意到禁园时间（Lockout Time）一定已经过了，因为周围有许多仙子（fairies），全都忙得顾不上注意他：她们在准备早餐，挤牛奶，打水，等等。看到水桶他觉得渴了，便飞到圆池（Round Pond）去喝水。他弯下腰，把嘴喙浸入池中——他以为那是他的嘴喙，其实不过是他的鼻子——所以只吸上来很少一点水，也不像往常那样解渴。于是他又去试一个水坑，结果"扑通"一声栽了进去。真正的鸟掉进水里后会展开羽毛，用嘴把水啄干，但彼得想不起该怎么做。他相当闷闷不乐地决定到婴儿步道（Baby Walk）的垂枝山毛榉上睡觉去。

===Original===

At first he found some difficulty in balancing himself on a branch, but presently he remembered the way, and fell asleep. He awoke long before morning, shivering, and saying to himself, "I never was out in such a cold night;" he had really been out in colder nights when he was a bird, but, of course, as everybody knows, what seems a warm night to a bird is a cold night to a boy in a nightgown. Peter also felt strangely uncomfortable, as if his head was stuffy, he heard loud noises that made him look round sharply, though they were really himself sneezing. There was something he wanted very much, but, though he knew he wanted it, he could not think what it was. What he wanted so much was his mother to blow his nose, but that never struck him, so he decided to appeal to the fairies for enlightenment. They are reputed to know a good deal.

===Chinese===

起初他发现要在树枝上保持平衡有些困难，但很快就记起了诀窍，沉沉睡去。他在天亮前很久就醒了，浑身发抖，对自己说："我从没在这么冷的夜里待过。"其实他当鸟的时候在外面待过更冷的夜晚，但众所周知，对鸟儿来说温暖的夜晚，对一个穿睡衣的小男孩来说就是寒冷的夜晚。彼得还觉得头昏昏沉沉的，很不舒服，听见很大的声响就急忙四下张望——其实那是他自己在打喷嚏。有样东西他非常想要，虽然知道自己想要，却想不出那是什么。他那样迫切想要的，是让妈妈来帮他擤擤鼻子，但这个念头从未闪过他的脑子。于是他决定向仙子们求教——据说她们知道的事很多。

===Original===

There were two of them strolling along the Baby Walk, with their arms round each other's waists, and he hopped down to address them. The fairies have their tiffs with the birds, but they usually give a civil answer to a civil question, and he was quite angry when these two ran away the moment they saw him. Another was lolling on a garden-chair, reading a postage-stamp which some human had let fall, and when he heard Peter's voice he popped in alarm behind a tulip.

===Chinese===

有两个仙子正挽着腰在婴儿步道上散步，他蹦蹦跳跳地下去跟她们搭话。仙子们和鸟儿们有时会闹别扭，但通常对有礼貌的提问都会给个客气的回答，所以当这两个一看到他就跑掉了，他相当生气。还有一个仙子正懒洋洋地靠在花园椅上，读一枚人类遗落的邮票，一听到彼得的声音，便惊慌地躲到一棵郁金香后面。

===Original===

To Peter's bewilderment he discovered that every fairy he met fled from him. A band of workmen, who were sawing down a toadstail, rushed away, leaving their tools behind them. A milkmaid turned her pail upside down and hid in it. Soon the Gardens were in an uproar. Crowds of fairies were running this away and that, asking each other stoutly, who was afraid, lights were extinguished, doors barricaded, and from the grounds of Queen Mab's palace came the rubadub of drums, showing that the royal guard had been called out. A regiment of Lancers came charging down the Broad Walk, armed with holly-leaves, with which they jog the enemy horribly in passing. Peter heard the little people crying everywhere that there was a human in the Gardens after Lockout Time, but he never thought for a moment that he was the human. He was feeling stuffier and stuffier, and more and more wistful to learn what he wanted done to his nose, but he pursued them with the vital question in vain; the timid creatures ran from him, and even the Lancers, when he approached them up the Hump, turned swiftly into a sidewalk, on the pretence that they saw him there.

===Chinese===

令彼得困惑的是，他发现每一个他遇到的仙子都在躲着他。一队工人正在锯一朵蘑菇，看到他立刻丢下工具跑掉了。一个挤奶女工把桶翻过来扣在自己头上藏了进去。很快花园里一片混乱。一群群仙子四处奔逃，一边大声互相质问："谁害怕了？"灯火熄灭，门被堵死，从麦布女王（Queen Mab）的宫殿里传来了"咚咚咚"的鼓声，表明王室卫队已经出动。一队轻骑兵（Lancers）沿着宽道（Broad Walk）冲杀过来，手持冬青叶——这种武器经过时能把敌人扎得够呛。彼得到处听到小仙子们在喊，说禁园时间过后花园里混进了一个人类，但他片刻也没想到那个人类就是他自己。他觉得头越来越昏沉，越来越迫切地想知道有人能帮他处理一下鼻子，但追着他们问这个问题只是徒劳；那些胆小的家伙一见他就跑，甚至那些轻骑兵，当他从驼背山（the Hump）上走近时，也飞快地拐进了人行道，假装在那里看见了他。

===Original===

Despairing of the fairies, he resolved to consult the birds, but now he remembered, as an odd thing, that all the birds on the weeping beech had flown away when he alighted on it, and though that had not troubled him at the time, he saw its meaning now. Every living thing was shunning him. Poor little Peter Pan, he sat down and cried, and even then he did not know that, for a bird, he was sitting on his wrong part. It is a blessing that he did not know, for otherwise he would have lost faith in his power to fly, and the moment you doubt whether you can fly, you cease forever to be able to do it. The reason birds can fly and we can't is simply that they have perfect faith, for to have faith is to have wings.

===Chinese===

向仙子们求助无望，他决定去请教鸟儿们，但这时他想起了一件奇怪的事：他落在垂枝山毛榉上时，树上所有的鸟都飞走了。虽然当时他并不在意，现在却看出了其中的含义。一切活着的东西都在回避他。可怜的小彼得·潘，他坐下来哭了。即便在那时他也不知道，对一只鸟来说，他坐的部位坐反了。不知道反倒是一种福气，因为否则他就会对飞行的能力丧失信心，而一旦你怀疑自己能不能飞，你就永远也飞不起来了。鸟儿能飞而我们不能，原因很简单——它们拥有完美的信念，因为信念就是翅膀。

===Original===

Now, except by flying, no one can reach the island in the Serpentine, for the boats of humans are forbidden to land there, and there are stakes round it, standing up in the water, on each of which a bird-sentinel sits by day and night. It was to the island that Peter now flew to put his strange case before old Solomon Caw, and he alighted on it with relief, much heartened to find himself at last at home, as the birds call the island. All of them were asleep, including the sentinels, except Solomon, who was wide awake on one side, and he listened quietly to Peter's adventures, and then told him their true meaning.

===Chinese===

现在，除了飞过去，没有人能到达九曲湖中的那个岛——人类的船不许靠岸，岛的周围竖着一圈木桩，立在水中，每根桩子上日夜坐着一只鸟哨兵。彼得飞到岛上，要把自己奇怪的遭遇告诉老所罗门·考（Solomon Caw）。他如释重负地落在岛上，终于到了鸟儿们称之为"家"的地方，这让他十分振奋。所有的鸟都睡着了，包括哨兵，只有所罗门一边醒着，他静静地听彼得讲述自己的经历，然后告诉他这一切的真正含义。

===Original===

"Look at your nightgown, if you don't believe me," Solomon said, and with staring eyes Peter looked at his nightgown, and then at the sleeping birds. Not one of them wore anything.

"How many of your toes are thumbs?" said Solomon a little cruelly, and Peter saw to his consternation, that all his toes were fingers. The shock was so great that it drove away his cold.

"Ruffle your feathers," said that grim old Solomon, and Peter tried most desperately hard to ruffle his feathers, but he had none. Then he rose up, quaking, and for the first time since he stood on the window-ledge, he remembered a lady who had been very fond of him.

"I think I shall go back to mother," he said timidly.

"Goodbye," replied Solomon Caw with a queer look.

But Peter hesitated. "Why don't you go?" the old one asked politely.

"I suppose," said Peter huskily, "I suppose I can still fly?"

===Chinese===

"看看你的睡衣，如果你不信我的话，"所罗门说。彼得瞪大眼睛看了看自己的睡衣，又看了看那些睡觉的鸟。没有一只穿着任何东西。

"你的脚趾头有几个是拇指？"所罗门说得有点残忍。彼得惊恐地发现，他的脚趾头全是手指。这一击太沉重了，把他的感冒都吓好了。

"抖抖你的羽毛，"冷酷的老所罗门说。彼得拼命想抖抖羽毛，可他根本没有羽毛。他颤抖着站起来，自从站在窗台上以来，这是他第一次想起了一个曾经非常疼爱他的女士。

"我想我要回去找妈妈，"他怯生生地说。

"再见，"所罗门·考用一种奇怪的表情回答。

但彼得犹豫了。"你怎么还不走？"老人礼貌地问。

"我想，"彼得声音嘶哑地说，"我想我还能飞吧？"

You see, he had lost faith.

"Poor little half-and-half," said Solomon, who was not really hard-hearted, "you will never be able to fly again, not even on windy days. You must live here on the island always."

"And never even go to the Kensington Gardens?" Peter asked tragically.

"How could you get across?" said Solomon. He promised very kindly, however, to teach Peter as many of the bird ways as could be learned by one of such an awkward shape.

"Then I shan't be exactly a human?" Peter asked.

"No."

"Nor exactly a bird?"

"No."

"What shall I be?"

"You will be a Betwixt-and-Between," Solomon said, and certainly he was a wise old fellow, for that is exactly how it turned out.

你瞧，他丧失了信念。

"可怜的小半吊子，"所罗门说——他其实并非铁石心肠——"你再也飞不起来了，就算刮大风的日子也不行。你必须永远住在这座岛上。"

"连肯辛顿花园也不能去了？"彼得悲惨地问。

"你怎么过得去呢？"所罗门说。不过他还是很慈祥地答应，尽量教给彼得一些他这种笨拙体型能学会的鸟儿的本领。

"不是。"

"你将是一个'亦此亦彼'（Betwixt-and-Between），"所罗门说。他确实是个睿智的老人，因为事情后来正是如此。

===Original===

The birds on the island never got used to him. His oddities tickled them every day, as if they were quite new, though it was really the birds that were new. They came out of the eggs daily, and laughed at him at once, then off they soon flew to be humans, and other birds came out of other eggs, and so it went on forever. The crafty mother-birds, when they tired of sitting on their eggs, used to get the young one to break their shells a day before the right time by whispering to them that now was their chance to see Peter washing or drinking or eating. Thousands gathered round him daily to watch him do these things, just as you watch the peacocks, and they screamed with delight when he lifted the crusts they flung him with his hands instead of in the usual way with the mouth. All his food was brought to him from the Gardens at Solomon's orders by the birds. He would not eat worms or insects (which they thought very silly of him), so they brought him bread in their beaks. Thus, when you cry out, "Greedy! Greedy!" to the bird that flies away with the big crust, you know now that you ought not to do this, for he is very likely taking it to Peter Pan.

岛上的鸟儿们始终没习惯他的存在。他的种种怪癖每天都能把她们逗乐，仿佛都是新鲜事，尽管新鲜的其实是那些鸟。她们每天从蛋里孵出来，立刻嘲笑他一番，然后很快飞走去做人，另一些鸟从另一些蛋里孵出来，如此循环往复，永无止境。狡猾的鸟妈妈们孵蛋孵烦了，就凑到鸟宝宝耳边悄悄说，现在是去看彼得洗漱或吃东西的好机会，引诱他们在该出壳的日期之前就啄破蛋壳。每天有成千上万的鸟围在他身边看他做事，就像你们看孔雀一样。当他用嘴——不，用手——拿起她们扔给他的面包皮时，她们就高兴得尖叫起来。他所有的食物都由鸟儿们按所罗门的吩咐从花园里带来。他不肯吃虫子和昆虫（她们觉得他很傻），所以她们就用嘴衔来面包给他。因此，当你对着那只衔着大块面包皮飞走的鸟喊"贪吃鬼！贪吃鬼！"的时候，你现在知道你不该这么做了，因为他很可能是在把面包送给彼得·潘。

===Original===

Peter wore no nightgown now. You see, the birds were always begging him for bits of it to line their nests with, and, being very good-natured, he could not refuse, so by Solomon's advice he had hidden what was left of it. But, though he was now quite naked, you must not think that he was cold or unhappy. He was usually very happy and gay, and the reason was that Solomon had kept his promise and taught him many of the bird ways. To be easily pleased, for instance, and always to be really doing something, and to think that whatever he was doing was a thing of vast importance. Peter became very clever at helping the birds to build their nests; soon he could build better than a wood-pigeon, and nearly as well as a blackbird, though never did he satisfy the finches, and he made nice little water-troughs near the nests and dug up worms for the young ones with his fingers. He also became very learned in bird-lore, and knew an east-wind from a west-wind by its smell, and he could see the grass growing and hear the insects walking about inside the tree-trunks. But the best thing Solomon had done was to teach him to have a glad heart.
