## I / 第一章

===Original===
It was, I believe, the evening of the eighteenth when we saw Saragossa in the distance. As we entered by the Puerta de Sancho we heard the clock in the Torre Nueva strike ten. We were in an extremely pitiful condition as to food and clothing. The long journey we had made from Lerma through Salas de los Infantes, Cervera, Ágreda, Tarazona, and Borja, climbing mountains, fording rivers, making shortcuts until we arrived at the high road of Gallur and Alagón, had left us quite used up, worn out, and ill with fatigue. In spite of all, the joy of being free sweetened our pain.

We were four who had succeeded in escaping between Lerma and Cogollos by freeing our innocent hands from the rope that bound together so many patriots. On the day of the escape, we could count among the four of us a total capital of eleven reales; but after three days of marching, when we entered the metropolis of Aragón and balanced our mutual cash, our common wealth was found to be a sum total of thirty-one *cuartos*. We bought some bread at a little place next the Orphanage, and divided it among us.

Don Roque, who was one of the members of our expedition, had good connections in Saragossa, but this was not an hour to present ourselves to anyone. We postponed until the next day this matter of looking up friends; and as we could not go to an inn, we wandered about the city, looking for a shelter where we could pass the night. The market scarcely seemed to offer exactly the comfort and quiet which our tired bodies needed. We visited the leaning tower, and although one of my companions suggested that we should take refuge in the plaza, I thought that we should be quite the same as if altogether in the open country. The place served us, none the less, for temporary refuge and rest, and also as a refectory, where we despatched happily our supper of dry bread, glancing now and then at the great upright mass of the tower, whose inclination made it seem like a giant leaning to see who was running about his feet. By the light of the moon that brick sentinel projected against the sky its huddled and shapeless form, unable to hold itself erect. The clouds were drifting across its top, and the spectator looking from below trembled with dread, imagining that the clouds were quiet and that the tower was moving down upon him. This grotesque structure, under whose feet the overburdened soil has settled, seems to be forever falling, yet never falls.

我想，那已是十八日的傍晚，我们远远望见了萨拉戈萨（Saragossa）。从圣乔门（Puerta de Sancho）入城时，听见新塔（Torre Nueva）上的钟敲了十下。我们衣食两缺，境况凄惨至极。从莱尔马取道萨拉斯-德洛斯因方特斯、塞维拉、阿格雷达、塔拉索纳、博尔哈，一路翻山越岭、涉水渡河、抄走捷径，直到踏上加柳尔至阿拉贡（Alagón）的大道，这趟漫长的旅程已把我们拖得筋疲力尽、形容枯槁、积劳成疾。尽管如此，重获自由的欢愉仍为我们冲淡了苦楚。

我们一行四人，是在莱尔马与科戈略斯（Cogollos）之间逃出来的，把我们清白无辜的双手从那根捆缚着众多爱国志士的绳索里解脱了出来。逃出那天，我们四人清点全部家当，共计十一里亚尔（reales）；可是徒步行军三日之后，待到进入阿拉贡（Aragón）的首府、把各人的现钱归拢合账，我们共同的家底拢共只剩三十一个夸阿托（cuartos）了。我们在孤儿院隔壁的一家小铺买了些面包，四人均分了。

唐·罗克（Don Roque）是我们这支逃亡队伍里的一员，在萨拉戈萨颇有些熟人故旧，但眼下这个时辰，实在不宜登门求见任何人。寻访亲友之事，只好推迟到次日；我们又住不起客栈，便在城里四处游荡，寻觅一处过夜的栖身之地。市场那地方，似乎断然给不了我们疲惫的身躯所急需的安逸与清静。我们逛到那座斜塔跟前，虽然有一个同伴提议到广场上落脚，我却觉得那同露宿荒郊野外没什么两样。不过这地方倒也够我们暂避一时、歇息片刻，还兼作饭堂，我们在那里快快活活地吃了一顿干面包当晚餐，不时抬眼望一望塔身那巍然矗立的庞然大物——它倾侧的姿势，活像一个巨人俯下身来，要看是谁在他脚边跑来跑去。月光下，这座砖砌的哨兵把蜷曲无形的一团影子投上天幕，怎么也直不起身来。云朵从塔顶飘掠而过，从底下仰望的人吓得发颤，恍惚只当云是静止的，倒是那塔正朝自己压将下来。这座奇形怪状的建筑，脚下不堪重负的泥土已然沉降，仿佛永远正在倾倒，却又永远倒不下来。

We passed through the avenue of the Coso again from this house of giants as far as the Seminary. We went through two streets, the Calle Quemada and the Calle del Rincón, both in ruins, as far as the little plaza of San Miguel. From here, passing from alley to alley, and blindly crossing narrow and irregular streets, we found ourselves beside the ruins of the monastery of Santa Engracia, which was blown up by the French at the raising of the first siege. The four of us exclaimed at once in a way to show that we all thought the same thing. Here we had found a shelter, and in some cosy corner under this roof we would pass the night!

The front wall was still standing with its arch of marble, decorated with innumerable figures of saints which seemed undisturbed and tranquil as if they knew nothing of the catastrophe. In the interior we saw broken arches and enormous columns struggling erect from the debris, presenting themselves, darkling and deformed, against the clear light flooding the enclosure, looking like fantastic creatures generated by a delirious imagination. We could see decorations, cornices, spaces, labyrinths, caverns, and a thousand other fanciful architectural designs produced by the ruins in their falling. There were even small rooms opened in the spaces of the walls with an art like that of Nature in forming grottos. The fragments of the altarpiece that had rotted because of the humidity showed through the remains of the vaulting where still hung the chains which had suspended the lamps. Early grasses grew between the cracks of the wood and stone. Among all this destruction there were certain things wholly intact, as some of the pipes of the organ and the grating of the confessional. The roof was one with the floor, and the tower mingled its fragments with those of the tombs below. When we looked upon such a conglomeration of tombs, such a myriad of fragments that had fallen without losing entirely their original form, and such masses of bricks and plaster crumbled like things made of sugar, we could almost believe that the ruins of the building had not yet settled into their final position. The shapeless structure appeared to be palpitating yet from the shock of the explosion.

Don Roque told us that beneath this church there was another one where they worshipped the relics of the holy martyrs of Saragossa; but the entrance to this subterranean sanctuary was closed up. Profound silence reigned, but, penetrating further, we heard human voices proceeding from those mysterious deeps. The first impression produced upon us by hearing these voices was as if the spirits of the famous chroniclers who wrote of the Christian martyrs, and of the patriots sleeping in dust below, were crying out upon us for disturbing their slumbers.

我们从这巨人之家再度穿过科索大街（Coso）的林荫道，一直走到神学院；又穿过克马达街（Calle Quemada）与林孔街（Calle del Rincón）两条街——两处都已沦为废墟——来到圣米格尔（San Miguel）小广场。由此再钻进一条条小巷，七拐八绕、深一脚浅一脚地穿过那些狭窄歪斜的街巷，我们就到了圣恩格拉西亚修道院（Santa Engracia）的废墟旁；这修道院是在第一次围城解除之际被法军炸毁的。我们四人齐声叫了起来，那口气表明大家想到一处去了：过夜的地方这不就寻着了嘛！就在这残顶之下觅个安逸角落，将就过上一夜！

正面那堵墙还立着，连着那道大理石门拱，拱上雕着数不清的圣徒像，个个安然恬静，仿佛对这场浩劫一无所知。往里面看，但见断裂的拱券和硕大的石柱从瓦砾堆中挣扎着挺立起来，衬着泻满这方废园的明亮天光，显得幽暗而扭曲，宛如谵妄的想象孕育出的种种怪物。还辨认得出种种雕饰、檐口、空穴、迷径、洞窟，以及废墟倾颓之际造出的千百种奇思妙想的建筑格局。墙身的空隙间甚至辟出了一个个小间，其手笔之巧，直可同大自然凿造洞窟的造化相比。祭坛后屏因潮气而朽坏的残片，从穹顶的残余间露出来，那里还悬着当年吊挂灯盏的铁链。新生的嫩草从木头和石头的缝隙间钻出来。在这一片毁败之中，竟有些东西完好无损，比如管风琴的几根音管，还有告解室的格栅。屋顶与地面已合为一体，塔楼的碎块同底下坟墓的残石混作一处。眼见这般坟冢的堆叠、这般坠落却大致未失原形的无数残片、这般酥脆得如同糖塑的砖块灰泥，我们几乎要相信，这栋建筑的废墟还没有落定到它最终的位置。这无形体的庞然巨构，仿佛仍在爆炸的震悸中颤动不息。

唐·罗克告诉我们，这座教堂底下还有一座教堂，供奉着萨拉戈萨诸位圣殉道者的圣髑；只是这座地下圣所的入口已被封堵。四下里万籁俱寂，但再往深处走，我们却听见人声从那神秘的幽深之处传来。乍闻这些人声，我们的头一个感觉是：仿佛那些撰写基督徒殉道者事迹的著名编年史家的英灵，连同长眠于下方尘土中的爱国志士，正在厉声呵斥我们搅扰了他们的沉眠。

On the instant, in the glare of a flame which illuminated part of the scene, we distinguished a group of persons sheltering themselves, huddling together in a space between two of the fallen columns. They were Saragossa beggars, who had made a palatial shelter for themselves in that place, seeking protection from the rain with beams of wood and with their rags. We also made ourselves as comfortable as might be in another place, and covering ourselves with a blanket and a half, prepared to go to sleep.

Don Roque said to me, “I know Don José de Montoria, one of the richest citizens of Saragossa. We were both born in Mequinenza. We went to school together, and we played our games together on the hills of Corregidor. It is thirty years since I have seen him, but I believe that he will receive us well. Like every good Aragonese, he is all heart. We will find him, fellows; we will see Don José de Montoria. I am of his blood on the maternal side. We will say﻿—”

But Don Roque was asleep, and I also slept.

===Chinese===
顷刻间，一蓬火光亮起，照亮了眼前的一角，我们这才辨出一群人正蜷作一团，在两根倒伏的石柱之间栖身。原来是萨拉戈萨的乞丐们，他们用木梁和身上的破衣烂衫遮挡雨水，在那里给自己营造了一座宫殿般的栖身之所。我们也在另一处各自安顿下来，能多舒服便多舒服，身上盖着一条半毯子，准备入睡。

唐·罗克对我说：“我认识唐·何塞·德·蒙特里亚（Don José de Montoria），萨拉戈萨数得着的富户之一。我们俩都是梅基嫩萨（Mequinenza）生人，一同上的学，一同在科雷希多尔山丘上玩耍。我有三十年没见他了，不过我相信他会好好接待我们。同所有地道的阿拉贡人一样，他是个热心肠。我们会找到他的，伙伴们；我们会见到唐·何塞·德·蒙特里亚的。论母系，我与他还有血缘之亲。我们要登门求见，就说——”

可是唐·罗克已经睡着了，我也睡着了。
