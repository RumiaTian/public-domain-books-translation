## XI / 第十一章

===Original===
The fortress of San José had surrendered, or rather the French had entered it when their artillery had reduced it to powder, and all of its defenders had fallen, one by one, to lie among its fragments. The Imperial soldiers, on entering, found heaps of bodies and stones matted together with blood. They could not establish themselves there because they were flanked by the batteries of Los Mártires and the Botanical Garden, so they continued operations by mining, in order to possess themselves of those two points. The fortifications which we held were so nearly destroyed that a general agreement was urgent, and the terrible orders, calling upon all the inhabitants of Saragossa to work in renewing them. The proclamation said that every citizen should carry a gun in one hand and spade in the other.

The twelfth and thirteenth were without rest, the fire diminishing a little because the besiegers, warned by sad experience, did not wish to risk any more hand-to-hand conflicts. Understanding that theirs was a work of patience and skill, rather than of boldness and bravery, they opened slowly, and with security, roads and mines which should lead to the possession of the redoubt without loss of men. It was almost necessary to build our walls anew, or rather to substitute sacks of earth for them, an operation in which many friars, canons, civil officials, children, and women were occupied. The artillery was almost useless, the fosse about filled up, and it was necessary to continue the defence at short range. And so we wore through the thirteenth, protecting the works as we rebuilt them, suffering much, and seeing ourselves constantly decreasing in numbers, although new men came to take the places of the many that we lost. On the fourteenth the enemy’s artillery tried to demolish our new walls, opening breaches for us on the front and at the sides. They did not dare to try a new assault, contenting themselves with opening a mine in such a direction that we could not in any way cover it with our fire, nor with that of any battery near by.

Our valorous tantalizing earthworks would soon be covered by the fires of the French batteries, which were hurling to the four winds the earth of which they were formed. In this situation, surrender was inevitable sooner or later. Indeed we were at the mercy of the French arms as a ship at the mercy of the waves of the ocean. Flanked by roads and zigzags, through which a strong and clever enemy might walk without danger, protected by all the resources of science, our bulwarks of defence were like one man surrounded by an army. We had no serviceable cannons, nor could we bring other new ones, because the walls would not have borne them. Our only resource was to keep watch of the redoubt in order to fly from it at the moment when the French should enter and destroy the bridge, in order to prevent them from following us. This was done; and on the night of the fourteenth they worked without rest on the mine, and we placed small mines at the bridge, hoping that the following day the enemy would try to mount by that wall. But this did not happen. Not daring to make another assault without all the precautions and security possible, they continued their work of digging very nearly up to our fosse. In this labor our indefatigable fusileers did them little damage. We were desperate, but without power to do anything. Our desperation was of no avail; it was a useless force, like the rage of a lunatic in his cage.

圣何塞要塞算是投降了——或者不如说，是等法军的炮火把它轰成了齑粉，守军一个接一个全都倒下、长眠在断石碎砖之间之后，法国人才进得去。帝国士兵开进去，只见成堆的尸骸与砖石血肉黏连，凝作一处。他们在那里站不住脚，因为洛斯·马尔蒂雷斯炮台和植物园炮台从两翼夹制着他们，于是便改用坑道作业继续进攻，要把这两处据点也夺到手里。我们据守的工事已被摧毁得所剩无几，全城协力已刻不容缓，于是颁下了那道声色俱厉的命令，号召萨拉戈萨全体居民出工翻修。文告说：每个市民应当一手持枪，一手执铲。

十二日和十三日两天没有一刻安歇；炮火稍见稀疏，因为围城的敌军被惨痛的经验教训过，不愿再拿人命去冒险近身肉搏。他们明白这差使靠的是耐心与技艺，而非大胆与剽勇，便不慌不忙、稳稳当当地开挖通路与坑道，指望兵不血刃便拿下棱堡。我们的城墙几乎等于要重新造过——说得确切些，是拿一袋袋土来顶替城墙；许多修士、座堂神父、文职官员、孩童和妇女都投入了这项劳作。炮兵几乎派不上用场，护城壕也填得差不多了，只能以近距离继续防守。十三日就这样熬了过去：我们一边重修工事一边加以掩护，吃足了苦头，眼见人数不断减少，尽管不断有生力军前来，补上我们折损的那些人的空缺。十四日，敌人的炮兵力图摧毁我们的新墙，在正面和两侧替我们“开”出了一个个缺口。他们不敢再尝试新的强攻，只满足于朝这样一个方向开掘坑道：我们无论用自己的火力、还是用左近任何一座炮台的火力，都无法把它压制。

我们那些英勇而又爱撩逗敌人的土垒，很快就要被法军各炮台的火力罩住了——人家正把构成这些工事的泥土轰得四散纷飞。处在这种情势之下，把棱堡拱手让出只是迟早的事。我们确实已任凭法兰西的武力摆布，就像一艘船任凭大洋的波涛摆布。大路和之字形堑壕从两翼包抄过来，强壮而精明的敌人尽可以毫发无伤地从中穿行，又有科学的一切手段护体；我们的防御壁垒，就像一个被整支军队围住的孤身汉子。我们一门堪用的大炮也没有，新的也运不上来，因为这些土墙吃不住。我们唯一的办法就是盯住棱堡，只等法军一冲进来便立刻脱身撤离，并把大桥炸毁，好教他们追不上我们。事情就这么办了：十四日夜间，他们不歇手地赶挖坑道，我们则在桥头埋下了小地雷，指望敌人第二天会企图从那道墙攀上来。可这一幕没有上演。他们不敢在未把一切防备和安全保障做到万全之前再来一次强攻，只顾把掘进的工程继续推进，几乎一直挖到了我们壕沟边上。在这场劳作里，我们那些不知疲倦的燧发枪手没能给他们多大的杀伤。我们急得发狂，却无能为力。我们的绝望于事无补；那是一股白费的力气，就像疯子在笼子里的狂怒。

We drew out the nails from the tablet which proclaimed ours to be the unconquerable redoubt, in order to take away with us that witness of our justifiable arrogance. At nightfall the fortification was abandoned, only forty remaining to keep it until the end, and shoot all they could, as our captain said that no chance might be lost to lose the enemy a couple of men. From the Torre del Pino we saw the retreat of the forty at about eight o’clock in the evening, after they had met the invaders with bayonet-thrusts; they retreated fighting bravely.

The interior mine of the redoubt had had little effect, but the small mines of the bridge acquitted themselves so well that the passage was destroyed and the redoubt isolated from the other bank of the Huerva. Gaining this position and San José, the French would have enough protection to open their third parallel and to demolish at their leisure the whole circuit of the city. We were saddened and just a little discouraged; but of what importance is a little depression when on the day following one has a diversion and a feast? After being madly discouraged, a little jollity does not come amiss, especially when time is wanting to bury the dead; nor was there room in the houses for the many who were wounded. It is true that there were hands for all that had to be done, thanks be to God.

The reason for the general rejoicing was that glorious rumors were in circulation of Spanish armies that were coming to succor us, on the heels of the French, in many parts of the Peninsula. The people crowded into the Plaza de la Seo, and in front of the Magdalene arch, waiting until the *Gazette* should appear; and at last it came out, cheering everybody’s spirits, and making all hearts palpitate with hope. I do not know if such rumors had really reached Saragossa, or if they originated in the wits of the chief editor, Don Ignacio Assor. It is certain that they told us in print that Reading was coming to succor us with an army of sixty thousand men, that the Marquis of Lazán, after routing the mob in the north of Catalonia, had entered France, spreading terror in every direction, and that also the Duke del Infantado was coming to our aid, who with Blake and la Romana had routed Napoleon, slaying twenty thousand men, including Berthier, Ney, and Savary, and that at Cádiz had arrived several millions in hard cash sent by the English for the expense of war. What did it all mean? Could the *Gazette* explain all this?

我们把那块宣告我们的棱堡“不可征服”的牌子连钉子一齐拔了下来，好把这件物证随身带走——它见证的是我们那份情有可原的狂傲。日暮时分，工事弃守，只留下四十人守到最后一刻、能放多少枪就放多少枪——用我们队长的话说，凡是能叫敌人折损一两个人的机会，一个也不许放过。晚上八点钟左右，我们从皮诺塔上望见那四十人撤退下来：他们先用刺刀迎了迎入侵者，然后一路苦战，英勇地退了下来。

棱堡内部的地雷没有起多大作用，大桥上的小地雷倒是不辱使命：通道被炸毁，棱堡与韦尔瓦河对岸隔绝开来。法军一旦把这个阵地连同圣何塞一并到手，便有了足够的掩护来开辟第三道平行壕，从容不迫地把全城一圈城墙逐一轰平。我们心里悲伤，也有点儿泄气；可是第二天就有热闹可看、有庆典可过，一点点意气消沉又算得了什么？疯也似的沮丧过一场之后，来点儿欢乐倒也不坏，尤其是在连掩埋死人的工夫都腾不出来、房子里也容不下那许多伤员的时候。千真万确，凡是该做的事都有的是人手——感谢上帝。

举城欢腾的缘由是：种种光荣的传闻正不胫而走，说西班牙的各路大军踩着法军的脚后跟，正从半岛各地赶来救援我们。百姓们拥进塞奥广场，聚在拉马格达莱纳拱门（Magdalene arch）前，一心等候《公报》出版；《公报》终于出来了，人人精神为之一振，颗颗心都让希望激得怦怦直跳。这些传闻究竟是当真传到了萨拉戈萨，还是出自主编唐·伊格纳西奥·阿索尔（Don Ignacio Assor）那颗灵光的脑袋，我不得而知。可以肯定的是，白纸黑字告诉了我们：雷丁（Reading）正率六万大军前来解围；拉桑侯爵（Marquis of Lazán）在加泰罗尼亚北部击溃匪兵之后，已经攻入法国，正向四面八方播散恐怖；因凡塔多公爵（Duke del Infantado）也在赶来相助——他与布莱克（Blake）、拉罗曼纳（la Romana）合力，已经把拿破仑打得大败，斩敌两万，贝尔蒂埃（Berthier）、内伊（Ney）、萨瓦里（Savary）一个不剩尽数毙命；又有英国人运抵加的斯（Cádiz）的数百万现银，专充战费。这一切到底算怎么回事？《公报》能把这一切统统自圆其说吗？

In spite of the size of these mouthfuls of rumor, we swallowed them; and there were demonstrations of joy, ringing of church bells, running through the streets, and singing the music of the jota, with many other patriotic excesses, which at least had the advantage of affording us a little of that cooling off of our mental temperature which was necessary. Do not believe that in consideration of our joy the rain of bombs had ceased! Very far from that! They seemed to jeer at the news of our *Gazette*, as they repeated their dose. Feeling a lively desire to laugh at them to their faces, we went to the walls. The musicians of the regiments played in a tantalizing fashion, and we all sang in an immense chorus the famous words﻿—

> The Virgin del Pilar says
 

 She wouldn’t like to be a Frenchwoman!

They were in a mood for answering jests, and in less than two hours a greater number of projectiles were sent into the city than during all the rest of the day. There was now no longer a secure refuge; there was not a hand’s breadth of ground or of roof free from that Satanic fire. Families fled from their homes, or took refuge in the cellars. The wounded, who were numerous in the principal houses, were carried to the churches, seeking shelter in their strong vaults. Others went dragging themselves along. Some more active ones carried their bedding upon their shoulders. Most of them were accommodated in the Pilar; and after the floor was all filled, they were stretched out upon the altars and crowded into the chapels. In spite of their misfortunes, they were consoled by looking at the Virgin, who seemed to say to them unceasingly with her brilliant eyes that she would not care to be a Frenchwoman!

> 皮拉尔圣母说道

 她可不想当法国女人！

他们倒也有心应答这场玩笑：不到两个钟头，打进城里的炮弹，比那一天其余时候的总数还要多。城里从此再没有一处可靠的避难之所了；一掌宽的地面、一掌宽的屋顶，都躲不开那撒旦般的炮火。各家各户纷纷逃出家门，或者躲进地窖。各主要宅邸里的大批伤员被抬往各处教堂，到坚固的穹顶底下去寻求庇护。另一些人则自己拖着伤躯一路挪过去；手脚利索些的，把铺盖扛在肩头。他们大都被安顿在皮拉尔圣母教堂里；地面躺满之后，人们便躺上了祭坛，挤进了一间间小堂。尽管遭逢种种不幸，他们望着圣母便得了安慰——圣母仿佛正用她那双明亮的眼睛，不住地对他们说：她可不愿意当法国女人！
