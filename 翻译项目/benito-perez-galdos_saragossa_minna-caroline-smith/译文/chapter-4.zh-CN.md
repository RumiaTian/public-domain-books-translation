## IV / 第四章

===Original===
Before we left his house, Montoria became vexed at Don Roque and me because we would not take the money that he offered us for our first expenses in the city; then were repeated the blows on the table, and the rains of “*porras*” and other words that I will not repeat. But at last we arrived at an arrangement honorable for both parties.

And now I begin to think I am saying too much about this singular man before I describe his personality. Don José was a man of about sixty years of age, strong, high-colored, of overflowing health, well placed in the world, contented with himself, fulfilling his destiny with a quiet conscience. His was an excess of patriotic virtues and of exemplary customs, if there can be an excess of such things. He was lacking in education, that is to say, in the finer and more distinguished training which in that time some of the sons of such families as his were beginning to receive. Don José was not acquainted with the superficialities of etiquette, and by character and custom was opposed to the amenities and the white lies which are a part of the foundations of courtesy. As he always wore his heart upon his sleeve, he wished everybody to do the same, and his savage goodness tolerated none of the frequent evasions of polite conversation. In angry moments he was impetuous, and let himself be carried to violent extremes, of which he always repented later. He never dissimulated, and had the great Christian virtues in a crude form and without polish, like a massive piece of the most beautiful marble where the chisel has traced no lines. It was necessary to know him to understand him, making allowances for his eccentricities, although to be sure he should scarcely be called eccentric, when he was so much like the majority of the men of his province.

His aim was never to hide what he felt; and if this occasionally caused him some trouble in the course of life in regard to questions of little moment, it was a quality which always proved an inestimable treasure in any grave matter, because, with his soul wholly on view, it was impossible to suspect any malice or any double dealing whatever. He readily pardoned offenders, obliged those who sought favors, and gave a large part of his numerous goods to those in need. He dressed neatly, ate abundantly, fasted with much scrupulousness during Lent, and loved the Virgin del Pilar with a fanatical sort of family affection.

还没等我们离开他家，蒙特里亚就跟唐·罗克和我怄上了气，因为我们不肯收下他掏给我们作进城后头一笔开销的钱；于是拍桌子声再度大作，“*波拉*”如骤雨般劈头盖脸，还夹着别的字眼，我就不复述了。不过到最后，我们总算达成了一项保全双方面子的安排。

写到这里我才发觉，还没描画这位奇人的品貌，我已经把他讲得太多了。唐·何塞年约六十，身子骨结实，面色红润，健康之气几乎要溢出体外；家道殷实，于世安稳，对自己心满意足，怀着一颗安宁的良心践行着自己的天命。他的爱国德性与模范操行多得近乎过剩——倘若这类东西也会“过剩”的话。他欠缺教育，确切地说，欠缺那种更精细、更上等的陶冶；在那个年头，像他这样人家的子弟，有的已经开始受这种陶冶了。唐·何塞不通礼节上的种种虚文，而且无论禀性还是习惯，都同社交场里的那些体面客套势不两立——须知那些温婉的应酬、无伤大雅的场面谎，本是人间礼数的根基之一。他自己素来把心挂在袖口上，也巴不得人人都这样，于是他那股子未加驯化的善良，便容不得文雅交谈里司空见惯的种种躲闪迂回。动起怒来他性如烈火，任着性子冲到暴烈的极端，事后却总是悔恨。他从不作伪，一身基督徒的大德还是粗坯，未经打磨，好比一块最华美的大理石，凿子尚未在上面划过一道线。要懂他，须先结识他，对他的乖僻多加担待；不过说句实话，他也实在算不上乖僻，因为他同本省大多数男人原本就是一个模样。

他立身处世，从不刻意掩藏自己的感受；这在日常无关紧要的小事上偶尔给他招来些麻烦，可一逢重大关头，这份品性却总是无价之宝——因为他的灵魂整个儿摆在明处，任谁也无法疑心他怀有什么恶意、耍什么两面手腕。冒犯他的人，他轻易就宽恕；有求于他的人，他慨然相助；偌大家私的一大部分，他都散给了穷乏之人。他衣着整洁，食量豪宏，大斋期斋戒得一丝不苟，对皮拉尔圣母更怀着一近乎狂热的、家人般的挚爱。

His language was not, as we have shown, a model of elegance, and he himself confessed, as the greatest of his defects, the habit of saying *porra* every minute, and again, *porra*! without the slightest necessity. But more than once I heard him say, knowing his fault, he had not been able to correct it, for the *porras* came out of his mouth without his knowing it.

Don José had a wife and three children. She was Doña Leocadia Sarriera, by birth a Navarraise. The eldest son and the daughter were married, and had given grandchildren to the old man. The younger son was called Augustine, and was destined for the church, like his uncle of the same name, the Archdeacon of La Seo. I made the acquaintance of all these on the same day, and found them the best people in the world. I was treated with so much kindness that I was overwhelmed by their generosity. If they had known me since my birth, they could not have been more cordial. Their kindness, springing spontaneously from their generous hearts, touched my very soul; and as I have always had a faculty for letting people love me, I responded from the first with a very sincere affection.

“Señor Don Roque,” I said that night to my friend as we were going to bed in the room which was given us, “I have never seen people like these. Is everybody in Aragón like this?”

“There are all kinds,” he answered; “but men made of stuff like Don José and his family are plentiful in this land of Aragón.”

前文已经说过，他的言谈算不得典雅的典范；他自己也供认不讳：他最大的毛病，就是每隔一分钟便要骂一声 *波拉*，跟着又是一声 *波拉*！全无半点必要。可我不止一次听他亲口说，他明知这个毛病，却怎么也改不掉，因为那些 *波拉* 总是不知不觉便从他嘴里溜了出来。

唐·何塞有一位太太、三个子女。太太是堂娜·莱奥卡迪娅·萨列拉（Doña Leocadia Sarriera），本是纳瓦拉（Navarre）人。长子与女儿都已成婚，给老人添了孙辈。次子名叫奥古斯丁，注定要献身教会，与那位同他同名同姓的叔父——塞奥的副主教——走的是同一条路。这一家人，我都在同一天里结识了，而且发现他们是天底下顶好的人。他们待我那样恩厚，那份慷慨简直压得我喘不过气。哪怕他们从我落生那天就认识我，也不过如此热诚了。他们的好意发乎慷慨的天性，自然流露，一直沁到我魂灵深处；而我素来便有一份招人疼爱的本事，也就从第一天起报以一片至诚。

当天夜里，在我们分到的那间屋子里准备就寝时，我对朋友说：“唐·罗克先生，我从没见过这样的人家。阿拉贡人人都是这般模样吗？”

“什么样的人都有，”他答道，“不过像唐·何塞和他一家人这种材质的人，在阿拉贡这片土地上可多得很。”

===Original===
Next day we occupied ourselves with my enlistment. The spirit of the men who were enlisting filled me with such enthusiasm that nothing seemed to me so noble as to follow glory, even afar off. Everybody knows that in those days Saragossa and the Saragossans had obtained a fabulous renown, that their heroism stimulated the imagination. Everything referring to the famous siege of the immortal city partook on the lips of narrators of the proportions and colors of the heroic age. With distance, the actions of the Saragossans acquired great dimensions. In England and Germany, where they were considered the Numantines of modern times, those half-naked peasants, with rope sandals on their feet and the bright Saragossan kerchief on their heads, became like figures of mythology.

“Surrender, and we will give you clothes,” said the French in the first siege, admiring the constancy of a few poor countrymen dressed in rags.

“*We do not know how to surrender*,” they made answer; “*and our bodies shall be clothed with glory.*”

The fame of this and other phrases has gone round the world.

===Chinese===
第二天，我们便张罗起我投军的事。应征入伍的人们那股子热气，把我烧得满腔激情，只觉得天底下再没有比追随荣耀——哪怕远远地追随——更高尚的事了。谁都知道，那些日子里，萨拉戈萨和萨拉戈萨人已经赢得了神话般的声名，他们的英雄事迹激荡着人们的想象。凡与这座不朽之城那场著名围城相关的一切，自讲述者口中道出，都带上了英雄时代的规模与色彩。距离一远，萨拉戈萨人的壮举便愈发显得宏大。在英国和德意志，人们把他们视为当代的努曼西亚人（Numantines，古西班牙抗罗马、死守孤城的英雄）；那些半裸着身子的庄稼汉，脚上蹬着麻绳凉鞋，头上扎着鲜艳的萨拉戈萨头巾，俨然已化作神话里的人物。

第一次围城时，法军眼见几个衣衫褴褛的贫苦乡民这般坚贞，不胜钦佩，便喊话说：“投降吧，我们给你们衣裳穿。”

他们回答道：“我们不懂怎样投降；我们的躯体，将以荣耀为衣裳。”

这一句以及另外几句豪言的名声，早已传遍全世界。

But let us go back to my enlistment. There was an obstacle in the way, Palafox’s manifesto of the thirteenth of December, in which he ordered the expulsion of all strangers within a period of twenty-four hours. This measure was taken on account of the numbers of people who made trouble, and stirred up discord and disorder; but just at the time of my arrival another order was given out, calling for all the scattered soldiers of the Army of the Centre which had been dispersed at Tudela, and so I found a chance to enlist. Although I did not belong to that army, I had taken a place in the defence of Madrid and the battle of Bailén. These were reasons which, with the help of my protector Montoria, served me in entering the Saragossan forces. They gave me a place in the battalion of volunteers of the Peñas of San Pedro, which had been badly weakened in the first siege, and I received a uniform and a gun. I did not enter the lines, as my protector had said, in the company of the clergyman of Santiago Sas, because this valiant company was composed exclusively of residents of the parish of San Pablo. They did not want any young men in their battalion; for this reason Augustine Montoria himself, Don José’s son, could not serve under the Sas banner, and enlisted like myself in the battalion of Las Peñas de San Pedro. Good luck bestowed upon me a good companion and an excellent friend.

From the day of my arrival I had heard talk of the approach of the French army; but it was not an incontrovertible fact until the twentieth. In the afternoon a division arrived at Zuera, on the left bank of the river, to threaten the suburb; another, commanded by Suchet, encamped on the right above San Lamberto. Marshal Moncey, who was the general in command, placed himself, with three divisions, near the canal, and on both sides of the Huerva. Forty thousand men besieged us.

It is known that the French, impatient to defeat us, began operations early on the twenty-first, attacking simultaneously and with great vigor Monte Torrero, and the Arrabal, the suburb on the left of the Ebro, points without whose possession it was impossible to dream of conquering the valiant city. But if we were obliged to abandon Torrero on account of the danger of its defence, Saragossa displayed in the suburb such audacious courage that that day is known as one of the most brilliant of all her brilliant history.

From four o’clock, from day-dawn, the battalion of Las Peñas de San Pedro guarded the front of the fortifications, from Santa Engracia to the Convent of Trinitarios, a line which seemed the least exposed in all the circuit of the city. Behind Santa Engracia was established the battery of Los Mártires; from there ran the battlements of the wall as far as the Huerva bridge, defended by a barricade; it deflected afterwards towards the west, making an obtuse angle, and joining another redoubt built in the Torre del Pino; it continued in a straight line as far as the Convent of Trinitarios, and enclosed the Puerta del Carmen.

闲话少叙，还是回到我投军的事上来。半路上横着一个障碍：帕拉福克斯十二月十三日的告示，限令二十四小时之内将一切外来人等驱逐出城。此举是因为生事捣乱、挑动嫌隙、搅扰治安的人实在太多；可不巧正赶上我进城的时候，又颁下另一道命令，召集在图德拉（Tudela）溃散的中央军团（Army of the Centre）的散兵，我便得了应募的机会。我虽不属于那支军队，却参加过马德里保卫战和拜伦（Bailén）战役。凭这些资历，再加上我的保护人蒙特里亚从中照应，我终于进了萨拉戈萨的行伍。他们把我安置进圣佩德罗岩（Las Peñas de San Pedro）志愿兵营——这个营在第一次围城中折损惨重——我还领到一套军装和一支枪。我并没有像我那位保护人先前说的那样，编入圣地亚哥·萨斯神父的连队，因为那支英勇的队伍清一色由圣巴勃罗教区的居民组成。他们营里不收年轻后生；为此，连唐·何塞的儿子奥古斯丁·蒙特里亚本人，也没能在萨斯的旗帜下效力，同我一样进了圣佩德罗岩营。好运赐了我一位好同伴、一位难得的挚友。

从我进城那天起，便听人纷纷议论法军将至；可直到二十日，此事才算铁板钉钉。当天下午，法军一个师开抵河左岸的苏埃拉（Zuera），威胁着城郊；另一个师由絮歇（Suchet）统带，在右岸圣兰贝托（San Lamberto）上游一带扎营。总司令蒙塞元帅（Marshal Moncey）亲率三个师，进驻运河近旁、韦尔瓦河两岸。四万兵马，把我们围了个严实。

众所周知，法军急不可耐地要击垮我们，二十一日一清早便动手开战，同时以雷霆之势猛攻托雷罗山（Monte Torrero）与阿拉巴尔（Arrabal）——埃布罗河左岸的那片郊区；不先夺下这两处，梦想征服这座英勇之城便无从谈起。托雷罗山因防守艰险，我们只得放弃；可在郊区一役，萨拉戈萨展现出的勇悍气概，使这一天跻身她全部煌煌历史上最光辉的日子之一。

从凌晨四点、天光初破时分起，圣佩德罗岩营便扼守着防线正面，从圣恩格拉西亚直到三一会修道院——在全城环围各段之中，这一线似乎最不吃紧。圣恩格拉西亚背后设着洛斯·马尔蒂雷斯炮台（Los Mártires）；城墙的雉堞自那里延伸至韦尔瓦河桥，由一道街垒扼守；随后城墙折向西行，转出一个钝角，与筑在皮诺塔的另一座棱堡相接；再成一直线延伸到三一会修道院，将卡门城门（Puerta del Carmen）围裹在内。

Whoever has seen Saragossa can well understand my imperfect description, for the ruins of Santa Engracia still remain, and in the Puerta del Carmen may still be seen, not far from the Glorieta, its ruined architrave and worm-eaten stones.

We were, as I have said, occupying the position described, and part of the soldiers had a bivouac in a neighboring orchard, next to the Carmen college.

Augustine Montoria and I were inseparable. His serene character, the affection he showed me from the moment we met, and the inexplicable concord in our thoughts, made his company very agreeable. He was a young man of beautiful figure, with large brilliant eyes and open brow, and an expression marked by a melancholy gravity. His heart, like that of his father, was filled by generosity which overflowed at the least impulse; but he was not likely to wound the feelings of a friend, because education had taken from him a great deal of the national brusqueness. Augustine entered manhood’s estate with the security of a kind heart, firm and uncorrupted judgment, with a vigorous and healthy soul; the wide world only was the limit of his boundless goodness. These qualities were enriched by a brilliant imagination of sure and direct action, not like that of our modern geniuses, who most of the time do not know what they are about. Augustine’s imagination was lofty and serene, worthy of his education in the great classics. Although with a lively inclination to poetry﻿—for Augustine was a poet﻿—he had learned theology, showing ability in this as in everything. The fathers at the Seminary, who were fond of the youth, looked upon him as a prodigy in the sciences, human and divine, and they congratulated themselves on seeing him with one foot at least over the threshold of the Church.

The Montoria family had many a pleasant anticipation of the day when Augustine would say his first mass, as a holy event that was fast approaching. Yet﻿—I am obliged to say it﻿—Augustine had no vocation for the Church. Neither his family nor the good fathers of the Seminary understood this, nor would they have understood it, even if the Holy Spirit had come down in person to tell them. This precocious theologian, this humanist who had Horace at the ends of his fingers, this dialectician who in the weekly discussions astonished the fathers with intellectual gymnastics of scholastic science, had no more vocation for the Church than Mozart for war, Raphael for mathematics, or Napoleon for dancing!

凡是见过萨拉戈萨的人，都不难会意于我这番粗陋的描述：圣恩格拉西亚的废墟至今犹存，而离格洛列塔（Glorieta）不远的卡门城门，那残破的门楣与虫蛀的石块也仍历历可见。

我已说过，我们占着上述阵地，一部分士兵在邻近一座果园里露营，紧挨着卡门学院。

奥古斯丁·蒙特里亚同我形影不离。他性情沉静，自我们相识那一刻起便待我亲厚，加之我们二人心思的契合奇妙得难以言喻，使他的陪伴令人极为熨帖。他是个身姿俊美的青年，眼睛大而明亮，额头开阔，神情带着一缕忧郁的庄重。他的心同他父亲的一样，盛满了稍一触动便要四溢的慷慨；但他不至于刺伤朋友的感情，因为教养已把他身上那种国民性的粗豪磨去了大半。奥古斯丁跨入成年时，怀着一颗善心的笃定、一份坚定而不受腐蚀的判断力，和一个强健而健全的灵魂；他那无边的善意，唯以广阔世界为界。这些资质又得了一副才华照人的想象力的滋养——那想象力行事笃定而直截，不像我们当今的天才们，多半时候连自己正干什么都不知晓。奥古斯丁的想象力高远而澄静，配得上他所受的伟大古典学问的教养。他虽怀着对诗歌的活泼倾慕——因为奥古斯丁是个诗人——学的却是神学，而且在这门学问上如同在一切事情上一样，都显露出才能。神学院里那些疼爱这青年的神父们，把他看作人间与神圣两门学问上的奇才，见他至少已有一只脚踏过教会的门槛，无不暗自庆幸。

蒙特里亚一家满怀欣悦，翘盼着奥古斯丁首祭弥撒那一天的到来，把那当作一件日渐临近的圣事。然而——我不得不说——奥古斯丁对教会并无圣召。这一点，他的家人不明白，神学院的好神父们也不明白；哪怕圣灵亲自降临、当面告知，他们照样不会明白。这位早慧的神学家，这位把贺拉斯（Horace）玩于指掌的人文学者，这位在每周论辩中用经院学问的智力体操令众神父叹服的论辩家，对教会的圣召，并不比莫扎特之于打仗、拉斐尔之于数学、拿破仑之于跳舞多出一分！
