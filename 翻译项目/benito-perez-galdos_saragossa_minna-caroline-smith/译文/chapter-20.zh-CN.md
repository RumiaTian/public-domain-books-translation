## XX / 第二十章

===Original===
I slept from three o’clock until daybreak, and in the morning we heard mass in the Coso. In the large balcony of a house called Las Monas at the entrance of the Calle de las Escuelas Pías all the priests had set up an altar and celebrated there the divine office. By the situation of the building, it was possible to see the priests from anywhere in the Coso. It was a profoundly moving sight, especially at the moment of the elevation of the host; and when all knelt in prayer, the low murmur of the service could be heard from one end of the street to the other.

A little while after the mass was ended, I heard a large number of people coming from the direction of the market﻿—an angry and noisy crowd. In the mob, and striving to quell its violence, were several friars; but it was a mob of men deaf to the voice of reason. They were yelling themselves hoarse, and as they came, they dragged along a victim who was powerless to free himself from their grasp. The maddened people took him to the place near the entrance of the Trenque where the gallows was; and in a few moments the convulsed body of a man was hanging from one of its ropes, and was jerked about in the air until it was lifeless. On the wood of the gallows an inscription soon appeared, which read﻿—

> An assassin 

 of human kind, 

 who 

 kept back twenty thousand beds.

===Chinese===
我从三点一直睡到天亮，早晨我们在科索大街望了弥撒。在埃斯库埃拉斯皮亚斯街（Calle de las Escuelas Pías）街口一座名叫拉斯莫纳斯（Las Monas）的宅子里，众神父在宽大的阳台上设起一座祭台，就在那里举行日课。这宅子位置得宜，站在科索大街的任何一处都能望见神父们。那景象动人心魄，尤其是在举扬圣体的时刻；待到众人跪下祈祷，低低的诵经声便从街这头一直传到街那头。

弥撒结束后不多时，我听见一大群人从市场方向涌来——那是一伙怒气冲冲、喧嚷吵闹的人。人堆里有几个修士，正竭力平息众人的暴怒；可这伙人对理性的呼声充耳不闻。他们叫嚷得声嘶力竭，一路拖拽着一个无力挣脱他们掌握的倒霉鬼。发了狂的人们把他拖到特伦克土丘口附近竖着绞刑架的地方；不多时，一个男人抽搐的躯体已吊上绞架的其中一根绳索，在空中被甩来荡去，直到断了气。绞刑架的木架上旋即出现一行题词，写道——

> 一个残害

 人类的凶手，

 他

 匿藏了两万张床。

===Original===
The wretch was one Fernando Estello, watchman of a storehouse of furniture. When the sick and wounded were breathing their last in the gutters and on the cold tiles of the churches, there was found a great collection of beds whose hiding the watchman Estello could not account for. The wrath of the populace was not to be restrained. I have heard that he was innocent. Many lamented his death; but when the firing in the trenches began again, no one remembered him more.

Palafox published that day a proclamation in which he tried to raise the spirits of the soldiers, promising the rank of captain to the man who should bring him a hundred recruits, threatening with the penalty of death and confiscation of property the man who should fail to hasten to the defence, or should leave the lines. All this showed great distress on the part of the commanding officers. That day was memorable for the attack on Santa Mónica, which the volunteers of Huesca were defending. During the greater part of the night the French had been bombarding the building. The batteries of the orchard were no longer serviceable, and it was necessary to take away the cannon, an operation performed by our valiant men, exposed without protection to the hostile fire. This opened a breach at last; and, penetrating into the orchard, they tried to gain possession of that also, forgetting that they had twice been repulsed on previous days. But Lannes, exasperated by the extraordinary and unprecedented tenacity of the Saragossans, had given orders to reduce the convent to powder﻿—a thing which was easier to accomplish with the cannon and howitzer than to take it by storm. At all events, after six hours of artillery fire, a large part of the eastern wall fell, and then the French showed their exultation, and, without loss of time, rushed forward to seize the position, aided by the crossfire from the Molino in the city. Seeing them coming, Villacampa, commander of the Huesca men, and Palafox, who had hurried to the point of danger, tried to close up the breach with sacks of wool and some empty musket-boxes. The French, reaching the spot, made a mad, furious assault, but, after a brief hand-to-hand struggle, they were repulsed. During the night they went on cannonading the convent.

这可怜虫名叫费尔南多·埃斯特洛（Fernando Estello），是一所家具仓库的看守。当伤病员们在街沟里、在教堂冰冷的砖地上咽下最后一口气的时候，城里却发现了一大批藏起来的床铺，而看守埃斯特洛交代不出这些床的下落。民众的怒火再也按捺不住。我后来听说他是无辜的。许多人为他的死哀叹；可是等堑壕里的枪声再度响起，就再没有人记得他了。

帕拉福克斯当天颁布了一道文告，力图振作士兵们的士气：凡给他带来一百名新兵的人，许以上尉军衔；凡不赶去奔赴防务、或擅离防线的人，则威吓以死刑并抄没家产。凡此种种，都显出指挥官们忧急之深。这一天因圣莫尼卡修道院遇攻而令人难忘，守卫它的是韦斯卡义勇军。大半夜里，法军一直在炮击那座建筑。果园里的几座炮台已被轰得不堪再用，必须把大炮撤走，我们勇猛的士兵暴露在敌人炮火之下、毫无遮蔽地干成了这桩活。法军终于轰开一个缺口；他们打进果园，还想把它也一并夺到手，全忘了前些天已两度被击退。可是拉纳已被萨拉戈萨人那非凡绝伦、史无前例的顽强劲儿激得怒不可遏，早已下令把这座修道院夷为齑粉——用大炮和榴弹炮办成这件事，总比强攻硬取来得容易。总之，经过六个小时的炮击，东墙塌下了好大一片；法军顿时得意忘形，毫不耽搁地扑上去抢夺阵地，还得着城中莫利诺磨坊交叉火力的支援。见他们冲来，韦斯卡人的统领比利亚坎普和急忙赶到险处的帕拉福克斯，设法用羊毛包和几只空弹药箱堵住缺口。法军扑到近前，发起疯狂暴烈的冲击，但经过短暂的白刃格斗，又被击退了。夜里，他们继续朝修道院轰击。

===Original===
The next day they decided to make another attack, certain that no mortal could defend that skeleton of stone and brick which every moment was crumbling to the earth. They assailed it at the door of the reception-room; but during all the morning they did not conquer a hand’s breadth of earth in the cloister.

The wall of the eastern side of the convent fell flat to the earth during the afternoon. The third floor, which was very much weakened, could not hold the weight, and fell upon the second. The latter, which was even weaker, could not help letting itself go upon the first; and the first, incapable of sustaining by itself the weight of the whole structure above, fairly poured itself out over the cloister, burying hundreds of men. It would have been but natural had the rest been intimidated by such a catastrophe, but they were not. The French gained possession of one part of the convent, but not of all; and, in order to gain the rest, they were obliged to clear a road through the ruins. While they were doing this, the men of Huesca who still survived, placed themselves in the stairway, and made holes through the floor, in order to throw hand-grenades against the besiegers.

Fresh French troops were, however, able to reach the church. They passed over the roof of the convent, and spread themselves in the interior; they descended to the cloisters and attacked the brave volunteers. Hearing the noise of this encounter, those below plucked up heart, redoubled their energy, and, with the loss of a great number of men, succeeded in reaching the stairway. The volunteers found themselves between two fires, and although it was still possible for them to get out by one of the two openings in the cloister, almost all of them swore that they would die before they would surrender. They all ran, seeking for a strategic point which would permit them to defend themselves to some advantage; but they were driven the length of the cloisters, and when the last gunshot was heard, it was the signal that the last man had fallen. A few inside the building were able to get out by an underground door. Don Pedro Villacampa, commander of the Huesca volunteers, came out into the city that way, and when he found himself in the street, he turned, looking about mechanically for his boys.

次日，法军决意再度进攻，笃信再没有哪个凡人守得住那副砖石骨架——它时时刻刻都在崩塌入土。他们从会客厅的门口发起攻击；可整整一个上午，他们在回廊里连一掌宽的地皮也没能夺得。

下午，修道院东侧的墙体整个儿平平地塌倒在地。三楼早已被轰得虚弱不堪，吃不住那份重量，砸落在二楼上；二楼更弱，撑持不住，便也顺势砸向一楼；一楼独自扛不住上方整座结构的重压，索性一股脑儿倾泻到回廊上，把几百号人埋在了底下。遭了这样一场大难，余下的人若是被吓倒，本也是人之常情，他们却没有。法军夺下了修道院的一部分，却没能全占；要把其余部分也拿到手，他们不得不从废墟里清出一条路来。趁他们干这活儿的当口，还活着的韦斯卡人据住楼梯间，在楼板上凿出窟窿，好朝围攻者投掷手榴弹。

然而，法军的新锐部队还是打进了教堂。他们翻过修道院的屋顶，向内里四下散开；下到回廊，向勇敢的义勇军扑攻。下方的人听见这场厮杀的动静，鼓起勇气，劲头倍增，折损了大批人马之后，终于赶到了楼梯间。义勇军发觉自己夹在两股火力之间，虽说此刻仍可从回廊两处出口之一脱身，他们几乎人人都发誓宁死不降。众人奔走，寻觅一处能让自己据守得略占便宜的地形；可他们被一路逼到了回廊尽头，当最后一声枪响传来，那便是最后一人倒下的信号。楼里少数人从一扇地下门脱了身。韦斯卡义勇军的统领唐·佩德罗·比利亚坎普便是从那里出到城里的；待他站到街上，回转身来，下意识地环顾张望，寻找他手下的小伙子们。

===Original===
During this fight we were in the houses about the Calle de Palomar, firing upon the French detachment sent to assault the convent. Before the battle was over, we learned that defence was no longer possible in Las Mónicas. Don José de Montoria himself, who was with us, confessed it.

“The volunteers of Huesca have not borne themselves badly,” he said. “They are known to be good fellows. Now we must busy ourselves defending these houses on the right. I do not suppose that one is left. There goes Villacampa alone. Then are not those Mendieta, and Paul, Benedicto, and Oliva? Let us go. I see that indeed none are left in that place.”

In this way the convent of Las Mónicas passed into the hands of the French.

===Chinese===
这场战斗进行的时候，我们正在帕洛马尔街一带的房屋里，朝奉命强攻修道院的法军分队射击。战斗尚未结束，我们便得知拉斯莫尼卡斯已再无守住的指望。同我们在一起的唐·何塞·德·蒙特里亚本人也亲口承认了这一点。

“韦斯卡的义勇军打得不算差，”他说，“谁都知道他们是好样的。眼下咱们得赶紧着守右边这些房子。我看那边怕是一个人也不剩了。瞧，比利亚坎普一个人过去了。那不就是门迭塔（Mendieta）、保罗（Paul）、贝内迪克托（Benedicto）和奥利瓦（Oliva）吗？走吧。我看那地方当真一个人也不剩了。”

就这样，拉斯莫尼卡斯修道院落入了法军之手。
