## XXIII / 第二十三章

===Original===
Incorporated into the battalion of Estremadura, we went along the Calle de Palomar into the Plaza de la Magdalena, whence we could hear the roar of battle at the end of the Calle de Puerta Quemada. As we have said, the enemy tried to take the Calle de Pabostre in order to get possession of Puerta Quemada, an important point whence they could rake with their artillery the street of the same name towards the Plaza de la Magdalena. As the possession of San Augustine and Las Mónicas permitted them to threaten that central point by the easy way to the Calle de Palomar, they already considered themselves masters of the suburb. In fact, if those in San Augustine managed to advance to the ruins of the Seminary, and those of the Calle de Pabostre to the Puerta Quemada, it would be impossible to dispute with the French the quarter of Las Tenerías.

After a short time they took us to the Calle de Pabostre, and as the battle of the outside and inside of the buildings and of the public way was now all combined, we entered the first block by the Calle de los Viejos. From the windows of the house in which we found ourselves, we could see nothing but smoke, and could tell but little of what was going on there. I saw later that the street was all filled with embrasures and trenches at certain distances made of heaps of earth, furniture, and rubbish. From the windows a tremendous fire was poured forth, and, remembering a phrase of the beggar Sursum Corda, I can say that our souls were turned into bullets. Inside the houses the blood flowed in torrents. The onset of the French was terrible, and that the resistance might not be less terrible the belfries summoned men unceasingly. The general dictated stern orders for the punishment of stragglers. The friars rallied the people of other districts, dragging them forward as in a leash. Some heroic women set an example, throwing themselves into danger, guns in hand.

A dreadful day, whose frightful roar resounds ever in the ears of him who was present! Its remembrance pursues him, an unescaped nightmare, through his whole life. He who did not see these horrors, who did not hear the noise of that shouting, knows not with what expression the depths of the horrible may be uttered to human feeling. Do not tell me that you have seen the crater of a volcano in the most violent eruption; or a great tempest in the open ocean when the ship, tossed to heaven on a mountain of waterfalls, descends next to a giddy depth﻿—do not tell me you have seen these things, for they are nothing at all like the volcanoes and tempests of man when his passions urge him to out-rival the disorders in Nature.

编入埃斯特雷马杜拉营之后，我们取道帕洛马尔街开进拉马格达莱纳广场（Plaza de la Magdalena），从那里听得见克马达门街（Calle de Puerta Quemada）尽头传来的战斗轰鸣。如前所述，敌人力图夺取帕博斯特雷街，以便占据克马达门——那是一处紧要据点，占了它，他们便可纵炮扫射与之同名的那条街，直扫到拉马格达莱纳广场。圣奥古斯丁与拉斯莫尼卡斯既已在其手中，他们尽可循帕洛马尔街这条便道威胁那一处中枢要点，于是已自视为这片近郊城区的主人了。事实上，倘若圣奥古斯丁方面的法军得以推进到神学院的废墟，帕博斯特雷街方面的法军得以进抵克马达门，那么特内里亚斯区便再也无法同法军相争了。

不多时，我们被调往帕博斯特雷街；此时房屋里外与街道之上的战斗已经浑然连成一片，我们便由洛斯别霍斯街（Calle de los Viejos）进入第一个街区。从我们所在那座房子的窗口望出去，除了硝烟什么也看不见，那里究竟进行到什么地步，几乎无从知晓。后来我才看清，那条街上每隔一段距离，便垒起一道道炮眼与堑壕，全用成堆的泥土、家具和瓦砾筑成。窗口喷吐出猛烈的火力；想起乞丐 Sursum Corda 的那句妙语，我可以说，我们的魂灵都化作了子弹。房屋之内，血如泉涌。法军的攻势凶猛骇人；而为了叫我方的抵抗不输这份凶猛，一座座钟楼不住地把人召唤上前。将军口授下一道道严令，惩办掉队者。修士们把别的教区的百姓聚拢起来，像牵着索链似的把他们朝前拽。一些英勇的妇女以身作则，端着枪直投到危险之中。

可怕的一天啊！那骇人的轰响，永远萦回在亲历者的耳畔！对这一天的记忆追逐着他，像一场再也逃脱不掉的梦魇，纠缠他一生。没有见过这些恐怖、没有听过那片喊杀声的人，便不知道“恐怖”的深处还能以怎样的言辞向人类的心灵诉说。别对我说你见过火山最猛烈喷发时的火山口，或见过外洋上的大风暴——船只被抛上一座飞瀑堆成的高山、甩向半天，转瞬又坠向叫人目眩的深渊——别对我说你见过这些东西，因为它们同人间的火山与风暴毫无一点相像：那要等到人的激情驱策他去赛过大自然的狂暴失序之时。

===Original===
It was difficult to hold us back, and not being able to do much where we were, we descended to the street without noticing the officers who tried to hold us back. The combat had an irresistible attraction for us, and called us as the deep calls unto a man who looks down upon it from a cliff. I have never considered myself heroic; but it is certain that in those moments I did not fear death, nor did the sight of catastrophes terrify me. It is true that heroism, as a thing of the moment, and the direct child of inspiration, does not belong exclusively to the brave. That is the reason it is often found in women and cowards.

I will not go into the details of those struggles in the Calle de Pabostre. They were much like those which I have described before. If they differed in any respect, it was in their excess of constancy, and energy raised to a height where the human ended and the divine began. Within the houses, scenes passed like those I have described elsewhere, but with greater carnage, because victory was believed more certain. The advantage the men of the Empire gained in one place they lost in another. The battles, begun in the attics, descended step by step to the cellars, and were finished there with clubbed muskets, with the advantage always on the side of our peasants. The tones of command with which one or another directed the movements within these labyrinths resounded from room to room with fearful echoes. They used their artillery in the street, and we did also. Often they tried to get possession of our pieces by sudden hand-to-hand struggles; but they lost many men without ever succeeding.

Alarmed on seeing that the force used at one time to gain a battle was not now sufficient to gain two yards of a street, they refused to fight, and their officers drove them forward, beating their laziness out of them with cudgels. On our side such measures were not necessary; persuasion was enough. The priests, without neglecting the dying, attended to everything. If they saw a weakening anywhere, they would hasten to tell the officers.

我们很难被拦得住；而既然待在原地出不了什么力，我们便下到街上去，全不理会那些想要拦住我们的军官。战斗对我们有一种无法抗拒的吸引力，召唤着我们，就像深渊召唤着一个自悬崖俯视它的人。我从不曾自命英雄；但可以肯定的是，在那些时刻我不惧怕死亡，大难临头的景象也吓不倒我。诚然，英雄主义既是一时之物，又是灵感的亲生儿，并不专属勇士。正因如此，它才常常在女人和懦夫身上寻见。

帕博斯特雷街上那些搏斗的细节，我就不去细述了。它们同我先前描述过的那些大同小异。若说有什么不同，那便是坚忍得过了限度，气力升腾到了人性告终、神性发端的高处。房屋之内，一幕幕场景同我在别处描述过的相仿，只是屠戮更甚，因为人人都相信胜利更加十拿九稳了。帝国士兵在一处占到的便宜，又在另一处丢还了回去。战斗从阁楼开打，一步一步降入地窖，最后在那里以倒持火枪、枪柄互殴收场，占上风的永远是我们这边的庄稼汉。号令的声调指挥着这一座座迷宫之内的调度，从一间屋子响到另一间屋子，激起可怖的回声。他们在街上使炮，我们也使。他们屡屡想凭突如其来的白刃格斗夺走我们的炮；可是人折损了许多，却从未得手一回。

法军眼见从前足以赢得一场会战的兵力，如今连夺取两码街道都不敷使用了，不免惊慌；他们不肯再战，军官们便把他们朝前驱赶，用棍棒把他们周身的懒怠痛打出去。在我们这一边，这样的手段全然用不着；好言劝勉就够了。神父们既不怠慢垂死的人，又事事照管。哪里一见松动，他们便赶紧告知军官们。

===Original===
In one of the trenches in the street, a woman, bravest of all, Manuela Sancho, after having fired with a gun, began serving cannon number eight. She remained unhurt all day, encouraging all with brave words﻿—an example to the men. It was perhaps three o’clock when she fell, wounded in the leg, and during a long time was supposed to be dead, because the hemorrhage made her seem lifeless; she looked like a corpse. Later, seeing that she breathed, we carried her to the rear, and she was restored, and had such good health afterwards that many years later I had the pleasure of seeing her still alive.

History has not forgotten that brave young Maid of Saragossa. The Calle de Pabostre, whose poor houses are more eloquent than the pages of a book, now bears the name of Manuela Sancho.

A little after three o’clock, a tremendous loud explosion shook the houses which the French had disputed with us in such a bloody manner during the morning. Amid the dust, and the smoke thicker than dust, we saw walls and roofs falling in a thousand pieces, with a noise of which I can give no idea. The French had begun to employ mines, in order to gain that which they could in no other way wrench from the hands of the sons of Aragón. They opened galleries; they charged the mines; then the men folded their arms, waiting for the powder to do it all.

===Chinese===
在街上的一处堑壕里，有一位妇女，是所有人当中最勇敢的——玛努埃拉·桑乔；她先是持枪放了一阵，随后便操持起八号大炮。整整一天她毫发无伤，用豪迈的话语鼓舞着众人——给男人们立了个榜样。约莫三点钟，她倒下了，腿部负伤；因为失血使她看上去气息全无，很长一段时间里大家都以为她死了；她那样子活像一具尸体。后来见她还有鼻息，我们把她抬到后方，她竟康复了，而且此后身子骨一直那样硬朗，以至许多年后我还有幸见到她依然健在。

历史没有遗忘那位萨拉戈萨的勇敢少女。帕博斯特雷街那些寒酸的房屋，比书页更有雄辩之力，如今这条街便以玛努埃拉·桑乔为名。

三点刚过，一声惊天动地的爆炸震动了法军上午同我们浴血相争的那几座房屋。尘土弥漫，烟尘更浓于尘土，只见墙垣与屋顶碎作千百块纷纷坠落，那声响我实在无从形容。法军开始动用坑道埋雷，好夺得他们再无别法从阿拉贡之子手中抢走的东西。他们开掘导坑，装药入雷，随后那些人便袖起双手，单等火药去包办一切。

===Original===
When the first house went, we stayed quietly in the next, and in the street. But when the second went with a still louder noise, the retreat began with plenty of disorder. Considering that so many unfortunate comrades were hurled into the air or buried beneath the ruins, men who had been unconquerable by force of arms, we felt ourselves too weak to contend with the new element of destruction. It seemed to us that in all the other houses, and in the street, horrible craters were going to burst forth which would send us flying, torn into a thousand bloody fragments.

The officers held us back, calling﻿—

“Courage, boys, stand firm! That is done to frighten us. We have plenty of powder, too, and we will open mines. Do you think this will give them an advantage? On the contrary, we shall see how they will defend themselves among a lot of fragments.”

===Chinese===
第一座房子飞上天时，我们还安安静静待在隔壁一座里、待在街上。可当第二座以更响的声势塌下去时，溃退便大乱着开始了。想到那么多不幸的战友被抛上半空、或被埋进废墟——那些凭刀枪无法征服的汉子——我们只觉得自己太孱弱，斗不过这新出现的毁灭之力。我们仿佛觉得，其余所有的房子里、连同街上，都有一个个恐怖的火山口即将迸裂开来，把我们撕作千百块带血的碎片抛上天去。

军官们拦住我们，喊道——

“鼓起勇气来，小伙子们，站稳了！那是吓唬咱们的把戏。咱们的火药也足着呢，咱们也会挖坑道埋雷。你们以为这样就能让他们占到便宜吗？恰恰相反，咱们倒要瞧瞧，他们在一堆碎砖烂瓦之间怎么招架。”

===Original===
Palafox appeared at the entrance of the street, and his presence restrained us for some time. The noise prevented me from hearing what he was saying, but by his gestures I understood that he wished us to go on over the ruins.

“You hear, boys! You hear what the Captain-General says!” a friar shouted beside us, one of those who had come with Palafox. “He says that if you will make a little exertion, not one Frenchman will be left alive.” “You are right!” cried another friar. “There will not be a woman left in Saragossa who will even look at you, if you do not hurl yourselves instantly upon those ruins of the houses, and drive the French out.”

“Forward, sons of the Virgin del Pilar!” cried out a third friar. “Do you see those women over there? Do you know what they are saying? They are saying that if you do not go, they will go themselves. Are you not ashamed of your cowardice?”

With that, we stood up a little more bravely. Another house fell on the right. Palafox came into the street. Without knowing how or why, we followed him when he put himself at our head. Now is the time to speak of that high personage whose name and fame are one with that of Saragossa. His prestige is due in large measure to his great courage, but also to his noble origin, and the respect in which the family of Lazán has always been held in Saragossa, and to his handsome and spirited presence. He was young. He had belonged to the Guards. He was much praised for having refused the favors of a very highly-placed lady, as famous for her position as for scandals about her. That which endeared the Saragossan leader more than anything else to his people was, however, his supreme, his indomitable courage, the youthful ardor with which he attacked the most dangerous and difficult obstacles, simply to reach his ideals of honor and glory.

帕拉福克斯在街口现身了，他的到场把我们稳住了一些时候。喧声震耳，我听不见他在说什么，但凭他的手势我明白了：他要我们踏着废墟继续前进。

“听见没有，小伙子们！听见总司令说什么了！”我们身旁一个修士高声喊道，他是随帕拉福克斯同来的那批人中的一个。“他说，只要你们肯再使一把劲，一个法国佬也别想活着留下。”“说得对！”另一个修士嚷道，“你们要是不立刻扑向那些房子的废墟、把法国佬赶出去，往后萨拉戈萨连一个肯抬眼看你们的女人都剩不下。”

“前进，皮拉尔圣母的儿女们！”第三个修士喊道。“瞧见那边那些女人了吗？知道她们在说什么吗？她们说，你们要是不上，她们就自己上。你们不为自己的怯懦害臊吗？”

听了这话，我们挺身站得勇敢了些。右边又有一座房子塌了。帕拉福克斯走进街来。也不知是怎么、又是为什么，当他把身子朝我们前头一放，我们便跟着他走了。现在正该说一说这位显赫人物了，他的名字与声名，同萨拉戈萨已是融为一体。他的威望，一大部分要归功于他的大勇，但也要归功于他高贵的出身、拉桑家族在萨拉戈萨素来享有的敬重，以及他英俊英武的仪表。他正当年轻，曾在王室卫队供职。他因拒不领受一位地位极高的贵妇人的恩宠而备受称誉——那位夫人既以地位著称，也一样以围绕她的风流丑闻闻名。不过，使这位萨拉戈萨的统帅最得其民之爱戴的，毕竟还是他那至高无上、不可征服的勇气，是他扑向最危险、最艰难的障碍时的那股青春热忱——他为的不过是抵达他心中荣誉与光荣的理想。

If he lacked intellectual gifts to direct an undertaking so arduous as this, he had the prudence to know his lack, and to surround himself with men distinguished for their judgment and wisdom. These men did everything. Palafox was the great figurehead, the chief actor in the scene. Over a people so largely ruled by imagination, that young general could scarcely fail to hold an imperious dominion, with his illustrious lineage and splendid figure. He showed himself everywhere, encouraging the weak, and distributing rewards to the brave.

The Saragossans beheld in him the symbol of their constancy, their virtues, their patriotic ideal with its touch of mysticism, and their warlike zeal. Whatever he ordered, everybody found right and just. Like those monarchs whom traditional laws have made the personal embodiment of government, Palafox could do no wrong. Anything wrong was the work of his counsellors. In reality, the illustrious commander did not govern, he reigned. Father Basilio governed, with O’Neill, Saint March, and Butron, the first, an ecclesiastic, the other three noted generals.

In places of danger, Palafox always appeared like a human expression of triumph. His voice reanimated the dying; and if the Virgin del Pilar had spoken, she would have chosen no other mouth. His countenance always expressed a supreme confidence. In his triumphal smile, courage overflowed, as in others it is expressed by a ferocious frown. He was vaingloriously proud of being the prop of that great hour in history. He understood instinctively that the outcome depended more upon him as an actor than upon him as a general. He always appeared in all the splendors of his uniform, with gold lace, waving plumes, and medals. The thundering music of applause, of huzzas, flattered him extremely. All this was necessary. Indeed there must always be something of mutual adulation between the army and the commander-in-chief, in order that the pride of victory may inspire one and all to deeds of heroism.

如果说他缺乏指挥这样一项艰巨事业的智力禀赋，他却有自知之明的审慎，知道自己的不足，也知道把以判断力与智慧见称的人士延揽到身边。凡事都是这些人做的。帕拉福克斯是那尊巨大的船首雕像，是这场大戏里的头号演员。对这样一个在很大程度上受想象力支配的民族，这位年轻将军凭着煊赫的世系与堂堂的仪表，几乎不可能不握有威严专断的统治权。他四处露面，激励怯弱者，给勇敢者颁发奖赏。

萨拉戈萨人在他身上看见了自身坚忍的象征、自身美德的象征、他们那带一抹神秘主义色彩的爱国理想与好战热忱的象征。他下的任何命令，人人都觉得又对又公道。正如那些被传统法制成政府之人格化身的君主们一样，帕拉福克斯是不会做错的。凡有错处，皆是谋臣的手笔。实际上，这位声名煊赫的统帅并不治理，他只是君临。真正治理的是巴西利奥·博希耶罗神父，与他共事的还有奥尼尔、圣马尔奇和布特龙——头一位是教士，其余三位皆知名将领。

在危险之地，帕拉福克斯露面时总像胜利的一个活人化身。他的嗓音能教垂死者回过魂来；倘若皮拉尔圣母开口说话，除他这张嘴也断不会另挑一张。他的面容永远流露着至高的自信。在他那凯旋的微笑里，勇气横溢而出，正如在另一些人身上，勇气靠狰狞的怒容来表达。他以骄矜得意之心，自居为历史上那伟大时刻的支柱。他本能地懂得，成败与其说系于他作为将军的才具，不如说更系于他作为演员的角色。他露面时总是军装的全部光华加身：金绦、飘拂的羽饰、勋章。掌声与欢呼汇成的雷鸣乐音，把他奉承到了极点。而这一切都是必要的。的确，军队与总司令之间，永远须有一点相互的颂扬，胜利的荣耀方能激励上下一心，共赴英雄的功业。
