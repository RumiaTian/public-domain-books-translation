## VI Love and Will / VI 爱与意志

===Original===
This steady effort towards the simplifying of your tangled character, its gradual emancipation from the fetters of the unreal, is not to dispense you from that other special training of the attention which the diligent practice of meditation and recollection effects. Your pursuit of the one must never involve neglect of the other; for these are the two sides—one moral, the other mental—of that unique process of self-conquest which Ruysbroeck calls “the gathering of the forces of the soul into the unity of the spirit”: the welding together of all your powers, the focusing of them upon one point. Hence they should never, either in theory or practice, be separated. Only the act of recollection, the constantly renewed retreat to the quiet centre of the spirit, gives that assurance of a Reality, a calmer and more valid life attainable by us, which supports the stress and pain of self-simplification and permits us to hope on, even in the teeth of the world’s cruelty, indifference, degeneracy; whilst diligent character-building alone, with its perpetual untiring efforts at self-adjustment, its bracing, purging discipline, checks the human tendency to relapse into and react to the obvious, and makes possible the further development of the contemplative power.

So it is through and by these two great changes in your attitude towards things—first, the change of attention, which enables you to perceive a truer universe; next, the deliberate rearrangement of your ideas, energies, and desires in harmony with that which you have seen—that a progressive uniformity of life and experience is secured to you, and you are defended against the dangers of an indolent and useless mysticality. Only the real, say the mystics, can know Reality, for “we behold that which we are,” the universe which we see is conditioned by the character of the mind that sees it: and this realness—since that which you seek is no mere glimpse of Eternal Life, but complete possession of it—must apply to every aspect of your being, the rich totality of character, all the “forces of the soul,” not to some thin and isolated “spiritual sense” alone. This is why recollection and self-simplification—perception of, and adaptation to, the Spiritual World in which we dwell—are the essential preparations for the mystical life, and neither can exist in a wholesome and well-balanced form without the other. By them the mind, the will, the heart, which so long had dissipated their energies over a thousand scattered notions, wants, and loves, are gradually detached from their old exclusive preoccupation with the ephemeral interests of the self, or of the group to which the self belongs.

You, if you practise them, will find after a time—perhaps a long time—that the hard work which they involve has indeed brought about a profound and definite change in you. A new suppleness has taken the place of that rigidity which you have been accustomed to mistake for strength of character: an easier attitude towards the accidents of life. Your whole scale of values has undergone a silent transformation, since you have ceased to fight for your own hand and regard the nearest-at-hand world as the only one that counts. You have become, as the mystics would say, “free from inordinate attachments,” the “heat of having” does not scorch you any more; and because of this you possess great inward liberty, a sense of spaciousness and peace. Released from the obsessions which so long had governed them, will, heart, and mind are now all bent to the purposes of your deepest being: “gathered in the unity of the spirit,” they have fused to become an agent with which it can act.

这种持续的努力——使你那纷乱纠结的性格趋于单纯，使它逐渐摆脱非实在之物的羁绊——并不能使你免除另一项对注意力的特殊训练，即勤勉操练默想与收敛心神所产生的训练。你对其中一方的追求，绝不可牵连对另一方的忽视；因为这两者是那一独特的自我征服过程的两个侧面——一为道德的，一为心智的——吕斯布鲁克称之为「把灵魂诸力聚集入心灵的合一」：也就是把你的一切能力焊接为一，将它们聚焦于一点。因此，无论在理论上还是实践上，两者都绝不应分开。唯有收敛心神的行动——那不断更新的、向心灵静谧中心的退隐——能给予我们那种保证：保证有一种实在，一种我们可达致的更平静、更有效的生命；正是这种保证，支撑着自我单纯化（self-simplification）的重压与痛苦，使我们即使顶着世界的残酷、冷漠与败坏，仍能继续怀抱希望；而唯有勤勉的品格建设——以其对自我调适永不懈怠的努力，以其振奋人心、施行净化的纪律——才能遏制人那种重新堕入浅显表象并对之作出反应的倾向，并使默观能力的进一步发展成为可能。

于是，正是借着、并经由你对待事物的这两种重大改变——首先是注意力的改变，它使你能知觉一个更真实的宇宙；其次是把你的观念、能量与欲望作有意的重新安排，使之与你已看见的那一切相和谐——你才获得保障，生命与经验将逐步趋于统一，你也被护卫起来，免受怠惰而无用的神秘性所含的种种危险。神秘主义者说，唯有真实者能认识实在，因为「我们所观照的，正是我们之所是」；我们所看见的宇宙，受那观看它的心灵之品格所制约。而既然你所寻求的并非对永恒生命的一瞥，而是对它的完全拥有，这种真实性就必须适用于你存在的每一方面，适用于品格的丰盈整体、全部的「灵魂诸力」，而不只适用于某种单薄而孤立的「灵性感官」。正因如此，收敛心神与自我单纯化——即对我们栖居其中的灵性世界的知觉与适应——乃是神秘生活必不可少的预备；缺了任何一方，另一方也无法以健全而均衡的形式存在。借着它们，那长久以来把能量耗散于千百种散乱观念、欲求与爱好之中的心智、意志与心灵，才逐渐脱离其旧有的、专一的沉迷——对自我或自我所属群体之短暂利益的沉迷。

你若操练它们，一段时日之后——也许是长长的一段时日——便会发现，其中包含的艰苦工夫确实已在你身上造成一种深刻而确定的变化。一种新的柔韧，已取代了你一向误认为性格之坚强的那种僵硬：对生活中的种种变故，你也有了一种更为从容的态度。你整个的价值尺度已然经历一场无声的转变，因为你已不再一味为自己打算争夺，不再把触手可及的世界当作唯一算数的世界。用神秘主义者的话说，你已变得「摆脱了无序的依恋」（free from inordinate attachments），「占有的灼热」不再烧灼你；正因如此，你拥有极大的内在自由，一种开阔与安宁之感。意志、心灵与心智既已从长久支配它们的种种执念中释放出来，如今便一齐转向你最深层存在的诸般目的：「聚集于心灵的合一之中」，它们已熔铸为一体，成为你的最深层存在借以行动的器官。

What form, then, shall this action take? It shall take a practical form, shall express itself in terms of movement: the pressing outwards of the whole personality, the eager and trustful stretching of it towards the fresh universe which awaits you. As all scattered thinking was cut off in recollection, as all vagrant and unworthy desires have been killed by the exercises of detachment; so now all scattered willing, all hesitations between the indrawing and outflowing instincts of the soul, shall be checked and resolved. You are to *push* with all your power: not to absorb ideas, but to pour forth will and love. With this “conative act,” as the psychologists would call it, the true contemplative life begins. Contemplation, you see, has no very close connection with dreaminess and idle musing: it is more like the intense effort of vision, the passionate and self-forgetful act of communion, presupposed in all creative art. It is, says one old English mystic, “a blind intent stretching … a privy love pressed” in the direction of Ultimate Beauty, athwart all the checks, hindrances, and contradictions of the restless world: a “loving stretching out” towards Reality, says the great Ruysbroeck, than whom none has gone further on this path. Tension, ardour, are of its essence: it demands the perpetual exercise of industry and courage.

We observe in such definitions as these a strange neglect of that glory of man, the Pure Intellect, with which the spiritual prig enjoys to believe that he can climb up to the Empyrean itself. It almost seems as though the mystics shared Keats’ view of the supremacy of feeling over thought; and reached out towards some new and higher range of sensation, rather than towards new and more accurate ideas. They are ever eager to assure us that man’s most sublime thoughts of the Transcendent are but a little better than his worst: that loving intuition is the only certain guide. “By love may He be gotten and holden, but by thought never.”

Yet here you are not to fall into the clumsy error of supposing that the things which are beyond the grasp of reason are necessarily unreasonable things. Immediate feeling, so far as it is true, does not oppose but transcends and completes the highest results of thought. It contains within itself the sum of all the processes through which thought would pass in the act of attaining the same goal: supposing thought to have reached—as it has not—the high pitch at which it was capable of thinking its way all along this road.

In the preliminary act of gathering yourself together, and in those unremitting explorations through which you came to “a knowing and a feeling of yourself as you are,” thought assuredly had its place. There the powers of analysis, criticism, and deduction found work that they could do. But now it is the love and will—the feeling, the intent, the passionate desire—of the self, which shall govern your activities and make possible your success. Few would care to brave the horrors of a courtship conducted upon strictly intellectual lines: and contemplation is an act of love, the wooing, not the critical study, of Divine Reality. It is an eager outpouring of ourselves towards a Somewhat Other for which we feel a passion of desire; a seeking, touching, and tasting, not a considering and analysing, of the beautiful and true wherever found. It is, as it were, a responsive act of the organism to those Supernal Powers without, which touch and stir it. Deep humility as towards those Powers, a willing surrender to their control, is the first condition of success. The mystics speak much of these elusive contacts; felt more and more in the soul, as it becomes increasingly sensitive to the subtle movements of its spiritual environment.

那么，这行动当取何种形式？它当取一种实际的形式，当以运动来表达自身：整个人格向外推进，热切而满怀信赖地向那等候着你的崭新宇宙伸展。正如一切散乱的思维已在收敛心神中被截断，一切游移而不配的欲望已在舍弃（detachment）的操练中被灭除；如今，一切散乱的意愿，灵魂那内敛与外流两种本能之间的一切踌躇，也都当受到检束而得以化解。你要去*推*，以你全部的力量：不是去吸纳观念，而是要倾泻出意志与爱。真正的默观生活，便始于这被心理学家称为「意动行动」（conative act）的行动。你看，默观与睡梦般的恍惚及无所事事的闲想并无多大干系：它更接近那种全力以赴的观看之努力，更接近那在一切创造性艺术中都预设了的、热切而忘我的契合行动。一位古英格兰神秘主义者说，它是「一种盲目的意向之伸展……一种隐秘之爱的迸逼」，朝着终极之美的方向，横越那躁动不安的世界的一切阻遏、障碍与矛盾；伟大的吕斯布鲁克则说，它是朝向实在的一种「爱中的伸展出去」——在这条路上，无人比他走得更远。张力与热忱是它的本质：它要求勤勉与勇气的不懈运用。

在诸如此类的定义里，我们注意到一种对人之光荣——纯粹理智——的奇特忽视；灵性上的自命不凡者正乐于相信，自己能凭这理智攀上净火天（Empyrean）本身。这几乎使人觉得，神秘主义者竟与济慈同一立场，主张感受高于思想；他们所伸向的，是某种新的、更高的感觉领域，而非新的、更准确的观念。他们总是急切地向我们保证：人对超越者所怀的最崇高的思想，也只比他最坏的思想略胜一筹；唯有爱的直观才是确实可靠的向导。「凭爱可以得着祂、持守祂；凭思想则永不能。」

然而，在此你不可陷入一种笨拙的错误，以为凡超出理性把握之外的事物，就必然是不合理性的事物。直接的感受，就其为真而言，并不与思想最高的成果相对立，而是超越它、成全它。它自身之内就包含着思想在达成同一目标的行动中所要经历的全部过程的总和——这里姑且假定思想已达到那样的高度，足以沿着这条路一路思想到底；而事实上它并未达到。

在你收拢自身的预备行动中，在你借以达到「如其实际地认识并感受你自己」的那些不懈探索中，思想无疑有其一席之地。在那里，分析、批判与推理的能力找到了它们能够胜任的工作。但如今，将支配你的种种活动、使你的成功成为可能的，乃是自我的爱与意志——自我的感受、意向与炽热的渴望。很少有人愿意去经受一场严格依理智路线进行的求爱所含的种种恐怖；而默观正是一种爱的行动，是对神性实在的求爱，而非批判性的研究。它是我们朝向某个「相异的他者」（Somewhat Other）——一个令我们怀有渴望之激情的对象——所作的热切倾注；是无论在何处发现了美与真，便去寻求、触摸、品尝，而非去思量、分析。可以说，这是生命体对那些外在的上界力量的回应行动——它们触及它，搅动它。对那些力量怀有深切的谦卑，甘愿降服于它们的支配，乃是成功的第一条件。神秘主义者对这类难以捉摸的接触谈论甚多；随着灵魂对其灵性环境的幽微动静日益敏感，这些接触在灵魂中便被感受得愈发真切。

But this new method of correspondence with the universe is not to be identified with “mere feeling” in its lowest and least orderly forms. Contemplation does not mean abject surrender to every “mystical” impression that comes in. It is no sentimental aestheticism or emotional piety to which you are being invited: nor shall the transcending of reason ever be achieved by way of spiritual silliness. All the powers of the self, raised to their intensest form, shall be used in it; though used perhaps in a new way. These, the three great faculties of love, thought, and will—with which you have been accustomed to make great show on the periphery of consciousness—you have, as it were, drawn inwards during the course of your inward retreat: and by your education in detachment have cured them of their tendency to fritter their powers amongst a multiplicity of objects. Now, at the very heart of personality, you are alone with them; you hold with you in that “Interior Castle,” and undistracted for the moment by the demands of practical existence, the three great tools wherewith the soul deals with life.

As regards the life you have hitherto looked upon as “normal,” love—understood in its widest sense, as desire, emotional inclination—has throughout directed your activities. You did things, sought things, learned things, even suffered things, because at bottom you wanted to. Will has done the work to which love spurred it: thought has assimilated the results of their activities and made for them pictures, analyses, “explanations” of the world with which they had to deal. But now your purified love discerns and desires, your will is set towards, something which thought cannot really assimilate—still less explain. “Contemplation,” says Ruysbroeck, “is a knowing that is in no wise … therein all the workings of the reason fail.” That reason has been trained to deal with the stuff of temporal existence. It will only make mincemeat of your experience of Eternity if you give it a chance; trimming, transforming, rationalising that ineffable vision, trying to force it into a symbolic system with which the intellect can cope. This is why the great contemplatives utter again and again their solemn warning against the deceptiveness of thought when it ventures to deal with the spiritual intuitions of man; crying with the author of *The Cloud of Unknowing*, “Look that *nothing* live in thy working mind but a naked intent stretching”—the voluntary tension of your ever-growing, ever-moving personality pushing out towards the Real. “Love, and *do* what you like,” said the wise Augustine: so little does mere surface activity count, against the deep motive that begets it.

然而，这种与宇宙相对应的新方法，绝不可与那最低级、最无秩序形态下的「单纯感觉」混为一谈。默观并不意味着对每一条闯进来的「神秘」印象都卑屈地降服。此刻邀你加入的，绝非感伤的唯美主义或情绪化的虔敬：理性的超越，也绝不能借属灵的愚妄来达成。自我的一切力量都将提升到其最炽烈的形态而在其中运用；只是其运用方式或许是新的。这三大官能——爱、思想与意志——你一向惯于在意识的外围倚仗它们大出风头；而在你向内隐退的进程中，可以说你已把它们收摄于内，并借着舍弃方面的教育，治好了它们动辄把力量虚掷于众多对象的痼疾。如今，在人格的正中心，你与它们独自相处；你把它们带在身边——就在那座「灵心城堡」（Interior Castle）里——暂时不受实际生存之种种要求的搅扰：这便是灵魂用以应对生活的三件伟大工具。

至于你一向视为「正常」的那种生活，爱——就其最广的意义、即欲望与情感倾向而言——始终支配着你的种种活动。你做种种事、求种种物、学种种东西，甚至受种种苦，归根结底都是因为你自己想要。意志做了爱所鞭策它去做的工作；思想则消化了这两者活动的成果，并替它们造出种种图景、分析与「解释」——针对它们不得不与之打交道的世界。但如今，你那纯净了的爱所辨识、所渴求的，你的意志所指向的，乃是某种思想无法真正消化——更谈不上解释——的东西。吕斯布鲁克说：「默观乃是一种决不……的认识；在其中，理性的一切运作尽皆失效。」理性所受的训练，本是对付暂世存在的质料。你若给它机会，它只会把你的永恒经验剁得粉碎：对那不可言说的异象裁削剪裁、改造翻新、加以理性化，竭力把它塞进一个理智所能应付的象征体系。正因如此，那些伟大的默观者才一次次发出庄严的警告，提防思想在斗胆处理人的灵性直观时的欺瞒性；他们与《未知之云》的作者一同疾呼：「务要留意，在你那运转着的心思里，*不可*让任何事物存活，只容一种赤裸的意向之伸展」——这伸展，便是你那不断生长、不断运动的人格向实在推出的自愿张力。智慧的奥古斯丁说：「去爱吧，然后随你*喜欢*去做什么」：与那生发行动的深层动机相比，单纯的表层活动算得了什么。

The dynamic power of love and will, the fact that the heart’s desire—if it be intense and industrious—is a better earnest of possible fulfilment than the most elegant theories of the spiritual world; this is the perpetual theme of all the Christian mystics. By such love, they think, the worlds themselves were made. By an eager outstretching towards Reality, they tell us, we tend to move towards Reality, to enter into its rhythm: by a humble and unquestioning surrender to it we permit its entrance into our souls. This twofold act, in which we find the double character of all true love—which both gives and takes, yields and demands—is assured, if we be patient and single-hearted, of ultimate success. At last our ignorance shall be done away; and we shall “apprehend” the real and the eternal, as we apprehend the sunshine when the sky is free from cloud. Therefore “Smite upon that thick cloud of unknowing with a sharp dart of longing love”—and suddenly it shall part, and disclose the blue.

“Smite,” “press,” “push,” “strive”—these are strong words: yet they are constantly upon the lips of the contemplatives when describing the earlier stages of their art. Clearly, the abolition of discursive thought is not to absolve you from the obligations of industry. You are to “energise enthusiastically” upon new planes, where you shall see more intensely, hear more intensely, touch and taste more intensely than ever before: for the modes of communion which these senses make possible to you are now to operate as parts of the one single state of perfect intuition, of loving knowledge by union, to which you are growing up. And gradually you come to see that, if this be so, it is the ardent will that shall be the prime agent of your undertaking: a will which has now become the active expression of your deepest and purest desires. About this the recollected and simplified self is to gather itself as a centre; and thence to look out—steadily, deliberately—with eyes of love towards the world.

To “look with the eyes of love” seems a vague and sentimental recommendation: yet the whole art of spiritual communion is summed in it, and exact and important results flow from this exercise. The attitude which it involves is an attitude of complete humility and of receptiveness; without criticism, without clever analysis of the thing seen. When you look thus, you surrender your I-hood; see things at last as the artist does, for their sake, not for your own. The fundamental unity that is in you reaches out to the unity that is in them: and you achieve the “Simple Vision” of the poet and the mystic—that synthetic and undistorted apprehension of things which is the antithesis of the single vision of practical men. The doors of perception are cleansed, and everything appears as it is. The disfiguring results of hate, rivalry, prejudice, vanish away. Into that silent place to which recollection has brought you, new music, new colour, new light, are poured from the outward world. The conscious love which achieves this vision may, indeed must, fluctuate—“As long as thou livest thou art subject to mutability; yea, though thou wilt not!” But the *will* which that love has enkindled can hold attention in the right direction. It can refuse to relapse to unreal and egotistic correspondences; and continue, even in darkness, and in the suffering which such darkness brings to the awakened spirit, its appointed task, cutting a way into new levels of Reality.

Therefore this transitional stage in the development of the contemplative powers—in one sense the completion of their elementary schooling, in another the beginning of their true activities—is concerned with the toughening and further training of that will which self-simplification has detached from its old concentration upon the unreal wants and interests of the self. Merged with your intuitive love, this is to become the true agent of your encounter with Reality; for that Simple Eye of Intention, which is so supremely your own, and in the last resort the maker of your universe and controller of your destiny, is nothing else but a synthesis of such energetic will and such uncorrupt desire, turned and held in the direction of the Best.

爱与意志的动力性力量，以及这一事实——心灵的热望，倘若炽烈而勤勉，便是关于可能成全的更好的保证，胜过关于灵性世界的最优雅的理论——这就是一切基督教神秘主义者永恒不变的主题。他们认为，诸世界本身就是借着这样的爱造成的。他们告诉我们：借着朝向实在的热切伸展，我们便趋于向实在运动，得以进入它的节律；借着对它谦卑而无质疑的降服，我们容许它进入我们的灵魂。这双重行动——我们在其中见到一切真爱的双重品格：既给予又领受，既退让又要求——只要我们有耐心、专心一意，就必得最终的成全。我们的无知终将被废除；我们将「把握」那真实与永恒者，正如天空无云时我们把握那阳光。因此，「当用渴慕之爱的锐利飞镖，击刺那厚重的未知之云」——云霎时间便要裂开，露出蔚蓝。

「击刺」「迫进」「推进」「奋勉」——这些都是强硬的字眼；然而默观者们在描述其艺术的较早阶段时，口中不断出现的正是它们。显然，推论式思维的废止，并不能解除你勤奋的义务。你要在新的平面上「热忱地焕发力量」；在那里，你将比以往任何时候都更强烈地看、更强烈地听、更强烈地触摸与品尝：因为这些感官如今使你得以实现的诸般契合方式，正要作为一个完全直观——即借着合一而有的爱中之知——那单一状态的诸部分而运作，你正是向着这状态生长。渐渐地你会看出：倘若事情果真如此，那炽烈的意志将成为你这一事业的首要行动者——这意志如今已成为你最深沉、最纯洁的欲望的主动表达。那收敛了、单纯化了的自我，要以此为中心而聚拢自身；并要从这里，以爱的眼睛，稳定地、审慎地，向世界展望。

「以爱的眼睛观看」，听起来像一句含混而感伤的劝言；然而灵性契合的全部艺术正总括于此，而这操练也涌流出确切而重要的成果。它所涉及的态度，是一种全然谦卑与领受的态度；不加批评，对所见之物不作机巧的分析。当你这样观看时，你交出了你的「我性」（I-hood）；终于像艺术家那样观看事物，为了事物自身的缘故，而非为你自己的缘故。你之内的根本合一，伸向它们之内的合一：于是你获得了诗人与神秘主义者的「单纯视象」（Simple Vision）——对事物的那种综合的、未经歪曲的把握，它与讲求实际的人的「单一视觉」恰成对立。知觉之门既经洗净，万物便如其本然地显现。仇恨、竞胜与偏见那些丑化事物的结果，尽都消散。在收敛心神带你抵达的那寂静之地，新的音乐、新的色彩、新的光，正从外部世界倾注进来。成就这视象的自觉之爱，可能——事实上也必定——起伏波动：「你只要活着，就受制于变易；是啊，纵然你不愿意！」但那爱所点燃的*意志*，却能把注意力持守在正确的方向上。它能拒绝退回到非实在的、唯我的对应方式；并能继续它被指派的任务，哪怕身处黑暗之中，身处这黑暗带给那已觉醒之灵的痛苦之中，也要劈开一条路，进入实在的诸般新层面。

因此，默观能力发展中的这一过渡阶段——在一种意义上是它们初等学业的完成，在另一种意义上是它们真正活动的开端——所关切的，是那意志的坚韧化与进一步的训练；自我单纯化已使这意志脱离了它旧日对自我的种种非实在欲求与利益的专注。这意志与你的直觉之爱相融合，便要成为你与实在相遇的真正行动者；因为那单纯意向之眼（Simple Eye of Intention）——它绝顶地属于你自己，并在最终的意义上是你这宇宙的创造者、你命运的支配者——无非是这样一种充满能量的意志与这样一种未经败坏的渴望的综合，被转向并持守于至善的方向。
