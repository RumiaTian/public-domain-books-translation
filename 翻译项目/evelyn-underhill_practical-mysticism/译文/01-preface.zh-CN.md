## Preface / 序言

===Original===
This little book, written during the last months of peace, goes to press in the first weeks of the great war. Many will feel that in such a time of conflict and horror, when only the most ignorant, disloyal, or apathetic can hope for quietness of mind, a book which deals with that which is called the “contemplative” attitude to existence is wholly out of place. So obvious, indeed, is this point of view, that I had at first thought of postponing its publication. On the one hand, it seems as though the dreams of a spiritual renaissance, which promised so fairly but a little time ago, had perished in the sudden explosion of brute force. On the other hand, the thoughts of the English race are now turned, and rightly, towards the most concrete forms of action﻿—struggle and endurance, practical sacrifices, difficult and long-continued effort﻿—rather than towards the passive attitude of self-surrender which is all that the practice of mysticism seems, at first sight, to demand. Moreover, that deep conviction of the dependence of all human worth upon eternal values, the immanence of the Divine Spirit within the human soul, which lies at the root of a mystical concept of life, is hard indeed to reconcile with much of the human history now being poured red-hot from the cauldron of war. For all these reasons, we are likely during the present crisis to witness a revolt from those superficially mystical notions which threatened to become too popular during the immediate past.

这本小书写于和平岁月的最后几个月，而今在大战爆发的最初几周付印。许多人会觉得：在这样一个冲突与恐怖的时刻，唯有无知、不忠或冷漠至极的人方能指望心境的安宁，此时出一本探讨所谓对待生存的「默观」态度的书，实在全然不合时宜。这种看法确实如此显豁，以致我起初曾打算推迟本书的出版。一方面，不久之前还许诺得如此美好的灵性复兴之梦，仿佛已在蛮力的骤然爆发中灰飞烟灭。另一方面，英格兰民族的思虑如今已转向——而且转得很有道理——各种最为具体的行动形式：抗争与坚忍、实际的牺牲、艰难而旷日持久的努力；而不是转向自我交付的被动态度，乍看之下，神秘主义的操练所要求的似乎唯有这种态度。再者，一切人之价值皆系于永恒价值、神圣之灵内居于人的灵魂之中——这一深植于神秘主义生命观根柢的深切信念，确实难以与眼下正从战争的熔炉中炽热倾泻而出的诸多人类历史相调和。基于这一切理由，在当前的危机里，我们多半将目睹人们背弃那些肤浅的神秘主义观念——就在不久之前，它们还险些蔚为风尚。



===Original===
Yet, the title deliberately chosen for this book﻿—that of “Practical” Mysticism﻿—means nothing if the attitude and the discipline which it recommends be adapted to fair weather alone: if the principles for which it stands break down when subjected to the pressure of events, and cannot be reconciled with the sterner duties of the national life. To accept this position is to reduce mysticism to the status of a spiritual plaything. On the contrary, if the experiences on which it is based have indeed the transcendent value for humanity which the mystics claim for them﻿—if they reveal to us a world of higher truth and greater reality than the world of concrete happenings in which we seem to be immersed﻿—then that value is increased rather than lessened when confronted by the overwhelming disharmonies and sufferings of the present time. It is significant that many of these experiences are reported to us from periods of war and distress: that the stronger the forces of destruction appeared, the more intense grew the spiritual vision which opposed them. We learn from these records that the mystical consciousness has the power of lifting those who possess it to a plane of reality which no struggle, no cruelty, can disturb: of conferring a certitude which no catastrophe can wreck. Yet it does not wrap its initiates in a selfish and otherworldly calm, isolate them from the pain and effort of the common life. Rather, it gives them renewed vitality; administering to the human spirit not﻿—as some suppose﻿—a soothing draught, but the most powerful of stimulants. Stayed upon eternal realities, that spirit will be far better able to endure and profit by the stern discipline which the race is now called to undergo, than those who are wholly at the mercy of events; better able to discern the real from the illusory issues, and to pronounce judgment on the new problems, new difficulties, new fields of activity now disclosed. Perhaps it is worthwhile to remind ourselves that the two women who have left the deepest mark upon the military history of France and England﻿—Joan of Arc and Florence Nightingale﻿—both acted under mystical compulsion. So, too, did one of the noblest of modern soldiers, General Gordon. Their national value was directly connected with their deep spiritual consciousness: their intensely practical energies were the flowers of a contemplative life.

然而，本书刻意择定的书名——「实用」神秘主义——若它所推崇的态度与操练仅宜于风和日暖之时，若它所代表的原则一经世事的重压便告失灵，无法与国民生活中更为严酷的义务相调和，那这书名便形同虚设。接受这种立场，便是把神秘主义贬为一件属灵的玩物。恰恰相反，如果它所依据的那些经验果真如神秘主义者所宣称的那样，对人类具有超越的价值——如果它们向我们揭示出的世界，比我们仿佛沉浸其中的具体事变世界具有更高的真理、更充盈的实在——那么，当直面当今排山倒海的不和谐与苦难时，这份价值只会愈增，绝不会稍减。值得注意的是，这些经验有许多正是在战争与困厄的年代传给我们的：毁灭的力量显得愈强大，与之相抗的灵性洞见便愈发炽烈。从这些记载中我们得知：神秘意识有能力把怀有它的人提升到一个任何斗争、任何暴行都无法扰乱的实在层面，并赋予他们一种任何灾祸都无法摧折的确信。然而它并不把它的入门者裹进一种自私而出世的宁静，不使他们与共同生活的痛苦与操劳相隔绝。毋宁说，它赐给他们焕然一新的生命力；它供给人类精神的，并非——如某些人所设想——一剂安抚的汤药，而是最为强效的振奋剂。这精神一旦安住于永恒实在之上，便远比那些全然听凭时势摆布的人更有能力承受我们的民族如今奉召去经受的严酷磨炼，并从中获益；更有能力辨明真实与虚妄的争端，对如今展露的新问题、新困难、新的行动领域作出裁断。或许值得提醒我们自己：在法兰西与英格兰的军事史上留下最深印记的两位女性——圣女贞德与弗洛伦斯·南丁格尔——都是在神秘的驱迫下行事的。现代最高贵的军人之一戈登将军亦是如此。他们对国家的价值与其深厚的灵性意识直接相连：他们那极为务实的能量，正是默观生活绽放的花朵。

We are often told, that in the critical periods of history it is the national soul which counts: that “where there is no vision, the people perish.” No nation is truly defeated which retains its spiritual self-possession. No nation is truly victorious which does not emerge with soul unstained. If this be so, it becomes a part of true patriotism to keep the spiritual life, both of the individual citizen and of the social group, active and vigorous; its vision of realities unsullied by the entangled interests and passions of the time. This is a task in which all may do their part. The spiritual life is not a special career, involving abstraction from the world of things. It is a part of every man’s life; and until he has realised it he is not a complete human being, has not entered into possession of all his powers. It is therefore the function of a practical mysticism to increase, not diminish, the total efficiency, the wisdom and steadfastness, of those who try to practise it. It will help them to enter, more completely than ever before, into the life of the group to which they belong. It will teach them to see the world in a truer proportion, discerning eternal beauty beyond and beneath apparent ruthlessness. It will educate them in a charity free from all taint of sentimentalism; it will confer on them an unconquerable hope; and assure them that still, even in the hour of greatest desolation, “There lives the dearest freshness deep down things.” As a contribution, then, to these purposes, this little book is now published. It is addressed neither to the learned nor to the devout, who are already in possession of a wide literature dealing from many points of view with the experiences and philosophy of the mystics. Such readers are warned that they will find here nothing but the restatement of elementary and familiar propositions, and invitations to a discipline immemorially old. Far from presuming to instruct those to whom firsthand information is both accessible and palatable, I write only for the larger class which, repelled by the formidable appearance of more elaborate works on the subject, would yet like to know what is meant by mysticism, and what it has to offer to the average man: how it helps to solve his problems, how it harmonises with the duties and ideals of his active life. For this reason, I presuppose in my readers no knowledge whatever of the subject, either upon the philosophic, religious, or historical side. Nor, since I wish my appeal to be general, do I urge the special claim of any one theological system, any one metaphysical school. I have merely attempted to put the view of the universe and man’s place in it which is common to all mystics in plain and untechnical language: and to suggest the practical conditions under which ordinary persons may participate in their experience. Therefore the abnormal states of consciousness which sometimes appear in connection with mystical genius are not discussed: my business being confined to the description of a faculty which all men possess in a greater or less degree.

人们常常被告知：在历史的危急时期，起决定作用的是民族的灵魂；「没有异象，民就放肆」。凡仍保有灵性自持的民族，绝不算真正战败；凡未能以无染的灵魂走出的民族，绝不算真正获胜。倘若如此，那么使个人公民与社会群体两方面的灵性生活保持活跃而旺盛、使其对实在的洞见不为时代纠缠不清的利益与激情所玷污，便成为真正爱国心的一部分。这是一项人人皆可尽其一分的任务。灵性生活并不是一种要求抽离事物世界的特殊生涯。它是每个人生命的一部分；人若尚未认识到这一点，便不是一个完整的人，便尚未领有自己的全部力量。因此，实用神秘主义的功用，正是要增进而非削弱尝试操练它者的总体效能、智慧与坚毅。它将帮助他们比以往任何时候都更完全地进入所属群体的生活。它将教他们以更真实的比例观看世界，在表面的无情之外与之下辨认出永恒的美。它将陶冶他们，使其拥有一种不带丝毫感伤主义气息的爱德；它将赋予他们一种不可征服的希望；并向他们担保：哪怕在大毁坏的时刻，「万物深处始终住着最珍贵的清新」。那么，这本小书如今刊行，便是为这些目的稍尽绵薄。本书既不是写给学者的，也不是写给虔修者的；从多种角度探讨神秘主义者的经验与哲学的浩繁文献，早已为他们所拥有。须提醒这类读者：他们在此只会看到对一些初等而熟悉的命题的重述，以及对一门渺远古老的操练的邀约。我绝不敢僭望教导那些一手材料既易得又合味的人，只为一个更广大的阶层写作：这一阶层被论述此题的更繁难之作的吓人外表所拒斥，却又愿意知道神秘主义究竟何谓、它能为普通人提供些什么：它如何帮助人解决自己的问题，又如何与人的积极生活中的义务和理想相和谐。有鉴于此，我假定读者对这个题目无论在哲学、宗教还是历史方面都一无所知。既然我希望我的呼吁面向大众，我也不力陈任何一种神学体系、任何一个形而上学学派的特殊主张。我只是尝试用平白而不用专门术语的语言，陈述一切神秘主义者共有的那种宇宙观以及人在宇宙中的位置：并提示普通人得以参与其经验的实际条件。因此，那些有时伴随神秘主义天才而出现的反常意识状态，本书不予讨论：我的任务仅限于描述一种人人或多或少都具备的能力。

The reality and importance of this faculty are considered in the first three chapters. In the fourth and fifth is described the preliminary training of attention necessary for its use; in the sixth, the general self-discipline and attitude toward life which it involves. The seventh, eighth, and ninth chapters treat in an elementary way of the three great forms of contemplation; and in the tenth, the practical value of the life in which they have been actualised is examined. Those kind enough to attempt the perusal of the book are begged to read the first sections with some attention before passing to the latter part.
E. U.
September 12, 1914.
