## III A Mysterious Summons / 三 一次神秘的召唤

===Original===
> "Without unspotted, innocent within,
> She feared no danger, for she knew no sin."

It was after a matinée performance at —— Hall some two weeks ago that I stopped to light a cigar in the small corridor leading to the back entrance. I was in a dissatisfied frame of mind. Something in the music I had been playing or the manner in which it had been received had touched unwonted chords in my own nature. I felt alone. I remember asking myself as I stood there, what it all amounted to? Who of all the applauding crowd would watch at my bedside through a long and harassing sickness, or lend their sympathy as they now yielded their praise, if instead of carrying off the honors of the day I had failed to do justice to my reputation. I was just smiling over the only exception I could make to this sweeping assertion, that of the pale-eyed youth you have sometimes observed dogging my steps, when Briggs came up to me.

===Chinese===
> 「外无瑕垢，内有天真，
> 她不畏危难，因她不知有罪。」

大约两周前，在——音乐厅的一场日场演出之后，我停在通往后门的那条小走廊里点一支雪茄。我心绪不宁。我所奏的那支乐曲中，或是它被接纳的方式中，有某种东西触动了我天性中平时不轻易拨动的弦。我感到孤独。我记得自己站在那里时曾问：这一切到底算得了什么？那一群鼓掌的人当中，有谁会在一场漫长而折磨人的病中守在我床边？又有谁会像此刻奉上赞辞那般奉上同情——倘若我未能保住名声、未能赢得当日的荣光？我正为这番斩钉截铁的断言中唯一的例外——便是你有时会注意到那个老跟在我身后、眼神苍白无力的青年——微微一笑，布里格斯（Briggs）走上前来。

===Original===
"There is a woman here, sir, who insists on seeing you; she has been waiting through half the last piece. Shall I tell her you are coming out?"

"A woman!" exclaimed I, somewhat surprised, for my visitors are not apt to be of the gentler sex.

"Yes sir, an old one. She seems very anxious to speak to you. I could not get rid of her no how."

===Chinese===
「先生，这里有位太太，非要见您不可；最后那支曲子她都等了半场了。要我去告诉她您这就出来吗？」

「一个女人！」我喊道，颇为惊讶，因为我的访客向来不大会是女性。

「是的先生，一位上了年纪的。她好像急着要跟您说话。我怎么也打发不走她。」

===Original===
I hurried forward to the muffled figure which he pointed out cowering against the wall by the door. "Well, my good woman, what do you want?" I asked, bending towards her in the hopes of catching a glimpse of the face she held partly concealed from me.

"Are you Mr. Mandeville?" she inquired in a tone shaken as much by agitation as age.

I bowed.

"The one who plays upon the piano?"

"The very same," I declared.

"You are not deceiving me," she went on, looking up with a marked anxiety plainly visible through her veil. "I haven't seen you play and couldn't contradict you, but—"

"Here!" said I calling to Briggs with a kindly look at the old woman, "help me on with my coat, will you?"

The "Certainly, Mr. Mandeville," with which he complied seemed to reassure her, and as soon as the coat was on and he was gone, she grasped me by the arm and drew my ear down to her mouth.

===Chinese===
我快步走向他所指的那个裹得严严的身影，她正蜷缩在门边的墙根。「好了，好婆婆，你有什么事？」我俯身向她问道，想借机瞥一眼她有意对我半遮半掩的面容。

「您是曼德维尔先生吗？」她问道，那声音因激动与年迈而同样地颤动着。

我鞠了一躬。

「是弹钢琴的那一位？」

「正是在下。」我答道。

「您没有骗我，」她接着说，抬起头来，隔着面纱也分明看得出那种显著的焦虑。「我没见过您弹琴，也没法反驳您，不过——」

「布里格斯！」我招呼他，同时和善地看了那老妇人一眼，「帮我把外套穿上，好吗？」

他那声「好的，曼德维尔先生」似乎使她安下心来，等外套一穿好、他一走开，她便一把抓住我的胳膊，把我的耳朵拉到她嘴边。

===Original===
"If you are Mr. Mandeville, I have a message for you. This letter," slipping one into my hand, "is from a young lady, sir. She bade me give it to you myself. She is young and pretty," she pursued as she saw me make a movement of distaste, "and a lady. We depend upon your honor, sir."

I acknowledge that my first impulse was to fling her back the note and leave the building; I was in no mood for trifling, my next to burst into a laugh and politely hand her to the door, my last and best, to open the poor little note and see for myself whether the writer was a lady or not. Proceeding to the door, for it was already twilight in the dim passage way, I tore open the envelope which was dainty enough and took out a sheet of closely written paper. A certain qualm of conscience assailed me as I saw the delicate chirography it disclosed and I was tempted to thrust it back and return it unread to the old woman now trembling in the corner. But curiosity overcame my scruples, and hastily unfolding the sheet I read these lines:

===Chinese===
「您若是曼德维尔先生，我便有一桩口信给您。这封信，」她把一封信塞进我手里，「是一位小姐写的，先生。她吩咐我亲手交给您。她又年轻又漂亮，」见我露出不悦之色她追了一句，「而且是一位小姐。我们全凭您的君子之风了，先生。」

我承认我的头一个念头是把信掷还给她、径自离开；我全无逢场作戏的心情；第二个念头是大笑一声、客客气气地把她送出门；最后一个，也是最好的一个念头，是拆开这可怜的小信，亲自看看写信的人是不是一位小姐。我走向门口——昏暗的过道里已是薄暮时分——撕开了那相当精致的信封，抽出一张写满了密密麻麻小字的信纸。看到纸上那娟秀的字迹，我心中涌起一阵良心上的不安，竟想把信塞回去，原封不动地还给此刻在角落里瑟瑟发抖的老妇人。但好奇心战胜了顾忌，我匆匆展开信纸，读到这样几行：

===Original===
> "I do not know if what I do is right; I am sure aunty would not say it was; but aunty never thinks anything is right but going to church and reading the papers to papa. I am just a little girl who has heard you play, and who would think the world was too beautiful, if she could hear you say to her just once, some of the kind things you must speak every day to the persons who know you. I do not expect very much—you must have a great many friends, and you would not care for me—but the least little look, if it were all my own, would make me so happy and so proud I should not envy anybody in the world, unless it was some of those dear friends who see you always.
> "I do not come and hear you play often, for aunty thinks music frivolous, but I am always hearing you no matter where I am, and it makes me feel as if I were far away from everybody, in a beautiful land all sunshine and flowers. But nurse says I must not write so much or you will not read it, so I will stop here. But if you *would* come it would make someone happier than even your beautiful music could do."

===Chinese===
> 「我不知道我这样做对不对；我敢肯定姑姑不会说它对；可姑姑除了去做礼拜、给爸爸读报纸，从不觉得什么事是对的。我只是一个小姑娘，听过您弹琴；倘若她能听您对她说上一次——哪怕只有一次——您每天必定对认识您的人说的那些亲切话语，她便会觉得这世界美得不像话了。我不敢奢望太多——您一定有很多朋友，您也不会在意我——但哪怕是最小的一个眼神，只要它是完全属于我的，就会让我那样快乐、那样骄傲，全世界的人我都不羡慕了，至多只羡慕那些总能见到您的亲爱的朋友们。
> 「我不常来听您弹琴，因为姑姑觉得音乐轻浮，但无论我在哪里，我总听见您；那使我感到仿佛自己远离了一切人，置身于一片阳光与鲜花的美地。可是保姆说我不能写这么多，否则您就不看了，所以我就写到这里。可要是您*肯*来，那会使一个人比您最美的音乐所能做到的还要快乐。」

===Original===
That was all; there was neither name nor date. A child's epistle, written with a woman's circumspection. With mingled sensations of doubt and curiosity I turned back to the old woman who stood awaiting me with eager anxiety.

"Was this written by a child or woman?" I asked, meeting her eye with as much sternness as I could assume.

"Don't ask me—don't ask me anything. I have promised to bring you if I could, but I cannot answer any questions."

===Chinese===
就这些；既没有署名，也没有日期。一封孩子的信，却带着一个成年女子的谨慎。怀着疑虑与好奇交织的心情，我转向那老妇人，她正焦灼地等着我的回答。

「这是一个孩子写的，还是一个女人写的？」我问道，尽可能板起脸与她对视。

「别问我——什么也别问我。我答应过，只要能便把您请去，可我什么问题也不能回答。」

===Original===
I stepped back with an incredulous laugh. Here was evidently an adventure. "You will at least tell me where the young miss lives," said I, "before I undertake to fulfil her request."

She shook her head. "I have a carriage at the door, sir," said she. "All you have got to do is to get into it with me and we shall soon be at the house."

===Chinese===
我退后一步，发出一声难以置信的笑。这显然是一桩奇遇了。「在我答应她的请求之前，你至少该告诉我这位小姐住在哪里吧。」我说。

她摇了摇头。「门口有马车等着呢，先生，」她说，「您只消跟我一起上车，我们很快就能到那宅子。」

===Original===
I looked from her face to the letter in my hand, and knew not what to think. The spirit of simplicity and ingenuousness that marked the latter was scarcely in keeping with this air of mystery. The woman observing my hesitation moved towards the door.

"Will you come, sir?" she inquired. "You will not regret it. Just a moment's talk with a pretty young girl—surely—"

"Hush," said I, hearing a hasty step behind me. And sure enough just then my intimate friend Selby came along and grasping me by the arm began dragging me towards the door. "You are my property," said he. "I've promised, on my word of honor as a gentleman and a musician, to bring you to the Handel Club this afternoon. I was afraid you had escaped me, but—" Here he caught sight of the small black figure halting in the doorway, and paused.

"Who's this?" said he.

===Chinese===
我从她的脸看到手中的信，不知该作何想法。信中所透出的那份质朴与天真，与眼前这种神秘气氛实在不相称。那妇人看出我的犹豫，便朝门边走去。

「您来吗，先生？」她问。「您不会后悔的。不过同一个漂亮小姑娘说几句话——总归——」

「嘘，」我说，听见身后有一阵急促的脚步声。果不其然，这时我的挚友塞尔比（Selby）走了过来，一把抓住我的胳膊，拽着我朝门口走。「你是我的，」他说，「我以绅士与音乐家的名誉担保过，今天下午要把你带到亨德尔俱乐部去。我还怕你溜了呢，不过——」说到这里，他瞥见那个停在门口的矮小黑影，便顿住了。

「这是谁？」他问。

===Original===
I hesitated. For one instant the scale of my whole future destiny hung trembling in the balance, then the demon of curiosity got the better of my judgment, and with the rather unworthy consideration that I might as well enjoy my youth while I could, I released myself from my friend's detaining hand and replied, "Someone with whom I have very particular business. I cannot go to the Handel Club today," and darting out without further delay, I rejoined the old woman on the sidewalk.

Without a word she drew me towards a carriage I now observed standing by the curbstone a few feet to the left. As I got in I remember pausing a moment to glance at the man on the box, but it was too dark for me to perceive anything but the fact that he was dressed in livery. More and more astonished I leaned back in my seat and endeavored to open conversation with my mysterious companion. But it did not work. Without being actually rude, she parried my questions in such a way that by the end of five minutes I found myself as far from any knowledge of the real situation of the case as when I started. I therefore desisted from any further attempts and turned to look out, when I made a discovery that for the first time awoke some vague feelings of alarm within my breast. This was, that the window was not covered by a curtain as I supposed, but by closed blinds which when I tried to raise them resisted all my efforts to do so.

===Chinese===
我犹豫了。有那么一刹那，我一生的命运如同一架颤抖的天平悬而未决；随后好奇的魔鬼压过了我的判断力，我怀着一种颇为卑下的念头——趁还能享受青春便享受罢——挣脱了朋友拉住我的手，答道：「我有一桩非常要紧的事要见的人。今天不能去亨德尔俱乐部了。」说罢便不再耽搁地冲了出去，重新回到人行道上那老妇人身旁。

她一言不发，把我拉向一辆我此时才注意到的、停在左边几步外路边的马车。我上车时记得停了一停，朝车夫座上那人瞥了一眼，但天色太暗，我只看出他穿着号衣。我越发惊异，靠在座位上，试图与我这位神秘的同伴攀谈。可这没有用。她虽不至真的失礼，却把我的问题挡得滴水不漏，等到五分钟过去，我发现自己对实情的了解，竟与刚出发时一样茫然。于是我不再尝试，转头朝外看去——这时我发现了一件事，头一次在我胸中唤起一阵隐隐的惊惶：那窗户遮蔽的并非如我所想的是一道帘子，而是紧闭的百叶窗，我试图把它们拉起来时，它们纹丝不动。

===Original===
"It is very close here," I muttered, in some sort of excuse for this display of uneasiness. "Cannot you give us a little air?" But my companion remained silent, and I felt ashamed to press the matter though I took advantage of the darkness to remove to a safer place a roll of money which I had about me.

Yet I was far from being really anxious, and did not once meditate backing out of an adventure that was at once so piquant and romantic. For by this time I became conscious from the sounds about me that we had left the side street for one of the avenues and were then proceeding rapidly up town. Listening, I heard the roll of omnibuses and the jingle of car-bells, which informed me that we were in Broadway, no other avenue in the city being traversed by both these methods of conveyance. But after awhile the jingle ceased and presently the livelier sounds of constant commotion inseparable from a business thoroughfare, and we entered what I took to be Madison Avenue at Twenty-third Street.

===Chinese===
「这里头好闷，」我咕哝了一句，多少算是为自己这份不安的流露找个借口。「能不能给我们透点气？」可我的同伴一言不发，我也不好意思再追问，只是趁着黑暗把身上带着的一卷钞票挪到了更稳妥的地方。

不过我远远谈不上当真焦虑，也压根没想过要退出这场既刺激又浪漫的奇遇。因为这时我从周围的声音中觉察到，我们已经从那条小街拐上了一条大道，正迅速向城北驶去。我侧耳倾听，听见公共马车的辚辚声与有轨马车的铃铛叮当声，这告诉我我们在百老汇（Broadway）——城里没有第二条大道同时通行这两种交通工具。但过了一阵，叮当声止息，不久，那条商业大街所特有的、一刻不停的喧嚣的更活泼的声响也消失了，我们驶入的，据我推断是二十三街处的麦迪逊大道（Madison Avenue）。

===Original===
Instantly I made up mind to notice every turn of the carriage, that I might fix to some degree the locality towards which we were tending. But it turned but once and that after a distance of steady travelling that quite overthrew any calculation I was able to make at that time of the probable number of streets we had passed since entering the avenue. Having turned, it went but about half a block to the left when it stopped. "I shall see where I am when I get out," thought I; but in this I was mistaken.

First we had stopped in the middle of a block of houses built, as far as I could judge, all after one model. Next the fact of the front door being open, though I saw no one in the hall, somewhat disconcerted me, and I hurried across the sidewalk and up the stoop in a species of maze hardly to be expected from one of my naturally careless disposition. The next moment the door closed behind me and I found myself in a well-lighted hall whose quiet richness betokened it as belonging to a private dwelling of no mean pretensions to elegance.

This was the first surprise I received.

===Chinese===
我立刻打定主意，注意马车每一个转弯，好多少固定一下我们所去之处的方位。可它只转了一次弯，而且是在笔直行驶了一段距离之后才转的——那段距离足以彻底推翻我当时所能做出的、关于自驶上大道以来经过了多少条街的任何估算。转弯之后，它只向左走了约莫半个街区便停下了。「下车时我总能看清自己在哪儿吧，」我心想；可这一点我想错了。

首先，我们停在了一排房屋的中段，那些房屋据我判断是照同一个模子建造的。其次，前门虽开着，门厅里我却看不见一个人，这多少使我手足无措，我几乎是迷迷瞪瞪地快步穿过人行道、登上台阶的——这种状态实在不像我向来大大咧咧的性子。紧接着，门在我身后关上了，我发现自己置身于一个灯火通明的门厅，那沉静的富丽说明它属于一座对雅致颇有讲究的私人宅邸。

这便是我的头一个意外。

===Original===
"Follow me," said the old woman, hurrying me down the hall and into a small room at the end. "The young lady will be here in a moment," and without lifting her veil or affording me the least glimpse of her features, she retired, leaving me to face the situation before me as best I might.

It was anything but a pleasant one as it appeared to me at that moment, and for an instant I seriously thought of retracing my steps and leaving a domicile into which I had been introduced in such a mysterious manner. Then the quiet aspect of the room, which though sparsely furnished with a piano and chairs was still of an order rarely seen out of gentlemen's houses, struck my imagination and reawakened my curiosity, and nerving myself to meet whatever interview might be accorded me, I waited. It was only five minutes by the small clock ticking on the mantelpiece, but it seemed an hour before I heard a timid step at the door, and saw it swing slowly open, disclosing—well, I did not stop to inquire whether it was a child or a woman. I merely saw the shrinking modest form, the eager blushing face, and bowed almost to the ground in a sudden reverence for the sublime innocence revealed to me. Yes, it did not take a second look to read that tender countenance to its last guileless page. Had she been a woman of twenty-five I could not have mistaken her expression of pure delight and timid interest, but she was only sixteen, as I afterwards learned, and younger in experience than in age.

===Chinese===
「跟我来，」那老妇人说，催着我穿过门厅，走进尽头的一间小屋。「小姐一会儿就来，」她说着便退了出去，既不揭开面纱、也不让我瞥见她容貌的一丝半点，把我丢在那里，让我自己尽量去面对眼前的处境。

那一刻我觉得这处境怎么也算不上愉快，有一瞬间我当真认真地想过转身离去，离开这座以如此神秘方式把我引介进来的宅子。然而那房间宁静的面貌——虽说陈设简素，只有一架钢琴和几把椅子，却仍是绅士之家之外罕见的格调——触动了我的想象，重新唤起了我的好奇，我壮起胆子准备应对任何可能获准的会面，便等着。壁炉台上那只小钟滴答了不过五分钟，我却觉得过了一个钟头，才听到门口怯怯的脚步声，看见门缓缓打开，露出——唉，我根本没停下追究来的是孩子还是女人。我只看见那瑟缩而矜持的身形、那张热切涨红的脸，便几乎触地般深深鞠了一躬，出于对向我展现的那份崇高的天真之骤然的敬意。是的，不用看第二眼便能把那张温柔的脸读到最后一页无邪。纵然她是二十五岁的女子，我也不可能误解她那种纯粹的喜悦与怯生生的关切；可她只有十六岁——这是我后来才知道的——而且阅历之浅甚于年纪之小。

===Original===
Closing the door behind her, she stood for a moment without speaking, then with a deepening of the blush which was only a child's embarrassment in the presence of a stranger, looked up and murmured my name with a word or so of grateful acknowledgment that would have called forth a smile on my lips if I had not been startled by the sudden change that passed over her features when she met my eyes. Was it that I showed my surprise too plainly, or did my admiration manifest itself in my gaze? an admiration great as it was humble, and which was already of a nature such as I had never before given to girl or woman. Whatever it was, she no sooner met my look than she paused, trembled, and started back with a confused murmur, through which I plainly heard her whisper in a low distressed tone, "Oh, what have I done!"

"Called a good friend to your side," said I in the frank, brotherly way I thought most likely to reassure her. "Do not be alarmed, I am only too happy to meet one who evidently enjoys music so well."

===Chinese===
她在身后关上门，站了片刻没说话，随即那红晕更深了一层——那不过是孩子面对生人时的窘迫——她抬起头，低声唤出我的名字，带着一两个字的感激致意；这原本会令我嘴角绽出微笑，若非她迎上我的目光时，脸上忽地掠过那一种变化叫我吃了一惊。是我把自己的惊讶表现得太露骨了，还是我的倾慕流露在了我的目光里？那种倾慕，既巨大又谦卑，而且已是我从未施与任何女孩或女子的一种。无论是什么，她一碰上我的目光便顿住、发颤，倒退一步，发出一声慌乱的低语，我清清楚楚听见她以痛苦的低声喃喃道：「哦，我都做了什么呀！」

「把一位好朋友叫到了你身边，」我以坦率的、兄长般的口吻说，我觉得这最能令她安心。「别害怕，能见到一位显然如此热爱音乐的人，我真是再高兴不过了。」
