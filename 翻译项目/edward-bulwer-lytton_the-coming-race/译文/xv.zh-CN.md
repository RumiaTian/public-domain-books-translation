## XV / 十五

===Original===
Kind to me as I found all in this household, the young daughter of my host was the most considerate and thoughtful in her kindness. At her suggestion I laid aside the habiliments in which I had descended from the upper earth, and adopted the dress of the Vril-ya, with the exception of the artful wings which served them, when on foot, as a graceful mantle. But as many of the Vril-ya, when occupied in urban pursuits, did not wear these wings, this exception created no marked difference between myself and the race among which I sojourned, and I was thus enabled to visit the town without exciting unpleasant curiosity. Out of the household no one suspected that I had come from the upper world, and I was but regarded as one of some inferior and barbarous tribe whom Aph-Lin entertained as a guest.

The city was large in proportion to the territory round it, which was of no greater extent than many an English or Hungarian nobleman’s estate; but the whole of it, to the verge of the rocks which constituted its boundary, was cultivated to the nicest degree, except where certain allotments of mountain and pasture were humanely left free to the sustenance of the harmless animals they had tamed, though not for domestic use. So great is their kindness towards these humbler creatures, that a sum is devoted from the public treasury for the purpose of deporting them to other Vril-ya communities willing to receive them (chiefly new colonies), whenever they become too numerous for the pastures allotted to them in their native place. They do not, however, multiply to an extent comparable to the ratio at which, with us, animals bred for slaughter, increase. It seems a law of nature that animals not useful to man gradually recede from the domains he occupies, or even become extinct. It is an old custom of the various sovereign states amidst which the race of the Vril-ya are distributed, to leave between each state a neutral and uncultivated borderland. In the instance of the community I speak of, this tract, being a ridge of savage rocks, was impassable by foot, but was easily surmounted, whether by the wings of the inhabitants or the air-boats, of which I shall speak hereafter. Roads through it were also cut for the transit of vehicles impelled by *vril*. These intercommunicating tracts were always kept lighted, and the expense thereof defrayed by a special tax, to which all the communities comprehended in the denomination of Vril-ya contribute in settled proportions. By these means a considerable commercial traffic with other states, both near and distant, was carried on. The surplus wealth of this special community was chiefly agricultural. The community was also eminent for skill in constructing implements connected with the arts of husbandry. In exchange for such merchandise it obtained articles more of luxury than necessity. There were few things imported on which they set a higher price than birds taught to pipe artful tunes in concert. These were brought from a great distance, and were marvellous for beauty of song and plumage. I understand that extraordinary care was taken by their breeders and teachers in selection, and that the species had wonderfully improved during the last few years. I saw no other pet animals among this community except some very amusing and sportive creatures of the Batrachian species, resembling frogs, but with very intelligent countenances, which the children were fond of, and kept in their private gardens. They appear to have no animals akin to our dogs or horses, though that learned naturalist, Zee, informed me that such creatures had once existed in those parts, and might now be found in regions inhabited by other races than the Vril-ya. She said that they had gradually disappeared from the more civilised world since the discovery of *vril*, and the results attending that discovery had dispensed with their uses. Machinery and the invention of wings had superseded the horse as a beast of burden; and the dog was no longer wanted either for protection or the chase, as it had been when the ancestors of the Vril-ya feared the aggressions of their own kind, or hunted the lesser animals for food. Indeed, however, so far as the horse was concerned, this region was so rocky that a horse could have been, there, of little use either for pastime or burden. The only creature they use for the latter purpose is a kind of large goat which is much employed on farms. The nature of the surrounding soil in these districts may be said to have first suggested the invention of wings and air-boats. The largeness of space in proportion to the space occupied by the city, was occasioned by the custom of surrounding every house with a separate garden. The broad main street, in which Aph-Lin dwelt, expanded into a vast square, in which were placed the College of Sages and all the public offices; a magnificent fountain of the luminous fluid which I call naphtha (I am ignorant of its real nature) in the centre. All these public edifices have a uniform character of massiveness and solidity. They reminded me of the architectural pictures of Martin. Along the upper stories of each ran a balcony, or rather a terraced garden, supported by columns, filled with flowering plants, and tenanted by many kinds of tame birds. From the square branched several streets, all broad and brilliantly lighted, and ascending up the eminence on either side. In my excursions in the town I was never allowed to go alone; Aph-Lin or his daughter was my habitual companion. In this community the adult *Gy* is seen walking with any young *An* as familiarly as if there were no difference of sex.

这户人家的人待我都甚友善，而主人家的年轻女儿，其好意的体贴与周到又是最深的。在她的提议下，我脱下了从上界下来时所穿的那身衣装，改穿了弗里尔-雅人（the Vril-ya）的服装，只是没有用他们那副精巧的双翼——步行时，双翼于他们乃是一件优美的披风。不过，弗里尔-雅人有许多在城市事务中忙碌的，并不佩这双翼，故而这一例外并未使我与我客居的这个种族之间生出显著的差别；我于是得以走访城中，而不致惹起令人不快的好奇。这户人家之外，无人疑心我是从上界来的；我只不过被当作某个低劣蛮野部落的一员——阿弗-林（Aph-Lin）待之为客而已。

这座城与四围疆域相比颇为宏大，而那疆域的广袤，也不过同英格兰或匈牙利许多贵族的领地相当；但其全部土地，直到构成边界的岩壁边缘为止，都耕治得精致入微，只有某些山地与草场的划块被仁厚地留置下来，任由那些无害的动物——他们驯养了它们，虽非供家用——自行觅食存活。他们对这些卑微生灵的仁慈如此深厚，以至于每当这些动物繁衍过多、故土划给它们的牧场不敷供养时，便从公共库府中拨出一笔款项，把它们遣送到愿意接收它们（主要是新辟的殖民地）的其他弗里尔-雅人社区去。然而它们的繁衍，并不至于同我们这里供屠宰而饲养的动物的增长比率相比拟。动物若于人无用，便逐渐退避出人所占据的地域，甚或归于灭绝——这似乎是自然的一条定律。弗里尔-雅这一种族分布所在的各个主权邦国之间，两两留有一带中立而未耕的边地，乃是一种古老的习俗。就我所说的这个社区而论，这条地带是一道荒岩的山脊，徒步无法通行，却轻易便可越过——或凭居民的翅膀，或凭飞舟，飞舟我往后还要谈到。其间也开凿了道路，供以弗里尔（vril）驱动的车辆往来。这些互相交通的道路经常保持照明，其费用由一项专门税课支付，凡在弗里尔-雅这一名目之下的所有社区，都按固定的比例捐输。凭借这些手段，与远近各邦开展了可观的商贸往来。这个特定社区的超额财富主要是农业性的。社区还以营造农艺器械的技艺著称。它以这类货品换取的物件，与其说供必需，不如说供奢华。进口货中难得有几样，比那种被教会合奏精巧曲调的鸣鸟更受他们珍视。这些鸟来自很远的地方，歌喉羽色俱极可观。据我所知，其饲养者与教师们在选育上费了非同寻常的心血，这一物种近几年来已有惊人的改良。在这个社区里，除了一些极为有趣、爱嬉戏的两栖类（Batrachian）生物——形似蛙，面相却甚聪慧，孩子们喜欢它们，养在自家的园子里——之外，我没有见过别的爱畜。他们似乎没有同我们的狗或马相近的动物，尽管那位博学的博物学家琪（Zee）告诉我，这样的生物从前在这一带地方确曾存在，如今或可在弗里尔-雅人以外其他种族所居的地域中找到。她说，自从发现弗里尔以来，它们便从较文明的世界里逐渐消失了，那一发现所带来的种种结果，使它们的用途成了多余。机械与双翼的发明，已取代了马作为驮兽的地位；狗也不再需要用来护卫或狩猎——从前弗里尔-雅人的祖先还畏惧同类的侵凌、或猎取小兽为食时，是用得着它的。不过说实在的，就马而论，这一带地域多岩，马在此地无论用于游娱或负重，本都没有多少用处。他们用于负重的唯一生物，是一种大山羊，农场上用得很多。这些地带四围土壤的性质，可以说最先启发了双翼与飞舟的发明。空地之广，与城池所占地面相比，其悬殊乃是由于每所宅子都环以一座独立花园的习俗。阿弗-林居住的那条宽阔大街，展开成一片广阔的广场，贤哲学院（College of Sages）和所有官署都设在广场上；中央有一座壮丽的喷泉，喷涌着那种我称之为石脑油的发光流体（它的真实性质我并不知道）。这些公共建筑一律具有厚重坚实的品格。它们使我想起马丁的建筑画。每座建筑的上层沿墙有一道游廊——不如说是一座阶梯园圃——由柱子支撑，满植花木，栖着许多种驯顺的鸟儿。从广场分出数条街道，全都宽阔而灯火辉朗，顺着两侧的高地徐徐上行。我在城中游历时，从不被准许独自前往；阿弗-林或他的女儿是我惯常的同伴。在这个社区里，成年的姬（Gy）与任何年轻的安（An）并肩同行，亲昵自然，仿佛两性之间不存在任何差异。

The retail shops are not very numerous; the persons who attend on a customer are all children of various ages, and exceedingly intelligent and courteous, but without the least touch of importunity or cringing. The shopkeeper himself might or might not be visible; when visible, he seemed rarely employed on any matter connected with his professional business; and yet he had taken to that business from special liking to it, and quite independently of his general sources of fortune.

Some of the richest citizens in the community kept such shops. As I have before said, no difference of rank is recognizable, and therefore all occupations hold the same equal social status. An *An*, of whom I bought my sandals, was the brother of the *Tur*, or chief magistrate; and though his shop was not larger than that of any bootmaker in Bond Street or Broadway, he was said to be twice as rich as the *Tur* who dwelt in a palace. No doubt, however, he had some country-seat.

The *Ana* of the community are, on the whole, an indolent set of beings after the active age of childhood. Whether by temperament or philosophy, they rank repose among the chief blessings of life. Indeed, when you take away from a human being the incentives to action which are found in cupidity or ambition, it seems to me no wonder that he rests quiet.

零售店铺并不很多；侍候顾客的都是各个年龄的孩子，极其聪敏有礼，却没有一丝强求纠缠或卑躬屈膝的气味。店主本人或见或不见；见到时，他也似乎难得忙于任何与他那门营生相关的事务；然而他从事那门营生，本是出于对它的特殊爱好，且与其一般的财源全然无涉。

社区里有些最富有的公民就开着这样的店铺。如我先前所说，这里辨不出等级的差异，因此一切职业都持有同等的社会地位。我向他买凉鞋的那位安，是图尔（Tur）——即行政长官——的兄弟；他的铺子虽不比邦德街或百老汇任何一家靴匠铺子大，据说却比那位住在宫殿里的图尔还要富上一倍。不过无疑，他总还有一处乡间宅邸。

这个社区的安-纳（the Ana），过了童年那个活跃的年纪之后，总体说来是一群懒散的生灵。无论出于气质还是出于哲理，他们都把安宁列为人生主要的福祉之一。的确，当你从一个人身上夺去贪婪与野心所供给的行动动力时，依我看，他安然而息，也就不足为奇了。

In their ordinary movements they prefer the use of their feet to that of their wings. But for their sports or (to indulge in a bold misuse of terms) their public promenades, they employ the latter, also for the aerial dances I have described, as well as for visiting their country places, which are mostly placed on lofty heights; and, when still young, they prefer their wings for travel into the other regions of the Ana, to vehicular conveyances.

Those who accustom themselves to flight can fly, if less rapidly than some birds, yet from twenty-five to thirty miles an hour, and keep up that rate for five or six hours at a stretch. But the Ana generally, on reaching middle age, are not fond of rapid movements requiring violent exercise. Perhaps for this reason, as they hold a doctrine which our own physicians will doubtless approve﻿—viz., that regular transpiration through the pores of the skin is essential to health, they habitually use the sweating-baths to which we give the name of Turkish or Roman, succeeded by douches of perfumed waters. They have great faith in the salubrious virtue of certain perfumes.

It is their custom also, at stated but rare periods, perhaps four times a year when in health, to use a bath charged with *vril*.3 They consider that this fluid, sparingly used, is a great sustainer of life; but used in excess, when in the normal state of health, rather tends to reaction and exhausted vitality. For nearly all their diseases, however, they resort to it as the chief assistant to nature in throwing off the complaint.

日常行动中，他们宁用双脚而不用双翼。但在他们的游戏——或者（容我大胆误用一下名目）他们公开的散步游观——中，他们便用后者；在我描述过的那种空中舞蹈中也用；去他们的乡居也同样——那些乡居多设在高处；而且年纪尚轻时，往安-纳的其他地域旅行，他们也是宁用双翼而不乘车舆。

惯于飞行的人，飞起来虽比某些鸟类慢些，却也能每小时飞 25 至 30 英里，并且一口气保持这个速度五六个小时。但安-纳人一般到了中年，便不喜欢需要剧烈运动的快速动作了。或许正因如此——他们持有一条我们的医生想必也会赞成的学说，即：皮肤毛孔经常的发汗排泄于健康必不可少——他们惯常使用我们称之为土耳其式或罗马式的汗浴，浴后再以香芬之水浇注。他们对某些香气的保健功效极有信心。

他们另有一种习俗，在固定而稀少的时期——健康时或许每年四次——使用一次充注弗里尔〔3〕的浴。他们认为这种流体用之有节，乃是生命的一大支撑；但健康正常之时用之过度，反倒容易引起反应、耗竭生气。然而对于他们几乎一切的疾病，他们又都求助于它，视之为自然在祛除病患时的主要助力。

In their own way they are the most luxurious of people, but all their luxuries are innocent. They may be said to dwell in an atmosphere of music and fragrance. Every room has its mechanical contrivances for melodious sounds, usually tuned down to soft-murmured notes, which seem like sweet whispers from invisible spirits. They are too accustomed to these gentle sounds to find them a hindrance to conversation, nor, when alone, to reflection. But they have a notion that to breathe an air filled with continuous melody and perfume has necessarily an effect at once soothing and elevating upon the formation of character and the habits of thought. Though so temperate, and with total abstinence from other animal food than milk, and from all intoxicating drinks, they are delicate and dainty to an extreme in food and beverage; and in all their sports even the old exhibit a childlike gaiety. Happiness is the end at which they aim, not as the excitement of a moment, but as the prevailing condition of the entire existence; and regard for the happiness of each other is evinced by the exquisite amenity of their manners.

Their conformation of skull has marked differences from that of any known races in the upper world, though I cannot help thinking it a development, in the course of countless ages of the Brachycephalic type of the Age of Stone in Lyell’s *Elements of Geology*, C. X., p. 113, as compared with the Dolichocephalic type of the beginning of the Age of Iron, correspondent with that now so prevalent amongst us, and called the Celtic type. It has the same comparative massiveness of forehead, not receding like the Celtic﻿—the same even roundness in the frontal organs; but it is far loftier in the apex, and far less pronounced in the hinder cranial hemisphere where phrenologists place the animal organs. To speak as a phrenologist, the cranium common to the Vril-ya has the organs of weight, number, tune, form, order, causality, very largely developed; that of construction much more pronounced than that of ideality. Those which are called the moral organs, such as conscientiousness and benevolence, are amazingly full; amativeness and combativeness are both small; adhesiveness large; the organ of destructiveness (i.e., of determined clearance of intervening obstacles) immense, but less than that of benevolence; and their philoprogenitiveness takes rather the character of compassion and tenderness to things that need aid or protection than of the animal love of offspring. I never met with one person deformed or misshapen. The beauty of their countenances is not only in symmetry of feature, but in a smoothness of surface, which continues without line or wrinkle to the extreme of old age, and a serene sweetness of expression, combined with that majesty which seems to come from consciousness of power and the freedom of all terror, physical or moral. It is that very sweetness, combined with that majesty, which inspired in a beholder like myself, accustomed to strive with the passions of mankind, a sentiment of humiliation, of awe, of dread. It is such an expression as a painter might give to a demigod, a genius, an angel. The males of the Vril-ya are entirely beardless; the *Gy-ei* sometimes, in old age, develop a small moustache.

照他们自己的方式说来，他们是最奢华的人民，但他们的一切奢华都是无罪的。他们可以说居住在一片音乐与芬芳的氛围之中。每个房间都有发出乐音的机械装置，通常调得低柔如呢喃，仿佛不可见的精灵发出的甜美低语。他们对这些轻柔的声音习以为常，不觉得它们妨碍交谈，独处时也不觉得它们妨碍沉思。但他们有一种想法：呼吸着充满连续不断的旋律与香气的空气，必然对性格的形成与思维的习惯产生一种既安抚又升华的作用。他们虽然如此节制——除乳类以外完全戒绝别的肉食，并戒绝一切醉人的饮料——在饮食上却精细考究到了极点；而在他们的一切游戏中，连老人也露出孩子气的欢快。幸福便是他们所瞄准的鹄的——不是作为一时的兴奋，而是作为整个存在的一贯境况；而他们对彼此幸福的顾念，就显现在他们举止的无比温文之中。

I was surprised to find that the colour of their skin was not uniformly that which I had remarked in those individuals whom I had first encountered﻿—some being much fairer, and even with blue eyes, and hair of a deep golden auburn, though still of complexions warmer or richer in tone than persons in the north of Europe.

I was told that this admixture of colouring arose from intermarriage with other and more distant tribes of the Vril-ya, who, whether by the accident of climate or early distinction of race, were of fairer hues than the tribes of which this community formed one. It was considered that the dark-red skin showed the most ancient family of Ana; but they attached no sentiment of pride to that antiquity, and, on the contrary, believed their present excellence of breed came from frequent crossing with other families differing, yet akin; and they encourage such intermarriages, always provided that it be with the Vril-ya nations. Nations which, not conforming their manners and institutions to those of the Vril-ya, nor indeed held capable of acquiring the powers over the *vril* agencies which it had taken them generations to attain and transmit, were regarded with more disdain than the citizens of New York regard the negroes.

I learned from Zee, who had more lore in all matters than any male with whom I was brought into familiar converse, that the superiority of the Vril-ya was supposed to have originated in the intensity of their earlier struggles against obstacles in nature amidst the localities in which they had first settled. “Wherever,” said Zee, moralising, “wherever goes on that early process in the history of civilisation, by which life is made a struggle, in which the individual has to put forth all his powers to compete with his fellow, we invariably find this result﻿—viz., since in the competition a vast number must perish, nature selects for preservation only the strongest specimens. With our race, therefore, even before the discovery of *vril*, only the highest organisations were preserved; and there is among our ancient books a legend, once popularly believed, that we were driven from a region that seems to denote the world you come from, in order to perfect our condition and attain to the purest elimination of our species by the severity of the struggles our forefathers underwent; and that, when our education shall become finally completed, we are destined to return to the upper world, and supplant all the inferior races now existing therein.”

我惊讶地发现，他们的肤色并不一律是我最初遇见的那几个人身上所见的颜色——有些人的肤色要白皙得多，甚至生着蓝眼睛和深金赤褐色的头发，不过其面色仍比北欧人温暖浓郁。

我被告知，这种颜色的混杂，来自与弗里尔-雅人其他更遥远部落的通婚——那些部落或因气候的偶然，或因种族的早期分化，肤色比组成这个社区的那些部落更白。人们认为，暗红的皮肤显示最古老的安-纳家族；但他们对此古老性不附任何自豪之感，反倒相信他们今日血统之优异，来自与别异而相近的家族的频繁杂交；他们鼓励这样的通婚，只要是与弗里尔-雅诸国通婚便始终如此。至于那些不使自己的风俗制度遵从弗里尔-雅人、也实在不被认为有能力获得对弗里尔种种作用之掌控的民族——那掌控是弗里尔-雅人花了世世代代才获得并传下的——他们看待这些民族，比纽约的公民看待黑人还要轻蔑。

我从琪那里得知——她所通晓的各方面的学问，比我与之亲昵交谈过的任何男性都多——弗里尔-雅人的优越，据认为起源于他们早期在他们最初定居的地方与自然障碍搏斗的剧烈。“凡是，”琪以说教的口吻说道，“凡是文明史上那种早期过程进行之处——生命借此成为一场搏斗，个人不得不倾其全力与同类竞争——我们总发现这样的结果：既然竞争中必有大批人归于灭亡，自然便只选择最强健的个体予以保留。因此，在我们的种族，甚至早在发现弗里尔之前，便只有最高等的机体得以保存；我们的古书中有一个传说，从前是众人相信的，说我们被从一个地方驱逐了出来——那地方似乎就是你所来自的世界——为的是借我们祖先所历经的搏斗之严酷，完善我们的境况，达到我们物种最纯粹的汰炼；还说，待我们的教养最终完成之时，我们注定要回到上界，取代如今存在于彼处的一切劣等种族。”

===Original===
Aph-Lin and Zee often conversed with me in private upon the political and social conditions of that upper world, in which Zee so philosophically assumed that the inhabitants were to be exterminated one day or other by the advent of the Vril-ya. They found in my accounts﻿—in which I continued to do all I could (without launching into falsehoods so positive that they would have been easily detected by the shrewdness of my listeners) to present our powers and ourselves in the most flattering point of view﻿—perpetual subjects of comparison between our most civilised populations and the meaner subterranean races which they considered hopelessly plunged in barbarism, and doomed to gradual if certain extinction. But they both agreed in desiring to conceal from their community all premature opening into the regions lighted by the sun; both were humane, and shrunk from the thought of annihilating so many millions of creatures; and the pictures I drew of our life, highly coloured as they were, saddened them. In vain I boasted of our great men﻿—poets, philosophers, orators, generals﻿—and defied the Vril-ya to produce their equals. “Alas!” said Zee, her grand face softening into an angel-like compassion, “this predominance of the few over the many is the surest and most fatal sign of a race incorrigibly savage. See you not that the primary condition of mortal happiness consists in the extinction of that strife and competition between individuals, which, no matter what forms of government they adopt, render the many subordinate to the few, destroy real liberty to the individual, whatever may be the nominal liberty of the state, and annul that calm of existence, without which, felicity, mental or bodily, cannot be attained? Our notion is, that the more we can assimilate life to the existence which our noblest ideas can conceive to be that of spirits on the other side of the grave, why, the more we approximate to a divine happiness here, and the more easily we glide into the conditions of being hereafter. For, surely, all we can imagine of the life of gods, or of blessed immortals, supposes the absence of self-made cares and contentious passions, such as avarice and ambition. It seems to us that it must be a life of serene tranquility, not indeed without active occupations to the intellectual or spiritual powers, but occupations, of whatsoever nature they be, congenial to the idiosyncrasies of each, not forced and repugnant﻿—a life gladdened by the untrammelled interchange of gentle affections, in which the moral atmosphere utterly kills hate and vengeance, and strife and rivalry. Such is the political state to which all the tribes and families of the Vril-ya seek to attain, and towards that goal all our theories of government are shaped. You see how utterly opposed is such a progress to that of the uncivilised nations from which you come, and which aim at a systematic perpetuity of troubles, and cares, and warring passions, aggravated more and more as their progress storms its way onward. The most powerful of all the races in our world, beyond the pale of the Vril-ya, esteems itself the best governed of all political societies, and to have reached in that respect the extreme end at which political wisdom can arrive, so that the other nations should tend more or less to copy it. It has established, on its broadest base, the *Koom-Posh*﻿—viz., the government of the ignorant upon the principle of being the most numerous. It has placed the supreme bliss in the vying with each other in all things, so that the evil passions are never in repose﻿—vying for power, for wealth, for eminence of some kind; and in this rivalry it is horrible to hear the vituperation, the slanders, and calumnies which even the best and mildest among them heap on each other without remorse or shame.”

“Some years ago,” said Aph-Lin, “I visited this people, and their misery and degradation were the more appalling because they were always boasting of their felicity and grandeur as compared with the rest of their species. And there is no hope that this people, which evidently resembles your own, can improve, because all their notions tend to further deterioration. They desire to enlarge their dominion more and more, in direct antagonism to the truth that, beyond a very limited range, it is impossible to secure to a community the happiness which belongs to a well-ordered family; and the more they mature a system by which a few individuals are heated and swollen to a size above the standard slenderness of the millions, the more they chuckle and exact, and cry out, ‘See by what great exceptions to the common littleness of our race we prove the magnificent results of our system!’ ”

“In fact,” resumed Zee, “if the wisdom of human life be to approximate to the serene equality of immortals, there can be no more direct flying off into the opposite direction than a system which aims at carrying to the utmost the inequalities and turbulences of mortals. Nor do I see how, by any forms of religious belief, mortals, so acting, could fit themselves even to appreciate the joys of immortals to which they still expect to be transferred by the mere act of dying. On the contrary, minds accustomed to place happiness in things so much the reverse of godlike, would find the happiness of gods exceedingly dull, and would long to get back to a world in which they could quarrel with each other.”

阿弗-林和琪时常私下同我交谈那个上界的政治与社会状况；对于那个上界，琪以如此哲学的确信，假定其居民迟早要因弗里尔-雅人的降临而被灭绝。他们在我的叙述中——我在其中继续竭尽所能（又不至堕入太过坐实的谎话，免得被听者的精明轻易识破）把我们和我们自身的力量摆到最讨好的角度——不断找到比较的材料，把我们最文明的人口同那些较低下的地下种族相比；后者在他们看来，是无可救药地沉沦于野蛮之中，纵然必归于灭绝，也只是缓慢地灭绝。但两人在一点上意见一致：他们不愿让他们的社区过早开启通向阳光照临之地的一切入口；两人都心怀仁慈，一念及消灭数以百万计的生灵便不寒而栗；而我为我们生活所画的图景，尽管着色已浓，仍使他们悲伤。我徒然夸耀我们的伟人——诗人、哲学家、演说家、将领——并向弗里尔-雅人挑战，要他们举出堪与匹敌者。“唉！”琪说道，她那庄严的脸柔和成一种天使般的怜悯，“这种少数人对多数人的宰制，正是一个无可救药地野蛮的种族最确凿、最致命的标志。你难道看不出：凡人幸福的首要条件，就在于消灭个人之间的那种争斗与竞争么？无论他们采用何种政体，那争斗与竞争总使多数从属于少数，摧毁个人的真实自由——不管国家的名义自由如何——并取消存在的那种平静，而没有那种平静，身心的至福便无从获得？我们的观念是：人生愈能同化为那样一种存在——我们最高贵的观念所能设想坟墓彼岸精灵的存在——那么，我们在此世便愈接近一种神圣的幸福，也愈容易滑入来生的种种境况。因为，凡我们对诸神或蒙福不朽者之生活所能想象的，必定没有自造的忧虑和好斗的情欲，诸如贪婪与野心。在我们看来，那必定是一种宁静安然的生活——并非没有智识或灵性能力的积极活动，但那些活动无论性质如何，须与各人的禀性相契，而非强迫与厌恶——一种因温柔情谊的自在交流而欢畅的生活，其中道德的氛围彻底杀灭仇恨与报复、争斗与竞胜。弗里尔-雅人的一切部落与家族所力求达到的，正是这样的政治状态；我们的一切统治理论，都是朝着那个目标塑成的。你看得出，这样的进步与你所来自的那些未开化民族的进步何其相反——后者以烦恼、忧惧与交战的情欲之系统地永续为鹄的，且其进步愈是奔腾向前，这一切便愈演愈烈。我们世界中弗里尔-雅人范围以外的一切种族里最强大的那一族，自认为是一切政治社会中治理最佳的，认为在这方面已达到政治智慧所能抵达的极致，故而其他各国都应或多或少起而仿效。它以最广的基础确立了库姆-波什（Koom-Posh）——即以人最众多为原则的无知者之治。它把至高的幸福安放在凡事彼此争胜之中，于是种种恶念永无宁时——争权、争财、争某种名位；而在这争胜之中，连他们中间最好最温和的人也毫无悔疚羞耻地互相堆加谩骂、诽谤与中伤，听来实在骇人。”

“数年之前，”阿弗-林说，“我访问过这个民族；他们的悲惨与堕落之所以愈发骇人，正因为他们总是自夸其幸福与宏大，说非其同类可比。而这个显然同你所属民族相像的民族，是无望改良的，因为他们的一切观念都趋向进一步的败坏。他们渴望把统治之地扩张得愈来愈广，直接违背了这条真理：超出一个极有限的幅度，便不可能使一个社会获得属于一个良序家庭的幸福；他们愈是使其制度成熟——借这制度，少数个人被烘热、胀大到远逾千百万人标准瘦削的体量——他们便愈是沾沾自得、索取无厌，并且大声喊道：‘看哪，凭我们种族普遍渺小之中的这些何等伟大的例外，证明了我们制度辉煌的成果！’”

“事实上，”琪接着说，“倘若人生的智慧在于接近不朽者那种宁静的平等，那么，凡以求把凡人的不平等与骚乱推到极致为宗旨的制度，便是朝着相反方向最直接的飞离。我也看不出，凭着任何形式的宗教信仰，如此行事的凡人怎能把自己训练得哪怕仅仅领略不朽者的种种快乐——他们还指望着仅凭一死便被迁到那些快乐中去呢。相反，惯于把幸福安放在如此迥异于神性之事上面的心智，会觉得诸神的幸福乏味之至，会渴望回到一个可以彼此争吵的世界里去。”
