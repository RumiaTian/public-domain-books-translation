## XX / 二十

===Original===
From the date of the expedition with Taë which I have just narrated, the child paid me frequent visits. He had taken a liking to me, which I cordially returned. Indeed, as he was not yet twelve years old, and had not commenced the course of scientific studies with which childhood closes in that country, my intellect was less inferior to his than to that of the elder members of his race, especially of the *Gy-ei*, and most especially of the accomplished Zee. The children of the Vril-ya, having upon their minds the weight of so many active duties and grave responsibilities, are not generally mirthful; but Taë, with all his wisdom, had much of the playful good-humour one often finds the characteristic of elderly men of genius. He felt that sort of pleasure in my society which a boy of a similar age in the upper world has in the company of a pet dog or monkey. It amused him to try and teach me the ways of his people, as it amuses a nephew of mine to make his poodle walk on his hind legs or jump through a hoop. I willingly lent myself to such experiments, but I never achieved the success of the poodle. I was very much interested at first in the attempt to ply the wings which the youngest of the Vril-ya use as nimbly and easily as ours do their legs and arms; but my efforts were attended with contusions serious enough to make me abandon them in despair.

These wings, as I before said, are very large, reaching to the knee, and in repose thrown back so as to form a very graceful mantle. They are composed from the feathers of a gigantic bird that abounds in the rocky heights of the country﻿—the colour mostly white, but sometimes with reddish streaks. They are fastened round the shoulders with light but strong springs of steel; and, when expanded, the arms slide through loops for that purpose, forming, as it were, a stout central membrane. As the arms are raised, a tubular lining beneath the vest or tunic becomes, by mechanical contrivance inflated with air, increased or diminished at will by the movement of the arms, and serving to buoy the whole form as on bladders. The wings and the balloon-like apparatus are highly charged with *vril*; and when the body is thus wafted upward, it seems to become singularly lightened of its weight. I found it easy enough to soar from the ground; indeed, when the wings were spread it was scarcely possible not to soar, but then came the difficulty and the danger. I utterly failed in the power to use and direct the pinions, though I am considered among my own race unusually alert and ready in bodily exercises, and am a very practiced swimmer. I could only make the most confused and blundering efforts at flight. I was the servant of the wings; the wings were not my servants﻿—they were beyond my control; and when by a violent strain of muscle, and, I must fairly own, in that abnormal strength which is given by excessive fright, I curbed their gyrations and brought them near to the body, it seemed as if I lost the sustaining power stored in them and the connecting bladders, as when air is let out of a balloon, and found myself precipitated again to the earth; saved, indeed, by some spasmodic flutterings, from being dashed to pieces, but not saved from the bruises and the stun of a heavy fall. I would, however, have persevered in my attempts, but for the advice or the commands of the scientific Zee, who had benevolently accompanied my flutterings, and, indeed, on the last occasion, flying just under me, received my form as it fell on her own expanded wings, and preserved me from breaking my head on the roof of the pyramid from which we had ascended.

“I see,” she said, “that your trials are in vain, not from the fault of the wings and their appurtenances, nor from any imperfectness and malformation of your own corpuscular system, but from irremediable, because organic, defect in your power of volition. Learn that the connection between the will and the agencies of that fluid which has been subjected to the control of the Vril-ya was never established by the first discoverers, never achieved by a single generation; it has gone on increasing, like other properties of race, in proportion as it has been uniformly transmitted from parent to child, so that, at last, it has become an instinct; and an infant *An* of our race wills to fly as intuitively and unconsciously as he wills to walk. He thus plies his invented or artificial wings with as much safety as a bird plies those with which it is born. I did not think sufficiently of this when I allowed you to try an experiment which allured me, for I have longed to have in you a companion. I shall abandon the experiment now. Your life is becoming dear to me.” Herewith the *Gy*’s voice and face softened, and I felt more seriously alarmed than I had been in my previous flights.

===Chinese===
自我方才叙述的那次与塔伊（Taë）同游之日起，这孩子便频频来访。他对我生出了好感，我也衷心报之以好感。的确，他还未满十二岁，尚未开始那个国度里于童年告终之际修习的科学课程，故而我的智力与他相比，差距尚不如与他种族年长者相比那般悬殊——与姬-埃（the Gy-ei）相比尤为悬殊，而与造诣精深的琪（Zee）相比则最为悬殊。弗里尔-雅人（the Vril-ya）的儿童，心头压着这许多活跃的职守与重大的责任，一般说来并不欢快；但塔伊尽管满腹智慧，却颇有一种爱嬉戏的好脾气——人们往往发现，这是天才老者的特征。他在与我的交往中感到的那种乐趣，正如上界同龄男孩在一只爱犬或猴子作伴时感到的一样。他试着教我他族人的种种行事之道，引以为乐，就像我的一个侄儿逗他的卷毛狗用后腿行走或钻圈，以此为乐一样。我心甘情愿地供他做这类实验，却从未取得那只卷毛狗那样的成功。起初，我极有兴趣地尝试挥动双翼——弗里尔-雅人中最年幼的孩子用起翼来，也如我们运用腿臂一般轻捷自如；但我的努力换来的是颇为严重的挫伤，足以令我绝望地放弃了这番尝试。

这双翼，如我先前所说，十分巨大，垂及膝部，静止时向后披拂，恰成一件十分优美的披风。它们由一种巨鸟的羽毛缀成——这种巨鸟繁生于这个国度多岩的高处——羽毛多为白色，有时带着淡红的条纹。双翼以轻巧而坚固的钢簧扣系于双肩；展开时，双臂穿过专为此设的环袢，仿佛连成一片坚韧的中央膜翼。双臂抬起时，背心或外衣之下的管状衬里便凭机械装置充气胀满，随手臂的动作随意增减，如凭气囊一般托浮着整个身躯。双翼和那气球般的装置都饱含弗里尔（vril）；身躯既被这般送上高空，其重量似乎便出奇地减轻了。我发觉从地面腾起十分容易；的确，双翼一经张开，几乎不可能不腾起，但随之而来的便是困难与危险。我在运用与驾驭翼羽的能力上全然失败了，尽管在我自己的种族中，我素被视为体格运动上异常敏捷利落之人，又是一位十分老练的泳者。我只能做出最为慌乱笨拙的飞行尝试。我是双翼的仆役；双翼却不是我的仆役——它们全然不受我的驾驭；而当我以猛烈的肌肉之力——我必须坦白承认，还凭着极度惊骇所赋予的那种反常的气力——遏制住它们的回旋、把它们收拢到身体近旁时，我仿佛便失去了贮存于双翼及其相连气囊之中的托浮之力，如同气球被放去了空气一般，发觉自己重新直坠地面；诚然，靠几阵痉挛似的扑腾，我免于摔得粉身碎骨，却免不了沉重一坠的瘀伤与震晕。若不是深谙科学的琪以劝告或命令相阻，我原会继续尝试下去；她好意地陪伴着我的屡屡扑腾，而最后一次，她正飞在我下方，用她自己展开的双翼接住了我坠落的身躯，使我免于在我们腾空而起的那座金字塔顶上撞破头颅。

“我看得出，”她说，“你的种种尝试终归徒劳：这既非双翼及其附件之过，也非你自身的微粒系统有何不完善或畸形，而是出于你意志之力中一种无可救药的缺陷——正因其为有机禀性，才无可救药。你须知道，意志与那种流体的种种作用——那流体已归弗里尔-雅人掌控——之间的联结，从未由最初的发现者们建立，也从未由单独一代人成就；它像种族的其他禀性一样，随着它自父母到子女的一律传承而不断增长，以致最终成为一种本能；我们种族中的婴孩安（An）想要飞翔，正如他想要行走一样，是出于直觉而不自知的。他这样挥动他那发明的、即人工的双翼，其安全正不亚于鸟儿挥动它与生俱来的翅膀。当初我容你尝试那项诱惑着我的实验时，对此想得不够充分，因为我一直渴望在你身上得到一位伴侣。现在我要放弃这项实验了。你的性命，于我正日益亲贵。”言毕，这位姬（Gy）的声音与面容都柔和了下来，而我感到的惊慌，比我先前历次飞行中所感到的都更为切实。

Now that I am on the subject of wings, I ought not to omit mention of a custom among the *Gy-ei* which seems to me very pretty and tender in the sentiment it implies. A *Gy* wears wings habitually while yet a virgin﻿—she joins the *Ana* in their aerial sports﻿—she adventures alone and afar into the wilder regions of the sunless world: in the boldness and height of her soarings, not less than in the grace of her movements, she excels the opposite sex. But from the day of marriage she wears wings no more, she suspends them with her own willing hand over the nuptial couch, never to be resumed unless the marriage tie be severed by divorce or death.

Now when Zee’s voice and eyes thus softened﻿—and at that softening I prophetically recoiled and shuddered﻿—Taë, who had accompanied us in our flights, but who, childlike, had been much more amused with my awkwardness than sympathising in my fears or aware of my danger, hovered over us, poised amidst the still and radiant air, serene and motionless on his outspread wings, and hearing the endearing words of the young *Gy*, laughed aloud. Said he, “If the *Tish* cannot learn the use of wings, you may still be his companion, Zee, for you can suspend your own.”

===Chinese===
既然说到了双翼，我便不该略而不提姬-埃中间的一种习俗；在我看来，这习俗所蕴含的情意十分美好而温柔。一位姬在尚为处子时惯常佩翼——她加入安-纳（the Ana）的空中游戏——她独自远赴探险，深入那不见天日的世界的蛮荒之域：论翱翔的大胆与高远，一如论动作的优雅，她都胜过异性。然而自成婚之日起，她便不再佩翼；她亲以甘愿之手，将双翼悬于新婚的卧榻之上，非至婚姻之纽带因离异或死亡而解断，永不复取。

其时，琪的声音与眼波既这般柔和起来——对这柔和，我如感谶兆般地畏缩战栗——塔伊便在我们头顶上方盘旋；他一路陪着我们飞行，却出于孩子气，对我的笨拙所觉的可乐，远过于对我恐惧的体恤或对我危险的觉察；他停驻在静谧而辉朗的空气之中，凭展开的双翼安然不动，听了这位年轻姬的亲昵言语，放声大笑起来。他说道：“倘若这蒂什（Tish）学不会用翼，琪，你依然可以做他的伴侣，因为你可以把你自己的那双悬起来呀。”
