## XII / 十二

===Original===
The language of the Vril-ya is peculiarly interesting, because it seems to me to exhibit with great clearness the traces of the three main transitions through which language passes in attaining to perfection of form.

One of the most illustrious of recent philologists, Max Müller, in arguing for the analogy between the strata of language and the strata of the earth, lays down this absolute dogma: “No language can, by any possibility, be inflectional without having passed through the agglutinative and isolating stratum. No language can be agglutinative without clinging with its roots to the underlying stratum of isolation.” —﻿*On the Stratification of Language*, p. 20.

Taking then the Chinese language as the best existing type of the original isolating stratum, “as the faithful photograph of man in his leading-strings trying the muscles of his mind, groping his way, and so delighted with his first successful grasps that he repeats them again and again,”2﻿—we have, in the language of the Vril-ya, still “clinging with its roots to the underlying stratum,” the evidences of the original isolation. It abounds in monosyllables, which are the foundations of the language. The transition into the agglutinative form marks an epoch that must have gradually extended through ages, the written literature of which has only survived in a few fragments of symbolical mythology and certain pithy sentences which have passed into popular proverbs. With the extant literature of the Vril-ya the inflectional stratum commences. No doubt at that time there must have operated concurrent causes, in the fusion of races by some dominant people, and the rise of some great literary phenomena by which the form of language became arrested and fixed. As the inflectional stage prevailed over the agglutinative, it is surprising to see how much more boldly the original roots of the language project from the surface that conceals them. In the old fragments and proverbs of the preceding stage the monosyllables which compose those roots vanish amidst words of enormous length, comprehending whole sentences from which no one part can be disentangled from the other and employed separately. But when the inflectional form of language became so far advanced as to have its scholars and grammarians, they seem to have united in extirpating all such polysynthetical or polysyllabic monsters, as devouring invaders of the aboriginal forms. Words beyond three syllables became proscribed as barbarous, and in proportion as the language grew thus simplified it increased in strength, in dignity, and in sweetness. Though now very compressed in sound, it gains in clearness by that compression. By a single letter, according to its position, they contrive to express all that with civilised nations in our upper world it takes the waste, sometimes of syllables, sometimes of sentences, to express. Let me here cite one or two instances: *An* (which I will translate man), *Ana* (men); the letter *s* is with them a letter implying multitude, according to where it is placed; *Sana* means mankind; *Ansa*, a multitude of men. The prefix of certain letters in their alphabet invariably denotes compound significations. For instance, *Gl* (which with them is a single letter, as *th* is a single letter with the Greeks) at the commencement of a word infers an assemblage or union of things, sometimes kindred, sometimes dissimilar﻿—as *Oon*, a house; *Gloon*, a town (i.e., an assemblage of houses). *Ata* is sorrow; *Glata*, a public calamity. *Aur-an* is the health or well-being of a man; *Glauran*, the well-being of the state, the good of the community; and a word constantly in ther mouths is *A-glauran*, which denotes their political creed﻿—viz., that “the first principle of a community is the good of all.” *Aub* is invention; *Sila*, a tone in music. *Glaubsila*, as uniting the ideas of invention and of musical intonation, is the classical word for poetry﻿—abbreviated, in ordinary conversation, to *Glaubs*. *Na*, which with them is, like *Gl*, but a single letter, always, when an initial, implies something antagonistic to life or joy or comfort, resembling in this the Aryan root *Nak*, expressive of perishing or destruction. *Nax* is darkness; *Narl*, death; *Naria*, sin or evil. *Nas*﻿—an uttermost condition of sin and evil﻿—corruption. In writing, they deem it irreverent to express the Supreme Being by any special name. He is symbolized by what may be termed the hieroglyphic of a pyramid, *A*. In prayer they address Him by a name which they deem too sacred to confide to a stranger, and I know it not. In conversation they generally use a periphrastic epithet, such as the All-Good. The letter *V*, symbolical of the inverted pyramid, where it is an initial, nearly always denotes excellence or power; as *Vril*, of which I have said so much; *Veed*, an immortal spirit; *Veed-ya*, immortality; *Koom*, pronounced like the Welsh *Cwm*, denotes something of hollowness. *Koom* itself is a cave; *Koom-in*, a hole; *Zi-koom*, a valley; *Koom-zi*, vacancy or void; *Bodh-koom*, ignorance (literally, knowledge-void). *Koom-Posh* is their name for the government of the many, or the ascendancy of the most ignorant or hollow. *Posh* is an almost untranslatable idiom, implying, as the reader will see later, contempt. The closest rendering I can give to it is our slang term, “bosh;” and thus *Koom-Posh* may be loosely rendered “Hollow-Bosh.” But when Democracy or *Koom-Posh* degenerates from popular ignorance into that popular passion or ferocity which precedes its decease, as (to cite illustrations from the upper world) during the French Reign of Terror, or for the fifty years of the Roman Republic preceding the ascendancy of Augustus, their name for that state of things is *Glek-Nas*. *Ek* is strife﻿—*Glek*, the universal strife. *Nas*, as I before said, is corruption or rot; thus *Glek-Nas* may be construed, “the universal strife-rot.” Their compounds are very expressive; thus, *Bodh* being knowledge, and *Too* a participle that implies the action of cautiously approaching﻿—*Too-bodh* is their word for Philosophy; *Pah* is a contemptuous exclamation analogous to our idiom, “stuff and nonsense;” *Pah-bodh* (literally stuff-and-nonsense-knowledge) is their term for futile or false philosophy, and applied to a species of metaphysical or speculative ratiocination formerly in vogue, which consisted in making inquiries that could not be answered, and were not worth making; such, for instance, as “Why does an *An* have five toes to his feet instead of four or six? Did the first *An*, created by the All-Good, have the same number of toes as his descendants? In the form by which an *An* will be recognised by his friends in the future state of being, will he retain any toes at all, and, if so, will they be material toes or spiritual toes?” I take these illustrations of *Pah-bodh*, not in irony or jest, but because the very inquiries I name formed the subject of controversy by the latest cultivators of that “science”﻿—4,000 years ago.

In the declension of nouns I was informed that anciently there were eight cases (one more than in the Sanskrit Grammar); but the effect of time has been to reduce these cases, and multiply, instead of these varying terminations, explanatory propositions. At present, in the Grammar submitted to my study, there were four cases to nouns, three having varying terminations, and the fourth a differing prefix.

In the elder inflectional literature the dual form existed﻿—it has long been obsolete.

The genitive case with them is also obsolete; the dative supplies its place: they say the House *to* a Man, instead of the House *of* a Man. When used (sometimes in poetry), the genitive in the termination is the same as the nominative; so is the ablative, the preposition that marks it being a prefix or suffix at option, and generally decided by ear, according to the sound of the noun. It will be observed that the prefix *Hil* marks the vocative case. It is always retained in addressing another, except in the most intimate domestic relations; its omission would be considered rude: just as in our old forms of speech in addressing a king it would have been deemed disrespectful to say “King,” and reverential to say “O King.” In fact, as they have no titles of honour, the vocative adjuration supplies the place of a title, and is given impartially to all. The prefix *Hil* enters into the composition of words that imply distant communications, as *Hil-ya*, to travel.

In the conjugation of their verbs, which is much too lengthy a subject to enter on here, the auxiliary verb *Ya*, “to go,” which plays so considerable part in the Sanskrit, appears and performs a kindred office, as if it were a radical in some language from which both had descended. But another auxiliary of opposite signification also accompanies it and shares its labours﻿—viz., *Zi*, to stay or repose. Thus *Ya* enters into the future tense, and *Zi* in the preterite of all verbs requiring auxiliaries. *Yam*, I go﻿—*Yiam*, I may go﻿—*Yani-ya*, I shall go (literally, I go to go), *Zam-poo-yan*, I have gone (literally, I rest from gone). *Ya*, as a termination, implies by analogy, progress, movement, efflorescence. *Zi*, as a terminal, denotes fixity, sometimes in a good sense, sometimes in a bad, according to the word with which it is coupled. *Iva-zi*, eternal goodness; *Nan-zi*, eternal evil. *Poo* (from) enters as a prefix to words that denote repugnance, or things from which we ought to be averse. *Poo-pra*, disgust; *Poo-naria*, falsehood, the vilest kind of evil. *Poosh* or *Posh* I have already confessed to be untranslatable literally. It is an expression of contempt not unmixed with pity. This radical seems to have originated from inherent sympathy between the labial effort and the sentiment that impelled it, *Poo* being an utterance in which the breath is exploded from the lips with more or less vehemence. On the other hand, *Z*, when an initial, is with them a sound in which the breath is sucked inward, and thus *Zu*, pronounced *Zoo* (which in their language is one letter), is the ordinary prefix to words that signify something that attracts, pleases, touches the heart﻿—as *Zummer*, lover; *Zutze*, love; *Zuzulia*, delight. This indrawn sound of *Z* seems indeed naturally appropriate to fondness. Thus, even in our language, mothers say to their babies, in defiance of grammar, “Zoo darling;” and I have heard a learned professor at Boston call his wife (he had been only married a month) “Zoo little pet.”

I cannot quit this subject, however, without observing by what slight changes in the dialects favoured by different tribes of the same race, the original signification and beauty of sounds may become confused and deformed. Zee told me with much indignation that *Zummer* (lover) which, in the way she uttered it, seemed slowly taken down to the very depths of her heart, was, in some not very distant communities of the Vril-ya, vitiated into the half-hissing, half-nasal, wholly disagreeable, sound of *Subber*. I thought to myself it only wanted the introduction of *n* before *u* to render it into an English word significant of the last quality an amorous *Gy* would desire in her *Zummer*.

I will but mention another peculiarity in this language which gives equal force and brevity to its forms of expressions.

*A* is with them, as with us, the first letter of the alphabet, and is often used as a prefix word by itself to convey a complex idea of sovereignty or chiefdom, or presiding principle. For instance, *Iva* is goodness; *Diva*, goodness and happiness united; *A-Diva* is unerring and absolute truth. I have already noticed the value of *A* in *A-glauran*, so, in *vril* (to whose properties they trace their present state of civilisation), *A-vril* denotes, as I have said, civilisation itself.

The philologist will have seen from the above how much the language of the Vril-ya is akin to the Aryan or Indo-Germanic; but, like all languages, it contains words and forms in which transfers from very opposite sources of speech have been taken. The very title of *Tur*, which they give to their supreme magistrate, indicates theft from a tongue akin to the Turanian. They say themselves that this is a foreign word borrowed from a title which their historical records show to have been borne by the chief of a nation with whom the ancestors of the Vril-ya were, in very remote periods, on friendly terms, but which has long become extinct, and they say that when, after the discovery of *vril*, they remodelled their political institutions, they expressly adopted a title taken from an extinct race and a dead language for that of their chief magistrate, in order to avoid all titles for that office with which they had previous associations.

Should life be spared to me, I may collect into systematic form such knowledge as I acquired of this language during my sojourn amongst the Vril-ya. But what I have already said will perhaps suffice to show to genuine philological students that a language which, preserving so many of the roots in the aboriginal form, and clearing from the immediate, but transitory, polysynthetical stage so many rude incumbrances, has attained to such a union of simplicity and compass in its final inflectional forms, must have been the gradual work of countless ages and many varieties of mind; that it contains the evidence of fusion between congenial races, and necessitated, in arriving at the shape of which I have given examples, the continuous culture of a highly thoughtful people.

That, nevertheless, the literature which belongs to this language is a literature of the past; that the present felicitous state of society at which the Ana have attained forbids the progressive cultivation of literature, especially in the two main divisions of fiction and history﻿—I shall have occasion to show later.

===Chinese===
弗里尔-雅人的语言殊为有趣，因为在我看来，它极其清晰地显示出语言在臻于形式之完美的过程中所经历的三次主要变迁的痕迹。

近代最负盛名的语文学家之一马克斯·缪勒，在论证语言的地层与土地的地层之间的类比时，立下了这条绝对的教条：“任何语言，都绝无可能未经胶着层与孤立层而径直成为屈折语。任何语言，也绝无可能身为胶着语，其词根却不依附于其下方的孤立层。”——《论语言的地层》（*On the Stratification of Language*），第 20 页。

既然把汉语当作原始孤立层现存最好的类型，当作“人类尚系着学步襻带之时的一幅忠实照片：他试着活动心智的筋肉，一路摸索，又因最初几次抓握得手而如此欢喜，以至于一遍又一遍地重复它们”2——那么，在弗里尔-雅人的语言里，我们便看到它仍“以词根依附于其下的地层”，保有原始孤立状态的证据。这种语言富于单音节词，它们正是全语言的根基。向胶着形式的过渡标志着一个时代，那个时代必定渐渐绵延过许多岁月，其书面文学仅存于象征神话的零篇断简，和若干已化入民间谚语的精炼语句之中。弗里尔-雅人的现存文学，便是从屈折层开始的。毫无疑问，那时必有种种原因并作：某个占支配地位的民族促成诸族的融合，又有某种伟大的文学现象兴起，使语言的形式就此凝定。及至屈折阶段胜过胶着阶段，令人惊异的是，语言的原始词根竟何等鲜明地从掩蔽它们的表层中突露出来。而在前一阶段的古老断片与谚语里，构成那些词根的单音节词消没在极其冗长的词语之中——那些词语容纳整个句子，其中任何一部分都无法同别的部分拆解开来单独使用。但等到屈折形式长进到自有其学者与文法家的地步，他们似乎曾同心协力，把这一切多式综合的、多音节的怪物尽数铲除，视之为吞食原初形式的入侵之敌。超过三个音节的词遂遭摈弃，目为野蛮；而语言既经这般简化，便也相应地在气力、尊严与甜美上日益增长。它如今音响极为紧凑，却正由这紧凑而赢得清晰。凭单独一个字母，视其位置而定，他们便能表达出我们上界文明民族须得耗费——有时是音节，有时是整句——才能表达的一切。请容我在此举一两个例子：*安*（An，我姑且译作男子），*安-纳*（Ana，众男子）；字母 *s* 在他们是一种含示众多的字母，视其所在位置而定；*Sana* 意为人类；*Ansa* 则是一大群男子。他们字母表中某些字母的前缀，一律表示复合的意义。例如 *Gl*（在他们是一个单独的字母，一如 *th* 在希腊人是一个单独的字母）置于词首，便含示事物的聚集或联合，有时是同类的，有时是异类的——如 *Oon*，房屋；*Gloon*，市镇（即房屋的聚集）。*Ata* 是忧苦；*Glata* 是公共的灾殃。*Aur-an* 是一个人的健康或安乐；*Glauran* 是国家的安乐、社会的公益；而他们口中常挂着的一个词是 *A-glauran*，它表明他们的政治信条——即“社会的第一原则是全体之善”。*Aub* 是发明；*Sila* 是一个乐音。*Glaubsila* 兼含发明与乐音两义，是诗歌的雅言——在日常谈话中略作 *Glaubs*。*Na* 在他们也同 *Gl* 一样只是单独一个字母，凡居词首，必含示与生命、欢愉或安适相敌之物，这一点类似雅利安词根 *Nak*——表灭亡或毁坏之意的。*Nax* 是黑暗；*Narl* 是死亡；*Naria* 是罪或恶。*Nas* 是罪与恶的极境——腐坏。在书写中，他们认为以任何专名指称至上者都属不敬。祂以一座金字塔的、可称为象形文字的 *A* 来象征。祷告时他们以一个名称呼祂——那名称他们视为过于神圣，不可告知外人，我因而无从知晓。言谈之间，他们一般用一种迂回的称谓，如至善者。字母 *V* 象征倒置的金字塔，凡居词首，几乎总表示卓越或力量：如*弗里尔*（Vril），关于它我已说了这么多；*Veed*，不死的精灵；*Veed-ya*，不朽。*Koom*（发音像威尔士语的 *Cwm*）表示某种中空之物。*Koom* 本身是洞穴；*Koom-in* 是孔穴；*Zi-koom* 是山谷；*Koom-zi* 是空虚或空无；*Bodh-koom* 是无知（字面义为“知识之空”）。库姆-波什（Koom-Posh）是他们给多数人之治、即最无知、最空洞者的优势地位所起的名称。*Posh* 几乎是一个无法移译的习语，含着——读者往后会看到——轻蔑之意。我所能给出的最近似的译法，是我们俚语中的“bosh”（胡话）；因此库姆-波什不妨宽泛地译作“空洞-胡话”（Hollow-Bosh）。但当民主制或库姆-波什从民众的无知堕入民众的激情或狂暴——那正是它临终之前的征象——例如（且从上界举例）在法国恐怖统治时期，或在奥古斯都得势之前罗马共和国的那五十年间，他们对那种局面的名称是 *Glek-Nas*。*Ek* 是争斗——*Glek* 是普遍的争斗。*Nas*，如我先前所说，是腐坏或朽烂；因此 *Glek-Nas* 可以解作“普遍的争斗之朽”。他们的复合词极富表现力；例如 *Bodh* 是知识，*Too* 是一个表示谨慎趋近之动作的分词——*Too-bodh* 便是他们称为哲学的词；*Pah* 是一声轻蔑的感叹，相当于我们的习语“胡说与废话”；*Pah-bodh*（字面义为胡说-废话-知识）是他们指无益或虚妄之哲学的术语，专指从前盛行的一种玄学的或思辨的推理，其法在于提出既无从回答、也不值得提出的问题；例如：“一个*安*的脚上为何有五根足趾，而不是四根或六根？至善者所造的第一个*安*，足趾的数目是否与其后裔相同？在来世朋友赖以认出一个*安*的那个形体里，他究竟还保不保留足趾？倘若保留，那足趾是物质的足趾，还是精神的足趾？”我举这些 *Pah-bodh* 的例子，并非出于讥讽或戏谑，而是因为我所点名的那些问题，恰是那种“科学”晚近的耕耘者激烈争辩过的题目——在 4,000 年前。

据告知，在名词的变格上，古时有八个格（比梵文文法多一个）；但时间的作用却是削减这些格，并且不去增多变化的词尾，反而增多解释性的词语。在提交我研习的那部文法里，名词现有四格：三格以变化的词尾相区别，第四格则以相异的前缀相区别。

在较早的屈折文学里，双数形式尚存——如今早已废弃。

他们的属格也已废弃，由与格代行其职：他们说“予某人的房屋”，而不说“某人的房屋”。属格偶一使用时（有时见于诗中），其词尾与主格相同；夺格亦然，标记它的虚词或作前缀、或作后缀，随人所欲，一般凭耳朵依名词的音响决定。可以看出，前缀 *Hil* 标记的是呼格。向人致辞时它总须保留，唯至亲密友的家庭关系间除外；省去它便会被视为失礼：正如在我们古时的言辞里，向君王致辞，直呼“王”会被认为不敬，称“王啊”方为恭敬。事实上，他们既无荣衔尊号，呼格的吁请便代行了头衔之职，并且不分彼此地施于一切人。前缀 *Hil* 还进入表示远隔交通之词语的构成，如 *Hil-ya*，旅行。

在他们动词的变位里——这个题目太长，此处无从细述——助动词 *Ya*（“去”）出现了，并且行使着一种与它在梵文中颇相类似的职分，仿佛它是某种语言的一个词根，而两种语言都自那语言递降而来。但另一个意义相反的助动词也与它相伴、分担其劳——即 *Zi*，停留或安息。于是 *Ya* 进入将来时，而 *Zi* 进入一切须用助动词的动词的过去时。*Yam*，我去——*Yiam*，我或许去——*Yani-ya*，我将去（字面义为“我去以去”）——*Zam-poo-yan*，我已去（字面义为“我自所去者而息”）。*Ya* 作为词尾，由类比而含示前进、运动、绽发；*Zi* 作为词尾，则表示凝定，或作褒解，或作贬解，视与其相配的词而定。*Iva-zi*，永恒之善；*Nan-zi*，永恒之恶。*Poo*（自、从）作为前缀进入表示嫌恶的词，或我们理应厌避的事物。*Poo-pra*，憎厌；*Poo-naria*，虚妄，恶中之最卑者。*Poosh* 或 *Posh*，我已承认无法直译。它是一种不无怜悯的轻蔑之辞。这个词根似乎源起于唇部发力与驱迫它的情感之间的固有感应：*Poo* 便是一种把气息或多或少带些猛劲从唇间迸出的发音。另一方面，*Z* 居词首时，在他们是一种把气息向内吸入的声音，于是 *Zu*——读作 *Zoo*（在他们的语言里是一个字母）——便是表示吸引人、怡悦人、动人之物的词的寻常前缀——如 *Zummer*，钟情人；*Zutze*，爱；*Zuzulia*，欢悦。*Z* 这一内吸的音，看来确实天然宜于柔情。甚至在我们自己的语言里，母亲们对婴儿也置文法于不顾，说“Zoo darling”（乖囡）；我还听波士顿一位博学的教授唤他的妻子（他们新婚方一月）“Zoo little pet”（小宝贝儿）。

然而，在离开这个题目之前，我不能不指出：同一种族不同部族所偏用的方言，只消发生轻微的变化，便足以使语音原有的意义与美紊乱而走形。琪愤愤然告诉我，*Zummer*（钟情人）这个词——照她发音的方式，仿佛被缓缓沉入她心底最深处——在弗里尔-雅人某些相距不很远的社会里，竟败坏成了 *Subber* 那种半带嘶声、半带鼻音、全然不悦耳的音响。我心里想，只消在 *u* 前添一个 *n*，它便成了一个英文词，所指的恰是多情的*姬*最不愿她的 *Zummer* 具有的最后一种品质。

我只想再提这种语言的另一个特点：它使其表达的形式兼具力量与简洁。

*A* 在他们，一如在我们，是字母表的首字母，且常单独用作前缀词，传达主权、首领之位或宰制原则的复合观念。例如 *Iva* 是善；*Diva* 是善与幸福之合；*A-Diva* 是无误而绝对的真理。我已提过 *A* 在 *A-glauran* 一词中的价值；同样，在*弗里尔*（他们把自己现今的文明状态溯源于其诸特性）中，*A-vril* 便如我所说，意指文明本身。

语文学家由上文可以看出，弗里尔-雅人的语言与雅利安语即印度-日耳曼语何等相近；但同一切语言一样，它也含有从截然相反的言语来源移借而来的词与形式。他们授予最高长官的*图尔*（Tur）这个头衔本身，便表明是从一种与图兰语相近的语言中窃来的。他们自己说，这是一个外来词，借自一个头衔——他们的史册表明，那头衔曾为一个民族的首领所佩戴；在极遥远的时代，弗里尔-雅人的祖先与那个民族交好，而那个民族久已灭绝；他们又说，自从发现*弗里尔*之后，他们重塑其政治制度之时，特地为他们最高长官选了这个取自绝种之族、死亡之语的头衔，以免此职染有任何令他们旧有联想的头衔。

倘若天假我年，我或将把旅居弗里尔-雅人中间时所获的这种语言的知识，整理成系统的形式。但我已说过的这些，或许已足以向真正的语文学学生表明：一种语言，既在原初形态中保存了这么多词根，又从那紧邻的、但转瞬即逝的多式综合阶段清除了这么多粗陋的赘累，而其最终的屈折形式又达到了如此简洁与容量的结合，那它必定是无数时代、无数种心智的渐进之功；它含着同气诸族融合的证据，而且，要达到我举例的那些形状，便必须有一个人深思远的民族持续不断的栽培。

然而，属于这种语言的文学乃是一种过去的文学；安-纳人所达到的当今这种幸福的社会状态，禁绝了文学的进步耕耘，尤其是在小说与历史这两大部门——这一点，我以后还有机会表明。
