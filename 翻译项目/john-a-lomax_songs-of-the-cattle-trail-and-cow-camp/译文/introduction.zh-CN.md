## Introduction / 导言

===Original===

’Tis the road that the sun goes down.”

===Original===
*The Songs of the Cattle Trail and Cow Camp* does not purport to be an anthology of Western verse. As its title indicates, the contents of the book are limited to attempts, more or less poetic, in translating scenes connected with the life of a cowboy. The volume is in reality a byproduct of my earlier collection, *Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads*. In the former book I put together what seemed to me to be the best of the songs created and sung by the cowboys as they went about their work. In making the collection, the cowboys often sang or sent to me songs which I recognized as having already been in print; although the singer usually said that some other cowboy had sung the song to him and that he did not know where it had originated. For example, one night in New Mexico a cowboy sang to me, in typical cowboy music, Larry Chittenden’s entire “Cowboys’ Christmas Ball”; since that time the poem has often come to me in manuscript form as an original cowboy song. The changes—usually, it must be confessed, resulting in bettering the verse—which have occurred in oral transmission, are most interesting. Of one example, Charles Badger Clark’s “High Chin Bob,” I have printed, following Mr. Clark’s poem, a cowboy version, which I submit to Mr. Clark and his admirers for their consideration.

===Chinese===
《牛径与牛营之歌》并不想自命为一部西部诗选。正如书名所示，本书内容仅限于种种或浓或淡带着诗意的尝试，去描摹与牛仔生活相连的场景。这部集子实际上是我早先编的《牛仔歌谣与边疆谣曲》（*Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads*）的副产品。在前一本书里，我汇集了在我看来最出色的那些歌——牛仔们干活时创作并传唱的歌。在收集过程中，牛仔们常常唱给我听，或寄给我一些我认出早已印行过的歌；尽管演唱者通常说，是别的哪个牛仔唱给他听的，他也不知道这歌打哪儿来。比方说，一天夜里在新墨西哥，一个牛仔用典型的牛仔曲调，给我唱完了拉里·奇滕登（Larry Chittenden）的整首《牛仔圣诞舞会》；打那以后，这首诗还常常以手稿的形式到我手里，被当作一首牛仔原创作。这些在口口相传中发生的变化——必须承认，通常让诗变得更好了——实在有趣。其中一例，查尔斯·巴杰·克拉克的《高下巴鲍勃》，我在克拉克先生的原诗之后印了一个牛仔版本，提交给克拉克先生和他的仰慕者们斟酌。

===Original===
In making selections for this volume from a large mass of material that came into my ballad hopper while hunting cowboy songs as a Traveling Fellow from Harvard University, I have included the best of the verse given me directly by the cowboys; other selections have come in through repeated recommendation of these men; others are vagrant verses from Western newspapers; and still others have been lifted from collections of Western verse written by such men as Charles Badger Clark, Jr., and Herbert H. Knibbs. To these two authors, as well as others who have permitted me to make use of their work, the grateful thanks of the collector are extended. As will be seen, almost one-half of the selections have no assignable authorship. I am equally grateful to these unknown authors.

===Chinese===
我作为哈佛大学的旅行研究员（Traveling Fellow）四处搜罗牛仔歌谣时，大批材料进了我的谣曲漏斗，本书即从中甄选：我收录了牛仔们直接给我的诗稿中的上品；另一些选目来自这些人的一再举荐；还有一些是从西部报纸上流浪来的诗行；更有一些取自小查尔斯·巴杰·克拉克和赫伯特·H·尼布斯（Herbert H. Knibbs）等人所写的西部诗集。对这两位作者，以及其余慨然允许我采用其作品的人，编者谨致谢忱。读者会看到，选目中几乎有一半查不出可归的作者。对这些无名的作者，我同样心怀感激。

===Original===
All those who found *Cowboy Songs* diverting, it is believed, will make welcome *The Songs of the Cattle Trail and Cow Camp*. Many of these have this claim to be called songs: they have been set to music by the cowboys, who, in their isolation and loneliness, have found solace in narrative or descriptive verse devoted to cattle scenes. Herein, again, through these quondam songs we may come to appreciate something of the spirit of the big West—its largeness, its freedom, its wholehearted hospitality, its genuine friendship. Here again, too, we may see the cowboy at work and at play; hear the jingle of his big bell spurs, the swish of his rope, the creaking of his saddle gear, the thud of thousands of hoofs on the long, long trail winding from Texas to Montana; and know something of the life that attracted from the East some of its best young blood to a work that was necessary in the winning of the West. The trails are becoming dust covered or grass grown or lost underneath the farmers’ furrow; but in the selections of this volume, many of them poems by courtesy, men of today and those who are to follow, may sense, at least in some small measure, the service, the glamour, the romance of that knight-errant of the plains—the American cowboy.

===Chinese===
相信凡觉得《牛仔歌谣》有趣的人，也会欢迎这部《牛径与牛营之歌》。其中许多篇目够格称作歌：牛仔们已为它们谱了曲；这些人在与世隔绝的孤寂里，曾从咏牛径诸景的叙事诗或写景诗中寻得慰藉。在本书中，借这些往日的歌，我们又一次可以领略几分大西部的精神——它的辽阔，它的自由，它掏心掏肺的好客，它实实在在的情谊。在这里，我们也能再看到牛仔劳作与嬉戏的模样；听见他那大号响铃马刺的叮当、套索的嗖嗖、鞍具的吱呀、千万只蹄子在那条从得克萨斯蜿蜒到蒙大拿的漫漫长路上的闷响；并多少了解那种生活——它把东部一些最优秀的青年血脉吸引过来，投入这份在征服西部的事业中不可欠缺的活计。那些牛径正被尘土掩埋，或长满荒草，或消失在农人的犁沟之下；但在本书的选目里——其中不少不过是勉强的诗——今天的人以及后来的人，至少还能隐约感到那位平原上的游侠骑士——美国牛仔——的功业、光彩与罗曼史。
