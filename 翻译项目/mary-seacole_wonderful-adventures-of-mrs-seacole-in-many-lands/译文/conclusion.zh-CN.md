## Conclusion / 结语

===Original===
I did not return to England by the most direct route, but took the opportunity of seeing more of men and manners in yet other lands. Arrived in England at last, we set to work bravely at Aldershott to retrieve our fallen fortunes, and stem off the ruin originated in the Crimea, but all in vain; and at last defeated by fortune, but not I think disgraced, we were obliged to capitulate on very honourable conditions. In plain truth, the old Crimean firm of Seacole and Day was dissolved finally, and its partners had to recommence the world anew. And so ended *our* campaign. One of us started only the other day for the Antipodes, while the other is ready to take any journey to any place where a stout heart and two experienced hands may be of use.

Perhaps it would be right if I were to express more shame and annoyance than I really feel at the pecuniarily disastrous issue of my Crimean adventures, but I cannot﻿—I really cannot. When I would try and feel ashamed of myself for being poor and helpless, I only experience a glow of pride at the other and more pleasing events of my career; when I think of the few whom I failed to pay in full (and so far from blaming me some of them are now my firmest friends), I cannot help remembering also the many who profess themselves indebted to me.

===Chinese===
我不是取最直接的路回英国的，而是趁此机会，再到些别的国度，多看看世人与风土。终于抵达英国后，我们雄心勃勃地在奥尔德肖特（Aldershott）重整旗鼓，想挽回败落的家业，堵住在克里米亚种下的祸根，可一切徒劳；最后，我们被命运击垮——但我想并无辱没名誉——不得不在十分体面的条件下投降了事。说白了，克里米亚的老商号「西科尔与戴」就此彻底解散，合伙人只得从头另起炉灶。我们这场征战就这样收了场。一位合伙人前些天刚动身去了对跖之地（the Antipodes，即澳洲），另一位呢，随时准备上路，去任何地方——只要一颗结实的心和两只历练过的手用得上。

倘若我对克里米亚冒险在钱财上的惨淡结局，表现出比真实感受更多的羞愧与懊恼，或许才算得体；可我不能——实在不能。当我试着为自己的穷困无助害臊时，心里反而涌起一阵自豪的热流，为的是我生涯里那些更愉快的遭际；想起少数几个我没能够全额偿付的人（他们非但不怪我，有几个如今还是我最铁的朋友），我便不由得也想起那许多自称欠着我情的人。

===Original===
Let me, in as few words as possible, state the results of my Crimean campaign. To be sure, I returned from it shaken in health. I came home wounded, as many others did. Few constitutions, indeed, were the better for those winters before Sebastopol, and I was too hard worked not to feel their effects; for a little labour fatigues me now﻿—I cannot watch by sickbeds as I could﻿—a week’s want of rest quite knocks me up now. Then I returned bankrupt in fortune. Whereas others in my position may have come back to England rich and prosperous, I found myself poor﻿—beggared. So few words can tell what I have lost.

But what have I gained? I should need a volume to describe that fairly; so much is it, and so cheaply purchased by suffering ten times worse than what I have experienced. I have more than once heard people say that they would gladly suffer illness to enjoy the delights of convalescence, and so, by enduring a few days’ pain, gain the tender love of relatives and sympathy of friends. And on this principle I rejoice in the trials which have borne me such pleasures as those I now enjoy, for wherever I go I am sure to meet some smiling face; every step I take in the crowded London streets may bring me in contact with some friend, forgotten by me, perhaps, but who soon reminds me of our old life before Sebastopol; it seems very long ago now, when I was of use to him and he to me.

===Chinese===
让我用尽量少的几句话，交代我克里米亚征战的成绩。的确，我回来时身体垮了。我是带着伤回家的，同其他许多人一样。说实话，谁的身子骨经得起塞瓦斯托波尔城前那几个冬天还更好？我操劳过度，不可能不落下印记；如今一点小劳作就叫我不堪疲倦——再不能像从前那样守在病榻边——缺上一星期的觉，就能把我彻底撂倒。其次，我回来时钱财上破了产。别人处在我这位置，或许个个衣锦还乡、富足兴旺，我却发现自己一贫如洗——彻底赤贫。寥寥数语，就道尽了我的所失。

可我得了什么呢？要公道地说清这个，恐怕得写上一整卷书；所得如此之多，而代价不过是比我经历过的苦十倍的苦难——何其廉价。我不止一次听人说，他们甘愿生病，好享受康复的欢愉，好以忍受几日的疼痛，换来亲人的疼爱与朋友的同情。循着这个道理，我为那些磨难而欢喜，正是它们给我带来了如今享受的这些快乐；因为无论走到哪里，我准会撞见一张笑脸；在伦敦拥挤的街头，我每走一步，都可能遇见哪位朋友——或许我已忘了他，他很快便提醒我想起我们在塞瓦斯托波尔城前的旧日生涯；如今想来恍如隔世，那时我于他有助，他于我有用。

Where, indeed, do I not find friends. In omnibuses, in river steamboats, in places of public amusement, in quiet streets and courts, where taking shortcuts I lose my way ofttimes, spring up old familiar faces to remind me of the months spent on Spring Hill. The sentries at Whitehall relax from the discharge of their important duty of guarding nothing to give me a smile of recognition; the very newspaper offices look friendly as I pass them by; busy Printing-house Yard puts on a cheering smile, and the *Punch* office in Fleet Street sometimes laughs outright. Now, would all this have happened if I had returned to England a rich woman? Surely not.

A few words more ere I bring these egotistical remarks to a close. It is naturally with feelings of pride and pleasure that I allude to the committee recently organized to aid me; and if I indulge in the vanity of placing their names before my readers, it is simply because every one of the following noblemen and gentlemen knew me in the Crimea, and by consenting to assist me now record publicly their opinion of my services there. 说真的，哪里没有我的朋友呢？在公共马车上，在河轮上，在公共游乐场里，在僻静的街巷和院落——抄近道时我常常在那儿迷路——一张张熟悉的老面孔会突然冒出来，让我想起斯普林希尔上的岁月。白厅（Whitehall）的哨兵，也从他们守着空无一物的重大职责里缓过神来，朝我投来相识的微笑；连报馆的门面，我走过时也显得友善；忙碌的印刷所院子（Printing-house Yard）堆起鼓舞人的笑意，舰队街（Fleet Street）的《笨拙》编辑部有时索性大笑出声。喏，倘若我是作为阔太太回英国的，这一切还会发生吗？断然不会。

收住这些自夸的话之前，再啰嗦几句。提到新近成立来资助我的委员会，我心里自然是又骄傲又欢喜；倘若我放纵这点虚荣，把他们的名字陈列在读者面前，那仅仅因为，下列每一位贵族和绅士都在克里米亚认识我，他们慨然允诺相助，即是公开记录下他们对我当年效劳的评断。而这件事，我还有另一层理由引以为豪：已有公开的说法道明，我今日的窘迫，正源于我对大军的慈善施与和不辍劳瘁。签名者如下：——
