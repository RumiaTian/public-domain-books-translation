## XXVIII / 二十八

===Original===
When Nachor had fully delivered this oration, the king changed countenance for very anger, but his orators and temple-keepers stood speechless, having nothing but a few weak and rotten shreds of argument in reply. But the king’s son rejoiced in spirit and with glad countenance magnified the Lord, who had made a path, where no path was, for them that trusted in him, who by the mouth of a foeman and enemy was establishing the truth; and the leader of error had proved a defender of the right cause.

But the king, although furiously enraged with Nachor, was nevertheless unable to do him any mischief, because of the proclamation already read before all, wherein he urged him to plead without fear in behalf of the Christians. So he himself made answer in many words, and by dark speeches hinted that Nachor should relax his resistance, and be worsted by the argument of the orators. But Nachor the more mightily prevailed, tearing to pieces all their propositions and conclusions and exposing the fallacy of their error. After the debate had been prolonged till well-nigh eventide, the king dismissed the assembly, making as though he would renew the discussion on the morrow.

Then said Ioasaph to the king his father, “As at the beginning, Sir, thou commandedst that the trial should be just, so too crown the end thereof with justice, by doing one or other of these two things. Either allow my teacher to tarry with me tonight, that we may take counsel together as touching those things which we must say unto our adversaries tomorrow: and do thou in turn take thine advisers unto thee, and duly practise yourselves as ye will. Or else deliver thy counsellors to me this night, and take mine to thyself. But if both sides be with thee, mine advocate in tribulation and fear, but thine in joy and refreshment, me thinketh it is not a fair trial, but a tyrannical misuse of power, and a breaking of the covenants.” The king, compelled to yield by the gracefulness of this speech took his wise men and priests to himself, and delivered Nachor to his son, still having hopes of him and thinking fit to keep his agreement.

===Chinese===
纳霍尔全然讲完了这篇演说，王因盛怒而变了脸色；他的众演说家与守庙人却哑口无言地站着，除了几片软弱朽败的论证残片，再无可作回答的。王的儿子却灵里喜乐，面带欢容，尊崇主——那位为倚靠他的人在无路之处开出路径的，那位借仇敌与对头之口坚立真理的；错谬的首领竟成了正义事业的辩护者。

王虽然向纳霍尔大发烈怒，却因着那已当众宣读的谕令——其中他曾劝勉纳霍尔放胆无惧地为基督徒辩护——而无从加害于他。于是王亲自以许多话作答，又用隐晦的言语暗示纳霍尔应当松弛抵挡，被演说家们的论证击败。但纳霍尔却愈加有力地得胜，把他们一切的命题与结论撕得粉碎，揭露出他们错谬的虚妄。辩论延长到将近黄昏，王才解散会众，装作明日要重新开论的样子。

于是约阿萨弗对王他父亲说：「主上，你既在起初吩咐这场审断应当公正，如今也当以公正为它作结，行这两件事中的一件：或是容我的教师今夜与我同住，使我们可以一同商议明天当向我们的对手说什么话；你也照样把你的谋士接到你那里，随你们的意思好好预演。或是今夜把你的谋士交给我，把我的接到你那里。但若两造都在你那里——我的辩护人处在患难惧怕之中，你的却处在喜乐宽舒之中——我看这就不是公正的审断，乃是专横地滥用权柄，是背弃盟约。」王被这番话的得体所折服，只得依从，便把自己的智慧人和祭司接到自己那里，把纳霍尔交给儿子，仍对他存着指望，也认为持守自己的约言是合宜的。


===Original===
The king’s son, therefore, departed unto his own palace, like a conqueror in the Olympic games, and with him went Nachor. When alone, the prince called him and said, “Think not that I am ignorant of thy tale, for I wot, of a surety, that thou art not saintly Barlaam, but Nachor the astrologer; and I marvel how it seemed thee good to act this play, and to think that thou couldst so dull my sight at midday, that I should mistake a wolf for a sheep. But well sung is the proverb, ‘The heart of a fool will conceive folly.’ So this your device and counsel was stale and utterly senseless; but the work that thou hast accomplished is full of wisdom. Wherefore, rejoice, Nachor, and be exceeding glad. I render thee many thanks, that thou hast been today advocate of the truth, and hast not polluted thy lips with foul words and crafty simulation, but hast rather cleansed them from many defilements, and thoroughly proven the error of the gods, as they be wrongly called, and hast established the truth of the Christian faith. I have been zealous to bring thee hither with me for two reasons; that the king might not privily seize and punish thee, because thou spakest not after his heart, and next that I might recompense thee for the favour that thou hast done me today. And what is my recompense for thee? To show thee how to turn from the evil and slippery road which thou hast trodden until now, and to journey along the straight and saving pathway which thou hast avoided, not in ignorance, but by wilful wrongdoing, throwing thyself into depths and precipices of iniquity. Understand then, Nachor, man of understanding as thou art, and be thou zealous to gain Christ only, and the life that is hid with him, and despise this fleeting and corruptible world. Thou shalt not live forever, but, being mortal, shalt depart hence ere long, even as all that have been before thee. And woe betide thee, if, with the heavy load of sin on thy shoulders, thou depart thither where there is righteous judgement and recompense for thy works, and cast it not off, while it is easy to rid thyself thereof!”

Pricked at heart by these words, spake Nachor, “Well said! Sir prince, well said! I do know the true and very God, by whom all things were made, and I wot of the judgement to come, having heard thereof from many texts of the Scriptures. But evil habit and the insolence of the ancient supplanter hath blinded the eyes of my heart, and shed a thick darkness over my reason. But now, at thy word, I will cast away the veil of gloom, and run unto the light of the countenance of the Lord. May be, he will have mercy on me, and will open a door of repentance to his wicked and rebellious servant, even if it seem impossible to me that my sins, which are heavier than the sand, be forgiven; sins, which, wittingly or unwittingly, I have sinned from childhood upwards to this my hoary age.”

When the king’s son heard these words, immediately he arose, and his heart waxed warm, and he began to try to raise Nachor’s courage which was drooping to despair, and to confirm it in the faith of Christ, saying, “Let no doubt about this, Nachor, find place in thy mind. For it is written, God is able of these very stones to raise up children unto Abraham. What meaneth this (as father Barlaam said) except that men beyond hope, stained with all manner of wickedness, can be saved, and become servants of Christ, who, in the exceeding greatness of his love toward mankind, hath opened the gates of heaven to all that turn, barring the way of salvation to none, and receiving with compassion them that repent? Wherefore to all that have entered the vineyard at the first, third, sixth, ninth or eleventh hour there is apportioned equal pay, as saith the holy Gospel: so that even if, until this present time, thou hast waxen old in thy sins, yet if thou draw nigh with a fervent heart, thou shalt gain the same rewards as they who have laboured from their youth upwards.”

于是王子回到自己的宫里，如同在奥林匹亚竞技中夺得冠冕的得胜者一样，纳霍尔也与偕行。独处的时候，王子唤他来说：「你不要以为我不知道你的底细，因为我确实知道，你不是圣洁的巴拉阿姆，乃是占星家纳霍尔。我希奇你怎么看为合宜，要上演这一出戏，又以为你能这样叫我白昼的眼目昏蒙，把狼当作羊。然而俗谚唱得好：『愚昧人的心必怀愚妄。』所以你们这计谋与策划固然陈腐而全然无识；但你所成就的工作却满了智慧。所以，纳霍尔啊，你要喜乐，并且大大喜乐。我多多感谢你，因你今日作了真理的辩护人，没有用污秽的言语和诡诈的假冒玷污你的嘴唇，反倒把它们从许多玷污中洁净了，又彻底证明了那些被妄称为神者的错谬，坚立了基督信仰的真理。我热心带你到这里来与我同在，有两个缘故：一是免得王因你所说的话不合他的心意，而暗中捉拿你、刑罚你；二是要我报答你今日向我所施的恩惠。我拿什么报答你呢？就是指示你，当怎样转离你直到如今所行的又恶又滑的路，走上那正直而施救恩的路——你避开那路，不是出于无知，乃是出于故意的恶行，把自己抛进了不义的深渊与悬崖。所以，纳霍尔啊，你既是通达人，就当明白这事，当热心只求赢得基督，并那与他一同隐藏的生命，轻看这飞逝而必朽坏的世界。你不会永远活着；你既是必死的，不久就要离开这里，正如在你以先的一切人一样。你若负着罪的重担在肩头，往那有公义审判、照各人行为施报的地方去，却不趁着容易摆脱的时候把它甩脱——你就有祸了！」

纳霍尔听见这些话，觉得扎心，就说：「说得好，王子殿下，说得好！我实在认识那又真又实的神，万物都是借他造的；我也知道将来的审判，因为曾从圣经的许多经文听过这事。但恶习和那古时欺哄者的狂妄，使我心中的眼睛瞎了，在我的理性上罩下了厚厚的黑暗。但如今，因着你的话，我要丢弃幽暗的帕子，奔向主面上的光。或许他会怜悯我，向他这邪恶悖逆的仆人开一扇悔改的门；尽管在我看来，我那比海沙更重的罪能得着赦免，似乎是不能的事——这些罪，是我从幼年直到这皓首之年，或有意、或无意地犯下的。」

王子听见这些话，立刻起身，心就火热起来，便设法扶起纳霍尔那颓丧到绝望的勇气，使它在基督的信仰上得以坚固，说：「纳霍尔啊，关于这事，你不要容任何疑惑在你心里寻得立足之地。因为经上记着说：『神能从这些石头中给亚伯拉罕兴起子孙来。』这话是什么意思呢（正如属灵之父巴拉阿姆所说）？岂不是说，那些本已无望、染了各样恶行的人也能得救，成为基督的仆人么？他向世人的爱是何等浩大，已为一切转回的人敞开天门，不向任何人封锁救恩之路，且怀着怜悯接待悔改的人。所以，凡在清早、巳初、午正、申初或酉初进入葡萄园的，所分得的工钱都是一样的，如圣福音所说：这样，即使你直到今日都在罪中老去，只要存着火热的心近前来，就必得着与那些从幼年劳苦直到如今的人同样的赏赐。」

With these and many other words did that saintly youth speak of repentance to that aged sinner Nachor, promising him that Christ was merciful, and pledging him forgiveness, and satisfying him that the good God is alway ready to receive the penitent, and with these words, as it were with ointments, did he mollify that ailing soul and give it perfect health. Nachor at once said unto him, “O prince, more noble in soul even than in outward show, well instructed in these marvellous mysteries, mayst thou continue in thy good confession until the end, and may neither time nor tide ever pluck it out of thine heart! For myself, I will depart straightway in search of my salvation, and will by penance pacify that God whom I have angered: for, except thou will it, I shall see the king’s face no more.” Then was the prince exceeding glad, and joyfully heard his saying. And he embraced and kissed him affectionately; and, when he had prayed earnestly to God, he sent him forth from the palace.

So Nachor stepped forth with a contrite heart, and went bounding into the depths of the desert, like as doth an hart, and came to a den belonging to a monk that had attained to the dignity of the priesthood, and was hiding there for fear of the pressing danger. With a right warm heart knelt Nachor down before him, and washed his feet with his tears, like the harlot of old, and craved holy Baptism. The priest, full of heavenly grace, was passing glad, and did at once begin to instruct him, as the custom is, and after many days, perfected him with baptism in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. And Nachor abode with him, always repentant of his sins, and blessing that God who never willeth that any should perish, but receiveth all that turn again unto him, and lovingly accepteth the penitent.

Now on the morrow when the king heard what had befallen Nachor, he despaired of the hopes that he once had in him: and, seeing those wise and foolish orators of his mightily discomfited, he was at his wits’ end. Them he visited with terrible outrage and dishonour, scourging some severely with whips of oxhide, besmearing their eyes with soot, and casting them away from his presence. He himself began to condemn the impotence of the gods falsely so called, although as yet he refused to look fully at the light of Christ, for the dense cloud of darkness, that enveloped him, still bound the eyes of his heart. Howbeit he no longer honoured his temple-keepers, nor would he keep feasts, nor make drink offerings to his idols, but his mind was tossed between two opinions. On the one hand, he poured scorn on the impotence of his gods; on the other, he dreaded the strictness of the profession of the Gospel, and was hardly to be torn from his evil ways, being completely in slavery to the pleasures of the body, and like a captive drawn towards sinful lusts, and being drunken, as saith Esay, but not with wine, and led as it were with the bridle of evil habit.

===Chinese===
那圣洁的少年就用这些话和许多别的话，向那年老的罪人纳霍尔讲论悔改，向他应许基督是满有怜悯的，向他担保必得赦免，又使他满心确信那良善的神随时都预备接纳悔改的人；他用这些话仿佛用膏油，软化了那患病的心灵，使它得着完全的健康。纳霍尔立刻对他说：「王子啊，你灵魂的高贵更胜于你外表的荣美，你又在这些奇妙的奥迹上受了好教训；愿你持守你美好的认信直到末了，愿时移世易都永不能把它从你心中夺去！至于我自己，我要立刻动身去寻求我的救恩，要以悔罪的工夫平息那被我触怒的神：因为除非你愿意，我必不再见王的面。」王子就大大欢喜，快乐地听了他这话，又深情地拥抱他、与他亲嘴，并且恳切地向神祷告之后，就打发他出宫去了。

于是纳霍尔怀着痛悔的心出来，像鹿一样奔跃，进入旷野的深处，到了一个属于修士的洞窟；那修士已登上司祭的尊位，因迫近的危险躲藏在那里。纳霍尔怀着十分火热的心跪在他面前，像古时的妓女一样，用眼泪洗他的脚，恳求圣洗礼。那司祭满有天上的恩典，极其欢喜，便立即照着惯例开始教导他；过了许多日子，就奉父、子、圣灵的名，以洗礼使他得以成全。纳霍尔便与他同住，常常为自己的罪悔改，又称颂那神——他不愿有一人沉沦，乃接纳一切归向他的人，以慈爱悦纳悔改者。

次日，王听见临到纳霍尔的事，就对自己素来在他身上的指望绝望了；又看见他那些又智慧又愚拙的演说家惨遭击溃，他就智慧无法可施。他便以可怕的暴虐和羞辱加在他们身上：用牛皮鞭狠狠鞭打其中一些人，把煤烟涂抹在他们眼睛上，又将他们从自己面前驱逐出去。他自己也开始谴责那些被妄称为神者的无能；只是他还不肯完全仰望基督的光，因为那笼罩着他的浓黑云雾，仍旧捆绑着他心中的眼睛。然而他不再尊荣他的守庙人，也不再设摆筵席，不再向他的偶像献奠祭；他的心思却在两种意见之间被抛来抛去。一方面，他讥诮他那些神的无能；另一方面，他又惧怕福音宣认的严格，很难从他邪恶的路上被拉回来，因为他完全作了肉体逸乐的奴仆，像俘虏一样被牵向罪恶的情欲，又如以赛亚所说，醉酒却非因酒，仿佛被恶习的嚼环牵引着走。


===Original===
While the king was thus wrestling with two opinions, his noble and truly royal-hearted son dwelt at peace in his palace, proving to all men by his deeds the nobility, order and steadfastness of his nature. Theatres, horse-races, riding to hounds, and all the vain pleasures of youth, the baits that take foolish souls, were reckoned by him as nothing worth. But he hung wholly on the commands of Christ for whom he yearned, his heart being wounded with love divine. For him he longed, who alone is to be longed for, who is all sweetness and desire and aspiration insatiable.

Now, when he came to think upon his teacher Barlaam, and as in a mirror saw his life, his soul was enchanted with love, and he much occupied himself a-thinking how he might see him; and ever carrying his sayings in his heart, he was like the tree in the Psalms planted by the river side, unceasingly watered, and bringing forth unto the Lord his fruits in due season. Many were the souls that he delivered from the snares of the devil, and brought safely unto Christ; for many resorted unto him, and profited by his wholesome words. And not a few left the way of error, and ran toward the word of salvation; while others bade a long farewell to the concerns of the world, and came to the wrestling-school of the monastic life. He himself spent his time in prayers and fastings, and would often offer up this prayer, “O Lord, my Lord and King, in whom I have trusted, to whom I have fled and been delivered from my error, render thou due recompense to Barlaam thy servant, because when I was in error he pointed thee to me, who art the way of truth and life. Forbid me not to behold once more that angel in bodily shape, of whom the world is not worthy, but grant me in his company to finish the residue of my life, that, treading in the footsteps of his conversation, I may be well-pleasing to thee my God and Lord.”

王这样在两种意见之间挣扎的时候，他那高贵而真具王者之心的儿子，却安然住在自己宫里，以自己的行为向众人显明他性情的尊贵、端方与坚定不移。戏园、赛马、纵犬行猎，和少年人一切虚妄的娱乐——那些捕捉愚妄灵魂的饵——在他都被算为不足介意。他却全然系于基督的诫命，切慕着基督，他的心为神圣之爱所伤。他所切慕的，是那位独一配受切慕的主——他就是全然的甘甜，就是愿望，就是那永难餍足的渴慕。

后来，当他思念起他的教师巴拉阿姆，如同在镜中观看他的生平，他的灵就为爱所陶醉，常常思量怎样才能见他一面；又将他的言语常存在心里，就像诗篇中那栽在溪水旁的树，不住地蒙浇灌，按时候向主结出自己的果子。他救拔了许多灵魂脱离魔鬼的网罗，平平安安地领他们归向基督；因为有许多人到他那里去，从他有益的言语中得益处。又有不少人离弃了错谬之路，奔向救恩之道；还有些人与属世的挂虑长久话别，来到修道生活的角力场。他自己则在祷告和禁食中度过光阴，又常常献上这样的祷告：「主啊，我的主、我的王，我所倚靠的，我所投奔、并蒙拯救脱离错谬的，求你将当得的报赏酬报你的仆人巴拉阿姆；因为我在错谬之中的时候，是他把你——真理与生命之道——指示了我。求你不要禁止我再看见那位具血肉之形的天使——本是世界不配有的人；求你恩准我在他的陪伴下度尽余下的年日，使我踏上他行谊的脚踪，得蒙你——我的神、我的主——的喜悦。」
