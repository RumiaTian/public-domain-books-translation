## XIV / 十四

===Original===
Again said Ioasaph, "The Lord God prosper thee, O thou wisest of men! For thou hast gladdened my soul with thine apt and excellent sayings. Wherefore sketch me yet another picture of the vanity of the world, and how a man may pass through it in peace and safety."

Barlaam took up his parable and said, "Hear then a similitude of this matter too. I once heard tell of a great city whose citizens had, from old time, the custom of taking some foreigner and stranger, who knew nothing of their laws and traditions, and of making him their king, to enjoy absolute power, and follow his own will and pleasure without hindrance, until the completion of a year. Then suddenly, while he was living with never a care in rioting and wantonness, without fear, and alway supposing that his reign would only terminate with his life, they would rise up against him, strip him bare of his royal robes, lead him in triumph up and down the city, and thence dispatch him beyond their borders into a distant great island; there, for lack of food and raiment, in hunger and nakedness he would waste miserably away, the luxury and pleasure so unexpectedly showered upon him changed as unexpectedly into woe. In accordance therefore with the unbroken custom of these citizens, a certain man was ordained to the kingship. But his mind was fertile of understanding, and he was not carried away by this sudden access of prosperity, nor did he emulate the heedlessness of the kings that had gone before him, and had been miserably expelled, but his soul was plunged in care and trouble how he might order his affairs. After long and careful search, he learned from a wise counsellor the custom of the citizens, and the place of perpetual banishment, and was taught of him without guile how to ensure himself against this fate. So with this knowledge that within a very little while he must reach that island and leave to strangers this chance kingdom among strangers, he opened the treasures whereof he had awhile absolute and unforbidden use, and took a great store of money and huge masses of gold and silver and precious stones and delivered the same to trusty servants and sent them before him to the island whither he was bound. When the appointed year came to an end, the citizens rose against him, and sent him naked into banishment like those that went before him. But while the rest of these foolish kings, kings only for a season, were sore anhungred, he, that had timely deposited his wealth, passed his time in continual plenty mid dainties free of expense, and, rid of all fear of those mutinous and evil citizens, could count himself happy on his wise forethought.

约阿萨弗又说：「愿主神使你亨通，你这众人中最有智慧的人哪！因你以合宜而卓越的言语，使我的心灵欢喜。求你再为我描画一幅世界虚空的图景，并指明人怎样才能平平安安、安然无恙地经过它。」

巴拉阿姆提起诗歌说：「那么，也请听这件事的一个比喻。我曾听说有一座大城，城中的居民自古有这样的风俗：取一个外方人客旅——他对他们的律法与旧规一无所知——立他作他们的王，让他享绝对的权柄，随自己的心意喜好而行，毫无拦阻，直到一年期满。那时，正当他无忧无虑、纵情宴乐放荡、毫无惧怕，总以为他的王位非到他性命终了决不终止，他们便突然起来攻击他，剥去他的王袍，使他赤身露体，牵着他在城里上下游行示众，随后把他遣出国境，送到一座遥远的大岛上；在那里，因缺粮少衣，他就在饥饿赤裸中悲惨地消耗殆尽，那如此意外倾注在他身上的奢华与逸乐，又同样意外地变成了灾祸。后来，按着这些居民从未间断的惯例，有一个人受立为王。但他的心思富于悟性，没有因这突如其来的昌盛而神魂颠倒，也不效法那些先于他作王、后来悲惨被逐之王的漫不经心，他的心反倒沉入思虑烦忧，盘算着怎样料理自己的事务。经过长久殷勤的查究，他从一位智慧的谋士那里得知了居民的风俗和那永久流放之地，又蒙他毫无诡诈地指教了保全自己、免遭这厄运的方略。他既知道，不多几时他必到那岛上去，要把这在陌生人中间偶然得来的国度留给陌生人，就打开了那些他一时得享绝对而无禁地使用的宝库，取出大宗的钱财和巨量的金银宝石，交给忠信的仆人，差他们先往他自己所要去的岛上。所定的年日一满，居民果然起来攻击他，叫他像先前的诸王一样赤身被放逐。但其余那些愚昧的王——只作王一季的王——都在挨饿受苦；惟独他，因及时寄存了财富，就在不花钱的珍馐之间常享丰足，又脱尽了那些悖逆凶恶之民的威胁，能因自己明智的先见而自庆有福。

"Understand thou, therefore, that the city is this vain and deceitful world; that the citizens are the principalities and powers of the devils, the rulers of the darkness of this world, who entice us by the soft bait of pleasure, and counsel us to consider corruptible and perishable things as incorruptible, as though the enjoyment that cometh from them were coexistent with us, and immortal as we. Thus then are we deceived; we have taken no thought concerning the things which are abiding and eternal, and have laid up in store for ourselves no treasure for that life beyond, when of a sudden there standeth over us the doom of death. Then, then at last do those evil and cruel citizens of darkness, that received us, dispatch us stript of all worldly goods﻿—for all our time has been wasted on their service﻿—and carry us off 'to a dark land and a gloomy, to a land of eternal darkness, where there is no light, nor can one behold the life of men.' As for that good counsellor, who made known all the truth and taught that sagacious and wise king the way of salvation, understand thou that I, thy poor and humble servant, am he, who am come hither for to show thee the good and infallible way to lead thee to things eternal and unending, and to counsel thee to lay up all thy treasure there; and I am come to lead thee away from the error of this world, which, to my woe, I also loved, and clave to its pleasures and delights. But, when I perceived, with the unerring eyes of my mind how all human life is wasted in these things that come and go; when I saw that no man hath aught that is stable and steadfast, neither the rich in his wealth, nor the mighty in his strength, nor the wise in his wisdom, nor the prosperous in his prosperity, nor the luxurious in his wantonness, nor he that dreameth of security of life in that vain and feeble security of his dreams, nor any man in any of those things that men on earth commend ('tis like the boundless rush of torrents that discharge themselves into the deep sea, thus fleeting and temporary are all present things); then, I say, I understood that all such things are vanity, and that their enjoyment is naught; and, that even as the past is all buried in oblivion, be it past glory, or past kingship, or the splendour of rank, or amplitude of power, or arrogance of tyranny, or aught else like them, so also present things will vanish in the darkness of the days to come. And, as I am myself of the present, I also shall doubtless be subject to its accustomed change; and, even as my fathers before me were not allowed to take delight forever in the present world, so also shall it be with me. For I have observed how this tyrannical and troublesome world treateth mankind, shifting men hither and thither, from wealth to poverty, and from poverty to honour, carrying some out of life and bringing others in, rejecting some that are wise and understanding, making the honourable and illustrious dishonoured and despised, but seating others who are unwise and of no understanding upon a throne of honour, and making the dishonoured and obscure to be honoured of all."

「所以你要明白：那城就是这虚妄而诡诈的世界；那些居民就是众鬼魔中的执政者、掌权者，就是管辖这幽暗世界的统治者；他们用享乐作柔软的饵诱惑我们，劝我们把那必朽坏、必衰残的事物看作不朽坏的，仿佛从它们而来的享受能与我们同存，与我们一样不死。我们就这样受了欺哄；从未思念那常存到永远的事，也没有为那边的人生为自己积存什么财宝，死亡的定案便突然临到我们头上。那时——唯有到那时——那些收留我们的幽暗中凶恶残忍的居民，才把我们的属世财物剥得一无所剩（因为我们全部的年日都虚耗在服事他们上了），把我们掳去，『送到黑暗阴沉之地，送到永远的黑暗之地，那里没有光，也没有人得见世人的生命』。至于那位良善的谋士——他向那精明智慧的王显明了一切真情，将救恩之道指教给他——你要明白，我，你这贫穷卑微的仆人，就是他；我到这里来，为要指示你那条美善而无误的路，领你进入永恒无穷的事，并劝你将一切的财宝都积存在那里；我来，也是要领你脱离这世界的错谬——这世界，可叹我自己也曾爱过，也曾紧恋它的逸乐与欢愉。但是，当我借着我心思无误的眼目看明，人的一生尽都虚耗在这些来去无常的事物上；又看见没有任何人在任何事物上有什么稳固坚定的分——富人的财富里没有，强者的力量里没有，智者的智慧里没有，顺遂者的顺遂里没有，宴乐者的放纵里没有，那在梦幻般虚妄脆弱的保障中梦想生命平安的也没有；地上众人所称赞的事物，没有一样例外（这就好比无边无际的急流倾泻入深海，今世诸事就是这样短暂易逝）——那时，我说，我才明白这一切尽是虚空，它们的享受算不得什么；又明白正如过去的事全被埋没在遗忘里——无论是往昔的荣耀、往昔的王位、尊衔的显赫、权势的浩大、暴政的骄横，或任何与之相类的事——现今的事也必照样消失在将来世代的黑暗中。我既属乎现今，也必受它素常的变迁；正如在我以先的列祖不容在今世永远享乐，我也要如此。因为我已观察过，这残暴多扰的世界怎样对待世人：把人从这里迁到那里，从富足迁到贫穷，又从贫穷迁到尊荣；把有些人带出人生，把另一些人带进来；撇弃一些聪明有见识的，叫尊贵显耀的蒙羞受贱；却使一些愚昧无见识的坐上尊荣的宝座，叫卑贱无闻的受众人尊崇。

===Original===
"One may see how the race of mankind may never abide before the face of the cruel tyranny of the world. But, as when a dove fleeing from an eagle or a hawk flitteth from place to place, now beating against this tree, now against that bush, and then anon against the clefts of the rocks and all manner of bramble-thorns, and, nowhere finding any safe place of refuge, is wearied with continual tossing and crossing to and fro, so are they which are flustered by the present world. They labour painfully under unreasoning impulse, on no sure or firm bases: they know not to what goal they are driving, nor whither this vain life leadeth them; this vain life, whereto they have in miserable folly subjected themselves, choosing evil instead of good, and pursuing vice instead of goodness; and they know not who shall gather the fruits of their many idle labours, whether it be a kinsman or a stranger, and, as oft times it haps, not even a friend or acquaintance at all, but an enemy and foeman."

"On all these things, and others akin to them, I held judgement in the tribunal of my soul, and I came to hate my whole life that had been wasted in these vanities, while I still lived engrossed in earthly things. But when I had put off from my soul the lust thereof, and cast it from me, then was there revealed unto me the true good, to fear God and do his will; for this I saw to be the sum of all good. This also is called the beginning of wisdom, and perfect wisdom. For life is without pain and reproach to those that hold by her, and safe to those who lean upon her as upon the Lord. So, when I had set my reason on the unerring way of the commandments of the Lord, and had surely learned that there is nothing froward or perverse therein, and that it is not full of chasms and rocks, nor of thorns and thistles, but lieth altogether smooth and even, rejoicing the eyes of the traveller with the brightest sights, making beautiful his feet, and shoeing them with 'the preparation of the Gospel of peace,' that he may walk safely and without delay, this way, then, I rightly chose above all others, and began to rebuild my soul's habitation, which had fallen into ruin and decay."

「由此可见，人这族类在世界残酷的暴政面前，总站立不住。正如鸽子逃避鹰或隼，从一处飞到一处，忽而扑向这棵树，忽而扑向那丛灌木，转眼又扑向岩石的裂缝和各样的荆棘蒺藜，寻不见一处安全的栖身之所，被不住的翻飞往返累得疲乏；凡被今世搅得慌乱的人，也是如此。他们在无理性的冲动之下痛苦劳碌，没有任何确实或稳固的根基；不知道自己奔向什么终点，也不知道这虚妄的生命把他们领往何处——这虚妄的生命，他们在可悲的愚昧中使自己服于其下，拣选恶而不拣选善，追逐败坏而不追逐良善；他们又不知道，他们许多徒然劳碌的果实，究竟要给谁来收——是亲族，还是外人；而且正如常常碰上的，往往连朋友故旧都全不是，竟是仇敌与仇雠。」

「对这一切事，以及与它们相类的别样事，我在我魂的法庭上秉公判断；我便恨恶我这虚耗在这些虚空里的整个生涯，虽然那时我仍沉溺于属地的事物。但当我把它的情欲从魂里脱下、从我身上抛开，那时，真正的善就向我显明了：敬畏神，遵行他的旨意；因为我看明，这是一切善的总纲。这也称为智慧的开端，是完全的智慧。因为凡持定她的，生命就无痛楚、无羞辱；凡倚靠她如同倚靠主的，就必安稳。这样，当我既把我的理性安置在主诫命那无误的路上，确实知道了其中毫无乖僻偏邪，那路也没有坑坎与岩石，没有荆棘与蒺藜，乃是全然平坦平直，以最明亮的光景愉悦行路人的眼目，美化他的脚，『用平安福音当作预备走路的鞋穿在脚上』，使他可以安然行走、毫不耽延——这时，我就正当地拣选了这条路，过于一切别的路，并开始重建我魂的居所，就是那已倾塌败落了的。」

"In such wise was I devising mine estate, and establishing mine unstable mind, when I heard the words of a wise teacher calling loudly to me thus, 'Come ye out,' said he, 'all ye that will to be saved. Be ye separate from the vanity of the world, for the fashion thereof quickly passeth away, and behold it shall not be. Come ye out, without turning back, not for nothing and without reward, but winning supplies for travelling to life eternal, for ye are like to journey a long road, needing much supplies from hence, and ye shall arrive at the place eternal that hath two regions, wherein are many mansions; one of which places God hath prepared for them that love him and keep his commandments, full of all manner of good things; and they that attain thereto shall live forever in incorruption, enjoying immortality without death, where pain and sorrow and sighing are fled away. But the other place is full of darkness and tribulation and pain, prepared for the devil and his angels, wherein also shall be cast they who by evil deeds have deserved it, who have bartered the incorruptible and eternal for the present world, and have made themselves fuel for eternal fire.'"

"When I heard this voice, and recognized the truth, I did my diligence to attain to that abode, that is free from all pain and sorrow, and full of security and all good things, whereof I have knowledge now only in part, being but a babe in my spiritual life, and seeing the sights yonder as through mirrors and riddles; but when that which is perfect is come, and I shall see face to face, then that which is in part shall be done away. Wherefore I thank God through Jesus Christ our Lord; for the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and of death, and hath opened mine eyes to see clearly that the will of the flesh is death, but the will of the Spirit is life and peace. And even as I did discern the vanity of present things and hate them with a perfect hatred, so likewise I counsel thee to decide thereon, that thou mayest treat them as something alien and quickly passing away, and mayest remove all thy store from earth and lay up for thyself in the incorruptible world a treasure that can not be stolen, wealth inexhaustible, in that place whither thou must shortly fare, that when thou comest thither thou mayest not be destitute, but be laden with riches, after the manner of that aptest of parables that I lately showed thee."

「我正这样经营自己的家业、坚定自己动摇的心思，忽然听见一位智慧教师的话，大声呼召我说：『你们出来吧，』他说，『凡愿意得救的，都要出来；务要从世界的虚空中分别出来，因为世界的样子快要过去，看哪，它必归于无有。你们出来吧，不可回转；这不是徒然的，也不是没有赏报的，乃是要赢得前往永生的旅途给养，因为你们要走一条长路，需要从这里带上许多粮饷。你们必到达那有两个境界的永恒之地，那里有许多住处：其中一处，是神为爱他、守他诫命的人所预备的，满了各样的美物；凡到达那里的，必永远活着、不复朽坏，享受无死的不朽，那里疼痛、忧愁、叹息尽都逃避。另一处却满了黑暗、患难与疼痛，是为魔鬼和他的使者所预备的，凡以恶行配得那地方的，也必被丢在其中——他们把不朽坏的、永远的换了今世，把自己作成了永火的燃料。』」

「我听见了这声音，认出了真理，就殷勤竭力，要达到那居所——那脱离一切疼痛忧愁、满有安稳与各样美物的地方。关于那地方，我如今所知道的还只是局部，因为我在属灵的生命上还不过是个婴孩，只能对着镜子、藉着谜语观看那边的景象；及至那完全的来到，我就要面对面看见了，那时，局部的必归于无有。所以，我藉着我们的主耶稣基督感谢神；因为赐生命圣灵的律，在基督耶稣里释放了我，使我脱离罪和死的律了；他又开我的眼睛，叫我清清楚楚地看见：体贴肉体的，就是死；体贴圣灵的，乃是生命、平安。我既辨明了现今之事的虚空而切切地恨恶它们，也就照样劝你在此事上下一决断，好叫你看待它们如同身外速逝之物，把你一切的积蓄从地上挪去，在那不能朽坏的世界里为自己积攒不能偷去的财宝、无穷无尽的财富，积在你不久必去的地方；这样，你到了那里，就不至穷乏，反得满载丰富的财物，照着我近来指给你看的那则最贴切的比喻。」
