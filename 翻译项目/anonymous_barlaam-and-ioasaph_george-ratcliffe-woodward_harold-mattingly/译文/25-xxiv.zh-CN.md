## XXIV / 二十四

===Original===
After the monks had made this godly end, the king bade Araches, his chief councillor, now that they had failed of their first plan, to look to the second and summon the man Nachor. At dead of night Araches repaired to his cave (he dwelt in the desert practising the arts of divination), and told him of their plans, and returned to the king at daybreak. Again he demanded horsemen, and made as though he went in quest of Barlaam. When he was gone forth, and was walking the desert, a man was seen to issue from a ravine. Araches gave command to his men to pursue him. They took and brought him before their master. When asked who he was, what his religion and what his name, the man declared himself a Christian and gave his name as Barlaam, even as he had been instructed. Araches made great show of joy, apprehended him and returned quickly to the king, and told his tale and produced his man. Then said the king in the hearing of all present, “Art thou the devil’s workman, Barlaam?” But he denied it, saying, “I am God’s workman, not the devil’s. Revile me not; for I am thy debtor to render me much thanks, because I have taught thy son to serve God, and have turned him from error to the true God, and have schooled him in all manner of virtue.” Feigning anger, again spake the king, “Though I ought to allow thee never a word, and give thee no room for defence, but rather do thee to death without question, yet such is my humanity that I will bear with thine effrontery until on a set day I try thy cause. If thou be persuaded by me, thou shalt receive pardon: if not, thou shalt die the death.” With these words he delivered him to Araches, commanding that he should be most strictly guarded.

On the morrow the king removed thence, and came back to his own palace, and it was blazoned abroad that Barlaam was captured, so that the king’s son heard thereof and was exceeding sad at heart, and could in no wise refrain from weeping. With groans and lamentations he importuned God, and called upon him to succour the aged man. Nor did the good God despise his complaint, for he is loving with them that abide him in the day of trouble, and knoweth them that fear him. Wherefore in a night-vision he made known the whole plot to the young prince, and strengthened and cheered him for the trial of his righteousness. So, when the prince awoke from sleep, he found that his heart, erstwhile so sore and heavy, was now full of joyance, courage and pleasant light. But the king rejoiced at that which he had done and planned, imagining that he was well advised, and showering thanks on Araches. But wickedness lied to itself, to use the words of holy David, and righteousness overcame iniquity, completely overthrowing it, and causing the memorial thereof to perish with sound, as shall be proven by our tale.

修士们这样敬虔地殉道之后，王见第一计既败，便吩咐首席谋臣阿拉凯斯着手第二条计策，去召那纳霍尔来。夜阑人静，阿拉凯斯去到他的洞穴（他住在旷野，修习占卜之术），把他们的计谋告诉了他，天一破晓就回到王那里。他又索要了骑兵，装作出去寻找巴拉阿姆的样子。他出城在旷野行走时，见有一人从峡谷中出来。阿拉凯斯吩咐手下人追赶。他们拿住他，带到主人面前。及至问他是什么人、信奉什么教、名叫什么，那人便自称是基督徒，报名叫巴拉阿姆，正如先前所授意的一样。阿拉凯斯装出极大的欢喜，拿住他，急忙回到王那里，禀明原委，献上他寻得的人。于是王当着在场众人说：「巴拉阿姆，你是魔鬼的工人么？」他却否认说：「我是神的工人，不是魔鬼的工人。不要辱骂我；因为我教你儿子事奉神，领他离弃错谬归向真神，又在各样德行上教训了他，你倒欠我许多的感谢才是。」王佯装忿怒，又说：「我本不该容你说一句话，不给你分辩的余地，只该不问情由将你处死；但我如此仁慈，且容忍你的放肆，直到定期之日审问你的案子。你若听我的劝，必得赦免；若不听，必被处死。」说完这话，他就把他交付阿拉凯斯，吩咐要极其严紧地看守。

次日，王从那里起行，回到自己的宫殿；又有话传扬开来，说巴拉阿姆被擒了。王的儿子听见这话，心里极其忧伤，怎么也忍不住流泪。他呻吟哀叹，恳切求告神，呼求他救助那位老人。良善的神也不轻看他的哀诉，因为在患难的日子，他以恩慈待投靠他的人，且认得敬畏他的人。于是神在夜间的异象中，将这全部计谋指示了年轻的王子，为着他义的试炼刚强他、安慰他。王子从睡中醒来，觉得自己的心——先前那样伤痛沉重——如今满了喜乐、勇气与悦人的光。王却为自己所行所谋的欢喜，自以为计虑得当，向阿拉凯斯频频称谢。然而，用可称颂的大卫的话说，恶孽欺哄了它自己；公义胜过了不义，将它完全倾覆，叫它的纪念随一声响灭没净尽，正如我们的故事所要证明的。

After two days the king visited his son’s palace. When his son came forth for to meet him, instead of kissing him, as was his wont, the father put on a show of distress and anger, and entered the royal chamber, and there sat down frowning. Thou hast dishonoured my grey hairs, and taken away the light of mine eyes, and loosed the strength of my sinews; ‘for the thing which I greatly feared concerning thee is come upon me, and that which I was afraid of hath come unto me.’ Thou art become a joy to mine enemies, and a laughingstock to mine adversaries. With untutored mind and childish judgement thou hast followed the teaching of the deceivers and esteemed the counsel of the malicious above mine; thou hast forsaken the worship of our gods and become the servant of a strange God. Child, wherefore hast thou done this? I hoped to bring thee up in all safety, and have thee for the staff and support of mine old age, and leave thee, as is most meet, to succeed me in my kingdom, but thou wast not ashamed to play against me the part of a relentless foe. And shouldst thou not rather have listened to me, and followed my injunctions, than have obeyed the idle and foolish pratings of that crafty old knave, who taught thee to choose a sour life instead of a sweet, and abandon the charms of dalliance, to tread the hard and rough road, which the Son of Mary ordereth men to go? Dost thou not fear the displeasure of the most puissant gods, lest they strike thee with lightning, or quell thee with thunderbolt, or overwhelm thee in the yawning earth, because thou hast rejected and scorned those deities that have so richly blessed us, and adorned our brow with the kingly diadem, and made populous nations to be our servants, that, beyond my hope, in answer to my prayer and supplication, allowed thee to be born, and see the sweet life of day, and hast joined thyself unto the Crucified, duped by the hopes of his servants who tell thee fables of worlds to come, and drivel about the resurrection of dead bodies, and bring in a thousand more absurdities to catch fools? But now, dearest son, if thou hast any regard for me thy father, bid a long farewell to these longwinded follies, and come sacrifice to the gracious gods, and let us propitiate them with hecatombs and drink-offerings, that they may grant thee pardon for thy fall; for they be able and strong to bless and to punish. And wouldst thou have an example of that which I say? Behold us, who by them have been advanced to this honour, repaying them for their kindness by honouring their worshippers and chastising the runagates.”

过了两天，王到他儿子的宫里去。儿子出来迎接，他非但不像惯常那样与他亲嘴，反装出忧愁忿怒的模样，进入王室内室，皱着眉坐下。然后唤儿子来，说：「孩子啊，这响在我耳中、使我魂里消沉的，是什么风声呢？我料想，人得子之时心里的欢乐，从来没有一人像我得你时那样满足；我也深信，人被儿子苦待得这样悲惨的，从来没有一人像我现在被你所待。你辱没了我的白发，夺去了我眼中的光，松解了我筋骨的力量；『因我所恐惧的临到我身，我所惧怕的迎我而来。』你成了我仇敌的喜乐，成了攻击我之人的笑柄。你以未受教的心思、孩童的判断，随从了那些欺哄者的教训，看重恶意之人的计谋过于我的；你离弃了对我们诸神的敬拜，作了生疏之神的仆人。孩子啊，你为什么行这事呢？我本指望平平安安地将你养大，使你作我老年的杖与扶持，又照最合宜的安排，留你继承我的国；你却不知羞耻，对我扮演了无情仇敌的角色。你岂不本该听我的话、遵我的禁令，而不该听从那奸猾老贼闲散愚妄的絮聒么？——他教你拣选苦涩的生活而舍弃甘甜，离弃温存嬉戏的媚惑，去踏那马利亚之子吩咐人行的又艰苦又崎岖的路。你难道不惧怕至能诸神的不悦，惟恐他们用闪电击打你，用雷轰扑灭你，将你吞灭在裂开的地中么？——因为你已弃绝并轻慢了那些厚厚赐福我们的神明：他们以帝王的冠冕装饰我们的额，叫人民众多的列邦作我们的仆役，又因着我的祷告恳求，超过我所盼望的，容你得以出生，得见白昼甘美的生活；你却受他仆人们所存之指望的欺哄，将自己联于那被钉十字架者——那些仆人向你讲述来世的虚谈，胡诌什么死人身体的复活，又引进千般别的荒谬来网罗愚人。但如今，最亲爱的儿子啊，你若对我你这为父的还有一点顾念，就同这些冗长的愚妄永远道别吧，来向恩慈的诸神献祭；让我们以百牛大祭与奠祭平息他们的怒气，好叫他们赦免你的堕落；因为无论祝福还是刑罚，他们都有能力大力施行。你要我所说的事要个例证么？看我们吧：我们蒙他们升到这尊荣，就报答他们的恩慈——敬重敬拜他们的人，惩治那些叛逃者。」

Now when the king had ended all this idle parleying, gainsaying and slandering of our religion, and belauding and praising of his idolatry, the saintly young prince saw that the matter needed no further to be hid in a corner, but to be lighted and made plain to the eyes of all; and, full of boldness and courage, he said:

“That which I have done, sir, I will not deny. I have fled from darkness and run to the light: I have left error and joined the household of truth: I have deserted the service of devils, and joined the service of Christ, the Son and Word of God the Father, at whose decree the world was brought out of nothing; who, after forming man out of clay, breathed into him the breath of life, and set him to live in a paradise of delight, and, when he had broken his commandment and was become subject unto death, and had fallen into the power of the dread ruler of this world, did not fail him, but wrought diligently to bring him back to his former honour. Wherefore he, the framer of all Creation and maker of our race, became man for our sake, and, coming from a holy Virgin’s womb; on earth conversed with men: for us ungrateful servants did the master endure death, even the death of the Cross, that the tyranny of sin might be destroyed, that the former condemnation might be abolished, that the gates of heaven might be open to us again. Thither he hath exalted our nature, and set it on the throne of glory, and granted to them that love him an everlasting kingdom and joys beyond all that tongue can tell, or ear can hear. He is the mighty and only potentate, King of kings and Lord of lords, whose might is invincible, and whose lordship is beyond compare, who only is holy and dwelleth in holiness, who with the Father and with the Holy Ghost is glorified; into this faith I have been baptized. And I acknowledge and glorify and worship One God in Three persons, of one substance, and not to be confounded, increate and immortal, eternal, infinite, boundless, without body, without passions, immutable, unchangeable, undefinable, the fountain of goodness, righteousness and everlasting light, maker of all things visible and invisible, containing and sustaining all things, provident for all, ruler and King of all. Without him was there nothing made, nor without his providence can aught subsist. He is the life of all, the support of all, the light of all, being wholly sweetness and insatiable desire, the summit of aspiration. To leave God, then, who is so good, so wise, so mighty, and to serve impure devils, makers of all sinful lusts, and to assign worship to deaf and dumb images, that are not, and never shall be, were not that the extreme of folly and madness? When was there ever heard utterance or language from their lips? When have they given even the smallest answer to their bedesmen? When have they walked, or received any impression of sense? Those of them that stand have never thought of sitting down; and those that sit have never been seen to rise. From an holy man have I learned the ugliness, ill savour and insensibility of these idols, and, moreover, the rottenness and weakness of the devils that operate in them and by them deceive you; and I loathe their wickednesses and, hating them with a perfect hatred, have joined myself to the living and true God, and him will I serve until my latest breath, that my spirit also may return into his hands. When these unspeakable blessings came in my path I rejoiced to be freed from the bondage of evil devils, and to be reclaimed from dire captivity and to be illumined with the light of the countenance of the Lord. But my soul was distressed and divided asunder, that thou, my lord and father, didst not share in my blessings. Yet I feared the stubbornness of thy mind, and kept my grief to myself, not wishing to anger thee; but, without ceasing, I prayed God to draw thee to himself, and call thee back from the long exile that thou hast imposed upon thyself, a runagate alas! from righteousness, and a servant of all sin and wickedness. But sith thou thyself, O my father, hast brought mine affairs to light, hear the sum of my resolve: I will not be false to my covenant with Christ; no, I swear it by him that bought me out of slavery with his own precious blood; even if I must needs die a thousand deaths for his sake, die I will. Knowing then how matters now stand with me, prithee, no longer trouble thyself in endeavouring to persuade me to change my good confession. For as it were a thankless and never ending task for thee to try to grasp the heavens with thy hand, or to dry up the waters of the sea, so hard were it for thee to change me. Either then now listen to my counsel, and join the household of Christ, and so thou shalt gain blessings past man’s understanding, and we shall be fellows with one another by faith, even as by nature; or else, be well assured, I shall depart thy sonship, and serve my God with a clear conscience.”

王这样把一切空言的支吾、对我们信仰的抗拒与毁谤、并对他偶像崇拜的夸赞颂扬说完之后，圣洁的少年王子看出，这事不必再藏在角落里，乃当点起灯来，显明在众人眼前；他就满有胆量与勇气，说：

「主啊，我所行的事，我不否认。我逃避了黑暗，奔向了光明；我离弃了错谬，归入了真理的家；我脱离了事奉鬼魔，加入了事奉基督——就是父神的子和道：万有是凭他的命从无有中被领出来的。他用尘土造了人之后，将生气吹在他里面，安置他住在悦乐的乐园；及至人违背了他的诫命，服在死权之下，落在今世可畏掌权者的权势里，他也没有丢弃他，倒殷勤劳作，要领他归回先前的尊荣。所以，他——万有的塑造者、我们族类的造作者——为我们成了人，自圣童女的胎中出来，在地上与人往来同处：为我们这些忘恩的仆人，主忍受了死，且死在十字架上，好叫罪的暴虐得以毁灭，从前的定罪得以废去，诸天的门得以重新向我们敞开。他已将我们的本性高举到那里，安置在荣耀的宝座上，又赐给爱他的人一个永远的国，并那舌头不能尽述、耳朵不能听闻的喜乐。他是大能者，独一的主宰，万王之王、万主之主；他的权能无可敌挡，他的主权无可比拟；他独一为圣，住在圣洁之中，与父与圣灵同受荣耀：我已受洗归入这信仰。我又承认、荣耀并敬拜独一的神，在三个位格中，同质而不可混滥，非受造而不死，永恒、无限、无涯，无形躯、无情欲，不变、不易、不可界说，是良善、公义与永远之光的泉源，是一切可见与不可见之物的造作者，包含万有、托住万有，眷顾万有，统辖万有、作万有之王。没有他，就没有一物造成；没有他的护理，就没有一物能以存立。他是万有的生命、万有的扶持、万有的光，全然是甘甜，是不能餍足的愿欲，是众望所归的极峰。这样，离开如此良善、如此智慧、如此大能的神，去事奉那些污秽的鬼魔——一切罪恶私欲的制造者——又将敬拜归于那些又聋又哑的像——它们不是存有的，也永不会存有——这岂不是愚妄与癫狂的极点么？何曾有人听见他们口中发出言语或乡谈？他们几曾向他们的祈求者给过哪怕最小的回答？他们几曾行走，或领受过任何官觉的印象？那些站着的，从未想过坐下；那些坐着的，从未被人看见起立。我从一位圣洁的人身上，学知了这些偶像的丑陋、恶味与无知觉，又学知了在其中运行、并借它们欺哄你们的鬼魔的朽坏与软弱；我憎恶它们的诸般邪恶，用完全的恨恨它们，已将自己联于那又真又活的神，并且我要事奉他，直到我末了一口气，好叫我的灵也归还他手。当这些不可言喻的福分临到我路途上时，我欢喜自己得脱离恶鬼的捆绑，从惨酷的俘掳中被赎回，蒙主面上的光照亮。但我的魂忧愁、被撕为两半，因为我主我的父啊，你未与我同得这些福分。只是我怕你心思的顽梗，便将忧伤藏在自己心里，不愿激怒你；却不住地求神吸引你归他自己，召你脱离你加在自己身上的长久流亡——哀哉，你这离弃了公义的逃亡者，作了一切罪恶不义的仆役！但父啊，你既亲自叫我如今的事显露出来，就请听我决志的总意：我必不背弃我与基督所立的约；不，我指着他起誓——他曾用自己的宝血将我从奴役中买来——哪怕我必须为他死上一千次的死，我也甘愿死。你既知道我如今的光景，就请你不要再劳心，想劝我背弃我美好的认信了。因为你若想以手握住诸天，或使海中的水枯干，那是无功而无尽的劳苦；你要改变我，也正是同样艰难。所以，你如今或是听从我的劝告，加入基督的家，这样你必得着那过于人所能领会的福分，我们在信仰上也必彼此为伴，正如在天性上为伴一样；不然，你要深信，我必退出你儿子的名分，存着无亏的良心事奉我的神。」

Now when the king heard all these words, he was furiously enraged: and, seized with ungovernable anger, he cried out wrathfully against him, and gnashed his teeth fiercely, like any madman. “And who,” said he, “is blameable for all my misfortunes but myself, who have dealt with thee so kindly, and cared for thee as no father before? Hence the perversity and contrariness of thy mind, gathering strength by the licence that I gave thee, hath made thy madness to fall upon mine own pate. Rightly prophesied the astrologers in thy nativity that thou shouldest prove a knave and villain, an impostor and rebellious son. But now, if thou wilt make void my counsel, and cease to be my son, I will become thine enemy, and entreat thee worse than ever man yet entreated his foes.”

Again said Ioasaph, “Why, O king, hast thou been kindled to wrath? Art thou grieved that I have gained such bliss? Why, what father was ever seen to be sorrowful in the prosperity of his son? Would not such an one be called an enemy rather than a father? Therefore will I no more call thee my father, but will withdraw from thee, as a man fleeth from a snake, if I know that thou grudgest me my salvation, and with violent hand forcest me to destruction. If thou wilt force me, and play the tyrant, as thou hast threatened, be assured that thou shalt gain nought thereby save to exchange the name of father for that of tyrant and murderer. It were easier for thee to attain to the ways of the eagle, and, like him, cleave the air, than to alter my loyalty to Christ, and that good confession that I have confessed in him. But be wise, O my father, and shake off the rheum and mist from the eyes of thy mind, lift them aloft and look upward to view the light of my God that enlighteneth all around, and be thyself, at last, enlightened with this light most sweet. Why art thou wholly given up to the passions and desires of the flesh, and why is there no looking upward? Know thou that all flesh is grass and all the glory of man as the flower of grass. The grass withereth, the flower thereof falleth away; but the word of my Lord, which by the gospel is preached unto all, shall endure forever. Why then dost thou thus madly cling to and embrace that glory, which, like spring flowers, fadeth and perisheth, and to beastly unsavoury wantonness, and to the abominable passions of the belly and the members thereunder, which for a season please the senses of fools, but afterwards make returns more bitter than gall, when the shadows and dreams of this vain life are passed away, and the lovers thereof, and workers of iniquity are imprisoned in the perpetual pain of dark and unquenchable fire, where the worm that sleepeth not gnaweth forever, and where the fire burneth without ceasing and without quenching through endless ages? And with these sinners alas! thou too shalt be imprisoned and grievously tormented, and shalt bitterly rue thy wicked counsels, and bitterly regret thy days that now are, and think upon my words, but there shall be no advantage in repentance; for in death there is no confession and repentance. But the present is the set time for work: the future for reward. Even if the pleasures of the present world were not evanescent and fleeting, but were to endure forever with their owners, not even thus should any man choose them before the gifts of Christ, and the good things that pass man’s understanding. Soothly, as the sun surpasseth in radiance and brightness the dead of night, even so, and much more so, doth the happiness promised to those that love God excel in glory and magnificence all earthly kinship and glory; and there is utter need for a man to choose the more excellent before the more worthless. And forasmuch as everything here is fleeting and subject to decay, and passeth and vanisheth as a dream, and as a shadow and vision of sleep; and as one may sooner trust the unstable breezes, or the tracks of a ship passing over the waves, than the prosperity of men, what simplicity, nay, what folly and madness it is to choose the corruptible and perishable, the weak things of no worth, rather than the incorruptible and everlasting, the imperishable and endless, and, by the temporal enjoyment of these things, to forfeit the eternal fruition of the happiness to come! Wilt thou not understand this, my father? Wilt thou not haste past the things which haste pass thee, and attach thyself to that which endureth? Wilt thou not prefer a home land to a foreign land, light to darkness, the spirit to the flesh, eternal life to the shadow of death, the indestructible to the fleeting? Wilt thou not escape from the grievous bondage of the cruel prince of this world, I mean the evil one, the devil, and become the servant of the good, tenderhearted, and all merciful Lord? 王听见这一切话，就狂烈地动怒：他被不能遏制的忿怒抓住，向他怒声呵斥，咬牙切齿，如同疯子一般。他说：「我这一切不幸，除了我自己，还能怪谁呢？我这样恩待你，照看得比从前任何为父的都周全。这样，你心思的乖僻与悖戾，仗着我所给的宽容渐渐蓄起势来，就叫你的癫狂落在我自己的头颅上。占星家论你的降生所预言的，果然不错：你必显为奸恶之徒与无赖，一个欺世盗名之人和悖逆之子。但如今，你若要使我的计谋落空，不再作我的儿子，我就要作你的仇敌，苦待你比历来任何人苦待仇敌更甚。」

约阿萨弗又说：「王啊，你为什么燃起了忿怒？你忧伤，是因我得着了这样的福分么？唉，何曾有父亲见儿子昌盛而忧愁的呢？这样的人，岂不当称为仇敌，过于称为父亲么？所以我若知道你妒忌我的救恩，以强暴的手逼我入于灭亡，我就必不再称你为父，必远离你，如同人逃避毒蛇一样。你若定要强迫我，如你所威吓的施行暴虐，你要确知，你必一无所获，不过把父亲的名换作暴君与凶手的名而已。你想达到鹰的路径、像鹰那样翱翔剖开空气，倒比改变我对基督的忠贞、改变我在他里面所认信的那美好认信更容易些。但我的父啊，你要有智慧，抖落你心思眼目上的眵翳与迷雾，将它们高举，向上仰望我神的光——那光照亮四围的一切——也愿你终于亲自蒙这至甜的光所照亮。你为什么全然沉溺于肉体的情欲和私欲？为什么不向上仰望呢？你要知道，凡有血气的尽都如草，他的美荣都像草上的花。草必枯干，花必凋谢；惟有我主的道——就是借福音传给众人的——必存到永远。那么，你为什么这样癫狂地恋慕、抱住那如春花般凋谢朽坏的荣耀，抱住那兽性无味的放荡，并那肚腹与其以下肢体的可憎情欲呢？这些事物一时之间悦媚愚人的官觉，过后却回报比苦胆更苦的味道——到那时，这虚妄人生的影子和梦都已过去，恋慕它的人和作孽的人都被囚在黑暗不灭之火的永刑里；在那里，不睡的虫永远咬啮；在那里，火焚烧不息、不得熄灭，直烧到无尽的岁月。哀哉，连你也要与这些罪人一同被囚，受重重的苦刑；你要痛悔你邪恶的计谋，痛惜你如今的日子，想起我的话来，只是悔改再无益处；因为在死亡里，没有认信，也没有悔改。现今是作工的定候，将来是得赏的定候。即使今世的种种逸乐不是转瞬即逝、而是与其主人永存的，也没有人该因此就在基督的恩赐、并那过于人所领会的美物之先，拣选它们。诚然，正如太阳在光辉明亮上胜过死寂的黑夜，照样，且更是如此，那应许给爱神之人的幸福，在荣耀华美上远胜地上一切亲谊与荣华；人实在必须拣选那更美的，而弃绝那更无价值的。况且，这里的一切都流变易朽，如梦之消逝，如睡中的影儿与幻象；人信无定的风、信船行波浪上留下的轨迹，还快过信人的顺遂；那么，拣选那能朽坏、必败亡、软弱无价值之物，而不拣选那不能朽坏、永远长存、不朽无尽之物，又因这些事物暂时的享受，丧失将来福乐永远的享受——这是何等的天真，不，是何等的愚妄与癫狂！我的父啊，你不明白这事么？你岂不当快快越过那快快越过你的事物，将自己联结于那常存的么？你岂不当拣选本乡过于异乡、光过于暗、灵过于肉体、永生过于死之荫影、不灭者过于易逝者么？你岂不当逃脱这今世残酷君王的沉重捆绑——就是那恶者魔鬼——作那良善、心慈、满有怜悯之主的仆人么？你岂不当断绝事奉你那许多枉称为神的神明，去事奉那独一又真又活的神么？虽然你屡次因谤渎他而得罪了他，屡次以可怕的酷刑杀戮他的仆人，然而我知道，你若回转，他必以恩慈收纳你，不再记念你的过犯：因为他不喜悦罪人之死，惟喜悦他回转而存活——他，就是那从说不出的高天降下、寻找我们这些迷失之人的；他为我们忍受了十字架、鞭笞和死亡；他以自己的宝血，买了我们这些被卖在罪下为奴的人。愿荣耀颂赞都归给他，直到永永远远。阿们。」

===Original===
The king was overwhelmed with astonishment and anger: with astonishment, at his son’s wisdom and unanswerable words; with anger, at the persistence with which he denounced his father’s gods, and mocked and ridiculed the whole tenor of his life. He could not admit the glory of his discourse because of the grossness of the darkness within, but natural affection forbad him to punish his son, or evilly to entreat him, and he utterly despaired of moving him by threats. Fearing then that, if he argued further with him, his son’s boldness and bitter satire of the gods might kindle him to hotter anger, and lead him to do him a mischief, he arose in wrath and withdrew. “Would that thou hadst never been born,” he cried, “nor hadst come to the light of day, destined as thou wert to be such an one, a blasphemer of the gods, and a renegade from thy father’s love and admonition! But thou shalt not alway mock the invincible gods, nor shall their enemies rejoice for long, nor shall these knavish sorceries prevail. For except thou become obedient unto me, and right-minded toward the gods, I will first deliver thee to sundry tortures, and then put thee to the cruellest death, dealing with thee not as with a son, but as with an enemy and rebel.”

王被惊异和忿怒压倒了：惊异，是因儿子的智慧与无从置答的言语；忿怒，是因他坚持指斥他父亲的神明，讥诮嘲弄他一生的全般行径。因着里面黑暗的粗浊，他不能领会他讲论的荣美；但天然的亲情又拦阻他惩罚儿子或苦待他，他也全然绝望于用威吓摇动他。他又惧怕，若再与儿子争辩下去，儿子的放胆和他对诸神的辛辣讥诮，恐怕要激动他更炽热的怒气，以致下手害他；便在忿怒中起身退去了。他喊着说：「巴不得你从来没有出生，从来没有来到白日光中，因为你命中注定要作这样一个人——谤渎诸神的人、离弃父亲的爱与训诲的叛徒！但你不得永远嘲弄那无敌的诸神，他们的仇敌也不会长久欢跃，这些奸猾的邪术也终不得逞。因为你若不顺服我、不向诸神存正意，我必先叫你受种种酷刑，然后将你处最残忍的死，待你不像待儿子，倒像待仇敌和叛贼。」
