## XXXVII / 三十七

===Original===

Thus this noble man went forth from his palace rejoicing, as when after long exile a man returneth with joy to his own country. Outwardly he wore the robes that he was wont to wear, but beneath was the hair-shirt which Barlaam had given him. That night he halted at a poor man’s cabin, and stripped himself of his outer raiment, which, as his last alms, he bestowed upon his poor host, and thus by the prayers of that poor man, as well as of so many others, he made God his ally, and put on his grace and help as a garment of salvation; and, clad in a coat of gladness, thus went he off to his hermit-life, carrying with him neither bread, nor water, nor any necessary food, with no garment upon him save the aforesaid rough shirt. For his heart was wounded with a marvellous longing and divine love for Christ the immortal King; he was beside himself with longing, mad for God, possessed by love of him; “For love,” he saith, “is strong as fire.” So drunken was he with this heavenly love, so parched with thirst, according to him that saith, “Like as the hart desireth the water-brooks, so longeth my soul after thee, O God. My soul is athirst for the mighty and living God”; or, as the soul that is sick of love crieth in the Song of Songs, “Thou hast ravished us, ravished us with the desire of thee”; and, “Let me see thy countenance, and let me hear thy voice, for thy voice is a sweet voice, and thy countenance is comely.”

It was the desire for this unspeakable comeliness of Christ that fired the hearts of the Apostolic Choir and of the Martyr folk to despise the things that are seen, and all this temporal life, and the rather to choose ten thousand forms of death and torture, being enamoured of his heavenly beauty, and bearing in mind the charm that the divine Word used for to win our love. Such was the fire that was kindled in the soul of this fair youth also, noble in body, but most noble and kingly in soul, that led him to despise all earthly things alike, to trample on all bodily pleasures, and to contemn riches and glory and the praise of men, to lay aside diadem and purple, as of less worth than cobwebs, and to surrender himself to all the hard and irksome toils of the ascetic life, crying, “O my Christ, my soul is fixed upon thee, and thy right hand hath upholden me.”

Thus, without looking back, he passed into the depth of the desert; and, laying aside, like a heavy burden and clog, the stress of transitory things, he rejoiced in the Spirit, and looked steadfastly on Christ, whom he longed for, and cried aloud to him, as though he were there present to hear his voice, saying, “Lord, let mine eyes never again see the good things of this present world. Never, from this moment, let my soul be excited by these present vanities, but fill mine eyes with spiritual tears; direct my goings in thy way, and show me thy servant Barlaam. Show me him that was the means of my salvation, that I may learn of him the exact rule of this lonely and austere life, and may not be tripped up through ignorance of the wiles of the enemy. Grant me, O Lord, to discover the way whereby to attain unto thee, for my soul is sick of love for thee, and I am athirst for thee, the well of salvation.”

These were the thoughts of his heart continually, and he communed with God, being made one with him by prayer and sublime meditation. And thus eagerly he pursued the road, hoping to arrive at the place where Barlaam dwelt. His meat was the herbs that grow in the desert; for he carried nothing with him, as I have already said, save his own bones, and the ragged garment that was around him.

于是这位尊贵的人欢欢喜喜地出了王宫，如同人在长久流放之后，欢然归回本乡一般。他外面穿着素常所穿的袍服，里面却贴身穿着巴拉阿姆所给他的粗毛衣。当夜他投宿在一个穷人的小屋里，脱下自己的外衣，作为最后的施舍，赠与那贫穷的屋主；这样，借着那穷人的祷告，并其余许多人的祷告，他使神作了他的盟友，将神的恩典与帮助当作救恩的衣服穿上；又身披喜乐的衣裳，就这样启程奔赴他的隐修生活，随身的饼没有，水没有，任何必需的食物也没有；身上除上述那件粗砺的衬衣以外，再无别的衣服。因为他的心已为对不朽君王基督的奇妙恋慕与神圣之爱所创伤；他为恋慕而神魂颠倒，为神癫狂，被对他的爱所占有，因为他曾说：「爱坚强如火。」他为这属天的爱如此沉醉，又如此干渴难忍，正如那作诗的所说：「神啊，我的心切慕你，如鹿切慕溪水。我的心渴想神，就是那大能的永生神」；又如那思爱成病的灵魂在《雅歌》中呼喊：「你以切慕之情把我们夺去了，夺去了」；又说：「愿我得见你的面貌，得听你的声音，因为你的声音柔和，你的面貌秀美。」

正是对基督这无可言喻之秀美的恋慕，点燃了使徒的行列与殉道众民的心，使他们轻看所见之物与这整个短暂的人生，宁择万千般死亡与酷刑，因他们醉心于他属天的美丽，又铭记神的道为赢得我们的爱所施展的魅力。在这俊美的青年——论身体高贵，论灵魂却至为高贵、满有王者之风——心里，点燃的也正是这样的火；这火引领他同等轻看一切属地之物，践踏一切肉体的逸乐，蔑视财富、荣耀与人的称赞，将冠冕与紫袍置于一旁，视之还不如蛛网贵重，又把自己交付于苦修生活一切艰苦烦劳的操劳，呼喊说：「我的基督啊，我心紧紧地跟随你，你的右手扶持我。」

就这样，他一去不回头，进入了旷野的深处；又将短暂世事的重压，如同重担与累赘一般卸下，在圣灵里喜乐，定睛仰望他所切慕的基督，仿佛他就立在眼前、听他声音一般，向他大声呼喊说：「主啊，愿我的眼目永不再看见这今世的美好事物。从此刻起，愿我的灵魂永不再被这些眼前的虚妄所激动，只求你使我的双眼满含属灵的眼泪；指引我的脚步行在你的道上，将你的仆人巴拉阿姆指示我。将那作成我救恩之器皿的指示我，使我可以从他学得这孤独而严峻之生活的准确法则，不致因不识仇敌的诡计而被绊跌。主啊，求你赐我得寻那条达到你面前的路，因为我为你思爱成病，我渴想你——那救恩的泉源。」

他心里常常存着的，正是这些思念；他与神相交，借着祷告与崇高的默观，得与神合而为一。他就这样切切赶路，盼望抵达巴拉阿姆所住的地方。他的食物就是旷野里所长出的草蔬；因为他如我上文所说，身边一无携带，除了他自己的骨头，和缠在他身上的那件褴褛衣裳。

But whilst he found some food, though scanty and insufficient, from the herbs, of water he was quite destitute in that waterless and dry desert. And so at noontide, as he held on his way under the fierce blaze of the sun, he was parched with thirst in the hot drought of that desert place, and he suffered the extreme of anguish. But desire of Christ conquered nature, and the thirst wherewith he thirsted for God bedewed the heat of thirst for water.

Now the devil, being envious and full of hate for that which is beautiful, unable to endure the sight of such steadfastness of purpose, and glowing love towards God, raised up against Ioasaph many temptations in the wilderness. He called to his remembrance his kingly glory, and his magnificent bodyguard, his friends, kinsfolk and companions, and how the lives of all had depended on his life, and he minded him of the other solaces of life. Then he would confront him with the hardness of virtue, and the many sweats that she requireth, with the weakness of his flesh, with his lack of practice in such rigours, the long years to come, this present distress from thirst, his want of any comfort, and the unendingness of his toils. In a word, he raised a great dust-cloud of reasonings in his mind, exactly, I ween, as it hath been recorded of the mighty Antony.

But, when the enemy saw himself too weak to shake that purpose (for Ioasaph set Christ before his mind, and glowed with love of him, and was well strengthened by hope, and steadfast in faith, and recked nothing of the devil and his suggestions), then was the adversary ashamed of having fallen in the first assault. So he came by another road (for many are his paths of wickedness), and endeavoured to overthrow and terrify Ioasaph by means of divers apparitions. Sometimes he appeared to him in black, and such indeed he is: sometimes with a drawn sword he leapt upon him, and threatened to strike, unless he speedily turned back. At other times he assumed the shapes of all manner of beasts, roaring and making a terrible din and bellowing; or again he became a dragon, adder, or basilisk. But that fair and right noble athlete kept his soul in quietness, for he had made the Most High his refuge: and, being sober in mind, he laughed the evil one to scorn, and said, “I know thee, deceiver, who thou art, which stiffest up this trouble for me; which from the beginning didst devise mischief against mankind, and art ever wicked, and never stintest to do hurt. How becoming and right proper is thy habit, that thou shouldest take the shape of beasts and of creeping things, and thus display thy bestial and crooked nature, and thy venomous and hurtful purpose! Wherefore, wretch, attempt the impossible? For ever since I discovered that these be the contrivances and bugbears of thy malice, I have now no more anxiety concerning thee. The Lord is on my side, and I shall see my desire upon mine enemies. I shall go upon the adder and basilisk, the which thou dost resemble; thee, the lion and dragon I shall tread under my feet; for I am strengthened with the might of Christ. Let mine enemies be ashamed and turned backward: let them be driven and put to shame suddenly.”

Thus speaking, and girding on that invincible weapon, the sign of the Cross, he made vain the devil’s shows. For straightway all the beasts and creeping things disappeared, like as the smoke vanisheth, and like as wax melteth at the fire. And he, strong in the might of Christ, went on his way rejoicing and giving thanks unto the Lord. But there dwelt in that desert many divers beasts, and all kinds of serpents, and dragon-shaped monsters, and these met him, not now as apparitions but in sober sooth, so that his path was beset by fear and toil. But he overcame both, for love, as saith the scripture, cast out fear, and longing made toil light. Thus he wrestled with many sundry misfortunes and hardships until, after many days, he arrived at that desert of the land of Senaar, wherein Barlaam dwelt. There also he found water and quenched the burning of his thirst.

然而，草蔬虽可供给一点食物，却稀少而不足；在那无水干旱的旷野里，他则全然无水可用。于是在正午，当他在烈日猛焰之下继续赶路时，他因那旷野之地的燥热干旱而渴得焦灼，受尽了极度的痛楚。但对基督的恋慕胜过了本性，他为神而发的干渴，如甘露滋润了他为水而受的燥热。

魔鬼素来嫉妒美善、满怀仇恨，忍受不得看见这般坚定的志向与向神炽燃的爱，就在旷野中向约阿萨弗兴起了许多试探。他叫他回想自己的王者荣耀、他显赫的卫队、他的朋友亲族与同伴，回想众人的性命曾如何系于他一人的性命，又拿人生其余的种种慰藉来提醒他。随后，他又拿德行的严苛、她所要求的多方汗劳，拿他肉体的软弱、他素未惯于这般严酷的操练、未来漫长的岁月、当前干渴的窘迫、安慰的匮乏、劳苦的无休无止，来当面攻逼他。一言以蔽之，他在他心思里掀起了一大团思虑的尘雾——我想，这与关于大能者安东尼的记载丝毫不差。

及至仇敌见自己力薄，摇撼不动这志向（因为约阿萨弗将基督摆在心思眼前，为他炽燃着爱，因盼望大得坚固，在信仰上坚定不移，全不把魔鬼和他的暗示放在心上），那敌对者便因初次进攻败落而羞愧。于是他改从另一条路来（因为他的邪恶路径本就繁多），企图借种种怪异的幻象推倒、恐吓约阿萨弗。有时他向他显现为黑色——他的本相确是如此；有时他持着出鞘的剑向他扑去，威胁要击刺，除非他速速折返。有时他又变作各样野兽的形状，咆哮吼震，发出可怖的喧声；或又变作龙、虺蛇或蛇怪。但那俊美而极尊贵的竞技者保守自己的灵魂于安静之中，因为他已以至高者为避难所；他心思清醒，嗤笑那恶者说：「我知道你是谁，你这骗子，是你在我身上搅起这番风波；你从起初就向人类谋算恶事，永远邪恶，从不收敛你的伤害。你取野兽与爬行之物的形状，以此显露你兽性而乖曲的本性、你恶毒而有害的存心——你这习性何等相称，何等合宜！所以，你这卑劣之徒，何必妄试那不可能的事？自从我发现这些不过是出于你恶毒的诡谋与唬人的伎俩，我从此就不再为你挂虑了。耶和华在我这一边，我必亲眼看见我仇敌遭报。我要行在虺蛇与蛇怪之上——你所酷肖的正是这些；至于你——狮子与龙——我必将你践踏在脚下，因为我是靠着基督的大能得以刚强。愿我的仇敌羞愧退后，愿他们被驱散，忽然蒙羞。」

他这样说着，又束上那无敌的兵器——十字架的记号，便使魔鬼的诸般幻景尽归无效。因为立时间，众野兽与爬行之物尽都消没，如烟消散，如蜡在火前熔化。他靠着基督的大能刚强前行，一路欢欣，称谢主。但那旷野中确实栖居着许多各类的野兽、各样蛇类与龙形的怪物；它们迎着他来，如今不再是幻象，而是千真万确，使他的路途步步布满惧怕与劳苦。但惧怕与劳苦这两样他都胜过了，因为爱，如经上所说，把惧怕除去，切慕也使劳苦轻省。就这样，他与种种不幸和艰苦角力，直到多日之后，来到示拿地的旷野，就是巴拉阿姆所住的地方。在那里，他也寻得了水，止息了灼烧着他的干渴。
