## XXII / 二十二

===Original===
After Barlaam was gone forth, Ioasaph gave himself unto prayer and bitter tears, and said, "O God, haste thee to help me: O Lord, make speed to help me, because the poor hath committed himself unto thee; thou art the helper of the orphan. Look upon me, and have mercy upon me; thou who willest have all men to be saved and to come unto the knowledge of the truth, save me, and strengthen me, unworthy though I be, to walk the way of thy holy commandments, for I am weak and miserable, and not able to do the thing that is good. But thou art mighty to save me, who sustainest and holdest together all things visible and invisible. Suffer me not to walk after the evil will of the flesh, but teach me to do thy will, and preserve me unto thine eternal and blissful life. O Father, Son, and Holy Ghost, the consubstantial and undivided Godhead, I call upon thee and glorify thee. Thou art praised by all creation; thou art glorified by the intelligent powers of the Angels forever and ever. Amen."

From that time forth he kept himself with all vigilance, seeking to attain purity of soul and body, and living in continency and prayers and intercessions all night long. In the daytime he was often interrupted by the company of his fellows, and at times by a visit from the king, or a call to the king's presence, but the night would then make good the shortcomings of the day, whilst he stood, in prayer and weeping until daybreak, calling upon God. Whence in him was fulfilled the saying of the prophet, "In nights raise your hands unto holy things; and bless ye the Lord."

But Zardan observed Ioasaph's way of life, and was full of sorrow, and his soul was pierced with grievous anxieties; and he knew not what to do. At the last, worn down with pain, he withdrew to his own home, feigning sickness. When this had come to the knowledge of the king he appointed in his place another of his trusty men to minister unto his son, while he himself, being concerned for Zardan's health, sent a physician of reputation, and took great pains that he should be healed.

巴拉阿姆出去之后，约阿萨弗便将自己献给祈祷与苦泪，说：「神啊，求你快快搭救我；耶和华啊，求你速速帮助我，因为无倚无靠的人将自己交托给你，你向来是孤儿的帮助。求你垂顾我，怜悯我；你愿意万人得救、明白真道，求你拯救我，坚固我——我虽不配——使我行走你圣诫命的道路，因为我是软弱可怜的，无力行那善的事。但你有大能拯救我；你扶持、维系着一切有形无形之物。求你不要容我随从肉体的恶欲而行，总要指教我遵行你的旨意，保守我进入你那永恒蒙福的生命。父啊，子啊，圣灵啊，同质而不可分的神性，我呼求你，荣耀你。你为一切受造之物所赞美，为众天使的明智权能所荣耀，直到永永远远。阿们。」

从那时起，他就以百般警醒保守自己，力求达到灵魂与身体的纯洁，过着节制的生活，整夜祈祷、代求。白昼之间，他常被同僚的相伴所搅扰，有时是王亲来看望，或是奉召来到王前；但到了夜间，他便补足白日的缺欠，站着祈祷流泪直到天明，呼求神。因此，先知的话便应验在他身上：「夜间要向圣所举起手来，称颂耶和华。」

扎尔丹却看出约阿萨弗的为人生活，满怀忧伤，魂被沉重的忧虑刺透，不知如何是好。末了，他被愁苦磨折得支持不住，便退回自己家中，诈称有病。这事传到王耳中，王便委派另一个亲信之人代替他伺候王子，自己则因挂念扎尔丹的病体，差遣一位有名望的医生，费心竭力要使他痊愈。

The physician, seeing that Zardan was in favour with the king, attended him diligently, and, having right well judged his case, soon made this report to the king; "I have been unable to discover any root of disease in the man: wherefore I suppose that this weakness is to be ascribed to distress of spirit." But, on hearing his words, the king suspected that his son had been wroth with Zardan, and that this slight had caused his retirement. So, wishing to search the matter, he sent Zardan word, saying "Tomorrow I shall come to see thee, and judge of the malady that hath befallen thee."

But Zardan, on hearing this message, at daybreak wrapt his cloak around him and went to the king, and entered and fell in obeisance on the ground. The king spake unto him, "Why hast thou forced thyself to appear? I was minded to visit thee myself, and so make known to all my friendship for thee." He answered, "My sickness, O king, is no malady common to man; but pain of heart, arising from an anxious and careful mind, hath caused my body to suffer in sympathy. It had been folly in me, being as I am, not to attend as a slave before thy might, but to wait for thy Majesty to be troubled to come to me thy servant."

Then the king enquired after the cause of his despondency; Zardan answered and said, "Mighty is my peril, and mighty are the penalties that I deserve, and many deaths do I merit, for that I have been guilty of neglect of thy behests, and have brought on thee such sorrow as never before." Again said the king, "And of what neglect hast thou been guilty? And what is the dread that encompasseth thee?" "I have been guilty," said he, "of negligence in my close care of my lord thy son. There came an evil man and a sorcerer, and communicated to him the precepts of the Christian religion." Then he related to the king, point by point, the words which the old man spake with his son, and how gladly Ioasaph received his word, and how he had altogether become Christ's. Moreover he gave the old man's name, saying that it was Barlaam. Even before then the king had heard tell of Barlaam's ways and his extreme severity of life; but, when this came to the ears of the king, he was straightway astonied by the dismay that fell on him, and was filled with anger, and his blood well-nigh curdled at the tidings. Immediately he bade call one Araches, who held the second rank after the king, and was the chief in all his private councils: besides which the man was learned in star-lore. When he was come, with much despondency and dejection the king told him of that which had happened. He, seeing the king's trouble and confusion of mind, said, "O king, trouble and distress thyself no more. We are not without hope that the prince will yet change for the better: nay, I know for very certain that he will speedily renounce the teaching of this deceiver, and conform to thy will."

By these words then did Araches set the king in happier frame of mind; and they turned their thoughts to the thorough sifting of the matter. "This, O king," said Araches, "do we first of all. Make we haste to apprehend that infamous Barlaam. If we take him, I am assured that we shall not miss the mark, nor be cheated of our hope. Barlaam himself shall be persuaded, either by persuasion or by divers engines of torture, against his will to confess that he hath been talking falsely and at random, and shall persuade my lord, thy son, to cleave to his father's creed. But if we fail to take Barlaam, I know of an eremite, Nachor by name, in every way like unto him; it is impossible to distinguish the one from the other. He is of our opinion, and was my teacher in studies. I will give him the hint, and go by night, and tell him the full tale. Then will we blazon it abroad that Barlaam hath been caught; but we shall exhibit Nachor, who, calling himself Barlaam, shall feign that he is pleading the cause of the Christians and standing forth as their champion. Then, after much disputation, he shall be worsted and utterly discomfited. The prince, seeing Barlaam worsted, and our side victorious, will doubtless join the victors; the more so that he counteth it a great duty to reverence thy majesty, and do thy pleasure. Also the man who hath played the part of Barlaam shall be converted, and stoutly proclaim that he hath been in error."

医生见扎尔丹正蒙王的恩宠，便殷勤照料他，把病情诊断得十分妥当，不久便向王如此回奏：「我在这人身上查不出任何病根，因此我推想，这软弱必是心灵愁苦所致。」王听了这话，却疑心是王子向扎尔丹动怒，这番冷遇叫他退居在家。于是王想要查究这事，便传话给扎尔丹说：「明天我要来看你，亲自察断你所患的病症。」

扎尔丹听了这传话，天一破晓便裹上外袍，去见王，进前俯伏在地叩拜。王对他说：「你何必勉强起身前来？我本想亲自探望你，好叫众人知道我对你的恩情。」他回答说：「王啊，我的病不是人间常患的病症，乃是出于忧惧焦思之心的心痛，累得身体也一同受苦。像我这样的人，不来在你大能跟前如奴仆伺候，反倒等我王屈尊枉驾到你仆人这里来，那就是愚妄了。」

于是王询问他何故如此沮丧；扎尔丹回答说：「我的危险何其大，我当受的刑罚何其重，我该死许多回，因为我犯了疏忽你命令的罪，给你带来了前所未有的忧伤。」王又说：「你犯了什么疏忽的罪？包围着你的恐惧又是什么？」他说：「我在悉心看顾我主你儿子的事上，有了疏忽。来了一个恶人、一个行邪术的，把基督徒信仰的规条传授给了他。」随后他便把老人与王子所谈的话，逐条述给王听，又讲约阿萨弗何等甘心领受他的话，如何已经完全属乎基督。他还说出那老人的名字，说名叫巴拉阿姆。其实在这以先，王早已听闻巴拉阿姆的行迹和他那极其严苛的生活；但这话传到王耳中，他立时被临到他的惊惶震住，满腔忿怒，听见这消息，血几乎凝结起来。他随即吩咐召一个名叫阿拉凯斯的人来——此人在王之下位居第二，是王一切机密议事的首臣，此外这人还通晓星象之学。他来到之后，王便满怀忧沮丧气，把所发生的事告诉他。他见王愁烦、心神迷乱，就说：「王啊，你不要再烦恼愁苦了。王子转好，我们并非没有指望；不但如此，我确切知道，他必快快弃绝这迷惑者的教训，顺从你的意愿。」

阿拉凯斯便用这些话使王的心境转为宽慰；二人又把心思转到对这事的彻底查究上。阿拉凯斯说：「王啊，我们要先这样行：赶紧去捉拿那声名狼藉的巴拉阿姆。我们若拿住他，我确信必不至失误，指望也不至落空。或是用劝诱，或是用种种刑具，总要打动巴拉阿姆本人，叫他违背己心，承认自己所说的话尽是虚假胡言，并劝我主你的儿子持守他父亲的信仰。但我们若捉不到巴拉阿姆，我认得一个隐修士，名叫纳霍尔，各面都与巴拉阿姆相像，两人简直无从分辨。他与我们见解相同，曾是我学业上的老师。我会给他暗中递话，夜里前去，把事情的原委全都告诉他。然后我们便四处张扬，说巴拉阿姆已被擒获；其实我们出示的是纳霍尔——他自称巴拉阿姆，装作是在为基督徒的事业辩护、挺身作他们的斗士。随后，经过多方争辩，他将被驳倒，彻底溃败。王子见巴拉阿姆败北、我方得胜，必无疑归从得胜的一方；何况他素来以敬畏你的威严、行你所喜悦的为大本分。再者，那扮演了巴拉阿姆的人随后也将回转过来，极力宣称自己从前是在错谬之中。」

The king was delighted with his words, and rocked himself on idle hopes, and thought it excellent counsel. Thereupon, learning that Barlaam was but lately departed, he was zealous to take him prisoner. He therefore occupied most of the passes with troops and captains, and, himself, mounting his chariot, gave furious chase along the one road of which he was especially suspicious, being minded to surprise Barlaam at all costs. But though he toiled by the space of six full days, his labour was but spent in vain. Then he himself remained behind in one of his palaces situate in the country, but sent forward Araches, with horsemen not a few, as far as the wilderness of Senaar, in quest of Barlaam. When Araches arrived in that place, he threw all the neighbour folk into commotion: and when they constantly affirmed that they had never seen the man, he went forth into the desert places, for to hunt out the Faithful. When he had gone through a great tract of desert, and made the circuit of the fells around, and journeyed afoot over untrodden and pathless ravines, he and his hosts arrived at a plateau. Standing thereon, he descried at the foot of the mountain a company of hermits a-walking. Straightway at their governor's word of command all his men ran upon them in breathless haste, vying one with another, who should arrive first. When they arrived, they came about the monks like so many dogs, or evil beasts that plague mankind. And they seized these men of reverend mien and mind, that bare on their faces the hallmark of their hermit life, and haled them before the governor; but the monks showed no sign of alarm, no sign of meanness or sullenness, and spake never a word. Their leader and captain bore a wallet of hair, charged with the relics of some holy Fathers departed this life.

When Araches beheld them, but saw no Barlaam﻿—for he knew him by sight﻿—he was overwhelmed with grief, and said unto them, "Where is that deceiver who hath led the king's son astray?" The bearer of the wallet answered, "He is not amongst us, God forbid! For, driven forth by the grace of Christ, he avoideth us; but amongst you he hath his dwelling." The governor said, "Thou knowest him then?" "Yea," said the hermit, "I know him that is called the deceiver, which is the devil, who dwelleth in your midst and is worshipped and served by you." The governor said, "It is for Barlaam that I make search, and I asked thee of him, to learn where he is." The monk answered, "And wherefore then spakest thou in this ambiguous manner, asking about him that had deceived the king's son? If thou wast seeking Barlaam, thou shouldest certainly have said, 'Where is he that hath turned from error and saved the king's son?' Barlaam is our brother and fellow-monk. But now for many days past we have not seen his face." Said Araches, "Show me his abode." The monk answered, "Had he wished to see you, he would have come forth to meet you. As for us, it is not lawful to make known to you his hermitage."

Thereupon the governor waxed full of indignation, and, casting a haughty and savage glance upon him, said, "Ye shall die no ordinary death, except ye immediately bring Barlaam before me." "What," said the monk, "seest thou in our case that should by its attractions cause us to cling to life, and be afraid of death at thy hands? Whereas we should the rather feel grateful to thee for removing us from life in the close adherence to virtue. For we dread, not a little, the uncertainty of the end, knowing not in what state death shall overtake us, lest perchance a slip of the inclination, or some despiteful dealing of the devil, may alter the constancy of our choice, and mis-persuade us to think or do contrary to our covenants with God. Wherefore abandon all hope of gaining the knowledge that ye desire, and shrink not to work your will. We shall neither reveal the dwelling-place of our brother, whom God loveth, although we know it, nor shall we betray any other monasteries unbeknown to ye. We will not endure to escape death by such cowardice. Nay, liefer would we die honourably, and offer unto God, after the sweats of virtue, the lifeblood of courage."

That man of sin could not brook this boldness of speech, and was moved to the keenest passion against this high and noble spirit, and afflicted the monks with many stripes and tortures. Their courage and nobility won admiration even from that tyrant. But, when after many punishments he failed to persuade them, and none of them consented to discover Barlaam, he took and ordered them to be led to the king, bearing with them the wallet with the relics, and to be beaten and shamefully entreated as they went.

王听了他的话，大为欢喜，在虚妄的指望中自我陶醉，认为这是极好的计谋。那时他得知巴拉阿姆才离去不久，便热心要擒拿他为囚。于是他派军兵将校占据了大部分关口，自己登上战车，沿着他格外起疑的那条路穷追不舍，一心要不惜任何代价出其不意地擒获巴拉阿姆。然而他劳苦了足足六天，辛劳尽付徒然。于是他本人留驻在乡间的一座宫中，却差阿拉凯斯带着为数不少的骑兵，径往示拿旷野，搜寻巴拉阿姆。阿拉凯斯到了那地方，把邻近的居民全都搅得骚动不安；他们既一口咬定从未见过那人，他便出动进入旷野之地，要搜捕信徒。他穿过了大片旷野，绕行了四周的山冈，又徒步跋涉过人迹未到、无路可循的沟壑，他和他的队伍便来到一处高台。他站在上面，望见山脚下有一群隐修士正在行走。随统领一声令下，他手下的众人立时气喘吁吁地扑向他们，彼此争竞，看谁先到。到了那里，他们就围着那些修士，如同一群狗，或一群扰害人类的恶兽。他们抓住这些仪表心志俱可敬、脸上带着隐修生活印记的人，把他们拖到统领面前；修士们却毫无惊惶之色，毫无卑劣愠怒之态，一句也不言语。他们的领袖和首领带着一只毛布行囊，里面盛着几位已经离世的圣教父的圣髑。

阿拉凯斯见了他们，却看不见巴拉阿姆——因为他认得巴拉阿姆的相貌——就忧伤不胜，对他们说：「那引诱王子走入迷途的骗子在哪里？」背行囊的人回答说：「断乎不然，他不在我们中间！因为他被基督的恩典赶逐，就躲避我们；他倒是在你们中间安身。」统领说：「那么你认得他？」隐修士说：「是的，我认得那称为迷惑者的，就是魔鬼，他住在你们中间，受你们的敬拜和事奉。」统领说：「我搜寻的正是巴拉阿姆，我问你的就是他，要知道他在哪里。」修士回答说：「那你为何说得这般含糊，问那迷惑了王子的人呢？你若寻找的是巴拉阿姆，就本当这样说：『那转离错谬、救了王子的人在何处？』巴拉阿姆是我们的弟兄、我们的同道修士。只是至今已有多日，我们不曾见他的面。」阿拉凯斯说：「把他的住处指给我看。」修士回答说：「他若愿意见你们，早就会出来迎见你们了。至于我们，把他的隐修之所告诉你们，是不合法的。」

统领就大起义愤，向他投以倨傲凶残的一瞥，说：「你们若不立即把巴拉阿姆带到我面前，就必不得好死。」修士说：「你在我身上看见有什么可恋慕的，叫我们贪恋性命、怕死在你手中呢？你若叫我们在紧紧依傍德行之中离开今世，我们倒应当感谢你。因为我们实在深深惧怕结局的不可确知，不知道死亡要在怎样的境况中追上我们，惟恐或因心志的一时滑跌，或因魔鬼的某种毒害，改变了我们抉择的坚贞，诱使我们思想或行出与我们对神所立之约相背的事。所以，要得着你们所想望知道的事，这指望你们尽管丢弃；要行你们的意愿，也不必缩手。我们弟兄——神所爱的那位的居所，我们虽然知道，也决不显露；你们所不知道的其他修院，我们也不出卖。我们决不甘心以这等怯懦逃脱死亡。宁要死得光荣，在德行的劳苦之后，将勇毅的生命之血献上给神。」

那大罪人容不下这等放胆直言，对这高尚尊贵的心志动了极烈的忿怒，便用许多鞭笞和酷刑苦害那些修士。他们的勇气与高贵，连那暴君也不禁心生钦佩。但他多方刑罚之后，既未能说服他们，也无一人肯供出巴拉阿姆，便下令将他们解送到王那里去，随身带着那盛圣髑的行囊，一路受鞭打、受凌辱。
