## XIV / 第十四章

===Original===
The Trapdoor

By Sunday evening, a week after the wreck, my inaction had goaded me to frenzy. The very sight of Johnson across the street or lurking, always within sight of the house, kept me constantly exasperated. It was on that day that things began to come to a focus, a burning-glass of events that seemed to center on me.

I dined alone that evening in no cheerful frame of mind. There had been a polo game the day before and I had lent a pony, which is always a bad thing to do. And she had wrenched her shoulder, besides helping to lose the game. There was no one in town: the temperature was ninety and climbing, and my left hand persistently cramped under its bandage.

===Chinese===
活板门

到星期天晚上，也就是车祸后整整一周，我的无所事事已经把我逼得快发疯了。光是看见约翰逊（Johnson）在街对面晃悠，或者在房子附近鬼鬼祟祟地蹲守——他总是待在看得见房子的地方——就足以让我怒火中烧。正是在这一天，事情开始聚焦了，像一个聚光镜把所有事件汇聚到我身上。

那天晚上我独自用餐，心情好不起来。前一天有场马球赛，我借出了一匹矮种马——借马这事儿从来就没有好下场。那匹马不光害我们输了比赛，还把肩膀给扭伤了。镇上一个人也没有：气温九十度还在往上爬，我的左手在绷带底下不停地抽筋。

===Original===
Mrs. Klopton herself saw me served, my bread buttered and cut in tidbits, my meat ready for my fork. She hovered around me maternally, obviously trying to cheer me.

“The paper says still warmer,” she ventured. “The thermometer is ninety-two now.”

“And this coffee is two hundred and fifty,” I said, putting down my cup. “Where is Euphemia? I haven’t seen her around, or heard a dish smash all day.”

“Euphemia is in bed,” Mrs. Klopton said gravely. “Is your meat cut small enough, Mr. Lawrence?” Mrs. Klopton can throw more mystery into an ordinary sentence than anyone I know. She can say, “Are your sheets damp, sir?” And I can tell from her tone that the house across the street has been robbed, or that my left hand neighbor has appendicitis. So now I looked up and asked the question she was waiting for.

克洛普顿夫人亲自照看我用餐，面包涂好黄油、切成小块，肉切好了等着我下叉。她像母亲一样在我身边转来转去，显然是想让我开心些。

"报纸说还会更热，"她试探着说。"温度计已经九十二度了。"

"这杯咖啡有两百五十度，"我把杯子放下说。"尤菲米亚（Euphemia）在哪儿？我一整天没看见她，也没听见摔盘子的声响。"

"尤菲米亚在床上躺着呢，"克洛普顿夫人郑重其事地说。"劳伦斯先生，您的肉切得够小了吗？"克洛普顿夫人能用最寻常的句子营造出最浓厚的神秘气氛。她说一句"先生，您的床单潮吗？"我就能从她的语气里听出街对面那家被抢了，或者我左手边的邻居得了阑尾炎。于是现在我抬起头来，问了那个她正等着我问的问题。

===Original===
“What’s the matter with Euphemia?” I inquired idly.

“Frightened into her bed,” Mrs. Klopton said in a stage whisper. “She’s had three hot water bottles and she hasn’t done a thing all day but moan.”

“She oughtn’t to take hot water bottles,” I said in my severest tone. “One would make me moan. You need not wait, I’ll ring if I need anything.”

Mrs. Klopton sailed to the door, where she stopped and wheeled indignantly. “I only hope you won’t laugh on the wrong side of your face some morning, Mr. Lawrence,” she declared, with Christian fortitude. “But I warn you, I am going to have the police watch that house next door.”

===Chinese===
"尤菲米亚怎么了？"我漫不经心地问。

"吓病了，"克洛普顿夫人压低声音说。"她放了三个热水瓶，一整天什么都没干，光在那儿哼哼。"

"她不该用热水瓶，"我用最严厉的口气说。"一个热水瓶就够我哼哼的了。你不用守着，有事我按铃。"

克洛普顿夫人翩然走向门口，到了门口又愤愤地转过身来。"我只希望您哪天早上别笑错了地方，劳伦斯先生，"她带着基督徒般的坚忍说道。"但我警告您，我打算让警察来监视隔壁那栋房子。"

I was half inclined to tell her that both it and we were under police surveillance at that moment. But I like Mrs. Klopton, in spite of the fact that I make her life a torment for her, so I refrained.

“Last night, when the paper said it was going to storm, I sent Euphemia to the roof to bring the rugs in. Eliza had slipped out, although it was her evening in. Euphemia went up to the roof—it was eleven o’clock—and soon I heard her running downstairs crying. When she got to my room she just folded up on the floor. She said there was a black figure sitting on the parapet of the house next door—the empty house—and that when she appeared it rose and waved long black arms at her and spit like a cat.”

===Chinese===
我差点告诉她，此刻那栋房子和我们自己都在警方的监视之下。但我喜欢克洛普顿夫人，尽管我让她过的日子苦不堪言，所以我忍住了没说。

"昨天晚上报纸说要暴风雨，我打发尤菲米亚上天台去收地毯。伊莱扎（Eliza）溜出去了，明明轮到她歇晚班。尤菲米亚上了天台——那时已经十一点了——不一会儿我就听见她哭着跑下楼来。到了我房门口，她直接瘫倒在地上了。她说隔壁那栋空房子的护墙上坐着一个黑影，她一出现，那黑影就站起来，朝她挥舞长长的黑手臂，还像猫一样'噗噗'地吐口水。"

===Original===
I had finished my dinner and was lighting a cigarette. “If there was anyone up there, which I doubt, they probably sneezed,” I suggested. “But if you feel uneasy, I’ll take a look around the roof tonight before I turn in. As far as Euphemia goes, I wouldn’t be uneasy about her—doesn’t she always have an attack of some sort when Eliza rings in an extra evening on her?”

So I made a superficial examination of the window locks that night, visiting parts of the house that I had not seen since I bought it. Then I went to the roof. Evidently it had not been intended for any purpose save to cover the house, for unlike the houses around, there was no staircase. A ladder and a trapdoor led to it, and it required some nice balancing on my part to get up with my useless arm. I made it, however, and found this unexplored part of my domain rather attractive. It was cooler than downstairs, and I sat on the brick parapet and smoked my final cigarette. The roof of the empty house adjoined mine along the back wing, but investigation showed that the trapdoor across the low dividing wall was bolted underneath.

===Chinese===
我吃完晚饭，点了一支烟。"就算上面有人——我表示怀疑——大概也只是打了个喷嚏，"我说。"不过你要是不放心，今晚睡前我去天台查看一下。至于尤菲米亚嘛，用不着替她担心——哪次伊莱扎偷跑出去，她不是闹点毛病出来？"

于是那天晚上我草草检查了一下窗户锁，顺带巡视了这栋自从买下来就没再看过的几个角落。然后我上了天台。显然这天台除了给房子遮风挡雨之外没有别的用途，因为跟周围的房子不同，它没有楼梯。一架梯子和一扇活板门通往上面，我只有一只手能使，爬上去费了不少平衡功夫。不过我还是上去了，发现这片从未涉足的领地还挺有吸引力。天台上比楼下凉快，我坐在砖砌的护墙上抽完最后一支烟。隔壁那栋空房子的天台沿着后翼跟我的连在一起，但查看发现，隔墙上那扇活板门从下面上了栓。

===Original===
There was nothing out of the ordinary anywhere, and so I assured Mrs. Klopton. Needless to say, I did not tell her that I had left the trapdoor open, to see if it would improve the temperature of the house. I went to bed at midnight, merely because there was nothing else to do. I turned on the night lamp at the head of my bed, and picked up a volume of Shaw at random (it was Arms and the Man, and I remember thinking grimly that I was a good bit of a chocolate cream soldier myself), and prepared to go to sleep. Shaw always puts me to sleep. I have no apologies to make for what occurred that night, and not even an explanation that I am sure of. I did a foolish thing under impulse, and I have not been sorry.

===Chinese===
一切都很正常，我这样向克洛普顿夫人保证道。不用说，我没告诉她我故意把那扇活板门打开了，想看看能不能改善房子里的温度。我半夜上了床，纯粹是因为没有别的事可做。我拧亮床头的夜灯，随手拿起一本萧伯纳的书（是《武器与人》（Arms and the Man），我记得当时苦涩地想，我自己倒是个不折不扣的巧克力奶油兵）。萧伯纳的书总能让我睡着。对那天晚上发生的事，我不打算辩解，甚至拿不出一个自己确信的解释。我凭冲动做了一件蠢事，但我不后悔。

===Original===
It was something after two when the doorbell rang. It rang quickly, twice. I got up drowsily, for the maids and Mrs. Klopton always lock themselves beyond reach of the bell at night, and put on a dressing-gown. The bell rang again on my way downstairs. I lit the hall light and opened the door. I was wide-awake now, and I saw that it was Johnson. His bald head shone in the light—his crooked mouth was twisted in a smile.

“Good Heavens, man,” I said irritably. “Don’t you ever go home and go to bed?”

He closed the vestibule door behind him and cavalierly turned out the light. Our dialogue was sharp, staccato.

“Have you a key to the empty house next door?” he demanded. “Somebody’s in there, and the latch is caught.”

===Chinese===
门铃响的时候大约过了两点。铃声急促地响了两下。我睡眼惺忪地爬起来，因为女佣们和克洛普顿夫人晚上总把自己锁在够不着门铃的地方，我披上晨袍。下楼的时候铃又响了一次。我打开走廊灯，开了门。这时我已经完全清醒了，认出是约翰逊。他的秃头在灯光下闪闪发亮——歪斜的嘴角扭曲成一丝微笑。

"老天爷，你这家伙，"我恼火地说。"你就不能回家睡一觉吗？"

"你有隔壁那栋空房子的钥匙吗？"他问道。"有人在里面，门闩是卡住的。"

“The houses are alike. The key to this door may fit. Did you see them go in?”

“No. There’s a light moving up from room to room. I saw something like it last night, and I have been watching. The patrolman reported queer doings there a week or so ago.”

“All kinds in the gun rack,” I replied, and going into the den, I came back with a Smith and Wesson. “I’m not much use,” I explained, “with this arm, but I’ll do what I can. There may be somebody there. The servants here have been uneasy.”

===Original===
Johnson planned the campaign. He suggested on account of my familiarity with the roof, that I go there and cut off escape in that direction. “I have Robison out there now—the patrolman on the beat,” he said. “He’ll watch below and you above, while I search the house. Be as quiet as possible.”

I was rather amused. I put on some clothes and felt my way carefully up the stairs, the revolver swinging free in my pocket, my hand on the rail. At the foot of the ladder I stopped and looked up. Above me there was a gray rectangle of sky dotted with stars. It occurred to me that with my one serviceable hand holding the ladder, I was hardly in a position to defend myself, that I was about to hoist a body that I am rather careful of into a danger I couldn’t see and wasn’t particularly keen about anyhow. I don’t mind saying that the seconds it took me to scramble up the ladder were among the most unpleasant that I recall.

约翰逊制定了作战计划。他建议由我上天台去，因为我对天台比较熟悉，可以截断那个方向的退路。"我已经安排了罗比森（Robison）在外面——就是巡逻的片儿警，"他说。"他在下面盯梢，你在上面堵截，我进屋搜查。尽量别出声。"

I got to the top, however, without incident. I could see fairly well after the darkness of the house beneath, but there was nothing suspicious in sight. The roofs, separated by two feet of brick wall, stretched around me, unbroken save by an occasional chimney. I went very softly over to the other trap, the one belonging to the suspected house. It was closed, but I imagined I could hear Johnson’s footsteps ascending heavily. Then even that was gone. A nearby clock struck three as I stood waiting. I examined my revolver then, for the first time, and found it was empty!

===Original===
I had been rather skeptical until now. I had had the usual tolerant attitude of the man who is summoned from his bed to search for burglars, combined with the artificial courage of firearms. With the discovery of my empty gun, I felt like a man on the top of a volcano in lively eruption. Suddenly I found myself staring incredulously at the trapdoor at my feet. I had examined it early in the evening and found it bolted. Did I imagine it, or had it raised about an inch? Wasn’t it moving slowly as I looked? No, I am not a hero: I was startled almost into a panic. I had one arm, and whoever was raising that trapdoor had two. My knees had a queer inclination to bend the wrong way.

===Chinese===

Johnson’s footsteps were distinct enough, but he was evidently far below. The trap, raised perhaps two inches now, remained stationary. There was no sound from beneath it: once I thought I heard two or three gasping respirations: I am not sure they were not my own. I wanted desperately to stand on one leg at a time and hold the other up out of focus of a possible revolver.

“Don’t move, or I’ll stand on the trap and break your arm,” I panted. What else could I threaten? I couldn’t shoot, I couldn’t even fight. “Johnson!” I called.

他们二话没说就穿过天台过来了。眼看他俩安全离开了那栋房子，我松了口气。到了书房里，我兑现了承诺，约翰逊举杯祝酒道："穿过黑麦地"（Coming through the rye）。他用行家的目光审视着我的枪架，临走时还恋恋不舍地回头看了一眼那些武器。

"当过兵吗？"他问道。

"没有，"我带着一种他注意到了却没能理解的苦涩说。"我是个巧克力奶油兵——你不读萧伯纳吧，约翰逊？"

"没听说过，"侦探淡漠地说。"好了，晚安，布莱克利先生。多谢了。"到了门口他犹豫了一下，清了清嗓子。
