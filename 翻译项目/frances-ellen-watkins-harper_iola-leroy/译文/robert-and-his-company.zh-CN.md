## XV Robert and His Company / 第十五章 罗伯特与他的连队

===Original===
“Good morning, gentlemen,” said Robert Johnson, as he approached Colonel Robinson, the commander of the post, who was standing at the door of his tent, talking with Captain Sybil.
“Good morning,” responded Colonel Robinson, “I am glad you have come. I was just about to send for you. How is your company getting on?”
“First rate, sir,” replied Robert.
“In good health?”
“Excellent. They are all in good health and spirits. Our boys are used to hardship and exposure, and the hope of getting their freedom puts new snap into them.”

===Chinese===
“早上好，二位先生。”罗伯特·约翰逊（Robert Johnson）说着，朝驻地指挥官罗宾逊上校（Colonel Robinson）走去。上校正站在帐篷门口，同西比尔上尉（Captain Sybil）谈话。
“早上好，”罗宾逊上校应道，“你来得正好，我很高兴。我正要派人去请你。你的连队近来带得怎么样？”
“好极了，长官。”罗伯特答道。
“弟兄们身体都好？”
“好得很。他们个个身体康健、精神抖擞。我们的弟兄们吃惯了苦、受惯了风霜，而获得自由的盼头，又给他们添了一股新的劲头。”

===Original===
“I am glad of it,” said Colonel Robinson. “They make good fighters and very useful allies. Last night we received very valuable intelligence from some fugitives who had escaped through the Rebel lines. I do not think many of the Northern people realize the service they have been to us in bringing information and helping our boys when escaping from Rebel prisons. I never knew a full-blooded negro to betray us. A month ago, when we were encamped near the Rebel lines, a colored woman managed admirably to keep us posted as to the intended movements of the enemy. She was engaged in laundry work, and by means of hanging her sheets in different ways gave us the right signals.”
“I hope,” said Captain Sybil, “that the time will come when some faithful historian will chronicle all the deeds of daring and service these people have performed during this struggle, and give them due credit therefor.”
“Our great mistake,” said Colonel Robinson, “was our long delay in granting them their freedom, and even what we have done is only partial. The border States still retain their slaves. We ought to have made a clean sweep of the whole affair. Slavery is a serpent which we nourished in its weakness, and now it is stinging us in its strength.”
“I think so, too,” said Captain Sybil. “But in making his proclamation of freedom, perhaps Mr. Lincoln went as far as he thought public opinion would let him.”

===Chinese===
“这我很高兴，”罗宾逊上校说。“他们都是能打的好手，也是极有用的盟友。昨夜我们收到一些逃奴送来的情报，十分宝贵——他们是从叛军防线那边逃出来的。我看，北方人有许许多多并不明白，他们在给我们递送消息、帮助我们的小伙子们逃出叛军监狱这两件事上，出了多大的力。我从没见过哪个纯血统的黑人出卖过我们。一个月前，我们在叛军防线附近扎营，有一位有色人种妇女想方设法让我们随时得知敌军的动向，办得漂亮极了。她是做洗衣活计的，把床单晾成各种不同的样式，就给我们送出了准确的信号。”
“我盼望，”西比尔上尉说，“将来总有一天，某位忠实可靠的历史家，会把这些人在这次斗争中建立的全部英勇功勋与辛劳贡献一一载入史册，并给他们应得的赞誉。”
“我们最大的失策，”罗宾逊上校说，“就是在还他们自由这件事上拖延了那么久；就连我们已经做了的，也不过是做了一半。边界各州仍旧蓄着他们的奴隶。我们本该把整个事情来个连根的大扫除。奴隶制是一条蛇，它弱小的时候，是我们把它喂养大的；如今它强大了，回过头来噬咬我们。”
“我也这样想，”西比尔上尉说。“不过，林肯先生颁布那份解放宣言的时候，多半已经走到他估量着舆论所能容许的极限了。”

===Original===
“It is remarkable,” said Colonel Robinson, “how these Secesh hold out. It surprises me to see how poor white men, who, like the negroes, are victims of slavery, rally around the Stripes and Bars. These men, I believe, have been looked down on by the aristocratic slaveholders, and despised by the well-fed and comfortable slaves, yet they follow their leaders into the very jaws of death; face hunger, cold, disease, and danger; and all for what? What, under heaven, are they fighting for? Now, the negro, ignorant as he is, has learned to regard our flag as a banner of freedom, and to look forward to his deliverance as a consequence of the overthrow of the Rebellion.”
“I think,” said Captain Sybil, “that these ignorant white men have been awfully deceived. They have had presented to their imaginations utterly false ideas of the results of Secession, and have been taught that its success would bring them advantages which they had never enjoyed in the Union.”
“And I think,” said Colonel Robinson, “that the women and ministers have largely fed and fanned the fires of this Rebellion, and have helped to create a public opinion which has swept numbers of benighted men into the conflict. Well might one of their own men say, ‘This is a rich man’s war and a poor man’s fight.’ They were led into it through their ignorance, and held in it by their fears.”
“I think,” said Captain Sybil, “that if the public school had been common through the South this war would never have occurred. Now things have reached such a pass that able-bodied men must report at headquarters, or be treated as deserters. Their leaders are desperate men, of whom it has been said: ‘They have robbed the cradle and the grave.’ ”
“They are fighting against fearful odds,” said Colonel Robinson, “and their defeat is only a question of time.”

===Chinese===
“真叫人称奇，”罗宾逊上校说，“这些分裂派居然撑持到了今天。让我吃惊的是，那些穷苦的白人——他们同黑人一样，也是奴隶制的牺牲品——竟然还聚拢在星杠旗（the Stripes and Bars，邦联旗）的周围。据我看，这些人一向受着趾高气扬的奴隶主们的轻贱，又遭那些吃得饱饱、住得安安逸逸的奴隶们的白眼；可他们偏偏跟着自己的头目，一直钻进死亡的血盆大口；挨饿、受冻、直面疾病与危险；这一切到底图的是什么？天底下，他们究竟是在为什么打仗？再看黑人吧，他虽然无知，却已经学会把我们的旗帜看作一面自由的旗帜，已经懂得指望自己的解救，将随着这场叛乱的覆灭一同到来。”
“依我看，”西比尔上尉说，“这些无知的白人上了天大的当。有人往他们的想象里灌输了一套关于分裂后果的彻头彻尾的谬见，又教他们相信：分裂一旦得逞，就会给他们带来在联邦里从未享受过的好处。”
“而我认为，”罗宾逊上校说，“这场叛乱的火，是那些妇女和牧师们添柴扇风烧旺的，出了大力的；他们还助长出一种舆论，把成群成群的蒙昧之人卷进了这场冲突。难怪他们自己人当中就有一个人说过：‘这是富人兴的战，穷人打的仗。’他们是因无知而被引进去的，又因恐惧而被扣在里面。”
“我认为，”西比尔上尉说，“倘若公立学校早就在南方各地普及，这场战争根本就不会发生。如今事势已经到了这般地步：凡年富力强的男子都必须到司令部报到，否则就按逃兵处置。他们的头目都是些铤而走险的亡命之徒，说起他们，有人已经说过：‘他们连摇篮和坟墓都抢劫一空了。’”
“他们顶着悬殊得可怕的劣势在打，”罗宾逊上校说，“他们的败亡只是个时间问题。”

===Original===
“As soon,” said Robert, “as they fired on Fort Sumter, Uncle Daniel, a dear old father who had been praying and hoping for freedom, said to me: ‘Dey’s fired on Fort Sumter, an’ mark my words, Bob, de Norf’s boun’ ter whip.’ ”
“Had we freed the slaves at the outset,” said Captain Sybil, “we wouldn’t have given the Rebels so much opportunity to strengthen themselves by means of slave labor in raising their crops, throwing up their entrenchments, and building their fortifications. Slavery was a deadly cancer eating into the life of the nation; but, somehow, it had cast such a glamour over us that we have acted somewhat as if our national safety were better preserved by sparing the cancer than by cutting it out.”
“Political and racial questions have sadly complicated this matter,” said Colonel Robinson. “The North is not wholly made up of anti-slavery people. At the beginning of this war we were not permeated with justice, and so were not ripe for victory. The battle of Bull Run inaugurated the war by a failure. Instead of glory we gathered shame, and defeat in place of victory.”
“We have been slow,” said Captain Sybil, “to see our danger and to do our duty. Our delay has cost us thousands of lives and millions of dollars. Yet it may be it is all for the best. Our national wound was too deep to be lightly healed. When the President issued his Emancipation Proclamation my heart overflowed with joy, and I said: ‘This is the first bright rift in the war cloud.’ ”

===Chinese===
“他们朝萨姆特堡（Fort Sumter）一开炮，”罗伯特说，“丹尼尔大叔（Uncle Daniel）——一位一直为自由祷告、为自由盼望的亲爱的老长辈——就对我说：‘他们炮打了萨姆特堡啦；鲍勃，你记住俺这句话，北方是一定要打赢的。’”
“倘若我们当初一开战就放了奴隶，”西比尔上尉说，“就不会给叛军这么多机会，让他们借着奴隶的劳力去种庄稼、筑壕垒、修工事，以此壮大他们自己。奴隶制是一颗致命的癌瘤，正啃噬着国家的生命；可是不知怎么，它给我们罩下了一重魔障，害我们行事竟有些像是：留着这颗毒瘤不割，倒比剜掉它更能保全国家的安全。”
“政治问题与种族问题，把这件事搅得糟透了，”罗宾逊上校说。“北方并非全是反奴隶制的人。这场战争开头的时候，我们心中还未浸透正义，因此也还未熟到能赢得胜利的地步。布尔溪（Bull Run）一役，以一场失败为这场战争揭了幕。我们收获的不是光荣，而是耻辱；不是胜利，而是溃败。”
“在看清自己的危险、尽自己的本分这两件事上，我们都迟了，”西比尔上尉说。“我们的拖延，已经使我们付出了千万条性命、千百万钱财的代价。然而这也许反倒是最妙的安排。我们国家的创口太深，本就不能轻轻巧巧地痊愈。总统颁布《解放宣言》的那天，我满心的喜悦直往外溢，我说：‘这是战云上裂开的第一道亮缝。’”

===Original===
“And did you really think that they would accept the terms of freedom and lay down their arms?” asked Robert.
“I hardly thought they would,” continued Captain Sybil. “I did not think that their leaders would permit it. I believe the rank and file of their army are largely composed of a mass of ignorance, led, manipulated, and moulded by educated and ambitious wickedness. In attempting to overthrow the Union, a despotism and reign of terror were created which encompassed them as fetters of iron, and they will not accept the conditions until they have reached the last extremity. I hardly think they are yet willing to confess that such extremity has been reached.”
“Captain,” said Robert, as they left Colonel Robinson’s tent, “I have lived all my life where I have had a chance to hear the ‘Secesh’ talk, and when they left their papers around I used to read everything I could lay my hands on. It seemed to me that the big white men not only ruled over the poor whites and made laws for them, but over the whole nation.”
“That was so,” replied Captain Sybil. “The North was strong but forbearing. It was busy in trade and commerce, and permitted them to make the Northern States hunting-grounds for their slaves. When we sent back Simms and Burns from beneath the shadow of Bunker Hill Monument and Faneuil Hall, they mistook us; looked upon us as a lot of money-grabbers, who would be willing to purchase peace at any price. I do not believe when they fired on the ‘Star of the West’ that they had the least apprehension of the fearful results which were to follow their madness and folly.”

===Chinese===
“那么，您当时当真想过，他们会接受自由的条件、放下武器吗？”罗伯特问道。
“我本来就不大信他们会，”西比尔上尉接着说。“我不认为他们的头目们会容许这样。我相信，他们军队的兵员，大部分是一大团愚昧之众，被一些受过教育、野心勃勃的恶徒牵引着、操纵着、揉捏着。他们在图谋倾覆联邦的时候，造出了一种专制与恐怖统治，像铁镣似的把他们自己箍住了；不到山穷水尽的最后关头，他们是不会接受这些条件的。至于这样的绝境是否已然临头，我看他们至今还不肯认账。”
“上尉，”他们走出罗宾逊上校的帐篷后，罗伯特说，“我这一辈子住的地方，一直有机会听那些‘分裂派’高谈阔论；他们把报纸文件丢得四处都是的时候，凡是能弄到手的，我都拿来读。依我看，那些白人大人物不光是统治穷苦的白人、替他们立法，而且是统治着整个国家。”
“确实是这样，”西比尔上尉答道。“北方强大，却一味忍让。它忙着经商贸易，听任他们把北方各州变成追捕奴隶的猎场。当我们把西姆斯（Simms）和彭斯（Burns）从邦克山纪念碑（Bunker Hill Monument）和法纳尔大厅（Faneuil Hall）的影子底下送还回去的时候，他们看错了我们；把我们看成了一伙见钱眼开的财迷，以为我们肯出任何价钱买个太平。他们朝‘西部之星’号（the Star of the West）开炮的那会儿，我决不相信，他们对自己的疯狂与愚蠢将要招来的种种可怕后果，有过一丝一毫的预感。”

===Original===
“Well, Captain,” asked Robert, “if the free North would submit to be called on to help them catch their slaves, what could be expected of us, who all our lives had known no other condition than that of slavery? How much braver would you have been, if your first recollections had been those of seeing your mother maltreated, your father cruelly beaten, or your fellow-servants brutally murdered? I wonder why they never enslaved the Indians!”
“You are mistaken, Robert, if you think the Indians were never enslaved. I have read that the Spaniards who visited the coasts of America kidnapped thousands of Indians, whom they sent to Europe and the West Indies as slaves. Columbus himself, we are informed, captured five hundred natives, and sent them to Spain. The Indian had the lesser power of endurance, and Las Cassas suggested the enslavement of the negro, because he seemed to possess greater breadth of physical organization and stronger power of endurance. Slavery was an old world’s crime which, I have heard, the Indians never practiced among themselves. Perhaps it would have been harder to reduce them to slavery and hold them in bondage when they had a vast continent before them, where they could hide in the vastnesses of its mountains or the seclusion of its forests, than it was for white men to visit the coasts of Africa and, with their superior knowledge, obtain cargoes of slaves, bring them across the ocean, hem them in on the plantations, and surround them with a pall of dense ignorance.”
“I remember,” said Robert, “in reading a history I once came across at our house, that when the Africans first came to this country they did not all speak one language. Some had only met as mutual enemies. They were not all one color, their complexions ranging from tawny yellow to deep black.”
“Yes,” said Captain Sybil, “and in dealing with the negro we wanted his labor; in dealing with the Indian we wanted his lands. For one we had weapons of war; for the other we had real and invisible chains, the coercion of force, and the terror of the unseen world.”

===Chinese===
“可是，上尉，”罗伯特问道，“连自由的北方都甘愿应召去帮他们捉拿奴隶，还能指望我们这些人怎样呢？我们一辈子只知为奴，不知道世上还有别的处境。倘或您最早的记忆，就是眼看着自己的母亲受尽糟践、父亲被狠命毒打、同为奴仆的伙伴被人凶残地杀害，您还能比这勇敢多少呢？我倒一直纳闷，他们怎么从来没把印第安人蓄成奴隶！”
“你要是以为印第安人从来没被蓄为奴隶，那就想岔了，罗伯特。我读到过，到美洲海岸来的那些西班牙人，拐走了成千上万的印第安人，当作奴隶送到欧洲和西印度群岛去。据我们所知，哥伦布（Columbus）本人就掳了五百名土人，送到西班牙去。印第安人的耐受力差些，拉斯·卡萨斯（Las Cassas）便出主意改蓄黑人，因为黑人似乎体格更壮阔、耐受力也更强。奴隶制本是旧世界的一桩罪恶，而据我所闻，印第安人自己人中间，是从不来这一套的。把印第安人压进奴隶的枷锁、再把他们牢牢拘住，恐怕本来就更难——他们面前横着一片广袤无垠的大陆，可以躲进莽莽群山的深处，也可以藏入层层密林的幽僻；这比起白人跑到非洲海岸，凭着一副更胜一筹的知识，弄到一船船的奴隶，漂洋过海运回来，圈禁在种植园里，再裹上一层浓黑的愚昧，总要难得多。”
“我还记得，”罗伯特说，“从前在家里偶然翻到过一部史书，上面说，非洲人初到这个国家的时候，并不是人人说同一种话。有些人先前相遇，只是互为仇敌。他们的肤色也不是清一色，从黄褐色到深黑色，深深浅浅，各不相同。”
“是的，”西比尔上尉说，“对付黑人，我们要的是他们的劳力；对付印第安人，我们要的是他们的土地。对这一方，我们手里是战争的兵器；对那一方，我们手里是有形与无形的锁链、暴力的强制，再加上对那个看不见的世界的恐怖。”

===Original===
“That’s exactly so, Captain! When I was a boy I used to hear the old folks tell what would happen to bad people in another world; about the devil pouring hot lead down people’s throats and stirring them up with a pitchfork; and I used to get so scared that I would be afraid to go to bed at night. I don’t suppose the Indians ever heard of such things, or, if they had, I never heard of them being willing to give away all their lands on earth, and quietly wait for a home in heaven.”
“But, surely, Robert, you do not think religion has degraded the negro?”
“Oh, I wouldn’t say that. But a man is in a tight fix when he takes his part, like Nat Turner or Denmark Veasy, and is made to fear that he will be hanged in this world and be burned in the next. And, since I come to think of it, we colored folks used to get mightily mixed up about our religion. Mr. Gundover had on his plantation a real smart man. He was religious, but he would steal.”
“Oh, Robert,” queried Sybil, “how could he be religious and steal?”

===Chinese===
“一点儿也不差，上尉！我小时候常听老人们讲，坏人到了另一个世界会落个什么下场；讲魔鬼把滚烫的铅水灌进人的喉咙，还拿草叉把他们搅动；我常吓得夜里连床都不敢上。我想印第安人从来没听说过这些事；就算听见过，我也从没听说他们甘心让出地上全部的土地，安安静静地等着到天上领一个家。”
“可是，罗伯特，你总不至于认为，是宗教把黑人给败坏了的吧？”
“哦，那话我可不说。可一个人要是像纳特·特纳（Nat Turner）或丹马克·维西（Denmark Veasy）那样挺身起来拼一回，处境就险了：人家叫他又怕今世被绞死，又怕来世被火烧。再说，我如今想起来，我们有色人种从前在宗教上头，也着实容易搅得稀里糊涂。冈多弗先生（Mr. Gundover）的种植园上有一个顶精明的人。他信教，可他就是要偷。”
“哦，罗伯特，”西比尔问道，“他又信教、又偷东西，这怎么成呢？”

===Original===
“He didn’t think,” retorted Robert, “it was any harm to steal from his master. I guess he thought it was right to get from his master all he could. He would have thought it wrong to steal from his fellow-servants. He thought that downright mean, but I wouldn’t have insured the lives of Gundover’s pigs and chickens, if Uncle Jack got them in a tight place. One day there was a minister stopping with Mr. Gundover. As a matter of course, in speaking of his servants, he gave Jack’s sins an airing. He would much rather confess Jack’s sins than his own. Now Gundover wanted to do two things, save his pigs and poultry, and save Jack’s soul. He told the minister that Jack was a liar and a thief, and gave the minister a chance to talk with Uncle Jack about the state of his soul. Uncle Jack listened very quietly, and when taxed with stealing his master’s wheat he was ready with an answer. ‘Now Massa Parker,’ said Jack, ‘lem’me tell yer jis’ how it war ’bout dat wheat. Wen ole Jack com’d down yere, dis place war all growed up in woods. He go ter work, clared up de groun’ an’ plowed, an’ planted, an’ riz a crap, an’ den wen it war all done, he hadn’t a dollar to buy his ole woman a gown; an’ he jis’ took a bag ob wheat.’ ”
“What did Mr. Parker say?” asked Sybil.
“I don’t know, though I reckon he didn’t think it was a bad steal after all, but I don’t suppose he told Jack so. When he came to the next point, about Jack’s lying, I suppose he thought he had a clear case; but Jack was equal to the occasion.”
