## XXVII Diverging Paths / 第二十七章 分道扬镳

===Original===
On the eve of his departure from the city of P﻿—﻿—, Dr. Gresham called on Iola, and found her alone. They talked awhile of reminiscences of the war and hospital life, when Dr. Gresham, approaching Iola, said:﻿—

“Miss Leroy, I am glad the great object of your life is accomplished, and that you have found all your relatives. Years have passed since we parted, years in which I have vainly tried to get a trace of you and have been baffled, but I have found you at last!” Clasping her hand in his, he continued, “I would it were so that I should never lose you again! Iola, will you not grant me the privilege of holding this hand as mine all through the future of our lives? Your search for your mother is ended. She is well cared for. Are you not free at last to share with me my Northern home, free to be mine as nothing else on earth is mine.” Dr. Gresham looked eagerly on Iola’s face, and tried to read its varying expression. “Iola, I learned to love you in the hospital. I have tried to forget you, but it has been all in vain. Your image is just as deeply engraven on my heart as it was the day we parted.”

“Doctor,” she replied, sadly, but firmly, as she withdrew her hand from his, “I feel now as I felt then, that there is an insurmountable barrier between us.”

===Chinese===
动身离开 P—— 城的前夜，格雷沙姆医生来拜访伊奥拉，见她独自在家。两人聊了一会儿战争与医院生活的往事；这时，格雷沙姆医生朝伊奥拉走近一步，说道：

“勒罗伊小姐，你毕生的大愿已经了却，亲人也都寻齐了，我真为你高兴。自我们分手，一年又一年过去，这些年里我一直想寻得你的一丝踪迹，却处处碰壁，可我终究找到你了！”他将她的手握在自己手中，继续说道，“但愿天从人愿，叫我永远不再失去你！伊奥拉，你可肯赐我这份荣幸，让我们的余生都由我来握着这只手，把它当作我的手？你寻找母亲的跋涉已经到了终点，她也被人妥善照应着。如今你终于自由了，可以与我共享北方的家园；终于自由了，可以做我的人——这世上，再没有别的什么像你这样归我所有。”格雷沙姆医生热切地望着伊奥拉的脸，想读出那上面变幻的神情。“伊奥拉，我是在医院里爱上你的。我试过忘记你，却全是枉然。你的形象依旧深深镌刻在我心上，一如我们分手的那一天。”

“医生，”她把手从他手中抽回，哀伤却坚定地答道，“我此刻的心意，一如当年——我们之间，横着一道无法逾越的障碍。”

===Original===
“What is it, Iola?” asked Dr. Gresham, anxiously.

“It is the public opinion which assigns me a place with the colored people.”

“But what right has public opinion to interfere with our marriage relations? Why should we yield to its behests?”

“Because it is stronger than we are, and we cannot run counter to it without suffering its penalties.”

“And what are they, Iola? Shadows that you merely dread?”

===Chinese===
“那障碍是什么，伊奥拉？”格雷沙姆医生焦急地问。

“是舆论——是它把我的名分划到了有色人种那一边。”

“可舆论有什么权利干涉我们的婚姻？我们凭什么要听它号令？”

“因为它比我们强大；我们一旦违逆它，就不能不承受它的惩罚。”

“那惩罚又是什么呢，伊奥拉？只是你凭空惧怕的影子么？”

===Original===
“No! no! the penalties of social ostracism North and South, except here and there some grand and noble exceptions. I do not think that you fully realize how much prejudice against colored people permeates society, lowers the tone of our religion, and reacts upon the life of the nation. After freedom came, mamma was living in the city of A﻿—﻿—, and wanted to unite with a Christian church there. She made application for membership. She passed her examination as a candidate, and was received as a church member. When she was about to make her first communion, she unintentionally took her seat at the head of the column. The elder who was administering the communion gave her the bread in the order in which she sat, but before he gave her the wine someone touched him on the shoulder and whispered a word in his ear. He then passed mamma by, gave the cup to others, and then returned to her. From that rite connected with the holiest memories of earth, my poor mother returned humiliated and depressed.”

“What a shame!” exclaimed Dr. Gresham, indignantly.

“I have seen,” continued Iola, “the same spirit manifested in the North. Mamma once attempted to do missionary work in this city. One day she found an outcast colored girl, whom she wished to rescue. She took her to an asylum for fallen women and made an application for her, but was refused. Colored girls were not received there. Soon after mamma found among the colored people an outcast white girl. Mamma’s sympathies, unfettered by class distinction, were aroused in her behalf, and, in company with two white ladies, she went with the girl to that same refuge. For her the door was freely opened and admittance readily granted. It was as if two women were sinking in the quicksands, and on the solid land stood other women with lifelines in their hands, seeing the deadly sands slowly creeping up around the hapless victims. To one they readily threw the lines of deliverance, but for the other there was not one strand of salvation. Sometime since, to the same asylum, came a poor fallen girl who had escaped from the clutches of a wicked woman. For her the door would have been opened, had not the vile woman from whom she was escaping followed her to that place of refuge and revealed the fact that she belonged to the colored race. That fact was enough to close the door upon her, and to send her back to sin and to suffer, and perhaps to die as a wretched outcast. And yet in this city where a number of charities are advertised, I do not think there is one of them which, in appealing to the public, talks more religion than the managers of this asylum. This prejudice against the colored race environs our lives and mocks our aspirations.”

===Chinese===
“不！不是的！那惩罚，就是南北方一致的社会放逐——固然，这里那里也有寥寥几位高尚卓绝的例外。我想，您并没有充分认识到，针对有色人种的偏见渗透社会有多深——它败坏着我们宗教的格调，又反过来损害着国家的生机。自由到来之后，妈妈住在 A—— 城，想加入当地的一家基督教会。她递交了入会申请，作为慕道候选人通过了考核，被接纳为教会成员。初次领受圣餐那天，她无意间在队列最前头坐了下来。主领圣餐的那位长老按她就座的次序把饼递给了她；可还没等他把酒递给她，就有人碰了碰他的肩膀，在他耳边低语了一句。于是他越过妈妈，先把杯递给了旁人，之后才折回她这里。从那项连着人间最神圣记忆的圣礼上，我可怜的母亲满怀屈辱、黯然归来。”

“真是可耻！”格雷沙姆医生愤然喊道。

“我见过，”伊奥拉接着说，“同样的精神在北方照样显露。妈妈有一回想在这座城里做传教工作。一天，她遇到一个沦落的有色人种姑娘，有心救她一把。她把姑娘带到一所堕落妇女收容所，替她提出收留申请，却被拒绝了——那里不收有色人种的姑娘。过后不久，妈妈又在有色人种中间遇到一个沦落的白人姑娘。妈妈的同情心不受阶级畛域的束缚，为这姑娘动了恻隐；她便约了两位白人女士，带着姑娘去了那同一处庇护所。为她，大门欣然敞开，收留一口应承。那情形，正像两个女子陷在流沙里渐渐下沉，坚实的岸上站着另一些女子，手里攥着救生的绳索，眼睁睁看着要命的流沙缓缓漫上不幸落难者的身子。对这一个，她们爽快地抛出了救援的绳索；对那一个，却连一股救命的绳股也不肯给。前些时候，还是这所收容所，来了一个可怜的堕落姑娘，是从一个恶妇的魔爪里逃出来的。若不是她所逃离的那个毒妇一路追到这处避难之地，揭出她属于有色人种的事实，那扇门原是会为她敞开的。单凭这一个事实，那扇门便对她关上了——把她重新送回罪恶与苦难之中，也许还要让她作为一名凄惨的被弃者死去。然而，在这座把种种慈善事业挂在口头标榜的城市里，我想不出还有哪一家，在向公众呼吁时，能比这所收容所的主事者们更满口的宗教。这种针对有色人种的偏见，围困着我们的生活，嘲弄着我们的抱负。”

===Original===
“Iola, I see no use in your persisting that you are colored when your eyes are as blue and complexion as white as mine.”

“Doctor, were I your wife, are there not people who would caress me as a white woman who would shrink from me in scorn if they knew I had one drop of negro blood in my veins? When mistaken for a white woman, I should hear things alleged against the race at which my blood would boil. No, Doctor, I am not willing to live under a shadow of concealment which I thoroughly hate as if the blood in my veins were an undetected crime of my soul.”

“Iola, dear, surely you paint the picture too darkly.”

“Doctor, I have painted it with my heart’s blood. It is easier to outgrow the dishonor of crime than the disabilities of color. You have created in this country an aristocracy of color wide enough to include the South with its treason and Utah with its abominations, but too narrow to include the best and bravest colored man who bared his breast to the bullets of the enemy during your fratricidal strife. Is not the most arrant Rebel today more acceptable to you than the most faithful colored man?”

===Chinese===
“伊奥拉，你的眼睛和我的一样蓝，肤色和我的一样白，我不明白你何必一口咬定自己是有色人种。”

“医生，倘若我做了您的妻子，难道没有这样的人吗——他们把我当白人女子一般亲热相待，可一旦知道我血管里流着哪怕一滴黑人的血，就会鄙夷地躲开我？当我被误认作白人女子时，我会听见他们加在这个种族头上的种种言语，听得我热血沸腾。不，医生，我不愿活在遮遮掩掩的阴影之下；我对这阴影深恶痛绝，就仿佛血管里的血，是我灵魂里一桩未被查出的罪。”

“伊奥拉，亲爱的，你把这幅画画得未免太黑暗了。”

“医生，这幅画，我是用心头的血画成的。罪行的污名还容易随着岁月褪去，肤色带来的桎梏却难以挣脱。你们在这个国家创制了一种肤色贵族制：它够宽，宽得足以把叛国罪身的南方和丑行累累的犹他（Utah）一并囊括；却又太窄，窄得容不下那位最优秀、最勇敢的有色人——在你们那场同室操戈的战火里，他可是袒露过胸膛、迎着敌人枪弹的。今天，一个最嚣张的叛党，岂不是比一个最忠心的有色人更合你们的心意吗？”

===Original===
“No! no!” exclaimed Dr. Gresham, vehemently. “You are wrong. I belong to the Grand Army of the Republic. We have no separate State Posts for the colored people, and, were such a thing proposed, the majority of our members, I believe, would be against it. In Congress colored men have the same seats as white men, and the color line is slowly fading out in our public institutions.”

“But how is it in the Church?” asked Iola.

“The Church is naturally conservative. It preserves old truths, even if it is somewhat slow in embracing new ideas. It has its social as well as its spiritual side. Society is woman’s realm. The majority of church members are women, who are said to be the aristocratic element of our country. I fear that one of the last strongholds of this racial prejudice will be found beneath the shadow of some of our churches. I think, on account of this social question, that large bodies of Christian temperance women and other reformers, in trying to reach the colored people even for their own good, will be quicker to form separate associations than our National Grand Army, whose ranks are open to black and white, liberals and conservatives, saints and agnostics. But, Iola, we have drifted far away from the question. No one has a right to interfere with our marriage if we do not infringe on the rights of others.”

===Chinese===
“不！不是这样！”格雷沙姆医生激烈地喊道，“你错了。我是共和国大军（Grand Army of the Republic）的一员。我们没有为有色人种另设州级分会；倘若有人提议这样办，我相信我们的大多数会员都会反对。在国会里，有色人坐的席位与白人并无二致；在我们的公共机构里，肤色界线也正在慢慢消隐。”

“可在教会里，又是怎样的呢？”伊奥拉问。

“教会天生是保守的。它守护古老的真理，只是在接纳新思想上有些迟缓。它有属灵的一面，也有社交的一面。社交是女性的领地。教会成员的大多数是妇女，据说她们正是我国的贵族成分。我担心，这种种族偏见的最后几座堡垒之一，将来就会盘踞在我们某些教会的阴影底下。我想，正是为了这个社交问题的缘故，那些人数众多的基督教戒酒会妇女和其他改革人士，即便是为了有色人种自身的好处而去接近他们，也会比我们全国一体的共和国大军更抢先另立分设的组织——而后者的队伍，向黑人与白人、自由派与保守派、圣徒与不可知论者，一律敞开。不过，伊奥拉，我们离本题扯得太远了。只要我们不侵犯他人的权利，谁也无权干涉我们的婚姻。”

===Original===
“Doctor,” she replied, gently, “I feel that our paths must diverge. My lifework is planned. I intend spending my future among the colored people of the South.”

“My dear friend,” he replied, anxiously, “I am afraid that you are destined to sad disappointment. When the novelty wears off you will be disillusioned, and, I fear, when the time comes that you can no longer serve them they will forget your services and remember only your failings.”

“But, Doctor, they need me; and I am sure when I taught among them they were very grateful for my services.”

===Chinese===
“医生，”她轻声答道，“我觉得，我们的路注定要岔开。我一生的事业已经定了：往后的岁月，我打算就在南方的有色人种中间度过。”

“亲爱的朋友，”他忧心忡忡地答道，“恐怕你注定要陷于悲哀的失望。等到新鲜劲儿一过，你就会幻灭；而且我怕，到了你再不能为他们效力的时候，他们会忘掉你的种种劳绩，只记得你的种种短处。”

“可是医生，他们需要我；而且我敢说，当年我在他们中间教书的时候，他们对我的付出是十分感激的。”

===Original===
“I think,” he replied, “these people are more thankful than grateful.”

“I do not think so; and if I did it would not hinder me from doing all in my power to help them. I do not expect all the finest traits of character to spring from the hotbeds of slavery and caste. What matters it if they do forget the singer, so they don’t forget the song? No, Doctor, I don’t think that I could best serve my race by forsaking them and marrying you.”

“Iola,” he exclaimed, passionately, “if you love your race, as you call it, work for it, live for it, suffer for it, and, if need be, die for it; but don’t marry for it. Your education has unfitted you for social life among them.”

===Chinese===
“依我看，”他答道，“这些人哪，道谢有余，感念不足。”

“我不这么看；就算真是这样，也拦不住我竭尽所能去帮助他们。我并不指望人间最高贵的品格，能从奴隶制与种姓偏见的温床里生长出来。他们纵然忘了那唱歌的人，只要不忘那首歌，又有什么要紧？不，医生，我不认为抛下他们、嫁给你，便是我为我的种族效力的最好方式。”

“伊奥拉，”他激昂地喊道，“倘若你爱你的种族——姑且用你的叫法——那就为它工作，为它生活，为它受苦，必要时为它去死；可不要为它去结婚。你所受的教育，已经使你不适合在他们中间过社交生活。”

===Original===
“It was,” replied Iola, “through their unrequited toil that I was educated, while they were compelled to live in ignorance. I am indebted to them for the power I have to serve them. I wish other Southern women felt as I do. I think they could do so much to help the colored people at their doors if they would look at their opportunities in the light of the face of Jesus Christ. Nor am I wholly unselfish in allying myself with the colored people. All the rest of my family have done so. My dear grandmother is one of the excellent of the earth, and we all love her too much to ignore our relationship with her. I did not choose my lot in life, and the simplest thing I can do is to accept the situation and do the best I can.”

“And is this your settled purpose?” he asked, sadly.

“It is, Doctor,” she replied, tenderly but firmly. “I see no other. I must serve the race which needs me most.”

“Perhaps you are right,” he replied; “but I cannot help feeling sad that our paths, which met so pleasantly, should diverge so painfully. And yet, not only the freedmen, but the whole country, need such helpful, self-sacrificing teachers as you will prove; and if earnest prayers and holy wishes can brighten your path, your lines will fall in the pleasantest places.”

As he rose to go, sympathy, love, and admiration were blended in the parting look he gave her; but he felt it was useless to attempt to divert her from her purpose. He knew that for the true reconstruction of the country something more was needed than bayonets and bullets, or the schemes of selfish politicians or plotting demagogues. He knew that the South needed the surrender of the best brain and heart of the country to build, above the wastes of war, more stately temples of thought and action.

===Chinese===
“我的教育，”伊奥拉答道，“正是靠他们分文未得报偿的劳作才换来的，而他们自己，却被逼着活在无知之中。我今日有能力为他们效力，这份能力本就是亏欠他们的。我但愿别的南方女子也怀着和我一样的心肠。我想，她们若能把耶稣基督面容的光，照在自家门前的机会上看，本可以为身边的有色人种帮上那么多的忙。我把自己同有色人种联结在一起，也并非全然出于无私。我家里其余的人，都已经这样做了。我亲爱的外祖母，是地上至善者中的一位；我们都太爱她了，不可能漠视与她的骨肉之亲。我这一生的境遇并非我自己的拣选；我能做的最简单的事，就是接受眼前的处境，尽我所能去做。”

“这就是你定下的志向了吗？”他黯然问道。

“是的，医生，”她温柔却坚定地答道，“我看不出还有别的路。我必须去为那个最需要我的种族效力。”

“也许你是对的，”他答道，“可我们的路当年那样愉快地相逢，如今却要这样痛苦地分开，我禁不住感到悲伤。然而，需要你这样乐于助人、甘于自我牺牲的教师的——你日后必会证明自己是这样的教师——不仅是获得自由的人们，还有整个国家；倘若恳切的祈祷与神圣的祝愿能够照亮你的路，那么用绳量给你的地界，必坐落在佳美之处。”

他起身告辞，临别投给她的那一眼里，交融着怜惜、爱慕与钦敬；但他明白，想劝她改弦更张已是徒劳。他知道，要真正重建这个国家，所需要的不止是刺刀与枪弹，也不止是自私政客的算计与结党煽动家的诡谋。他知道，南方需要全国最优秀的头脑与心灵倾诚相付，在战争的废墟之上，筑起更加庄严的思想与行动的殿堂。
