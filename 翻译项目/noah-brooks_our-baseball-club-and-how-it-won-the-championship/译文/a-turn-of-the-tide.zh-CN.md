## VIII A Turn of the Tide / VIII 潮势逆转

===Original===
Defeat, utter and overwhelming, followed the Catalpas to Bluford, where they played the “Zoo-Zoo Nine” of that city. The “Zoo-Zoos” were picked players, the lineal descendants of a company of Illinois Zouaves renowned in the Civil War for their bravery, dash, and skill as skirmishers. The original founders of the club had long since disappeared from the field of action, but their successors bore up the banner of their illustrious namesakes with infinite credit. None of the Catalpa people had gone to Bluford to witness the game, Al Heaton being sick at home and the other immediate friends of the Nine being too busy with their farms and merchandise. And so it happened that the only news that came to the town from Bluford dribbled in from the Keokuck evening papers, sent by wire to the editor of *The Catalpa Leaf*, late at night. Mr. Downey did not think it worth while to post on his bulletin board the discouraging news that the “Zoo-Zoo Nine” had beaten the Catalpas by a score of eleven to one. But the news got out, of course, for the whole town was on the alert to hear the result from Bluford.

Albert Heaton was sitting up in bed, alternately shaken with ague and parched with fever, when his little sister brought him the unwelcome tidings. He groaned aloud and asked if Alice Howell had heard the news. Mrs. Heaton, a motherly woman who had no patience with baseball players that go about the country, like circus-riders, remarked, with some asperity, that she should suppose that Judge Howell would put a stop to Alice’s giving so much time and attention to baseball. For herself, if she had a grown-up daughter, she would try and put something else into her head than baseball and such mannish and vulgar doings. If Alice’s mother was alive, it would be mighty different in the Howell family. As it was, the Judge allowed Alice to do just about as she pleased, and it was a shame, so it was, for a nice young girl like Alice to be permitted to make a tomboy of herself. Flirting with that young Irish fellow from Sugar Grove! Did anybody ever hear of the like?

===Chinese===
彻底而惨重的失败，一路跟着卡塔帕队到了布卢福德（Bluford），他们在那儿同该市的「祖祖九人队」（Zoo-Zoo Nine）交了手。「祖祖队」是精选出来的好手，是一支伊利诺伊祖阿夫连队的嫡系传人——那支连队在南北战争中以骁勇、剽悍和散兵战的技艺闻名。俱乐部的创始人们早已从征场上消失，但他们的后继者把那响亮名号的旗帜扛得光彩照人。卡塔帕没有人去布卢福德观战，阿尔·希顿在家病着，九人队的其他至交好友又都忙于农场和买卖。于是，从布卢福德传回镇上的唯一消息，是从基奥卡克（Keokuck）的晚报上一点一滴渗漏来的——电报深夜拍给了《卡塔帕叶报》（*The Catalpa Leaf*）的编辑。唐尼先生（Mr. Downey）觉得，把「祖祖九人队」以十一比一击败卡塔帕队这个令人丧气的消息贴上公告栏，实在不值得。可消息当然还是传开了，因为全镇的人都竖着耳朵等着布卢福德的战报。

阿尔伯特·希顿正靠坐在床上，一会儿疟疾发作抖个不停，一会儿又烧得口干舌燥，他的小妹妹给他带来了这个不受欢迎的消息。他大声呻吟起来，问爱丽丝·豪厄尔听说了没有。希顿太太是位慈母般的妇人，对那些像马戏团骑手一样四处闯荡的棒球球员毫无耐心；她语带尖刻地说，她倒以为，豪厄尔法官早该制止爱丽丝把这么多时间和心思花在棒球上。换成她自己，若有个成年女儿，她定要设法往女儿脑袋里装点别的东西，而不是棒球这类男人气的粗俗玩意儿。爱丽丝的母亲若是还在世，豪厄尔家绝不会是这个样子。如今呢，法官任由爱丽丝想干什么就干什么，这真是丢脸事——千真万确是丢脸事——像爱丽丝这样一个好端端的年轻姑娘，竟被纵容成了个野丫头。还跟舒格格罗夫（Sugar Grove）来的那个爱尔兰小伙子眉来眼去！谁听说过这种事？

===Original===
“Oh, mother,” sighed poor Albert. “If you only knew how sick and sore I am for the boys, you would let up on Larry. If you had let me go with the Nine, perhaps I might have helped them out of the defeat. At any rate, it might have been less of a clean-out than it is. Dear me! How cold I am! Cover me up and let me be.”

With a pang of remorse at having added unwittingly to Albert’s sufferings, his mother soothed the sick boy and left him to sorrowful meditations. “And I was fool enough to think that the boys would be able to challenge the Calumets.” With these repentant meditations, Albert sunk into a feverish and uneasy sleep. He might have dreamed (perhaps he did) that at that very moment, Alice Howell was looking out into the gloom of the moist summer night and lamenting with bitterness the defeat of “our nine.”

===Chinese===
「唉，妈妈，」可怜的阿尔伯特叹道。「您要是知道我为伙计们有多难受、多痛心，就不会再数落拉里了。您要是让我跟九人队一起去，说不定我还能帮他们跳出这场败局。至少，也不至于输得像现在这样一败涂地。天哪！我好冷！给我盖好被子，别管我了。」

母亲想到自己无意中加重了阿尔伯特的病痛，一阵悔恨袭上心头，她安抚了病中的孩子，留下他独自黯然神伤。「我竟然傻到以为，伙计们能有本事向卡柳梅特队挑战。」怀着这些懊悔的念头，阿尔伯特沉入了燥热不安的睡眠。他也许梦见了（说不定真梦见了）——就在那一刻，爱丽丝·豪厄尔正望着潮湿夏夜的幽暗出神，为「我们九人队」的败绩黯然神伤。

===Original===
Next day, when *The Leaf* came out, and fuller particulars of the game were made known in a despatch from Charlie King, there was nothing to mitigate the gloom of the friends of the Catalpas. Singularly enough, some of the Dean County Nine, who had been among the most enthusiastic boomers of the Catalpa Nine, now assumed a most discouraging attitude. They were sure, so they said, that the Catalpas would be defeated all along the line. They had won the game at Sandy Key by a scratch. They had found their true level in Bluford. They would be beaten along the river, for it was well known that the nines in the river towns were far ahead of those in the interior of the state.

===Chinese===
第二天，《叶报》出来了，查利·金发来的电讯披露了比赛的更多细节，可没有什么能减轻卡塔帕队朋友们的阴郁。奇怪的是，迪安县九人队（Dean County Nine）里的一些人——他们本是卡塔帕九人队最起劲的吹鼓手——如今却摆出一副大泼冷水的架势。他们说，卡塔帕队准会一路输到底。他们在桑迪基（Sandy Key）那场球赢得侥幸。他们在布卢福德才现出了真实的斤两。到了沿河一带他们还要输，因为河畔城镇的九人队远比本州内陆的高明，这是人所共知的。

===Original===
Something of this talk reached the ears of Al Heaton, who was still suffering from fever-and-ague. He took up his bottle of cholagogue and shook it at his terrified little brother (who had retailed the gossip of the drug store, where he had been sent on an errand), and said, “If you hear any such infernal nonsense as that, downtown, Dan, you go and tell Tom Selby that I want him to lick the first fellow that says anything against our nine. Do you mind me?”

Little Dan promised stoutly that he would give Tom the message. Whether he did or not, it came to pass that Henry Jackson and Thomas Selby had a discussion, that very night, and that Dr. Selby sent his son home with strict injunctions to cover his face with brown paper and vinegar, while the big-fisted Henry went to bed with a bit of raw beef on his eye.

===Chinese===
这些闲话有几句传到了阿尔·希顿的耳朵里，他的寒热病还没有好利索。他抓起那瓶驱胆药水，朝吓坏了的小弟弟晃着（这孩子是被打发去药店办事的，把那边的闲话学舌了回来），说：「丹（Dan），你要是在街上再听见这种混账胡话，就去告诉汤姆·塞尔比，我要他狠揍第一个说咱们九人队坏话的家伙。听明白了没有？」

小丹一口应承，说一定把话带到。不管他带没带到，反正当天夜里，亨利·杰克逊（Henry Jackson）和托马斯·塞尔比（Thomas Selby）果然「理论」了一番：塞尔比医生严令儿子回家用棕纸蘸醋敷脸，而那位拳头硕大的亨利则贴着一片生牛肉上了床，敷在眼睛上。

===Original===
There is nothing like news from the field of battle to bring out the partisan feelings of a community far from the scene of strife. Catalpa was stirred to its very depths by the ill tidings brought from Bluford. Those who disapproved of baseball asserted themselves in the most unexpected and exasperating manner. Nobody had suspected that there were in Catalpa so many who sympathized not with the home nine and who secretly wished that they might be defeated. But the fact that the nine had met with disaster only stimulated their friends to new courage and stronger hopes for the future. This was a time, they said, for the friends of the nine to show themselves. Mr. Heaton sent an encouraging despatch to Larry Boyne, assuring him that the temporary reverse had only strengthened the confidence of home friends of the club. Even Judge Howell, who was greatly concerned lest the nine should be unduly depressed by their reverses, authorized Lewis Morris to write to Hiram Porter, as Captain of the club, and say to him that the club must be prepared for occasional defeats and that the next news from “the front” would undoubtedly be inspiring to the many supporters of the Catalpas.

===Chinese===
再没有什么比战场传来的消息，更能激发远离战场的乡镇的党派情绪了。布卢福德传来的噩耗把卡塔帕从底到面搅了个透。那些不赞成棒球的人，以最出人意料、最叫人冒火的方式跳了出来。谁也没想到，卡塔帕竟有这么多不向着主队、暗地里巴不得他们输球的人。然而九人队遭遇惨败这一事实，反倒激发他们的朋友们鼓起了新的勇气、怀着更强烈的希望。他们说，如今正是九人队的朋友们挺身而出的时候。希顿先生给拉里·博因发去一封鼓舞人心的电报，向他保证，这一时的挫败只会增强俱乐部家乡友人对它的信心。就连豪厄尔法官——他十分挂念，唯恐九人队被连番失利压垮——也授权刘易斯·莫里斯（Lewis Morris）写信给俱乐部队长海勒姆·波特，告诉他俱乐部必须做好偶尝败绩的准备，而「前线」传来的下一个消息，无疑将令卡塔帕队众多的支持者为之一振。

===Original===
“The Judge is a brick!” said Larry Boyne, when this message was read to the members of the club, as they lounged in one of the bedrooms of Quapaw House, in Galena, where the boys were waiting to begin the championship series of games with the Red Stockings.

“That’s just what he is!” exclaimed “The Lily,” bringing his somewhat battered fist down with emphasis on a convenient pillow. Bill had had hard luck in the late contest. His fingers had been badly sprained and twisted, and he had played with infinite difficulty on account of the battering that he had received in a game played with the Fulton City Nine, when the Catalpas were on their way to Bluford from Sandy Key. But he was still confident and determined.

===Chinese===
「这位法官真是条汉子！」有人向全体队员念这封信时，拉里·博因说。他们正懒散地躺在加利纳（Galena）夸帕旅馆（Quapaw House）的一间卧室里，小伙子们在那儿等着同红袜队（Red Stockings）拉开冠军系列赛的战幕。

「他正是这样的人！」「百合」喊道，把他那只吃了些苦头的拳头重重地砸在顺手的一只枕头上。比尔在最近那场比赛里运气糟透了。他的手指扭伤得不轻，都有些变形了；卡塔帕队从桑迪基前往布卢福德途中同富尔顿城九人队（Fulton City Nine）打了一场比赛，他在那场球里挨了不少磕碰，打起球来困难重重。但他依然信心十足、意志坚定。

===Original===
“I suppose some of the folks at home think that we are going to get beaten right along, every day from this out,” he continued, with a scornful laugh. “They don’t know us, do they, Larry? They don’t know what we had to contend with in Bluford, what with being used up with that hard ride on the strap-iron railroad and the lame fingers of your humble servant. Oh, yes, I suppose there is downheartedness among the boys at home.”

“But I know one chap who is not downhearted,” said Larry Boyne, cheerfully, “and that is Al Heaton. He will never get discouraged, whatever happens. And then there is his father, his despatch shows where he stands. Al is clear grit and so is his father; you may depend on that, boys.”

「我猜家里有些人以为，从今往后我们天天都要吃败仗。」他带着轻蔑的笑继续说。「他们不了解我们，对吧，拉里？他们不知道我们在布卢福德吃了什么苦头——乘那条铁带铁路一路颠簸得筋疲力尽，外加鄙人这双残废的手指。哦，是啊，我看家乡的伙伴们中间准是有人灰心丧气了。」

「可是有一个人我知道他绝不灰心。」拉里·博因欢快地说，「那就是阿尔·希顿。无论出了什么事，他绝不会泄气。还有他父亲，他那封电报表明了他的立场。阿尔是条硬汉，他父亲也是；这一点你们尽管放心，伙计们。」

===Original===
Ben Burton, who had virtually lost the game in Bluford by his repeated muffing of the ball, as well as by his failure at the bat, sneered as he said, “I suppose a certain young lady in North Catalpa prompted the Judge’s despatch, didn’t she, Larry?”

Larry, with reddening cheeks, protested that he had no idea that Judge Howell needed any prompting from anybody to send a good word to the boys when they were away from home; he was too kindhearted a man, although a little stiff, to require any hint from outsiders to do the fair thing by the Baseball Club in whose welfare he had already shown great interest.

“I didn’t say ‘outsiders,’ Larry,” replied Burton, persistently. “I said that he was probably prompted by a young lady.”

At this, Larry deliberately rose and walked out of the room, without a word.
