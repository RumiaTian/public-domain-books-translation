## XXII The Darkest Hour / 第二十二章 至暗时刻

===Original===
“The time,” said Mr. Campion, “is nine o’clock.”

Chris Kennedy stretched himself wearily.

“Six hours since that swine left us,” he said. “Do you think we’ve got an earthly?”

There was a stir in the room after he had spoken, and almost everybody looked at the pale-haired bespectacled young man who sat squatting on his haunches in a corner. Jeanne and Prenderby were alone unconscious of what was going on. The little girl still supported the boy’s head in her lap, with her timid little figure crouched over him, her face hidden.

===Chinese===
“时间嘛，”坎皮恩先生说，“现在是九点整。”

克里斯·肯尼迪疲惫地舒展了一下身子。

“那个猪猡丢下咱们，已经六个钟头了。”他说。“你说咱们还有半点儿指望吗？”

他话音落下，屋里起了一阵骚动，几乎人人都望向角落里那个蹲坐着的淡色头发、戴眼镜的年轻人。唯有让娜和普伦德比两个，对眼前发生的一切浑然不觉。小姑娘仍把小伙子的头托在膝上，怯生生的瘦小身子伏在他上方，脸埋着看不见。

===Original===
Albert Campion shook his head.

“I don’t know,” he said, but there was no hopefulness in his tone, and once again the little group relapsed into the silence that had settled over them after the first outburst which had followed von Faber’s departure.

Whatever their attitude had been before, they were all now very much alive to the real peril of their position.

Von Faber had not been wasting his time when he had spoken to them, and they had each been struck by the stark callousness which had been visible in him throughout the entire interview.

===Chinese===
阿尔伯特·坎皮恩摇了摇头。

“不知道。”他说，可语气里没有半点指望。这一小群人再度沉入静默——冯·法贝尔离去后那阵最初的骚动平息下来，这静默便一直笼罩着他们。

不管先前各人态度如何，此刻他们全都清清楚楚地感到了自身处境的真正凶险。

冯·法贝尔同他们说话时，字字句句都不是白说的；那场谈话自始至终，他浑身上下透出的赤裸裸的冷酷无情，在场每个人都看在眼里，心头凛然。

===Original===
At last Campion rose to his feet and came across to where Meggie and Abbershaw were seated. Gravely he offered Abbershaw his cigarette-case in which there was a single cigarette neatly cut into two pieces.

“I did it with a razor blade,” he said. “Rather neat, don’t you think?”

Abbershaw took the half gratefully and they shared a match.

===Chinese===
终于，坎皮恩站起身，走到梅吉和阿伯肖坐着的地方。他郑重其事地把烟盒递给阿伯肖——盒里孤零零一支香烟，齐齐整整剖成了两半。

“我用剃刀片弄的。”他说。“挺利索的吧，你说？”

阿伯肖感激地接过那半支，两人合用了一根火柴。

===Original===
“I suppose,” said Campion suddenly, speaking in a quiet and confidential tone, “I suppose you did really burn that junk, Doc.”

Abbershaw glanced at him sharply.

“I did,” he said. “God forgive me. When I think what I’m responsible for I feel I shall go mad.”

Mr. Campion shrugged his shoulders.

===Chinese===
“我猜啊，”坎皮恩忽然开了口，语气平静而推心置腹，“我猜你真把那玩意儿烧掉了，大夫。”

阿伯肖锐利地瞥了他一眼。

“烧了。”他说。“上帝饶恕我。一想到我如今担着多大的干系，我就觉得自己快要疯了。”

坎皮恩先生耸了耸肩。

===Original===
“My dear old bird,” he said, “I shouldn’t put too much stress on what our friend von Faber says. He doesn’t seem to me to be a person to be relied upon.”

“Why? Do you think he’s just trying to frighten us?”

Abbershaw spoke eagerly, and the other shook his head.

“I’m afraid not, in the sense you mean,” he said. “I think he’s set his heart on this little conflagration scene. The man is a criminal loony, of course. No, I only meant that probably, had someone handed over his million-dollar book of the words, the Guy Fawkes celebrations would have gone forward all the same. I’m afraid he’s just a nasty vindictive person.”

===Chinese===
“我亲爱的老伙计，”他说，“冯·法贝尔朋友的话，不必太往心里去。依我看，他不是个靠得住的人。”

“为什么？你觉得他不过是想吓唬咱们？”

阿伯肖说得急切，对方摇了摇头。

“恐怕不是，不是你说的那层意思。”他说。“我看他是铁了心要上演这出小小的纵火戏。这人当然是个犯罪疯子。不，我不过是说，多半就算有人把他那本价值百万美元的密码册子交了出来，这场盖伊·福克斯（Guy Fawkes）庆典照样照办不误。我看他就是个歹毒记仇的家伙。”

===Original===
Meggie shuddered but her voice was quite firm.

“Do you mean to say that you really think he’ll burn the house down with us up here?” she said.

Campion looked up at her, and then at Abbershaw.

“Not a nice type is he?” he murmured. “I’m afraid we’re for it, unless by a miracle the villagers see the bonfire before we’re part of it, or the son of our friend in the attic calls earlier than was expected.”

===Chinese===
梅吉打了个寒噤，声音却十分镇定。

“你是说，你真的认为他会把咱们困在这儿，一把火烧了这宅子？”她说。

“不是个善茬儿，对吧？”他喃喃道。“恐怕咱们在劫难逃，除非奇迹出现——村里人赶在咱们变成篝火的一部分之前望见了火光，要不然，就是咱们阁楼上那位朋友的儿子，来得比预想的早。”

===Original===
Meggie stiffened.

“Mrs. Meade,” she said. “I’d forgotten all about her. What will Mr. Dawlish do about her, do you suppose?”

Mr. Campion spoke grimly.

“I could guess,” he said, and there was silence for a while after that.

===Chinese===
梅吉浑身一僵。

“米德太太。”她说。“我把她忘得一干二净了。你猜道利什先生会怎么处置她？”

坎皮恩先生说得冷冷的。

“我猜得出来。”他说。此后有好一阵子，谁都没有作声。

===Original===
“But how terrible!” Meggie burst out suddenly. “I didn’t believe that people like this were allowed to exist. I thought we were civilized. I thought this sort of thing couldn’t happen.”

Mr. Campion sighed. “A lot of people believe things like that,” he said. “They imagine the world is a well-ordered nursery with Scotland Yard and the British Army standing by to whack anybody who quarrels or uses a naughty word. I thought that at one time, I suppose everybody does, but it’s not like that really, you know. Look at me, for example—who would dream of the cunning criminal brain that lurks beneath my inoffensive exterior?”

The other two regarded him curiously. In any other circumstances they would have been embarrassed. Abbershaw was the first to speak.

===Chinese===
“多可怕啊！”梅吉突然脱口喊道。“我从前不信，世上竟会被容许有这样的人存在。我以为我们是文明人。我以为这种事根本不可能发生。”

坎皮恩先生叹了口气。“许多人都是这么信的。”他说，“他们把世界想象成一间秩序井然的育儿室，苏格兰场和大英陆军守在一旁，谁吵架、谁讲顽皮话，就揍谁一顿。我从前有一阵也这么想——我猜人人都这么想过——可实情并不是那样，你知道。就拿我来说吧——谁能梦想得到，我这副人畜无害的外表底下，竟潜藏着一颗狡诈的犯罪头脑？”

另外两人好奇地望着他。换在任何别的场合，他们准会觉得尴尬。阿伯肖先开了口。

===Original===
“I say,” he said, “if you don’t mind my asking such a thing, what on earth made you take up your—er—present profession?”

Mr. Campion regarded him owlishly through his enormous spectacles.

“Profession?” he said indignantly. “It’s my vocation. It seemed to me that I had no talent for anything else, but in this line I can eke out the family pittance with tolerable comfort. Of course,” he went on suddenly, as he caught sight of Meggie’s face, “I don’t exactly ‘crim,’ you know, as I told the doc here. My taste is impeccable. Most of my commissions are more secret than shady. I occasionally do a spot of work for the Government, though, of course, that isn’t as lucrative as honest crime. This little affair, of course, was perfectly simple. I had only to join this house-party, take a packet of letters from the old gentleman, toddle back to the Savoy, and my client would be waiting for me. A hundred guineas, and all clean fun—no brain-work required.” He beamed at them. “Of course I knew what I was in for,” he went on. “I knew that more or less as soon as I got down here. I didn’t expect anything quite like this, though, I admit. I’m afraid the Gay Career and all that is in the soup.”

===Chinese===

===Original===
He spoke lightly, but there was no callousness in his face, and it suddenly occurred to Abbershaw that he was doing his best to cheer them up, for after a moment or two of silence he remarked suddenly:

“After all, I don’t see why the place should burn as he says it will, and I know people do escape from burning houses because I’ve seen it on the pictures.”

His remarks were cut short by a thundering blow upon the door, and in the complete silence that followed, a voice spoke slowly and distinctly so that it was audible throughout the entire room.

“You have another hour,” it said, “in which to restore Mr. Dawlish’s property. ===Chinese===

And then, quite suddenly, from somewhere far across the fields there came a sound which everybody in the room recognized. A sound which brought them to their feet, the blood returning to their cheeks, and sent them crowding to the window, a new hope in their eyes.

===Chinese===
“叫他烧吧，让他下地狱去！”马丁喊道。

“答得妙极！”坎皮恩先生喃喃道，可他嗓音里那份轻快已经没了，这句话也淹进姑娘歇斯底里的呜咽里，听不真切了。

接着，毫无征兆地，远远的田野那头传来一个声音——屋里人人认得的声音。一个让他们霍然起身、血色重回双颊的声音；一个让他们涌向窗前、眼中燃起新的希望的声音。

===Original===
It was the thin far-off call of a hunting horn.

Martin, his head jammed between the bars of the narrow window, let out a whoop of joy.

“The Hunt, by God!” he said. “Yes—Lord! There’s the pack not a quarter of a mile away! Glory be to God, was that a splodge of red behind that hedge? It was! Here he comes!”

===Chinese===
那是猎号的鸣声，细弱而遥远。

马丁把脑袋挤在窄窗的栏杆中间，爆发出一声欢呼。

“是猎队，老天作证！”他说。“没错——主啊！猎犬群就在不到四分之一英里外！荣耀归于上帝，树篱后头那一小抹红是什么？是红衣！他来啦！”

===Original===
His voice was resonant with excitement, and he struggled violently as if he would force himself through the iron bars.

“There he is,” he said again; “and yes, look at him—look at him! Half the county behind him! They’re in the park now. Gosh! They’re coming right for us. Quick! Yell to ’em! God! They mustn’t go past! How can we attract them! Yell at ’em! Shout something! They’ll be on us in a minute.”

===Chinese===
他的声音因激动而洪亮震耳，身子拼命挣扎，仿佛要硬从铁栏杆中间挤出去了。

“他在那儿。”他又喊道。“没错，快看他——快看他！半个郡的人跟在他后头！他们进园地了。天哪！他们径直朝咱们来了。快！朝他们喊！上帝！千万不能让他们走过去！怎么才能引他们注意！朝他们喊！随便喊点什么！一转眼他们就要到跟前了！”

===Original===
“I think,” murmured a quiet, rather foolish voice that yet had a note of tension in its tone, “that in circumstances like this a ‘view-halloo’ would be permissible. Quickly! Now, are you ready, my children? Let her go!”

There was utter silence after the shout died away upon the wind, and then Campion’s voice behind them murmured again:

“Once more. Put your backs into it.”

===Chinese===
“我想，”一个平静而略显傻气的声音咕哝道，语气里却绷着一丝紧张，“在这般情形之下，喊一声‘见狐啦’（view-halloo）想必是合乎礼数的。快！好了，孩子们，都准备好了吗？放开了喊！”

===Original===

Martin suddenly caught his breath.

“他们听见了。”他说，声音激动得发噎。“有个人正朝这边过来了。”
