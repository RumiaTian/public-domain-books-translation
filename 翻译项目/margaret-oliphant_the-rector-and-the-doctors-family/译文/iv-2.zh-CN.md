### IV / 四

===Original===

“Take me down this long pretty road. There must be delicious houses inside the walls. Look here; drive slowly, and let us have a peep in at this open door,” said Nettie. “How sweet and cosy! and who is that pretty young lady coming out? I saw her in the chapel this morning. Oh,” added Nettie, with a little sharpness, “she knows *you*—tell me who she is.”

“That is Miss Lucy Wodehouse—one of our Carlingford beauties,” said Dr. Rider.

“Do you know her very well?” asked the inquisitive Nettie. “How she stares—why does she stare, do you suppose? Is there anything absurd about my dress? Look here—don’t they wear bonnets just like this in England?”

===Chinese===

“带我走这条又长又好看的马路。围墙里头准是些叫人心爱的宅子。瞧这儿；把车赶慢点儿，让我们从这扇敞开的门里瞅上一眼，”妮蒂（Nettie）说。“多甜美、多安逸！打里头出来的那位漂亮小姐是谁？今儿早上我在礼拜堂里见到过她。哦，”妮蒂带着几分尖利补了一句，“她认得*你*——告诉我，她是谁。”

“那是露西·伍德豪斯（Lucy Wodehouse）小姐——我们卡林福德（Carlingford）的美人儿之一，”莱德医生（Dr. Rider）说。

“您同她很熟吗？”打破砂锅问到底的妮蒂问。“瞧她直盯着我看——您说，她干吗要盯着我看？我这身穿戴有什么可笑的地方吗？瞧瞧——在英国，人们不就戴的正是这种软帽吗？”

===Original===

“So far as I am able to judge,” said the doctor, looking at the tiny head overladen with hair, from which the bonnet had fallen half off.

“I suppose she is surprised to see me here. Drive on faster, Dr. Edward, I want to talk to you. I see Fred has been telling us a parcel of stories. It would be cruel to tell Susan, you know, for she believes in him; but you may quite trust me. Is your brother good for anything, Dr. Edward, do you suppose?”

“Not very much now, I fear,” said the doctor.

===Chinese===

“就凭我的眼力判断，”医生说，一面望着那颗压满浓发的小脑袋——软帽已从上头滑落了一半。

“我想，她看见我在这儿，是吃惊了。把车赶快点儿，爱德华医生（Dr. Edward），我有话要同您说。我看弗雷德（Fred）给我们编了一大篇瞎话。这要是告诉了苏珊（Susan）就太狠心了，您知道，她是一心信他的；不过您尽可以放心信我。您哥哥还能派上一点儿用场吗，爱德华医生，您看呢？”

“眼下怕是派不上什么了，”医生说。

===Original===

“Not very much *now*. I suppose he never was good for much,” said the indignant Nettie; “but he was said to be very clever when he first came out to the colony. I can’t tell why Susan married him. She is very self-willed, though you would fancy her so submissive. She is one of those people, you know, who fall ill when they are crossed, and threaten to die, so that one daren’t cross her. Now, then, what is to be done with them? He will not go back to the colony, and I don’t care to do it myself. Must I keep them here?”

“Miss Underwood—” began the perplexed doctor.

“It would save trouble to call me Nettie—everybody does,” said his strange companion; “besides, you are my brother in a kind of a way, and the only person I can consult with; for, of course, it would not do to tell one’s difficulties to strangers. Fred may not be very much to depend upon, you know, but still he is Fred.”

===Chinese===

“*眼下*派不上。我看他从来就没大派上过用场，”气鼓鼓的妮蒂说；“不过他刚到殖民地那阵子，人人都说他非常能干。我真说不上苏珊为什么嫁他。她的性子其实极有主意，尽管你准会当她是百依百顺。她就是那种人，您知道，一不顺心就闹病，动不动拿死来吓唬人，闹得人不敢拂逆她。好啦，那么，把他们怎么办呢？他不肯回殖民地去，我自己又不情愿去。难道非得我把他们留在这儿？”

“安德伍德小姐（Miss Underwood）——”给难住的医生刚开口说。

“叫我妮蒂倒省事——人人都这么叫，”他这位古怪的同伴说；“再说，你也算得上我半个哥哥，又是我唯一能商量事的人；因为，当然，自己的难处总不能讲给外人听。弗雷德这个人指靠不大上，您知道，可他到底是弗雷德。”

===Original===

“Yes,” said the doctor, with a little self-reproach, “still he is Fred; but pardon me, the name suggests long aggravations. You can’t tell how often I have had to put up with affronts and injuries because it was Fred. I shouldn’t like to grieve you—”

“Never mind about grieving me;—*I* am not in love with him;—let me hear all about it!” said Nettie.

Dr. Rider paused a little; seeing the abyss upon the brink of which this brave little girl was standing, he had not the heart to aggravate her by telling the failures of the past. Better to soften the inevitable discovery if possible. But his hesitation was quite apparent to Nettie. With considerable impatience she turned round upon him.

===Chinese===

“是啊，”医生说，带着几分自责，“他到底是弗雷德；不过请恕我直言，这个名字叫人想起的是一长串的气恼。您想不到，就因为他叫弗雷德，我咽下过多少回羞辱和损害。我不忍叫您伤心——”

“别管我伤心不伤心；——*我*又没有爱上他；——把事情原原本本讲给我听吧！”妮蒂说。

莱德医生顿了顿；眼见这个勇敢的小姑娘正站在深渊边上，他不忍心再把往日的败绩一件件抖出来，加重她的伤心。若能办到，还是让那桩不可避免的揭穿来得和缓些为好。可他的迟疑在妮蒂眼里一清二楚。她相当不耐烦地转过身来冲着他。

===Original===

“If you think I don’t know what I am doing, but have gone into this business like a fool, you are quite mistaken, Dr. Edward,” she said, a little sharply. “I see how it is as well as anybody can do. I knew how it was when I left the colony. Don’t be alarmed about me. Do you think I am to be turned against my own flesh and blood by finding out their follies; or to grumble at the place God put me in?—Nothing of the sort! I know the kind of situation perfectly—but one *may* make the best of it, you know: and for that reason tell me everything, please.”

“But, Miss Underwood, consider,” cried the doctor, in consternation. “You are taking responsibilities upon yourself which nobody could lay upon you; you! young—tender” (the doctor paused for a word, afraid to be too complimentary)—“delicate! Why, the whole burden of this family will come upon you. There is not one able to help himself in the whole bundle! I am shocked!—I am alarmed!—I don’t know what to say to you—”

“Don’t say anything, please,” said Nettie. “I know what I am about. Do you call this a street or a lane, or what do you call it? Oh, such nice houses! shouldn’t I like to be able to afford to have one of them, and nurses, and governesses, and everything proper for the children! I should like to dress them so nicely, and give them such a good education. I don’t know anything particular to speak of, myself—I shall never be able to teach them when they grow older. If Fred, now, was only to be trusted, and would go and work like a man and make something for the children, I daresay I could keep up the house;—but if he won’t do anything, you know, it will take us every farthing just to live. Look here, Dr. Edward: I have two hundred a-year;—Susan had the same, you know, but Fred got all the money when they were married, and muddled it away. Now, how much can one do in Carlingford with three children upon two hundred a-year?”

===Chinese===

“您要是以为我不知道自己在干什么，是像个傻子似的闯进这桩事里来的，那您就大错特错了，爱德华医生，”她说，口气有点儿尖利。“事情是什么情形，我看得同任何人一样清楚。离开殖民地的时候，我就知道是什么情形。不必替我担惊。您以为，发现了自家骨肉的愚妄，我就该同他们离心背德；或是埋怨上帝把我安放在什么位置？——全没有的事！我对自己这样的处境清楚得很——可人*总还*能尽力把日子过好，您知道：所以就为这个，请把一切讲给我听吧。”

“可是，安德伍德小姐，您掂量掂量，”医生惊慌失措地喊道。“您这是把谁也没法强加给您的责任揽到自己肩上；您！这样年轻——柔嫩”（医生斟酌字眼，停了一停，怕恭维得太过了）——“娇弱！唉，这一大家子的担子都要落到您身上。这一窝里头，没有一个养活得了自己！我震惊！——我担忧！——我不知道该对您说什么才好——”

“请什么都别说，”妮蒂说。“我知道自己在做什么。您管这个叫大街还是小巷，还是管它叫什么？哦，多好的房子！我要是能住得起其中一座，再配上保姆、家庭女教师，孩子们该有的样样齐全，那该多美！我要把他们打扮得漂漂亮亮，让他们受那么好的教育。我自己谈不上有什么拿得出手的学问——等他们长大了，我压根儿教不了他们。要是弗雷德呢，靠得住，肯像个男子汉那样去做工，为孩子们挣出一份家业，我敢说我还能把这个家撑持起来；——可要是他什么都不肯做，您知道，光维持生活就得把我们每一个小钱都花光。您听着，爱德华医生：我一年有两百镑；——苏珊本来也有这个数，您知道，可他们结婚的时候，钱全叫弗雷德拿了去，糊里糊涂败了个精光。如今，在卡林福德，带着三个孩子，靠一年两百镑，能办得了多少事？”

===Original===

“Fred will be the meanest blackguard in existence,” cried the doctor, “if he takes his living from you.”

“He took his living from *you*, it appears,” said Nettie, coolly, “and did not thank you much. We must make the best of him. We can’t help ourselves. Now, there is the pretty church, and there is our little house. Come in with me and answer for me, Dr. Edward. You can say I am your sister-in-law, you know, and then, perhaps, we can get into possession at once; for,” said Nettie, suddenly turning round upon the doctor with her brilliant eyes shining out quaintly under the little brow all puckered into curves of foresight, “it is so sadly expensive living where we are now.”

===Chinese===

“弗雷德要是把你们的生计也夺了去，”医生嚷道，“那他就是天底下最卑劣的无赖。”

“照看起来，他的生计是从*您*手里拿去的，”妮蒂冷冷地说，“也没怎么谢您。我们只好将就着他。此外别无办法。瞧，那就是那座好看的教堂，那儿就是我们的小房子。陪我一块儿进去，替我作个保，爱德华医生。您就说我是您的姻亲妹子，您知道，这样也许咱们马上就能把房子接过来住了；因为，”妮蒂说，说着突然朝医生转过身来，一双明亮的眸子古怪地闪着光，衬着那小巧的、全皱成一道道深谋远虑弧线的额头，“眼下住的地方，开销实在贵得叫人发愁。”

===Original===

To look at the creature thus flashing those shining eyes, not without a smile lurking in their depths, upon him—to see the triumphant, undaunted, undoubting youthfulness which never dreamt of failure—to note that pretty anxiety, the look which might have become a bride in her first troubles ‘playing at housekeeping,’ and think how desperate was the position she had assumed, how dreary the burden she had taken upon her—was almost too much for the doctor’s self-control. He did not know whether to admire the little heroine as half-divine, or to turn from her as half-crazy. Probably, had the strange little spirit possessed a different frame, the latter was the sentiment which would have influenced the unimaginative mind of Edward Rider. But there was no resisting that little brown Titania, with her little head overladen with its beautiful hair, her red, delicate mouth closing firm and sweet above that little decided chin, her eyes which seemed to concentrate the light. She seemed only a featherweight when the bewildered doctor helped her to alight—an undoubted sprite and creature of romance. But to hear her arranging about all the domestic necessities within, and disclosing her future plans for the children, and all the order of that life of which she took the charge so unhesitatingly, bewildered the mistress of the house as much as it did the wondering doctor. The two together stood gazing at her as she moved about the room, pouring forth floods of eager talk. Her words were almost as rapid as her step—her foot, light as it was, almost as decided and firm as her resolutions. She was a wonder to behold as she pushed about the furniture, and considered how it could be brightened up and made more comfortable. Gazing at her with his silent lips apart, Dr. Rider sighed at the word. Comfortable! Was she to give her mind to making Fred and his children comfortable—such a creature as this? Involuntarily it occurred to Edward that, under such ministrations, sundry changes might come over the aspect of that prim apartment in which he had seen her first; the room with the bookcase and the red curtains, and the prints over the mantelpiece—a very tidy, comfortable room before any bewitching imp came to haunt it, and whisper suspicions of its imperfection—the doctor’s own retirement, where he had chewed the cud of sweet and bitter fancies often enough, without much thought of his surroundings. But Nettie now had taken possession of the prosaic place, and, all unconscious of that spiritual occupation, was as busy and as excited about Mrs. Smith’s lodgings at St. Roque’s Cottage as if it were an ideal home she was preparing, and the life to be lived in it was the brightest and most hopeful in the world.

===Chinese===

瞧着这个小东西就这样把一双亮眼睛——眼底深处还潜伏着一丝笑意——向他闪过来；看着那副志在必得、毫无畏惧、从不生疑的青春神气，做梦也不曾梦见过失败；留意到那份好看的忧心——那神情，本该属于一个在最初的烦难里“玩家家操持家务”的新嫁娘——再想想她给自己揽下的处境何等险恶，她挑起的担子何等凄惶——这几乎是医生的克制力再也承受不住的了。他不知道，该把这位小女杰当作半是神明来敬慕，还是该把她当半个疯子，掉头而去。这个奇异的小精灵倘若生就另一副身形，那么多半便是后一种情绪，会支配爱德华·莱德那缺乏想象力的头脑了。可是谁也拗不过那只棕色的小泰坦妮亚（Titania）：小小的脑袋叫一头秀发压得沉甸甸，红润纤巧的小嘴，在那份果决的小下巴上方抿得又坚定又甜美，一双眼睛仿佛能把光都聚拢到里头。晕头转向的医生扶她下车时，她轻得仿佛只有一根羽毛的分量——一个如假包换的小精灵，传奇故事的造物。可是听着她在屋里安排一切家用必需的琐事，听她把为孩子们筹划的将来，以及她毫不迟疑接管的整个生活章程一一和盘托出，宅子的女主人听得同那个惊愕不已的医生一样发懵。两个人一道站在那里，望着她在屋里走来走去，倾泻出滔滔不绝的热切谈锋。她的话语几乎同她的脚步一样迅疾——她那双轻盈的脚，也几乎同她的决心一样果断、坚定。看她把家具推来挪去，盘算着怎样能拾掇得鲜亮些、更舒服些，真是一大奇观。莱德医生默默张着嘴唇凝望她，听到那个字眼，不由长叹了一声。舒服！竟要叫这样一个人物去操心，把弗雷德和他的孩子们安顿得舒舒服服？爱德华不由自主地起了念头：经过这样的调理，他初见她的那间古板体面的屋子，说不定会改换出好些新模样；那间有书柜、有红窗帘、壁炉架上挂着版画的屋子——在任何魅惑人的小妖精跑来作祟、悄悄挑剔它不完满之前，本是间十分齐整、舒服的屋子——医生自己的退居静地，他在那里多少回咀嚼那些又甜又苦的遐想，却不大理会四周的陈设。可如今妮蒂已经把这平淡无奇的地方占了去，对她这场精神上的占领浑然不觉，正为史密斯太太（Mrs. Smith）在圣罗克小屋（St. Roque’s Cottage）的这处赁屋忙得起劲、兴奋不已，仿佛那是她正在布置的一个理想的家，仿佛将要在这里过起来的日子，是世上最明亮、最有盼头的日子。

When Dr. Rider reached home that night, and took his lonely meal in his lonely room, certain bitter thoughts of unequal fortune occupied the young man’s mind. Let a fellow be but useless, thankless, and heartless enough, and people spring up on all sides to do his work for him, said the doctor to himself, with a bitterness as natural as it was untrue. The more worthless a fellow is, the more all the women connected with him cling to him and make excuses for him, said Edward Rider in his indignant heart. Mother and sister in the past—wife and Nettie now—to think how Fred had secured for himself such perpetual ministrations, by neglecting all the duties of life! No wonder an indignant pang transfixed the lonely bosom of the virtuous doctor, solitary and unconsoled as he was. *His* laborious days knew no such solace. And as he fretted and pondered, no visions of Bessie Christian perplexed his thoughts. He had forgotten that young woman. All his mind was fully occupied chafing at the sacrifice of Nettie. He was not sorry, he was angry, to think of her odd position, and the duties she had taken upon herself. What had she to do with those wretched children, and that faded spiteful mother? Edward Rider was supremely disgusted. He said to himself, with the highest moral indignation, that such a girl ought not to be permitted to tie herself to such a fate.

那天夜里，莱德医生回到家中，在自己那间冷清的屋里吃他冷清的饭食，一些关于命运不公的辛酸念头占据了年轻人的心。一个人只要够无用、够忘恩、够没心肝，四面八方自会有人跳出来替他把事办了，医生对自己说，那份酸楚来得自然，却于事理不合。一个人越是不中用，同他沾亲带故的女人们就越是死缠住他，处处替他开脱，爱德华·莱德在心里愤愤地说。从前是母亲和妹妹——如今是妻子和妮蒂——想想弗雷德吧，把人生的一切本分都置之不理，倒给自己挣下了这份永不断绝的伺候！难怪一阵愤懑的刺痛扎透了这位品行端方的医生的孤寂胸膛——他孤身一人，无人慰藉。*他*那些劳碌的日子，可从没尝过这样的安慰。而且在他烦恼寻思的时候，也没有贝西·克里斯琴（Bessie Christian）的影子来搅扰他的思绪。他已经把那位年轻女人忘了。他满副心肠都被一件事占着：为妮蒂的牺牲愤愤不平。想到她那古怪的处境、她揽到自己身上的那些职分，他不是难过，而是气愤。那些可怜巴巴的孩子，那个憔悴而怨毒的母亲，同她有什么相干？爱德华·莱德厌恶到了极点。他怀着最崇高的道义愤慨对自己说：这样的姑娘，决不该容许她把自己拴到这样一种命运上。
