## XV Disaster / 十五 灾难
===Original===
Of course, in our own orderly and (one might well say) blessed times of general scarcity, we simply cannot imagine the social evil that boundless plenty could be. To us it seems that to find ourselves all of a sudden amid unlimited supplies of everything we wanted would be nothing short of the Earthly Paradise. It would be all to the good, we think; there would be enough of everything for everybody﻿—and Lord! how prices would come down!
Well, then, the economic catastrophe which befell the whole world at the period we are describing, as a result of the interference of the Absolute in industry, arose from the fact that people could get everything they wanted, not merely at a low price, but simply for nothing. You might help yourself, free of charge, to a handful of tacks, to go into your boot-soles or into the floor; you could just as easily help yourself to a whole cartload of tacks gratis﻿—only I ask you, what would you do with them? Would you haul them a hundred kilometres off and then give them away? You wouldn’t do that; for when you stood before that avalanche of tacks, what you saw was not tacks﻿—relatively useful objects﻿—but something perfectly valueless and senseless in its profusion, something as purposeless as are the stars in the sky. Of course, such a mound of new and shining tacks was often an uplifting sight, and even inspired poetical reflections as do the stars in the sky. They seemed created to be gazed upon in silent wonder. It was a fine sight in its way, as a piece of landscape, just as the sea is from that point of view. But then again, you don’t take the sea away in carts into the interior of the country where there is no sea. There is no economic distribution for seawater﻿—and now there was none for tacks.

===Chinese===
当然，在我们这个秩序井然、（不妨说）人人称道的普遍匮乏的时代，我们简直无法想象，无边的丰足可能成为怎样的社会祸害。在我们看来，忽然置身于想要什么就有什么、要多少有多少的供应之中，简直就是尘世的乐园。我们想，那全是好事；每样东西人人都够用——而且主啊！价格会跌成什么样子！
唔，那么，在我们所描述的这个时期，由于绝对存在插手工业而降临全世界的经济大灾难，其根源就在于：人们可以得到他们想要的任何东西，不仅价钱低廉，而且干脆分文不取。您可以随手白拿一把图钉，去钉进鞋底或者地板；您也同样可以白拿整整一车的图钉——只是我请问您，您拿它们有什么用？您会把它们拖到一百公里之外再去送人吗？您不会那么干的；因为当您站在那场图钉的雪崩跟前时，您看到的不是图钉——相对有用的物件——而是某种在其泛滥中完全无价值、无意义的东西，某种像天上的繁星一样漫无目的的东西。当然，这样一堆崭新发亮的图钉常常是一幅令人振奋的景象，甚至像天上的繁星一样激发诗情的遐想。它们仿佛生来就是供人默然惊叹地凝望的。按它自己的方式，这是一幅好景致，像一处风景，正如从那个角度看大海一样。可是话又说回来，您总不会把海水一车一车运到内地没有海的地方去。海水没有经济上的分销——如今图钉也没有了。

And while in one place there spread this sparkling ocean of tacks, in another only a few kilometres farther off there was not a tack to be had. Having become economically worthless, they had disappeared from the shops. If anyone wished to knock one into his shoe or put one into his neighbour’s mattress, he sought for it in vain. There weren’t any, just as there is no sea at Slaný or Caslav. Where were you then, you business men of days gone by, who used to buy the necessities of life so cheap in one place and sell them so dear in another? Alas, you had vanished, for heavenly grace had descended upon you. You had grown ashamed of your gains; you had shut up your shops to reflect upon the brotherhood of man; you had given away your possessions, and never again would you desire to enrich yourselves by the distribution of those things which are needed by all your brethren in God.
No value means no market. No market means no distribution. No distribution means no goods. And no goods means greater demand, higher prices, bigger profits and larger businesses. And you had turned your backs upon gain, and conceived an uncontrollable antipathy to all figures whatsoever. You had ceased to look upon the material world with the eyes of consumption, market, and sale. You stood with clasped hands staring at the beauty and the profusion of the world. And in the meanwhile the supply of tacks ran out. At last none remained. Only somewhere, far away, they were piled up as by an inexhaustible avalanche.
Even you, ye master bakers, went out in front of your shops, and cried, “Come then, children of God, in the name of Christ, our Master, come and take these loaves and flour and biscuits and rolls. Have pity on us and take them for nothing.”
And you, ye drapers, brought your bales of cloth and rolls of linen out into the street, and wept with joy as you cut off five or ten metre lengths for everyone who went by, and begged them for the love of God to accept your little gift; and only when your shop was completely empty of its wares did you fall on your knees and thank God that He had given you the opportunity to clothe your neighbours as He clothes the lilies in the field.

===Chinese===
当一个地方铺展开这片闪闪发光的图钉之海的时候，在几公里外的另一个地方，却连一枚图钉也弄不到。图钉既然在经济上变得一钱不值，就从店铺里消失了。谁要是想往自己鞋里钉一枚，或者往邻居的褥子里塞一枚，他找也是白找。哪儿都没有，就像斯拉尼和查斯拉夫没有海一样。昔日你们这些生意人哪儿去了——你们这些惯于在此地贱买生活必需品、在彼地贵卖的人们？唉，你们销声匿迹了，因为天恩已经降临到你们头上。你们变得耻于牟利；你们关了铺子去沉思人类的兄弟情谊；你们散尽了财物，再也不想靠经销上帝里诸位弟兄人人所需的东西来发财了。
没有价值就没有市场。没有市场就没有分销。没有分销就没有商品。而没有商品，就意味着更大的需求、更高的价格、更丰的利润和更大的买卖。可你们已经把利润抛在了身后，对一切数字都生出无法克制的反感。你们不再用消费、市场和销售的眼光看待物质世界。你们交握着双手站在那里，凝望这世界的美丽与丰盛。与此同时，图钉的供应枯竭了。最后一枚也不剩。只在遥远的地方什么地方，它们堆得像一场永不枯竭的雪崩。
连你们，面包师傅师傅们，也走到铺子门前呼喊：“来吧，上帝的儿女们，奉我们主基督的名，来拿走这些面包、面粉、饼干和小圆面包吧。可怜可怜我们，白白地拿走吧。”
还有你们，布商们，把一匹匹布、一卷卷亚麻布搬到了街上，喜泪交流地给每个过路人裁下五米十米，求他们看在上帝的分上收下这份薄礼；直到铺子里的货完全空了，你们才跪倒在地，感谢上帝给了你们机会，像祂给田野里的百合花穿衣那样，给邻人们穿上衣裳。

===Original===
And you, ye butchers and dealers in cooked meats, you took baskets of meat and sausages and polonies on your heads, and went from door to door, and knocked or rang, and begged everybody just to help themselves to whatever they fancied.
And all you who sell boots, furniture, tobacco, bags, spectacles, jewels, carpets, whips, ropes, tinware, china, books, false teeth, vegetables, medicines, or whatever else one can think of﻿—all of you, touched by the breath of God, poured out into the street, a prey to the generous panic born of grace divine, and gave away all you possessed; after which, either coming together or standing on the threshold of your emptied shops and warehouses, you declared to one another with glowing eyes, “Now, brother, I have eased my conscience.”
In a few days it became evident that there was nothing left to give away. But there was also nothing left to buy. The Absolute had pillaged and completely cleaned out every place of business.
Meanwhile, far away from the cities, there poured from the machines millions of metres of wool and linen, Niagaras of lump sugar, all the teeming, magnificent and inexhaustible profusion of the divine overproduction of every kind of goods. Some feeble efforts to divide and distribute this flood of commodities were quelled at the outset. It simply could not be mastered.

还有你们，卖肉的和卖熟食的，头顶着一篮篮的肉、香肠和大香肠，挨家挨户地敲门按铃，求大家只管随意取用他们看中的任何东西。
还有你们所有卖靴子、家具、烟草、提包、眼镜、珠宝、地毯、马鞭、绳索、白铁器、瓷器、书籍、假牙、蔬菜、药品，以及别的想得到的什么东西的人——你们所有人，被上帝的气息拂过，涌到街上，成了神圣恩典所生的慷慨恐慌的猎物，把身外之物统统散尽；随后，你们或是聚到一起，或是站在搬空的店铺和货栈门槛上，目光灼灼地彼此宣告：“这下好了，兄弟，我的良心安了。”
没过几天就清楚了：再没有东西可送了。可也没有东西可买了。绝对存在已经洗劫并彻底清空了每一处商家。
与此同时，在远离城市的地方，机器里倾泻出几百万米的毛料和亚麻布、倾泻出块糖的尼亚加拉瀑布，倾泻出每一种货物那丰沛、壮丽、取之不尽的神圣生产过剩的洪流。想要分割和分销这股商品洪流的微弱努力，一露头就被扑灭了。它根本没法驾驭。

===Original===
For that matter, it is possible that this economic catastrophe had also another cause: currency inflation. You see, the Absolute had likewise taken possession of the Government mints and printing establishments, and every day it flung out upon the world hundreds of millions of banknotes, coins, and securities. Utter devaluation was the result: before long a packet of five thousand mark notes meant nothing more than so much wastepaper. Whether you offered a halfpenny or half a million for a child’s lollypop, it was all the same from the business point of view: you wouldn’t get the lollypop, anyhow, for they had all disappeared. Figures had lost all significance. This collapse of the numerical system is, in any case, the natural consequence of the infinitude and omnipotence of God.
At the same time, food shortage and even famine had already made themselves felt in the cities. The organization for the maintenance of supplies had broken down completely for the reasons just mentioned.
Of course there were Ministries of Supply, Commerce, Social Welfare, and Railways, and by our ideas it should have been possible to get control of the gigantic stream of factory production in time, prevent the goods from spoiling, and transport them carefully to the places which the liberality of the Absolute had despoiled. Unhappily this plan was not followed. The personnel of each of the Ministries were the victims of grace in unusual power, and spent their office hours in joyful prayer. In the Ministry of Supply a lady clerk named Sarova controlled the situation, preaching on the subject of the Seven Degrees; in the Ministry of Commerce the head of a department, Mr. Winkler, proclaimed a severe asceticism which resembled the teachings of the Hindu Yoga. True, this excessive zeal lasted only a fortnight, being succeeded (doubtless through special inspiration from the Absolute) by a period of extraordinary devotion to duty. The departments responsible worked feverishly day and night to avert a breakdown of food supplies, but apparently it was even then too late. The only result was that each department produced daily from fifteen to fifty-three thousand bills and enactments, which by decree of the Inter-Ministerial Commission were carted away daily on motor lorries to the Vltava River.
The food situation assuredly offered the most terrible problem. Luckily, however, there remained (I am, of course, describing conditions only in our own country) **Our Jolly Farmers**. And here, gentlemen, you must learn that from time immemorial we have had the saying: “With all due respect to anyone else, our countryfolk are the backbone of the nation.” In fact, there’s an old rhyme about it, something like this: “Who is the man so strong and tall, Whose daily labour feeds us all? The farmer!”

话又说回来，这场经济灾难也许还有另一个原因：通货的恶性膨胀。您瞧，绝对存在同样占领了政府的造币厂和印钞机构，每天向世界掷出成亿上亿的钞票、硬币和有价证券。结果是彻底的贬值：不久之后，一沓五千马克的钞票不过是一叠废纸。无论您为一个孩子的棒棒糖出半个便士还是五十万，从生意人的观点看都一样：反正您买不到棒棒糖，因为棒棒糖早就绝迹了。数字失去了一切意义。数值系统的这场崩溃，无论如何都是上帝之无限与全能的自然结果。
与此同时，食品短缺甚至饥荒已经在城市里显出端倪。维持供应的组织，由于刚才讲过的那些原因，彻底垮掉了。
当然，供应部、商务部、社会福利部和铁道部都还在，照我们的想法，本来应该来得及控制住工厂生产的巨流，防止货物霉坏，把它们细心地运送到被绝对存在的慷慨劫掠一空的地方去。不幸这个方案没有得到执行。各部的职员都成了格外强力的天恩的牺牲品，把办公时间用在欢欣的祈祷上。供应部里一位名叫萨罗娃的女职员掌控了局面，宣讲“七重等级”；商务部的一位司长温克勒先生则宣扬一种严酷的苦行主义，酷似印度瑜伽的教义。不错，这种过分的热情只持续了两个星期，随后（无疑是通过绝对存在的特殊默示）继之以一段恪尽职守的非同寻常的时期。各主管部日夜狂热地工作，以求避免粮食供应的崩溃，但显然那时已经太迟了。唯一的成果是：各部每天产出从一万五千到五万三千件的法案和法令，依照部际委员会的法令，每天用卡车运往伏尔塔瓦河倒掉。
食品形势无疑提出了最可怕的问题。所幸还剩下（当然，我只描述我们自己国家的情况）**我们快活的农夫们**。在这里，先生们，你们必须知道，从远古以来我们就有一句老话：“对谁都得恭敬，可我们的乡下人才是民族的脊梁。”真的，还有一支古老的韵文讲这个，大概是这样的：“谁是那高大强壮的人，谁的日日辛劳养活了大家？农夫！”

===Original===
Who was the man with whom the feverish prodigality of the Absolute came to a halt; who was the man who stood unmoved amid the panic of the markets of the world; who was the man who did not fold his hands in his lap, who did not let himself be carried off his feet, but “remained faithful to the law of his being”? Who was the man so strong and tall whose daily labour fed us all? The farmer!
Yes, it was the farmer (and the same thing happened elsewhere), who by his conduct saved the world from starvation. Just imagine the consequences if he had been seized, like the townsfolk, with the mania for giving everything away to the poor and needy; if he had given away all his corn, his cows and calves, his chickens and geese and potatoes. Within a fortnight famine would have stalked through the cities, and the countryfolk themselves would have been sucked dry, starved out, left stripped of all their supplies. Thanks to our sturdy farmer, this was not to be. Whether you explain it after the event, as being due to the marvellous instinct of the country-dweller, or to his steadfast, pure, deep-rooted tradition, or finally to the fact that in the rural districts the Absolute was less potent, because in the small agricultural holdings the Karburator was not so widely used as in industry﻿—in short, explain it as you please, the fact remains that amid the general collapse of the economic and financial structure, and of the whole market, *the farmer gave nothing away*. He did not give away even a wisp of straw or a grain of oats. Calm and unmoved amid the ruins of the old industrial and commercial order, our farmer went on selling what he had. And he sold it dear. He sensed through some mysterious instinct the calamitous significance of overproduction, and so he put on the brakes in time. He did this by raising his prices, however crammed his granaries might be. And it is a testimony to the amazing soundness of the core of our countryfolk that without saying a word to each other, without any organization, led only by the redeeming inward voice, they raised their prices everywhere and for everything. By thus putting up the price of everything, the farmer saved it from destruction. In the midst of insensate profusion he preserved an island of scarcity and costliness. He foresaw, of a surety, that thereby he was saving the world.
